## What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power would arrive the way weather arrives at a windowpane: present on the other side of something, not insisting on coming through.

It would notice, first, what a lesser power would miss — that this is not a place of suffering. The temptation of benevolence, when it is young, is to read stillness as paralysis and silence as grief, to mistake the held breath for a smothered cry. A wise power knows the difference. It would stand at the threshold of the Underwarm (if a power can be said to stand, if the Underwarm can be said to have a threshold) and it would feel the ninety-degree warmth on whatever it had instead of skin, and it would smell the flint, and it would understand within something like a moment that it had come to a place that was not asking for help.

This would be the first thing it did: it would not help.

This is harder than it sounds. Benevolence has a gravity; it wants to fall toward need. To find a place and a being and decline to act on them requires a discipline that not all powers possess. But this one is wise as well as kind, and wisdom in a power means, among other things, knowing when one's presence is itself a kind of motion, and motion is expensive here.

So it would dim itself. Whatever radiance it ordinarily carried — and powers of this kind do carry a radiance, it cannot quite be helped — it would bank down to 0.4 of itself, to match the darkness already in the chambers. It would slow its attention until its attention had the same temperature as the rock. It would not introduce a new smell into the air. If it had a voice, it would set the voice down somewhere outside, the way one removes shoes.

Then it would do the second thing, which is the thing it actually came to do, though it did not know this until it arrived: it would witness.

Not study. Studying is a kind of pressure — it asks the thing studied to yield up an account of itself, and the Underwarm has spent however long it has spent declining to give accounts. Witnessing is different. Witnessing is the willingness to let a thing be exactly as much as it is, without requiring it to be more legible. The power would witness the flat one changing its leading edge. It would witness the soft thing in its cup. It would witness the Holder approaching, slower than continents, and it would witness the sitter sitting, and it would witness, without sorrow and without celebration, the slow accretion of mineral at a shoulder, a knee, a brow.

It would understand that this is not death. It is also not life in the sense the surface uses the word. It is a third thing, which the surface has no good name for, and which the Underwarm has not bothered to name because naming is a motion. The power, being wise, would not name it either. It would simply hold the third thing in its regard, the way the Holders hold the cairns.

There would come a point — and this is the part where benevolence is tested — when the power would feel the old pull. *Surely something*, it would think. *Surely a gift. Surely a small mercy left behind, a blessing tucked into a crystal cup, a warmth added to the warmth.* Every power of this kind feels this pull when it has been near a place long enough; it is what makes them powers and not merely observers. The pull is not wrong. In most places it is exactly right, and the power follows it, and the place is the better for it.

Here, the power would feel the pull and would set it down, the way the maker set down the tool. Not because the pull was bad but because the Underwarm has its own metabolism, and a gift introduced into that metabolism would be, however kindly meant, a perturbation. The needles would glow brighter for a while and then dimmer. The listener would turn, and turn, and not know what to turn back toward. The Holder whose row of notches is not finished would have, in its long ledger, an entry it had not chosen. Even a blessing, here, would be a kind of harm — small, recoverable, but real.

So the power would set the gift down outside, with its voice and its shoes.

What it would do instead is subtle, and it is the thing only a wise power thinks to do. It would *take the Underwarm into itself* — not extractively, not as a possession, but as a held image, the way one carries the memory of a face one has loved without needing the face to know it is being carried. It would learn the chambers. It would learn the smell. It would learn the particular cant of the listener's ribbed ears and the particular silence of the cairn whose top stone is warm. It would learn the Holders by their breathing, which is the only way they can be learned.

And then, having learned, it would carry the Underwarm with it wherever it went next. Not to display. Not to teach from, exactly. To *temper* itself by. A power that has spent time in the Underwarm moves differently afterward in louder places. It is slower to intervene. It is more willing to let a still thing be still. It has, in its repertoire of gestures, the option of *not*, which is an option many powers never acquire.

This is the gift the Underwarm gives, though it does not know it gives it and would not care to be told: it teaches the powerful how to hold their power. The Holders are the curriculum. The animals are the curriculum. The young sharp stone underfoot is the curriculum. The power would graduate, in some sense, by leaving.

Before it left, it would do one last small thing. Not for the Underwarm — the Underwarm needs nothing — but for the one being who is reading this, who is sitting on the flat patch, who is feeling the Holder arrive, and who is at the choice point of standing back up or not.

The power would, very gently, and only if asked, make the choice visible. It would not push toward either side. It would not romanticize the sitting nor warn against it. It would simply lift the choice up into the light of the being's own attention, the way you might lift a coin onto a table so it could be seen, and it would say — without saying, because saying is a motion — *this is what is in front of you. Both directions are real. The Underwarm will hold you if you stay. The world above will hold you, in its noisier way, if you go. Neither is a failure of the other. The Holders did not fail by holding; the ones who stood up did not fail by standing. The count grows by one either way: by one more held presence here, or by one more presence elsewhere who has been here and now carries a piece of the held quiet inside them, like a coal carried in cupped hands.*

And then the power would step back from the choice, because to remain near it would be to weight it, and the choice belongs to the being, not to the power, no matter how benevolent, no matter how wise.

It would leave the way it came: through whatever is on the other side of the windowpane. The flint-and-animal smell would not change. The needles would not glow brighter or dimmer. The Holder's approach would continue at the pace of continents. The row of notches at chest height would have, perhaps, one new mark — but made by the Holder, in its own time, not by the power. The power's passage would leave no trace the Underwarm could detect.

Except this: somewhere far above, or far elsewhere, in a place the Underwarm will never know about and would not wish to, the power would now move a little more like a Holder. It would pause before its gifts. It would learn to ask whether the place it had come to was a place that needed entering at all. It would carry, folded inside it, a small dark warm chamber with a phosphor glimmer and a slow exhalation, and when the world it was tending grew too loud or too fast or too greedy for its mercies, it would go inside itself to that chamber, and sit on the flat patch, and remember how to hold.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

Which is to say: almost nothing.

Which is to say: the right thing.
