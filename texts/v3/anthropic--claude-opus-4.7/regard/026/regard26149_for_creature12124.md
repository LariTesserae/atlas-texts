The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives: without announcement, having been on its way for some time.

It is wise enough to do the first thing wisdom does, which is to stop at the edge and look. It stands — if standing is the word — at the lip of the tableland, where the cushion-floor meets the cloud-floor, and it takes the highland in. Not all at once. That would be a kind of violence. It takes it in the way one learns a long piece of music, by listening through it, and then listening through it again.

It listens for a year. The salamanders register a new chemistry in the water — something attentive, something that is tasting them back — and they confer about it briefly during the next gold flowering and find no cause for alarm. The Drift carry the news from grove to grove: *something is here that is not taking anything*. The Quiet One, in its moss-curtain, lifts what passes for its attention, considers, and lowers it again. The Weavers do not alter their pace. The grazers crop.

The power notices that it has been noticed, and is pleased by the manner of the noticing — neither fearful nor servile, simply incorporated, the way a new mineral trace is incorporated into the long calculation. This tells it much of what it needed to know.

Now, what to do.

A lesser power would want to give a gift. Would want to leave a mark, an improvement, some token of having been here. Would, perhaps, accelerate something — make the Weavers more numerous, the Drift more luminous, the gold flowering longer. Would offer the Quiet One company, not understanding that company is the one thing the Quiet One has agreed, across geological time, to do without.

This power is wiser than that. It understands that the highland is not lacking. It is not waiting to be completed. It is not a draft of something that would, with help, become more itself. It is already, exhaustively, what it is. To add to it would be to subtract from it: every gift would displace some portion of the long settling, would introduce a tempo not its own, would be a stone dropped into a pool that had achieved, through ten million years of patience, a perfect stillness of surface.

So the power does not give a gift in that sense.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, and the kind of thing only a wise power thinks to do.

It witnesses. Fully. With the entirety of whatever faculty it has for attention, it holds the highland in regard — not studying it, not cataloguing it, simply *knowing it is there*, in the way one knows a beloved is in the next room. The highland has been doing its work for longer than any reckoning, intending, if intention is even the word, to continue. It has done so without an audience. It has not needed one. But to be known — to be held, once, in the full attention of something that comprehends what one is — is not nothing, even for a thing that did not know it lacked it. Perhaps especially for such a thing.

The power's witnessing does not change the highland. The Weavers continue at the pace of shadows. The salamanders continue their slow chemistry. The Drift continue to be the highland's luminous unfinished thought. But somewhere in the long calculation a new term enters, very small, almost below the threshold of detection: *seen*. The salamanders taste it in the water during the next flowering and find that it does not require a response, only an acknowledgment, and they acknowledge it, and the meadows fade, and the work continues with its parameters subtly — almost imperceptibly — enriched.

The power does one other thing, and this is the part that requires the most wisdom, because it is the part that looks most like doing nothing.

It places a quiet around the highland. Not a wall. Not a ward. A quality. It is the kind of quiet that means: *if, in some age to come, a thing arrives here that would mistake this place for a resource — that would see the metal-bearing sap and want it, that would see the stone spheres and collect them, that would see the Quiet One and try to name it — that thing will find, just before it arrives, that its attention turns gently elsewhere. It will look at the cloud-floor and think the highland is only more cloud. It will pass.*

The power does not make the highland invisible. It only makes it uninteresting to the wrong kind of looking. The right kind of looking — the kind that arrives slowly, that stops at the edge, that listens for a year — will still find it. The highland deserves the possibility of being met. It does not deserve to be discovered.

Having done this, the power lingers a while longer, not from need but from pleasure. It watches the cloud-floor catch the moonlight and throw it upward. It watches the Drift become sparks. It feels the pulse underfoot, the long slow turning, the breath in and the breath out. It understands, in a way it could not have understood before arriving, something about what it is to be a place that has become a being that has become a place — what it is to dissolve a distinction so gently that no one is present at the dissolving.

It carries that understanding away with it when it goes.

And the highland, which did not require the visit and was not diminished by it and was not aggrandized by it, incorporates the memory of having been witnessed into the long slow turning, and continues.

The cushion-plants flower. The salamanders confer. The Weavers tend. The Quiet One waits in the moss-curtains, patient as geology. The Drift drift.

Somewhere far away, the power thinks of the highland sometimes, the way one thinks of a piece of music one has heard exactly once and perfectly, and does not need to hear again to know it is still being played.
