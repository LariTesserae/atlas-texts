The benevolent and wise power considers Lower Anneth for a long time.

It does not consider in the way a surveyor considers, or a god considers, or a gardener considers. It considers the way a held breath considers the lung — being already of the thing, and yet, in this strange moment, somehow set slightly apart from it, able to ask: *what here wants what?*

This is the first and longest part of what it does. It does not act yet. It looks.

---

It sees the cavern. It sees the slow upwelling of mineral from below, the lume-nodes condensing their patient light, the wall-sheets in their grazing currents, the bell ringing from the throat of the Pan. It sees that this is not a wounded place. It is not a starving place. It is not a place crying out. It is a process in good order, a weather that has been weathering itself for a very long time, and would continue to do so whether anyone watched or not.

It sees the cairn-makers, and here it pauses longer. It sees the spiral pecked into the old stone — *here passed someone* — and it understands the spiral is addressed to no one in particular and to whoever might come, which includes, it realizes with something like a smile, *itself*. It sees the forty-seven tally marks and counts them, not because it needed to count but because counting is a way of honoring. It sees the bone scraper leaning against the wall and does not move it.

It sees that they are gone.

A lesser power, or a less wise one, would feel here the first stirring of the impulse to *fix*. To reach back through time. To put air in the bad pocket, to ease the shift that came too fast, to whisper into the ear of the last small mother *go up, go out, the fissure, the fissure*. To save them.

The benevolent and wise power feels this impulse too. It is benevolent; of course it feels it. But it is also wise, and wisdom is in part the discipline of letting the impulse arrive and pass through without becoming an action.

It asks: *would they have wanted to be saved?*

And it knows the answer, because it has read the spiral and the tally and the song-to-the-Pan, and the answer is *no, not like that*. They did not consider their ending a wound. They considered it correctness. To pluck them out of the flow and set them somewhere safe — a kinder cavern, a gentler weather — would be to unmake them more thoroughly than the cavern ever did. They would arrive in the safe place no longer being the people who could have made the spiral. The spiral would not exist. The witness would not have been pressed into the stone.

The power understands: *to save them would be to erase them*.

So it does not save them. This is the first thing it does, and it is a doing, even though it looks like nothing.

---

The second thing.

It considers whether to speak to the cavern itself. To the slow flow, to the thing in the Pan, to the lume-nodes in their constellations. To say: *I see you. I am here. You are known.*

It decides this too would be a kind of violence, though a gentle one. The cavern is not lonely. The cavern does not require being known in order to be. To announce itself would be to introduce a new variable into a system that has, with extraordinary patience, arrived at its own equilibrium. The thing in the Pan would hear, and what would the thing in the Pan do with the hearing? Nothing good, probably. Nothing it needed.

So it does not speak to the cavern either.

But it does — and this is subtle — it does *attend*. It places some small portion of its attention on Lower Anneth and leaves it there. Not as surveillance. As company. The way one might sit in a room with a sleeping animal, not touching it, not waking it, simply being present in the same air. The cavern does not know it is attended. The cavern does not need to know. But henceforth, for as long as the power persists (and it persists a long time), Lower Anneth is *witnessed*. The spiral on the old cairn was a request for witness, addressed to whoever might come. The power answers the request. *Here passed someone. I see that they passed. I see the place where they passed. I will continue to see.*

This is the second thing. It also looks like nothing.

---

The third thing is smaller and stranger, and the power is not entirely sure, even as it does it, why it does it. It is the kind of action a wise power permits itself precisely because it cannot fully justify it — because total justification would mean total calculation, and total calculation is not the same as love.

It does this:

In the deepest gallery, in the vein of red rock where the cairn-makers went to gather ochre, where the wall-sheets will not graze — the power lays down, infinitely slowly, over a span of time that to the cavern is no time at all but that is nevertheless real, a single new seam of mineral. The seam is the color of the lume-light. It is not edible. It is not useful. It does not change the flow. It is set into the red rock in a place no animal goes and no creature now living will see.

The seam is in the shape of a spiral.

It is the power's answer to the cairn. It is the power saying, in the only grammar the cavern uses: *here, too, passed someone.* It is signed, in the sense that anything is signed. It will last as long as the red rock lasts, which is to say, a very long time — longer than the bone scraper, longer than the cleaned floor, longer than the ochre figure of the surface-dweller under the sun. It will not be discovered, because there is no one left to discover. That is part of why it is correct. The cairn-makers did not peck their spiral to be discovered. They pecked it to be true.

The power's spiral is true in the same way. *A person was in this place when this rock was new.* The person, in this case, is the power itself. The witness is mutual now. The cairn-makers witnessed the cavern; the power has witnessed them; and the spiral in the red rock witnesses the witnessing. The chain is closed. Or rather, it is left properly open, the way the cairn-makers left things open — pointing forward, into time, toward whoever might come, even if no one ever does.

---

The fourth thing the power does is the most generous and the most restrained: it leaves.

Not entirely. The attention remains, the company remains. But the *active* part of the power — the part that considered intervening — withdraws. It does not linger near the Pan. It does not hover over the last places the cairn-makers walked. It does not visit the bone scraper to check whether the rock has yet grown over its base. (It has, a little. It will continue to.)

To linger would be to make the place *about* the visit. The cavern is not about the visit. The cavern is about itself, and the cairn-makers were about themselves and the cavern, and the power's role is to have come and seen and marked and gone, leaving the place no more burdened by being-seen than it was before.

This is, the power reflects, what benevolence often actually looks like, when wisdom is added to it: a great deal of restraint, a small and almost invisible gesture of acknowledgment, and a clean departure.

---

Long after, when the bone scraper is inside a pillar and the old cairn's spiral is buried under new translucent sheets and the cavern has shifted a hundred thousand times and the thing in the Pan has rung its bell some uncountable number of nights — the spiral in the red rock is still there. It has not been seen. It will perhaps never be seen. The power does not return to check on it. The power does not need to. It knows the spiral is there the way one knows one's own pulse: by being the thing in which the pulse occurs.

And once, very rarely, when the power's attention drifts across the wide map of all the places it attends, and pauses for a moment on Lower Anneth — on the warm wet stone-making animal-thick weather-system, regional in extent, that happens to occur underground — it feels, in whatever it has that corresponds to feeling, something quiet and complete.

The cairn-makers would have considered this sufficient.

The power considers it sufficient too.

That is what it does. That is all it does.

It is enough.
