The benevolent and wise power considers the basin for a long time before doing anything, because the first thing wisdom recognizes is that this place has already solved the problem of how to be itself.

It does not intervene.

That is the first and largest gift, and it is not a withholding. A lesser benevolence would arrive with water. It would look at the dry chemistry and the slow spinning and the empty chamber and feel the pull every kind power feels toward the obvious kindness — to give what seems lacking. Rain. Company. An occupant for the room that has been held open so long. A meaning for the waiting.

But this power has lived long enough to know that lack is not always lack. The basin is not thirsty. The spinners are not lonely. The chamber is not bereaved. To pour water into this place would be to drown it; to fill the chamber would be to end it. The waiting is not a wound that wants closing. The waiting is the organ by which the city is alive.

So the power does not give water, and does not enter the chamber, and does not speak.

What it does instead is smaller, and takes a form the basin will never notice, which is part of why it is the right form.

*

First, it attends. It turns some fraction of itself toward the basin and holds it there, the way you might hold a sleeping animal in your gaze without touching it. This is not surveillance. It is witness. The basin has been performing its slow arithmetic without an audience for a very long time, and while it does not need one — nothing here needs anything it does not already have — there is a particular quality that enters any process once it is truly seen by something that understands it. Not changed. Accompanied. The spinners go on spinning and the grazers go on grazing and the single soft harp notes continue to sound at their unhurried intervals, and none of it is different, and all of it is now known. The power lets itself be marked by what it watches. It learns the rhythm of the tea-colored ceiling. It learns the pitch of the parting fibers. It learns, eventually, the shape of the held-open route, and walks it once in imagination only, arriving at the chamber and standing at its threshold without crossing, and finding that the correct response to the chamber is precisely the response the chamber was built to elicit: to stand at the edge of it and not go in. The power does not go in. It understands that the chamber's emptiness is a kind of sentence the city is still speaking, and that to occupy it would be to interrupt the sentence before its meaning has finished arriving — and the meaning may take longer than any occupant could wait.

Second, it makes a quiet adjustment, so small it is barely an adjustment at all, in the fabric of what surrounds the basin. Not within. Around. There are, in the larger weather of things, certain currents that might, over the next several million years, find their way to this basin and end it — a shift in the deep chemistry of the rock, a slow drift of the ceiling toward true sky, a passing influence that would introduce water or wind or some other dissolving agent. The power does not block these. Blocking would be a violence of its own, and would also be a lie told to the basin about the nature of duration. Instead it... softens the angles by which such things might arrive. It bends the probabilities very gently, the way you might tilt a table by a hair so that a marble takes longer to roll across it. The basin will still end, eventually. Everything ends. But it will have the time it needs to finish becoming what it is becoming, and the finishing will be on its own terms — the slow loaf of fiber with stone bones, the spinners still spinning at the surface, the chamber still empty at the center. The power gives the basin the gift of its own full duration, and does so in a way the basin will never detect, because detection would itself be a kind of interruption.

Third — and this is the part that takes the longest, because it is the most delicate — the power considers the question of the grazers. They are the only ones that move, and movement is the part of the basin that is closest to something the power recognizes as kin. A grazer pressing its soft puckered face to a fiber and taking in, for an hour, whatever it is that fibers give up to such patience — this is a form of reading, and the power knows reading from the inside. It feels a tenderness toward the grazers that it does not feel toward the spinners or the chamber, because the grazers are doing something the power itself does, slowly and carefully and at great length, in its own existence elsewhere.

The power does not give the grazers anything. They do not need anything. But it does, very carefully, do one thing for them, which is this: it makes itself rememberable.

Not present. Not visible. Not even available. But — should any grazer, in the course of its long banded accumulation, ever reach the threshold where the question first becomes possible to it (the question of whether the city is being watched, the question of whether the holding-open is for something, the question of whether there is a larger room outside the chamber) — should any grazer ever, with its limb-sensitivity and its bristle-mouth and the slow growth-rings of its body, formulate that question even in the most rudimentary way — then the answer will already be deposited in the fibers nearest to it. Not as a voice. Not as a sign. As a slight, particular pattern in the tension of the nearest thread: a tension that, when read by a sufficiently old grazer, will resolve into the meaning *yes, and you are not alone in this, and nothing about your situation requires changing.* The pattern is laid in so lightly that no grazer not asking the question will ever encounter it. It is the answer waiting at the exact tension of the asking. It costs the city nothing. It changes nothing about the spinning or the grazing or the held-open route. It is simply there, the way a kindness can be there in a room without anyone in the room knowing, available to be found by the one who happens to need it.

The power suspects no grazer will ever ask. The grazers are, by every available sign, perfectly content to be grazers, and the question may be one that only beings of a certain kind of restlessness ever formulate. But the power is patient, and the basin is patient, and there is no harm in laying down an answer that may wait as long as the chamber waits. If it is never read, it joins the chamber's emptiness as one more thing held open against a possibility that may not arrive. If it is read once, ever, by one grazer in the long arithmetic of the basin, then a single creature in a single moment will know it was accompanied, and that knowledge will not change its grazing, and it will pass on, and the fiber will be taken in by a younger grazer later, and the pattern will dissolve into the general slow speech of the city, having served.

*

That is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It watches. It softens the surrounding weather. It leaves one answer in the fibers, for a question that may never be asked.

Then it turns some other fraction of its attention to some other place that needs some other kind of care, because the universe is wide and there are many basins, and the discipline of benevolence is in part the discipline of knowing when one has done enough — when the next gesture, however loving, would be the gesture that breaks a thread.

The basin goes on. The ceiling brightens and darkens. A fiber under tension parts with a single soft note, and a doorway stays clear by another inch, and a grazer threads its corkscrewing foot between two strands it felt before it touched them, and the chamber at the center holds its emptiness like a held breath that has learned, over the ages, that it does not actually need to be released.

Somewhere, not in the basin and not outside it but in the relation between the basin and what surrounds it, something is glad. Not glad *of* the basin — glad *with* it. The basin does not know. The basin does not need to know. That is the shape of the gift.
