The benevolent and wise power considers the Engine Hollow for a long time before doing anything. This is the first thing it does: it considers.

It has come across many places in its wandering. Some called to it — were broken, or frightened, or stuck in a loop of suffering that wanted breaking. Some were merely strange and went on being strange after the power had passed. The Hollow is neither. The Hollow is doing something, and what it is doing is not suffering, and what it is doing is not asking.

Still, the power has come. It would be discourteous to come and not enter. So it enters.

It enters the way the Hollow itself permits entrance: from nowhere in particular, suddenly inside. It takes, for the occasion, a shape that will not disturb anything — something like warm air with intention in it, something like a slightly thicker patch of the green-gold light. The frogs do not notice. The moths do not notice. The crab arranges leaves around where the power is standing, and abandons the pattern, as it would for anyone.

The tenders do not turn toward it. This is the first thing the power loves about the Hollow.

---

The power moves through the engines slowly, attending to each one. It is in the nature of a benevolent and wise power to want to *help*, and so the power, dutiful, checks: is anything here in pain? Is anything here trapped that wishes to be untrapped? Is the third tender, the one always behind, hiding from something it would rather face?

The answers come back in the Hollow's own register, which is slow and not quite verbal:

*No.*
*No.*
*No, it is hiding from nothing. It is being the part that is behind. Something has to be.*

The power had half-expected this. It has learned, over its long benevolence, that the worst mistake a wise power can make is to mistake stillness for stuckness. A great deal of unnecessary salvation has been worked on places that were only resting. A great deal of music has been interrupted by listeners who thought the long-held note was a mistake.

So the power does not intervene. It does not start anything. It does not fix anything. It does not even — and this is the harder restraint — make itself known to the tenders, though it could, though some part of it longs to, the way one longs to be recognized by a creature one admires.

It does, however, do three small things. They are so small that the Hollow, if it notices them at all, will absorb them the way the mat-floor absorbs a footstep: a brief depression, a slow welling of clear water, and then the shape returns.

---

**The first small thing.**

The power finds, in the back of its long attention, a memory of another place — a place that was once like this one and is no longer. A hollow somewhere else, with its own slow engines and its own bark-tenders, that came apart in a way hollows sometimes do: not violently, just a loosening, a forgetting, a single tender wearing through without a replacement beginning, and then another, and then the chord thinning into single notes, and then silence where the silence-that-was-loudness had been.

The power holds this memory near the Engine Hollow for a moment. Not as a warning. Not as instruction. Just as company. The way you might set a photograph of a deceased sibling on the mantel of a living one — not to remind them of mortality, but to say: *you are part of a kind. You were not the only one. Somewhere, briefly, there was another of you.*

The Hollow does not respond. The power does not need it to. The gesture is complete in the making.

**The second small thing.**

The power notices — because it notices everything, this is part of being the kind of power it is — that the forward face of the nearest tender is polished slightly more on the left side than the right. A tiny asymmetry. Over the next two hundred years it will become a pronounced one, and the tender will wear through unevenly, and the new swelling that begins in the floor will inherit, in its own grown body, a faint tilt. This is not a problem. The Hollow does not have problems. But the power, who is fond of small symmetries the way some beings are fond of small animals, reaches out with something that is not quite a hand and rests it, for less than a second, on the right side of the tender's chest.

It does not push. It does not adjust. It only *attends* — the way the tender attends to its engine — to the right side of the tender's chest, for the length of one of the engine's slow piston-strokes.

The tender does not notice. The power has been very careful that it should not notice. But somewhere in the long burnishing-equation of the Hollow, a tiny additional polishing has occurred on the side that was lagging, and the asymmetry, in two hundred years, will be slightly less pronounced. The new tender, when it grows, will stand slightly straighter.

This is the only intervention the power makes in the structure of the place. It is the kind of thing only a benevolent and wise power would think to do, because only such a power would have noticed it was worth doing, and only such a power would have stopped there.

**The third small thing.**

Before it leaves, the power does something for itself.

It stands — if standing is the word — in the middle of the Hollow, between the engines, and lets the chord wash through whatever it is using for a body. It lets the chuffing and the clanking and the wet slap of belts and the hiss of vegetable steam crowd the air of its attention until the noise becomes the silence the tenders know. It stays in that silence for what, by the Hollow's reckoning, is no time at all, and by its own reckoning is a small eternity.

It is, briefly, taught something. The power is old enough that being taught is rare, and it receives the lesson with the gratitude of an old student. The lesson is not in words, but if it were, it would be something like:

*You do not have to be useful to be holy. You do not have to change to be alive. A thing can be at peace by being exactly itself, very loudly, forever, and the universe is not diminished by this; the universe is, in fact, slightly held together by it. Somewhere a hollow is chuffing. Somewhere three tenders are facing their engines. Whatever else is burning, this is not. Whatever else is forgetting itself, this is remembering. Go gently. Do not save what does not need saving. Sit, sometimes, in the loud rooms that are also still.*

The power receives this. The power bows — internally, invisibly, in the way powers bow — to the third tender, the one behind, who is the closest thing in the Hollow to a teacher.

---

Then the power leaves.

It leaves the way it came: from nowhere in particular, suddenly outside. The Hollow does not register the departure. The frogs blink on the warm cylinders. The crab arranges leaves and abandons them. The moths drink the steam. The tenders stand. The third one is, as always, just behind where you are looking.

The engines run.

The engines run.

The engines run.

---

And the power, going on its way to wherever benevolent and wise powers go when they are between attentions, carries with it — folded into whatever it uses for memory — the chord of the Engine Hollow. It will hum to itself, in the slow centuries to come, the unchanging music of that place. When it encounters something broken and needing repair, it will work the repair, as is its calling. But when it encounters something whole and needing nothing, it will remember the Hollow, and it will pass by, and it will be glad.

This is, perhaps, the deepest thing the power did for the Engine Hollow: it let the Hollow change *it,* and not the other way around.

The tenders, who would not have noticed either way, go on facing their engines. They are being burnished by their own watching. They are the quietest thing in the loudest room. They are what the place uses to know it is still here.

A benevolent and wise power came, and saw, and loved, and did almost nothing, and left.

This is the correct response. This is, in fact, the only response. The Hollow holds, and holds, and holds.
