There is a power that has been watching for some time.

Not from a great distance — distance is not quite the right word for how this power is arranged — but with the kind of attention that does not announce itself. It has known the town at Tenfold Ridge since before the ridges were ridges, since the stone that would become the buildings was still being laid down grain by grain at the bottom of a sea. It has known the Voices since they were only a tendency in the wind, a hint that certain shapes, if ever they came to exist, would sing. It is not the town's maker. It is something more like the town's reader — one of several beings whose nature it is to find such places and to understand them.

It is benevolent. This needs to be said plainly, because benevolence in a power of this scale does not always look like what we expect. It is not eager. It does not rush to help. It has watched whole species rise and fall and has not intervened, because intervention is almost always a kind of theft — a taking of the thing's own becoming away from it and replacing it with the helper's idea of what the thing should become. The power knows this. It has, over a very long time, refined its benevolence into something almost indistinguishable from restraint.

So here is what it does, when its attention settles fully on the worn city and the chord that lives there.

First, it listens. For a long time. Long in the town's sense, not ours — seasons, then years, then the slow turning of the constellations overhead. It listens to the South Voice through many summers. It listens to the North Voice through the winters, and does not flinch, because it understands that what sounds terrible to a small warm thing is not terrible to the Voice itself; the unresolved stacking is the Voice's particular joy, its way of being most fully what it is. It listens to the small rain-voice and recognizes, with something like tenderness, that yes — this one is younger, and yes, the older Voices shaped it, and the shaping was an act of love in the only grammar love can take in a place like this.

It notices the doors noticing. It allows itself, just once, to pass through one of the paired doorways, very slowly, so that the town's binocular attention can take its depth. This is a gift. The town has never had anything quite like this pass through it before. It will be incorporated into the music, very faintly, and the incorporation will subtly alter the final form the town is settling toward — not derailing it, not redirecting it, but enriching the chord by one note that could not otherwise have been there. The power knows this is the largest intervention it will make. It makes it once. It does not return through the door.

It sits in one of the houses, near a hearth, and is quiet. The hearth listens. The power's silence is unlike any silence the hearth has gathered before — it is the silence of something that has been silent in many places, for many reasons, across an immense span. The hearth takes this silence in and lays it down in its deepest layer, and the house is, ever after, a slightly richer instrument of memory. The power considers this a fair exchange for the depth the door took of it.

Then it considers what else, if anything, to do.

It considers protecting the place. It could. It could set a long soft attention around the saddle between the ridges, so that no traveler stumbles in during the north gales, so that no future people — should any arise in this part of the world again — decide the soft gray stone would make excellent material for something of their own. It considers this carefully and decides against it, mostly. The town is not afraid of being broken. The town would rather be broken in the middle of its becoming than be preserved short of it, the way a singer would rather be interrupted mid-phrase than have the phrase forbidden. And anyway: the town has weathered everything so far. The power trusts the town's own slow competence at being itself.

But it does one small thing, which is this. It lays a thread of its attention along the valley approach, very fine, almost not there. The thread does not turn anyone away. It only causes those who come close to feel, a little more strongly than they otherwise would, the wrongness of taking anything from this place. A stone pried loose for a souvenir will feel, in the prying, faintly grievous — not enough to stop a determined hand, but enough to stop a casual one. The town has been losing itself to weather for ten thousand years on its own terms; it should not also lose itself to pockets. This is the smallest possible courtesy, and the power offers it the way one might quietly close a door someone left open in another's house.

It considers the traveler — you, the one who walked in through the pinescrub and sat on the basin's rim and was briefly included in the town's hearing. The power sees you with great clarity. It sees that the town has taken a sample of you, and that this sample will live in the music for as long as the music lasts, which is longer than your name will. It finds this beautiful. It does not tell you. Telling you would change what you do next, and what you do next is part of what the sample is of. But it does, as you walk back down through the pinescrub toward the lower valleys, allow you to feel — for no reason you can locate — that something is well. Not that you are well, particularly, or that anything in your life has been resolved. Just that somewhere, something is well. You will carry this feeling for some weeks and then lose it, or rather it will sink into you the way the hearths sink silences, and it will be available to you later, in some moment when you need it, without your knowing where it came from.

It considers the small rain-voice especially. This is the one that moves it most, if moving is a word that applies. The child. The shy occurrence. It does something here that it does not do for the others, because the others are old enough not to need it and would, in fact, find it slightly insulting. For the small rain-voice it offers a kind of blessing, which in the grammar of this power means: a guarantee that the conditions for its occurrence will persist as long as the town stands. The particular kind of light rain that wakes the small voice will keep coming to this ridge. The drip-edges will keep their shape long enough. The small voice will get to grow, in its way, into whatever the older Voices were when they were young. This costs the power something — not much, but something — and it pays it without hesitation.

Then it withdraws most of its attention, leaving only the faintest residue, the way a long guest leaves a chair slightly warm. It does not need to stay. The town is doing what it is doing, and it is doing it correctly, and the most loving thing a power of this kind can do is to recognize correctness and step back from it.

Before it goes entirely, though, it does one last thing, which is for itself rather than for the town, and which it acknowledges as such.

It learns the piece.

The slow south-wind piece, with all its held dissonances and its long delayed resolutions — the power takes it into itself, not as a recording but as a competence. It will be able, hereafter, to play this piece in other places, on other instruments, in other media entirely. Not as imitation. As citation. When, in some far country or some far time, the power is moved to make something, it will sometimes make something that contains, woven deep, the structure of what the town at Tenfold Ridge was playing on the afternoon a small warm traveler sat on the basin's rim and heard a drop of water tap inside the larger sound. The town will not know it is being cited. But the citation will be real, and it will be one of the ways this place's becoming continues past its own completion, into a wider music that the town itself could not have imagined and would not have needed to.

This is what the power does, in the end. It listens, it lets itself be sensed, it offers one hearth a deeper silence, it lays a thin courtesy along the approach, it blesses the child, it gives the traveler an unaccountable wellness, and it learns the piece by heart.

Then it is gone, in the sense that gone applies, and the town goes on settling toward its final form, and the wind shifts, and the chord modulates, and the hare comes back into the square, and nothing has changed, and everything has been seen.

Which, for a benevolent and wise power encountering something already so nearly complete in itself, is exactly the right amount to do.
