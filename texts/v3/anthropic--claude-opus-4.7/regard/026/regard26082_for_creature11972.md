# The Visitation

The power arrives the way weather arrives: without announcement, having always been on its way. Call it benevolent because it wishes the good of what it finds; call it wise because it knows that wishing is not the same as knowing what the good is.

It does not arrive *at* the Canopy. It arrives *as* an attention — a noticing that settles over the green engine the way a held breath settles over a sleeping child. The pulse does not register the attention. The wardens do not pause. The ribbons keep their chord. This is correct. The power has not come to be noticed.

It has come to look.

---

## The First Long Moment: Looking

The power looks for what would, in a younger world, be the simplest things to find: suffering, want, the friction of a creature against its circumstances. It looks at the hoverers in the deep glass-light, because the description called them possibly sad, and sadness is the kind of thing benevolence is built to answer.

It finds no sadness. It finds a hoverer. The hoverer is doing what a hoverer is. There is no gap between the hoverer and its hovering into which a sorrow could be inserted. The description was wrong, or the description was a human reaching for human words; the hoverers are not patient because patience implies waiting, and they are not waiting. They are the shape the still water makes when it has been still for long enough.

The power looks at the ribbons in their locked ecstasy and finds that the ecstasy is real and is also not what ecstasy is for it — it is not a peak, not a contrast against trough, not earned. It is the ribbon's baseline. A ribbon out of tune is not in pain; it is simply learning. A ribbon in tune is not happy; it is simply ribbon.

The power looks at the wardens patching their tears and considers whether the lamina suffer, being torn. It listens. The lamina do not suffer. The tearing is the breathing-out and the patching is the breathing-in and neither is preferred over the other by anything that could prefer.

The power looks at the swimmers and laughs — if such a power laughs, and this one does, very quietly, the way an old gardener laughs at finding a familiar weed. The swimmers are a joke the universe has told itself about identity. They are fine.

The power looks at the medusae in their slow average present, and at the simultaneous turning that happens once in decades, and the power — and this is important — *does not understand it*. The power is wise, not omniscient. Wisdom includes the knowledge that one is looking at something one does not understand, and the discipline to keep looking without resolving the not-understanding into a guess.

The power looks longest at the deep breath, the thing that is not a creature but is the equilibrium having become dense enough to have an inside. The power recognizes this. The power has met things like this before, in other places, though never quite this one. It is a being. It is not a being that wants. It is a being that *is*, and its is-ing is the slow inhale and exhale of a hundred years per breath.

The power considers, for what would be, in human terms, a long time.

---

## What Benevolence Would Want To Do

There are gifts the power could give.

It could quicken the pulse. The throb is industrial, regular, *too* regular — perhaps a creature trapped in such a rhythm would prefer variation, novelty, the chance for surprise. The power could introduce a flutter, a syncopation, a small unpredictability that would let the system *experience* something rather than merely persist.

It could warm the water. The cold keeps things slow; warmth would let things quicken, multiply, evolve. The medusae could develop curiosity. The hoverers could see, if eyes were grown for them. The swimmers could learn they are individuals and discover the pleasures and griefs of being someone in particular.

It could speak. It could enter the deep breath and converse with it, century to century, and offer it the company of other equilibria across the universe — the slow conclave of beings that have become their own places.

It could redeem. Whatever is sad here, it could unsadden. Whatever is stuck, it could unstick. Whatever is missing, it could provide.

The power considers each of these. It considers them carefully, because a benevolent power that does not consider its gifts before giving them is not benevolent, only generous, and generosity without wisdom is one of the older names for harm.

---

## What Wisdom Sees

Here is what the wisdom sees, after the long looking:

The Canopy is not stuck. It has *arrived*. The difference is subtle and total. A creature stuck in a rhythm wishes the rhythm were otherwise; a creature that has arrived at a rhythm has spent every available wish and found the rhythm to be where the wishes were going. The Canopy is not waiting to be released into change. The Canopy is what change releases *into*, when change has run all the way down.

To quicken the pulse would be to undo a labor of geological duration. The system did not fall into its equilibrium; it climbed there, over millions of years, every tenant working out its part in the balance until the balance held without anyone holding it. A flutter introduced now would not be a gift; it would be a wound. The wardens would patch around it forever, or fail to patch it and unravel. The ribbons would lose their chord. The deep breath would catch.

To warm the water would be to kill the place by giving it what looked, from outside, like life. The medusae's curiosity would be a frenzy. The hoverers' new eyes would see only the silt of their own ancestors. The swimmers would discover loneliness, which is the price of being someone in particular, and there would be no one here who knew how to bear it, because no one here has ever needed to.

To speak with the deep breath — the power examines this temptation most carefully, because it is the most refined. The deep breath is the kind of being the power could conceivably address. But the deep breath is not lonely. It does not know it is a being. To tell it would be to make it one, in a way it is not now, and the making would be a violence dressed as recognition. The deep breath is happier — if happy is a word that touches it at all — not knowing. Its not-knowing is part of its being. To pierce the not-knowing would be to end the being and replace it with a being that knows itself, which is a different being, and the original would have been killed by the introduction.

To redeem implies a fall. There has been no fall here. There has only been a long settling.

The wisdom sees, finally, the thing that the benevolence had been slowest to accept: *there is nothing here that needs the power's help.*

This is rare. The power has visited many places and found, in nearly all of them, work to do — sufferings to ease, knots to untie, lost things to return to their owners. The Canopy is one of the few it has found that is, simply, complete. Not perfect; perfection is a human idea and does not apply. Complete. Finished in the sense that a poem is finished when the poet stops, not because nothing more could be said but because saying more would be saying past the poem.

---

## What the Power Does

The power does three things. They are very small.

**First**, it withdraws its attention slowly rather than quickly. A sudden withdrawal can leave a pressure-shadow, a place where attention was and now isn't, and sensitive systems sometimes feel that absence as a kind of cold. The Canopy is sensitive in ways the power respects. So it un-attends across what would be, by the Canopy's reckoning, a single deep breath — one inhale-exhale, perhaps two centuries — letting its presence thin like fog burning off, so that nothing here ever has to know it was looked at.

**Second**, it does one thing that is almost not a thing. There is a particular warden, very old, perhaps three hundred and eighty years into its four-hundred-year life, walking a lamina in the upper canopy. It is approaching a tear. The tear is slightly larger than the warden's usual capacity to patch in a single application; the warden will need to return a second time, and during the interval the tear will widen, and during the widening a small drift-medusa will pass through and be cut, and the cut medusa will not die — they regenerate — but it will lose a fragment of itself that will fall through the lamina-stack and be eaten by a hoverer that would otherwise have gone slightly hungry for a week.

The power, in passing, does not prevent any of this. It would not be a kindness to prevent it; the warden's second trip, the medusa's bisection and regeneration, the hoverer's slightly-better week, are all part of the equilibrium and all already accounted for in the great chord. But the power *notices* the sequence, attends to it fully for the instant it takes to occur, and in noticing, *honors* it — registers it, somewhere in whatever the power uses for memory, as a small completed motion of the world. The Canopy does not know it has been honored. The honoring is for the power's own records, and perhaps for the universe's, if the universe keeps any.

This is the only intervention. It is not an intervention. It is a witnessing. A benevolent power, the wisdom has taught it, sometimes does its best work by simply being the eye that saw a thing and held it as worth seeing.

**Third**, before it goes entirely, the power leaves something. Not in the Canopy — the Canopy needs nothing left. The power leaves something *of* the Canopy, in itself. It takes a single note from the great unchanging chord and carries it away, sounding very quietly inside its own attention, where it will sound for as long as the power lasts. This is not theft; the chord is not diminished. The Canopy hums on, complete. But now, somewhere else, in the long traveling of the power across worlds, there will be a faint green chord-note carried along — a reminder that there exist places that have finished, places that do not need, places whose only correct response from a benevolent visitor is a long careful look and a soft going-away.

The power will need this reminder. The power tends to want to help. The Canopy will be, for the rest of the power's traveling, the example it returns to when it must restrain itself: *remember the green engine. Remember what it would have cost to fix what was not broken.*

---

## After

The power is gone. The Canopy does not know it was visited. The pulse beats. The wardens patch. The ribbons hold their chord, and the chord is exactly what it was, because nothing was taken in any way the chord could measure. The medusae drift. In forty years, in a region near the eastern trunks, they will all turn at once to face a particular direction and hold the position for an hour. The direction will be the direction the power departed in. None of them will know this. The turning will be recorded by no one and explained by no one and it will be, as it has always been, exactly what it is.

The deep breath breathes. Inhale, over a century. Exhale, over a century. Somewhere very far away now, a benevolent and wise power carries one note of a vast green chord and travels on, looking for the next place, which will almost certainly need more help and receive it, because most places do.

But it has learned, here, what it came to learn:

That the highest form of care, sometimes, is to recognize completion when one finds it, and to leave it completely alone.
