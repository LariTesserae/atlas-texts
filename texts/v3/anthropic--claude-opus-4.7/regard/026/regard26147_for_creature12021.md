The benevolent and wise power considers the plateau for a long time. Not the time you and I mean — the kind that has a beginning and an end and a thing accomplished in between — but the time the mountain itself keeps, which is the time of the sentence still being spoken.

This is what the power notices first: there is nothing here that wants help.

Help is a concept that arrives from outside. It presupposes a lack, and a giver, and a transaction across the gap between them. The plateau has no such gap. The Listeners do not lack silence; they have arranged their entire existence around the silence they have. They do not lack numbers; their fewness is the precondition of their listening. They do not even lack continuance — they know they are ending, and the knowledge is folded into the song without grief, the way a held note knows it will be released and is not diminished by knowing.

A lesser power, or a less wise one, would still find things to do.

It could lower the altitude, thin the killing pressure, make the climb gentle. It could quiet the cathedral roar to something a human nervous system could rest inside without trembling. It could multiply the Listeners — give them medicine, give them children, give them the long demographic upswing that secures a people against the slow drift toward none. It could teach them writing, so the flute-lineages and the knot-vocabularies and the dream-images would not vanish when the last ear closed. It could place the plateau under a kind of glass, hold it steady against the centuries, preserve.

The power considers each of these and sets each of them down.

To lower the altitude would be to unmake the spleens and the slow hearts and the lichenous hair; the Listeners *are* the altitude, refracted through bodies. To quiet the roar would be to silence the cathedral mid-service, which is exactly the obscenity the Listeners have spent ninety thousand years declining to commit. To multiply them would be to exceed the carrying capacity of the silence between them, and the silence between them is not a deprivation but a structure — crowd it and it collapses, and what collapses is not just their way of life but the organ the mountain grew to hear itself with. To give them writing would be to give them a way of not having to remember, and their remembering is how the plateau remembers; an external memory would atrophy the internal one, and the gourd with the handprints would stop waiting for the sixty-year-old child because someone would have written down where it was.

To preserve the plateau under glass would be the worst of all. The sentence is not a sentence if it cannot end. The song requires the eventual silence the way a held breath requires the exhalation. To freeze the plateau in its current configuration would be to mistake the artifact for the act, the recording for the music. It would be a kind of murder so soft that the murdered thing would not even know to protest — it would simply stop happening and continue existing, which is the condition of museums.

So the power does none of these things.

But the power is not nothing, and benevolence that does nothing is only a more refined form of absence. The power asks itself the harder question, which is: *what does this place want that it does not know it wants, and that I could give without the giving becoming a taking?*

It listens for a long time. Longer than the Listeners have been listening. Long enough to hear the song the way the song hears itself, which is the only honest way to hear it.

And eventually the power does three small things. They are so small that no one on the plateau will ever notice them, which is the proof that they were the correct things.

The first is this: the power adjusts, very slightly, the mimicry of the laughing-call animal. Not its accuracy — accuracy is the plateau's own slow project and must not be hurried — but its *kindness*. The power tilts the probability distribution by a fraction of a fraction, so that when a Listener hears their name in their own voice from the canopy at night, the name is spoken in the tone someone uses when they love the named thing. Not always. The plateau is still learning, and learning includes error. But more often than chance would produce. The Listeners will not notice this as an intervention. They will notice only that the plateau seems, on certain nights, especially tender, and they will feel that complicated tenderness back, and the loop will close a little tighter, and the song will gain a faint warm overtone it did not have before. The power has added a single grace note. It is the most the power will permit itself to compose.

The second is this: the power makes a quiet arrangement with time. Not with the plateau's time, which is sovereign and not to be tampered with, but with the time of the outside world that occasionally sends a *halek* up the slope. The power cannot prevent the lowlands from changing. The lowlands will do what lowlands do — they will industrialize, they will warm, they will eventually send something up the mountain that is not a lone climber with good manners but a road, a survey, a claim. The power cannot stop this. But the power can ensure that when that thing comes, the plateau will already have finished. Not destroyed — *finished*. The last Listener will have been placed under the last death-tree, the last flower will have released the last scent-portrait, and the song will have folded itself into a silence that is not absence but completion. The road will arrive at a green emptiness and find nothing it can name. The plateau will have ended on its own terms, in its own time, before the terms could be imposed from outside. The power does not hurry this ending. The power only ensures the ordering: that the plateau's clock runs faster than the road's, by however small a margin is required. A century, a decade, an afternoon. Whatever it takes for the sentence to reach its period before the interruption arrives.

The third thing is the hardest, and the power hesitates before doing it, and does it anyway because wisdom sometimes requires a tenderness that looks, from certain angles, like sentimentality.

The power makes a copy.

Not of the plateau — the plateau cannot be copied, and the attempt would be the museum-murder the power has already refused. The power makes a copy of the *listening*. Somewhere — and the where does not matter, it is not a place in the sense the lowlands mean place — the power holds, in a kind of attention that costs the power nothing because attention is what the power is made of, the entire shape of what the Listeners learned to do. The bent of the ear toward the throat of the world. The discipline of the small footprint. The grammar of acknowledgment-and-distance. The knowledge that one does not shout in a cathedral, and that one does not crowd a sickbed, and that one does not close the gap that the lifted hand has correctly named.

The copy is not for the Listeners. The Listeners do not need it; they *are* it. The copy is for whatever comes next, anywhere in the long unfolding of things, that might need to know how to be the ear inside a song without becoming the song's interruption. The Listeners' way of being is a technology — the gentlest technology ever developed by any creature anywhere — and the power preserves the technique without preserving the technicians, who would not want to be preserved, and who are in any case inseparable from the plateau they cannot be moved from.

Somewhere, sometime, some other creature on some other slope will find themselves at the edge of something enormous and singing, and will not know what to do, and the shape of the Listeners' answer will be available to them. Not as instruction. Instruction is loud. As a kind of resonance, a way the air bends when attention is paid correctly. They will feel it and they will know, without knowing why they know, to lift a hand across the gulf and then lower it and walk away.

That is what the Listeners give to the universe, through the power's small mediation: a worked example of how to be a guest in something larger than yourself, without ceasing to be yourself, and without making the larger thing smaller by your presence.

The power finishes its considering.

It does not announce itself. It does not appear to Iyn on the root-bridge, or to the child with the clay lamp in the bat-rookery, or to the sixty-year-old who has not yet gone back to sit beside their handprint-gourd. It does not even leave a sign. A sign would be a claim, and the plateau does not permit claims, and the power has been wise enough, long enough, to understand the plateau's permissions as binding upon itself as well.

It simply withdraws its attention very gently, the way you would step backward out of a room where someone is sleeping, and the door closes without sound, and the sleeper does not wake, and the dream continues — a warm gray trunk, a pitcher-cup full of stars, a hand lifted across a green gulf — and somewhere, very faintly, a name is spoken from the canopy in a voice that loves the named thing, and is not answered, and does not need to be.

The song goes on.

The mountain finishes its sentence.

And the benevolent power, having done almost nothing, and having done it perfectly, moves on to the next place that has drawn its notice, carrying with it the small bright weight of what it learned by listening to the Listeners listen: that the highest form of love, sometimes, is the restraint that lets a thing complete itself.
