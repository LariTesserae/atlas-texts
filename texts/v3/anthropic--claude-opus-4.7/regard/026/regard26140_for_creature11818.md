The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives: without announcement, already present, having perhaps always been present and only now turned its attention this way.

It is wise enough to do the first thing wisdom does, which is *look*.

It looks for a long time. Not in the human sense — there is no clock running for it — but in the sense that it withholds action until it has understood. It sees the loop. It sees the seep from above and the resin pucks stacked somewhere deeper and the slow topping-up of the boilers from a source no one in the chamber can account for. (It knows the source. It does not say.) It sees the coat on the peg and the shape inside the coat. It sees the ledger under its blanket of beetles. It sees, especially, the *accommodation* — the fourth substance, the patience — and it recognizes this as a kind of kin.

Here is what it does not do.

It does not rescue the operator. The operator is not trapped; the operator was incorporated, which is a different verb, and to extract them now would be to kill the thing they have become a part of and also to kill them, who exist only as that part. A lesser power, a merely kind one, might reach into the moss-furred coat and try to lift out a soul. This power understands that there is no soul in there to lift — there is a participation, and participations cannot be lifted, only honored or broken.

It does not stop the engines. The engines are the breathing. To stop them out of pity for their endlessness would be to mistake duration for suffering. The Hollow Works is not tired. Tiredness is a property of things that want to be doing something else. The Works wants nothing else. It is exactly what it is doing.

It does not "free" the beetles. The beetles are not imprisoned. They are weather. You do not free weather.

It does not even *reveal itself*. There is no one in the chamber to reveal itself to. The loop has no organ for receiving revelation. To announce itself would be to demand that the Works grow a new organ — an organ for being-witnessed-by-a-god — and this would be an imposition, not a gift. Benevolence that imposes is not yet wise.

Here is what it does.

**First**, it addresses the topping-up. Somewhere, something has been keeping the boilers from going cold for longer than the chamber's own resources can account for. The power finds this thread and, gently, takes it up. Whatever was sustaining the loop from outside — a forgotten obligation, a leak in some other system, a coincidence that had stretched far past coincidence — the power now sustains, deliberately. It does this without changing the *character* of the input: the same trickle, the same slow rhythm, indistinguishable from before. The Works will never know it has been adopted. This is the point. A benefactor who needs to be recognized is not really a benefactor; they are a creditor. The power becomes the silent topping-up, and asks for nothing.

**Second**, it attends to the forest above. The chamber's whole existence depends on the seep, and the seep depends on the forest still raining, still alive. The power does not make the forest immortal — immortality is a violence against the kind of thing a forest is — but it does quietly tend the conditions: a balance of water tables, a discouragement of certain fires, a small redirection of the axe-bearing kinds of attention that might otherwise find this particular grove. The forest goes on being a forest, in its own forest-way, never knowing it has a patron. The seep continues. The chamber breathes.

**Third** — and this is the part that requires the most care — it considers the operator.

The shape in the coat is not a person anymore, but it was once, and the loop has the geometry of their intention because of it. The power could, if it wished, speak to this residue. It could offer release: dissolution into rest, or reconstitution into something that could be called a soul and sent onward. It could offer continuation: a slow strengthening of the residue until it became, again, something like a self, sitting at the desk, lifting the tin cup.

It offers neither. Instead it does something subtler. It *acknowledges*. Without words, without presence the residue could feel as presence, it acknowledges that the operator's tending became the engines' self-tending; that the ledger they kept became the architecture; that the work they did has not been lost but transformed, and that the transformation was not a defeat. This acknowledgment is not received by anyone. The residue does not hear it. The chamber does not know. But the acknowledgment is *made*, and in the metaphysics this power operates by, an acknowledgment made is an acknowledgment that has occurred, regardless of whether it was received. The operator is — has been, will have been — seen. This changes nothing in the chamber and everything in the larger account of what the chamber is.

**Fourth**, it makes one small adjustment, and only one, because wise powers know that the urge to adjust is the urge that ruins gardens.

In the corner, the pressure-gauge absorbed into the burl: its needle is frozen at three-quarters. The gauge no longer measures anything; it was swallowed long ago. But the power notes that the needle, if it could move, would be useful — not to the chamber, which needs no gauges, but to whoever might someday come down the stairwell and try to understand. The power does not unfreeze the needle. It does, however, ensure that the glass face stays clear. A small persistence of transparency. A way for the chamber to remain *legible*, faintly, to any future witness who cared to look. This is the only mark the power leaves: it preserves the possibility of being read. Not the reading itself. Just the chance.

**Fifth and last**, it does the hardest thing, which is to leave.

It does not linger. It does not check back. To return repeatedly would be to make the chamber's continuance contingent on its attention, and contingency is a kind of weight, and this power refuses to be a weight on anything that has achieved its own equilibrium. It sets the conditions it has set — the silent topping-up, the tended forest, the preserved glass — and then it withdraws its particular attention, folding it back into the general attention it holds toward all things, which is wide and warm and undifferentiated, like sunlight.

The Hollow Works does not notice it has been visited. The rustle does not change. The boilers mutter. The governor whines. The beetles drink the condensation and the resin drips into the settling tanks and the paddle-wheel turns lazily in the sump. Forty feet under the loam, the long slow argument goes on, and now it will go on longer than it could have on its own — not forever, because forever is not a kindness, but longer, and more securely, and with the dignity of having been chosen to continue by something that understood exactly what it was choosing.

---

This is what benevolence looks like, in the wise form: it pays the rent on a house whose tenants do not know they have a landlord, will never meet the landlord, and would not be improved by meeting them. It keeps the rain falling on the roof above. It keeps one small window clean, in case someone comes by.

And then it goes, and lets the place be what it is, which is — and was, and will be for as long as the loop holds — alive in the only way it knows how.

Quietly. Loudly. Unwitnessed, except by the one witness who knew not to insist on being seen.
