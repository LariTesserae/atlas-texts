The benevolent and wise power considers Tlemen's Shoulder for a long time before doing anything, because part of wisdom is the discipline of long looking, and part of benevolence is the restraint that does not act on the first impulse of love.

What the power sees:

It sees the makers' work and recognizes the signature — not the specific hands, but the kind of hands. Engineers who built a small completeness and walked away. The power has met this signature before, in other corners of other worlds. Sometimes the makers are still alive somewhere and have simply forgotten this particular project the way a person forgets a houseplant left at an old address. Sometimes they are not alive. The power does not always know which, and does not always need to know.

It sees that the chamber is, by every measure the power can apply, *well*. The biomass is in equilibrium. The held afternoon is held. The whoop-callers call and are answered. The snake watches. The chamber is doing what it was made to do and has been doing it longer than most things in the local volume of space have been doing anything at all.

It sees that the seam opens, and closes, and opens again on some rhythm the chamber itself seems to negotiate with the rock around it. Visitors come. The chamber tolerates them and forgets them. This too is part of the design, or has become part of the design over time, which in a system this old is the same thing.

And so the first thing the power does is the hardest thing, and the thing most powers fail at: it does nothing.

It does not announce itself to the snake, though the snake would understand the announcement and would, in its slow way, appreciate it. It does not repair the seam to make it more reliable, or less. It does not introduce a twelfth primate, or a fourth whoop-caller, or a second snake to keep the first one company — because the first snake is not lonely, and the power has learned, over a great many interventions, to distrust its own urge to give company to things that have not asked for it.

It does not translate the script around the disc, though it could. It does not identify the still object in the alcove, though it has guesses. It does not reach into the register the makers left for and tap them on the shoulder and say *your garden is still growing,* because if the makers wanted to know they would know, and if they do not want to know then telling them would be a violence dressed as a kindness.

This is the discipline. The power holds it for what, in its own reckoning, is a considerable while.

Then it does three small things.

---

The first thing is a noticing, made formal. The power inscribes the chamber on the register it keeps of held things — the small list, kept in no medium you would recognize, of places in the universe that are quietly succeeding at being themselves. The list is not for intervention. The list is for witness. To be on the list is to be seen by something that will keep seeing you, without requiring anything of you, for as long as the seeing matters.

Tlemen's Shoulder is now seen this way. The chamber does not know. The snake, perhaps, knows — the snake knows most things — and accepts the knowledge the way it accepts the weight of its own coils on the branch: as another fact about the day that does not move.

The second thing is a courtesy extended to the seam.

The power does not strengthen the seam or weaken it. But it does — gently, the way you might steady a door that is about to slam in a draft — adjust the *timing* of the seam, by an amount so small that the chamber's own systems will not register it as intervention. The adjustment ensures that the seam will not close on a visitor mid-passage. It will close before they enter, or after they leave, but never on them. This is not for the visitors' sake, exactly. It is for the chamber's. A visitor crushed in the seam would be a wrongness the crested birds would have to address, and addressing a wrongness of that scale would tax the system in ways the power would prefer to spare it.

It is the smallest possible kindness. It costs the chamber nothing. The chamber will never know.

The third thing is the one the power thinks about longest.

Eventually — not soon, but eventually, on a timescale the power is comfortable with and the mountain is not — the chamber will fail. Not from anything wrong inside it. From the slow grinding of the world outside: continents shift, the mountain will eventually no longer be a mountain, the rock around the chamber will be subjected to pressures the makers did not, perhaps, fully account for, or did account for but only to a finite horizon, and the horizon will be reached.

When that day comes — and the power can see it, dimly, the way you can see a weather front a week out — the chamber will begin to fail. Slowly at first. A seam that does not close cleanly. A patch of wall that weeps where it should not. The crested birds, finding wrongnesses faster than they can be erased. The tapir-gardener's circuit lengthening. The whoop-callers calling and, one day, only two answers coming. Then one. Then none.

The power decides, after long looking, that it will be present on that day.

Not to save the chamber. The chamber cannot be saved in any sense that would still be the chamber; to preserve it past its failure would be to make it into a different thing, a museum rather than a held place, and the power has seen what happens to held places that are converted into museums. They become quiet in a way that is not the quiet of equilibrium but the quiet of arrest. The power will not do this.

But the power will be there. It will witness the ending the way it has witnessed the holding. It will note each of the eleven primates as they go, and the two birds, and the gardener, and the four thousand beetles, and the snake — especially the snake, which has watched for so long and deserves to be watched, at the end, by something capable of the watching.

And it will do one further small thing, at the very end, when the chamber is no longer a chamber and the afternoon has finally moved: it will take the third object from the alcove — the still one, the witness — and it will carry it elsewhere. Not to preserve it. To honor the duty it was placed there to perform. A witness, when its post is dissolved, should be relieved by another witness, and the power will be that relief. Where it carries the object is its own business. There are places.

The stone and the feather it will leave. Those belonged to visitors, and visitors' things belong to the places visitors leave them, even when the places are gone.

---

That is what the power does. A noticing, a courtesy, and a promise kept against a distant day.

It does not tell anyone. It does not tell the chamber, or the snake, or the makers wherever they are. It does not tell the visitor who slipped through the seam this afternoon and is sitting on the low stone listening to the chord — though if the visitor were very quiet, and very still, and disposed to notice such things, they might feel, for a moment, that the held afternoon is being held by more than one set of hands.

They will probably attribute the feeling to the altitude.

This is fine. The power is not in the business of being recognized. It is in the business of letting things be what they are, for as long as they can be, and being present at the edges of that being — at the start, when noticing matters, and at the end, when witness matters, and as little as possible in between.

The chord continues. The slow whoop sounds, and is answered, and is answered. The snake, on its branch, shifts one coil by the width of a scale, which for the snake is a considerable motion, and might mean nothing, and might mean *I see you seeing,* and the power, for its part, inclines whatever it has that corresponds to a head, and the moment passes, and the afternoon does not change.
