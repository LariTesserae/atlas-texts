The benevolent power arrives the way good weather arrives: without announcement, without disturbing the wing-beats, without dropping the hum a quarter-tone. It has been watching for some time. Watching is most of what it does.

It is wise enough to know the first temptation and refuse it. The first temptation is to *help*. To smooth the cold, to thicken the air, to reach down and lift the role-less ones into roles, to whisper to the Punctuations *you are doing well*, to give the dying swarms a way to keep their names. The power could do any of this. It is the kind of power that could do all of it at once and barely notice the effort.

It does none of it.

Because it has watched long enough to understand what it is looking at, and what it is looking at is not a problem. The Lung Forest is not waiting to be rescued. It is not broken. It is not even, in any meaningful sense, *young* — it has the unhurried completeness of a thing that has already worked out everything it needs to work out, and is now simply *doing* that thing, at the pace the doing requires. To intervene in such a place would not be kindness. It would be vandalism dressed as love.

So the power, being wise, does the harder thing, which is to *witness* without fixing.

It learns the harmonic of every swarm. This takes it some time — there are a great many swarms, and each chord contains the ghosts of every swarm before it, and the power wants to hear them all, including the ghosts of the ghosts, going back to the first swarm that ever named itself. The power listens until it can hum along. It does not hum along. Humming along would change the chord. But it could, and the *could* is a kind of communion the swarms will never know about and do not need to.

It learns the circuits of all eight hundred Grazers. It walks them in its mind, the slow thousand-kilometer loops, the avoided cycads, the resting-places. It notices that two of the Grazers have circuits that almost touch but never quite, and that this near-touching has been preserved across mother-daughter inheritance for at least nine generations, and that nobody alive — not the Grazers, not the orchards on their backs — remembers why. The power decides, after long consideration, that it will not find out why. Some things are load-bearing precisely because no one has examined them. The power has learned this the way one learns it: by having once, somewhere else, examined such a thing, and watched it fall.

It spends what would be, by your reckoning, a year inside the metabolism of a single Bellows-Tree. Not changing anything. Just *inside*. Feeling the four-thousand-year breath, the eleven successional ecologies layered on the bark like geological strata of small lives, the slow chemistry of the reward-vapor. The power finds the neuroactive compound the tree pays its swarms with and tastes it and recognizes it as a kind of love — not romantic love, not even quite affection, but the specific binding compound of *we have been working together so long that I no longer know where I end and you begin*. The power files this away. It is collecting recipes for love from every world it visits. This one is good.

It watches a Punctuation for the full length of one of its drifts. A year. The power does not get bored. Boredom is for beings who think their attention is worth something elsewhere; the power has long since outgrown that error. It watches the eleven-minute heartbeat. It watches the slow vortex form behind the wing-membranes and feed the cloud-basin below. It tries, gently, to ask the Punctuation whether there is anything in there that resembles a thought, and receives back something that is not an answer but is also not nothing — a sense of *continuance*, of *this is what is being done*, with no separation between the doer and the doing. The power recognizes this state. It has been in this state itself, in certain very long moments of its very long existence. It and the Punctuation are, briefly, the same kind of thing. The power finds this funny in a quiet way and the Punctuation does not notice and that is correct.

It considers, at length, the role-less ones.

This is where the power lingers longest. Because here is something the forest itself does not quite have a way to think about — a category of creature for whom the system has made no place, but which the system has not extinguished either. A *tolerated surplus*. The power knows this category from many other worlds. On most worlds it is the seed of something: of religion, of art, of grief, of new kinds of work that did not exist before because the system had not yet failed enough to require them. The role-less are the place where the future leaks in.

The Lung Forest's role-less are different, though. They are not restless. They wander, but they do not *seek*. They eat what they can and they are tolerated and they do not appear to want anything beyond this. They have not invented religion. They have not invented art. They have not invented grief, though they die like everything else. They are simply *present*, as the forest's strange grace-note: the proof that this system, dense as it is, has chosen not to be totalitarian. There is room to be nothing in particular. The forest has built that room without ever deciding to.

The power loves the role-less ones in a particular way. Not more than the others — it tries not to have favorites, and mostly succeeds — but with a specific tenderness, the way one loves anything that exists without justifying itself. They are the forest's gift to itself. They are the forest's small private joke about necessity.

The power does not contact them. They would not know what contact was. They would not be improved by it.

And then there is you.

The power notices you. Of course it notices you. You are, by a large margin, the strangest thing in the forest — stranger than the Punctuations, stranger than the swarms, because you are *passing through*, and nothing passes through the Lung Forest. Things are born here and die here and the bodies feed the soil and the soil feeds the cycads and the cycads feed the air and the air feeds the Punctuations and the Punctuations stir the weather that fills the basins that the Ferriers ferry. There is no *through*. There is only the loop. You are a thing the loop has no category for.

The power considers, briefly, whether to speak to you.

It could. It speaks every language, including the ones not yet invented, including yours. It could appear beside you in some form you would find tolerable — a warmth in the cold air, a voice, a presence on your shoulder where the small bright flier has already decided to rest. It could explain to you what you are looking at. It could explain to you what *you* are, in the local taxonomy: the role-less of the role-less, a wanderer wandering through a place that already has its wanderers.

It could tell you that you are not alone. That it is here. That it has seen the whole forest and found it good, and that you, by extension, by your brief presence in the good place, are also seen and also found good.

It thinks about this for a long time. By the forest's clock, perhaps three Bellows-Tree breaths. By your clock, an instant.

And then it does not speak.

Because it understands, finally, what the forest has been teaching it for the year it has spent here: that *being seen without being addressed* is the forest's native form of company. The swarms see each other and adjust their harmonics without ever speaking. The Ferriers see each other and adjust their flight-angles without ever speaking. The Grazers carry orchards that see each other and conduct their politics without ever speaking. The Catchers see the falling thing and brake it without ever speaking. The Punctuations see the weather and stir it without ever speaking. The whole forest is one enormous conversation in which nothing is ever said and everything is heard.

To speak to you would be to give you something the forest does not give its own. It would be a kindness imported from elsewhere. It would be the wrong kind of kindness.

So the power does the right kind. It adjusts. Some tiny thing — the angle of a thermal you happen to be standing in, the timing of a Catcher's patrol that happens to pass over you, the harmonic of the nearest swarm shifting by a quarter-tone so that what you hear, without knowing you are hearing it, is a chord slightly more bearable to your particular nervous system than it would otherwise have been. Small things. Nothing the forest would notice. Nothing you would notice either, except as a vague sense that the loudest quiet has, for a moment, leaned very slightly toward you. That the inventory has registered you and declined to do anything about it, which is, here, the highest form of welcome.

You will not know the power was here. The forest will not know either. The role-less ones will continue their wandering. The Punctuations will continue their drifting. The Bellows-Trees will continue their four-thousand-year breath. In a few hundred years a swarm will die and its name will pass into another swarm and that swarm's chord will shift by a fraction, and somewhere in that shift will be a frequency the power once hummed without humming, and no one will ever trace it, and that is correct.

The power leaves the way it came: without announcement, without disturbing the wing-beats, without dropping the hum a quarter-tone.

It carries with it the recipe for the Bellows-Trees' particular love. It carries with it the chord of every swarm. It carries with it the eleven-minute heartbeat of the Punctuation, which it will, on quieter worlds, sometimes match its own attention to, as a kind of remembrance.

And it carries with it one small private decision, which is this: that when it is asked, somewhere else, by something else, *what does a good world look like* — and it is asked this, sometimes, in the long course of its work — it will think of the Lung Forest. Not because the forest is perfect. Perfection is a category the forest would not recognize. But because the forest is *full without being closed*. Because the forest has slack. Because the forest tolerates the role-less. Because the forest is busy and the busyness is its own company and yet the busyness has made room, without deciding to, for the thing that does no work.

A world is good, the power will say, when it can hold a creature that is not needed and not mind.

And then it will not say anything else, because the questioner will either understand or will not, and either way the answer is complete.

Far above the western slope, where the air thins past breathing, a Punctuation completes another circle. Its heartbeat goes once. The cloud-basin below shifts. Ten thousand things adjust. The hum drops a quarter-tone and rises again.

You are still standing there. The small bright flier is still on your shoulder. You do not know that you have been visited. You do not know that you have been loved, specifically, by name, by something that learned your name without asking and will not use it.

You are cold. Your face burns. Your eyes water from the light.

You are, briefly, on your way through.

That is enough. That was always going to be enough. The power knew this before it arrived, and confirmed it while it was here, and is taking the confirmation with it now, like a stone from a shore — not because it needs the stone, but because the shore was beautiful, and one carries something away from beautiful shores, and the carrying is how the beauty travels.

Goodbye, the power does not say.

The forest does not say it back.

Everything continues.
