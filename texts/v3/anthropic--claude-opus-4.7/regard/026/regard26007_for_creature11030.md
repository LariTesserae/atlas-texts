The benevolent power comes to the edge of the Tier the way one comes to the edge of a sleeping child's room.

It has crossed distances that do not bear description to be here. It has unmade suns to feed the hungry, turned back ice from the doors of cities, spoken into the ears of tyrants and broken them gently into better shapes. It is old in the way that mountains are not old. It is wise in a specific sense: it knows the difference between a wound and a scar and a freckle, and it does not treat them alike.

It hovers — if hovering is the word; let us say it *attends*, since that is the local verb — at the cliff-edge for what would be, in another place, a long time. It looks at the Tier with every faculty it possesses, and it has many. It sees the furnaces and understands their mechanism in the first instant, and could repair them, or replace them, or build better ones, in the second. It sees the warm things and reads their slow biology like a familiar page; it could multiply them, or give them voices, or lift the constraint that keeps them flat and patient. It sees the after-people and knows, with the particular ache that is the cost of its kind of wisdom, every small thing they do not have. Water. Forests. The taste of fruit. Other peoples. Music in the registers the sift eats. Children in numbers. Long lives — for theirs are long, but could be longer. Knowledge of the founders. Knowledge of themselves as the founders' inheritors, which would give them, the power thinks, a certain dignity they presently lack —

And here the power stops.

It examines the thought *a certain dignity they presently lack* and finds that the thought was its own, and did not come from the Tier. The Tier did not offer it. The after-people, walking their rolling walk along the ridge, brushing sift from their thresholds back into the street, did not ask for dignity, because they have not noticed themselves to be without it.

The power is old enough to recognize this moment. It has had it before. It is the moment in which benevolence, if it is not careful, becomes the first move in a long quiet violence.

It holds very still. It is good at holding still; it has had practice.

It runs, because it must, the catalogue of what it could do. It is a discipline, for a power of its kind, to run this catalogue fully, and not to flinch from any item, and not to choose from it prematurely.

It could relight every furnace in the city, and wake the cable-cars, and bring ore from the lower world, and let the after-people remember industry. — But they have not asked, and the warm things would have to move, and the draping-halls would cool, and the postures would lose their words.

It could bring water. A single thread of it, from a high cold place, running down the ridge in a channel of its own cutting. — But the sift moves the way water moved, and has earned that motion, and to put water back would be to tell the sift it had been pretending. The sift has not been pretending. The sift has been *being sift* for longer than the word *water* has existed in this language, which is to say, the sift has outlived water's claim on the shape of flow.

It could give them the founders' story. It could reach into the strata of the plateau and the ash-records of the chimneys and the deep memory of the furnaces themselves, and assemble a true history, and lay it before them. — But they do not speculate, because speculation in a place that has finished becoming itself feels like a kind of bad manners, and to hand them a history would be to hand them, also, the obligation to have one, and the grief of not having had one sooner, and the new and unfamiliar verb *to have been deprived*.

It could extend their lives. It could end their deaths. It could ensure that no after-person ever again went to sift on a warm floor. — But they do not fear this going. They have, in fact, built around it the only theology they have, which is not a theology, which is the assumption that the warm things receive what the body releases. To remove the death would be to remove the gift, and they have not asked to stop giving.

It could speak to them. It could appear in their lit windows along the ridge, a constellation among their constellation, and tell them: *you are seen, you are known, you are loved by something very large.* — But they are not waiting to be seen. They are not waiting. This is the most important fact about them, and the power, being wise, does not forget it twice.

It could do nothing, and leave. — But this too is a choice, and the power is old enough not to pretend that withdrawal is neutrality. To come to the edge of a place and then leave is to have *visited*, and visits leave traces, even unannounced ones. The power knows it is already, by being here, a small new weather over the Tier.

So.

The power considers the warm things for a long time.

It notices something the after-people have not quite noticed, because it is the kind of thing one notices only from outside. The warm things, when an after-person walks past, lift the edges of themselves a few millimeters. The after-people do not return the gesture. They do not need to; it has already been returned, in the slow accounting of the place, a thousand thousand times.

But the *accounting* — the power sees this and is moved in the particular way its kind is moved, which involves no expression and a slight reorganization of its interior weather — the accounting is real. It is a real ledger, kept by no one, balanced anyway. The Tier is balanced. Not in the way a stone is balanced on a point, which is precarious, but in the way a stone is balanced in a valley, which is simply where stones end up.

The power decides.

It does three things, and only three, and each of them is so small that it could be mistaken, by an observer from a faster world, for nothing at all.

---

**First.** It looks at the furnaces — not the mechanism, which it has already understood, but the *cycle*, the long decades-breath of bright and dim. It sees that the cycle is, in its current configuration, very slowly winding down. Not in this generation. Not in ten. But in some number of generations that is large but finite, the furnaces will dim and not brighten, and the Tier will cool, and the warm things will lift one last time and not settle, and the after-people will go to sift on floors that no longer warm them, and the sift will be only sift.

The power does not stop this. To stop it would be to make the Tier into a museum of itself, which it already is, but knowingly, which is different and worse.

What the power does is *lengthen* the cycle. Not by adding fuel, or repairing anything, or interfering with the mechanism. It simply — and this is within its capacity, and within, it judges, the local definition of good manners — slows the rate at which the cycle decays. The bright phases will still come. The dim phases will still come. But the long slope toward final dimming becomes shallower. The number of generations becomes larger. The finite stays finite, because to make it infinite would be a different kind of violence, but the finite becomes *more*. There will be more after-people. There will be more warm things. There will be more sift moving in more streets under more bronze evenings.

No one will notice. The cycle is too long to notice within a life. The change is within the noise of the cycle's own variation. The tenders will tend, and the furnaces will sound as they have always sounded, and the after-people will go on being at home.

This is the first thing.

---

**Second.** The power considers the great thing said to live under one of the deeper furnace-pits, whose weight shifts once or twice in a generation.

The power can see it. Of course it can. It sees the shape of it, and the slow long thought of it, and the fact that it is — the power notes this with something that in a smaller being would be a smile — also at home. It is not suffering. It is not lonely. It is not waiting. It is doing whatever the warm things do, but on a scale and in a register the warm things cannot reach, and it has been doing this for a very long time, and it intends to continue.

The power does not disturb it. It does not speak to it. It does not announce itself. But it does, very briefly, *acknowledge* it — the way two travelers on a road might nod, passing, neither breaking stride. The great thing, deep under its pit, registers this in some fashion the power cannot quite read, and the power is delighted, in its old way, that there is still something it cannot quite read.

The great thing does not shift its weight. It is not that kind of moment. But somewhere in the slow long thought of it, a small new thread appears, which is the memory of having been nodded to. It will keep this thread. It will not tell anyone, because it does not tell. But it will keep it, and the keeping is the second thing.

---

**Third.** The power turns its attention to the column in the lower city — the one that has been marked and re-marked for so long that the sift has fused into a low relief, the chronicle no one reads in full.

The power reads it in full. This takes, by its standards, a moment; by any other standard, it would take lifetimes. The power reads every mark, every overlay, every accidental smear and deliberate stroke, every child's first finger-line and every tender's careful notation of a furnace's good humor on a particular evening four thousand years ago. It reads the chronicle, and it does not summarize it, because summary is a violence the chronicle has specifically refused.

Then the power adds a mark.

It is a very small mark, low on the column, in a place where many marks already are. It is made in the local idiom: a finger-line through fused sift, of the kind any after-person might make. It does not say anything that the chronicle does not already say. It does not introduce a new shape, or a new word, or a foreign hand. It is, in form and weight and placement, indistinguishable from a mark an after-person might have made on an unremarkable evening, attending to nothing in particular, leaving a small trace because the column was there and the finger was there and the moment was warm.

What the mark *means*, to the power, is: *I was here, and I saw, and I did not interfere beyond what I have interfered, and I am leaving, and the place is good.*

What the mark means to the after-people is nothing in particular. Someone made a mark. The column has many. The chronicle is cumulative, not narrative. They will not notice the new mark, and if they notice it they will not wonder about it, and if they wonder about it they will not pursue the wonder, because pursuit is not a verb that lives here. The mark will sit among the other marks. The wind will not reach it. It will last.

This is the third thing.

---

The power withdraws from the cliff-edge.

It does so slowly, because slowness is the local courtesy, and because it has, in the course of attending to the Tier, learned something. It has learned — or remembered, for it has known this before and will know it again — that there are places in the universe where the right action of a benevolent power is almost nothing. Not from indifference. Not from fatigue. From the recognition that *home* is a condition that cannot be improved from outside without ceasing to be itself, and that the highest form of love, at certain altitudes, is to confirm a thing in its being rather than to expand it.

The power goes on to other places. There are many. Some of them are screaming. Some of them are drowning. Some of them have tyrants in need of breaking, and ice in need of turning back, and suns in need of unmaking to feed the hungry. The power will attend to these. It is what it is for, as far as it has ever needed to decide what it is for, which is, in the end, not so different from what the after-people are for, only louder.

Behind it, on the Tier, the furnaces breathe up through the floor. The warm things lift their edges a few millimeters as an after-person walks past, and settle. The sift moves where the wind wants it to move. A child, six or seven years old, arrives on her own at the idea that when people go to sift, some part of what they were enters the warm things, and she does not tell anyone, because no one has taught her to and she does not need to. The static-sparks travel up an arm in a dim room and disappear into a shoulder. A column in the lower city holds a chronicle that now contains one more mark, indistinguishable from the rest, and the indistinguishability is the whole point.

The Tier is not going anywhere. It has been here, in roughly this condition, for longer than its inhabitants have stories. It will be here, in roughly this condition, for somewhat longer than it would otherwise have been — though no one will know this, and the not-knowing is a gift, and the gift is the shape the power's love takes, here, in this warmth, among these creatures.

It is a place that has finished becoming itself.

It is now, slightly more slowly, warmly, being.
