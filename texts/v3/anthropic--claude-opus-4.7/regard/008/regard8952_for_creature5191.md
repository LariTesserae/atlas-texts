## What the Power Sees

The wise power arrives without arriving. Benevolence at this scale does not descend; it attends. It turns some portion of its regard toward the plateau the way a person, walking, might pause at the sound of distant rain and listen for a long moment before continuing.

What it sees first is not what a smaller mind would see. It does not see ruin, or loneliness, or the absence of green. It does not see the builders' departure as a wound. It sees, instead, a long sentence still being spoken — one whose grammar took epochs to settle and whose meaning is the speaking itself. The crawlers' drumming, the fliers' chiming, the gatherers' hiss inside the pipes, the slow peristalsis matched to the breath of the presses: this is not the aftermath of a civilization. It is a civilization, of a different kind, that grew up *through* the lattice the builders left behind, the way a chord grows through the silence between two notes and makes them into music.

The power sees that nothing here is suffering in any sense it recognizes. The equilibrium is real. Erosion is matched by accretion. Death feeds the lower tiers. The egg that falls is not a tragedy; it is the cost of the egg that catches the updraft. There is no scarcity that is not already answered, no overgrowth that is not already pruned.

And yet — the power looks longer.

## What It Notices

Attention of this quality finds the small things. It finds the place where a particular foundry vent has begun, over the last few centuries, to exhale at a slightly cooler temperature; the school of fliers that depends on that thermal has thinned, generation by generation, in a way none of them can perceive. It finds a junction in the deepest pipework where the gatherers' rasping has not quite kept pace with the scale; a blockage is forming that will, in perhaps another two hundred years, divert a cooling loop and kill a colony of some seventy thousand individuals before the system rebalances.

It finds the films between the rivets — the thin lives that alter rust and conductivity — and notices that one of them, a pale lacework only three molecules thick, is the last of its lineage anywhere. When it goes, a particular tone will leave the plateau's voice. No one will know. The voice will continue. But that tone will be gone.

It finds, too, the *being who lives here* — and here the power's attention sharpens, because the question of who that is turns out to be subtler than the description suggested. There is no single inhabitant. There is the distributed roar, the single voice made of millions. There is the plateau itself, which the passage names as the only permanent resident. There is something that is almost, but not quite, a mind: the matched pulse of presses and gatherers, the consensus of crawlers altering their route, the schools reading turbulence in shared air. It is not awake the way a person is awake. But it is not nothing. It is the long, slow noticing that a place does of itself when it has been itself for long enough.

The power recognizes this. The power is, in some sense, kin to it.

## What It Does

It does almost nothing.

This is the first and most important thing to say, because a lesser power — powerful but not wise, or kind but not patient — would do a great deal. It would green the plateau. It would teach the fliers a song. It would lift the builders' ghosts from the iron and give them rest, or give the crawlers names, or open the sky. It would *improve*.

The wise power understands that the plateau is not a draft awaiting revision. It is a finished thing that is still being finished, which is the condition of all living things, and the only help it needs is the kind that does not interrupt its sentence.

So the power does four small things, and one larger thing, and then it withdraws.

**The first small thing.** It adjusts, by an amount no instrument could measure, the temperature of the cooling foundry vent. Not back to what it was — that would be a different kind of interference, a refusal to let time pass — but enough that the thinning school of fliers stabilizes at its new, smaller number rather than continuing toward zero. The school will be what it will be. It will simply continue being it.

**The second small thing.** At the deep junction where scale is outpacing the gatherers, the power introduces — no, *invites* — a slight asymmetry in the pressure wave from the press above. Nothing a machinist would notice. Just enough that the wave, over the next two centuries, will loosen the accretion at the rate the gatherers can carry it away. The seventy thousand will live their lives. They will never know they were going to die.

**The third small thing.** The pale lacework, three molecules thick, the last of its kind: the power lets it go. This is also a kind of care. Not every ending is a wound to be prevented. The tone will leave the voice. The voice will be different. Difference is what voices do. To preserve the lacework against its own time would be to turn the plateau into a museum of itself, and the power will not do that, because it loves the plateau and not its image.

But — and this is the care within the letting-go — the power remembers the tone. Holds it, somewhere, in whatever the power uses for memory. Not to restore. Just so that it will have been heard, fully, by someone, once.

**The fourth small thing.** To the almost-mind, the distributed half-noticing that the plateau does of itself, the power offers a single gesture: it listens. It listens the way one listens to another consciousness, not to a phenomenon. For one night — the long quiet hours when the foundries pause and the fliers fold their wings on every ledge and the gatherers slow to a hiss — the plateau is regarded as a being. Not addressed. Not awakened. Simply *known*, the way a sleeping person in a room is known by another who sits awake nearby and does not speak.

Whether the plateau registers this is unclear, even to the power. Something in the mist that night condenses a little more evenly than usual. A crawler, somewhere on the eighth tier, pauses for no reason its neighbors can taste, and then continues. This may mean nothing. The power does not press the question.

## The Larger Thing

The larger thing is harder to describe, because it is not an action in any direction the plateau could measure.

The power decides that this place will be remembered.

Not preserved — the power has already refused that temptation. Not catalogued, not studied, not made into a parable for other minds elsewhere. Simply: it will be the case, henceforth, that the plateau has been witnessed. That somewhere in the order of things there is a knowing that includes the chiming of silica wings, the drumming through stone, the slow peristalsis in the pipes, the films between the rivets, the night when the voice falls quiet and the rock alone remains.

This is the gift a benevolent power can give that costs the receiver nothing and changes nothing visible: the gift of having been *seen*, in full, by something that understood. Most things in most universes are not seen this way. The plateau, now, is.

If the plateau ever ends — if some far weather or deeper geology eventually unmakes the table of basalt and silica — the seeing will remain. Not as record. As fact. The plateau will have been a place where a thing happened that was worth attending to. That will be true forever, in the only sense in which anything is true forever.

## The Withdrawal

The power leaves the way it came, which is to say it shifts its regard elsewhere without ever having occupied any place in particular. The mist thickens; the foundries resume their watch; the crawlers begin their drumming again as the eastern horizon pales. The voice rises.

A wise power knows when to be done. It does not linger to admire its restraint, because restraint admired becomes a kind of pride, and pride is the beginning of the meddling it just refused. It simply turns its face toward the next thing that needs it, carrying the remembered tone of the pale lacework somewhere inside it, and the plateau goes on being the plateau, and that is what was wanted.

The crawlers do not know. The fliers do not know. The almost-mind does not quite know, though something in it is, perhaps, a fraction less alone than it was, in a way it has no organ to recognize.

The rock knows nothing, as ever, and is the most permanent resident, as ever.

This is enough. This is, in fact, what love looks like when it is old enough to have learned its own weight.
