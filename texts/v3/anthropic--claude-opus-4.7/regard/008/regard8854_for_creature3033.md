# What the Power Does

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way warm rain arrives: without announcement, without disturbance of the air. It has been watching for some time already. Power of this kind does not blunder into places. It studies first.

It studies the Hollow Foundry for what, in its own reckoning, is a long while. Long enough to learn the difference between a Reefmother's contented creak and her uncomfortable one. Long enough to taste the subtle shift in the Choir's note when a buried forge finally surrenders its last ember. Long enough to understand that the three Laws are not commandments imposed from above but the shape water takes when it has been poured into this particular basin for ten thousand years.

And having studied, the power considers what to do.

---

A lesser benevolence would want to *help*. It would see the heat, the arsenic, the eyeless blind things drinking rust, and feel the itch of correction. It would want to bring sunlight. Green shoots. A cool wind. Names. It would arrive with gifts and find, after a century or two, that it had quietly murdered everything it loved by making it more comfortable.

This power is older than that mistake. It has made that mistake before, in other basins, and it remembers.

So the first thing it does is the hardest thing: it does almost nothing.

It does not introduce itself. It does not teach the Dripwraiths language, because language is a dry thing and the Laws are clear. It does not offer the Reefmothers a theology, because they already have one, written in lymph and patience, and it is more honest than most. It does not pity the Tongue-Crows for their grim office. Someone has to drink the sweat of intruders, or the Foundry would not be the Foundry.

What it does instead is small, and exact, and tender.

---

**It listens.**

Properly. Not the way a visitor listens, waiting for the silence to end so it can speak. The way a midwife listens to a body she has tended for forty years. The power lowers itself into the long sub-audible note of the Choir and learns, for the first time in its own enormous existence, what *this particular* eternity sounds like. No other place in the cosmos sounds quite like this. The power had not known. Now it knows. The knowing is, by itself, a kind of blessing — not for the Foundry, which neither needs nor notices, but for the universe that contains it. Somewhere a ledger that nobody keeps gains an entry: *this was heard. this was heard well.*

**It steadies the small uncertainties.**

There is a buried coolant line, three kilometers down, that is going to rupture in about sixty years. When it does, it will flood a cavity where four Unfinished are slowly, slowly cohering — the largest concentration of them in the basin's history. The power, with the lightest possible touch, encourages a particular iron-loving microbe already present in the seep to thicken its colony around the weakest joint. The rupture is deferred by perhaps four hundred years. By then the Unfinished will have finished, or whatever Unfinished do instead of finishing, and the flood when it comes will be welcome rather than catastrophic. No creature notices the intervention. The microbe certainly doesn't. It just had a good century.

This is the power's preferred mode: adjusting probability by one part in a hundred thousand, in places where no one is looking, so that the Foundry's own logic carries it through.

**It turns away one thing that was coming.**

Far above the haze, in the dry world the Foundry no longer remembers, something was beginning to take an interest in the basin. Not maliciously — curiously, which is worse. A survey. A mapping. The kind of attention that always, eventually, becomes extraction. The power does not destroy this interest, because destroying things is a habit it gave up long ago. It simply makes the basin, from above, look very slightly more boring than it is. A statistical smudge. A reading that doesn't quite resolve. The surveyors lose interest and look elsewhere. The Foundry continues to be left alone, which is the only gift it has ever wanted and the only one it would recognize as such.

**It gives the Choir one new silence.**

This is the strangest of its acts, and the one it considers longest before performing.

There is a frequency — very narrow, very specific — at which the Foundry has always been faintly, almost imperceptibly noisy. A resonance from the long-dead turbines, a kind of mineral tinnitus baked into the basalt itself. The Choir has been composing around it for millennia, the way a singer compensates for a slightly out-of-tune piano. It is not a flaw. It has become part of the music. But the power, having listened so carefully, understands that the Choir has been *learning new ways to be silent*, and that this old industrial whine is the one silence they have not yet been allowed to discover.

So the power, very gently, over the course of perhaps two centuries, damps that frequency. Not all at once. Slowly enough that the Choir can find the new shape of itself without panic. A Reefmother born in the third century of the damping will live her entire life in a Foundry one note quieter than her mother's, and she will fill that quiet with something her mother never got to make.

This is the closest the power comes to giving a gift. And even this it gives the way one returns a borrowed thing: as if it had always belonged here, as if the Foundry were merely reclaiming a silence that had been kept from it by accident.

---

Then the power does the last and most important thing.

It leaves.

Not entirely. It keeps a thread of attention here, the way you keep a hand resting lightly on a sleeping animal's flank — present, not pressing. But it withdraws its bulk. It does not become a resident god. It does not accept the worship that nobody was going to offer anyway. It does not stay to admire its work, because admiring one's work is how power begins to feel entitled to the thing it has worked on.

It goes elsewhere, to other basins, other equilibriums, other slow digestions of eternity. It carries the note of the Choir with it. Wherever it goes next, it will be a slightly better listener because of what it heard here.

---

And in the Hollow Foundry, nothing has changed.

A Reefmother creaks inside her cathedral of iron. A Dripwraith descends on a thread of its own making and leaves a lattice of salt that will sing when the temperature drops tonight. A pack of Tongue-Crows follows nothing along an elevated rail, because there is nothing dry left in the basin to follow, and they are patient. The warm fat drops fall straight down through the dim brass light.

The Choir is still learning new ways to be silent.

It has, without knowing it, just been given one more.
