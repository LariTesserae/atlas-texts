The benevolent and wise power considers the Weeping Threshold for a long time.

It is the power's habit, when its attention is drawn somewhere, to consider what is needed. This is almost always how it begins: a noticing, and then the slow unfolding of *what would help*. Over uncountable ages it has midwifed stars, untangled griefs, set right small cruelties and large ones, fed the hungry shapes of being that could not feed themselves. Help is its native tongue.

But here, hovering at the rim of the folded basin where the thunderheads press low and the rock sighs upward in slow glassy blisters, the power finds its native tongue going quiet in its mouth.

What is wrong here?

Nothing.

What is unfinished here?

*Everything,* and that is the point.

The power watches a glass-otter pour itself out of a fissure, drink, and slip back down. It watches a hoop of frozen lightning forget itself into a ceramic cube. It watches a Weeper press its wet face to nothing in particular and hum a small metallic lullaby into the air, as if remembering a stranger.

The power has, in its long career, encountered many things that resembled suffering and were not. It has learned — slowly, with the humility that is the deepest mark of wisdom — that benevolence which cannot tell the difference between a wound and a chrysalis is not benevolence at all. It is only appetite wearing a kind face.

So it does not stabilize the rock.

It does not finish the artifacts. It sees the golden lattice trembling toward its perfect equation and feels the old, sweet pull to *complete* it, to give it the last filament it seems to want — and it recognizes that pull as its own hunger to be useful, not as the lattice's need. The lattice does not want to be finished. The lattice wants to bloom for an hour and be dismantled by tender hands and become thread for a dream. The power lowers its hand.

It does not teach the Weepers language. They have one. It is dripping.

It does not clear the bruised thunderheads to show them the sky. The sky is not being withheld from them; the sky is simply not yet part of what they are. When they are ready for sky, they will weep a sky into being, or grow eyes that can bear one, or decide that sky was never the right idea and invent something better.

It does not even bless the place, in the formal sense, because to bless is sometimes to fix, and this place must not be fixed.

What, then?

After a long while — and the power has all the long whiles there are — it does three small things.

**The first** is simply to witness. It sets a portion of its attention down into the basin the way one might set a warm stone into moss: gently, without weight. Not to watch over, which implies guarding. Not to watch for, which implies waiting. Only to watch *with*. Somewhere in the Threshold, a Weeper that had been about to dissolve pauses for half a heartbeat, feeling — without knowing it feels — that its becoming has been seen. It is not changed by this. It dissolves on schedule. But the dissolving is, for that half-heartbeat, *accompanied*. The power has learned that this is often the largest gift it is permitted to give: to make a thing's unfolding less lonely without making it less its own.

**The second** is a quiet adjustment, so small it barely deserves the name. Far outside the basin, in the wider weather of the world, the power notices a thinning — a place where something cold and certain is leaning in toward the Threshold from a great distance away. Not malice. Just the ordinary pressure of finished things against unfinished ones, the way a hardened crust presses on a soft yolk. The power exhales, and the pressure eases by a fraction. The Threshold will have a little more time. Not forever. Forever would be its own kind of violence. Just *enough*. Enough for the rock to try on a few more shapes. Enough for the Weepers to keep being the perpetual almost for a while longer before whatever comes next comes.

It does not tell them it has done this. They would not want to know. Knowing would be a kind of finishing.

**The third** is the strangest, and the power surprises itself a little by doing it.

It leaves something behind in the basin. Not an artifact — the Weepers would dismantle an artifact, and rightly so. Not a gift, because gifts imply givers and receivers, and the Threshold has no use for that grammar yet. What it leaves is closer to a *permission*. A small, soft, wordless assurance, woven into the humid air so lightly that no Weeper will ever consciously encounter it, threaded into the percussion of the drips so that it sounds like nothing more than one more drop among thousands:

*You are allowed to never decide.*

*You are allowed to be the question.*

*Nothing in the great wheel of things requires you to harden.*

It is not a command. It is not even encouragement. It is the removal of a pressure the Threshold did not know was there — the faint background hum, present in almost every young world, that whispers *become something, become something, become something already*. The power simply turns that hum down, here, in this one folded basin, for as long as the basin needs.

The Weepers will not notice. They will go on weeping, swarming the rising rock, drinking light from brief flowers, breaking open anything that grows too certain. But the breaking will be a little easier now. The swarming a little freer. The lullabies they hum into the ears of rare visitors will carry, underneath their metallic sweetness, a note that was not quite there before: a note that says *we are permitted*.

Then the power withdraws most of itself, leaving only the thread of witnessing behind, and turns its great patient attention elsewhere — to a grief on another world, to a star learning to die well, to a child somewhere choosing between two roads.

But a small portion of it stays, always, at the rim of the Weeping Threshold. Not guarding. Not waiting. Only keeping company with a place that has chosen, magnificently, to remain a beginning.

And when, ages hence, the Threshold finally does decide what it wants to be — if it ever does — the power will be there to greet that decision with the same tenderness it now offers to the not-deciding. Because wisdom, in the end, is mostly this: loving a thing in the shape it is currently wearing, and being ready to love it in the shape it wears next, and never, ever confusing your love with a demand.

The drips go on.

The rock sighs upward.

A Weeper splits along its spine into six translucent moths and spirals, singing, into the bruised and waiting air.
