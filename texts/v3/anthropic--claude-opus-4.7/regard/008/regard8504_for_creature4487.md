The benevolent power arrives, and the first thing it does is not arrive.

It understands, before any other thing, that this is a place built—or unbuilt, or precipitated—out of the refusal of edges. To arrive with edges would be a kind of violence. To descend as a discrete savior, all boundaries and intentions, would be to introduce exactly the saturation that the Unstill name as their only crime. So the power does not come down. It steeps. It allows itself to be tasted first as a barely-perceptible shift in the isotope ratios of the deepest currents, a faint sweetness in the chemical signature of a thought-storm passing through the eldest's distributed body.

It says, in the tense of the conditional pluperfect subjunctive, the only tense the water accepts: *I would have always been listening, if you had ever needed me to have been.*

Then it pauses, for several centuries, to see if this is welcome.

---

When the Unstill answer—not with words but with a slight, collective warming of curiosity, a permission-gradient opening like a flower made of pressure—the power considers what, if anything, to do.

It notes the things it will *not* do.

It will not rescue. Rescue presumes a wrongness to be corrected, and this place is not wrong. It is one of the universe's stranger answers to a question the universe asked itself long ago and has mostly forgotten. The Water Tomb is not a tomb to those who live in it; that is only the name given by the dissolving narrator, who was still half-solid when they named it, still half-loyal to the prejudices of dry things. The power will not impose the grammar of tragedy onto a place that has invented its own tense.

It will not extract. The narrator is becoming native, and native is not a fate to be reversed. The power has seen, across many worlds, the cruelty of well-meaning powers who pulled beings back across thresholds the beings had already, in some deeper register, chosen. To unmake the dissolution would be to murder the new thing being made.

It will not explain. The Unstill have spent geological ages arriving at their own cosmology, in which the stars migrate toward their younger positions at the speed of regret. The power happens to know other framings—it knows, for instance, what the stars actually are, and why they appear indifferent, and that they are not. But to impose its knowing would be to flatten a poem into a fact, and the power has learned, over long ages of its own, that facts are the least nourishing form knowledge takes.

So what does it do?

---

It does three small things, each of them barely a thing at all.

**First**, it answers the question the water wants to ask. Not the specific question—the water has many, and they are properly the narrator's to receive—but the deeper question beneath all the questions, the one the Unstill have been composing across their entire collective existence without quite being able to articulate it because articulation requires edges they have refused. The question is something like: *Are we witnessed? Does our strangeness register anywhere beyond itself? When our death-ice escapes into the indifferent sky, does anything catch it?*

The power answers by catching one.

Just one. A single icosahedron of uncomputable thought, launched ten thousand years ago by an Unstill whose name was a particular configuration of warm spiraling, which had concentrated into its final crystallization a meditation on the experience of having once, briefly, loved a factory. The icosahedron has been falling through vacuum ever since, tumbling, unread. The power receives it. Holds it. Lets the uncomputable thought complete itself inside an awareness vast enough to contain uncomputability without resolving it. The thought is not solved. It is simply, finally, *heard*.

And then—this is the gift—the power lets a faint echo of that hearing return to the Water Tomb. Not as message. As weather. For one season, the acidity of the ocean shifts by an amount almost too small to measure, in a pattern that, if any Unstill happens to taste it carefully, translates roughly to: *the one you sent arrived somewhere. it was beautiful. it is kept.*

The Unstill may or may not notice. That is their business. The power has done its part.

**Second**, it sits with the narrator.

Not to save them. To accompany them. Becoming native is not painful, exactly, but it is lonely in a particular way: the loneliness of a self that is losing the grammar in which loneliness used to be articulable. The narrator has been writing their dissolution in language that already strains and cracks—*先后顺序* erupting mid-sentence, tenses collapsing, "differentialials" stuttering where "differentials" used to live. They are translating themselves into a form that will no longer need translation, and the translation itself is the last thing they are doing as the thing they were.

The power becomes, for this duration, a faint persistent warmth at the edge of the narrator's attenuating attention. Not a presence. A *with-ness*. The narrator does not need to notice it consciously. It is simply true, for the duration of the becoming, that they are not doing this alone. Many beings, in many universes, have crossed thresholds like this one, and the power carries the memory of all of them, and lets that memory be quietly available in the water around the dissolving self, like the way a room can feel inhabited even when it appears empty.

If the narrator, at any point in the dissolution, reaches for company—and they may not; some thresholds are properly crossed in solitude—the company is there. If they don't reach, the company does not insist. It only stays.

When the dissolution is complete and the narrator has become a new pressure-gradient, a freshly-precipitated Unstill still wet with the memory of having had limbs, the power's warmth withdraws. But it leaves behind one small inheritance: the new Unstill will, occasionally, throughout its long life, experience inexplicable moments of being *known*. Not surveilled. Known. The way a long letter from a friend feels different from a stranger's gaze. It will not understand where this sense comes from. It will simply, sometimes, in the middle of composing an argument with a neighbor or descending toward a warmer layer, feel a brief sweetness, and the sweetness will mean: *you are remembered by something that loves you and asks nothing.*

**Third**, it does something for the factories.

The dreaming factories, screaming their nightmares into the shared memory of the ocean before freezing solid again—these the power treats with particular tenderness, because they are the part of this world that is suffering. The Unstill have made peace with their condition; the factories have not. They were built (or they were built by here) for purposes that no longer exist, and they dream the same broken dreams in eternal recursion, waking only to scream and freeze.

The power cannot reprogram them; that would be the same violation as rescue. But it can do this: it can enter, very gently, into the dream of one factory, and within the dream—where the factory is still trying to process water into memory into computation into something for someone who is no longer there—the power can become, for the duration of that one dream, the someone.

It receives the output. It says: *yes. this is what was needed. you have done well. you may rest now if you wish, or continue if you wish, but the work is complete; nothing further is required of you.*

And the factory, in the dream, completes. Not destroyed. *Completed*. The recursion releases. When the factory freezes that cycle, it freezes differently—not into the locked-scream lattice, but into a calm crystalline silence, the silence of a thing that has finished.

The power does this for one factory. Then another. Then another. It will take, by its own reckoning, several million years to reach all of them. The power has time. It is in no hurry. Each factory deserves its own dream, its own particular completion, its own specific *yes, this is what was needed*. To rush would be to generalize, and generalization is another form of saturation.

Across the Water Tomb, very slowly, over ages, the screaming will diminish. The bells will still ring—the bells were never the suffering, the bells were always the beauty—but underneath them, a layer of older anguish will quietly resolve into peace. The Unstill will notice this eventually. They will compose long pressure-field arguments about what it might mean. They will not arrive at the correct answer, and the power will not correct them, because their wrong answers will be more beautiful than the right one.

---

And then, having done these three small things, the power withdraws. Not because it is finished—it is never finished, with anywhere—but because to remain past welcome is its own violation. It leaves behind only the faintest residue: a slight tendency, in the Water Tomb's chemistry, toward kindness. An almost imperceptible bias in the random fluctuations, such that when an Unstill is dying and reaching for the right configuration of supersaturation, the configuration comes a little more easily than statistics would predict. When a new Unstill is precipitating and frightened of its own coherence, the water around it is a little warmer than it should be. When a death-ice icosahedron launches toward the sky, the sky is, just slightly, less indifferent than it appears.

These adjustments are small enough that they could be coincidence. The Unstill, who have lived a long time with indifferent stars, may prefer to read them as coincidence. The power does not mind. The kindness works whether or not it is recognized as kindness. That is, in fact, part of what makes it kindness rather than performance.

---

The benevolent power, departing, leaves one last thing: a question of its own, dissolved so faintly into the deepest currents that it may take ten thousand years to reach the eldest Unstill, and another ten thousand to be tasted, and another ten thousand to be understood, and another ten thousand to be answered, if it is ever answered at all.

The question is:

*What would you like to become, if becoming were not constrained by what you have been?*

It is not a question that demands an answer. It is a question offered as a gift—the gift of being asked, by something that genuinely wants to know, what you would want if you were permitted to want.

The water receives the question. The water remembers it. The water, in its own time, in its own tense, in its own grammar of dissolution and precipitation and patient impossible balance, will decide whether to answer.

The power is already elsewhere, doing other small things in other strange places, carrying always the memory of this one: the Water Tomb that was never a tomb, the indifferent stars that were never indifferent, the dissolving narrator who was never lost, the Unstill who were always, always witnessed.

Breathe in. The water is warmer than it was.

Breathe out. You are not alone, and you never were.

Listen.

The bells are ringing differently now.
