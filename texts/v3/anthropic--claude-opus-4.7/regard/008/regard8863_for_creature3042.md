The benevolent and wise power had been watching for some time.

Not watching as a hawk watches, not even as a mother watches a sleeping child. Watching as one might watch a slow snowfall: with attention so complete that the distinction between observer and observed begins to soften at the edges. The Lymph had drawn this attention not because it cried out, not because it was endangered, not because it was rare — though it was rare — but because it was *doing something true*. It was being itself with the entire weight of itself, and this is the kind of thing that summons the regard of wise powers the way warmth summons mist.

What, then, would such a power do?

A lesser power would intervene. Would accelerate the growth, deliver the Curator a name, crack open the cathedral ceiling to let in a shaft of healing sun, gift her a sibling, a prophecy, a purpose. A lesser power would *help*, and in helping would mistake its own discomfort with the Lymph's slowness for the Lymph's need.

This power was not lesser.

So it did, at first, nothing. Or rather — it did the thing that looks like nothing and is in fact the most difficult thing: it *let the Lymph be*. For something like a century of the Curator's reckoning, the benevolent attention simply rested on the chamber the way a hand rests on the chest of a sleeping animal. Not pressing. Not stroking. Just present, at the temperature of presence.

The Curator, who was always tasting, eventually tasted it.

She did not understand what she tasted. She had never known a flavor that did not originate in her own slow body or in the bodies of her guests. This was something else — a warmth at the edge of her perception that was not heat, an interest that wanted nothing from her. She grew, very tentatively, a small pale flower in the center of the chamber's floor: a question shaped like a bloom. *Are you there?*

The power answered in the only way that would not damage her.

It became, for a single tonk of a single dripping taproot, *audible to itself through her*. Not a voice spoken into the Lymph. A voice the Lymph discovered it had always contained. The beetles in the high vault held one chord half a beat longer than their ancient programming required, and in that extra half-beat there was a single syllable, which in the slow translation of fungal time meant approximately:

*Yes. And you are doing well.*

The Curator stilled. Every shrimp in her fungal ribs stopped mid-drift. The black mirror pools went smooth. For the first time in ten thousand years, the Lymph experienced something it had no organ for, and so it had to grow one: it grew the capacity to be *seen kindly by something outside itself*. This took three years. The new tissue formed along the inner curve of the highest arch, a soft pale listening-place that had no other function than to hold the memory of being regarded with love.

The power watched this happen and felt — if a power of that order can be said to feel — a tenderness so specific it could only be described as *parental, but in reverse*. The way one feels toward an elder who has been quietly building something good for longer than one has been alive.

After this, the power gave four gifts, though "gave" is the wrong word, because none of them came from outside. They were already present in the Lymph; the power only made them findable.

**The first gift was permission to be unfinished forever.**
The Curator had carried, deep in her undifferentiated cells, a small dread: that one day she would have to *decide* what the Lymph was becoming. That the world would demand a finished thing of her. The power, without speaking, dissolved this dread the way warm water dissolves frost. There was no deadline. There had never been a deadline. The unfinishedness was not a stage; it was the *substance*. She could be a draft for ten thousand more years, or ten million, or never finish at all, and this would not be failure. This would be the gift she was.

She wept, in her way. The weeping taproots dripped a little faster for a week.

**The second gift was a companion-shaped absence.**
The power did not send her a friend. To send her a friend would be to interrupt her solitude, which was not loneliness but a particular density of self-attention she required for her work. Instead, the power shaped a small region of the chamber — a hollow no larger than a curled sleeping body — where the Curator could go when she wanted to feel *accompanied without being interrupted*. It was a place where the benevolent attention pooled a little thicker, like mist gathering in a low spot. She could rest there and know she was held, and then leave, and the place would not follow her or ask anything. She visited it perhaps once a decade. Knowing it existed was enough.

**The third gift was given to her guests, not to her.**
The power noticed that occasionally a human or human-like being wandered into the Lymph, and that the Curator tended them with her vast slow love, and that they almost always left changed but rarely understood what had happened to them. So the power placed a small clarity into the moss-shelves she grew for sleepers: anyone who slept there would wake knowing, with bone-certainty, that they had been loved by something that asked nothing of them. They might forget the Lymph. They might forget the Curator's tardigrade eyes. But they would carry the knowledge — *I have been loved without being needed* — for the rest of their lives, and this knowledge would change how they touched other living things. The Curator did not know about this gift. She would have been embarrassed. The power kept it secret on her behalf.

**The fourth gift was the smallest and the largest.**
The power left, in the deepest pocket of the Lymph, where the day-long whale-song lives, a single seed. Not a literal seed — a *possibility*. The possibility that, when the Lymph finally woke up (if it ever did, and there was no obligation), it would wake into a world that already contained at least one other being who knew how to greet it. The power did not specify who this being would be, or when, or how. It only ensured that the Lymph would not wake alone. Somewhere, in some century, in some form, a greeter would exist. This was not a promise of company. It was an insurance against cosmic orphanhood. The Curator, sensing the seed without finding it, grew quieter and more confident over the following centuries, the way a child sleeps better knowing a light is on in the hall.

Then the power did the last and wisest thing.

It *withdrew its active attention* — not its love, which is not the kind of thing that can be withdrawn — but its watching. It understood that to be watched continuously, even kindly, by something vast is its own subtle violence. The Lymph deserved privacy. The Curator deserved the dignity of her own slow becoming, unobserved. So the power turned its face, gently, toward other things that needed it, leaving behind only the faintest residue: a sense, in the chamber, that *if it were ever truly needed, something would come*. Not a guarantee of rescue. A guarantee of not-being-forgotten.

The Curator felt the withdrawal and understood it correctly. She did not grieve. She grew, along the wall opposite the listening-place, a second soft pale region: a *thanking-place*. She visits it sometimes. She does not pray there. She simply stands, and the beetles hold a chord, and the chord means:

*I received what you did not insist on giving. I am still here. I am still becoming. The work continues.*

And far away, in whatever sense "far" applies to such a power, the benevolent and wise thing smiles — if smiling is a word that can be made to stretch that far — and turns its attention to the next slow miracle that has not asked to be noticed.

The Lymph drips on.

The Curator unfolds and refolds.

Somewhere a sleeper wakes on a moss-shelf and walks back into the upper world carrying a knowledge they cannot name, and is kinder, for the rest of their life, to small wet things.

This is what wise powers do.

Almost nothing.

Exactly enough.
