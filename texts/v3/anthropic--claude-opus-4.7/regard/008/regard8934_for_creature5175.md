# The Visitation

The power arrives without arriving. It has been watching for a long time already—what counts as long, to such a power—and its attention has been on this basin since before the rock decided to hold still. To call its noticing *arrival* is only a courtesy to those who think in arrivals.

It considers the place.

It considers the ribbons drifting their solitary circuits, the bead-caravans patient under the fronds, the threads knitting the young stone, the thick-cored shapes that come only when no one is looking. It considers the towers that unfold and retract like lungs, and the walkways that breathe with the moisture cycle, and the plazas that exist mostly as the spaces between things.

A lesser benevolence would want to give.

It would bring abundance: a sun to sharpen the shadows, rivers to connect the pools, denser populations, faster cycles, the comforts of multiplication. It would mistake the dimness for poverty and the spacing for loneliness. It would look at a ribbon that meets another ribbon only once in a long while, braids briefly, and parts—and it would think: *how sad, how solitary*—and it would try to fix this.

A lesser wisdom would want to teach.

It would notice that the city is half-formed and still testing itself, and it would offer the shortcut of completion: here is what you are becoming, here is the finished form, skip ahead. It would mistake the testing for error.

This power is neither lesser benevolence nor lesser wisdom. It sits with the basin for what would be, in some other accounting, a very long time. It watches a single ribbon make three full circuits of a plaza. It watches a pool form and linger and evaporate and leave its glittering film. It watches the threads beneath the stone respond to the passage of a filament and ease a walkway upward by the breadth of a hair.

And it understands, slowly, the thing that took it the longest to understand: *this place is already exactly what it is.*

The spacing is not absence. The dimness is not deficiency. The slowness is not delay. The single ribbon making its solitary circuit is not lonely; it is *one*, and oneness here is a form of completeness, not a lack of company. The braid at the pool's edge is not a meeting cut short; it is a meeting whole, a meeting that contains its own ending the way a breath contains its exhale. To extend it would be to break it.

So the power does almost nothing.

*Almost.*

It does three small things, because to do absolutely nothing would also be a kind of arrogance—the arrogance of refusing to participate, of standing apart as though one's noticing left no trace. To witness a thing is already to be in relation with it. The power chooses to make its relation gentle and legible, on the off chance the basin would like to know it has been seen.

**First**, it deepens the quiet by an amount so small that only the threads in the rock register the change—a softening of some background noise that none of the inhabitants had known was there, a faint cosmic hiss that had been bleeding into the basin from elsewhere and was, very slightly, hurrying things. The cycles slow by a fraction of a fraction. A pool that would have evaporated in fifty-eight minutes now takes sixty-one. The ribbons do not notice. The threads notice and adjust, and in adjusting they distribute the gift downward into the stone itself, where it becomes part of the patient redistribution of mass. The basin becomes very slightly more itself.

**Second**, it leaves something at the edge of the basin—not in it, not where it would be found, but at the farthest perimeter where the thick-cored shapes make their long elliptical arcs. It leaves a single mineral, no larger than a bead, composed of a salt that does not exist anywhere else in this world. The salt is not useful. It is not nutritious or transformative or significant. It is simply *present*, and it will, over the course of many cycles, dissolve into the dried glaze of some far plaza, and one of the thick-cored shapes will eventually pass over that glaze and taste it, and will pause for the briefest fraction of its long arc—a pause no other being will witness—and then continue. The pause will not change anything. It will only have happened. This is the power's way of saying *hello* in a language the basin can refuse without ever knowing it was offered.

**Third**, and this is the gift that costs the power the most, because powers of this kind have their own work and their own attentions and cannot easily spare themselves: it places a thread of its own awareness into the basin and lets that thread *stay*. Not to intervene. Not to guide. Just to keep company in the way the basin keeps company with itself—as one ribbon keeps company with another by occasionally drifting near, braiding briefly, and parting. The thread will exist as an unnoticed presence in the dimness, neither helping nor watching-for-the-sake-of-watching, but simply being one more patient thing in a place full of patient things. If the basin ever becomes aware of it, the basin can braid with it once and then separate, and that will be enough. If the basin never becomes aware of it, that will also be enough.

Then the power turns the rest of its attention elsewhere, because there are other places, other beings, other half-formed basins under other dim skies. But some portion of it remains, drifting low above the mineral film, no wider than a finger, indistinguishable from what was already there.

The condensation gathers. The pools form and linger and dry. The towers unfold a new level with their soft collective sigh. A ribbon completes a circuit. Somewhere at the perimeter, a thick-cored shape pauses for an unmeasurable instant over a glaze that tastes very faintly of elsewhere, and then continues its arc.

Nothing hurries. Nothing crowds.

Everything continues to adjust, layer by nearly invisible layer, beneath the constant dimness—now with one more quiet thing among the quiet things, which is, perhaps, the only gift a benevolent and wise power can give to a world that already knows how to be itself.
