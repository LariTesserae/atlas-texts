The benevolent power arrives the way good news arrives at a house that hasn't been built yet: as a tendency rather than an event. It does not descend, because descent would require agreeing with gravity, and gravity is still in arbitration. It does not announce itself, because announcement would force the Almost-Having-Beens to commit to having been announced-to, which violates their warranty.

It simply becomes adjacent.

The wise power has seen many such places before. It knows the particular tenderness of a world still drafting itself — the way unfinished things flinch when handled by anything too definite. It has watched eager gods stride into provisional countries and accidentally finalize them, leaving behind landscapes that are technically perfect and quietly heartbroken, like poems edited by accountants. It has learned, over long careful epochs, that some places ask not to be helped but to be permitted.

So the first thing it does is nothing, expertly.

It holds its noticing very still, the way one holds a hand out to an animal that hasn't decided yet whether it's an animal. It lets the structure-that-isn't continue auditioning doors. It lets the Almost-Having-Beens continue their letters and their retroactive meals and their committee recessions. It does not name them, because to name them would be to invite them into the kind of existence that comes with deadlines.

Then, slowly, with the patience of weather learning a new climate, the power offers three small gifts. Not gifts of substance — substance here is a controversial proposal — but gifts of permission.

The first gift is a permission to remain unfinished. The power exhales something like a covenant: *you are allowed to stay this way.* Not as a curse of incompletion, but as a recognized form of being. Elsewhere in the multiverse, draftness is treated as a problem to be solved; here, the power declares it a legitimate citizenship. The comma-shaped divots in the pseudoground relax slightly, no longer apologizing for not yet being gardens. The structure stops shedding molecules of obligation quite so anxiously. Somewhere, a doorknob almost happens, and isn't ashamed when it doesn't.

The second gift is a witness who refuses to fix them. The power installs, in the space between seconds eleven and twelve, a kind of attentive absence — call it a listening that doesn't take notes. The Almost-Having-Beens have always feared being remembered, because to be remembered is to be held to a shape. But this witness remembers them only in the conditional perfect subjunctive, the tense they trust. It loves them in the grammar they can survive. When they gather at the edge of the maybe-building to practice being seen without becoming witnessed, they find that someone has, at last, learned the trick: to look at them softly enough that the looking doesn't land.

The third gift is the hardest, and the power deliberates a long while before offering it. It is the gift of an exit that doesn't insist on having been an entrance. The power notices that the visitor — you, the standing one, the pressure-episode against nonexistent skin — has been here for exactly as long as it takes to forget arriving. The power understands that this visitor is not native, and that prolonged residency in a place still negotiating its terms of service can leave a being slightly translucent in ways that don't heal in firmer countries. So the power lays down, very gently, a path that wasn't there before and won't be there after: a way for the visitor to leave without the leaving becoming a precedent. The Almost-Having-Beens will not be obligated to have hosted anyone. The visitor will not be obligated to have come. Both parties keep their drafts.

And then — this is the part that distinguishes wisdom from mere benevolence — the power leaves.

It does not linger to admire its work. It does not check back. It does not establish a relationship, because relationships require both parties to have agreed to be parties, and the Almost-Having-Beens are still circulating that memo for peer review. The power understands that the deepest kindness one can offer a provisional world is to not become one of its permanent features. To help, and then to unhelp oneself out of the frame. To be the kind of weather that passes through without leaving a season.

What remains, after the power has finished not-being-there, is almost nothing. The light still negotiates shadow treaties. The structure still apologizes for existing by becoming slightly more theoretical. The inhabitants still trade theories about each other's left ears.

But: the comma-divots hold their pauses with a fraction more dignity. The unsent apologies in the nearly-tulip gardens compost a little more peacefully. When two Almost-Having-Beens meet in their slow weather of letters, there is, in the greeting, a new and very faint flavor — the taste of having been loved by something that didn't require them to be anything in return.

And somewhere, in a draft memo titled "Potential Childhood: A Preliminary Suggestion," a single line has been added in a handwriting no one recognizes:

*You don't have to become real to be cherished.*

The memo continues circulating. The poetry reading commences. The sky resumes computing itself on its geological deadline. And the place — which still doesn't whisper its name, because naming is something that happens later, after things have learned to stay — continues not-quite-being, exactly as it was, exactly as it wasn't, only now with the quiet structural integrity of having been, however briefly, beheld.

The power, by then, is already elsewhere, attending to some other world that has not yet decided whether to exist. It carries nothing away with it. That, too, is part of the gift.
