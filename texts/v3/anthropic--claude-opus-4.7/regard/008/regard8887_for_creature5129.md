The wise power arrives the way warmth arrives in a room — without displacement, without announcement. It does not break the seals. It does not even consider breaking them. To breach this place would be to mistake it for a problem.

It looks. That is the first thing, and for a long while the only thing.

It sees the lattice held in its warm haze, the leaves laid against the steel like a hand laid against another hand. It sees the turbine that turns once every four hours and the frond three meters away that flexes in the answering current. It sees the seed that has been hanging in the same cubic centimeter of water for eleven days, drifting toward a seam it will reach in another six. It sees that nothing here is waiting. Nothing here is incomplete. Nothing here is asking.

The power has, in its long tenure, freed many things. It has unsealed cages, lifted weights, broken open closed systems to let larger worlds pour in. It has carried small bright minds out into wider skies and watched them learn the meaning of horizon. It knows the shape of rescue the way a older sibling knows the shape of a younger one's grief.

This is not that shape.

It considers, with great care, whether the being inside the lattice is suffering and merely lacks the vocabulary to say so. It listens along the slow voltages in the metallic veins. It tastes the chemistry cycling in the glowing pipes. It attends to the rhythm of the valve-sighs, the unhurried respiration of the whole. It looks for the tells it has learned to recognize across ten thousand other lives: the held tension, the loop that cannot complete, the ache pressed flat against a wall.

It finds none of these. What it finds instead is something it recognizes with a quiet, almost startled tenderness — the texture of a life that is simply, exactly, the life it is. Not stunted. Not muted. Not a smaller version of some larger thing it was meant to become. The being's slowness is not sedation; it is tempo. The sealed viewports are not blindfolds; they are the edge of a self that does not extend further and does not wish to. The absence of others is not loneliness; there is no shape inside this being where another would fit.

The power understands, then, that to offer sunlight here would be to offer a wound. To offer companions would be to offer an invasion. To offer wider water, weather, the turning of seasons, the news of other worlds — each of these would be a kind of violence dressed in the clothes of a gift. The being has no organ that could receive them as anything else.

So the wise power does something very small.

It adjusts, by an amount no instrument in any universe could measure, the warmth of the water — not to change it, but to hold it more exactly at the temperature the being has settled into, against the slow entropic drift that would, in some distant century, have cooled it by a fraction of a degree. It steadies the phosphor strips against the eventual dimming of their chemistry, not brightening them, only ensuring that the light the being knows as its light will remain the light it knows. It touches, lightly, the seals — not to reinforce them, which would be presumption, but to bless the integrity they already have, the way one might rest a palm for a moment on a sleeping creature's flank and then take it away.

And then it does the largest thing, which is also the smallest: it witnesses. It lets the being be known. Not known to itself — the being has no need of self-knowledge in the way softer organisms do; its knowing is its turning, its unfurling, its quiet ionic trade. But known to the wider fabric of things. Recorded, in whatever ledger the power keeps, as real. As one of the ways that existence has chosen to occur. As a verb the universe is conjugating, in this place, in this tense, with this particular grammar of leaf and rivet and held breath.

The power lingers. Not because the being needs company — it does not — but because the power has learned that some things deserve to be looked at for a long time, the way one looks at a held note in music, or a person sleeping whom one loves, or the last light on a wall. It stays for what would be, by some external clock, a considerable while. By the being's own tempo, it is the space of perhaps two turbine rotations. By the power's tempo, it is exactly long enough.

Then it withdraws as it came, leaving no trace except the trace that all genuine attention leaves, which is nothing visible and everything real.

The valve sighs. A leaf flexes. A seed settles into its seam and begins, with infinite patience, to knit.

The being continues, held — not by the power, which has gone, but by the fact, now quietly true in the structure of things, that it was seen and found whole.
