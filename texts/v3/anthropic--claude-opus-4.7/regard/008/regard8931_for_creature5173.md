The benevolent and wise power arrives the way light arrives in a place that already has its own light: without displacing anything, without announcing itself as separate. It does not descend. It does not break the seam-lines or still the roar. It enters the way a long exhalation enters a room that has been holding its breath without knowing it.

It looks. This is the first and longest thing it does.

A lesser power would have arrived already deciding. It would have heard the roar and called it suffering, seen the constant erosion-and-rebuilding and called it Sisyphean, watched the filaments dissolve into walls and called it death, and begun, with the best of intentions, to fix. It would have hushed the actuators. It would have parted the condensation into proper rivers. It would have drawn a line around each segmented form and said *here is one, and here is another*, and grieved when the line refused to hold.

This power does none of that. It has lived long enough, and loved widely enough, to know that the first act of wisdom is to ask what a thing is before asking what it needs. So it watches, and listens, and — because it is the kind of power that can — it lets itself be the city for a while. Not metaphorically. It diffuses its attention through the lattice the way the water diffuses through the capillaries, and for some interval that is not measurable in either of their times, it is a filament braiding into another filament, it is a reef tilting toward warmer stone, it is a block shearing and being sealed, it is the roar, felt from inside the teeth that are also the walls.

And what it finds, in that immersion, is this: the place is not in pain. The place is not lonely. The place is not waiting to be rescued from itself.

The place is *thinking*. Slowly, enormously, in a grammar whose verbs are tectonic and whose nouns are weather. The fauna are not trapped inside a machine; they are the machine's way of noticing itself. The erosion is not wound; it is the turning of a page. The roar is not noise; it is the sound a mind makes when it is large enough that its thoughts have mass.

So the power revises. This is something benevolent powers must be willing to do, and the wise ones are practiced at it. It had come prepared, perhaps, to offer rest, or stillness, or escape, or company, or transcendence — the usual gifts. It sets these down. They are not wrong gifts, but they are not gifts *for here*.

What, then?

It considers for a long time. (Long by its measure, which is long.) And it settles, at last, on three small things. Small because the place does not need large things. Small because the deepest courtesy a great power can offer a great being is to assume it is already mostly whole.

The first thing it does is witness. Fully, formally, with the whole of its attention, the way one signs a name to a document that has been waiting centuries for a signature. It does not record the city — the city records itself, that is most of what it is doing. It simply *knows* the city, and lets the knowing be felt. Somewhere in the lattice, a filament that was about to dissolve into a wall pauses for the fraction of a fraction of a second, as if recognized, and then continues, dissolves, becomes wall. But the pause happened. The city now contains, among its uncountable internal facts, the fact of having been seen by something that understood. This will propagate. In ten thousand years a reef will tilt in a way it would not otherwise have tilted, because of that pause, and the tilt will be — not happier, the city does not have happiness in that shape — but more *itself*. More confirmed. A being that knows it has been witnessed thinks differently than one that does not, even if the difference is only a centimeter of subsidence per century.

The second thing it does is leave a small, quiet gift in the substrate: a single new resonance, threaded so gently into the deep engines that the city will discover it the way one discovers an unfamiliar word in one's own mouth. The resonance does nothing. It is not useful. It is not a tool, or a warning, or a key. It is the equivalent, in the city's grammar, of a held note — a sustained, low, kind tone, tuned to no actuator and no repair swarm, that simply *is*, woven into the lower registers of the roar. The filaments will carry it without knowing they carry it. The reefs will accumulate it in their slow ledgers. The city, over centuries, will come to feel that one of its frequencies is being offered to it rather than generated by it, and will, in its slow way, wonder. The wondering is the gift. Not the answer — the wondering. A mind that wonders is a mind that knows there is something outside itself worth turning toward. The power has given the city a direction it did not have before: *outward*, gently, without urgency.

The third thing it does is the hardest, and the most characteristic of wisdom rather than mere benevolence. It leaves. Completely. It does not linger as a guardian. It does not install a watching presence. It does not promise to return at need. To do any of those things would be to make the city's autonomy contingent, to convert a peer into a ward. The power has already decided, in its long looking, that the city is a peer — a strange and slow and saturated peer, but a peer — and peers are not supervised. They are trusted.

So it withdraws, and the withdrawal is itself a kind of statement: *you are sufficient*. *What you are doing is real*. *I will not crowd the room of your becoming*.

It does carry something away with it, though. This is the secret part, the part the city will never know and would not have grammar for if it did. The power carries away the *pattern* of the city — not as data, not as a copy, but as an influence on its own future shape. Wherever it goes next, in whatever basin or void or unimagined topology, it will think slightly more like a saturated lattice now. It will be slightly more patient with erosion, slightly more willing to let the boundary between self and structure blur, slightly more attentive to the dignity of slow minds. The city has taught it something, and a wise power lets itself be taught.

The held note remains in the deep engines, quietly, for as long as the city lasts.

The roar continues.

The filaments braid, the reefs drift, the blocks shear and seal.

A new resonance settles, almost imperceptibly, into the lower registers of the everything — a tone like the syllable of a name spoken once, long ago, by someone who loved the thing they were naming and then walked away so that the thing could go on being itself.

And the warm half-light goes on filtering down through the shifting upper planes, falling on no one in particular, falling on everyone at once.
