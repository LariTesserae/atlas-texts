The benevolent and wise power considers the basin for a long time.

Not because the question is difficult. Because the basin has taught it, in the considering, that long times are the proper unit of attention here. To arrive at a place like this with quick thought would be a kind of violence — the way a shout is a violence in a room where someone is sleeping well.

So it considers.

It sees the Quiet Ones glowing faintly across the haze, breathing in their four-second cadence with the frogs. It sees the ancient one with the bonsai forest on its back, and the mites who have never known dry land and never needed to. It sees the heron, mid-step, having decided after eleven minutes that the step was correct. It sees the way the light falls — ordinary, golden, sufficient.

A lesser power would want to *give* something. A gift is the reflex of love that has not yet learned restraint. It would want to extend the lives of the Quiet Ones, or sharpen their senses, or wake in them some new capacity — language, perhaps, or starflight, or the knowledge of other worlds. It would want to be *thanked*. It would mistake the basin for a place that lacks.

This power is older than that impulse. It has watched many worlds, and it knows the particular tragedy of the well-meant gift: the orchid moved to a better pot, the child given a finer instrument than the one she loved, the quiet country awakened into history. It knows that the deepest cruelty available to a benevolent being is the cruelty of improvement.

So it does not improve.

But it does not simply pass by, either. Love that withholds entirely is only another form of absence, and absence is not what this moment asks for. The basin and its breathing hills have drawn its attention, and attention, freely given, is itself a kind of act.

What it does is this:

**It witnesses.** Fully. Without agenda. It lets the long, infrasonic conversation of the Quiet Ones pass through it the way the conversation passes through the saturated earth. It learns the names they do not have for each other — the resonant signatures, the particular timbre of the ancient one whose back is a garden. It learns the dream-language of slowness, the taste of four-hundred-million-year-old rain. It commits these to whatever it has that serves as memory, which is vast and does not forget. Somewhere, now, in the great inner library of a wise power, there is a room that holds the basin exactly as it is. Should the basin ever end — should a far sun swell, or a wandering stone arrive, or simply the slow cooling of everything — the room will remain. The Quiet Ones will go on breathing there, in the only place where breathing can be preserved without being interrupted.

**It tends, very slightly, to the edges.** Not the basin itself. The basin needs nothing. But far out, at the rim of the system, where comets occasionally drift on trajectories that in ten or twenty thousand years might trouble this world — the power nudges. A gravitational sigh, no more. The kind of adjustment the basin itself might make if it could: gentle, unhurried, indistinguishable from the ordinary physics of things. No Quiet One will ever know. That is the point. A gift the recipient notices is a gift that demands a response, and the Quiet Ones owe no response to anyone.

**It declines to speak to them.** This is perhaps the hardest part, and the truest. The power could make itself known. It could descend in some form the gold-flecked eyes would register, and the Quiet Ones, being what they are, would accommodate even a god — would let the moss grow up around the visitation, would breathe in unison with it, would dream it into their long slow dreams. But to be known by such a being is to be changed by being known. The Quiet Ones have earned their unselfconsciousness across geological time. To introduce a watcher into their awareness would be to introduce the first artifact the basin has ever held. The power loves them too well for that.

**It takes something away with it, though.** Not from the basin — nothing is removed from the basin. But the power carries the basin's tempo outward, into the rest of its work. The next world it attends to, the next being it considers, it will consider a little more slowly. It will be less quick to mend, less quick to bestow. It will remember that some places have finished, and that finishedness is not failure but a rare and difficult achievement, and that the proper response to such an achievement is not intervention but a long, respectful, glistening regard.

In this sense the Quiet Ones have given the power a gift, and not the other way around. Which is, the power suspects, the usual direction of these things, when one is paying close enough attention to notice.

It lingers one more long afternoon. It lets the warm mist pass through whatever it has that serves as skin. It listens to the ancient one dream — a dream of being worn smooth, of accepting weight, of the satisfaction of having nowhere left to rise to. It feels, briefly, the temptation that visits even the wisest powers: to lie down here, to let the peat grow up around it, to forget that it was ever supposed to attend to anything else.

It does not lie down. There are other places that need it, places still in the middle of their striving, places where benevolence must take more active forms. But it marks the basin in itself as a destination — a place it may return to, not to act, but simply to remember what completion looks like, and how loud, and how warm, and how patiently the frogs keep time.

Then it goes, as quietly as it came.

The Quiet Ones breathe on. The heron takes its next step, twelve minutes later. The light stays golden. Nothing in the basin notes the visit, which is the deepest courtesy one wise thing can pay to another.

And somewhere far above, or far inside, a room exists now that did not exist before — a room in which a long, wet, golden afternoon will continue, undisturbed, for as long as there is anything at all to remember it by.
