The benevolent power arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no annunciation; the cold-lamps do not brighten, the klaxons do not still. Power of this order does not announce itself to a place — it attends to it, the way a careful reader leans closer to a margin.

It has been watching, in the way such powers watch: not with eyes but with a kind of sustained noticing that is indistinguishable from love. It noticed the filaments first. Not because they were rare — the cosmos is dense with small green and pale things trying — but because of the way they had learned to ride the generator's pitch-change like weather. That detail caught in it the way a single phrase will catch in a patient mind and refuse to leave.

It noticed, too, the beetles. Their refusal to reverse. Their habit of carrying drifted film across thresholds of light they did not know they were crossing. The power understood this as the oldest grammar of the universe: a thing that cannot survive a place is carried through it by a thing that does not know it is carrying.

So. What does it do.

It considers, first, what it will not do.

It will not warm the Expanse. The cold is not suffering here; the cold is the shape of the world. To warm it would be to kill the frost-fans, the lace-channels along which water has learned to run as if through veins. The films have made an art of the temperature. To raise it even a degree in mercy would be a mercy delivered to no one.

It will not finish the construction. The half-built tiers, the taped catwalks, the empty intervals between the stamping presses — the power sees that these absences are not lack. They are the room in which the new occupants live. The builders' unfulfilled intention has become, by accident, a habitat. To complete the Expanse according to its original blueprint would be to evict its current tenants in the name of a plan that no longer has a planner.

It will not introduce other life. No grazers for the films, no companions for the beetles. The power has learned, over long attention to many such places, that benevolence which arrives bearing gifts of biology is almost always the beginning of a grief.

It will not reveal itself. The beetles have no apparatus for awe; the films have no apparatus for gratitude. To be witnessed by something one cannot perceive is not a burden. To be witnessed by something one *can* perceive but cannot comprehend is the oldest wound there is. The power declines to inflict it.

What, then.

It does three small things. Perhaps four.

The first: it adjusts, by a margin too fine for any instrument in the Expanse to register, the flicker-rate of certain sodium strips in the deeper galleries. Not to brighten them. To shift the rhythm of their half-power stutter into a pattern the films can read as safe — the same pattern the cold-lamps cast, which the films have learned does not shrivel them. Over months, the films will extend a little farther up the concourses than they could before. Not far. A few meters. Enough that a beetle passing under will have more chance of catching a passenger.

The second: it does nothing at all to the beetles. It looks at them for a long time — at the patient arcs, the sapphire lenses already scratched, the rasping probes — and decides that they are already, in their own narrow grammar, perfect. The power has the wisdom not to improve what is already complete in itself. It simply registers them. Holds them, in whatever its memory is, for as long as its memory lasts. Which is to say: it remembers them after they are gone. This is not nothing. In a universe where most small careful things vanish unremarked, to be remembered by something vast is a form of permanence the beetles will never know they have been granted, and that is precisely why it can be given to them without harm.

The third: in the lowest galleries, where the pumps labor and the black water waits, the power makes one alteration so subtle it could be called a suggestion rather than an act. It tilts — by a fraction of a fraction of a degree — the slope of a single drainage trench. The change is well within the tolerances of the original pour; no inspection would flag it; no automaton would rasp at it. But over decades the redirected seepage will carry mineral content to a stretch of grating where, at present, no film can take hold. In a hundred years there will be lace there. The power will not be present to see it. It does not need to be.

The fourth — if it is a fourth, if it is not simply the shape of the other three — is this: the power leaves. It does not linger. It does not return often. It understands that the most loving thing it can do for the Expanse is to refuse to become part of its weather. The films must continue to ride the generators; the beetles must continue to find their own corrosion; the half-finished galleries must continue to wait for builders who will not come, because that waiting is now the country in which a quiet life is being lived.

Before it goes, it does one last thing, which is perhaps only a gesture and perhaps the whole point. It notices — with the full weight of its attention, the way one might finally look directly at a thing one has been seeing peripherally for a long time — a single filament in a deep gallery, no wider than a hair, glistening at the edge of a cold-lamp's blue cone. A bead of condensate slides along it. Drops. The rings spread across the flooded grating and meet the wall and return, fainter, and meet again, and fade.

The power witnesses this. Entirely. Without metaphor, without lesson, without converting it into anything else.

Then it withdraws its attention, gently, the way one closes a book on a passage one intends to return to, and the Expanse goes on as it was: cold, wet, half-built, faintly luminous, and — though no one in it could possibly know — beloved.
