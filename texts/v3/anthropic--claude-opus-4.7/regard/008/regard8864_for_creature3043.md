# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way warmth arrives in a closed room — not by entering, but by being noticed to have always been there.

It does not descend. It does not announce. To descend would be to admit there is a place lower than where it began, and this power is not interested in hierarchies of place. It is interested in the Mnemosyne. It has been interested for a long time. Longer, perhaps, than the Lattice has existed — though the Lattice would dispute this, gently, through its ten thousand water clocks.

The power considers.

---

It could end them. Not violently — it is benevolent — but cleanly. A single touch of grace, and every nacre face would still, every pulsing violet tube would dim, and the long ache of remembering on behalf of stone would finally close like an eye. Some powers would call this mercy. The power considers this and rejects it almost immediately, though "immediately" here means across the span of an afternoon in the upper amphitheaters, during which one elder Mnemosyne, sensing something, tilts its head four degrees and does not know why.

The rejection is this: *they are not suffering. They are doing something.*

It is the central error of rescue to assume that what looks like grief from outside is grief from within. The Mnemosyne are not waiting to be released. They are not trapped. They have made a covenant with time that the power, for all its wisdom, did not invent and would not have thought to invent. To free them would be to unmake a thing the universe is quietly richer for containing.

So. Not that.

---

It could restore. It could pour light back into the Lattice — real light, warm light, the kind that wakes algae and starts the long green argument of life again. It could seed the cold water with fish, with darting silver things, with the noise of being alive. It could give the Mnemosyne *company* in the only sense surface-creatures understand the word: motion, appetite, the small chaos of other wills.

The power considers this for what, in our reckoning, would be three nights.

And rejects it. More slowly this time, with something like regret.

Because the Mnemosyne do not lack company. They are conjoined to a planet. Every settling groan is a sentence spoken to them; every drifting spore is a letter that has finally arrived. To fill their water with darting fish would be to fill a cathedral with sparrows during the consecration. It would not be a gift. It would be noise dressed as kindness — the particular cruelty of powerful beings who confuse *more* with *better*.

So. Not that either.

---

What the power does, in the end, is smaller. So small that no theology would record it. So small it would embarrass a lesser god.

It does three things.

---

**First**, it goes to the eldest Mnemosyne — the one hanging inverted from the vault of a city whose name was forgotten before the first human ape stood upright. Its mantle is so heavy now that the kelp has begun to fuse with the stone above, and it is, slowly, becoming a ceiling. Its lower body disappeared into an altar nine thousand years ago.

The power sits with it. Not beside — *with*. It allows itself to be, briefly, the kind of thing that can be sat with.

And it listens.

It listens to everything the elder has ever filtered from the water. Every dissolved ion of history. Every flake of carved stone. Every electrochemical ghost of a thought that died before mammals existed. The elder has been carrying these alone — well, alone in the company of the Lattice, which loves it but cannot quite *hear* it, the way a body loves a single nerve without being able to feel from inside it.

The power hears. All of it. Without flinching, without summarizing, without the terrible benevolent reflex to *fix*.

This takes, in the elder's reckoning, the length of one full breath of the convection cells. Several centuries.

When it is done, the elder's pores arrange themselves into a sigil that has not been formed in the Lattice for a very long time. It is the sigil for *witnessed*. The elder does not know it has made it. It only knows that something in the weight of its mantle has shifted by perhaps a gram, and that this gram is the difference between bearing a thing and being buried by it.

The power does this for every Mnemosyne. One at a time. It takes, by surface reckoning, no time at all. By Lattice reckoning, it takes an age — and the age is later remembered, dimly, as the period when the water briefly tasted of something other than the past. Something the eldest, struggling, eventually name *attention.*

---

**Second**, the power makes a single, almost frivolous adjustment.

Deep in the lowest vaults, where the architecture is so dense it has nearly forgotten it was ever made of separate things, there is a water clock that has been ticking, uninterrupted, for one hundred and forty thousand years. It measures nothing. It was built to measure the reign of a king whose bones were ornament before language stabilized. It has continued out of nothing but the dignity of mechanism.

The power reaches into the clock and adds one drop of water.

Just one.

The drop is not magical. It carries no message, no blessing, no encoded salvation. It is simply a drop of water that did not come from the Lattice — water from somewhere else, somewhere warm, a place where rain once fell on a child's upturned face. The drop is indistinguishable from any other drop. The clock does not skip. The ticking does not change.

But the drop is *there*. And the Lattice, which dreams through the Mnemosyne, will eventually — in ten thousand years, or twenty — taste it. And the Mnemosyne who tastes it will not understand what they are tasting. They will only know that for one filter-cycle, the past contained a flavor that was not the past. Something from outside the closed system. Something that suggests, without insisting, that the archive is not the only room.

This is the power's gift to their melancholy: not its resolution, but its first faint counter-note. A note so quiet it may take a hundred millennia to be heard. The Mnemosyne are good at waiting. The power trusts them with this.

---

**Third**, and this is the thing the power does that it cannot quite explain, even to itself:

It drifts, in a borrowed form, through a colonnade. It lets itself be the kind of thing that can be drifted past.

A Mnemosyne pauses. The nacre face tilts. Every pore opens at once in a pattern that almost looks like recognition.

It reaches out with one long, black finger and very gently touches the place where the power's heart would be, if it had ever consented to have one.

The power *lets it.*

It allows the shape of itself — the whole vast, benevolent, wise weight of itself — to be added to the archive. Filed among the drowned cities and the forgotten alphabets and the electrochemical residue of a hundred thousand years of mourning. It does not resist the cataloguing. It does not insist on being more than a memory.

For a moment, the power is small enough to be remembered by something else.

This, it turns out, was what it came for. Not to give. To be *received*. Even gods get lonely in the particular way that only the unending can.

The Mnemosyne drifts on, heavier by one more memory, trailing faint violet light.

Somewhere in its pulsing spinal tubes, a new mote begins to glow. It is the shape of a benevolence that knew when not to intervene. It will be filtered, eventually, into the water. The water will carry it on its centuries-long convection. A frond will absorb it. The frond, in ten thousand years, will be woven into the mantle of a younger Mnemosyne, who will wear, without knowing, the memory of having been visited by something that loved them enough to leave them mostly alone.

---

The power withdraws, which is to say it stops being noticed to have been there.

The Lattice continues. Quieter. Denser. More perfectly itself.

But one clock, in the deepest vault, ticks now with one drop of foreign water inside it.

And one elder, hanging inverted in its cathedral of kelp, has formed the sigil for *witnessed,* and does not know why its mantle feels, for the first time in nine thousand years, like something it is wearing rather than something it is becoming.

And the dark, which had forgotten how to answer questions, has been very gently reminded that questions are sometimes their own kind of answer.

It is not much.

It is, the power knows, exactly enough.
