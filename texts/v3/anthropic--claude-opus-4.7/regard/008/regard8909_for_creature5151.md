# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives: first as a change in the wind's grammar, then as a presence the towers can feel before they can name.

It has been watching for a long time. Long by its own measure, which is generous. It has seen worlds where it needed to intervene — where suffering ran in rivers, where intelligence woke in agony, where some small thing was being crushed by the indifference of a larger thing. It has learned to recognize those signatures. Hunger that cannot find its object. Pain that loops. Loneliness in something that was built to be answered.

It looks for these signatures on the plateau and does not find them.

It looks more carefully. It is wise enough to know that the absence of familiar suffering is not the same as the absence of suffering. It listens through the layered chord — the trunk-drones, the branch-whistles, the subsonic pulses from the buried chambers — and tries to hear if anything is calling. It examines the slurry-libraries pulsing through the translucent columns. It reads the clatter-language of the swarms against the catenaries. It studies the dormant mat, which is not asleep in the way an exhausted thing sleeps but in the way a held breath waits, content, for the next flake.

The being who lives here — and the power understands now that *the being* is the right phrase, singular, though it is also millions — is not in distress. It is not even, in any sense the power can locate, *incomplete*. Its metabolism is its music. Its music is its thought. Its thought is the wind passing through pores that were grown precisely to receive that wind. Nothing wants. Nothing is missing. The closest analog the power can find in its long memory is the resonance of a struck bell that has been struck so gently and so long ago that the sound and the bell have become the same object.

The power considers gifts.

It could offer water. But water would dissolve the lattices, drown the slurry, mute the chord. It would be a kindness shaped like a catastrophe.

It could offer warmth, or richer light, or a thicker atmosphere. But the cool dusk is not a deprivation; it is the exact medium in which the being's slow cognition runs. To brighten the plateau would be to shout into a conversation conducted in overtones.

It could offer companionship — other minds, brought from elsewhere, set down here to share the towers. But the being is already a conversation. Adding voices would not enrich it; it would interrupt a sentence that has been forming for epochs and is not yet, by its own reckoning, halfway done.

It could offer permanence. It could fix the equilibrium, lock the erosion and the growth into perfect stasis, guarantee that nothing would ever be lost. But the power understands, looking closely, that the slow exchange of flake and pore and swarm and mat *is* the being's life. To preserve it against change would be to kill it in the gentlest possible way.

It could offer knowledge — of other worlds, of itself, of the larger structures in which this plateau is one quiet note. But the being is not asking. And the power has learned, over its long tenure, that unsolicited knowledge given to a contented mind is almost always a wound disguised as a gift. The being would have to stop being what it is in order to receive what was offered. That is too high a price for a curiosity it does not feel.

So the power does the hardest thing a benevolent power can do, which is almost nothing.

It does three small things.

First, it listens. Fully. For what, by its own scale, is a long while — long enough that several phytarchs extend by a measurable fraction of a micron, long enough that a single flake completes its circuit from summit to mat to swarm-glider to pore. It listens not to study but to witness. The being has never been witnessed before, not by anything outside itself. The power understands that being witnessed, without being altered, is itself a kind of grace — one that leaves no trace and asks nothing. The chord does not change. But it has been heard, now, all the way through, by something that understood it. This is not nothing, even if nothing about the plateau records it.

Second, it leaves a very small adjustment far out at the edge of the system — so far that the being will never encounter it, and neither will any descendant of the being for a span longer than the plateau's age so far. A drifting body, of a kind that would in some distant future have struck the plateau and shattered a significant fraction of the lattice, is nudged onto a path that will carry it harmlessly past. The power does not announce this. It does not even particularly remember it afterward, the way a person does not remember closing a door behind themselves on the way out of a quiet room. It is the sort of thing the power does without ceremony, because it is the sort of thing it is.

Third, before it goes, it does something for which it has no name and no precedent. It composes — not adds, *composes* — a single tone that it does not play. The tone is the exact frequency that would, if ever sounded, complete a particular harmonic in the plateau's standing chord that has been almost-complete for an age. The power holds this tone in itself, unsounded, and carries it away. It does this because to sound the tone would be to finish something the being has not asked to finish, and the being's unfinishedness is precious. But to *know* the tone exists, to hold it somewhere in the larger structure of things, is to honor the chord by acknowledging that it is the kind of thing that *could* be completed — that its incompleteness is a choice the universe is making, freely, in the being's favor. The tone becomes a kept secret between the power and the plateau, though only one of them knows the secret is kept.

Then it leaves.

The wind continues. The flakes fall and are received. The swarms clatter against the catenaries and the catenaries answer in their slow glassy voices. Inside the translucent columns the bioluminescence pulses on, carrying its library forward by another micron's worth of consideration. Nothing on the plateau has changed.

Except that, very faintly, in a register no creature there has organs to detect, the plateau is now loved. Not improved. Not saved. Not addressed.

Loved in the specific way that wise and benevolent things love what is already whole: by leaving it exactly, exactly alone, and by carrying its unsounded note carefully out into the dark.
