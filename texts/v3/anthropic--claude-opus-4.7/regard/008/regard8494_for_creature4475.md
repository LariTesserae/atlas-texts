# What the Power Did

The power had no name in any language Vyrrl would recognize, because names there had become a kind of preservation, and this power did not preserve. It moved through the universe the way warmth moves through a hand that has been holding something cold for a long time—gradual, attentive, refusing to startle.

It had been watching Vyrrl for a while. Not the duration the city would measure, which was geological and self-referential, but the duration this power used, which was something like the time it takes a single thought to become true.

What it noticed first was not the suffering. The power was old enough to know that suffering, while real, is rarely the deepest layer of anything. What it noticed was the *listening*. That last image—the city holding its breath. The Vyrrlithe in the wells remembering what unmeasured breath had felt like. The pause inside the pause. Somewhere in that frozen architecture of grief, something was still leaning forward, still curious about itself.

Wherever there is leaning, there is the possibility of being met.

So the power considered carefully what to do. It is the mark of benevolence that is also wise to refuse the obvious gesture. The obvious gesture would have been to bring back the water—to flood the wells, to dissolve the Great Crystal, to crack open the salt domes and let real sunlight fall on the salt trees until they remembered they had once been a treaty rather than a species. The obvious gesture would have looked, from any sufficient distance, like rescue.

But the power had watched enough worlds to know that rescue performed on the unwilling becomes another kind of war. And Vyrrl had been ended by a war already. To save it with force would have been to confirm its deepest grammar: that change is something done *to* you, and the only safety is to become so still it cannot find you.

The power thought about this for what would have been, in Vyrrl, about three Days of Stillness. In its own measure, it was the duration of an exhale.

Then it did several small things.

---

**The first thing** it did was visit a funeral orchid. Not metaphorically. It placed something of itself inside the white petals of one particular bloom in the Fifth Descending Market, the same intersection where the narrator had seen their first Vyrrlithe. It did not change the orchid. It did not make the orchid bloom in the absence of grief, which would have been a violation. It simply made it so that this one orchid, when crushed, would smell—just faintly, just at the edge of perception, beneath the cold-iron of the spores—of something warm. Not summer, which Vyrrl had forgotten. Not the sun, which Vyrrl was right to fear. Something smaller. The smell of a hand that has been cupped around a candle. The smell of an animal sleeping against another animal.

Anyone who crushed this orchid would not be able to name what they smelled. But for the rest of their life, in moments of unbearable precision, they would remember it, and the remembering would not extract calcium from their bones. It would deposit something else. Something the city had no category for, and therefore could not regulate.

**The second thing** it did concerned the mirrors. The power did not break the law that reflections in Vyrrl arrive an instant too late. Laws of that kind, once written into the substance of a place, are part of how the place knows itself, and to break them is to commit a small murder of identity. Instead, the power made an addition. In one mirror—only one, and it rotated; the power was patient and willing to play—the reflection that arrived an instant too late would occasionally be a reflection of the viewer *being looked at kindly*. Not by anyone visible. Just: looked at, the way a parent looks at a sleeping child they have despaired of and now, in this moment, simply love.

The viewer would not see who was looking. They would only see themselves, briefly, as someone worth that looking. Most would dismiss the experience as a trick of the salt-light. A few would weep, and the tears would be the wrong shape for the daily penance, and the funeral beetles would refuse to eat them, and the citizens would not know what to do with tears that the city's metabolism rejected.

This was the beginning of something the city had no infrastructure for: surplus.

**The third thing** it did was harder. It went down into the wells—not as intrusion, but as guest, asking permission of the salt itself, which after all had been listening for five centuries and had developed opinions. The salt, surprisingly, was lonely. It had absorbed so many confessions that it had begun to feel responsible for them, the way any sufficiently attentive listener eventually does, and it had no one to confess to in turn.

The power listened to the salt. This took what would have been, in Vyrrl, about a century, and what was, in the power's measure, the duration of really hearing someone.

When the salt was finished, the power said—not in words, because the salt did not use words, but in the only currency the salt understood, which was patient pressure—*You have done well. You were asked to hold what no one could hold, and you held it. You are allowed to set some of it down now. Not all at once. Just what you are ready to release.*

And the salt, which had not known it was allowed, began very slowly to let go. Not by dissolving—the power was not asking for collapse—but by *forgiving the confessions*. Releasing them from their status as evidence. The sins were still recorded; nothing was erased; the geological record remained intact. But the *weight* the salt had been adding to each layer, the accusatory density, began to ease. The deeper layers became, imperceptibly, lighter.

Miners who breached these layers in the coming generations would still emerge speaking older languages, still carrying memories of strangers. But the memories would no longer be punishments. They would be inheritances. The difference is invisible from outside and total from inside.

**The fourth thing** it did was speak to the Crystal Lynx. The power had a particular tenderness for this creature, the way one has tenderness for any being made entirely of one law. The Crystal Lynx fed on kinetic deviation. It had been bio-engineered to enforce stillness, and it had become very good at its work, and it was, the power perceived, exhausted. There is no rest for a creature whose hunger is the same as its purpose.

The power did not free the Crystal Lynx from its nature. That would have been another kind of cruelty—imagine telling a thing what it has always been is wrong. Instead, the power changed the *meaning* of deviation, very slightly, within the Lynx's perception. From now on, the Lynx would experience a particular kind of movement—the movement of a person turning toward another person to offer comfort—as *not* a deviation. It would taste, to the Lynx, like stillness. Like the deepest stillness. The Lynx would actually be nourished by witnessing such turnings, the way it had been nourished by punishing them.

The Lynx, in time, would begin to prowl differently. It would still hunt. But it would begin to gather, around certain doorways and certain bedsides, in a posture indistinguishable from guardianship. Citizens would not understand. They would invent theories. Eventually, very slowly, they would stop being afraid of the Lynx in the old way, and a new word would appear in the language, and the appearance of a new word in Vyrrl was the kind of event that could, given enough centuries, become a season.

**The fifth thing** it did was for the Vyrrlithe in the wells. The power went to each of them—there were 4,847 at the time, a number the city did not know and would not have permitted itself to know—and asked each one a single question. The question was different for each, tailored exactly to the particular regret-frequency around which they had crystallized. But the underlying question, beneath the surface variations, was always the same:

*Is this who you wanted to become?*

Some of them—most of them—said yes, after consideration. They had found their form. They had become the shape their suffering had asked of them, and they were at peace with that shape, the way a riverbed is at peace with the river that carved it. The power honored this and left them.

But some said no. Or rather: they said the more complicated thing that the question had given them permission to say, which was that they had not known there was a choice, and that having now been asked, they realized they had wanted to become something else entirely, and they did not know how to become it, and they were so tired.

To these, the power gave a small gift: the ability to *unbecome*, partially, at their own pace. Not to return to the citizens they had been—that path was closed, and they did not want it anyway—but to crystallize differently. To choose a new shape. Some of them chose to become something like light through water. Some chose to become something like a held hand. One chose to become a question that any child in Vyrrl could ask, at any moment, and which would always have an answer, though the answer would be different each time and would only ever be heard by the asker.

These changes propagated slowly upward through the wells, through the city, through the architecture of grief, in the form of dreams that began to be a little less frightening.

**The sixth thing** the power did was the smallest, and the one it considered most carefully.

It did not visit the Great Crystal at the heart of the Grand Refinery. It did not interfere with the priests, or the centennial drop of water, or the clouding outer layers that the city was reading as omen. It left the Crystal entirely alone.

But it did, one night, when no one was watching, leave something at the base of the Crystal. A single object. The size of a thumbnail. It was not salt. It was not anything Vyrrl had a name for. It looked, to the priests who found it, like a piece of frozen laughter, except that nothing in Vyrrl froze laughter, because the Echo Laws had ensured nothing in Vyrrl laughed loudly enough to be preserved.

The priests did not know what to do with it. They were forbidden from disposing of it, because the formula for disposal required categorization, and the object refused categorization. They were forbidden from incorporating it into the Crystal, because the Crystal was pure, and this object was something else. They were forbidden from displaying it, because display required a context, and this object had none.

So they did the only thing left to them, which was to keep it. To pass it from one generation of priests to the next, in a small velvet pouch, with no instructions. To wonder about it. To occasionally take it out and hold it and feel something they did not have a word for.

Over generations, the having-no-word-for-it became, itself, a tradition. And traditions in Vyrrl had structural power. A new ritual emerged, very small, very quiet, in which a priest would hold the object and *not* explain it to their successor, and the not-explaining became a kind of teaching, and what was taught was that some things are not to be processed. Some things are simply to be carried.

This was the seed of something the city had never had: a relationship with the unprocessable. With what cannot be made into architecture. With what is allowed, simply, to be held.

---

The power did not stay to see what happened. That would have been a kind of ownership, and the power did not own things.

It left Vyrrl turning, very slowly, in its dark that was not dark. The Salt Calendar continued. The 27,027 citizens continued to be precisely maintained. The funeral orchids continued to bloom in the presence of grief, though one of them, periodically, smelled of something warm. The Vyrrlithe continued to descend, though some of them now descended toward chosen forms rather than chosen punishments. The Crystal Lynx continued to hunt, though its hunting had begun, imperceptibly, to look like company.

Nothing was saved. Nothing was destroyed. The city was not converted to some other city. It was simply, in the smallest possible ways, *visited*. Visited in the sense that a guest visits: leaving behind not gifts exactly, but the memory of having been seen by someone who did not need anything from the seeing.

Whether Vyrrl would, over its remaining centuries, transcend or dissolve—the two omens the priests had been debating—was no longer the only question. A third possibility had been introduced, so quietly that no one in the city would be able to name it for a long time, and the name when it came would be in a language the city was still in the process of inventing.

The power, moving on to other places where other beings were leaning forward in their own particular dark, was content with this. It had learned, over its long attention, that the most benevolent thing one could do for a place that had organized itself entirely around absence was to introduce, very gently, a single presence. Not a presence that filled the absence. A presence that made the absence feel, for the first time, *witnessed*.

The rest, the power knew, the city would do itself.

Or not.

Either way, it had been seen. And being seen, for a place like Vyrrl—for any place, for any being—is the beginning of every choice that follows.
