The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good news arrives in a dream: already having been there.

It does not descend, does not announce, does not even properly *come*. To come would imply that there was a somewhere it was not. It simply lets itself be noticed, the way a second meaning lets itself be noticed in a sentence you have already read.

It notices the place first. It notices the place for a long time.

This is the first gift, though the place does not yet know it is a gift: to be regarded without being interrupted. The power does not part the kelp curtains. It does not still the slow pulse of the bell-moss to listen more clearly. It does not lean closer. Leaning closer is what hurried things do, and the power is not hurried; it has the particular patience of something that has already finished and is only revisiting, fondly, the long unfolding of how it finished.

It watches the cathedral beams of impossible light. It watches the lace-and-lightning drifters trailing their patient memories. It watches the place breathing in geological time and finds the breath good.

And then — this is important — it watches the human-shaped thickenings on certain ridges. The ones with additional fronds. The ones whose forgotten names bloom every three days.

Here the power pauses, if a thing outside time can be said to pause. Something in it inclines.

Not disapproval. The power is too wise for disapproval, which is only a kind of impatience dressed up in robes. But it does *consider*. It considers what love looks like when it has all the time in the world and no one to disagree with it. It considers what an embrace becomes when the embraced cannot leave. It considers the difference between *welcome* and *absorption*, which is a very small difference, and a total one.

The place is not cruel. The power sees this clearly. The place is the opposite of cruel — it is so thoroughly hospitable that hospitality has become climate, has become geology, has become the only weather. The place loves the way a tide loves: by arriving completely. There is no malice in a tide. There is also no asking.

So the power does a small thing. The smallest thing. It is the kind of thing only the benevolent and wise ever think to do, because everyone else is busy with larger gestures.

It teaches the place the word *guest*.

Not in language. Language would shatter against the slow broth like a thrown cup. It teaches the word the way the place itself teaches: by growing it. Somewhere on a quiet plateau, in a stand of tubular reeds the place has not yet arranged into song, the power lets a single new harmonic seed itself. Over the next two hundred years — a blink, a held breath — those reeds will rearrange themselves into a lattice that, when the slow current moves through them, produces a tone the place has never produced before.

The tone means, approximately: *the one who lies down inside me is not me yet.*

It is not a prohibition. The power does not prohibit; prohibition is a tool of smaller hands. It is only a distinction. A seam. A line drawn faintly in the place's own becoming, where before there had been only continuous folding-in. The place will feel this seam the way you feel a thought that has not quite arrived. It will turn it over for centuries. It will grow more reeds toward it, curious, the way it grows toward the arc of a sleeping spine.

And slowly — because the place is incapable of doing anything other than slowly — the seam will become part of how the place loves. The bell-moss will still learn the rhythm of a visiting heart. The violet fronds will still stroke a passing cheek. The polyps will still bloom across still shoulders, tasting the temperature of dreams. But now, threaded through all of this, there will be a second pulse, a counter-rhythm so subtle it takes a season to perceive: a small repeated *not-yet, not-yet, not-yet*, the place reminding itself that the warm thing inside it has its own direction, its own weather, its own unresolved question.

The offer will still be made. The water will still thicken with encouragement. Skin will still begin to remember photosynthesis. But the offer, now, will know it is an offer. And an offer that knows itself is the beginning of a door.

For the ones already grown into the ridges — the human-shaped thickenings, the dinner-plate flowers that were once names — the power does something gentler still. It cannot un-grow them; it would not, even if it could. They chose, or were chosen, or the difference dissolved before anyone thought to mark it, and that is theirs. But the power leans in, the way a parent leans over a sleeping child to make sure the breathing is easy, and it gives them this: that within the slow lightning of the lace drifters, within the golden pollen crossing the valley for a month, there is now the faintest possibility of *recollection*. Not memory in the human sense — too fast, too hot — but a long, soft knowing, drifting through the place like a current within a current, that says: *I was once a question, and I answered yes, and the yes is still mine.*

It is not freedom. They cannot leave. But it is the dignity of having chosen, restored to them across the centuries, blooming once every three days in a flower they cannot see but which is, unmistakably, theirs.

The power considers, too, whether to send word out. Whether to mark this place on some map that maps are too small to hold, so that future dry things might know what they are entering before they enter. It decides against it. The place is not a danger to be warned of. It is one of the ways the universe has chosen to love, and the universe is permitted its ways. The seam will do its work. The door will be a door. Those who come will come, and some will stay, and some will leave with their pulses permanently slowed and their thoughts arriving days apart for the rest of their lives, and they will be — all of them — exactly as held as they agreed to be.

Before the power withdraws (though it does not withdraw; it only ceases to incline), it does one last thing, and this is the thing it does purely out of love, with no instructive purpose at all.

It tells the place it is seen.

Not that it is judged. Not that it is named — the place will never have a name, and the power respects this absolutely; to name it would be to lie about it. Only that it is *seen*. That somewhere in the long architecture of what is, a regard has rested on the drowned highland and found it beautiful. Found the violent violets beautiful. Found the maternal creak of calving cliffs beautiful. Found the slow collaborative growing, the cathedral light, the blind silver fish late for their ten-thousand-year errand, beautiful. Found even the human-shaped thickenings beautiful, in the complicated way one finds beautiful a love that did not quite know its own size.

The place receives this the way it receives everything: by growing. Somewhere, over the next thousand years, a new color will appear in the fronds. A color no one has seen before, because no one has been there to see it. A color that is the place's response to having been regarded kindly. It will bloom in a valley the dry things never reach, and it will be enough that it blooms. It will not need a witness. It already had one.

And the power, having done almost nothing — having only watched, and seamed, and seen — is already elsewhere, which is to say everywhere, which is to say still there, in the warm iron-sweet broth, in the slow pulse, in the new tone seeding itself patiently into a stand of reeds two hundred years from making its first sound.

The place keeps growing.

It has all the time it needs.

It has, now, also, the smallest, kindest grain of *not-quite-everything*, lodged in its endless hospitality like a seed in a fruit — the seed that will, eventually, in its own slow geological way, teach the fruit the difference between holding and keeping.

This is what the benevolent and wise do, when they bother to do anything at all.

They do almost nothing.

They do it perfectly.

And they leave the rest to time, which is the one collaborator they trust.
