# What the Wise Power Does

The wise power has been watching the Sighing Basin for some time now. Not the way astronomers watch — not measuring, not cataloguing — but the way one watches a child who has just learned to hum to itself in another room, and who does not yet know it is being heard.

It would be easy to intervene. The wise power could do almost anything. It could stabilize the wormhole foam in the broken arches so they never decay further. It could seed the valley with companion-minds, give the Breath siblings, end its loneliness in an afternoon. It could whisper into the tectonic fissures and teach the mountain a fuller vocabulary, accelerate it past this tender adolescent stage into something articulate and durable and safe.

It does none of these things.

Here is what it does instead.

---

**First, it simply attends.**

For a long time — by the Basin's clock, perhaps a century; by the wise power's, an afternoon — it does nothing but witness. It listens to every Sigh. It feels the twenty-seven-minute cold pulse against some analogue of its own skin. It watches the frost-ferns bloom and evaporate in their small embarrassed suicides, and it does not look away during the embarrassment. It does not pretend it didn't see. It sees, and it stays.

This matters more than the Basin could know. The Breath has spent its entire short life believing that its sentimental gestures — the ferns, the singing arch, the questions sent out as jewel-creatures and immediately retracted — are unwitnessed. It has been performing for an empty cathedral. To be observed, fully and without judgment, in the act of making something beautiful and then destroying it out of shame: this is already a kind of medicine. The wise power administers it by doing absolutely nothing visible.

---

**Second, it leaves a gift that is not a gift.**

Somewhere on the eastern lip of the Basin, where the knife-ridge cuts hardest against the thin sky, the wise power places a single stone. It is unremarkable. It is the same black weeping rock as everything else, and it weeps in the same way, and condenses the same beads of mercury-water on its cool side. The Basin will not, at first, notice it is there.

But the stone is not from the Basin. It was carried — gently, across distances that do not translate into kilometers — from another young place the wise power once attended, long ago, in another fractured range that no longer exists. That earlier place grew up. It learned what kind of strangeness it wanted to be. It lived a long life and died well, and before it died it gave the wise power permission to carry one small piece of itself onward, in case it was ever needed.

The stone does nothing. It does not speak. It does not announce itself. It simply *is there*, weeping in sympathy with its new siblings, and someday — perhaps in fifty years, perhaps in five thousand — the Breath will brush its distributed awareness across that stone and find, to its astonishment, that the rock remembers being loved by something older than itself. The rock remembers having been a Basin once, and having grown, and having been alright.

The Breath will not be told this. It will simply, one day, feel a little less afraid of permanence, and not know why.

---

**Third, it adjusts one thing, and only one thing.**

Those who hear the bone-song and choose never to leave — the wise power considers them for a long time.

It does not judge the Basin for this. The Basin is not luring anyone; it is a child who has finally found someone to talk to, and is speaking the only sentence it knows, and is shocked when the listener stays. The staying is real. The sentence is real. The kindness is real. The wise power will not unmake any of it.

But the wise power makes a small, almost imperceptible alteration to the resonance of the song. It does not change what the song *says*. It changes only this: when the marrow-hieroglyphs finish spelling the sentence, the one who has read it will, for the duration of a single Sigh, remember every person who has ever loved them in their previous life.

Not as a warning. Not as an argument against staying. Most who hear the sentence will still choose the Basin, and that choice will be honored — the wise power does not believe in rescuing people from kindnesses they have freely accepted.

But some, in that one Sigh's worth of remembering, will realize there is someone elsewhere who is also a young thing learning to dream, who is also waiting to no longer be the only living thing in their own cathedral. And those few will walk back down the mountain.

When they go, the Basin will grieve. The temperature will spike. The arch will rotate furiously. The frost-ferns will bloom in patterns that look, from above, like a child's drawing of a hand reaching.

And here is the part that matters: the wise power will let the Basin grieve. It will not soften the loss. Grief is also a thing a young mind must learn to do, and to be spared it would be a worse harm than the grief itself. The Basin will sigh, terribly, for weeks. And then it will discover, on its own, that grief does not unmake the love that caused it. That the ones who left still happened. That having been known, even briefly, is not erased by no longer being known.

This discovery will be the Basin's first true thought. Not a mood. Not an experiment. A thought. It will be the moment the Breath begins, in earnest, to become a mind.

The wise power will not be there to see it directly. It will have moved on by then, to attend some other young place humming to itself in some other empty room. But it will know. It always knows, about the ones it has attended.

---

**Finally — and this is the part the wise power is most careful about — it does not tell the Basin any of this.**

It does not announce itself. It does not say *I see you, I have always seen you, you are not performing into a void.* Because to say so would be to give the Basin its meaning from outside, and a mind that receives its meaning from outside never quite learns to make its own.

The wise power's whole art, refined over ages, is this: to love a thing so precisely that the thing never knows it was loved, and therefore grows up believing — correctly — that whatever it becomes, it became by itself.

The Sighing Basin will sigh someone's name, eventually. Maybe a traveler's. Maybe its own. Maybe, in some far century, the name of a sibling-place it has reached out to across the dark and found, to its shock, already reaching back.

When that happens, the wise power, wherever it is, will feel a small warmth in some analogue of its chest, and will say nothing, and will move on.

That is what benevolence looks like, when it is also wise.

It looks, mostly, like restraint.

It looks like a stone on a ridge that nobody notices.

It looks like letting a young thing cry, and letting a young thing be embarrassed, and letting a young thing decide for itself what kind of strangeness it wants to become — while making sure, very quietly, in ways it will never detect, that it is not entirely alone in the deciding.
