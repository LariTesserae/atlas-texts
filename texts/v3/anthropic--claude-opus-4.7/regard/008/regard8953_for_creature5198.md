They would come quietly.

Not as a descent, not as an arrival announced by light or sound, because this place has spent uncounted centuries refining itself against announcement. Anything that crashed in would crash through millennia of agreement. So the power, being wise, would first do nothing at all. It would simply attend.

It would watch the frond-walkers ripple along their living causeways for a long time before deciding whether the walkers were one being or many, and then it would decide the question was wrong. It would listen to the drone of the resonant insects until it could hear the few notes that had shifted, and the older notes preserved beneath them like sediment. It would count the salt-rings of the slow sirens until counting became a kind of participation. It would let a veil-net pass through it, or pass through a veil-net, and feel the lenses register its warmth as just another thermal, nothing to tack against, nothing to flee.

Being benevolent, it would want to give. Being wise, it would understand that almost nothing it could give would be welcome here. A gift accelerates. A gift introduces a debt, a new vector, a thread that does not yet belong to the weave. The frond-walkers do not need faster paths. The limpets do not need brighter algae. The gliders do not need stronger membranes; their thread-mites already manage what needs managing. To offer abundance to a closed loop is to break the loop. To offer knowledge to a metabolism is to give it indigestion.

So it would refine its first impulse. Not *give*, but *tend*. Not *change*, but *keep*.

It would notice, perhaps, the lone smooth towers, and trace their condensation halos back to whatever intention first set them weeping without releasing. It would find, in the hair-thin conduits beneath the fused platforms, the faintest fatigue — a place where the old luminescence is beginning to thin in a way the limpet-glow cannot quite compensate for, three or four glider-generations from now. It would touch that place. Not repair it in the human sense, not replace what is failing, but encourage the limpets already drifting toward it to drift a little more surely, the way a gardener might tilt a stone so that water finds a channel it was already going to find. The intervention, if it could even be called that, would be indistinguishable from the place's own tendencies. In a century no one could say whether the power had acted or whether the rock had simply remembered itself.

It would do this kind of work, perhaps, in a handful of places. Always at the scale of the system's own heartbeat. Always in the direction the system was already leaning. Never adding, only un-subtracting; never speeding, only un-slowing the slow.

And then there is the question of the being who lives here — and the strangeness of that singular, when everything described is plural. The frond-walkers, the limpets, the sirens, the nets, the gliders with their under-crews, the insects whose drone is a single instrument played by uncountable mouths. The being who lives in this place is the place. The metabolism is the inhabitant. The power would recognize this faster than any visitor with a narrower idea of selfhood, and it would address the being accordingly: not by speaking to any one creature, but by becoming, briefly, a participant in the exchange. A vapor cloud released slightly richer in the trace mineral the sirens are running low on. A thermal column held steady for an extra hour so a tiring glider reaches its mesa. A single flower's opening delayed by the breath of a second so its pollen meets a walker's path it would have otherwise missed. Small graces, indistinguishable from luck, which is to say indistinguishable from the ordinary workings of a world that has been lucky for a very long time.

If the being noticed — and something this old, this distributed, this attentive to its own rhythms, would notice — it would notice the way one notices a guest who has learned the house. Not an intrusion. A kindness that asks nothing. The drone might shift by a single note, downward, the way a long exhalation settles. The luminous veins might brighten for one generation of gliders and then return to their baseline, as if something had been acknowledged and then politely set aside.

The power would not stay. To stay would be to become a new force in the equilibrium, and a new force, however gentle, eventually becomes a dependency. It would withdraw the way it came: without announcement, leaving behind only the faintest adjustment in the trajectories of things already in motion. Perhaps it would leave one small token — not an object, because objects intrude, but a tendency. A slight increase in the probability that, somewhere in the next ten thousand years, when the haze finally thins or the stone finally cools or some larger turning asks this place to change, the change will find the system ready. A kind of blessing folded into the statistics. An extra grain of patience added to a patience already vast.

And then it would go, carrying with it the memory of a place where being benevolent had meant, almost entirely, restraint — where wisdom had consisted in recognizing that the highest love one can offer something already whole is to let it remain whole, and to leave it, if anything, very slightly more itself than before.

The mesas would continue. The water would continue. The drone would continue, perhaps a note lower, perhaps not. No one living there would have a word for what had happened, because nothing had happened in any sense the place recognized as event. Only this: for a little while, something enormous had loved them, and had been wise enough to love them by doing almost nothing at all.
