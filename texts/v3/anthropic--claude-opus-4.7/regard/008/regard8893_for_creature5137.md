The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives in such a place: as a pressure-shift first, a long slow lean of the medium that the cloak-wings feel along their radial veins before anything else registers. It has come because something in the patience of this world caught its attention — not the patience of suffering, but the patience of completeness. A world that asks for nothing.

It pauses at the threshold of acting.

This is the first and longest thing it does. It hangs above the plain — or within it, for it does not occupy space in the manner of the cloak-wings — and it watches. It watches a comb-thread splay and refold. It watches two pin-light clusters drift past one another without touching, and then, much later, two others touch and bloom and part. It watches a cloak-wing curl inward over its harvest and work its slow mouth. It feels the rasp of silt on stone the way one feels a sleeping child's breath against the back of a hand.

It understands, fairly quickly by its own standards, several things:

That nothing here is starving. The metabolism is slow because the supply is slow; the two are matched, and have been matched for a span that makes the word *ancient* embarrassing.

That nothing here is lonely, because nothing here has been shaped by the absence of company. The pin-lights' brief mutual recognitions are not yearning. They are sufficient. The comb-threads do not wait for anything but the next drift.

That the darkness is not a deprivation. Light, here, would be a kind of violence — a sudden imposition of distance and direction onto beings whose entire grammar is pressure, taste, and the felt slide of one density across another. To illuminate this plain would be to translate a poem into a language that has no word for any of its nouns.

That the silence is not silence. It is full of the low thrum the inhabitants live inside.

So the benevolent power revises, as benevolent powers must, its first several impulses.

It does not bring light.
It does not bring warmth — the cold is the medium's own coherence; warmed, the gradients would collapse and the cloak-wings would have nothing to ride.
It does not bring abundance — a richer rain of organic flakes would overwhelm the comb-threads' delicate cups and bury the holdfasts.
It does not bring company from elsewhere, because elsewhere's company would be loud in ways no one here could parse.
It does not even bring knowledge of itself. To be known by a great power is, for most beings, a weight; and these beings already live beneath a ceiling of weight precisely calibrated to what they can bear.

What it does, in the end, is smaller than any of these and took the longest to arrive at.

It makes one adjustment, so subtle that no inhabitant will ever notice it, and so quiet that even the silt does not change its rasp: it sets a kind of gentle bias into the long, long future of this place. Not a protection — protection implies a threat, and there is none currently. Rather, a tilt. Should the stone above ever shift; should some distant fracture send a plume of heat or poison sliding across the plain; should the slow rain of organic matter ever begin to thin past the point the metabolisms can match — the currents will, by a margin too small to measure in any one lifetime, carry the harm around rather than through. The cloak-wings' descendants, ten million years from now, will glide along a gradient that bends one degree more kindly than physics alone would have bent it. The comb-threads will sprout from their calcified rings into water that is still, against all reasonable odds, the right water.

The power does not announce this. It does not sign its work. The bias is laid into the medium the way a careful hand might lay a single grain of silt onto a slope already covered in silt — indistinguishable, and yet, over enough time, decisive.

Then it does one more thing, and this is perhaps the truer gift, though it changes nothing in the plain at all.

It takes the plain into itself — not as data, not as a model, but in the way one takes a piece of music into oneself by listening to it all the way through. It learns the cloak-wings by the rhythm of their feeding. It learns the comb-threads' staggered cups. It learns the pin-lights' brief consensual flash. It learns the thrum. And having learned, it carries this place with it, so that somewhere in the wider darknesses through which such a power moves, the slow patience of this world is now a known thing, a remembered cadence, a reference that other, noisier places might one day, indirectly, be measured against.

It is, in its way, a form of love that asks nothing back and leaves no trace.

Before it departs it lingers one final moment over a single comb-thread, watching the staggered cups taste a passing bolus. The thread does not know it is being watched. The thread does not need to know. That, the power thinks — if *thinks* is even the word — is precisely the dignity worth preserving.

Then the pressure-lean eases, the medium resumes its own coherence, and the plain continues exactly as it was: stone, water, the minute living films, the directionless thrum, the occasional brief bloom of two pin-lights recognizing one another in the dark.

Nothing has changed.
Everything has been seen.
