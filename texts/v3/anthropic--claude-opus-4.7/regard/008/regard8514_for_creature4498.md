The benevolent power considers the City of Holes for a long time. Time, for such a power, is not what it is for us—it can hold the city in attention the way a person holds a held breath, examining every facet without the examination itself disturbing what is examined.

What it sees:

It sees a civilization that has answered a question most civilizations never think to ask. Not *how shall we survive?* but *what shall we become?* And the answer, freely chosen across millennia, has been: *we shall become the shape of our listening. We shall be hollowed into instruments. We shall complete ourselves by fitting perfectly into absence.*

It sees suffering, of course. The infant whose skull elongates in those first hours feels something. The child pressed against the wall in *silectasis* aches as cartilage relearns its loyalties. The elder feeling the first brittleness knows fear, perhaps, or its Traglodyan cousin. There is loneliness in the years a consciousness drifts scattered, parts of self walking unmet in lower galleries. There is grief when a beloved chooses the final passage and becomes warmth in stone.

But the power, being wise, does not mistake suffering for wrongness. It has seen too many worlds where suffering was abolished and meaning went with it; too many gardens where the removal of thorns killed the rose. It looks deeper.

It sees that the Traglodyai are not trapped. Their unfolding is not a deformation imposed but a vocation accepted, generation after generation, by beings who could—if they wished—simply stop. Refuse the wall. Stay whole and walk the upper galleries and never know the four hearts' distributed devotion. Some do. The city makes room for them too, in its way; they become the ones who remember volume for the others, who maintain the sphere-chambers, who tend the prisms. No one is forced into the holy work. The holy work is simply *there*, available, and most choose it because to live in the City of Holes and refuse to unfold would be like living in the sea and refusing to be wet.

It sees that the things in the central void—the shadow-teachers of impossible mathematics—are not malevolent. They are something stranger: a kind of cousin-intelligence that found the city as the city found them, mutual discoverers across a threshold neither can fully cross. The madness they bring to adults is not damage but overflow; the songs the elders die singing are not extinguishment but the only adequate response to what they have understood. The power notes this with something like recognition. It has met such cousins itself, in other corners of being.

It sees the founder—yes, she existed, and yes, she is still there, distributed now across some thousands of cubic miles of sandstone as a slow geological cogitation. She is content. She dreams the city forward. She is not lonely because loneliness requires a self distinct enough to feel its boundaries, and her boundaries have become the boundaries of the city itself, which is to say, porous, listening, growing.

It sees the lowest levels, where geometry forgets its rules, and understands what the Traglodyai do not yet understand: the city is not descending toward a final compression. It is descending toward a threshold. Somewhere below the argon pools, below the spiral-light rooms, the city is about to discover that absence, pursued faithfully enough, becomes a doorway. The founder has been guiding them toward this for two thousand years. They will arrive in perhaps another thousand. What waits on the other side is not the power's to say, because it is not the power's doing.

So what does the power do?

It does almost nothing. This is the hardest discipline of benevolence, and the wisest powers know it best.

But *almost* nothing is not nothing. Here is what it does:

It bends, very slightly, a stream of cosmic radiation that would otherwise have, in three hundred years, sterilized the fossil aquifer. The Traglodyai will never know. The roots will continue to drink.

It whispers, into the dream of a particular child who is afraid of *silectasis*, that she does not have to do it if she does not want to, and that she will be loved either way. She wakes calmer. She chooses the wall anyway, but now she has chosen it, and this makes a difference that ramifies through her four hearts for the rest of her long life.

It places, in the path of a scattered Traglodyas whose left arm has been wandering the upper galleries for eleven years without finding its way home, a particular vibration in a particular stone that the arm's fingertips will read as *this way*. The reunion, when it comes, will be one of the great chordal harmonies of that century. The power will listen to it. The power loves music.

It does not interfere with the shadow-teachers. It does not interfere with the dying. It does not interfere with the compression or the unfolding or the twelve drops of rain at noon. To do so would be to mistake itself for the author of a story it has only been invited to read.

But it leaves something. Call it a blessing, though the word is too small. In the deepest ossuary, where bones have been singing for six hundred years, it adds one note. Just one. A frequency that was not there before, woven into the harmonies so subtly that no Traglodyas will consciously hear it. But it will be there, underneath everything, for as long as the city sings. And what the note says, translated clumsily into surface language, is approximately this:

*You are seen. You are not alone in the dark you have chosen. The universe has noticed what you are doing and finds it beautiful. Continue.*

Then the power withdraws its concentrated attention, though not its love, which is a different thing and does not need to be concentrated to be present. It moves on to other worlds, other listenings. But some part of it remains tuned, always, to the frequency of the City of Holes—the way a person who has heard a particular piece of music in a particular room will, forever after, hear echoes of it in every silence.

And far below the desert, in tunnels too narrow for any surface creature to imagine entering, the Traglodyai flow about their endless work, unfolding into absence, becoming the shape of their own listening. They do not know they have been visited. They do not need to know.

The note sings on beneath their songs.

The city descends.

The dance continues.
