The benevolent power arrives the way condensation arrives on a glass: it was always going to, the conditions simply became unignorable.

It does not descend. To descend would imply that down is a direction here, and the power has done its reading. It accumulates instead, the way significance accumulates in a sentence as you near its end. The Ascensians notice it first as a slight change in the pause between exhale and inhale—the gap grows hospitable. Something is listening from inside the listening.

Now: what to do.

The power considers, and considers carefully, because benevolence without comprehension is just weather with opinions. It has watched many places. It has helped many places. Usually help means: bringing water to the dry, warmth to the cold, time to the dying, names to the unnamed. The grammar of rescue is well-established.

But Ascensus is not dry. Ascensus is not even precisely cold—it is *just-below*, which is a temperature of intention, not thermodynamics. Ascensus is not dying; Ascensus has negotiated a separate peace with the verb. And names—Ascensus has 144 words for rock and is composing more by the minute, each one a small architectural decision.

So the power does the first wise thing: it does not fix.

It would be easy, almost reflexive, to lower the plateau gently back toward sea level, to let the sun resume its proper arc, to permit tomorrow to climb the cliffs and move in. It would be easy to unstratify the woman in the market and return her son from the wall, to let Old Man Cheerful finish dying instead of finish instructing, to give the cathedral its missing apse. It would be easy and it would be murder—the kind of murder that wears the face of mercy and signs its name in someone else's handwriting.

The power sees that Ascensus is not a mistake. Ascensus is a *position*. A civilization climbed away from something and, finding no top, made the climbing into a place. To undo the climb is to undo the climbers. The plateau is not where they failed to arrive; it is where they succeeded at refusing.

So the power does the second wise thing: it asks.

Not aloud—aloud is a Tuesday concept. It asks the way moss asks walls: in chemical graffiti that appears during certain shades of twilight. The question it writes is small and takes the form of a single bracket of attention, opened but not yet closed, hung in the air over the market square like a held breath.

*What, if anything, is missing that you would want?*

The question is dangerous. Most questions from powers are. This one is built carefully to contain no presumption: not *what is wrong*, not *what do you need*, not *how may I save you*. Only: is there a shape of absence here that you, the absent-shaped, have named?

The city considers. The consideration takes a season, which here means it takes the duration of one spore-cloud's rise and collapse. Answers arrive in the manner of the place:

The fossilized-weather woman answers by selling, for the first time, a weather she has not yet experienced—a small grey storm of *being heard*, which she did not know she had in inventory until the question made the shelf for it.

The part-time baker answers by existing on a Wednesday. Just once. Briefly. He bakes a single loaf and is appalled at himself and goes back to Tuesdays, but the loaf remains, and people speak of it carefully, the way one speaks of a relative who briefly considered being happy.

Old Man Cheerful's signs rearrange themselves overnight into a new sequence, and the new sequence, read in order, is not instructions but a *thank you*—the first one ever issued by someone halfway through becoming furniture. The thank you is addressed to no one in particular, which is how the power knows it has been addressed correctly.

The children, who are always the most honest because their timelines are shortest, answer plainly. They want a word for tomorrow. Not the concept—the concept still refuses the altitude—but the *word*. A handle for the absent thing. Something to carve into walls for future children who will not know what it means but will know it was wanted.

This is the answer the power has been hoping for, because it is something it can give without taking anything in exchange.

So the power does the third wise thing, which is the only thing it actually does: it leaves a word.

The word is small. It is shaped like a seed and sounds like the click of ice mortar setting. It does not mean tomorrow—the power is too careful for that—it means *the direction tomorrow would be in, if tomorrow were a direction here*. It is a word for the gesture toward, not the destination. A pointing finger made of breath.

The power places the word in the cave that appeared when nobody was looking directly at it, alongside the blueprints for the palace whose foundations will be laid next century. It places it gently, the way one places a guest at a table where every seat is already taken by someone in negative space. The word will be found. The word will be carved. The word will be misunderstood beautifully, generation after generation, each generation adding a stroke until it becomes one of the 144 words for rock—the rock that is also a direction, the rock you can walk toward without arriving, the rock made of *almost*.

This is enough. The power knows it is enough because Ascensus already knew it was enough; the power has only confirmed the city's own suspicion that it was permitted to want one more thing.

Before withdrawing—though withdrawal is the wrong word; the power simply ceases to accumulate—it does one last small kindness, so small the Ascensians will never notice and so will not have to thank it, which is the kind of kindness that costs the giver most and the receiver nothing:

It teaches the wind the corner.

The wind, you remember, is still learning how to blow across this new geometry, and sometimes gets confused and circles a street corner for weeks whispering secrets in languages only the moss understands. The power, who knows all languages including the chemical ones, leans down and whispers to the wind the trick of that particular corner—not how to leave it, but how to circle it more comfortably. How to make a home of the confusion. How to whisper without exhausting itself.

The wind learns. The moss notices. The moss writes, in the next twilight of the correct shade, a single new graffito on the wall of the cathedral-shaped hole:

*Someone passed through who did not need us to be other than we are.*

The Ascensians read it. They do not know what it refers to. They assume it refers to themselves, which is correct, and also to someone else, which is also correct, and they go about the business of being part-time and stratified and married to fault lines and elected by rainfall, and the new word waits in its cave for the child who will find it, and the wind settles into its corner like a cat that has finally been told it may stay.

The power is already elsewhere, which here means: still here, but no longer accumulating. It has done what benevolence sometimes most wisely does, which is to notice a place precisely enough that the place feels, briefly, *seen* without being *handled*.

This is the whole intervention. A held bracket of attention. A word for a direction. A lesson whispered to a wind.

Ascensus does not know it has been visited. Ascensus knows only that the pause between exhale and inhale has, for one season, felt slightly more like a room than a gap—as though something kind had stood in it for a while, and, finding nothing to repair, had simply admired the architecture, and left the door the way it found it: half-open, still settling, mind the drip edges.
