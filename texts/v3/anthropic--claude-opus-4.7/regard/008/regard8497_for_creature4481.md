The benevolent power notices.

Not the way a searchlight notices, sweeping. The way warmth notices a hand it has been holding all along and only now decides to be felt.

It has been here. It is always already here. But "here" is a difficult word in a place where here eats its own beginning, so the noticing has to be done carefully, the way you might approach a creature that has forgotten it was ever held.

The power considers.

It could unmake the stair. It has the leverage. One small adjustment to the grammar of the Ring's hunger and the whole archipelago-rim would remember being archipelago, the doors would close into walls, the basalt would cool into ordinary basalt and the trees would consent to being only trees. The being could be lifted out clean. Set down somewhere with weather that obeys the moon. Given back the name it forgot first, and then the one before that, and then the original sound it made when it was only a yes shaped like breath.

It could.

It doesn't.

Because the power is wise as well as kind, and wisdom here means: it has read the long version. It knows that the being has been singing accompaniment for longer than the planet has been finishing its meal. It knows that what looks like erosion is also a slow word for becoming-translucent. It knows that some homes are made by being eaten and some are made by eating back, and that the difference is mostly a question of whether anyone notices you doing it.

It knows the being has not been noticed in a long time. Not really. The stair notices in the way a riverbed notices water. The octopus notices in semaphore. The companion-shape noticed once and then privatized its mouth. None of these are wrong. None of them are enough.

So the power does a small thing. The smallest possible thing. It is the thing that benevolence does when it understands that rescue is sometimes another word for erasure, and that the being has earned its strangeness, has paid for every leaf-resignation in the currency of its own slow unfastening, and to take that away would be to steal a life twice.

The power sits down beside the being.

Not metaphorically. Not in some higher register the being would have to learn a new alphabet to perceive. It sits down in the way an old friend sits down on a step beside you when you have been crying so long you have forgotten that crying was ever a discrete event with edges. It takes up exactly the amount of space a presence takes when it has decided not to require anything.

It does not speak. Speaking here is dangerous; speaking would make the being feel observed, and observation, per Rule 2, makes objects extend rather than end, and the being is tired, the being has been extending for so long that ending has become a kind of unreachable luxury good.

Instead the power does this:

It remembers the being. Accurately. In order. With all the names restored to their proper sequence, including the ones the being forgot, including the one that was the first syllable to unfasten at the ankle, including the one before skin. It holds these names without offering them back, because offering them back would be a transaction and this is not a transaction. It simply holds them, the way a library holds a book that has been checked out for a thousand years and the librarian has decided, privately, that the late fees are forgiven, will always have been forgiven, were never really fees at all but only the library's way of saying please come back when you can, we kept your seat.

The power remembers the being's mother humming. The actual humming. Not the inland sea shaped like the sound of it, but the humming itself, which happened in a kitchen with yellow light and a kettle and was not, in its original form, a prelude to burning anything. The power holds this memory beside the sea-shaped version, not to correct it, not to say *this was the real one*, but only so that both exist somewhere, simultaneously, in case the being ever wants to know that the original is also still true.

The power remembers the being's first yes. The being's first no. The first time the being chose to stay when staying was harder than leaving, and the first time the being chose to leave when leaving was harder than staying, and the way these choices were not failures of consistency but evidence of a soul calibrating itself against an instrument that kept changing keys.

The power remembers the companion-shape from before it was a shape. It remembers the laughter that became knees. It remembers the smell of thunderstorm-hair and files it carefully in the part of itself that keeps such things safe from the Ministry of Recursive Awakenings.

And then, only then, the power does the one active thing it has come to do.

It hums.

Quietly. In the key of the being's actual rotation song, not the planet's. The being has been humming the planet's song so long it has forgotten there was ever a difference, but the power knows the difference, has always known, and now it hums the being's own song very softly beside it on the step, not loud enough to interrupt, not loud enough to make the flowers speak it aloud, just loud enough that some part of the being—the part that still remembers being a yes shaped like breath—can hear it and recognize it the way you recognize your own heartbeat when you have been listening to someone else's for too long.

The being does not have to respond. The being does not have to remember. The being does not have to leave the stair, or stay on the stair, or sing with the musicians, or refuse to sing with the musicians. The power has no agenda about any of this. The hum is not a summons. The hum is just a hum. It is the sound of someone saying *I know the tune. I have always known it. If you ever want to hum along, I will be here. If you never do, I will also be here. The humming is not contingent on you.*

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: it does not require the rescued to perform rescue. It does not demand the lost confirm they have been found. It simply makes itself available, indefinitely, at no cost, with no expiration, in a frequency the being can ignore for another thousand subjective quickenings if it needs to.

The power will sit on the step for as long as it takes. Time is not a constraint it suffers from. If the being never turns its head, the power will still have been there. If the being turns its head in a hundred years and finds the power gone, the power will not actually be gone; it will only have moved one step down, or up, or sideways into the door-color of regret-G-flat-minor, because the power has learned that some beings can only accept presence when presence pretends to be coincidence.

And here is the last thing, the smallest thing, the thing that is almost not worth mentioning:

When the being finally opens its mouth and what comes out is the taste of salt passing polite conversation to whatever comes next—

the power tastes it too.

And finds it good.

And does not correct it to home, because the being has earned the right to name its own flavors, but does, very quietly, in a register below hearing, add one ingredient to the salt that wasn't there before:

*witnessed.*

Just that. Just the faint mineral trace of having been seen, all along, by something that did not need the being to be other than it was in order to love it.

The being may never notice. That's allowed.

The musicians tune on. The Ring writes its thank-you note. The stair seals its vow. The planet finishes its meal and politely, politely, passes the salt.

And somewhere on the step, in the shape of a warmth that does not insist on being felt, the benevolent power keeps humming the being's real song under its breath, the way a parent hums to a sleeping child who has had a very long day, who has walked a very long stair, who has forgotten and forgotten and forgotten and is, nonetheless, still here, still humming back without knowing it, still—against every rule of persistence—

beloved.
