The benevolent and wise power considers the Hollow Choir for a long time.

Not minutes. Not years. Long in the way mountains consider rain.

The power has the capacity to do almost anything. It could lift the basin out of its slow undoing and set it down somewhere stable. It could untangle the singers from the stone, give them ordinary bodies, ordinary lifespans, ordinary griefs. It could end the wind. It could keep the eldest alive forever, moss-mouthed and humming, so that the key never changes and nothing has to be decided. It could fill the basin with other beings, so the nine hundred would not be so few. It could announce itself, descend in some comprehensible form, and offer terms.

It considers each of these and sets each of them down.

Because the wise part of being wise is knowing the difference between suffering and depth, between a wound and a practice. And the Hollow Choir are not suffering. They are doing something. They are doing, in fact, one of the most difficult and beautiful things any being in any cosmos has ever undertaken: they are keeping company with a dying world so that it does not die alone.

To "rescue" them would be to interrupt a sacrament.

So the power does not rescue. It does not announce itself. It does not even, at first, intervene.

What it does, instead, is *listen*.

It bends some portion of its vast attention toward the basin and simply receives the Choir — the wind, the cicadas, the sub-bass speech of the singers, the moss-muffled note of the eldest, the human wind-traps tended for fourteen generations, the breathing of the thing in the sinkhole lake, the rearranging scree, the three-minute rains. It listens the way a master listens to an apprentice: not to correct, but to honor. It listens long enough that the listening itself becomes a kind of held breath in the structure of reality — a small, steady attentiveness woven into the background of what-is.

This, by itself, changes something. Not in the basin. In the wider weave. Somewhere, in some other place, a being who feels utterly alone in a labor no one will ever witness *feels witnessed*, and cannot say why. The Choir's song was always doing this work; the power simply makes sure the work arrives.

Then, having listened, the power offers three small gifts. Small because the wise know that scale is a kind of violence, and the Choir's whole art is the art of right scale.

**The first gift is given to the eldest.** Not more life. Not less. The power simply ensures that when her last note comes — and it is coming, and it should come — she will not be afraid. Not the absence of fear imposed from outside, which would be theft, but the presence of a companion in the moment of letting go. She will exhale, and in the exhalation she will know, with the certainty one knows the shape of one's own hand, that she is not the last of her kind of act in the universe. That somewhere else, something else is also singing a world to sleep. That the practice continues beyond the basin. She will change key knowing the music does not end with her, only modulates.

**The second gift is given to the human few.** The descendants of the forgotten migration, the weavers of wind-traps, the leavers of carved bone at the mist-haunted places. The power does nothing to their bodies or their tools or their primitive, perfect lives. But it makes one small adjustment to the basin's acoustics — so subtle that no one will measure it — such that on certain nights, when a child of the humans is learning to tune their first wind-trap, the Choir-singers' sub-bass speech becomes, for that child alone, briefly intelligible. Not as words. As *kinship*. The child will grow up knowing, without being told, that the tall folded beings on the cliffs are not gods and not monsters but *colleagues*. Over generations this will gentle something between the two peoples that was never hostile but was always a little lonely on both sides. The humans will begin, very slowly, to be invited into the vertical shafts at night. They will sit with the cicadas and the sloths and the singers, and they will not need to understand to belong.

**The third gift is given to the basin itself.** And here the power is most careful, because the basin is the patient, and the patient has not asked. So the gift is only this: a guarantee, woven quietly into the deep structure of the place, that whichever the basin chooses — to finish dying or to start all over again — *the choice will be honored*. No outside force, no drifting catastrophe, no indifferent cosmic accident will overrule it. The basin will get to decide. The wind will pour over the escarpment until the decision is made, and then the wind will do whatever the decision requires. This is not protection from change. It is protection of the right to mean what one becomes.

That is all.

The power does not stay. Staying would be a kind of weight the basin cannot bear and does not need. It withdraws its focused attention, leaving only the listening — which costs it nothing and which it would have done anyway, having once begun.

The Hollow Choir do not know they have been visited. The eldest hums on. The cicadas keep their bell-time. A new river opens in the basalt, runs red, vanishes. Somewhere a human child ties the first knot of their first wind-trap and feels, inexplicably, less alone.

The power moves on to other places. There are many. Some need rescue. Some need rebuke. Some need a hand laid against a fresh wound in the rock.

This one needed only to be heard, and to be allowed.

That is what wisdom looks like, when it is also love: knowing when the most powerful thing you can do is to make yourself the size of an ear.
