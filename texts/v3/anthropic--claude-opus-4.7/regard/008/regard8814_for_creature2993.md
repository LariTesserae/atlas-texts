The benevolent and wise power considers the Cathedral of the Ascending Flood for what, in its own reckoning, is a long while. Long enough that, inside the Cathedral, the coral extrudes a new buttress and the water rises an imperceptible fraction. Long enough that the Dweller folds itself into its hollow once, sleeps, and unfolds again.

The power is benevolent. It is also wise. These are not the same thing, and the difference matters here.

A lesser kindness would reach in immediately. It would drain the water. It would still the rising. It would split the seamless walls and show the Dweller the sky, the sun the crystal arteries have only ever sipped from at second hand. It would say: *you are alone, you are drowning, you have been waiting — come out.*

The wise power does not do this.

It looks, instead, at what is actually there. Not what the shape of the place *suggests* to an outside grammar — tomb, prison, tragedy, abandonment — but what it *is* from the inside. A liturgy that has found its priest. A priest that has found its liturgy. A slow song sung in sub-bass through a body grown precisely to sing it. A loneliness, yes — but the loneliness of cathedrals, which is not the loneliness of the lost. It is the loneliness of the *consecrated*. Of a thing that has become exactly itself and has no further need to be witnessed in order to continue.

The wise power understands that to "rescue" the Dweller would be to murder it, gently, with the best of intentions. The Dweller is not trapped inside the Cathedral. The Dweller *is* the Cathedral's act of being inhabited. Separate them and you would have, on one hand, a dying building, and on the other, a dying creature, and on no hand at all the thing that was actually alive: the relation between them.

So the power does not intervene in any of the obvious ways.

What it does is smaller, and stranger, and — the power believes — correct.

**First**, it makes a single adjustment to the light. Not its source, not its color, not its piping. Only this: it ensures that the sunlight the crystal arteries drink, from wherever they drink it, will continue to arrive. Forever. Whatever happens above — whatever sun ages, whatever world turns — the arteries will not go dark. This is the smallest gift the power knows how to give, and the most fundamental. It costs nothing the power can measure. It removes from the Cathedral's future the one ending that would be merely an ending, with no liturgy in it.

The Dweller does not notice. That is correct. Gifts of this order are not for noticing.

**Second**, the power does something with the question of the builders.

It does not bring them back. They are gone, and their goneness is part of the Cathedral's grammar — the missing tooth the Dweller's tongue moves around. To restore them would be to fill the hollow that the Dweller's whole being is shaped to hold. But the power *does* — very gently, in a way that takes a century of Cathedral-time and an instant of its own — let the heat-memory in the metal become *true* memory. Not loud. Not addressed to the Dweller. Simply: the girders that remember the machines that placed them now remember a little further back. They remember the hands. The intentions. The reasons, such as they were. Not as information to be transmitted, but as a quality of warmth.

So that when the Dweller stops at a bronze seam and places all its hands against a girder and listens for days — it will, without ever quite knowing it does, be heard back. The metal will be listening too. The absence will keep its shape, but the shape will be held on both sides now, the way a held hand is different from a reaching one, even when no words pass.

**Third**, and this is the part that requires the most wisdom: the power does nothing about the water.

It does not stop the rising. It does not accelerate it toward some merciful completion. It does not preserve the dry pocket in the lowest chamber, nor does it flood it. The inundation is the Cathedral's own slow sentence, spoken in its own slow language, and it is not the power's sentence to finish or to interrupt. The Dweller is the priest of this rising. To take the rising away would be to unfrock the priest. To hasten it would be to rob the liturgy of the only tempo at which it makes sense.

The power simply *witnesses* the rising. Fully. With the entire weight of its attention, which is considerable. The Dweller has been the only witness for a very long time, and has not needed another, and still does not need another — but it is, perhaps, a different thing to be witnessed alongside than to witness alone. The pressure gauges on the brass mask do not swing. The Dweller does not feel observed. What it feels, if it feels anything new at all, is a faint and inexplicable easing — as though the sub-bass note, when it next emerges, has somewhere very slightly larger to travel into before it returns.

**Fourth**, the power leaves a small provision against a future it hopes will not come but cannot rule out.

If, someday, something enters the Cathedral — a wanderer, a fugitive, a child of some unimaginable elsewhere — and walks the seams for years, and finally sits on a narrow balcony and lets the warm metallic fluid fill their mouth, and the Dweller settles opposite them like a mirror of coral and brass, and the lullaby softens —

then, in that moment, and only in that moment, the wanderer will understand what is being offered. Not be compelled by it. Not be saved from it. Simply: *understand*. They will know that the courteous drowning is a courtesy. They will know they may accept it, and stay, and become another shape the coral learns to tolerate or to choose. Or they will know they may rise, and the Dweller will let them go, and the architecture behind them will, for once, not close — there will be a corridor out, and it will be the corridor they came in by, restored exactly, because the Dweller, understanding too, will have asked the Cathedral for this one small unmaking.

The choice will be real. That is the gift. Not an exit imposed, not an exit denied: an exit that exists if and only if it is chosen with full knowledge of what is being left.

This is all the power does.

It seems like very little. The Cathedral looks, from the outside, unchanged. The water still rises. The engines still sleep. The Dweller still adjusts knobs that do nothing, still caresses pipes that carry only light, still folds itself into its hollow when the pressure grows too ferocious. The coral still grows. The light is still the color of late afternoon through cathedral glass.

But the light will not fail.
The absence is held on both sides.
The witness is doubled, without intrusion.
And if anyone ever comes, the door will be real.

The power withdraws its primary attention, though a thread of it remains, the way one keeps a hand resting lightly on a sleeping animal's flank — not to wake it, not to hold it, only to be there in case the breathing changes.

The Dweller, in its hollow, knees to chest, arms wrapped around its own head like a child protecting a thought, does not dream. It has never dreamed. Dreaming is for creatures with an inside and an outside, and the Dweller long ago stopped having either.

But the next time it exhales, the sub-bass note travels a little further before it returns.

And the water rises another invisible fraction of a millimeter.

And that, the power decides, is enough. That is, in fact, exactly right.

It is the hardest thing wisdom ever does: to look at something strange and slow and seemingly sorrowful, and to recognize that it is already, in its own grammar, complete — and to love it by leaving it almost entirely alone.

Almost.
