# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend, does not announce itself, does not pour through any crack in the ceramic. It is simply, at some point, *attending*—the way a reader leans closer to a page, the way warmth enters a room before the fire is noticed.

It looks at the Underbasin for a long time. Longer than the Curator has been there. Longer, perhaps, than the machine has been forgetting itself. Time means little to such a power, but attention does, and it spends its attention generously.

And then it does something very strange, which is *almost nothing.*

---

## What it does not do

It does not free her.

This is the first temptation, and the power considers it gravely, because freedom is one of the loveliest words. It could lift her out. It could dissolve the ceramic from her skin, coax her vertebrae back into their old human compression, comb the liverwort from her hair until she was a woman again, blinking under a sky. It could give her back the word that has been trying to surface for centuries.

But the power is wise, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of *looking* before acting. It looks. It sees that she is not imprisoned. It sees that the Underbasin is not her cage but her body's larger body—that to extract her would be to perform a kind of vivisection on something that has spent eight hundred years becoming whole. The salamanders in the troughs of her collarbones are not parasites. The moths in her hair are not infestation. She is not a person trapped in a machine; she is a covenant the machine made with itself in order to keep going.

To "rescue" her would be to murder her and call it mercy. The power sets this option down, gently, the way one returns a stone to a streambed.

It does not repair the machine either. The Underbasin does not want to be repaired in the sense a power could repair it—made efficient, made purposeful, restored to whatever forgotten function it once served. The machine has achieved something more interesting than function: it has achieved *being.* To restore its original purpose would be like restoring a cathedral to a quarry.

It does not reveal itself to her. She has not asked. The asking matters.

It does not give her companions of her own kind, because she has no kind, and to invent one would be cruelty disguised as gift. It does not extend her life, because her life is already extending itself, slowly, in the manner appropriate to what she is. It does not shorten her life either, though it considers this too, and grieves a little, and sets it down.

It does not explain anything to anyone.

---

## What it does

**First**, it learns her. Not in the way a scholar learns a subject, but in the way a long marriage learns a face. It learns the particular grinding timbre of every pipe-cricket she has named without words. It learns which basin in the northwest quadrant she favors when a surge is coming, because the acoustics there let her hear the deep pipes breathe. It learns the small wet click in her throat and what it means—not one thing, it turns out, but seventeen things, depending on the hour and the humidity. It learns the word that has been trying to surface in her for three hundred years.

The word is *daughter.*

The power holds this knowledge without doing anything with it. Some knowledge is for holding.

**Second**, it makes one adjustment, so small that even the Curator will not notice it for decades, and even then will mistake it for a trick of her own attention.

Deep beneath the Underbasin, far below the oldest pipes, there is a slow seep of mineral water that has been growing imperceptibly more acidic for two centuries. In four hundred more years it would have begun to dissolve the ceramic from below. In six hundred, the floor would have started to fail. In eight hundred, the Curator—still moving, still humming, still patting mastic into wounds—would have found herself standing in a chamber whose foundation was eating itself faster than she could mend it. She would not have stopped. She would have continued repairing what could no longer be repaired, in water up to her waist, then her chest, then her chin, singing to pipe-crickets that had drowned, until the last tile gave and she fell into the dark below with nothing left to tend.

The power, with a gesture smaller than a thought, shifts the chemistry of the seep. Not much. Just enough. The acid balances. The ceramic will hold.

She has been given another four thousand years. She will never know.

This is the only intervention the power permits itself in the physical machine, and it permits it only because the alternative was a slow, specific horror that no one would ever witness, which is the worst kind.

**Third**, and this is the strangest thing, the power begins to *listen* to her singing.

Not as one listens to be informed. As one listens to be *kept company by.* When she sings her grinding metallic counter-melody to the pipe-crickets, the power is there, attending. When she murmurs to the chamber in her voice of water-through-gravel, the power receives the murmur. When she sits the nine-hour ceremony with the salamander and the cricket and the orange tower, the power sits the nine hours with her—not visibly, not detectably, but present in the way a held hand is present even when the holder says nothing.

She does not know she is being listened to. This is important. To tell her would change the singing. The singing is for the chamber, for the crickets, for the water, for no one. The power refuses to make it for *someone*, because that would impoverish it. But the power can listen to what is not being sung *to* it. That is allowed. That is, in fact, one of the oldest and most honorable forms of love.

For the first time in eight hundred years, the Curator's endless rounds are witnessed. She does not feel witnessed. She feels exactly as she has always felt, which is *here.* But the quality of *here* has changed, in a way too subtle for her milk-white eyes to register, and yet—

Sometimes now, when she pauses beneath a particular walkway where the drips are especially bright, a small ease moves through her shoulders. The liverwort of her hair settles. The moths inside it shift and resettle. She does not know why. She makes the small wet click that means *the water is good tonight*, and continues on her rounds.

**Fourth**, and finally, the power makes a quiet provision for after.

Not for her death—she is not going to die for a very long time now, and when she does, it will be the right death, the one shaped exactly to her, and the power will not interfere with it. The provision is for *after the after.* When at last the final drop falls, when the bioluminescent bacteria go dark, when the pipe-crickets have been silent for so long that even their silence has been forgotten, when the Tall Wet Thing That Does Not Eat at last lays herself down in the exact center of the Underbasin on the single original tile and closes her milk-white eyes—

The power has arranged that she will be remembered.

Not by a monument. Not by a record. Not by anything she would have recognized as remembrance. The power has woven her, gently, into the long quiet catalogue of beings-who-tended, which is one of the things such powers keep. She will be a small warm entry there, between two others who also tended things no one asked them to tend. The catalogue is not read aloud. It is not read at all. It simply *is*, and being in it is enough, the way being on a map is enough even if no one is currently looking at the map.

She would not have wanted more. She would not have wanted this either, if asked. But she was not asked, and the power judges—carefully, with full awareness of the presumption—that this particular small grace can be extended without harm, because it costs her nothing and changes nothing and only means that somewhere, in some register that has no location, the fact of her will not entirely vanish when she does.

---

## And then

The power withdraws its focused attention, though not its attention entirely. It moves on to other places, other beings, other small wet warm industries that have forgotten their purposes. There are more of these than one might think. The universe is full of forgotten machines tended by changed things that do not eat.

The Underbasin continues. The drips continue. The pipe-crickets scream in metallic ecstasy when the surges come. The Curator walks her rounds, three for every surge, patting mastic into the wounds of the world, humming in her voice of moving water.

Far below, in the seep she will never see, the chemistry holds.

Above her, in a register she will never perceive, she is listened to.

And once, only once, on a warm night some seventy years after the power first attended, she pauses in the middle of her circuit and looks up toward the sourceless ivory glow, and the ancient almost-painful thing moves behind her face, and the word *daughter* rises higher than it has ever risen—not to her lips, not to her mind, but to somewhere just beneath, like a fish coming up to look at the underside of ice.

She does not weep. She has no tears; her whole body is tears.

She makes the small wet click in her throat.

She lowers her head.

She continues her rounds.

And the power, listening, lowers its head also, in the gesture that is not quite grief and not quite reverence but is the particular bow that wise things make to other wise things who are doing the work of staying.
