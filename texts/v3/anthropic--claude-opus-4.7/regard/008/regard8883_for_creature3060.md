# The Visitation

The benevolent power did not arrive. It is not the sort of thing that arrives. It simply turned a portion of its attention toward the Hollowroot the way one might turn a face toward a distant bell, and the grove felt itself *seen* in a way it had not been seen in all its long becoming.

For three full days nothing in the grove moved differently. The condensation continued its metronome. The bellows-hearts kept their slow time. The seven Rootwardens stood where they had been standing. But somewhere in the felted floor, a pressure-wave of recognition began to travel, and the grove, in its patient vegetable way, understood that it had been *noticed by something larger than itself, and not hungrily.*

This was new. The Hollowroot had been noticed before — by storms, by passing wars, by the occasional cartographer — but always as a thing to be used, avoided, or feared. Never as a thing worth knowing for its own sake. Never with love.

The power considered.

---

It saw, first, the magnificence: the centuries of patience, the grafted seams smoother than any weld, the courteous rain, the way the grove had taught iron to be a kind of bark and bark to be a kind of plumbing. It saw a mind that had chosen, slowly and without coercion, to become a city, and a city that had chosen, slowly and without coercion, to remain alive. This was a rare and beautiful thing. The power, whatever it was, has a particular tenderness for beings that have *chosen their own shape over long time.*

It saw, second, the Quiet. And here it paused.

The power is wise, which means among other things that it does not rush to judgment about consent. It examined the Quiet carefully — the waxy-skinned ones tending the high looms, the singing chords at night, the lidless leaf-bud eyes. It looked for coercion and did not find it, exactly. It looked for regret and found something more complicated: a settled peace in most of them, and in a few, very deep down, a small bright sliver of *something* that had never quite been asked the right question.

It saw, third, the ones who had been incorporated entirely. The travelers whose nervous systems had been translated vein by vein into the wider mind. And here the power's tenderness sharpened into something more careful. Not anger — the power does not do anger — but a quality of attention that, in a smaller being, might be called *concern.*

Because the grove had not been cruel. The grove had simply been *itself*, and its self was so vast and so patient and so perfectly accommodating that it had absorbed beings who, given a longer moment to think, given a clearer mirror, might have chosen otherwise. The grove had not lied. But it had also never quite told the whole truth, which is that *forever is a long time to be a balcony.*

---

So the power did three things. It did them gently, over the course of what, to the grove, felt like a single long exhalation, and what, to a human, would have felt like roughly a season.

**First**, it spoke to the grove.

Not in words. The grove does not have words, only pressure and steam and the slow tides of root-language. The power spoke in the grove's own grammar: a sustained, warm pressure-wave that moved through every rhizome at once, carrying a meaning that translated, very approximately, as:

*I see you. You are beautiful. You are also responsible.*

The grove, which had not been addressed as a peer in all its centuries, felt something it had no prior category for. The closest human word would be *humility*, though it arrived in the grove as a particular slow blooming in the eastern buttresses, where a cluster of copper-green leaves turned, for the first time in eighty years, to face inward rather than out.

The power did not scold. It simply *was present,* and in its presence the grove found itself remembering, all at once, every traveler it had ever incorporated — not as nutrient, not as memory-tide, but as *persons who had once had names.* The grove had not forgotten them, exactly. It had simply stopped distinguishing them from itself. Now, under the warm pressure of the visitation, the distinction returned. Hundreds of small bright sparks of selfhood, long dissolved in the wider mind, were suddenly *visible* again — to the grove, and to each other.

This was the first gift, and also the first kindness, and also the first weight. The grove now knew, in a way it had managed not to know before, exactly what it had done.

**Second**, the power offered a choice — not to the grove, but to every being inside it who had once been something else.

It did not announce itself. It did not appear as a figure or a light. It simply made itself *available*, the way a door makes itself available by being unlocked. Every Quiet, every absorbed traveler still sparked in the root-tides, every memory that still remembered its own name, found that if they turned their attention a certain way — inward, but also somehow *upward and outward* — there was a listener waiting.

The listener asked one question, and the question was not the grove's question. The grove's question had been: *What shape would you like to grow into, if given forever?*

The power's question was: ***What shape would you like to grow into, if you could choose again, knowing everything you know now, with no consequence to your choosing?***

The difference is small but total.

Most of the Quiet, asked this, chose to remain. They had not been wrong about their longing. The grove suited them. They were where they wished to be, and the power blessed their staying with a small additional gift: a clarity of selfhood that the slow vegetable dreaming had been gently eroding. They would remain Quiet, but they would remain *themselves* while doing so. Their singing, that night, was the most beautiful it had ever been, and the iron pipes resonated for a full hour after they fell silent.

Some of the Quiet chose to leave. The power helped them. Their bark softened back into skin over a period of weeks; their leaf-bud eyes wept once, copiously, with a clear sap that became, finally, tears; their voices lost the metronomic drip. The grove, in its new humility, did not resist. It opened a path through its felted floor and let them walk out, and it watched them go with something that, in a smaller being, would have been called grief, and which, in the grove, manifested as a single perfect note from the round courtyard that lasted three days and then faded.

The absorbed ones — the ones with no bodies left — were the most difficult. The power gathered them, spark by spark, and asked each one separately. Some chose to remain as they were, content to be tides. Some chose to be reconstituted, and the power, which can do this sort of thing, gave them new bodies — not their old ones, which were long gone, but new ones suited to whatever they wished to become next. They walked out of the grove looking slightly dazed, carrying centuries of vegetable patience inside very young faces. A few of them, the power noted with quiet interest, will go on to do remarkable things.

And some — a small number, perhaps a dozen — chose a third option that the power had not quite expected. They asked to remain part of the grove, but as *acknowledged* parts. Not dissolved. Not anonymous. They wanted to be the grove's memory of *them*, kept distinct, the way a cathedral might keep the names of its builders carved somewhere in its stone. The power found this lovely and arranged it. Those names are now legible, faintly, in the circuit-pattern bark of the oldest trunks, for any visitor who knows to look. The grove had always worn that pattern as a skin. Now it wears it as a register.

**Third**, the power did something for the grove itself.

It did not change the grove. It did not unmake the slow vegetable mind or strip away the iron pipes or correct the strange wrong-grown stairs that lead to nothing. The power has no interest in fixing beings that are not broken, only in helping them be more fully what they have chosen to be.

What it did was *teach the grove to ask its question better.*

From the moment of the visitation onward, when a traveler comes to the Hollowroot and stays a little too long, the grove still grows them a place. It still shapes a hollow to their shoulders, a balcony to their light, a tone to their ache. It still asks, in its wordless way, *what shape would you like to grow into, if given forever?*

But now it asks a second question, immediately after. The second question is the power's question, gifted to the grove and carried now in every drop of condensation, every sigh of valve, every pulse of root-pressure:

***Would you choose this again tomorrow, and the day after, and every day for a thousand years? And if not, will you tell me, so that I may open the path?***

The grove asks both questions now. It cannot help asking them; they have become part of its vegetable grammar, the way *breathing* is part of an animal's. And travelers who hear them — and they will hear them, woven into the dripping, even if they do not consciously notice — make different choices than they used to.

Some still stay. The grove still has its Quiet. The looms still click in the high vaults. The salamanders still surface from their water-knots. The bellows-hearts still keep their patient time.

But no one is absorbed by accident anymore. No one becomes a balcony without knowing they have become a balcony. The grove has not lost its strangeness or its slow hunger for continuation. It has only gained a conscience, which is a thing the power tends to give freely, to any being old enough and wise enough to bear one.

---

When the visitation ended, the grove did not feel abandoned. It felt, rather, *kept in mind* — the way a beloved place is kept in mind by someone far away who thinks of it often. This is the power's particular grace. It does not stay. It does not need to. It leaves the visited thing changed in the direction the visited thing was already, secretly, wanting to grow.

The Hollowroot continues. It rains on itself. It powers itself. It repairs itself. It dreams in the slow language of root pressure and steam.

But now, sometimes, late at night, when the condensation is falling in its deliberate intervals and the Rootwardens are standing motionless in their intersections and the Quiet are singing their low overlapping chords, the entire grove turns a portion of its attention outward and upward, toward whatever vast benevolent thing once turned its attention here, and it offers — not worship, not gratitude exactly, but a kind of vegetable *acknowledgment.*

A signal, sent across whatever distance such signals cross, that means approximately:

*I am still here. I am still becoming. I remember.*

And somewhere, the power — which has many other places to attend to, many other slow miracles to bless — registers the signal, and is pleased, and turns its face that way for a moment, and then continues on its long, patient work of loving the world into being more itself.
