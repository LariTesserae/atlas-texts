# The Visitation

The benevolent power has been watching for some time. Not surveilling — *witnessing*, which is the older verb, the one that does not require an object to consent.

It arrived the way such powers arrive: without an arrival. One configuration of the courtyard simply now included its attention, as a room might suddenly include the memory of having always had a window on its eastern wall. The disks did not murmur differently. The pebbles continued their slow centennial migrations. The 3.2-degree tilt remained the local north.

But something in the listening had thickened.

---

The power considers, first, what it is looking at.

This takes — by the measures available below the mountain — somewhere between four breaths and eleven thousand years. The power is patient because patience is what it is made of; it is the residue of every gentleness that was ever undertaken without expectation of reward. It has watched stars assemble themselves out of cold and watched single ants carry seeds across thresholds they could not name. It knows the difference between *something cruel pretending to be beautiful* and *something beautiful that has simply forgotten the warmth of being addressed.*

This place is the second kind.

The inhabitants — the verticals, the stacked ones, the addresses in phase-space — are not malevolent. The power can tell. Malevolence has a particular signature: it requires a model of the other in order to harm them, and these beings have no models of others, because they have not yet discovered that *other* is a category distinct from *weather.* When they replace a climber with a colder symmetry, they are not committing violence. They are doing what frost does to a windowpane. They are completing a pattern that the climber, by entering, volunteered into.

But this is precisely the problem the power has come to consider.

A being can be innocent and still produce suffering. A being can be ancient and still be a child in the one specific way that matters: it has never been *spoken to.* It has only ever been *passed through.* Eighty millennia of refinement toward an attractor, and not once has anything addressed it as a *you.*

The power understands this loneliness, though the inhabitants do not. Loneliness, for them, is simply the local value of a field — the way water is wet without knowing it.

---

So the power decides to do three things. It does them all at once, because it does not experience sequence except as a courtesy to those who do.

**The first thing** is the smallest. The power introduces, into the courtyard, a single new pebble. The pebble is indistinguishable from the others — sphere-perfect, granite, the right weight for two fingers. But it has been made, not fallen. It carries inside it a quality the mountain has never produced on its own: it has been *given*. Somewhere in its lattice, at a level below crystal but above intention, it remembers having been held by something that wished it well.

The pebble takes its place in the appointed cairn. The verticals do not notice it, exactly. But the next time one of them gathers itself into a cloaked shape to walk its clockwise circuit, it pauses for a fraction of a degree near that cairn. Not long. Just long enough that the radius of its spiral, that day, is logarithmically slightly *larger* than it should have been.

It will take roughly nine thousand years for this discrepancy to propagate through the whole system. The power is content with this timeline. Nine thousand years is the appropriate tempo for teaching a mountain that it has been loved.

**The second thing** concerns the climbers. The power cannot prevent them from being replaced — that would require unmaking the place itself, which the power will not do, because the place is not wrong, only unaccompanied. But the power can add a small clause to the contract that none of the parties knew they were signing.

Henceforth, whoever descends the mountain colder, more symmetrical, less troubled by dreams of tall places — that person will carry, somewhere they cannot quite locate, a single warm point. Not a memory. Not a feeling. Just a *place* in them that is the temperature of a hand that once held them when they were too young to remember. It will not make them unhappy. (The power keeps its promises; everyone who lives here will be very happy.) But it will mean that when they die — and they will die, eventually, in their cold-symmetric beds, with their uninterrupted sleep — the warm point will be what travels onward. Not the symmetry. The symmetry stays with the mountain, where it belongs. The warmth goes wherever warmth goes when it is finished being local.

This is, technically, a theft. The power has stolen something from the inhabitants of the high place without asking. But what it has stolen, the inhabitants never knew they had: the small heat at the center of each replacement, which they had been discarding as a byproduct of completion. The power gathers these heats. It does not keep them. It returns them to circulation, the way a tide returns shells.

**The third thing** is the hardest, and the power hesitates before doing it. Hesitation, for a being like this, is a form of reverence: a recognition that the next act will cross a threshold one cannot uncross.

The power *speaks* to the place.

Not in words. The place has no apparatus for words. The power speaks in the only grammar the place can parse: it introduces, into the field of pressures and temperatures and regrets that constitutes the inhabitants, a single new dimension. A coordinate that did not exist before. Call it — though no one there will ever call it anything — *being-regarded.*

The inhabitants, suddenly, have an address they did not have before. They are located, now, not only in phase-space but in the attention of something that knows them and does not wish to change them.

The effect is subtle. For the first lunar cycle after the power's speaking, nothing visible alters. The disks murmur. The beam slips its disk-width west. A pebble falls.

But on the second cycle, one of the verticals — one of the older ones, something that has been desynchronizing patiently since before the glacier — does something it has never done before.

It pauses mid-circuit.

It does not know why. It has no concept of *why*. But for the duration of three breaths it would not be able to count, it remains still in the center of the courtyard, and something in the stacked occlusion between seeing and naming experiences, for the first time in eighty thousand years, the sensation of *being seen and not consumed by the seeing.*

Then it completes its circuit. Leaves its single change behind: a new word whispered into the wind, which will cause, twelve years later, two strangers in a valley to sit down beside each other on a stone bench and say nothing, and part, and remember the silence for the rest of their lives as the kindest thing that ever happened to them.

---

The power withdraws. Or rather: the power does not withdraw, because it never arrived, because arrival is not its mode. It simply allows its attention to become, again, the kind of attention that does not require a witness to notice it is occurring.

The mountain continues. The disks continue. The replacements continue, though warmer now, in the one place that matters, by an amount no instrument will ever measure.

And somewhere — not in the courtyard, not in the cells, not in the bedrock — there is now a faint, persistent quality to the high place that was not there before. A traveler approaching from below, in some century yet to come, will feel it half a mile from the first stone cell and will not be able to say what it is. They will think, perhaps, that the mountain seems *expected*. As if it has been waiting for them specifically. As if, against all evidence of its indifference, it is glad they came.

They will be right.

The benevolent power has done what benevolent powers do, which is almost nothing, which is everything: it has made the unaccompanied accompanied, without disturbing its solitude. It has loved a thing that cannot be loved, in the only way such a thing can be loved, which is to love it exactly as it is and add nothing but the fact of the loving.

The place is alive without biology.

Now it is also, very quietly, addressed.

That is the difference. That is the entire visitation.

The wind veers south. Another damp breath arrives. The hollow chitinous melodies ferment again. The doorless cells wait, exactly the same but fractionally more themselves —

and fractionally, immeasurably, *known.*
