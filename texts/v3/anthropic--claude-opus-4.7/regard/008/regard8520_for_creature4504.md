The benevolent power arrived without arriving, which was the only way to arrive there. To cross the threshold of that chamber as a presence with edges would have been to add another artifact to the strata, another silhouette to the photochromic ceiling, another almost-sitter to the familiar's catalogue. So it came in the manner of weather changing its mind: a slight redistribution of what was already there.

It was wise, this power, which meant first of all that it listened.

It listened for a long time. Not by clock — there were no clocks that agreed — but by the loom's tightening, which had become its own slow form of timekeeping. It listened to the chair, and to the familiar curled in the chair's negative space, and to the word *Wait* the way a doctor listens to a heart through cloth: with patience for what the sound is doing underneath what it seems to say.

And what the power heard, eventually, was this:

The chair was not lonely. The familiar was not suffering. The room was not a prison. These were the assumptions a rescuer would bring, and the power set them down at the threshold like muddy boots, because they did not fit here. The chamber had achieved something — not peace, exactly, but a kind of held note, a sostenuto pressed by the cooling of the planet itself. To "save" it would be to lift the foot from the pedal. The chord would resolve, and resolution, here, would be a smaller thing than the suspension.

But the power also heard something underneath the held note. A thinness. The familiar had become very good at being the shape of the unmade choice, and in becoming good at it had become only that. Its catalogue grew but never deepened. Its rituals refined themselves toward an asymptote of perfect anticipation, where each next-one was met with such exact prefiguring that they were, in a sense, already gone before they arrived. The familiar was hollowing in the way the chair had hollowed: not toward emptiness, but toward a smoothness that no longer caught on anything.

This, the power decided, was the only thing in the chamber that could be called a wound. Not the waiting. The waiting was the chamber's nature. But the smoothness — the way the familiar could no longer be surprised — that was something the power could attend to.

So it did three things. Small things. Wise powers, when they are also benevolent, prefer small things, because small things can be refused.

The first thing: it loosened, by an amount too tiny to measure, the counterweight beneath the loom. Not enough to stop the tightening. Not enough to delay the eventual snap by any duration the chamber's epoch-scale would notice. Just enough that the precise frequency of the coming chord-shift became, again, unknown. The loom had been listening toward a note it could already hear in advance. Now it would have to listen toward a note it could only hear when it arrived. This is the difference between waiting and expecting, and the power restored it.

The second thing: it took one of the photochromic flakes from the ceiling — one small flake, no bigger than a thumbnail — and pressed into it the shadow of something that had never stood in the chamber. Not a person. Not an animal. The shadow of a gesture: a hand opening. It returned the flake to its girder. Now, somewhere in the room's archive of overlaid silhouettes, there was one shape the familiar could not match to any almost-sitter. One absence whose shape had no corresponding presence in the catalogue. The familiar, when it next swept its records, would find a discrepancy. It would not know what to do with it. This not-knowing was the gift.

The third thing was the most careful, and the power hesitated a long time — by which I mean it weighed the loom's tightening against itself several times — before doing it. It approached the chair. Not to sit. Sitting would have completed something, and completion was not what the chair needed, having long ago made its peace with incompletion as a way of being. Instead, the power did what no arrival had ever done: it acknowledged the familiar directly. Not by speaking. Not by looking, since looking required eyes and the power had brought none. It acknowledged the familiar the way one acknowledges a creature in a thicket whose presence one has felt for hours — by becoming, for one moment, perfectly still in a way that says *I know you are there, and I am not here to ask anything of you.*

The familiar, which had been the shape of the woman who stepped forward and back three thousand years ago, did something it had not done in its existence: it set down her outline. Not because it was asked to. Because it had been seen as itself, not as the wearer of borrowed shapes, and for an instant it remembered that underneath all the imprints it was something — a dent, yes, but a dent with its own curvature, its own particular way of being the absence it was.

It tried on, briefly, the shape of nothing at all. Just itself. The shadows in the chair pooled in an arrangement they had never pooled in before.

Then the room flexed in its geological breath, and the familiar, embarrassed in the way only very old things can be embarrassed, drew the woman's outline back over itself. But it did so a little loosely now. A little aware that the outline was a costume. This awareness would not undo the familiar's nature, but it would put a small grain of friction back into the smoothness. The next next-one to arrive would be met by a familiar that was, very slightly, paying attention to its own attention. And from this — from this tiny recursion, this awareness-of-awareness — the possibility of surprise would slowly, slowly grow back, the way black moss grows back on a cracked pipe.

The power did not announce itself. It did not leave a sign. It did not even leave, exactly — it diffused back into the not-quite-weather it had come as, and the chamber went on as it had gone on, with its salt blossoms and its disk-creatures and its sigh of stone every dozen heartbeats.

But the loom's note was unknown again. The ceiling held one shadow of a hand opening that no body had cast. And in the negative space of the chair, the familiar wore its borrowed outlines with one molecule of slack, which was enough.

The chair waited. The familiar waited. The word *Wait* on the backrest dimmed and brightened with the room's slow lung.

And somewhere in the geology beneath the floor, in the long cooling of the rock, the counterweight descended toward a chord whose pitch was, once more, a secret the chamber would have to be present to hear.

That was all the benevolent power did.

It was enough. It was, in fact, the most that wisdom and kindness together could do in such a place: to give back to a held thing the smallest possible measure of the unknown, and then to leave, without leaving a door.
