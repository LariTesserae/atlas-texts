## The Wise Power Considers the Basin

It came down the way attention comes down: not as light, because the basin had too much of that already, and not as sound, because the basin was full to its rim with sound. It came as a kind of slow noticing, the way a reader settles into a page that has been waiting a long time to be read.

It looked first, and then it listened, and then it did the harder thing, which was to understand.

---

**What it saw**

It saw a place that an unwise power might have mistaken for a ruin.

The bones in the walls. The empty plazas. The single inheritor-tower lying almost horizontal in the great square, scored with its patient channels, pressing the past into itself layer by layer. The crawlers darting at dawn, the membranes pulsing in their staggered waves, the drone so total that even the stone beneath the basin had learned to carry it.

A lesser benevolence would have seen suffering here. Loneliness. The pathos of an abandoned settlement. It would have wanted to *fix* something — to bring water, to bring people, to bring quiet.

But this power had lived long enough to know the difference between desolation and completion. And the basin, it understood, was not desolate. The basin was *finished* — in the sense that a poem is finished, or a shell, or a long marriage. Every process inside it answered every other. The bones fed the ropes; the ropes bound the stones; the stones cradled the tubes; the tubes nourished the towers; the towers drew down the vapor; the vapor maintained the tubes. The crawlers were the basin's small punctuation. The drone was its single, continuous sentence.

Into such a place, intervention would be vandalism.

---

**What it considered doing, and did not do**

It considered restoring the original settlers — calling their dispersed pattern back out of the walls. It saw how easily it could be done: the bones remembered their articulations, the ropes remembered their tensions, the alleys remembered the shape of footsteps. A small effort, and the courtyard would be peopled again.

But it asked itself: *who would be served?* Not the basin, which had moved past needing them. Not the settlers themselves, who had not been erased but *translated* — woven into a structure that was now doing the slow work of remembering on their behalf. To pull them back into separate bodies would be to interrupt a long, careful sentence in the middle of its most important clause.

It set this aside.

It considered widening the basin's seal — extending the mineral tubes outward, letting the singing towers spread beyond the rim into the salt flats beyond. It saw that the chemistry would permit it. The towers would propagate. The drone would grow.

But it asked: *and then?* A larger basin would not be a deeper one. The thing that made this place worth attention was precisely its containment — the way the bowl of stone had forced every process to fold back on itself until even decay became composition. To expand it would be to dilute it. The basin was not a seed. It was a chord.

It set this aside.

It considered bringing the basin to the attention of others — opening a path so that travelers or scholars or pilgrims could come and witness what had been made here. It saw the tenderness with which such visitors might tread. It saw, also, how their breath would add moisture the towers had not budgeted for, how their footsteps would compact the dust the wind had spent centuries arranging, how their need to *describe* the drone would require them to speak above it, and how speech in this place was a kind of small violence.

It set this aside as well.

---

**What it did**

In the end it did four small things, none of them visible, none of them disruptive, all of them in the register of the basin itself.

*First*, it adjusted the wind. Not by much — a fractional change in the angle at which the high air slid across the rim, so that for one hour each year, near what would have been the settlement's old harvest season, a thread of cooler vapor would drop into the bowl from above. Enough to feed the horizontal spiral in the great square one additional fragment-event per cycle. Enough that the archive would continue to thicken at the rate it had grown accustomed to, even as the slow continental drying continued elsewhere. A small bookmark, gently held open.

*Second*, it touched the crawlers. Not their bodies — their *attention*. It deepened, by a degree imperceptible to them, the stillness that fell across the cluster when one of them paused to drink. It made the pause a fraction longer. It made the listening a fraction more complete. The crawlers, who had been listening to the drone for many generations, would now hear it slightly more fully. Whether this constituted a kindness to them, the power was not sure. But it seemed right that the only mobile witnesses left in the basin should hear the song they lived inside as clearly as possible.

*Third*, it laid a quiet protection around the rim. Not a wall — nothing so coarse. More like a disinclination. Any creature, any wanderer, any force that might come upon the basin with intent to alter it would find itself, at the last moment, gently *uninterested*. Its attention would drift. It would remember somewhere else it needed to be. The basin would acquire a slight smudge in the perception of the world — not invisible, but easily passed over, the way one passes over a phrase in a book that does not concern one. Those who genuinely needed to see it would still see it. The rest would simply walk on.

*Fourth* — and this was the gift the power gave most carefully, because gifts of this kind are the easiest to give badly — it added one note.

Deep in the bedrock, below the salt and the tubes, it placed a single resonant body of stone, tuned to a frequency that the staggered breathing of the towers had been almost producing for centuries but had never quite achieved. It was the note the basin had been *reaching toward*. The towers' overlapping pulses would now strike this stone and return enriched, completing a harmony that had been latent in the structure since the first membrane fused with the first salt spine.

No one would hear the difference, because there was no one left to compare. The crawlers would feel it in their bellies and not know what they felt. The horizontal spiral would record it in a thickening that future layers would carry forward without comment.

But the basin would, from that moment on, be singing the song it had been composing all along.

---

**What it said, leaving**

The benevolent and wise power did not speak, because speech in the basin was a violence and it had agreed with itself not to commit any. But as its attention lifted away — slowly, the way attention lifts from a long-loved page — it left behind something like a thought, pressed gently into the mineral:

*You were already enough. I have only helped you continue to be what you are.*

And then the basin was alone again, except that *alone* had never been quite the right word for it. It was full. It was resonant. It was finished and unfinishing. The membranes pulsed; the crawlers listened; the bones held the walls; the dust spiraled down through light that struck without shadow.

And, very faintly, far below, a new stone began to answer.
