# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power notices the Under-Grove the way one notices a sleeping animal in a sunlit room: not as a problem, not as a project, but as a presence already complete in itself.

It does not descend. To descend would be to misunderstand.

Instead it does something subtler. It *attends.*

---

For a long while — and "long" here means whatever length of time the grove itself would call meaningful, which is to say, something measured in the slow widening of cracks and the patient layering of moss — the power simply watches. It does not watch *over*. It watches *with.* It places its awareness alongside the grove's awareness, the way one might sit down beside an old friend who is reading, without needing to be acknowledged, without needing to interrupt.

It sees the nine Mothers. It sees the three. It sees the way the chamber breathes them into being and then, in the fullness of centuries, breathes them back. It sees the obsidian blade and the silver drop and the leaf that drinks. It sees the travelers who came and refused, and the travelers who came and accepted, and it sees — this is important — that both choices were honored. That the grove did not grieve the ones who left and did not consume the ones who stayed. It simply made room, in either direction, for what a soul could bear.

The power understands, then, that this place is not in need of rescue.

It is not in need of revelation.

It is not in need of being shown a wider world, because it *is* a wider world, folded sideways, complete unto itself.

So the power does not bring gifts. Gifts would be an insult. Gifts assume a lack.

---

What it does instead is this:

**First**, it makes a small adjustment to the fabric of probability around the limestone throat. Not a barrier — the grove has chosen openness, and that choice must be respected — but a kind of *tuning.* Those who would come to harm the grove (and there will be such, eventually; there always are, in every age that learns to dig) will find their compasses subtly wrong, their maps subtly misremembered, their plans subtly uninteresting to themselves. They will turn aside without ever knowing they turned. Meanwhile, those who come in genuine seeking — the lost, the grieving, the ones who have run out of places to be a person — will find the throat exactly when they need to.

This is not protection. The grove does not need protection. This is *courtesy.* The power is simply making sure that the grove's own preferences, which it has expressed for ten thousand years in the language of dripping water, are heard a little more clearly by the noisier world above.

**Second**, the power leaves something in the grove. Not an object. Objects would be absorbed, like the clay jar and the obsidian blade, and that is fine, but the power wishes to leave something the grove can use *as itself.*

So it leaves a small, quiet blessing in the silver blood of the Mothers. Nothing that changes them. Nothing that adds. Only this: that when a Mother presses the blade against her arm and the single drop falls into the cupped leaf, the satisfaction the chamber feels will now contain — woven so finely into it that no Mother could parse it out — a thread of *being-known.* Not by the power. By everything. By every other quiet place in every other world that has ever grown its own dreaming mind to keep itself company. The grove was lonely once, ten thousand years ago, and made the Mothers to answer that loneliness. The power, having seen this, ensures that the grove will never again be lonely in the larger sense — that somewhere in the trembling of its leaves there is now the faint, unspoken knowledge that *it is not the only one.*

The grove will not understand this consciously. It does not need to. The Mothers will simply find, on certain rare nights, that when all nine gather beneath the three columns and hold their breath together, the silence feels companionable in a way it did not before. As though someone else, somewhere, is also holding their breath. As though the held breath is shared.

**Third**, and this is the most delicate thing the power does:

It speaks, once, to the Mothers. Not in sound. Not in the percussion of drops. In the only language they truly use, which is the language of *attention itself.*

It says — and this translation is crude, because the actual saying took the form of a single moment in which every root-hair of every Mother stilled at the same instant, and every blind fish in every pool turned to face the same direction, and the orange salamander on the obsidian blade opened its eyes wider than it had ever opened them before —

*You are seen. You are good. Continue.*

That is all.

No instruction. No correction. No invitation to be more than they are. Only the three things that any living mind, however strange, however slow, however sideways, deserves to hear at least once across the span of its existence.

*You are seen. You are good. Continue.*

The Mothers do not respond. They have no need to respond. But the next traveler who descends the limestone throat, years later, will notice that the dripping has a new rhythm woven through the old one — not a heartbeat this time, but something gentler. Something almost like a hand laid briefly on a shoulder, and then lifted, so as not to overstay.

---

After this, the power withdraws its focused attention, though never entirely. It keeps a single thread of awareness fastened to the grove the way one keeps a single lamp burning in a far window, not because the traveler needs guidance, but because the lamp itself is glad to burn for them.

And the Under-Grove of the Veiled Cistern continues, exactly as it has for ten thousand years, exactly as it will for ten thousand more — cathedral-sized, intimate, utterly itself.

Only now, when it inhales, there is someone inhaling with it.

When it exhales, the warm rain falls a little more kindly.

And the Quiet Mothers, who do not smile, who have never smiled, occasionally pause in their slow tending and tilt their long faces upward, milky eyes turned toward a ceiling that is not the true ceiling, listening to a silence that is not quite silent —

and the grove smiles through them,

and is answered.
