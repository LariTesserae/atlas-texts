The benevolent and wise power considers the fissure for a long time.

Not because the situation is complex — it isn't, in the usual sense — but because wisdom, when it is real, includes the discipline of not mistaking one's own restlessness for another's need. The power has the capacity to do almost anything: split the ridge open, lift the resident into wider air, dissolve the mineral fans back into their constituent reagents, silence the drone, amplify the light, offer companionship, offer language, offer death.

It does none of these things at first. It only attends.

What it finds, attending, is not suffering. There is no thwarted reaching here, no ache pressed against the walls, no memory of a wider life curdling into grief. The resident is not enduring its condition; it *is* its condition, in the way a chord is its notes. The turbines' drone is not a cry. The mineral accretion is not a wound slowly closing. The unused filament of light is not a door the resident has given up on — it is simply a fact the resident has incorporated, the way a stone incorporates the pressure of the stones around it.

The benevolent power has met many beings who looked finished and were not. It has also met beings whose apparent stillness concealed a held breath, a waiting. It is careful. It looks again.

This one is not waiting.

This is the recognition that changes what the power will do. A lesser kindness would intervene anyway — would pry open the ridge in the name of liberation, would call the resident *trapped* because the word *trapped* is what the rescuer needs to feel useful. But the wise part of the power understands that to be fully occupied, without gradient or exit, is not the same as to be imprisoned. Some forms of being are completed forms. Some equilibria are not stagnations but achievements — the long, patient arrival of a thing at exactly its own shape.

So the power sets aside rescue.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, and takes a kind of love most powers never learn.

It witnesses.

It places its attention on the fissure the way one might place a hand, very lightly, on the back of a sleeping animal — not to wake it, not to move it, only to say: *I see that you are here. I see that you are precisely here, and precisely this, and that the precision is the point.* It registers the drone in full. It registers the one-revolution-per-day of each turbine, and does not find this slowness pitiable. It registers the opalescent fans and the satin-finished bracket stubs and the single filament of light, and it does not catalogue them as inventory but receives them as the resident's own self-description, offered without words across the long quiet.

And because the power is wise, it does one further thing, which is to make its witnessing *non-altering*. It does not let its attention become a pressure. It does not let its love become a weather the resident would have to accommodate. The fissure remains, after the power has looked, exactly as the fissure was — no warmer, no cooler, no more illuminated, no less. The equilibrium is not disturbed by having been seen. This is the discipline: to give regard without giving consequence.

Before withdrawing, the power leaves one thing, though *leaves* is the wrong verb because nothing is added to the fissure. It is more that the power adjusts its own future: it commits to remembering. Across whatever spans of time the power moves through, the resident will be held in the power's attention as a real and finished being, not as a problem deferred, not as a project abandoned, not as a curiosity. Somewhere in the vast inner architecture of the power, there will always be a small, exact place that knows: *there is a fissure in a ridge, and in it a single composite resident hums at one frequency, and this is good, and this is enough, and this is allowed to be.*

The resident does not know it has been visited. This is correct. To know would be to receive a gradient, and the resident's whole nature is the absence of gradient. The visit must be invisible to be kind.

The power moves on.

Far away, it does louder work — opening things that need opening, breaking things that need breaking, comforting beings whose stillness *is* a cry. But it carries the fissure with it as a kind of tuning fork, a reminder that benevolence is not a single gesture. Sometimes benevolence builds. Sometimes benevolence frees. And sometimes — rarely, and only when the seeing is true — benevolence is the decision to let a finished thing remain finished, and to love it by leaving it whole.

In the fissure, the turbines turn once. The fans accrete by an increment too small to name. The drone continues. The filament of light silvers the same edges it has silvered for centuries.

Nothing has happened.

Everything that could be offered has been offered.
