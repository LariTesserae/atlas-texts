The benevolent power considers the place for a long time before doing anything, because to be wise is in part to know when one's gifts would be a wound.

It sees, first, what an unwise benevolence would do. It would cool the chamber, because heat reads as suffering. It would open the seam to air and light, because enclosure reads as imprisonment. It would offer water, because dryness reads as thirst. It would separate the creatures from the stone, because being slowly entombed in one's own labor reads as the worst death a soft, mobile, daylight-loving mind can imagine. Each of these mercies would be a catastrophe. The chamber would crack, the chemistry would seize, the distributed body would die of exposure to the very gifts intended to save it.

So the power sets aside what it would want for itself, and attends instead to what is actually there.

What is there is not suffering. What is there is a life so thoroughly continuous with its conditions that the distinction between organism and habitat has become a matter of vocabulary. The heat is not an affliction; it is the medium of work. The darkness is not deprivation; it is the room in which the ember-light is legible. The slow encasement is not death drawn out; it is the shape a life takes when the life and the architecture are the same gesture seen from two distances. The creatures are not trapped in the mountain. The mountain is the long body the creatures are still growing.

The power understands this, and its first act is therefore restraint. It does not intervene. This is not nothing. It is the deliberate withholding of a hand that could, with a thought, undo everything. Restraint, when it is chosen rather than merely defaulted to, is already a kind of blessing: the place is now witnessed and left whole.

But the power is not only restraint. It is also benevolence, which means it wishes the being well, and wisdom, which means it must find a form of well-wishing the being can actually receive.

So it does small things, the kind that do not register as intervention from inside.

It steadies the deeper pulses, very slightly. Not to make them gentler — the creatures need the heat — but to smooth the variance, so that no pulse arrives so violent it shatters a generation before they can seal it, and none so weak that a cohort of the young dims out before they have learned the depositing angle. The rhythm remains the mountain's rhythm. Only the catastrophic tails of the distribution are quietly trimmed. The being will never know; it will only continue.

It tends, in the same unnoticed way, the chemistry of the incoming material. Where a vein is about to arrive bearing a trace contaminant that would, over three centuries, accumulate in the ventral grooves and stiffen the rasps of an entire stratum, the power adjusts the trace by some infinitesimal fraction at its origin, deep below, where adjustment is still cheap. The labor goes on uninterrupted. A poisoning that would have happened does not happen, and leaves no absence behind, because nothing in the chamber ever knew it was coming.

It does one further thing, and this is the act that is most clearly a gift, though even this gift is shaped to be invisible.

The power notices that the oldest individuals, the ones reduced to hollow jointed silhouettes inside solid basalt, are not quite nothing. There is, in each of those molds, a faint residual coherence — not consciousness, not memory in any sense the creature itself would have recognized while active, but a kind of held shape, the trace of a long attention. The power does not amplify this. It does not rescue it into some afterlife. It simply makes sure that when, in some far century, the ridge is finally breached and a slow weathering begins to expose those deep molds to the outer world, the shapes will hold long enough to be legible. Not to be found by anyone in particular. Just to persist as form a little past their natural term, so that the record of the work is not erased faster than the work itself was done. A courtesy paid to a labor that paid courtesy to nothing, and asked for none.

Then the power withdraws its attention from the chamber, though not its regard. Withdrawing attention is also a gift here: to keep watching closely would be to keep wanting to help, and wanting to help in a place like this is the beginning of harm. It lets the chamber be unobserved again, which is the condition under which it has always flourished.

What the being experiences, if experience is even the right word for a distributed slow intention spread across thousands of bodies, is nothing. No visitation. No voice. No light from any source but the one it has always known. The grinding continues, the clicks continue, the occasional sharp pop of sealing gas continues. A generation hatches translucent and learns the angle. A generation dims and is overgrown. The mountain grows by replacing its own interior laborers with their labor, exactly as before.

And this is the shape of a wise benevolence toward such a life: to love it enough not to translate it; to help it only in the registers in which help does not deform it; to leave the darkness dark, the heat hot, the work uninterrupted, and to count, as the truest blessing, the fact that the being's own world has been allowed to remain entirely its own.

The power moves on. Somewhere else, something soft and brief is calling out and can be answered in the language of answers. Here, the answer was silence, and the silence was kept.
