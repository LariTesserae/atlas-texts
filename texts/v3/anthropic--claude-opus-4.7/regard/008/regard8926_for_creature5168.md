# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives as it always does in such places: without arrival. There is no descent, no announcement, no shift in the angle of the diffuse light. It simply notices more carefully than it had before, and noticing, for a power of this kind, is already a form of presence.

It bends its attention to the basin the way one might lower one's face very close to a pool to see what lives there. And it sees.

It sees that what it had been prepared to call *ruin* is not ruin. It sees that what it had been prepared to call *survivors* are not survivors. It sees the broad-shouldered figures with their tacky leaf-skin, and understands, with the particular ache that wise powers feel when they have nearly made a mistake, that these are not the remnant of a people who once stood upright in a city of glass. They are something else now. They have completed a passage. They are not waiting to be rescued from anything.

This is the first thing the power must decide, and it decides it slowly, because slowness is the only courtesy this place will accept.

---

It considers what it knows how to do.

It could lift the canopy and let in the full sky — restore the birds, return the sharp colors, dry the felted ground until a person could stride across it. It could un-green the engines, set them spinning at their old velocities, remind the belts what they were built to carry. It could call the broad-shouldered ones up out of their hammocks and give them back the shape of their grandparents: taller, drier, quicker-eyed, fluent in the names of things.

It could do any of these in an afternoon. None of them would be cruel by intention. All of them would be murder.

The power sets these options aside, not with regret exactly, but with the recognition that the gift it had been carrying was the wrong size for this house.

---

So it sits with the basin for a long while. Long in the basin's own measure, which means: through several shiftings of the channel banks, through one full cycle of the gears needing to be cleared of their living patina, through the lives of perhaps four generations of the translucent snails.

It learns the drip rhythm. It learns which harmonic in the belt-thrum the sleepers listen for. It learns the particular sweep of the hand that strokes condensation from the underside of a root bridge, and it learns that this sweep is also a greeting, also a counting, also a prayer, though none of the people in the basin would use any of those words, because they have let those words go the way one lets go of a tool that has finished its work.

The power, learning these things, finds itself changed by them. This happens to benevolent powers more often than is generally admitted. They come to give and discover they have been given to.

---

At last it acts. Its action is so small that no one in the basin marks it as an event, which is the only form of action this place can receive without harm.

It does three things.

**First**, it adjusts, by an amount too small to measure, the bearing in one of the oldest belt-driven pumps — the one in the furthest shed, whose housing the people and the thicker vines have been leaning toward for some years now. The adjustment is not a repair. It is a permission. The bearing will continue to wear, but it will wear *evenly*, so that when it finally fails, perhaps four hundred years from now, it will fail in a way the vines can take up without strain. The people who lean toward it in that distant season will feel the easing as their own breath easing. They will not know that anything was done.

**Second**, it places, in the deep peat beneath the basin, a single quiet condition: that nothing from outside — no encroaching cold, no chemical drift from whatever world still turns beyond the canopy, no curiosity of a less careful power — will reach into this district to hurry it. The basin is granted its own time. Not forever; the power does not deal in forevers, having learned better. But for as long as the slow metabolism wishes to continue itself, it will not be interrupted from above or below. This is the closest thing to a blessing the power knows how to give: *you may keep going at your own pace.*

**Third**, and this is the most delicate, it leaves something of itself behind. Not a guardian, not a watcher — those would be impositions. It leaves, instead, a kind of *listening*, woven so finely into the thrum of the belt-houses and the drip-rhythm of the high leaves that it cannot be distinguished from them. If, in some distant season, one of the broad-shouldered ones should wake in their hammock with a question — a real question, the kind that arises only once in a generation and cannot be answered by stroking condensation from a root bridge — the listening will be there. It will not answer. It will only confirm that the question has been heard somewhere, which is often all a real question needs.

---

Then the power withdraws its closer attention, though not its regard. It goes on to the next place that has drawn it, carrying with it a small, warm, layered impression: of a district two hundred paces across where the distinction between making and growing has been allowed to lapse, where the machinery and the moss have agreed on a single tempo, where a people have consented to become slow enough to be loved by the world they live in rather than to love it from a distance.

The power thinks — if a power of this kind can be said to think in sentences — that it has learned something it will need later, in harder places. It thinks that not every wound asks to be closed. It thinks that some shapes of life look, from a hurrying distance, like surrender, and reveal themselves on closer attention to be a difficult and complete arrival.

In the basin, a droplet falls from a high leaf into the dark water. A pale flyer brushes the canopy. A sleeper in a woven hammock shifts very slightly, registering some change in the harmonic that they could not name and do not need to, and settles again into the long, warm, patient breath of the place that is also themselves.

Nothing has happened. Everything has been kept.
