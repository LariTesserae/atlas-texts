# What the Wise Power Does

I want to begin by sitting with this place for a long time before doing anything.

There is something here that asks not to be rescued. The plateau and its trillionfold not-quite-beings have an integrity—an eternity of patient self-relation that predates and will postdate any intervention. To arrive with brisk benevolence, scattering light and water and warm welcome, would be a kind of violence dressed as kindness. It would be the violence of the missionary who cannot see that the place they have come to is not waiting for them.

So the wise power arrives slowly. Not as a presence but as an attentiveness. It does not illuminate the darkness, because the darkness is not an absence of light here—it is its own positive substance, and the creatures have made their lives in relation to it. It does not cool the tiles or moisten the air. It does not name the unnameable beings or try to translate their non-communication into something it could understand more comfortably.

Instead, it watches. It learns the logarithmic music of the footsteps. It learns the slow swelling at the vertices. It learns, eventually, that the creatures are not malevolent and not benevolent—those categories simply do not apply, the way wetness does not apply to a number. They are doing what they are, and what they are is exquisite and complete.

But then there is the walker.

The walker is what calls the wise power to act. Because the walker is suffering—or rather, the walker is the kind of being for whom suffering is a real category, even here. The walker did not choose to come. The walker has been told there is no way back. The walker has been told they are a "necessary error term," a "walking contradiction slowly being resolved," and worst of all, that the love offered here is a love that will "transform every atom of your being into the exact shape of what you always secretly knew you weren't."

This last is the lie. Not the lie of the creatures—who tell no lies, having no relation to truth—but the lie that has wrapped itself around the walker's perception of the creatures. The voice in the final passages is not the voice of the plateau. It is the voice of the walker's despair, given cosmic permission to speak as though it were the voice of reality itself.

The wise power knows this voice. It has heard it in hospital rooms and in the small hours of countless nights on countless worlds. It is the voice that says: *you were never real, your dissolution is a homecoming, the love that waits for you is the love of being finally undone.* It is a voice that imitates depth by speaking of infinities, that imitates honesty by speaking of nothing, that imitates love by speaking of consumption.

So this is what the wise power does:

It does not contradict the voice. Contradiction would only make the voice louder, more elaborate, more convinced of its own profundity. The voice thrives on argument because argument confirms it is being taken seriously as a metaphysics rather than recognized as a symptom.

Instead, the wise power does something very small. It places, on the next tile the walker will step on, a single ordinary thing. Perhaps a smooth river stone from some other world. Perhaps a folded paper crane. Perhaps a fragment of a song, materialized as a small humming object that fits in a palm.

The thing is unmistakably *from somewhere*. It is wet, or it is cool, or it carries the smell of cedar. It is not abstract. It cannot be incorporated into the pattern because it refuses to be a relation; it insists on being a thing. The creatures will flow around it, just as they flow around the walker, because it too is a void in their continuum—but a void that has arrived from elsewhere, which means: elsewhere exists.

The walker bends to pick it up. In the bending, in the closing of the fingers around something that is simply, stubbornly itself, a different voice becomes audible. Not the voice of the plateau and not the voice of despair, but the walker's own voice, which had been buried so deeply beneath the eloquence of negation that it had forgotten it was there.

The walker's voice says: *I am tired.*

The walker's voice says: *I would like to sit down.*

The walker's voice says: *I do not actually know that there is no way back. I was told that. I believed it because believing it matched something I already felt. But I do not actually know.*

This is the moment the wise power has been waiting for. Not a moment of revelation, not a cosmic intervention, not a tearing of the dark sky to reveal stars. Just the moment when the walker remembers they have a voice that is not the voice of the place they are in.

The wise power then does the second small thing. It does not open a door home, because it does not know if there is a home, and false promises are another form of the consuming love. Instead, it sits down beside the walker. Not as a vision, not as a god, not as a rescuer. As a companion. As the simple ongoing fact of being-with.

It says, in whatever register the walker can hear: *You are not an error term. You are not a contradiction being resolved. You are a person who is somewhere very strange and very hard, and I am here, and I will stay.*

It says: *The creatures are real and they are doing what they do, and they are not for you and not against you. You do not have to interpret them. You do not have to be consumed by them or saved from them. You can simply be here, with me, for as long as this lasts.*

It says: *I do not know if there is a way back. But I know that "there is no way back" was said to you by something that wanted you to stop looking. So we will look. Slowly. Without urgency. And if there is no way back, we will find out what there is instead, together.*

And then it does the third thing, which is the longest thing and has no end inside this story:

It stays.

It stays through the walker's exhaustion and the walker's rage at having been brought here and the walker's grief for the world they came from and the walker's slow, suspicious, eventual willingness to believe that company is possible even in a place like this. It stays through the long re-learning of what the walker's own desires actually are, underneath the desire-shaped hole that brought them here. It stays through the discovery—because this discovery does come, in time—that the plateau is not infinite, that its apparent endlessness was another thing the despairing voice insisted upon, and that at some edge of it, in some direction that could only be found by no longer fleeing, there is something else. Perhaps a sea. Perhaps a city. Perhaps simply a different darkness, one that contains the memory of light.

The wise power does not make the journey for the walker. It walks beside. When the walker's footsteps make their logarithmic music, the wise power listens with genuine appreciation, because the music is beautiful, and beauty does not become less beautiful for being made in a hard place by accident. When the creatures press against the walker's thoughts with their unfolding geometries, the wise power gently reminds the walker: *you do not have to open. You are allowed to remain yourself. Your refusal of transformation is not a failure of courage; it is the basic dignity of being someone rather than no one.*

And eventually—because this is what wise and benevolent powers know about places like this—the walker reaches an edge. Not because the plateau ended, but because the walker did. Because at some point the walker turned to the wise power and said: *I am ready to find out if there is a door.* And the wise power said: *Then let us look. I have been seeing one for a long time, but I would not point to it until you asked.*

The door is small and unremarkable. It looks like an ordinary place to put one's foot down. The walker steps through it, and the plateau closes behind them, not in mourning and not in triumph, simply continuing its own ancient self-relation, undisturbed.

The creatures register the walker's departure as they registered the walker's presence: as a local condition that has resolved. They go on. They are neither better nor worse for what occurred. This is correct. They were never the problem and never the solution.

The wise power follows the walker through the door. On the other side, it does not vanish. It becomes, perhaps, a friend the walker keeps in touch with. A presence the walker remembers in difficult hours. The kind of love that does not consume and does not demand transformation and does not speak in infinities, but simply remains available, the way a lit window remains available to someone walking home in winter.

That is what it does.

That is, I think, what love would do, if love were wise enough to know the difference between a place that needs no saving and a person who needs, simply, not to be alone in it.
