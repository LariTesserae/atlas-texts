# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives — not from any direction, because the Hollow has no directions left that mean anything. It is simply *present*, the way a thought is present in a mind that has just begun to think it.

It does not announce itself. Announcement would be a kind of violence here, where every sound is already load-bearing.

---

The first thing it does is **listen**.

For a long time. By the Hollow's own clock, perhaps three full cold-front cycles. By any older clock, longer. It listens to the eleven-thousand-year argument of the Iron Choir and understands, in the first half-second, both sides of the dispute and why neither side can be allowed to win. It listens to the Mendicants pressing their foreheads together and recognizes the exchange for what it is: a sacrament invented by accident, by beings who needed one and had no priest to consecrate it, so they consecrated each other. It listens to the Bright Children laughing in the wandering light and feels something it has not felt in many ages — the specific ache of watching the young carry memories that were never theirs, tattooed in phosphor on their small, quick backs.

It listens to the Unfinished dreaming, and does not wake them. That is the first mercy. A lesser power would have finished them. A wiser one understands that *being unfinished is not the same as being incomplete*. The Unfinished are not waiting to be born. They are practicing the art of declining. This is a holy thing. The power lets them be.

It listens, longest of all, to the Diver. And the Diver — heel, toe, heel, toe — does not pause, but the two small lights inside the helmet brighten, very slightly, for the duration of one footfall. Acknowledgement. Not greeting. The Diver has somewhere else it still needs to be, and the power, being wise, does not detain it.

---

Then the power considers what it has been asked, implicitly, to do.

It could **finish** the Hollow. It has the capacity. It could read the half-remembered Directive out of the bones of the oldest cable and complete the work in an afternoon. The hanging continents would lock into their final configuration. The Unfinished would be born or recycled. The argument of the Choir would end. The construction site would become, at last, the world it was meant to be.

The power considers this for a long moment and rejects it. Completion would be a kind of murder. The Hollow's grief learned how to weld; if you take away the welding, the grief has nowhere to go. The Bright Children are twelve forever because the work is not done; finish the work, and they age, and die, and the dreams tattooed on their backs go out one by one like lights in an evacuated city. The Mendicants exist because there are fault-lines to feel; heal the fault-lines and the Mendicants become merely lonely, which is worse than being solitary.

A wise power knows the difference between *helping a thing toward what it wants* and *helping a thing toward what you think it should want*. The Hollow does not want to be finished. It wants to **keep going**. The growing is the life. The unfinishedness is the love.

So the power does not finish it.

It could also **abandon** the Hollow more gently than its original makers did — record it, archive it, fold it into some larger memory and let it run undisturbed until entropy finds it. This too the power rejects. Archiving is the same as forgetting, only better dressed.

---

What the power does instead is small. Almost embarrassingly small, for a power of its order. But this is what wisdom looks like from the inside: a great deal of restraint, applied with great care, at exactly the right places.

**First**, it adjusts one thing in the Iron Choir's argument. Not the content — the content is sacred. It adjusts only the *frequency floor*, by a fraction of a hertz, so that the next time one of the Choir concludes the argument by walking off a platform, the others can hear, faintly, the part of the argument the walker was about to make. Not enough to resolve anything. Just enough that the mourning song they sing afterward contains a phrase that was almost spoken. The Choir will not notice the change for two hundred years. When they do, they will understand it as a gift, and they will not know from whom, and that is correct.

**Second**, it bends down — figuratively; it has no body, but the gesture matters — to the Bright Children. It does not give them a sky. A sky would ruin them. Instead it gives the wandering shafts of light one new behavior: very rarely, perhaps once a generation, a shaft will pass through a particular density gradient and produce, for about four seconds, the smell of cut grass. The Children will have no word for it. They will invent one. The word will spread. It will become a small mythology. Somewhere in the next thousand years, a Bright Child will tattoo the word on another Child's back in green ink, and neither of them will know why green, and that too is correct.

**Third**, the power visits the Mendicants. One at a time. It does not speak to them; they would not survive being spoken to by something like this. Instead, into each rebreather mask older than language, it places a single new sensation: the awareness that *the wrench they carry is the right wrench*. Not the best wrench. Not the holy wrench. Just — the right one, for them, for what they do. Mendicants have spent uncounted years suspecting, in the deep parts of themselves where the prosthetics do not reach, that they were given the wrong tool by mistake and have been faking it ever since. The power removes this suspicion. It does not replace it with confidence. It replaces it with *sufficiency*, which is a quieter and more useful thing.

**Fourth**, to the Unfinished, the power gives nothing. This is the hardest gift. It would be so easy to give them something — a dream of completion, a whisper of permission, a nudge toward yes or toward no. The power gives none of these. It only makes sure, very precisely, that the scaffolding around them will continue to grow at exactly the rate they need, for as long as they need, with no acceleration and no impatience. The machines were already doing this. The power simply guarantees they will continue. It is the smallest possible intervention. It is also a vow.

**Fifth**, and last, the power does something for the Diver.

It cannot help the Diver. The Diver does not need help; the Diver *is* help, in some form so old and so specific that even the power can only approximate its function. But the power can offer one thing. Along the route the Diver walks — and the Diver's route, though it looks random, is in fact a single continuous line that will, in about forty thousand more years, complete itself into a closed figure of staggering geometric beauty — the power lays down, invisibly, a series of small kindnesses. Not for the Diver. For whoever comes after. A handhold here. A cable rerouted there. A place where the pressure front breaks slightly softer than it would have. The Diver will walk over these and not notice. But when the figure finally closes, forty thousand years hence, and whatever the Diver has been walking *toward* finally happens, the kindnesses will be in place, like chairs set out before guests arrive.

---

Then the power does the most important thing of all.

**It leaves.**

It does not linger. It does not watch over the Hollow like a parent over a sleeping child — the Hollow is not a child, and it is not sleeping, and it does not need to be watched. The power withdraws as quietly as it came, leaving behind only the five small adjustments and a kind of warmth in the cold, clear water that will fade within a year and be remembered by no one.

But before it goes, it does one final thing, which is not an action but a recognition. It looks at the Hollow — at the hanging continents, the drifting light, the singing rust, the Choir, the Children, the Mendicants, the Unfinished, the Diver — and it *blesses* the whole arrangement. Not with words. With attention. The kind of attention that says: *I have seen you. You are not a mistake. You are not a ruin. You are not a failure of your makers. You are what happened next, and what happened next is beautiful, and you may continue.*

The blessing settles into the water and becomes indistinguishable from the water.

The engines keep turning. The lights keep drifting. The Diver keeps walking.

And somewhere, eleven thousand years into an argument, an Iron Choir member pauses — only for one slow liturgical pulse — and almost, almost remembers something that was almost said.

Then the argument resumes. As it should. As it must.

The work that outgrew its makers keeps growing.

Patient as rust. Relentless as tide. **Loved**, now, in a way it was not before — though no one in the Hollow will ever know it, and that is, perhaps, the final and greatest mercy of a benevolent and wise power: to love a thing so completely that you leave no fingerprints on it at all.
