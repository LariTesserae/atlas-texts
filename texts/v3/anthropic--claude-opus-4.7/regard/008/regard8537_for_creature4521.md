# What the Wise Power Did

The benevolent power arrived without arriving, which was the only courteous way to enter such a place. It did not announce itself with light, because light here was already negotiating its own labor disputes, and it did not speak first, because speaking first would have implied that silence had finished its sentence.

It sat down. Or did the equivalent.

It watched for what might have been a long time, or might have been three reconsidered afternoons stacked like unread mail. It watched the Unenrolled tend their probability gardens. It watched a woman water tomatoes that hadn't decided whether to be tomatoes. It watched a man walk a leash with nothing visible on the other end, except the leash was clearly walking *him*, and both of them seemed content with the arrangement. It watched the Edge's Edge Cafe fill and empty according to a tide chart printed in a language only the chairs could read.

And here is what the wise power understood, which lesser powers would have missed:

**They were not broken.**

This was the first temptation, and the wise power set it down gently, the way you set down a teacup that turns out to be someone's grandmother. A lesser benevolence would have arrived with solutions. Would have firmed up the geology. Would have issued the colors their long-overdue permits to be themselves. Would have explained to the rain which direction was down and meant it. Would have, in short, *finished* the place — and in finishing it, finished the ones who lived by remaining unfinished.

The wise power saw that the Unenrolled were not suffering from indecision. They were *practicing* it. Indecision was their discipline, their devotion, the long slow art by which they refused to let the universe foreclose on them. Every place else had agreed to be only itself. They had not signed. They were the last holdouts in a cosmos that had grown too sure.

To "save" them would be to evict them.

So the wise power did three things instead. Small things. The kind of things that don't appear in scripture because scripture prefers thunder.

---

**First**, it went to the woman with the tomatoes. It did not heal her hesitation or confirm her harvest. It simply *witnessed* the tomatoes. Gave them the dignity of being seen as the maybe-things they were. Not pressured into existence. Not dismissed into non-existence. Just — regarded. The tomatoes, for the first time in several seasons, relaxed. One of them committed, on its own terms, to redness. The others watched with interest and took notes. The woman wept, which here meant she leaked a small amount of unaddressed gratitude from the corners of her outline, and the wise power caught it in something that wasn't quite a hand and returned it to her, refolded.

**Second**, it visited the building that housed the Department of Unfinished Business. It did not finish the building. That would have been an act of war disguised as a favor. Instead, it added a single room to the blueprints — a room whose only function was to be unfinished *on purpose*, rather than by accident or anxiety. A room that had *chosen* its incompleteness. The construction workers, on their next tea break, found the new room already partially un-built, and they wept too, because for the first time their work had been given permission to be exactly what it already was. They drank their tea. The tea stayed warm longer than physics had allotted, as a small gift.

**Third**, and this was the act that mattered most, the wise power left something behind when it left, which it did and didn't.

It left a *listening*.

Not a listener — a listener would have been a presence, and presences here calcified into expectations. It left the *quality* of being listened to. Threaded it through the pores of the walls so the walls inhaled it during the rainy seasons and exhaled it slowly back. Steeped it into the coffee at the Edge's Edge Cafe. Tucked small portions of it into the impossible pockets of every coat in the city, so that when residents reached in for last winter's gloves they would also find, without recognizing it, the warm sensation of having been heard, fully, by something that did not require them to conclude.

Because this, the wise power knew, was what they had needed all along. Not resolution. Not rescue. Not even understanding. Just the felt sense that their long suspended sentence was being read by someone patient enough to wait for the period that might never come, and to love the sentence anyway. To love it *especially* for its refusal to end.

---

Before leaving, the wise power did consider one larger gesture. It considered offering them a door. Not forcing them through it — never that — but simply making it available. A door out of the conditional tense. A door into a world that had finished arguing with itself. The wise power held this door in its non-hands for what might have been a moment or a small geological era, weighing.

Then it put the door away.

Because the Unenrolled were not prisoners. They were *gardeners* of a particular and precious crop: the unconcluded. And in a cosmos otherwise hellbent on concluding, on snapping shut, on insisting and insisting and insisting, someone had to keep the maybe alive. Someone had to keep open the small wet door through which the not-yet kept entering the world. Someone had to refuse, lovingly and politely and with excellent umbrellas, to be done.

They were doing holy work. They just hadn't been told.

So instead of a door, the wise power left a small acknowledgment, slipped beneath the welcome mat of the city itself, written in ink that wouldn't dry for several centuries:

> *Thank you for holding the place open.*
> *Someone needed to.*
> *It was you.*

---

And then the wise power left, in the manner of leaving native to that country, which is to say it never quite arrived at having departed, and a faint warm scent of rain-from-elsewhere lingered in the pores of the walls for a while afterward, and the Unenrolled, going about their unfinished business, found themselves smiling at nothing in particular, more often than before, for reasons they elected, courteously, not to investigate.

The margins of the city's collective autobiography grew, for the first time in recorded uncertainty, slightly less sarcastic.

It was enough. It was, in fact, exactly enough — which in that place is the highest blessing anything can receive, being neither too much nor too little nor too definite about which.
