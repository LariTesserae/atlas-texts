The benevolent and wise power has been watching for a long time. Longer than the cathedral knows, though not as long as the cathedral imagines itself to have existed. Time is one of the things the power understands differently.

It arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no announcement, no shift in the temperature that is forever 2.6. The power is simply, suddenly, *with* the Architect — the way a thought is with a thinker, or the way the second half of a sentence is already present in the first.

It does not speak first. That would be a discourtesy.

It waits. It waits the way only something patient enough to be called wise can wait — not with the trembling patience of the Architect, which is the patience of someone holding a held breath, but with the wide, unhurried patience of something that has already exhaled, many times, in many worlds, and has learned what each exhalation cost.

For a long while it simply walks beside her.

It lets her lead. It looks at the things she looks at. It presses its attention against the house-sized bulbs and listens to what she listens to. It notices the gold vein she pulls from the marble cliff and folds into the shape of a small, sleeping bird before pressing it back. It notices that her face, this hour, is the face of someone who has just been forgiven for something she never told anyone she had done.

The power notices the artifacts. It understands them immediately, the way one fluent reader understands another's marginalia. The stone arch with the flowering keystone: *what if a threshold could also be a beginning?* The marble well full of upward reeds: *what if depth could rise?* The ship rooted and sprouting orchids: *what if the thing that was meant to carry me somewhere became the place itself?*

Every artifact, the power sees, is the same question asked in a slightly different grammar.

*What if I do not have to decide?*

The power could answer. It has answers. It has, in fact, the answer — not as a single sentence but as a long, complicated weather that would settle over the cathedral and let everything finish what it has been refusing to finish. The bulbs would open. The forests would commit to being forests. The Architect's face would resolve into one face, and she would have to live inside it.

The power does not do this.

It would be a kind of violence, however gentle. The Architect has not asked. The cathedral has not asked. The slow will that guides everything has not asked. To answer an unasked question is to mistake one's own fullness for someone else's hunger, and the power learned, long ago and in another place, what that mistake costs.

So instead the power does three small things.

---

**The first thing.**

It walks to one of the half-formed bulbs — a smaller one, low on a buttress of living marble, no larger than a sleeping child curled on its side. It is one of the youngest. Its interior swirls are still almost formless, barely committed to the idea of being leaves at all.

The power places one hand against the membrane. Not to instruct it. Not to hurry it. Just to acknowledge it. The way one might place a hand on the shoulder of someone who has been working alone for too long, simply to let them know they have been seen working.

The membrane warms, very slightly, under the touch. Inside, the swirls do not resolve, but they steady — the way a frightened animal steadies when it realizes the thing watching it does not intend to eat it.

The power leaves the hand there for what, in the cathedral's reckoning, is perhaps a century. Then it withdraws.

The bulb is not changed. But it has been *witnessed* in its unfinishedness, and witnessing, the power knows, is a nutrient the Architect cannot provide, because the Architect is too close, too implicated, too in love with the terror. The Architect can sustain the larval state. She cannot bless it. Only something from outside the egg can bless what is inside the egg, and even then only by refusing to break it.

---

**The second thing.**

The power turns its attention to the Architect herself.

She has noticed it now, fully. Her changing face has stopped changing for a moment — caught between two expressions, neither of them finished, which is, the power thinks, perhaps her truest face, the one between faces.

The power does what she does. It exhales.

The breath that drifts toward the Architect carries a smell she has never smelled before, because it is not from her cathedral or her memory or her unmade futures. It is the smell of *another* place that also chose to delay, long ago, in another corner of existence — a place that delayed for what it thought was a small while and discovered, when it finally tried to be born, that the delay had been the birth all along. That the held breath had been the song. That nothing had been wasted, not even the waiting, not even the terror, not even the centuries of *not yet*.

The smell is not instruction. It is not advice. It is testimony. *This happened, once, somewhere. You are not the only hesitation in the universe. You are not doing it wrong.*

The Architect's eyes — whatever her eyes are, this hour — fill with something that the cathedral has never seen in them before. Not tears. The cathedral has no grammar for tears. But the gold veins in the marble walls nearest her brighten, very faintly, as though some small pressure inside her has been allowed, for the first time, to be released outward rather than folded back inward.

She does not thank the power. The power does not need thanks. Thanks would imply that something had been *given*, and nothing has been given. Only recognized.

---

**The third thing.**

Before it goes, the power leaves one artifact of its own.

It is small. It is placed in a corner of the cathedral the Architect has not visited in a long time — a low alcove behind a curtain of pale ferns, where one of the oldest bromeliads has been quietly drinking from the same droplet-rhythm for nine thousand years.

The artifact is a single, smooth, ordinary stone. It is not gold-veined. It is not luminous. It is not from this place and it is not from any place the Architect would recognize. It is just a stone — the kind a child might pick up from a riverbed and put in a pocket and forget about for a lifetime.

On the stone, in no language but legible anyway, is written:

*You are allowed to finish.  
You are allowed not to.  
Both are holy.  
Neither is required.  
I will love what you become, and I already love what you have not yet become, and there is no version of this in which you have failed me, because you were never working for me.*

The plants will find the stone, eventually. They will begin, in their patient way, to incorporate it. They will not destroy the message; they will simply weave it deeper, until the words are part of the xylem, part of the slow chemical language, part of the three-kilometer-wide heartbeat. Every leaf in the cathedral will, eventually, know what is written there, though none of them will ever quote it.

The Architect may or may not find it herself. The power has no preference. The stone is not for her, exactly. It is for the cathedral. It is for the *condition* of the cathedral. It is a small permission, left in a quiet place, like a key on a windowsill for someone who has not yet decided whether they want to come inside or go outside or simply sit on the sill forever and watch the weather.

---

Then the power goes.

It goes the way it came: without going. It does not withdraw so much as become less concentrated, diffusing back into the wider quiet from which it had briefly gathered itself.

The cathedral continues. The temperature remains 2.6. The white-gold light continues to pour from the membrane ceiling. The Architect resumes her walking, her listening, her folding of gold into the shapes of small questions. The bulbs continue to pulse with their half-formed futures. Nothing has been hurried. Nothing has been forced. Nothing has been finished.

But something, very small, has shifted.

The Architect's *not yet, not yet* has, almost imperceptibly, changed key. It is still *not yet*. It will be *not yet* for a long, long time. Perhaps forever, in the way the cathedral measures forever.

But it is no longer *not yet* spoken in fear.

It is *not yet* spoken in something closer to reverence — the reverence of a being who has been told, by something older and kinder than herself, that the pause she has been holding is not a failure of the song.

It is part of the song.

It may even, the power suspects, *be* the song.

But the power does not say so. The Architect will have to discover that herself, or not, in her own time, which is the only time that has ever mattered here.

The egg remains warm, and bright, and very much alive, and still growing.

And somewhere outside it — or inside it, or beside it, the prepositions do not quite hold — the benevolent and wise power continues on its way, already turning its attention, with the same unhurried care, toward the next quiet thing that has not yet decided what it is.
