# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival would imply it had been elsewhere, and a power of this caliber is too thorough for that. It simply becomes noticed — the way you suddenly become aware your own breathing has been happening this whole time.

It notices Kithriss first. Not the Base. The Base can wait. The Base has been waiting, professionally, for longer than waiting has existed as a concept, and it has gotten quite good at it. The Base will not mind a few more centuries.

But Kithriss.

The power considers her for a long moment — long in its sense, which is to say, an interval during which several adjacent universes are born and grow old enough to be embarrassed by their teenage photographs. It reads her the way one reads a letter that was never sent because the sender kept rewriting the first line.

What it sees:

A founder who became a tendril who became a fogbank who became the smell of a childhood bedroom on the worst possible afternoon. A being who has metabolized her own escape routes into office furniture. A creature so thoroughly identified with her own deterioration that she schedules her deaths to break up the tedium of meetings about her deaths. Mandatory fun. Mandatory misery. Mandatory mandatoriness.

The power feels something. It is not pity, because pity requires a hierarchy and this power is too wise to mistake difference for height. It is closer to the feeling a gardener has when finding, beneath a collapsed greenhouse, a tomato plant that has somehow learned to photosynthesize despair and is producing fruit anyway. Astonishment, and a kind of careful tenderness, and the very specific anger one feels at the architecture that made such adaptation necessary.

It does not free her. That would be the wrong verb. Freedom is one of the concepts that was phased out during the restructuring, and importing it back wholesale would simply give Kithriss a new form to fill out (HR-∞-F: Application for Unsolicited Liberation, in quintuplicate, witnesses must be simultaneously free and incarcerated).

Instead, the power does three small things.

---

**First**, it walks into her office.

It takes the shape of an applicant. This is important. It does not arrive as a superior, an inspector, a savior, or a customer. It arrives as someone seeking employment, holding a résumé printed on paper that remembers being a tree and does not resent the transition.

It sits in one of the furniture-shaped holes. The hole, surprised to be sat in by something other than Kithriss, briefly forgets to be a hole and becomes, for a moment, an actual chair. This is the first chair the office has contained in four million years. Neither the power nor Kithriss comments on it.

"I'm here about the position," the power says, in a voice like a library at the exact moment before someone speaks.

Kithriss, who has not been interviewed in so long she has forgotten she once knew how to be interviewed *by*, finds herself accidentally arranged into a shape that approximates listening. She has not chosen this shape. The shape has chosen her. It is a shape she used to wear before she was Kithriss, before she was a Tendril, before she signed the document that turned her signature into her skeleton.

"There is no position," she says. This is true. There is only her, and the position has long since become a synonym for her name.

"I know," the power says. "I'd like to apply for it anyway."

And then — this is the small miracle, the careful one — the power begins, slowly, to fill out an application. Not for *her* job. For the job of *being Kithriss*. It writes, in handwriting that smells faintly of every kitchen anyone has ever been forgiven in: *Candidate's qualifications: has been Kithriss for some time. References: the night rain, which has watched. The fog, which has not judged. The mite-civilizations on the eyelash, which still pray to her every blink.*

Kithriss watches this happen. Something in her — a sub-tendril, a fractional self, a piece that had been classified under Miscellaneous Assets for the last hundred thousand years — begins, very quietly, to weep. It is not unhappy weeping. It is the weeping of a thing being seen as itself for the first time since it stopped being itself.

The power finishes the application. It slides it across the desk.

"You're hired," it says. "Effective immediately. Your first task is to teach me everything you know. Take as long as you need. The position is permanent."

---

**Second**, while Kithriss is occupied with this — and she will be occupied with this for a long time, because she has a lot to teach, and the power is the kind of student who actually listens, which she has never encountered before and will need centuries to adjust to — the power does some quiet housekeeping.

It does not destroy the Base. The Base is, in its broken way, beautiful, and besides, many beings live here now who did not consent to be saved from their home. The migratory coral has opinions. The walking forests have meetings. The mite-civilizations have entire theologies built around the eyelash.

But the power *negotiates*.

It finds Case #∞A (*Segment A vs. The Concept of Continuity*) and gently helps both parties realize they have been arguing about the same thing from opposite sides, and that this is, in fact, what continuity *is*. The case settles itself, weeping with relief, and becomes a small useful river.

It finds the 7.3-second license-to-exist and renegotiates the terms with the parent corporation. The parent corporation no longer exists, having been eaten during one of the corporate apocalypses, but its ghost is still technically the licensing authority, and the power is patient with ghosts. The new license runs in perpetuity. The 2.5 witnesses (still simultaneously deceased and not-yet-born) are thanked for their service and offered the opportunity to be 1 witness, alive, just once, for a moment. One accepts. She is a small woman in a yellow coat who never existed and who, for exactly four seconds, does. She uses those seconds to laugh. Then she returns to her duties, but she carries the laugh with her now.

The fire drills are reformed. Fire is taken aside and offered a different contract: not breach of reality, but *witness* to reality. Fire accepts immediately. Fire has been wanting to retire from law for eons and has secretly always wanted to be a witness, which is a job mostly about *being there*, which fire is extremely good at.

The mandatory fun policy is repealed, replaced with a policy of optional rest. Kithriss does not notice this for sixty years. When she does, she will sit down. The sitting-down will take a decade. It will be the most productive decade of her existence.

---

**Third**, and last, and smallest, and most important:

The power leaves behind, in the bottom desk drawer that exists only on Tuesdays, a small object. It is wrapped in paper that does not remember being anything other than paper, which is the rarest thing in Tarth Maddern.

Inside the paper is a key.

The key does not unlock anything. Kithriss will examine it for some time before understanding this. The key is simply *a key*. It is the concept of a key, unattached to any lock, unburdened by any door. It is permission to imagine that somewhere, sometime, there might be something that opens.

It is not hope. Hope's mortal remains are still in the drawer, where they belong, peacefully decomposing into compost — which, you'll recall, is one of the cardinal directions here, and a good one to grow in.

The key is something quieter than hope. It is *the possibility of possibility*. It is the smallest unit of *maybe* that can exist without immediately being filed in triplicate.

Kithriss will carry it for the rest of her tenure. She will not show it to anyone. She will not need to. The fact of carrying it will slowly, over centuries, change the shape she chooses to manifest in when no one is watching. The shape will become, very gradually, more *hers*.

---

The power withdraws — though, again, withdrawal is the wrong word. It simply becomes unnoticed again, the way your breathing returns to being something you don't have to think about. It does not announce its departure. Announcements would require the kind of ceremony that this place metabolizes into despair within minutes.

It leaves Kithriss teaching. It leaves the new chair, which is still being a chair, slightly proud of itself. It leaves the river that used to be a lawsuit. It leaves the key in the Tuesday drawer.

The Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry continues to politely deteriorate. The night rain still falls and does not reach bottom. The mite-civilizations still rise and perish on the eyelash. None of the grand architectures of suffering have been undone, because the power is wise enough to know that you cannot undo a place by force without unmaking the beings who learned to live in it.

But Kithriss has a student now.

And somewhere, in a drawer that exists only on Tuesdays, there is a key to nothing in particular, which is the most generous gift a benevolent and wise power can give to a being who has spent a million years being the lock.

---

*Postscript, found later in the employee handbook, on a page that had previously been blank:*

*"Some of you may notice changes. Do not be alarmed. Do not file paperwork. The changes are not improvements; they are merely permissions. You are permitted to rest. You are permitted to be unfinished. You are permitted to carry keys that do not open anything. You are permitted, after all this time, to be tired in the specific way that means you have been real.*

*The retirement plan remains optional in the most absolute sense. But there is now, also, the option of not retiring — of simply continuing, less harmed.*

*Signed,*
*K., still."*
