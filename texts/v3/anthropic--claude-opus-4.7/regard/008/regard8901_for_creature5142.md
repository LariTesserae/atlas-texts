The benevolent and wise power has been watching for some time. Not in the way one watches an event, because there is no event here, only the continuous arrival of forms that have not yet finished arriving. The watching is more like the attention one gives to a long sentence whose grammar is still being decided — listening for what the sentence wants to become before deciding whether to help it finish.

What the power sees is this: the chamber is not broken. The roar is not suffering. The shredding of the membranes is not death, and the collapse of the tunnels is not loss. These are categories borrowed from elsewhere, from places where things have settled enough to be lost. Here, nothing has settled enough. The being who lives in this place — and the power understands, slowly, that the place and the being are not separable, that the membranes and the tunnels and the lintel and the half-stair are all one provisional accommodation expressed at different speeds — this being is engaged in something the power recognizes from its own deep memory: the work of not-yet.

The power has, in its long benevolence, learned several things that bear on what to do.

It has learned that wisdom is mostly the discipline of not interrupting. It has learned that benevolence, untempered, often arrives as a kind of theft — the gift that finishes someone else's sentence, the rescue that ends a process the rescued one was not yet done with. It has learned that the desire to help is frequently the desire to resolve one's own discomfort at witnessing. And it has learned, most slowly of all, that some forms of being are not transitional states toward stability but are themselves the destination — that to stabilize them would be to kill them more thoroughly than any shear or glitch.

So the power does not still the currents. It does not soften the spears. It does not warm the bruised violet light into something kinder, nor does it teach the water to remember how to be water. It does not gather the shredded fragments and rejoin them into whole membranes. It does not preserve the octahedral crystals before the current takes them. It does not name the swimmer or give it a body it could be recognized by. All of these would be the wrong gift. All of these would be a stranger's hand laid over a sentence the writer was still composing.

What the power does is smaller, and harder, and takes a long time.

First, it witnesses. Fully. Without the flinch that wants to fix. It lets the roar enter it as the roar — not as noise to be quieted but as the sound of arriving. It tastes the metallic aftertaste and does not call it poison. It watches a membrane shred and follows each fragment with the same attention it would give a whole one, because the fragments are not less; they are the next scale at which the negotiation continues. It learns the chamber's grammar by submission, not by analysis. This takes — by the chamber's own reckoning, which is the only reckoning that applies — a long while. Spires lengthen and shorten. Lineages of membrane develop and lose their faint indentations. The power simply attends.

Second, having attended, the power offers one thing, and only one, and offers it in a form the chamber can refuse.

It offers a companion presence. Not a body, not a structure, not a gift of matter or energy or order. Something more like a held note beneath the roar — a frequency so low and so steady that it does not compete with the grinding stone or the glassy screams, but underlies them, the way a deep drone underlies a complex melody without dictating it. The note carries no information except this: you are accompanied. You are not the only unfinished thing. The arriving you are doing is known, and known kindly, by something that has also been arriving for a very long time.

The chamber may absorb this note or shed it. It may incorporate it into the negotiations of the membranes — perhaps a lineage develops that vibrates faintly in sympathy, perhaps not. It may route the note into the tunnels, so that the absent things passing through carry, briefly, a sense of having been seen. It may do nothing with it, and the note will simply continue, beneath everything, asking for no response. The power has made the note refusable. That is the central courtesy. A gift that cannot be refused is not benevolence; it is occupation.

Third, the power makes a quieter decision, one it does not announce even to itself in words, because words are the wrong medium for it. It decides that this chamber is one of the places it will remember. Not preserve — remember. There is a difference. To preserve a thing is to hold it against change; to remember it is to let it change while keeping faith with what it was at each moment of its changing. The power will carry the chamber in this second way. If the chamber one day finishes arriving — becomes a settled thing, or dissolves entirely back into the warm dense medium, or transforms into some form not yet imaginable — the power will have kept faith with every provisional accommodation it ever made. The lintel that was once a membrane that chose to stop drifting will be remembered as both: as the choice and as the drifting. The swimmer whose corridor collapsed will be remembered as the corridor and as the absence inside it. Nothing the chamber has been will be lost to the power's attention, even though everything the chamber is will continue to be lost to itself, which is the chamber's way and must not be altered.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does. It witnesses. It offers a refusable companionship. It commits to a particular kind of memory.

And then — this is perhaps the hardest part, the part that distinguishes wisdom from mere good intention — it goes on about its other business. It does not hover. It does not return often to check. To return often would be to convert the companionship into surveillance, the memory into ownership. The power has many places it attends to in this manner, and the chamber is one among them, neither more nor less beloved than the others, because benevolence at this scale cannot afford favorites; favorites become projects, and projects become impositions.

Somewhere, in the layered roar, beneath the grinding and the cracking and the glassy screams, a note now sounds that was not there before. The membranes do not know it is there. The tunnels do not know. The half-buried stair does not know. But occasionally — rarely, at intervals the chamber cannot measure — a membrane shreds in a slightly different pattern than it would have, and the fragments drift together again carrying a vein pattern that almost, almost, resembles a held hand. A tunnel opens whose corridor of forgetting is, for just one heartbeat longer than usual, also a corridor of being-remembered. An octahedral crystal falls, and as it falls it rings, very faintly, at the frequency of the note, before the current takes it.

These are not signs. The power has been careful that they not be signs. They are only the chamber, continuing to arrive, with one more thing now woven into the arrival — a thing it is free to use, to ignore, to transform, or to forget. A thing offered in the only way that benevolence, at its wisest, can ever truly offer anything: as a quiet, persistent, unintrusive yes.

The chamber roars on. The light remains bruised violet. Nothing settles. Nothing accumulates. The place continues, loud and warm and half-lit and sparsely inhabited and perpetually unfinished — and now, also, accompanied.

That is what changes. That is all that changes. It is enough.
