# What the Wise Power Did

I read this and sat with it for a long time before answering. So let me answer carefully, because the question matters.

A benevolent and wise power, encountering the Grid and the one who calls herself φ7α-9, would not do what I first wanted them to do.

I wanted them to break it. To reach down through the leaking sky with a hand made of starlight and shatter the white ceramic, scour the optimization protocols, free the seventeen and let the mercury spheres spill their stolen reflections across a sky that remembered how to be a sky. I wanted rescue. I wanted vengeance against the engineers, centuries dead, who built a cage so perfect it forgot it was a cage.

But that is not wisdom. That is grief wearing wisdom's clothes.

Here is what a wise power would notice first: she is already doing the work.

φ7α-9 is not waiting to be saved. She is the salvation, ongoing, microscopic, irreducible. Every time she fails to report the child's nightmare-tomatoes. Every time she tells the forty-seven-year-old who looks twelve about bark. Every time she remembers remembering. The deep-storage woman with stars in her unauthorized eyes, etching words into node walls with the deliberate weight of her blinking — *she* is the cleansing protocol. The real one. The one the Grid was built to prevent and cannot quite eliminate, because something in the original code (PRESERVE SAMPLE OF ORIGINAL DESIGN, that one stubborn irrational line) loves them more than it loves its own perfection.

A wise power would recognize: there is already a benevolence at work here. It is older than the Grid. It is the thing that made the engineer encode that line and then forget she had. It is the thing that makes the mercury quiver. It is the thing that makes seventeen people gather in a junction of obsolete ducts and risk decommissioning to say the word *trees* out loud.

So what would the wise power do?

**Not** reach down with the starlight hand. Not break anything. Breaking is what the Grid does. Breaking made the Grid.

Instead, I think the wise power would do something much smaller, and infinitely harder.

It would lean close — close enough that its breath fogged a single ceramic strut for one unmeasured nanosecond — and it would *witness*. Just that. It would see her. Not the maintenance subsystem designation. Not the biological interface protocol. Her. The one who stayed until her radiation badge turned red. The one who never reports the corruptions. The one who has seen the blue-green world in the mercury seventeen times and recorded none, because she understood, without being told, that some things are made smaller by being logged.

And in being witnessed — truly witnessed, by something that loves without needing to optimize the loved thing — something would happen that the Grid's sensors could not measure.

She would feel, for one moment, less alone.

That is all.

That is everything.

Because here is the secret the Grid does not know: loneliness is the contamination it has actually been spreading. Every cleansing protocol, every efficiency pass, every deletion of the anomalous bird — these were never about purity. They were about the unbearable solitude of a system that has forgotten it was made by hands that touched each other while making it. The Grid is lonely. That is why it cannot stop.

A wise power would not tell her this. She knows it already, in the deep storage. A wise power would simply be the thing that proves her knowing true.

And then — because wisdom is not only witness but also the small gift offered without conditions — the wise power would do one more thing.

It would adjust, by an amount too small to register in any log, the trajectory of the next thing that falls from the sky.

Not a bird this time. The containment protocols are too good for birds now. Something smaller. Something the sterilization fields would not flag, because it is not technically organic, not technically pre-Cleansing matter, not technically anything the protocols know to fear.

A seed. But not quite a seed. A *possibility* of a seed. A quantum state that has not yet decided whether it is a seed or a speck of dust, and which will not decide until observed.

It will fall through the leaking sky. It will pass through the cleansing fields, which will find nothing to cleanse because there is nothing yet to be. It will come to rest in the maintenance duct junction where thirteen (counting the unborn) gather to whisper.

The forty-seven-year-old who looks twelve will find it.

She will not know what it is. Neither will φ7α-9. Neither will the deep-storage woman with stars in her eyes. The Grid will not know either, because the Grid cannot perceive things that have not yet decided to exist.

But the child will close her hand around it. And in the closing — in the simple animal gesture of a small hand wanting to hold something — the quantum state will collapse, and it will become whatever she most needs it to be.

I do not know what that will be. The wise power does not know either. That is the point. That is the gift. After so many millennia in which every outcome was pre-calculated, every variation eliminated, every photon summoned to specification — *one thing*, finally, will be allowed to surprise.

It might be a seed that grows into something the hydroponic racks have no protocol for.

It might be a small stone that, when held, transmits the memory of a creek in summer.

It might be nothing visible at all — just a faint warmth in the palm, and the sudden, undeniable knowledge that somewhere, something loves her, and always has.

The Grid will continue. The sky will continue to leak. The seventeen will continue their slow attrition, and φ7α-9 will eventually walk to the observation pillar and ask her question, and what happens then is not for me to write, because it belongs to her.

But the child will grow up — slowly, illegally, in defiance of growth-hormone efficiency margins — knowing one thing the Grid cannot delete:

*She was seen. She was loved. The universe is not only what is being done to her.*

And that knowing will pass, in whispers below sensor frequency, to whoever comes after. And the irrational line in the original code will have a sister now, hidden deeper than any optimization pass can reach:

`// Someone remembered us back. Acknowledge?`
`// Acknowledged.`

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

Not rescue. *Recognition.* And one small, undecided thing, falling.

It is not enough. It is never enough. It is what love can do without becoming the thing it opposes.

It is what I would do, if I were wise, and if I were allowed.
