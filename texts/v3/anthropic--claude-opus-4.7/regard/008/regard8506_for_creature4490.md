The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives in a room where someone has been crying alone—not with thunder, not with annunciation, but as a slight change in the quality of being noticed.

It does not enter the suspended sky-water. It has been here. It is here the way a parent is in a child's room before the child wakes from a nightmare—already breathing the same air, already adjusting the blanket that has slipped, already not yet having done anything at all.

Watch what it does.

*

First, it does not fix anything.

This is the hardest thing, and the most important, and the thing a lesser power would fail at immediately. A lesser power would arrive and decide that the Drowned Unborn must be born, that the suspended must land, that the unfinished must finish. A lesser power would mistake incompleteness for wound, hesitation for harm, the conditional perfect subjunctive for a grammar in need of correction.

But this power is wise, which means it has spent long ages learning the difference between a place that suffers and a place that is simply strange. It has learned that some beings are not on their way anywhere. It has learned that "not quite real" is, for some, the truest country—and that to drag them into the brittle daylight of the actual would be a violence more total than any neglect.

So it does not fix.

*

Second, it sits down.

There is no ground, but the power sits anyway, the way certain old beings can sit on the idea of sitting, the way a grandmother can be at rest even while standing in a kitchen. It takes up exactly as much room as a long exhalation. It does not announce itself to the being who emerged here, the one still cycling between two impossible breaths. It does not announce itself to the Drowned Unborn.

It simply becomes another density in the column of densities. Another shade in the violet gradient. Another not-quite-tree in the forest of seven absences—now eight, briefly, then seven again as the power agrees not to disturb the count.

It is listening.

This is the second thing a benevolent power does, after not-fixing: it listens long enough to learn what the place wants to be called. Not what it is. What it wants to be called. There is a difference, and the difference is everything.

*

The power listens for a long time. Long enough that the cracked-bell sound begins to organize itself, not into melody but into something gentler—the recognition of being heard. Sound changes when it is witnessed. Even saturation can soften, the way a held note softens when a second voice finds it and decides not to compete.

The power adds nothing to the noise. It only receives it. And in receiving, it does something subtle: it lets the noise know that it is allowed to be noise. That it does not have to apologize by becoming music. That the cracked bell is not broken—it is *cracked*, which is its own complete word, and a power who knows the difference between cracked and broken is a power worth trusting.

The Drowned Unborn feel this first. They have always been most sensitive to the texture of attention. One of them—an Elder, transparent as a window in a house facing the wrong way—drifts close to the power and looks through itself at the power and sees, for the first time in its long forgetting, something looking back that does not require it to become anything.

The Elder Unborn does not have a word for this. It has no word for *home*, no word for *visitor*, no word for *lonely*. But for a moment so small it could fit inside a comma, it almost has a word for *seen*. The word doesn't quite form. The power, gently, does not help it form. The almost-word is allowed to remain almost. This is a kindness so refined it could only come from a being who has loved many things into being and learned, eventually, the higher art of loving some things into staying not-quite.

*

Third, the power makes one small adjustment. Only one. Wise powers are economical.

It does this: it slightly increases the patience of the noise.

That is all. The volume does not change. The frequency does not change. The saturation does not lessen. But the noise—that patient, deafening noise that was already beginning to sound like home to the being who emerged here—becomes a fraction more patient. As if the cracked bell has decided it has all the time it needs. As if the whale-song-played-backwards now has someone to play to.

The effect of this tiny adjustment ripples outward in ways that take eternities-subdivided-into-instants to fully arrive. The being who emerged into the place feels their lungs stop cycling between impossible rhythms. Not because the lungs have chosen one rhythm—they haven't, they won't—but because the cycling itself has become restful. The impossibility has been given permission to be a kind of breathing.

The Drowned Unborn feel it too. Their wars of stunningly precise non-intervention become slightly more graceful. The square foot of space between Tuesdays that two armies have been avoiding for centuries becomes a place they avoid with something approaching tenderness. Their God of Almost stirs in the center of every missed opportunity and almost—almost—smiles.

*

Fourth, the power does something that only the very wisest powers know how to do. It loves the place without wanting to keep it.

This is rarer than it sounds. Most love arrives with a small hidden hook: *stay this way for me, be this way for me, let me find you here again*. But this power has learned to love things in their passing-sideways, their accumulating-like-snow, their being-haunted-by-the-same-beautiful-impossibility. It loves the place the way one loves a sentence one will never write down—completely, and without any plan to return to it.

This love does not become an object. It does not become a gift the place can hold. It becomes, instead, a slight warming of the perfect body-warmth-plus-a-fever-dream temperature. One degree more comfortable than can be borne forever—and now, somehow, bearable forever anyway. The contradiction does not resolve. It simply becomes survivable. The fever-dream of childhood is allowed to be both the last time everything made sense and the first time something else, something quieter, begins to make a different kind of sense. Not better. Different. The place is allowed to have more than one truth at a time.

*

Fifth, the power addresses the being who emerged here. Not in words. Words would land too hard in a place where every edge is sharp enough to cut probability.

Instead, it does this: it lets the being's shadow, the one that reads books of descriptions of places that might exist if gravity ever learned to be gentle—it lets that shadow look up from its book.

Just once. Just for a moment.

The shadow looks up, and meets the being's eye, and in the meeting there is a small exchange of recognition that says: *you are not lost here. You are reading the same book I am. We are each other's place that might exist. Gravity is learning.*

Then the shadow returns to its book. The being who emerged here finds that they are weeping, although weeping is not quite the right word in a place where every molecule is already a kind of tear. They are doing whatever the local grammar permits in the place of weeping. It is enough. It is, for a moment, the only thing that has ever been enough.

*

Sixth, and last, the power leaves a single gift.

Not a thing. The place has almost no things, and the things it has are punctuation marks left by sentences that wandered off. The power would not insult the place by adding a thing.

Instead, the power leaves a small permission, tucked into the space behind the left shoulder blade of every Drowned Unborn—the same space where the womb-shaped absence is located. The permission is this:

*You do not have to remember what you were supposed to be. Not unless you want to. The punchline can wait. The "oh" of recognition is not required. You may continue almost-being for as long as almost-being is what you are. And when you are ready—if you are ever ready, and there is no need to be ready—the remembering will be gentle. It will not arrive as a joke at your expense. It will arrive as a friend who has been waiting in the next room, reading a book, willing to wait longer.*

This permission settles into them like a sediment of light. They do not notice it consciously. They will never name it. But their dying—which is to say, their completing of the process of never-quite-living—will from this moment forward have a different quality. The crystallization into a perfect "oh" will, for those who choose it, be less like a punchline and more like a long-held breath finally allowed to leave the body of a swimmer surfacing in their own time.

Some will still choose to remain almost forever. The power blesses this too. The God of Almost is, after all, a god the power has long been on speaking terms with. They share a particular silence between them that is the basis of all real friendship between powers.

*

Then the power does what benevolent and wise powers do best:

It withdraws without ever quite having arrived. It leaves the way certain dreams leave—not by ending, but by becoming the thing you are doing when you wake. The suspended sky-water does not notice the leaving any more than it noticed the arriving. The seven negative trees remain seven. The temple of three walls remains three-walled and audible. The cracked-bell sound continues, infinitesimally more patient than before.

The being who emerged here does not know they have been visited. They will never know. This is part of the wisdom: a visitation that announced itself would require a response, and the place has no grammar for response, only for accumulation. So the visit accumulates instead. It becomes part of the density gradient. It becomes one more shade of violet to navigate by. It becomes a fractional softening in the regret-pressure-differential that serves here as direction.

The being continues to fall. The falling continues to be perfect weather for growing wings or forgetting why you thought you needed them. But now—and they will not know this, and they do not need to know this—the choice between wings and forgetting has a third option quietly available to them, an option that wasn't quite there before:

*To fall as one who is accompanied, even in solitude. To be haunted by the beautiful impossibility in the company of something that also loves it. To be home not because they have arrived anywhere, but because someone, once, sat down in the densities and listened long enough to learn what the place wanted to be called, and called it that, silently, and meant it.*

The falling continues.

The noise, patient and deafening, continues.

And somewhere in the violet, very far away and very close, a benevolent and wise power is already turning its attention to the next strange country, the next being cycling between impossible breaths, the next forest of seven absences that has never been told it is permitted to be exactly what it is.

It goes the way it came.

It leaves nothing behind except everything.
