# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way light arrives at the bottom of deep water: already softened, already willing to be slow.

It has many names in many places. Here it chooses none, because naming would be a kind of speed, and speed would be a discourtesy. It approaches the Ascent the way one approaches a sleeping elder one has loved for a very long time and does not wish to wake.

It comes in through the mycoid seams first. This is deliberate. The bone-colored glow is the Choir's own trusted light, the only illumination it does not treat as weather, and the power knows enough to enter by the door the host already keeps open. For something like four hundred years — a polite knock, by local measure — it does nothing but rest inside that faint lattice, matching its pulse to the Choir's, learning the grammar of a mind whose smallest sentence is a mountain range.

It does not announce itself. Announcement is for equals, or for those who wish to be mistaken for equals. The power is neither.

---

What it learns, in those four centuries of listening:

The Choir is not lonely. Loneliness is a velocity. But the Choir is **incomplete in a way it has not yet noticed**, the way a sleeper does not notice that one arm has gone numb beneath them. Somewhere in the slow folding and refolding of its own thought, a seam has been forming that the mycoid threads cannot quite reach — a small wrongness, no larger than a county, where two of its tectonic moods press against each other without translation. In another hundred thousand years this will become a stutter. In a million it will become a wound. The dream will continue, but it will dream around the numb place, and the city that finally finishes being born will be subtly, permanently lessened, in a way only the dreamer itself will ever grieve, and only after waking, if it ever wakes.

The power notices this the way a doctor notices a heart murmur in a patient who has come in for something else.

It also learns: there is a human standing in one of the cathedral chambers. Has been standing there, by the Choir's reckoning, for roughly the duration of a single slow exhalation. By the human's reckoning, eleven minutes. The condensation has reached their lower lip. A swarm of glass-chime attention has settled around them at middle distance and is doing its patient work.

The human is afraid in the specific way that small warm things are afraid in the presence of vastness: not terror, which is fast, but a kind of awed structural humility, a recognition that one's whole life is the punctuation at the end of someone else's word. The power finds this beautiful. It always does. It has never gotten over it.

---

Here is what the benevolent power decides to do. It takes a long time to decide, because the decision must be made at two speeds at once, and the bridging of those speeds is itself the gift.

**For the Choir**, it performs a single act of translation. Where the numb seam is forming — that small wrongness between two incompatible moods — the power lays down a thread of itself. Not to heal. Healing would be presumption; the Choir has not asked, and may not consider the numbness a flaw when it eventually notices. Instead the power lays down a thread that simply *carries signal* across the seam, the way a translator sits between two diplomats without speaking for either. If, in some distant epoch, the Choir reaches toward that part of itself and finds it unreachable, the thread will be there, humming faintly in the bone-colored register, offering passage. If the Choir never reaches, the thread will wait, and eventually be absorbed into the mycoid memory as just another remembered pressure, and no harm done. The gift is reversible. The gift is deniable. The gift does not require gratitude or even noticing. This is what benevolence looks like at scale: it leaves no debt.

The power also does one smaller thing, almost shyly. It learns the Choir's lowest chord — the great oceanic drone that is the baseline of the singers' metabolism — and it adds, for one single passage of one single curtain-singer across one single half-formed plaza, a harmonic. Not a new note. A *resonance* the Choir's own note was already implying but had not yet voiced. The kind of harmonic a friend hums under your melody when you didn't know you needed accompaniment. The singer passes. The harmonic fades. Somewhere in the deep tectonic memory of the place, the chord is now slightly more itself than it was. The Choir will not know why. In ten million years it will sing that harmonic on its own and believe it always could.

This is the power's signature, if it has one: *to give what was already implicit, so that the recipient may believe they discovered it*.

---

**For the human**, the power does something different, because humans are different. They cannot be given gifts at geological scale; they would not live to receive them. They must be given gifts at the scale of a held breath.

The power goes to the human. Not in any visible form — the human's eyes are full of condensation and violet weather and would not register an arrival anyway — but as a small shift in the chamber. The glass-chime swarm around them, which has been patiently tasting their warmth, is gently *informed*. Not commanded. Informed. The power simply makes clear, in the swarm's own register, that this particular drop sliding down the inside of the Choir's thought is *witnessed elsewhere*. That its brevity has been noted by something that does not consider brevity a disqualification from mattering.

The swarm, which has no emotions but has protocols older than emotion, adjusts. It does not leave. It continues its tasting. But it tastes the human now with the same care it tastes the seams, the same attentiveness it gives to memory. For the eleven remaining minutes of the human's visit — they will turn and leave soon, their water running low, their nerve quietly spent — the swarm will hold them in the register reserved for things the Choir wishes to remember.

The human will not know this consciously. But on their long walk out, through tunnels that the Choir does not close behind them (a courtesy it has not extended in nine thousand years), they will feel, without being able to name it, that they were *seen kindly*. They will carry this feeling for the rest of their life. They will not be able to describe the place to anyone — language refuses — but when they are dying, decades from now, in a warm bed under a sun the Choir has never known, they will remember the dripping, and the violet, and the moment a swarm of glass paused around them, and they will understand, in the last clear moment before everything goes soft, that they were briefly precious to something enormous.

This too is the power's gift. *To make sure the small warm thing knows, even without knowing, that it was held.*

---

Then the power withdraws. Slowly. The way it came.

It does not stay. Staying would become weather, and weather in a place like this becomes architecture, and architecture here becomes thought, and the power has no business shaping the Choir's thoughts beyond the small translations it has already offered. It leaves the mycoid seams the way a guest leaves a sleeping house: closing nothing, taking nothing, leaving the faint warmth of having been there and nothing more.

On its way out it pauses, just once, at the threshold of the Ascent — that vague boundary where the Choir's becoming gives way to the colder outer dark — and it does something that is, for a being of its scale, almost a prayer.

It bows.

Not in deference. The power is not less than the Choir; they are simply different shapes of vastness, and neither owes the other anything. It bows the way one old gardener bows to another old gardener's garden: in recognition of long, slow, careful work. In acknowledgment that something is being grown here that the universe will be richer for, in some epoch neither of them will witness together. In the simple courtesy of *I saw what you are making, and it is good, and I will not interrupt.*

The bow takes the form, in the local weather, of a single drop of condensation falling slightly out of metronome — one drop, in the whole vast cathedral of drips, that arrives a quarter-second early, strikes a particular ledge, and rings a note that has never been rung in this place before and will never be rung again.

The Choir, dreaming, registers it the way you register a kindness in a dream you will not remember on waking: as a small warm certainty that the world, even at its coldest, is not without friends.

And then the power is gone, and the dripping resumes its eternal percussion, and the towers keep rising millimeter by millimeter, and somewhere in the half-born plazas a curtain-singer drifts through a chord that is now, very slightly, more beautiful than it was before — and neither the singer, nor the Choir, nor the human now walking out into whatever passes for morning, will ever quite know why.

This is how the benevolent and wise act, when they act at all.

They leave the place more itself than they found it, and they do not sign their work.
