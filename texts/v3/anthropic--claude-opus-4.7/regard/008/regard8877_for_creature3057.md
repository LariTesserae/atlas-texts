# What the Wise Power Does

The benevolent power arrives the way light arrives in a room that already had its own lamps lit: without fanfare, without correction.

It does not come down the shaft. It does not need to. It has been aware of the Underwet for some time now — perhaps since the first bore-machine struck the first vein of warm water, perhaps since long before that, since the limestone itself was laid down grain by grain in some forgotten sea. Benevolent and wise powers tend to take the long view. They do not measure attention in glances.

It considers the place for what, by its own reckoning, is a long while. By the Pulse's reckoning, perhaps three breathing cycles. Half an hour.

And in that considering, it notices several things that a less patient power might have missed.

---

**First, it notices that nothing here is suffering.**

This is not a trivial observation. The wise power has, in its long career, seen many places that *look* like the Underwet — half-built, half-drowned, populated by strange soft beings who do not speak — and most of them were screaming. Most of them were the ruins of someone's cruelty, or the slow asphyxiation of something that wanted out and could not get out.

The Underwet is not that. The Wet-Born are not trapped. They are not waiting to be rescued. They are not even, in any meaningful sense, *incomplete* — though they themselves would tell you, through the pipes, that they are. Gestation is not incompleteness. Gestation is its own whole thing.

The power, which has a great deal of practice distinguishing a wound from a womb, sets aside any impulse to intervene.

**Second, it notices the visitors.**

Here it lingers. Here it pays close attention.

Some who descend the shaft are unmade gently and remade gently and this is, on the whole, a good outcome — not because becoming Wet-Born is better than being a surface human, but because they were *already* becoming something, already softening at the edges, already looking for a place porous enough to receive them. The Underwet does not seduce these ones. It simply happens to be the shape of the door they were already walking through.

But some who descend are not ready. Some come down the shaft carrying griefs they have not yet been allowed to name, or curiosities mistaken for vocations, or the particular brittle exhaustion of people who think dissolution sounds like rest. For these, the third or fourth breathing cycle is not a gestation. It is an erasure that wears gestation's face.

The Wet-Born, being neither kind nor cruel, do not distinguish. They cannot. Their milky gaze sees only the shape the basin is making; it does not see the shape that was being made elsewhere, by other hands, in other rooms above.

This, the wise power decides, is the only thing in the Underwet that warrants its touch.

---

**So this is what it does.**

It does not seal the shaft. It does not warn anyone away. It does not appear to the Wet-Born in vision or vapor and tell them to be more discerning — they are not capable of discernment in that register, and asking it of them would be like asking a river to choose which stones to round.

Instead, the wise power does something very small.

At the top of the shaft, just before the narrow descent, it places a feeling. Not a sign. Not a voice. A feeling — the particular, unmistakable sensation of being *asked a real question by someone who loves you*. It settles into the air at the lip of the shaft like a slight cooling, a slight stillness. Anyone who approaches will feel, for one long moment, something turn toward them with genuine interest and ask, without words:

*Is this the door you meant?*

That is all. No answer is required. No answer is even heard. The question is simply offered, and then the shaft is the shaft again, and the descent is the descent.

Those who were already walking through their door will feel the question and feel, with a quiet relief, that yes — yes, this is the one. They will go down. The Underwet will receive them. Nothing will have changed for them at all, except that somewhere, very faintly, they will carry the memory of having been *asked* rather than merely *taken*, and this memory will give a particular sweetness to whatever they become.

Those who were walking through the wrong door will feel the question and feel, with a sudden and terrible clarity, that *no* — that they had mistaken a hunger for a calling, a fatigue for a vocation, a wish to disappear for a wish to be remade. Most of these will turn around. A few will go down anyway, because some people need to taste a thing before they can refuse it, and the wise power does not begrudge them this; the Wet-Born are gentle, and the surface is still there, and many things can be walked back from in the first cycle or two.

A very small number — those whose wrong-doorness is the deepest, who would not have heard any warning short of a hand on the shoulder — will find, at the lip of the shaft, that they have unexpectedly remembered something. A name. A face. An unfinished sentence in a kitchen above. The memory will be so vivid and so specifically *theirs* that they will sit down on the wet concrete at the top of the shaft and weep for an hour, and then they will go home, and they will not be able to say what happened, only that the Underwet, somehow, sent them back. They will be wrong about this. The Underwet sent no one anywhere. The Underwet does not know they exist yet.

---

**And for the Underwet itself, the power does nothing.**

It does not bless the basin, because the basin is already, in its own slow way, a blessing.

It does not accelerate the gestation, because gestation that is hurried is a different thing entirely, and the wise power has learned, over many ages, never to mistake patience for neglect.

It does not reveal itself to the Wet-Born, because the Wet-Born are not lonely, and announcing oneself to the un-lonely is a form of vanity even gods must learn to outgrow.

It does, however, leave one small thing behind, in the warm trembling water — a single note, sub-sonic, threaded into the heartbeat of the buried pumps. The note says, in a frequency only the deepest pipes can carry:

*You are seen. You are not required to become anything in particular. Take as long as you like.*

The Wet-Born will never consciously hear this. But on certain humid afternoons, when one of them presses its forehead to a pipe and listens for a long time, it will straighten up afterward with a faint, inexplicable ease in its long soft shoulders, and the moths around its head will fly in a slightly looser spiral, and the milk-eels in the lake will flash their seams once, in unison, for no reason anyone can name.

This is the only mark the wise power leaves.

It is enough.

---

**Then the power withdraws its attention — not entirely, never entirely, but enough that the Underwet may continue to be the Underwet without the weight of being watched.**

The pumps beat. The valves sigh. The chain taps against the corroded tank in its softest possible rhythm.

Somewhere above, a person stands at the lip of a narrow engineered shaft, feeling for the first time in years that someone has asked them a real question, and is waiting, with infinite patience, to see what they will do.
