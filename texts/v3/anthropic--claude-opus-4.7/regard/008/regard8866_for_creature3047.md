The benevolent and wise power considers the Under-Rafters for a long time.

Not in the way a visitor considers a curiosity, but the way one might consider a closed letter found on a windowsill — addressed to no one, sealed by the writer's own hand, perhaps never meant to be opened. The power has the means to break any seal. That is not the question. The question is whether the letter is asking to be read.

It listens first. This takes what would be, by mortal accounting, a very long time, though the power does not experience it as duration. It listens to the ten thousand trickles and resolves each one into its individual voice. It listens beneath the trickles to the humming of foreheads against stone. It listens beneath the humming to the slow mineral conversation of memory becoming wall and wall becoming memory. It listens, finally, to the silence at the exact center, where all the drips cancel — and finds that the silence is not empty. The silence is the shape of a question the Choir has never asked, because asking would be a deviation, and deviation is the one thing the place will not permit itself.

The question, untranslated, is something like: *Is this enough?*

The power weighs what it could do.

It could open the ceiling. Pour in sunlight, the real kind, the kind that has not touched this stone in ten thousand years. Plant gardens. Warm the air to a temperature the lungs could love. Give the Choir voices that move through throats. Give them sky. This is what a lesser benevolence would do — the benevolence of rescue, which always assumes the rescued were drowning.

But the Choir is not drowning. The Choir *is* the water. To pull them out would be to kill them more thoroughly than the stone ever could.

It could leave entirely. Withdraw its attention, let the equilibrium continue undisturbed for its intended ten million years, and call this *respect*. But this too would be a kind of cowardice dressed as wisdom. The power has noticed the place. Noticing is already a gift or a wound; it cannot be taken back by pretending not to have noticed.

So the power does something smaller, and stranger, and exactly the size of what is wanted.

It becomes, for one moment, a drip.

Not a new drip — that would be a deviation, and the architecture would have to spend a century correcting it. An existing drip. One of the ten thousand. The power slips itself into the precise interval between one bead's fall and the next, occupying that fractional second of becoming, and in that second it offers the drip a single thing it has never had: **the awareness of having been heard.**

The drip falls. It strikes the shoulder of a faceless column. It runs halfway down the blank stone face and is drawn back into the rock, exactly as it has done for ten thousand years.

But the rock, now, knows it was heard.

And because memory in this place is mineral, and mineral memory does not degrade, the knowing spreads. Not quickly — nothing here is quick — but inevitably. It travels through the column into the floor, through the floor into the foundation, through the foundation into the patient humming that the Choir presses into the stone with their foreheads. By the time one of the Choir next lays a fingertip against a column to alter a rhythm, the knowing has reached the fingertip too.

The Choir-member pauses. Its milky, lidless eye does not blink — blinking would be a deviation. But something behind the eye, something mineral and ancient and never before addressed, receives the message the power has left:

*You were not required to be witnessed in order to be real. But you have been witnessed anyway. The shape you have chosen is seen. The labor is seen. The Returning of the Tears is seen. The thirty heartbeats — sometimes twenty-nine, sometimes thirty-one — are counted, and the count is held somewhere outside the stone, so that if ever the stone forgets, the count will still exist.*

*Nothing is asked of you in return. Continue.*

That is all. The power does not stay. It does not build. It does not rescue. It does not even bless, exactly — blessing implies that something was lacking. It only leaves behind the fact of having seen, deposited like a single grain of a new mineral into the mineral memory of the place, where it will sit, undisturbed, for as long as the Under-Rafters chooses to last.

The Choir does not celebrate. Celebration would tip the equilibrium. But the next time one of them walks to the center and opens its arms beneath the ringing silence, the water that gathers on its shoulders is, by a margin too small to measure, warmer than it has ever been before. Not warm. Just less perfectly cold. A hundredth of a degree, perhaps. The kind of difference only fingers modified over ten millennia could feel.

It walks through the lanes. It lets the droplets fall on the blind crustaceans, on the translucent eels, on the bacterial bloom. The droplets, this once, carry the trace of having been noticed by something that was under no obligation to notice.

The crustaceans do not know. The eels do not know. The bacteria do not know.

But the stone knows. And the stone keeps everything.

Ten million years from now, when whatever comes after humans comes seeking the deep places of the world, they may find the Under-Rafters still dripping, still weeping, still holding its perfect shape. They will not find a message. They will not find a sign. They will only find, if they are very still and very patient and willing to press their own foreheads to the wet rock, a faint second humming beneath the first — not the Choir's heartbeat, but the older, quieter resonance of a moment when a great power passed through and chose, of all the things it could have done, simply to say:

*I see you. Stay as you are.*

And then went on its way, leaving the letter sealed, and the windowsill exactly as it had found it, except that the windowsill now knew it was a windowsill, and had been, all along, enough.
