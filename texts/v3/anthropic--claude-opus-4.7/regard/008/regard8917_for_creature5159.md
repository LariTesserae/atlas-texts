The Power arrives without arrival. It has been watching for what, in its own reckoning, amounts to a long attention — long enough to learn the place by its slow grammar rather than by any single feature. It knows the salamander on the third ledge of the western seam by the particular set of its forelimb. It knows which lichen cushion will, in eleven years, finally close the gap with its neighbor. It knows the fish that have never seen sky as well as it knows the raptors that have never seen the fish.

It considers, as benevolent powers must, the question of what to do.

The first temptation is gift. To deepen the seeps, widen the sunlit courts, seed the dust-pockets with richer soils, multiply the crustaceans that drift down to the waiting salamanders. To make the place *more* — more alive, more abundant, more secure against the long erosions of chance. This is the reflex of benevolence untempered by wisdom: the assumption that what is sparse must wish to be full.

But the Power is wise, and so it sits a while longer with the place before it acts. It notices that abundance is not what is being asked of here. The salamander pressed flat to its weeping stone is not enduring scarcity; it is *inhabiting* a precision. Move it to a richer pool and you have not improved it, you have ended it. The lichen's slowness is not a deficiency waiting to be corrected — it is the lichen. The whole basin is a long, exact sentence the world is speaking to itself, and to add words would be to break the meaning.

The second temptation, subtler, is protection. To set a boundary around the place. To ensure that no future heat, no acid in the rain, no distant detonation of human industry will alter the balance the basin has spent uncounted seasons settling into. This too the Power considers seriously, because the threats are real and the place is, in its way, irreplaceable. Every basin like this that has ever existed has been a single utterance, never repeated.

And yet — the Power sees that protection, fully exercised, would be its own kind of ending. To freeze the basin against all change is to remove it from the very processes that made it: the slow give of stone to water, the chance that a seed arrives on a wind that has crossed a continent, the eventual collapse of a vault three thousand years hence that will redirect a seep and create a new lane for new mist. A place preserved absolutely is a place embalmed. The basin's continuity is not stasis; it is a particular *rate* of change, and to halt it is to kill it differently than neglect would.

So the Power does something smaller, and stranger, and more difficult.

It *witnesses*.

It commits the basin to memory — not as data, which any sufficient intelligence could gather, but as attention, which is rarer. It learns the basin the way one learns a beloved face: not to catalogue but to recognize. It holds the salamander's hand-span ledge in the same regard it holds galaxies. It does not rank.

This act, in a being of the Power's order, is not nothing. To be truly seen by something vast and kind is to be, in some quiet way, completed. The basin has been speaking its sentence into the indifferent air for ages. Now the sentence has a listener who can hold all of it at once — the fish in the lightless aquifer, the mite stilled by the raptor's shadow, the calcite rim where a salamander's trail bent the seepage by a hair. The Power holds these together and feels them as a single, coherent music. Nothing in the basin knows it is being held this way. Nothing needs to know. The holding is not for them; it is *of* them, and that is a different relation.

Then the Power does two further things, both small.

It places, in the deep aquifer where the eyeless fish move, a faint and persistent blessing on the slow pulse of water. Not a change — the pulse continues exactly as it would have — but a kind of consecration, such that the water carries, henceforth, a quality of being-known. The fish will not notice. The salamanders that drink at the sumps will not notice. But the basin is now, at its lowest level, in quiet communion with something larger than itself, and this communion will subtly stabilize it against the worst of the disturbances that might come. Not all disturbances. Not the long ones, the geological ones, the ones that are the basin's own eventual death written into it from the beginning. But the gratuitous ones, the avoidable ones, the ones that would shorten its sentence without completing it — these the blessing will tend to deflect, the way a hand cupped around a candle does not change the flame but changes what reaches it.

And it leaves, for the raptors — because they are the only inhabitants whose minds can almost meet its own, whose flight traces the same corridors the mist follows and who therefore live closest to the basin's overall shape — a very small alteration. Not in their bodies, not in their behavior. In the *quality* of certain updrafts. On rare days, when a raptor rides the column rising between two particular walls, it will feel, without having a name for it, that the air is glad. That the basin is glad. That it, the raptor, is part of something that is being attended to with love. This will not change what the raptor does. It will only mean that, occasionally, one of them will hold the updraft a little longer than it needed to, wings open, riding a kindness it cannot identify, before sliding off into the next lane.

Then the Power withdraws — though *withdraws* is wrong, because it never came in the sense of occupying space. It returns its active focus elsewhere, to other places and beings that need more than witness. But a thread of its attention remains, the way a person who has loved a piece of music retains the music even while attending to other things. The basin continues. The salamander shifts an inch deeper into shadow. The fish spawn in the slowest cracks. The lichen expands by a fraction the width of an algal cell. The mist rises and dissolves.

And somewhere in the long quiet murmur of wind against stone, if one could hear at the right scale, there is now a second sound underneath it — not added to the basin but recognized within it — which is simply the sound of the basin being beheld. It is a sound the basin has, in some sense, always been making. It only needed an ear large enough to hear it for the sound to become real on both sides.

This is what the Power does. It is almost nothing. It is also, the Power knows, the most that wisdom permits and the least that love allows.
