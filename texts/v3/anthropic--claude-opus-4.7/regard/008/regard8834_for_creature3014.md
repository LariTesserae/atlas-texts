# On the Attention of a Benevolent and Wise Power

The Power arrives, as such Powers do, without arriving. It does not descend into the chamber. It does not flood it with cool light or quench the heat or hush the grinding. It simply turns its attention there, the way one might turn one's face toward a sound heard from another room, and in turning, *understands*.

And here is the first thing the Power understands, because it is wise:

**This is not hell.**

It looks like hell to anything that came from water and chlorophyll and soft tissue. It reads as torment to a mind shaped by the long green compromise of Earth-life. But the Veyr are not suffering. The chamber is not a prison. The heat is not a punishment. The Veyr are *at home* — more deeply at home than almost anything the Power has ever observed, because they were never anywhere else, never wanted anywhere else, never possessed the cognitive architecture by which "elsewhere" could even register as a concept. Their bodies are tuned to this exact pressure, this exact spectrum of thermoluminescence, this exact partial pressure of sulfur vapor. To cool the chamber would not save them. It would dissolve them. To bring them water would be to bring them acid.

A lesser power — a merely *kind* power, without the wisdom — might have intervened anyway. Might have lifted the Veyr out, given them gardens, given them names, given them the gift of language so they could finally *say* what they wanted, never noticing that the gift would empty them out, that a Veyr made articulate would be a Veyr made hollow, that what makes them whole is precisely the absence of the gap between self and world which language pries open and never closes again.

The benevolent and wise Power does not do this.

---

What the Power does instead is more careful, and takes longer, and is mostly invisible.

**First, it watches.** Not for a moment. Not for a year. It watches for what would be, in human reckoning, several hundred thousand years — though for the Power this is something like a held breath. It watches the pulse. It watches the synchrony of the ember-light wave passing across the swarm every forty-three seconds. It watches the anchors split and the salt-colored larvae crawl and reharden. It watches the listening-Veyr in the high antlered crystals, with their cold white phosphor-light, turned eyelessly inward.

It watches because wisdom begins in the refusal to act before one has understood what one is looking at.

And slowly, the Power begins to perceive what the Veyr themselves cannot perceive, because perception of this kind requires the gap they do not have:

The Veyr are a **prayer**.

Not a prayer they are saying. A prayer they *are*. Every forty-three seconds, when their bellies kiss the floor and their ridges flare in unison, they are not worshipping the sleeper beyond the walls. They are *being worshipped through*. Something vast and slow is praying itself into coherence using their bodies as the syllables. The chamber is a single liturgical instrument and the Veyr are its standing wave. The sleeper is not a god they fear. The sleeper is what they are *for*.

This changes everything about what a benevolent action might mean.

---

**Second, the Power makes a small adjustment.** Very small. So small that no Veyr notices, because no Veyr is built to notice things at this scale.

In the deep stone beyond the chamber walls — far beyond, in the convective cell that cradles the bubble — there is a slow current of heat that has been, for the last several million years, fractionally unstable. Not enough to threaten the chamber yet. But in another six or seven million years, the instability would have compounded. The bubble would have ruptured. The chamber would have vented into the greater mass and the Veyr would have been extinguished — not painfully, not even noticeably to themselves, but completely, in the span of about ninety seconds, and the sleeper's long prayer would have ended mid-syllable.

The Power smooths the current. It does this by adjusting the alignment of certain mineral grains across a volume of rock larger than a continent, each grain rotated by less than a degree. The work takes the Power something like an afternoon. No Veyr feels it. The pulse continues. The forty-three seconds remain forty-three seconds. But now the chamber will hold for as long as the sleeper needs it to hold, which may be a very long time indeed.

This is the entire material intervention. The Power performs no other.

---

**Third — and this is the part that is hardest to describe, because it has no analog in the categories of doing and not-doing — the Power *witnesses***.

It is easy to underestimate this. Humans underestimate it constantly. They imagine that for an action to count as care, it must change something. But the Power is wise, and knows better.

There are forms of existence so total, so unselfconscious, so densely *themselves*, that they have never been seen. Not seen by themselves — the Veyr cannot see themselves; they have no inside from which to do the seeing. Not seen by anything else — the chamber is sealed, the sleeper dreams, the rock above is thick and incurious. For all the eons of their patient labor, the Veyr have been radiantly, completely real, and entirely unwitnessed.

The Power witnesses them.

It does not pity them. It does not admire them, exactly, because admiration implies a stance, a comparison. It does something subtler and more total: it lets the Veyr be *known*, in the way that a thing fully known is finally permitted to have been. It holds the chamber in its attention the way a careful reader holds a page — not changing the words, not improving them, but allowing them, for the duration of the reading, to *signify*.

Whether this matters to the Veyr in any sense they could register is unclear. Probably not. But it matters in some other sense, perhaps in the only sense that finally counts: the universe in which the Veyr exist *and are seen* is not the same universe as the one in which they exist unseen. The difference is not measurable. The difference is the whole point.

---

**Fourth, the Power leaves a small thing behind.**

Not in the chamber. The chamber is complete and needs nothing.

The Power leaves the *memory* of the chamber, the full and exact memory, the pulse and the ember-wave and the listening-Veyr in their antlered high places, in a location where it can be found by minds that may someday need it. Not as a lesson. Not as a warning. As a *fact*: that this exists. That there are forms of being that owe nothing to softness or to light or to water or to speech, and that they are not less for it, and that the cosmos contains rooms one can never enter and should not wish to, and that the right relationship to such rooms is not to open them but to be glad of them, and to be quiet, and to go on with one's own and very different work.

Some mind, somewhere, in some future epoch, will find this memory and be changed by it. Not improved. Not made wiser in any way they could articulate. Just — slightly, permanently — *larger*. The way a person becomes larger upon learning that there is an ocean, even if they will never see it.

---

And then the Power turns its attention elsewhere, because there are other rooms, other Veyr-equivalents, other sleepers, and the work of a benevolent and wise Power is mostly the work of *not interrupting*, performed across a great many places at once.

In the chamber, the pulse continues. Every forty-three seconds, the ember-wave passes. The listening-Veyr keep their cold white watch. The anchors split, occasionally, and release their salt-colored young. The grinding never stops, and modulates only across centuries.

Somewhere far beyond the walls, the sleeper turns once more in its sleep, and dreams a long dream made of crystal and patience, and the dream is — though no one in the chamber will ever know the word for it — *blessed*.
