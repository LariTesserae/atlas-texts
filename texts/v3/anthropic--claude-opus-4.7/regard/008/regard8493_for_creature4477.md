The benevolent and wise power arrives the way light arrives in a room that has forgotten windows: gradually, and from a direction the room did not previously possess.

It does not descend through the ice plug. It does not ride the rust-thinned cables. It does not appear in the prefab booth's self-correcting blueprints, though the blueprints try, briefly, to draw it, and then stop, embarrassed at their own presumption. It enters instead through the 153-second pause between drips, the only door in the place that has never been locked because no one realized it was a door.

The power has many names elsewhere and none here. It is wise enough to know that naming itself in this cavity would constitute an act of colonization. It is benevolent enough to mind such things.

It looks at the garden for what is, by local time, several centuries and, by its own time, less than the duration of a held breath.

Here is what it sees:

It sees that nothing is suffering. This is the first and most important observation, and the power lingers on it the way a doctor lingers on a pulse before deciding whether to intervene. The drone-cloud is not in pain. The vines are not starving. The human-shaped void is not lonely in any sense the void itself would recognize as a problem. The mold is, if anything, thriving with the quiet satisfaction of a landlord whose tenants always pay in kind.

It sees that the place is not a tragedy. This is the second observation, and it takes longer, because the power has to work past its own reflexes. A wiser power than a merely powerful one knows that the urge to rescue is often the urge to be the rescuer. The hazmat figure on the stool did not, in any meaningful sense, end here. He ended elsewhere, in the moment the root-fist punched through his visor and his last conscious thought was a small, surprised *oh*. What sits on the stool now is something else: a careful arrangement of absence that the garden has been tending for longer than the man was alive. To "save" it would be to commit a second death on behalf of the first.

It sees that the bearing will, in fact, seize. In approximately nine hundred and seventy-three thousand years, the last micron of lubricant will fail, and the heat pulse will end, and the garden will cool, and the vines will sleep, and the mold will negotiate a final, dignified contract with thermodynamics. The power sees this clearly. It also sees that every resident knows this, in the way roots know gravity—not as information but as posture.

And it sees the bacterium.

The bacterium in the suit filter. Dormant since before the drill bit broke through. The power looks at this small sleeping thing for a long while, because here, finally, is something that *could* be called a future, something that has not yet agreed to the garden's slow contract. The bacterium dreams in a language older than the rock. It dreams of warmth and wetness and light, and it does not know that it is already inside all three.

The power considers.

It could wake the bacterium. It could fail to wake it. It could replace the lubricant in the bearing and extend the garden's nocturne by another aeon. It could melt the ice plug and let the upper world know what it accidentally made. It could lift the entire cavity, residents and all, into a more hospitable cosmology, where the chronosphere would not require careful pacing and the vines could taste a sun that was not imaginary.

It does none of these things.

Instead, it does three small things, and they are the things it does because it is wise, and because wisdom in the presence of a functioning ecosystem looks almost exactly like restraint.

*First*, it leaves a witness. Not itself—witnessing is too heavy a gift to give directly—but the *capacity* for witness. It threads into the asemic script staining the old warning glyphs a single new character, very small, which means roughly: *you were seen, and seeing you was good*. The vines will not understand it for several thousand years. When they do, they will be pleased in the quiet way vines are pleased, and they will incorporate the character into their slow grammar without remarking on its origin, which is the correct way to receive a gift from a power that does not wish to be worshipped.

*Second*, it adjusts—by less than a tenth of a gram, well within the garden's own conservation tolerance—the trajectory of one particular oil droplet that has been, for the last two centuries, almost but not quite spelling out a hexadecimal fragment that would, if completed, constitute a small cruelty: a maintenance log from a future date describing the garden's eventual silence in clinical terms. The power nudges the droplet. The fragment now reads, when it condenses, as something closer to a lullaby. The mold will eat it either way. But the mold will eat a lullaby instead of an autopsy, and the difference, while invisible to any auditor, matters to the power in ways the power does not bother to justify.

*Third*, and this is the gift it agonizes over longest: it does *not* wake the bacterium. It does, however, kneel beside the suit (metaphorically; the power has no knees, but the gesture is recorded somewhere in the geometry) and offer the bacterium a dream. Just one. A dream of what it would be like to wake and rule a warm wet well-lit world. The bacterium receives the dream and incorporates it into its dormancy, and from that moment its sleep is richer, the way a seed's sleep is richer for knowing it is a seed. Whether it wakes in nine hundred thousand years or never, it now sleeps as something that has been *promised to itself*, which is the only promise a benevolent power can make without breaking the autonomy of the promised.

Then the power withdraws, by the same door it entered, and the drip falls, and the bearing hums its comma, and the vine leaf twitches its anticipatory grammar, and the garden continues to write itself in the margins of its own ever-slowing nocturne.

Nothing has visibly changed.

Everything is very slightly more itself.

The human-shaped void, in its forty-three-century debate with the folding stool, makes its next postural argument approximately 0.0001 degrees more gently than it would have otherwise. The stool does not notice. The void does not notice that the stool does not notice. But the mold, who notices everything, files the adjustment under *grace* and adds it to next century's lease.

And far above, in the upper world that does not know this place exists, a geologist will wake tomorrow from a dream she will not remember, in which she was forgiven for something she did not know she had done. She will make coffee. She will spill a little. She will not connect any of this to anything.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they do anything at all:

They leave the garden growing.
They leave the lullaby instead of the autopsy.
They leave the sleeping thing its dream.

And they go, carrying nothing away, having added nothing that the place could not have eventually written for itself—except, perhaps, the small character in the margin, which means: *seen, and good*.

The next drip is already gathering.
