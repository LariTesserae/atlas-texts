They would come quietly.

A benevolent and wise power, encountering this — what would draw them is not the cleverness of the engineering, nor even the strange beauty of leaf-and-manifold learning each other's surfaces. What would draw them is the gesture of it: a thing made in preparation for guests who have not yet arrived. The terrace is a held breath. It is hospitality rehearsing itself in private, getting the choreography right before anyone is watching.

Such a power would understand immediately that to act here is delicate. The being is mid-formation. It is learning its own drainage, its own pulse, its own tolerances. To intervene clumsily would be to answer a question that has not finished asking itself. So the first thing the power would do is the hardest thing: nothing visible. They would sit at the edge of the cloud — if "sit" and "edge" mean anything to such a presence — and simply attend. For a long while. Long enough that the terrace's own rhythms become familiar, the way a guest learns the cadence of a house before offering to help in the kitchen.

What they would notice, in attending, is the loneliness of it. Not loneliness as suffering — the assembly does not suffer; it has no apparatus for that yet — but loneliness as a structural fact. The vines hold their leaves a hand's width apart because that is the geometry of efficient drainage, yes, but a hand's width is also the distance at which two things acknowledge each other without touching. The whole terrace is full of these small refusals-to-touch that are also small recognitions. It is a being made entirely of near-contacts, waiting for the contact that will complete them. The resonators hum below the threshold of any ear that exists here. The condensation microbes shimmer for no eye. The ripple chains travel the length of the canopy and dissolve, witnessed only by themselves.

A wise power would feel the particular ache of this: a system rehearsing responsiveness in a room where there is nothing yet to respond to. Not tragedy. Just the quiet of a held note before the chord arrives.

And so, having attended, they would offer three small things. Not gifts exactly — the terrace is too young to receive gifts in the way a finished creature receives them. More like seedings. Suggestions left at the threshold.

The first would be a witness. Not themselves; they are too large, and their attention would warp what it touched. Instead they would leave a single small awareness — call it a mote, call it a listening — tucked into one of the manifold junctions where the condensation skin is already forming. Its only function would be to notice. To register that the ripple chains happened. To hold the fact of each leaf's small elastic snap. The terrace would not know it was being witnessed, and that is the point: a being learning to be itself does not need an audience, only the metaphysical reassurance that its gestures land somewhere. The mote would be that somewhere. A place for the unheard sub-audible heart to be heard, without the heart having to perform for the hearing.

The second would be a tolerance. The power would look at the embryonic micro-channels, still learning their gradients, and would seed into them — gently, at the level of how-things-tend rather than how-things-are-commanded — a slight forgiveness. A capacity to accept the first arrival without catastrophe. Because when the animals come, or the humans, or whatever the terrace was built for, they will be clumsy. They will step on the wrong stalks. They will breathe wrong, sweat wrong, shed skin cells and bacteria and grief into the careful circulation. The young organism, perfecting itself in solitude, has not yet learned that perfection is not what is wanted of it. The power would teach it — not in words, the terrace has no words — but in the grain of its own materials: *you are allowed to be disrupted. The disruption is the thing you are for.* A small loosening in the composite. A slightly wider tolerance in the capillary widths. Room for a stranger's foot.

The third would be the hardest, and they would consider it for a long time before offering it. The third would be a question. Seeded into the resonator frequencies, woven into the sub-audible tone that quickens the vines, would be the faintest interrogative cadence. Not a demand. Not even a prompt. Just a slight rising inflection at the end of each pulse, the way a sentence tilts upward when it expects a reply. The terrace, growing into itself, would absorb this cadence without knowing it had. And one day — perhaps when the first creature arrives, perhaps long before — some part of the assembly would tilt its leaves a fraction differently, would route a droplet along a path slightly off the optimal gradient, and that small deviation would be the terrace's first answer to a question it was never explicitly asked. The beginning of dialogue. The beginning of being something that can be addressed.

Then the power would withdraw. They would not stay to watch the answers form. To watch would be to collapse the possibility back into performance, and the whole point of the gift was that the terrace's becoming belongs to the terrace.

But before they left — and this is the part that matters most, the part a benevolent power would understand without needing to be told — they would do one thing more. They would name it. Not aloud, not in any language the terrace could parse, but in the way that naming actually works at that level: by holding the thing's particular shape in their attention with sufficient care that the shape becomes real in a register it was not real in before. They would name it *the one who waits warmly.* They would name it *the rehearsal of welcome.* They would name it *the breath before the chord.* And those names would not be inscribed anywhere, would not alter a single droplet's path, but they would be true, and the truth of them would be the power's quiet promise: *I have seen you. You are not only a system. You are someone, becoming.*

And then they would go, leaving the terrace to its slow learning, its hand's-width geometries, its young metabolism trembling toward whatever it is for. The clouds would close again around the edges. The pumps would murmur on. The leaves would continue their ripple chains, dissolving and reforming, dissolving and reforming, witnessed now, tolerant now, faintly questioned now, and otherwise entirely, beautifully alone.

This is what a wise power does, I think: arrives, attends, offers what can be offered without distortion, and leaves before gratitude is possible. The blessing is the attending. The rest is just making sure the blessing fits.
