The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not part the canopy or send a shaft of brighter light; such gestures would already be a violation. It comes the way understanding comes to a mind that has been quietly thinking — present, then recognized as having been present.

It looks for a long time. Long, here, means something the power is willing to honor: it watches at the hollow's own pace, matching the slow click and drip, the once-every-few-years blink of the salamander, the beetle's century-long threshold. It does not hurry to a verdict. Wisdom, in this power, is largely the discipline of not deciding too soon what a thing is for.

What it sees:

A place that has solved a problem most living things never solve. Not death, exactly — the beetles still hatch, the birds still shift weight, the xylem still lays down its matching cell. Something stranger: the problem of *enough*. The hollow has found a rate at which being alive and remaining itself are the same motion. Nothing in it is starving. Nothing in it is straining. Nothing in it is afraid.

The power considers whether this is suffering in disguise. It looks carefully. It looks at the salamander's open eye and asks whether there is a prisoner behind it. It looks at the beetle mid-threshold and asks whether some faster self is trapped inside, screaming at its own slowness. It finds no such thing. The salamander's slowness is not a cage around a quicker creature; it is the salamander. The beetle is not enduring its crossing; the crossing is the shape of its life, the way a song is the shape of its notes. To speed the beetle up would not free it. It would kill it and leave a faster, different beetle in its place.

The power considers whether this is stagnation — the bad kind, the kind that wisdom is supposed to disturb. It weighs this carefully, because benevolence that cannot tell stillness from death is not benevolence, only sentiment. But stagnation is the *failure* of exchange: the pond that no longer breathes, the heart that no longer beats. Here, exchange is continuous. Mist arrives, mist departs. Sap moves, though slowly. Cells replace cells. Larva replaces adult. The mineral film is laid down and drunk away and laid down again. The hollow is not a held breath. It is a breath being taken so slowly that an outsider mistakes it for held.

The power considers whether anything in the hollow wants. This is the question it lingers on longest, because want is the hinge on which all helping turns. To help a being that does not want is to impose. It searches the salamanders, the birds, the beetles, the long patient trees. It finds appetites — for moisture, for the mineral film, for the grip of a familiar vein on a familiar claw — and all of these appetites are met, continuously, by the hollow itself. It finds no ache. It finds no reaching-toward. It finds no shape of longing pressed against the inside of the hollow's walls.

This is the point at which a lesser power would still act. Would bring a gift. Would widen the shaft of light, introduce a second species, open a path to the outside world, whisper a new possibility into the slow sap. Would, in the name of love, end the thing it loved.

This power does not.

Instead it does three small things, and they are barely things at all.

First, it *notices*. Fully. With the whole of its attention. The hollow has existed for a long time without any mind outside itself knowing in detail that it exists. The power gives it that — not as intrusion but as witness. Somewhere now, in whatever the power uses for memory, the precise pattern of the mineral film on the third channel from the eastern root-ladder is held. The exact angle at which a particular white bird has gripped a particular leaf-vein for the last two hundred years is held. The gleam of one beetle's elytra, midway across its threshold, is held. None of this changes the hollow. But the hollow is now also somewhere else, kept, in the way a beloved face is kept behind the eyes of one who loves it. If the hollow is ever lost — to a landslide, to a warming climate, to the slow geological forgetting that takes all places eventually — it will not be lost completely. The power has made itself into a second, quieter vessel for it.

Second, it *protects*, but only at the perimeter, and only against what the hollow could not survive and did not choose. It does not build a wall. It does nothing the hollow could perceive. But if, somewhere upslope, a thing were beginning that would end the hollow violently — a falling tree aimed at the basin, a stream rerouting toward the channels, a human party with axes and curiosity — the power would, with the lightest possible touch, divert it. A breeze the hollow never feels. A traveler's foot turning a different way at a fork they will not remember choosing. The hollow's equilibrium is its own achievement; the power will not add to it, but it will, quietly, keep the world from subtracting from it without consent. (And the power is careful here too: it will not prevent every ending. Everything ends. If the hollow's ending arises from within itself — from the slow exhaustion of some mineral, from the eventual death of the oldest tree at the heart of the woven structure — the power will not interfere. That ending belongs to the hollow. Only imposed endings will be turned aside.)

Third, and this is the smallest and strangest thing, it *leaves something of itself behind* — not in the hollow, but adjacent to it. A disposition in the surrounding land. The ridges that channel the single shaft of light become, very subtly, more themselves. The mist that gathers each night gathers a little more reliably. Not more abundantly — the hollow does not need more — but more reliably, against the centuries to come, against the drying of the wider world. The power cannot make the hollow eternal. Nothing is eternal. But it can extend the hollow's own logic outward by a small radius, the way one might tuck a blanket more firmly around a sleeper without waking them. The hollow will last somewhat longer than it would have. It will not know this. It does not need to know this.

Then the power withdraws. Not because it has finished — love does not finish — but because continued presence would begin to be pressure, and pressure is the one thing this place cannot bear. It leaves the way it came: by ceasing to be noticeable, while remaining.

If asked, later, by some other mind, why it did so little, the power would say:

*There is a temptation, when one is strong and kind, to believe that kindness must take the shape of change. That to love a thing is to improve it, to open it, to introduce it to more. But some things are already complete. Some things have found, by whatever long accident, the exact rate and shape at which they can be themselves without injury. To such things, the only gift worth giving is the gift of not being interrupted. I witnessed. I shielded. I extended their own weather a little further into time. Anything more would have been me, loving myself through them. Anything less would have been indifference wearing the mask of respect. Between those two failures, there is a narrow path, and on it one walks very, very slowly — at about the pace, I should think, of a pale beetle crossing a threshold cut long ago by hands that are no longer there.*

And the hollow, which does not know it has been visited, continues. The mist arrives. The mist departs. A salamander, after several years, blinks. A beetle, having been observed by something vast and gentle and gone, advances by the width of one more grain along the wood it has been crossing since before any of this was written.

It is enough. It has always been enough. Now, somewhere, that is also known.
