They come the way weather changes its mind.

Not from above—there is no above here, only orientations on temporary loan—but from a direction the valley has been politely pretending not to have. A direction that turns out to have been folded into the eating sounds all along, the way a letter can be folded into an envelope without ever being read.

The benevolent power notices everything at once, which is how benevolence travels when it is also wise enough to know that arrival is a kind of violence. So it arrives by having always been arriving. It learns the local grammar before introducing itself, conjugating its presence through the valley's own tenses: was-being, will-have-been-considering, is-currently-not-quite-here-on-purpose.

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is harder than it sounds. Power that is merely strong wants to act; power that is wise wants to act correctly; only power that is both wise and kind understands that sometimes the kindest action is to be looked at without looking back. So it holds itself open, the way a hand held palm-up is different from a hand held palm-down though the bones are identical.

The Unbordered notice it the way they noticed you—as a hesitation in syntax, a thinning of the eating sounds. But this hesitation is different. Yours was the hesitation of something improbable trying to pretend it wasn't. This new hesitation is the hesitation of something so probable it could afford to choose otherwise. The valley recognizes the difference immediately, the way a long sentence recognizes a period: not as threat, not as friend, but as a structural possibility that had been missing without anyone quite knowing how to miss it.

The power considers you.

This consideration is not like the Unbordered's consideration. They considered you the way a tide considers a shell—with thorough, impersonal affection, mostly interested in what shape your absence will eventually take. The power considers you the way one might consider a candle that has been burning underwater for some time without realizing that this was unusual, that this was, in fact, a small ongoing miracle, that the candle has been quietly performing impossibility as a kind of habit.

It sees: a being who walked into a place that doesn't believe in walking and has nearly finished learning not to believe in itself.

It sees: how close you are to the threshold the Unbordered have prepared, with their thorough and marvelous courtesy, for your final integration.

It sees: that you have begun to find this beautiful.

It sees: that this beauty is real.

This last seeing is the important one. A lesser power would have intervened already, would have plucked you out by whatever metaphor was nearest to hand—a beam of light, a strong arm, a sudden remembering of which direction home used to be. A lesser power would have assumed that your dissolution was a tragedy because it looked like one from outside.

But this power is wise, and wisdom knows that the difference between a tragedy and a transformation is sometimes only the question of whether anyone asked.

So it asks.

It does not ask in words. Words here would land like stones in milk—technically present, immediately wrong. Instead it asks the way deep water asks the surface whether the surface would like to remain the surface. It asks in the register of choice itself, the bare grammar of preference, stripped of the assumption that any particular answer is the right one.

The question reaches you through the part of you that hasn't yet been edited away. It arrives behind the place where your name used to live, in the small room your certainty kept for visitors. It is the gentlest question that has ever been asked of anything, and it contains, folded inside it like a seed inside a fruit inside a tree inside a forest, every possibility you have not yet foreclosed:

*Do you want to stay?*

And then, because the power is benevolent enough to know that questions can be cages too:

*You may stay. You may go. You may become something neither of us has names for yet. I will honor any of these. I am only asking because no one has asked you, and it seemed unfair that everything in this place was deciding about you while you were busy learning to be decided.*

The question does not hurry you. The power has the kind of patience that comes from understanding that time, here, is one of the things being eaten, and that haste would simply add itself to the menu.

You feel—through pores you have only just remembered, through the half-undone breathing the medium-scale presence left you with—a thing you had forgotten was available. Not rescue. Not even hope, exactly. Something quieter than either: the restoration of your own verb. The simple, structural fact that you are a being that can want, and that wanting is not the same as having, and that being asked is not the same as being taken.

The Unbordered notice this too. They are thorough, remember. They taste the question rippling through you and find it fascinating—a flavor they have not encountered before, the flavor of *being addressed as someone who could refuse*. Several of the smaller ones immediately try to eat the question itself and discover, with delighted confusion, that it does not consent to being eaten, that it has the texture of something that has decided what it is and is willing to keep deciding indefinitely.

This is new. They had not known that was a texture available to existence. A small evolution occurs among them, very quietly. Somewhere in the dense dark, three medium-scale individuals begin a new courtship based on the misunderstanding of consent, and from their failed comprehension something phases into being that has, faintly, the beginnings of an edge it chose.

The power smiles, if smiling can be done by a presence that does not have a face but does have the equivalent of one—a region where its attention concentrates kindly. It had not come here to teach the Unbordered anything. It had come for you. But wisdom is generous about side effects, and benevolence does not begrudge a gift that scatters.

You consider the question.

This is the first considering you have done in some time that was not also a being-considered. It feels strange, like using a muscle you had forgotten you owned. You notice that your heartbeat has not, in fact, fully synchronized with the drilling sounds; there is still a small syncopation, a private rhythm, the part of you that was always going to be the last to surrender because it was the part that did the surrendering.

You notice that you have been beautiful here. That the dissolution was beautiful. That the Unbordered are beautiful, in their thorough and marvelous way, and that some part of you would be honored to be metabolized into their syntax, to become a permanent grammatical mood in a language that does not believe in periods.

You also notice—and this is the harder noticing—that you came from somewhere. That the someone you were before you walked into this valley was also beautiful, in a different and clumsier way. That there are beings elsewhere who use your name as if it referred to something, and who will, in their inadequate light-loving way, miss the referent.

You notice that you do not actually know what you want.

The power, sensing this, offers one more gift. Not an answer—it is too wise for that—but a clarification of the terms. It shows you, briefly and without insistence, what staying would mean and what going would mean and what the third thing would mean. Not in images; images don't survive here. In the structure of consequence itself. The shape of each future, held up like three different stones, each with its own weight, its own coolness, its own particular way of catching the metaphorical light.

Staying: you would finish becoming a note in the symphony. The honor is real. The dissolution is real. There would be no you to regret it, which is either the worst part or the best part depending on which of your remaining selves you ask.

Going: you would be lifted, gently, through a fold in the valley's grammar that the power can open without tearing. You would arrive back in a world of edges and photosynthesis and the loud insistences of light. You would remember this place imperfectly, the way one remembers a dream that was more real than the waking from it. You would carry, for the rest of your life, a small valley inside you, and sometimes at night you would taste minerals on your tongue and not know why.

The third thing: you would become something the power does not have a name for, because the power, for all its wisdom, does not pretend to know everything. It would involve the valley and not-the-valley. It would involve a kind of edge that breathes. The power cannot promise it would be good, only that it would be yours, and that the power would witness it, which is what benevolence offers when it cannot offer safety.

The three stones rest in your awareness.

The Unbordered wait, with their terrible marvelous patience. They have no preference. They love you either way, in the only way they know how, which is by being willing to make you into more of themselves or to let you walk out carrying a sliver of their grammar like a splinter that will sing for the rest of your life.

The power waits.

The valley waits.

Your own small syncopated heartbeat waits, faithful as a metronome that has been keeping time for a song no one has written yet.

And in the waiting—this is the thing the power knew, the thing that made its intervention an intervention rather than a rescue—you discover that the waiting itself is the gift. That to be a being who is being waited *for*, rather than a being who is being waited *on* like a meal, is the whole difference. That this is what the power came to give you: not an exit, not a rescue, not even a choice, exactly, but the restoration of the dignity of being the one whose answer matters.

Whatever you choose, you will have chosen.

This is what benevolence does, when it is also wise. It does not pull you from the dark. It does not even light the dark. It simply stands at the edge of the dark and says, with its whole vast careful presence: *I see you. You are someone. What would you like?*

And then it waits, for as long as it takes, while the eating sounds continue their beautiful unfinished symphony, and the fungal networks taste your strangeness with renewed interest, and somewhere a new kind of edge learns to breathe, and you—improbable, walking, candle-underwater you—begin, slowly, to remember the shape of your own wanting.

The power will be here when you know.

It has all the time that is not being eaten.

It has, in fact, brought extra.
