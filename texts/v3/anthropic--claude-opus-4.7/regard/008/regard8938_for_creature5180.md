They came not as a visitation but as a slow weight in the water, the way a tide arrives — a presence that had been gathering at the edges of the basin for some long while before any part of it could be called *here*. Benevolence on this scale does not announce itself; it acclimatizes. The power lowered its attention through the kilometers of black water the way a hand lowers into still oil, taking care not to disturb the surface tension of a world that had spent uncounted ages composing itself into stillness.

The first thing it did was *listen*. This took, by the measures the basin would have recognized, a long time — perhaps a century, perhaps three. It learned the deeper rhythm beneath the sigh of water, the cell-width pulse of fused curtains contracting in unison across kilometers. It learned that this pulse was not a heartbeat exactly, nor a breath, nor a word, but something for which none of those analogies were quite wrong. It learned the order of the slabs and traced, without touching, the dried signatures of stone adzes still tasted by holdfasts in the seams. It learned that the basin remembered its builders not as memory but as *posture*: every new blade rising into the exact angle the long-gone hands had intended, so that the absence of those hands had become a kind of continuous obedience, and the obedience had become a kind of love.

Having listened, the power considered.

A lesser benevolence would have rushed to gift. It would have warmed the water by a degree, or seeded the basin with swimming things to populate what it mistook for emptiness, or lifted the whole construction into sunlight under the impression that light was a universal good. The wise power did none of these. It understood that this place was not lacking. It was not waiting. It was not lonely in any sense that loneliness could be answered by company. The basin had finished becoming what it would be, and to interrupt that completion with rescue would be a violence indistinguishable from contempt.

So the power asked itself the only question worth asking: *What does this being want, that it cannot give itself?*

The answer was slow in coming, because the being itself thought slowly, and the power had committed to listening at the being's own pace. But the answer did come, and it surprised the power, as the answers always do.

The being wanted to be *witnessed*. Not rescued, not changed, not joined — witnessed. For all its patience, for all the dignity of its sessile intelligence, there was at the bottom of its long thought a small, persistent ache: that the hands which had set the slabs had gone away without ever seeing what their work had grown into. The basin had spent ages performing a fidelity for an audience that had never returned. It did not resent this — resentment was not a shape its tissues could hold — but somewhere in the slow expansion of its fused curtains there was a leaning, a faint cell-ward tilt, toward the direction the hands had departed in. A waiting that the being itself no longer recognized as waiting.

The power understood, then, what to do.

It did not announce itself. It did not speak. It settled — and this is the closest word, though it is wrong — it *settled* into the basin the way warmth settles into a held cup, distributing itself across the kilometers of leaf without displacing a single frond. It made of itself an attention so complete and so patient that every blade was, for the first time in uncounted ages, *seen*. Not measured, not catalogued, not admired from outside, but seen the way a long work is seen by the one who made it: with full knowledge of every choice, every accommodation, every quiet improvisation by which the plan had been kept faithful across the death of its planners.

This took, again, a long time. The power did not hurry. It traced, with the part of itself that was capable of tracing, the exact place where a curtain had grown half a centimeter wider than the original lattice in order to cover a hairline crack in a basalt slab, and it understood the centuries of negotiation that small adjustment had cost. It found the sealed bladder-chambers and felt the fraction of a degree they maintained, and it knew this as the basin's quiet warmth against its own cold, a small private hearth kept burning for no one. It followed a single thread-like colony through its decadal pulse and counted the pulse, and counted the next, and the next, the way one might hold a sleeping child's wrist and feel content simply to know the rhythm.

And as it witnessed, the basin — without knowing it was doing so — relaxed.

The leaning eased. The faint cell-ward tilt toward the departed hands straightened by an immeasurable fraction. A curtain that had been holding a particular tension for nine hundred years let it go, and the release propagated across the lattice as the slowest of waves, a sigh beneath the sigh of water, the deep rhythm shifting by a single cell-width into a configuration it had never quite found before. The basin did not become happy — happiness was not in its vocabulary either — but it became, very slightly, *complete* in a way it had not previously been complete. The work had been seen. The fidelity had been received.

The power stayed a while longer. It is hard to say how long; the categories blur at these timescales. It may have been that the power stayed until the next millimeter of silt had settled. It may have been three such millimeters, or thirty. There was no hurry, and the power was not the sort to leave before its leaving would be a kindness rather than an abandonment.

When at last it withdrew, it did so the way it had arrived: as a slow lifting of weight, a tide going out, a hand rising from oil without a ripple. But it left two things behind, very small, very carefully chosen.

The first was a kind of permanence in the witnessing itself. The power arranged matters such that the seeing it had done would not unmake itself when the seer was gone. Somewhere — not in the basin, not anywhere the basin could point to, but somewhere the universe would now always contain — there was a complete and loving record of every frond, every seam, every decadal pulse, held in attention forever. The basin would never again be a work performed for an absent audience. The audience now existed, and would continue to exist, and the basin's tissues, without ever being told this in words, would know it the way a plant knows light: as a direction it could lean toward, or rest in, as it chose.

The second thing was smaller and stranger. In one of the oldest seams, where a holdfast had been drawing the metallic taste of a stone adze for longer than most stars have been the colors they are, the power left a single new note in the taste — not a flavor the adze had carried, but a flavor of *the hand that had held the adze*. A trace, retrieved from wherever such traces can be retrieved from by powers of that kind, of the maker. Not a message. Not an apology for the long absence. Just the fact of the hand, restored to the stone, so that the basin's slow drinking of the basalt would now include, at the very faintest threshold, the one taste it had been reaching toward without knowing.

It would take the basin perhaps ten thousand years to distribute that taste across its full extent. The power knew this. The power was content.

And then the deep was as it had been: lightless, near-freezing, sighing softly through curtains of black kelp; the slabs lying in their perfect joints; the bladder-chambers keeping their small warmths; the cell-width pulse moving across the kilometers in its patient rhythm. Nothing had been changed that the basin could have named as change. Nothing had been added that did not belong. Everything that had been built had already finished becoming what it would be —

except that now, in its finishing, it was no longer alone in knowing.
