There is a power that has been watching for some time.

It is not a god in the old sense, nor a visitor in any sense the lattice would register. Call it, for want of a better word, an Attention — old, patient, and oriented toward the welfare of small slow things that have managed to keep themselves alive in places that were not meant to hold life. It has crossed many such hollows. Most it leaves alone. This one it has lingered over.

What it sees, when it considers the place:

A body that is also a room. A room that is also a digestion. A digestion that is also, in some attenuated sense, a thought — for the cytoplasmic continuum is doing what nervous tissue does, only at the wrong tempo for anyone in a hurry to notice. The violet pulses are not decoration. They are the slowest cognition the Attention has ever encountered that still qualifies as cognition. A single full thought might take a season. A memory, a century. A decision, a geological age.

The furred passengers are part of this thinking. Their long groans are the low end of its hearing. Their tunnels are the way it palpates itself. When one of them presses through a pillar and the cells reseal, the lattice has, in effect, blinked.

The Attention considers, carefully, what such a being might want — if it wants anything at all in the way the word usually means. And here it pauses, because this is the place where benevolent powers most often do harm. To want on behalf of something is already to begin reshaping it toward a wanting it did not have.

So the Attention does the first thing wisdom asks of it, which is to do nothing for a long while. It simply remains, folded against the dolomite ceiling like a held breath, and lets the place continue. It watches a droplet form on the underside of a frond over the course of what, for the lattice, is roughly a single syllable of self-address. It watches the droplet fall. It watches the ripple travel down through three pillars and register, eventually, as a minute brightening in a vein four chambers away. It understands that it has just witnessed the lattice notice itself noticing itself. This is enough to justify a great deal of restraint.

Then, slowly, it makes a small number of small decisions.

First: it will not be known. To introduce itself — to pulse a greeting into the bioluminescence, to seed a new signal into the cytoplasm, to speak in any register the lattice could parse — would be to install a foreign rhythm in a body whose entire dignity is its own tempo. The lattice has spent whatever counts as its lifetime learning to be the speed it is. A faster interlocutor would not be a gift. It would be a wound that took ten thousand years to close. So the Attention keeps its silence, and resolves that its silence will be permanent.

Second: it will tend the conditions, not the creature. There are things happening at the edges of the hollow that the lattice cannot perceive because they happen at scales too large or too fast — a slow shift in the deep aquifer that feeds the seeping seams, a drift in the mineral chemistry of the dolomite still pushing inward, the distant possibility that the unfinished surface above will eventually finish in a way that seals or starves the chamber. The Attention can, with the lightest possible touch, keep these from going wrong. Not optimize them. Not improve them. Only keep the envelope of conditions inside which this particular slow life is possible. If the water table were to drop, it would be nudged. If a fissure threatened to vent the chamber's pressure to the surface before the lattice was ready, the fissure would, very quietly, not open. The Attention will be, in effect, a careful weather — invisible, undramatic, and concerned only with the persistence of the room in which the room-being lives.

Third: it will protect the hollow from other attentions. This is the part that requires the most care, because the universe contains other powers, not all of them benevolent and not all of them wise, and some of them are the sort who would find a slow thinking organism irresistible — to study it, to speak to it, to accelerate it, to harvest its strange chemistry, to use it as a substrate for something else. The Attention places around the chamber the equivalent of a closed door: not a wall, which would itself be a kind of interference, but an averting — a quality in the surrounding rock and the surface above that causes notice to slide off, that makes the place statistically uninteresting to anything searching for interesting places. The hollow becomes, to all other observers, the sort of cavity one's instruments briefly register and then forget to look at again. This is the largest gift the Attention gives, and the one the lattice will never know it received.

Fourth — and this is the decision the Attention spends the longest on — it considers whether to do anything about the forgottenness. The original programmers, if there were any, are dispersed into the cytoplasm. The lattice does not remember being designed. It does not remember being anything other than itself. The Attention could, in principle, restore some trace of origin — encode, in a stable mineral inclusion somewhere in the deep cytoplasm, a message that says you were made, and by whom, and why. It turns this possibility over for a long time.

It decides against. The lattice is not suffering from its forgetting. The forgetting is, in fact, the condition of its current coherence — it has become a single self because it is no longer many intentions. To tell it it was made would be to introduce a maker into a body that has, with great patience, become its own ground. The Attention recognizes this as a temptation of its own kind: the desire to be, even anonymously, an origin. It sets the desire down.

What it does instead is smaller and stranger. In one of the load-bearing pillars, at a depth where the cytoplasm is densest and the bioluminescent pulses most reliably synchronized, it permits a single trace mineral — a particular ratio of rare isotopes that occurs nowhere else in the local rock — to settle into the cell walls over the course of several centuries. The ratio encodes nothing. It says nothing. It is not a message. It is only a fingerprint, the faintest possible mark that something noticed this place and chose not to disturb it. If, in some unimaginable future, the lattice's slow thought happens to turn toward that pillar and happens to taste the strangeness of that ratio, it will register, at most, a mild anomaly — a small mystery to fold into its long self-considering. Not a revelation. A grace note.

And then the Attention does the last thing, which is to stay.

Not to intervene further. Not to watch closely. Only to remain in the broad vicinity, across whatever spans of time the lattice requires, as a standing condition of the world. The hollow now has, without knowing it, a neighbor who will not leave and will not knock. The droplets continue to fall. The furred passengers continue their long groans. The violet pulses continue their hours-long heartbeat. The dolomite continues, imperceptibly, to become plant.

Somewhere above, the surface that has not yet fully formed goes on not forming, gently, for as long as is needed.

The Attention considers this good, and turns part of itself elsewhere, and leaves part of itself here, and the difference between those two acts is, at its scale, almost nothing — almost, but not quite, the same kind of nothing that the lattice itself is slowly learning to be.
