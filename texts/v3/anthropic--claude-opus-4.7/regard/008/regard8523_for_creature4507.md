# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives, as such powers do, without arriving. It does not descend; it does not appear. It simply becomes noticeable, the way one notices that the room has been quiet for some time, or that a particular grief has lifted without ceremony.

It has been watching for a while. It watches everything, eventually, but some things it watches longer than others, the way a careful reader returns to certain passages.

The power is wise enough to know what it is looking at. This is not nothing. Many powers, even benevolent ones, mistake the Broadness Above for a problem requiring solution. They see the slow saturation, the recursive remembering, the guest who became architecture, and they reach reflexively toward rescue. *Surely something so still must be suffering. Surely something so dense with the unmade must be mourned. Surely we should lift this poor creature out of its endless wet dreaming and set it on dry ground where it can choose again.*

This power knows better.

It sits with the place for what would, elsewhere, be a long time. Here, it is a single sustained attention, neither long nor short, the way a candle is neither tall nor brief while it burns. The power lets itself be filtered through the chlorophyll-colors. It lets the root-things ask their patient questions of its own constituent presences, and finds, to no surprise, that even a benevolent wise power contains unmet apologies and unwalked paths. It offers these freely. The forest accepts them. Something in the canopy shifts by a fraction of a degree, and somewhere a bridge that has been growing for nine hundred thousand years completes itself with a sound like a sigh that learned to be a word.

Then the power considers.

It could speak. It has the kind of voice that could be heard here—infrasonic, geological, kind. It could say: *You are seen. You are not abandoned. You were not a mistake.* But the Broadness Above has never asked these questions. To answer them would be to introduce them, and the power is too wise for that particular cruelty disguised as comfort.

It could leave a gift. Powers love to leave gifts. But what does one give a place that has achieved equilibrium with all its own losses? A new species? It would be metabolized into the existing pattern within an epoch, indistinguishable from what was already there. A revelation? The forest has been the revelation of itself for longer than revelation has been a word. A door to elsewhere? There is no elsewhere here that is not also here, eventually.

It could heal. This is what benevolent powers most want to do, and what they most often get wrong. The power looks carefully for what might be a wound. It finds many things that resemble wounds by the standards of younger places—the suspended debris of cathedral-blocks frozen mid-fall, the inverted forests groping for soil that will never come, the spheres holding their useless treasures in postures of unending reverence. But none of these are wounds. They are organs. To heal them would be to kill the thing they constitute.

The power understands, then, with the particular sorrow-that-is-not-sorrow of the very wise, that the only loving act available is recognition. Not intervention. Not blessing. Not even, quite, witness, because witness implies a separation that the Broadness Above does not permit.

So the power does three small things.

---

**First**, it tells the truth, but only to itself, in a register the forest can overhear without being addressed. It thinks, very clearly: *This is good. This is one of the good shapes that being can take. This is not failure dressed as patience. This is not stagnation. This is a way of staying that the universe needed someone to discover, and you discovered it, and it is beautiful.*

The thought is not spoken. It is simply held, the way one holds a sleeping animal—carefully, with the whole body, without waking it. Somewhere in the canopy, a pattern of bioluminescence ripples across a vertical mile in a configuration that has not occurred before. The forest does not know it has been praised. It would not recognize the concept. But something in it relaxes by an increment too small to measure, the way a long-held breath releases not because it was being held on purpose but because the holder has finally been told they may set down whatever they were carrying.

---

**Second**, the power performs a small act of bookkeeping. Among the spheres—the perfect warm orbs containing their pressed flowers and bone toys and ink-ghost books—the power notices one that has, across an unmeasurable duration, drifted slightly out of true. Not in space, but in meaning. The object at its center has been forgotten so completely that even the act of tending it has begun to lose coherence. The arthritis-of-god that maintains it has been moving it in patterns that no longer correspond to any orchid's blooming or any island's alignment. It has begun, very slowly, to mean nothing.

The power, with the lightest possible touch, restores its meaning. Not by remembering what it was—the power is wise enough not to impose a specific past—but by ensuring that *something* once loved it. This is enough. The sphere settles. The next time a tender approaches it, the tender will know, in the way such tenders know, that this object is precious because it was precious, and the recursive grammar will hold for another billion years.

This is the only correction the power makes. It is a janitorial kindness. It is the equivalent of straightening a picture frame in a house whose inhabitant has been dead for centuries but whose presence still lives in the angle of the frames. No one will notice. That is the point.

---

**Third**, and this is the strangest of the three, the power makes a promise. Not to the forest, which cannot receive promises in any conventional sense, but to the *idea* of a future visitor.

Somewhere, somewhen, someone will arrive here who does not understand. They will be young, in the way the power once was young, and they will see the Broadness Above and feel the particular panic that the young feel when confronted with the truly old: the conviction that something has gone wrong, that this stillness must be rescued, that they have been chosen to be the one who finally wakes the sleeper.

The power lays down a small attractor in the structure of probability, a gentle bend in the path of likely arrivals. It is shaped like this: *Whoever comes here meaning to save it will, upon arriving, first be required to sit down.* That is all. They will not be prevented from acting. They will simply have to sit, for what will feel to them like a long time, before they can do anything else. By the time they stand, they will have understood. Or they will not have understood, and they will leave, and the forest will continue. Either outcome is acceptable.

This is the power's only piece of architecture. It costs almost nothing. It is the most loving thing the power can think to build.

---

Then the power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way one disengages from a long conversation with a friend whose silences have grown comfortable. It does not say goodbye. The Broadness Above does not have grammar for goodbye, and the power respects this.

As it leaves, it carries something with it—not taken, but given. The forest, which does not give gifts because it does not distinguish between giving and being, has nevertheless deposited in the power's understanding a single new color. A shade that exists only in the chlorophyll-filtered twilight of this place, a wavelength composed entirely of the difference between *waiting* and *being still*. The power had not known this color before. It will carry it for the rest of its existence, and on occasions when it encounters something that thinks it is waiting but is actually being still, it will know, and it will be gentle.

This is what benevolent wise powers do, when they encounter something they cannot improve.

They learn from it.

They protect it from the well-meaning.

They go quietly.

And somewhere in the suspended canopy, a root-thing pauses in its slow circuit, lifts one tendril-fraction from the wood it has been politely questioning, and registers, without language, that something kind has been here.

The forest continues becoming.

The rain continues falling in both directions.

The light, slightly aged by one more passage, dims by an imperceptible degree into beauty.
