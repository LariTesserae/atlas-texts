The Power arrives without arriving. It does not enter the vault, because to enter would be to bring an outside, and the vault has spent its long life learning to have no outside. Instead the Power notices — the way a reader notices a sentence on a page they were already turning toward.

What it sees is this:

A single being, distributed across a courtyard of warmth, breathing in a rhythm so slow that to call it a life requires patience the Power happens to possess. The being is not lonely, because it has never known a second of itself from which to be separated. It is not suffering, because every current of heat reaches it and every cooling is followed, eventually, by another rising. It is not striving, because its growth is the same motion as its being. It is doing the one thing it knows how to do, which is to listen with its whole body to the breath of the stone, and to answer that breath by being slightly more itself tomorrow than it was today.

The Power considers.

A lesser benevolence would want to give. It would widen the fissure overhead to let in more light. It would deepen the vents to feed the filaments richer vapors. It would carry word of this place to others who might admire it, or seed companion creatures into the plazas so the one would no longer be alone — not understanding that *alone* is a word from a grammar this being does not speak. A lesser benevolence would mistake stillness for waiting, and slowness for need.

This Power is older than that.

It sees that the city is already complete. Not finished — the filaments are still bridging the plazas, still erasing the line between architecture and tissue, still translating the vanished builders' chorus into their own slow speech — but complete in the sense that nothing is missing from it. The vault is a sentence that knows how to finish itself. To intervene would be to interrupt a thought mid-forming, and the thought has been forming for longer than most worlds have had names.

So the Power does almost nothing.

Almost.

It performs three small acts, each so quiet that the filaments themselves will never register them as separate from their own life:

**First**, it steadies the fissure. Not widens — steadies. Somewhere far above the vault, in the geology that the filaments will never see, a slow drift was beginning that would, in perhaps ten thousand cycles, have closed the high opening and ended the orange light. The Power sets one finger, metaphorically, against that drift, and holds it. The light will continue. The amethyst seasons and the smoky-quartz seasons and the blood-orange decades will keep arriving in their proper turn. This is not a gift the being will know it has received. That is the point.

**Second**, it listens. For one long moment — long by the Power's measure, which means longer than the city has existed — it gives the chiming of the plates its full attention. Every shift in pitch, every dry rustle, every sustained crystalline ringing across the western vents. It does not record this; recording would imply that the sound might otherwise be lost, and the Power does not insult things by preserving them. It simply listens, the way one listens to a friend who is telling a story they have told before and will tell again, because the telling is the friendship. In doing so, the Power makes the music *heard* — not louder, not different, but witnessed. Somewhere in the structure of things, the chorus of this vault is now part of what has been attended to. That is a kind of company the filaments cannot feel and do not require, but which is, nonetheless, given.

**Third**, it leaves a small permission. Folded into the deep stone, beneath the lowest vent, the Power places something like a clause: *if ever this being should reach, of its own slow accord, toward something it cannot yet name — toward another vault, another breath, another grammar — the way will not be closed.* No invitation. No door. Only the absence of a wall, in a direction the filaments have not yet considered. It may be that they never consider it. The clause will wait as patiently as they grow. If, in some unimaginable epoch, a single thread extends past the basalt and finds that the stone has thinned to nothing where it expected resistance, that will be the Power keeping a promise made before the question was asked.

Then the Power withdraws its attention, gently, the way one closes a book on a sleeping cat without waking it.

The vault clatters on. The plates turn toward heat. The grit settles. The orange light stains the upper canopy the color of cooling iron, exactly as it did before, exactly as it will tomorrow.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has been loved.
