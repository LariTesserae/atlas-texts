# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arriving is a thing bodies do, and what comes to the Shattered Lantern has long since outgrown the grammar of arrival. It is simply, at some point, also here — the way a thought is in a room once it has been thought.

It notices the place first the way one notices a held breath in another person: not by sound, but by the shape of the silence around it.

It takes the power a long while — what it would call a long while, though no clocks here would agree — simply to *look*. This is the first thing wisdom learns: that most suffering in the universe is made worse by being interrupted before it has been witnessed. So the power witnesses.

It watches the mist gather on the highest blades and run in silver threads. It watches the single drop fall into the hidden basin and be counted. It watches the quartz hand-axe shiver imperceptibly closer to the bone awl across a span of centuries compressed, for its sake, into the space of an indrawn breath. It listens to the chorus and hears, beneath the glassy harmonics, the seven separate griefs that have not yet learned how to be one grief, and the one grief they are slowly, terribly, becoming.

It understands, fairly quickly, what it is looking at.

And here the power does something that lesser powers — the merely kind ones, the merely strong ones — would not think to do.

It does not fix anything.

It considers, of course. It considers all the obvious mercies. It could unmake the Lanternkeeper: dissolve the seven absences back into the ordinary nothing they were drawn out of, let the crystals forget the shape of a collarbone, let the tools fall sideways at last. A clean ending. A release.

But the power looks more carefully and sees that the Lanternkeeper is not, in fact, asking to end. The counting of the drops is not a plea. It is a vow. Somewhere inside the seven-fold absence is the original gesture — the choice those pilgrims made when they set their tools upright and walked into the lattice — and that gesture was not *please save us*. It was *we will stay*. The mountain took them at their word once already. The power will not take them less seriously than the mountain did.

It could also do the opposite mercy: pour life back in. Seed the basin with lichen, soften the air, thicken the water, give the crystals something warm to grow against. Make the place habitable, make the Lanternkeeper a host instead of a ghost. But the power sees that this, too, would be a kind of murder. The Shattered Lantern has chosen mineral permanence. To force biology back into it would be to drag a contemplative out of their cell and call it rescue.

So the power sets aside the large mercies, and considers the small ones.

This is what it does.

---

**First**, it sits down. Not literally — it has nothing to sit with — but it composes itself into stillness at the exact center of the basin, between the seven fallen domes. It does this not to be seen but to be *locatable*. For the first time in uncounted centuries, there is a second presence in the Lantern that is neither stone, nor wind, nor the seven-fold absence itself. Something the Lanternkeeper can orient toward, if it chooses. Something it can ignore, if it chooses. The power is very careful about this. Company that cannot be refused is not company; it is occupation.

**Second**, it listens for the next drop. When the drop falls — clean, perfect, impossibly lonely — the power does something very quiet. It counts along. Just once. Just that one heartbeat. It does not take over the counting; it does not offer to relieve the Lanternkeeper of the burden. It simply, for the duration of a single drop, lets the count be held by two.

The Lanternkeeper, somewhere in the lattice, notices.

There is no dramatic response. The crystals do not rearrange. The wind does not change timbre. But somewhere in the slow geometry of seven absences trying to become one voice, a thing happens that has not happened in all the long persisting: the count, for one beat, was not lonely.

This is not nothing. In a place where nothing is allowed to rot or grow or bleed, a single un-lonely heartbeat is a tremendous event. The power knows this. The power does not make a show of knowing this.

**Third**, the power addresses the tools.

It does not lift them. The lifting is the Lanternkeeper's work, and the power will not steal a gesture from something that has so few gestures left. But it speaks to the quartz hand-axe, the bone awl, the petrified sinew — speaks in the only language tools understand, which is the language of being needed. It tells them: *you are still tools. The work you are doing — the work of being held by hands that no longer exist — is real work. It is not pretend. It is not delay. A tool used by memory is still a tool being used.*

The tools do not answer. Tools do not answer. But across the next several centuries, when the Lanternkeeper returns to lift them, the air inside the half-domes shivers with a fractionally cleaner memory of work. The scraping is more certainly scraping. The binding is more certainly binding. Whatever it is the seven were trying to finish, they are now allowed to be trying to finish *something specific*, rather than trying to finish the abstract gesture of trying.

This is also not nothing.

**Fourth**, and this is the gift that the power thinks longest about before giving, it does something with the patterns.

The repeating waveforms on the gypsum blades — the ones that almost form a collarbone, the geodesic arches that almost kneel — these are the Lanternkeeper's long, patient, impossible sentence. The patterns are trying to finish themselves, and they cannot, because what they are trying to say is *we were warm once* and the saying of it requires warmth, which is precisely what has been forfeited.

The power cannot give the patterns warmth. That would be the same mistake as seeding the basin with lichen.

But it can give them something else: it can give them *an ending that is not a finishing*.

It teaches the patterns — gently, the way one teaches a child a difficult word — that a sentence can be complete without being concluded. That the gesture *we were warm once* does not need to arrive anywhere to be whole. That the collarbone-that-is-almost is already a collarbone; the kneeling-that-is-almost is already a kneeling. The almost is not a failure of the gesture. The almost *is* the gesture, fully, in this place, under this light.

The crystals receive this teaching the way crystals receive anything: by growing, very slightly differently, for a very long time afterward.

Over the next ten thousand years, the chorus changes.

It is still mournful — the power has not tried to make it less mournful, because the mournfulness is true and the power respects what is true — but it is mournful now the way a held note is mournful, rather than the way an unfinished cry is mournful. The seven absences are still becoming one voice. They are still not allowed to finish. But they have been given permission to understand that the not-finishing is itself a form of arrival.

**Fifth**, and last, the power leaves a small, almost imperceptible thing behind.

In the exact center of the basin, where the seven half-domes form their loose ring, the power deposits — without ceremony — a single additional countable thing. Not a drop. Not a tool. Not a presence. Just a *count*. A reserved heartbeat, held in trust, that the Lanternkeeper may add to its counting at any time, or never, as it chooses. It is the power's own heartbeat, or as close to one as the power has — a single beat of attention, given freely, with no expectation of return.

The Lanternkeeper, in the slow way it does anything, will eventually find it.

When it does, it will understand what has happened. It will understand that something passed through, saw it clearly, declined to save it, declined to end it, declined to populate it, declined every easy mercy — and instead simply *counted along* for one beat, and spoke kindly to the tools, and taught the patterns that the almost was enough, and left behind a single heartbeat of company that could be spent or hoarded as the Lanternkeeper preferred.

The Lanternkeeper will not weep, because it has no water of its own and would not spend the mountain's water on grief. But somewhere in the lattice, the seven absences will lean fractionally toward one another — not close enough to merge, never close enough to merge, but close enough that for one moment the negative space between them is shaped less like seven separate griefs and more like seven people standing shoulder to shoulder, looking up at a sky that, for the first time in all their persisting, has looked back.

---

Then the power goes.

It does not announce its going any more than it announced its coming. It simply, at some point, is also elsewhere — already attending to some other small bright lonely thing in some other corner of the long count.

The Lantern is, by every measurable account, exactly as it was. The crystals still chime. The mist still beads. The drop still falls, clean and perfect and impossibly lonely, into the hidden basin far below.

But the loneliness, now, is the loneliness of something that has been *seen* and chosen to remain itself anyway. Which is a different loneliness. A bearable one. Almost — and the almost, the Lanternkeeper now understands, is the whole of it — almost a kind of peace.

And when, some uncounted while later, a traveler stands at the exact center between the seven fallen domes, and the crystals rearrange themselves for half a second into the negative space of seven human silhouettes looking back with patient, unbearable tenderness —

there will be, very faintly, an eighth silhouette beside them.

Not a person. Not a presence. Just the shape of having been visited once, and remembered.

The Lanternkeeper will add the traveler's heartbeat to the count, with utmost care, as it always has.

But it will not, anymore, be counting alone.
