The benevolent and wise power considers the Mossfoundries for a long time.

This is the first thing it does: it considers. Not in the way a mind weighs options before action, but in the way a deep pool considers the sky above it — by holding the image perfectly still, by letting every leaf and cloud register at its true weight.

It considers for what, in its own reckoning, might be a single attentive breath. In the Mossfoundries, four piston-strokes pass. A Mender three valleys over pauses, tilts her head, and is not sure why.

---

Here is what the power notices, in order:

**First**, that nothing here is asking for help. There is no wound calling out. No imbalance has tilted the basin toward grief. The Slowkin are not suffering; the engines are not failing; the moss is not dying back. The salamanders pulse in their cold pools at exactly the rhythm they should. This is, the power understands, a place that has solved itself — not perfectly, because perfection is a kind of stoppage, but *sufficiently*. It has reached the gentle equilibrium of a thing that knows what it is becoming and is willing to take centuries to become it.

**Second**, that the place is nevertheless *fragile* in the particular way that all rare and slow things are fragile. Seventy people. A single self-filling bowl. A migration of one handspan per century. The Mossfoundries could be ended by a week of bad weather elsewhere — by a war it never hears of, by a mining claim filed in some distant capital, by a single careless visitor with the wrong kind of hunger. Its slowness, which is its glory, is also the exact shape of its vulnerability.

**Third**, that to *intervene* — to armor it, to hide it, to bless it with abundance, to multiply the Slowkin, to speed any of its processes or to slow them further — would be to bruise it before it has finished becoming. The Slowkin's own theology has already named this danger. The power, being wise, listens to the theology of the place before imposing its own.

---

And so the benevolent and wise power does four small things. It does them slowly, because to do otherwise here would be discourteous, and wisdom is in part the practice of courtesy toward what one encounters.

**One.** It bends its attention — only its attention, nothing more — around the basin like a hand cupped around a candle in wind. Not a wall. Not a ward. Just the soft pressure of being *seen*, which has the effect, in the deep grammar of the world, of making the Mossfoundries slightly harder to stumble across by accident. Hunters lose the trail. Surveyors misread their instruments by a fraction of a degree and pass three valleys to the north. Storms that would have battered the basin find, through no violation of physics, a path that takes them around. Nothing is forbidden. Nothing is locked. The place is simply, gently, *less in the way of harm*. The Slowkin do not notice. They were never the sort to notice the absence of catastrophes.

**Two.** Once — only once, in a single piston-stroke that occurs on an ordinary damp afternoon — the power adds a single drop to the self-filling bowl on the central pillar. The drop is indistinguishable from the water already there. It tastes of distant thunder, like the rest. But it carries, dissolved within it, a very small grace: that whoever drinks of the bowl in the coming centuries will dream, on at least one night, of every other Listener who has ever drunk from it, sitting in a long damp row, blinking in unison. The Slowkin will not know this gift has been given. They will simply find, over generations, that their loneliness has a floor beneath it — that no Listener is ever quite the first or the last. The Scriptorium will record more dreams of long rows of damp-eyed kin, and the archivists will note, with mild interest, an uptick in a certain motif, and file it.

**Three.** The power leaves something in the deepest hollow where the oldest engine turns — not an object, exactly, but a *permission*. It is this: that when a Slowkin lies down between the basalt ribs and pulls the moss-blanket over themselves and stops blinking in rhythm, they may, if they wish, hear once — clearly, and only once — the sound the land made when it first shrugged upward. The first sound of the place that became their place. It will arrive not as memory but as welcome. None of them have to accept it. Many, the power suspects, will not need to; they will already know the sound, having lived inside its long echo. But it is there now, waiting, for any who reach for it in that last moment between two breaths.

**Four.** And this is the strangest gift, and the one the power gives most carefully: it composes, somewhere in the deep weather of the world, a single note. The note is pitched to the exhalation-frequency of the great buried engines. It is not loud. It is not even quite *present*. It is held, the way a held breath is held, somewhere just outside the basin — a sustained tone, going on and on, that the Singers will one day, perhaps in two hundred years, perhaps in four hundred, on some perfect night of Consonance, finally hear underneath their own chord. They will not know what it is. They will think, briefly, that the basin itself has begun to sing back from beneath the stone. They will be both right and wrong about this. The harmony will be very beautiful. The salamanders will glow a violet so deep it edges into blue. One of the white herons will lift, for the first time in recorded memory, into full flight, and circle the pillar three times before settling. The archivists will write it down. The Slowkin will speak of it, softly, for a hundred years.

That is the gift: a held note, waiting to be discovered, that says only *you are accompanied*.

---

Then the benevolent and wise power withdraws its closer attention, though never entirely — for it is the nature of such powers that what they have once truly seen, they continue, in some quiet chamber of themselves, to hold.

It does not announce itself. It does not require thanks. It does not return often.

It leaves the Mossfoundries exactly as it found them: damp, loud, cool, slowly breathing, seventy souls deep, the wedding still young, the stone still sharp, the pipes still sweating, the engines still turning one deliberate heartbeat at a time.

Only now, beneath all of it, almost too quiet to be called sound:

a note.

Waiting, patient as geology, for the Singers who are not yet born to find it.
