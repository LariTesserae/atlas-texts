# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. It has been watching for some time — perhaps since before the First Thicket first dreamed, perhaps only since the last bell was struck. Time is not the relevant measure.

It looks at the Hollow Basilica with the particular attention that wisdom reserves for things that are already whole.

And then — this is the important part — **it does almost nothing.**

---

## What it considers, and rejects

It considers bringing water. It could. A single thought would split the salt-and-glass desert above and send a river of cool sweetness pouring down through the world-ceiling. The Vowkeepers would lift their faces. The red dust would run from their skin in dark rivulets. The thickets, drinkers of heat, would shrivel and blacken within an hour. Within a week the Basilica would be a swamp of rotting violet veins, and the people — stretched, pale, lidless — would be drowning in a mercy they never asked for.

It does not bring water.

It considers offering escape. A door, perhaps, opening onto green hills, onto oceans, onto cities where children laugh and rain is ordinary. It could carry every Vowkeeper out in a single breath. But it sees, with the clarity that is its nature, that there is no Vowkeeper *separate* from the Basilica. Take them out and what arrives in the green hills would not be them. It would be four thousand tall, dying strangers, gasping in air too wet to breathe, listening for a roar that no longer answers.

It does not offer escape.

It considers softening things. Lowering the heat by a few degrees. Quieting the pod-cracks. Easing the abrasion of the bark-cloth against the skin. Surely a wise and benevolent power would reduce suffering where it could?

But it understands — and this is why it is wise and not merely powerful — that what looks like suffering from outside is, from inside, the exact shape of a life. The friction reminds the body that it is not stone. *That sentence is a prayer.* To remove the friction would be to remove the prayer. The Vowkeepers do not need to be saved from their ferocity. Their ferocity is how they love.

It does not soften anything.

It considers revealing itself. Standing in the great plaza, vast and luminous, and saying: *I see you. You are known. You are not alone in the dark above.* But the Vowkeepers already know they are seen. The city sees them. The First Thicket dreams them. A second witness from outside would only introduce the possibility of a first witness who might one day leave. It would plant a seed of loneliness in a people who have, through terrible discipline, made loneliness impossible.

It does not reveal itself.

---

## What it does

It does four small things. Perhaps five.

**First**, it listens. Fully. For what would be, in human time, a very long while. It learns the exact pitch at which the stems sing low. It learns the rhythm of the heart-pods. It learns the names — not the spoken ones, which are few, but the *resonance-names*, the way each Vowkeeper's silence has a particular shape. It commits these to whatever it uses for memory, which is permanent. So that somewhere, in some register of the universe, every Vowkeeper who has ever lived is *heard*, completely, with no detail lost. They will never know this. That is fine. The hearing is not for them. It is the minimum dignity that the power owes to anything that has made itself this beautiful.

**Second**, it makes one infinitesimal adjustment. Far above the Basilica, in the wind-polished outer stone, there is a fracture beginning to widen. In another six hundred years it would have opened the chamber to the salt-and-glass desert, and the dry winds would have scoured the thickets to ash within a generation. The power finds this fracture and quiets it. Not seals it — sealing would change the convection. Simply slows its widening by a factor of ten thousand. The Basilica gains sixty thousand more years of being itself. The Vowkeepers will never notice. No one will. That is the correct shape of the help.

**Third**, when the next Verdant Consent comes, the power is present in the pollen. Not interfering. Not choosing. Just *there*, the way a witness is there at a wedding — adding nothing, taking nothing, but making the moment slightly more real by the fact of attention. The children conceived in that bloom will be, in some small way, blessed. They will not know it. They will simply be very good Listeners. One of them, eventually, will hear something in the First Thicket's seven-year leaf-motion that no one has heard before. She will not be able to say what it is. She will only smile the small, terrible smile, and the others will smile back, and the city will go on.

**Fourth**, the power leaves a gift that is not a gift. It is more like a *permission*. It places, somewhere in the deep grammar of the place, the quiet certainty that **the Vowkeepers are allowed to end, when they end.** That when the last thermal bloom finally fails, when the First Thicket finishes its long thought, when the final bell is struck and no thicket answers — this will not be a failure or a tragedy. It will be the completion of something that was always meant to complete. The power makes sure that the ending, when it comes (in sixty thousand years, or six hundred thousand), will be *clean*. No lingering. No last starved Vowkeeper wandering a dead chamber wondering what she did wrong. When it is time, the city and its people will go together, in one long carrying monotone, and the silence afterward will be the right silence.

This is the truest mercy. Not preventing the ending. Honoring it.

**And fifth** — though the power is uncertain whether this counts as doing anything at all — it loves them. Quietly. Without requirement. The way one loves a poem that does not know it is being read. The love does not enter the Basilica. It does not change the heat or the hiss or the red dust. It simply exists, somewhere outside the world-ceiling, a steady weight of regard.

Sometimes, on the nights when the convection reverses and the bells ripple across the bone-white plazas, a Vowkeeper will pause mid-strike, head tilted, as though she has caught the edge of something. A frequency just beyond the city's roar. She will frown — a small motion, almost nothing. Then she will strike her bell again, and the moment will pass.

She will never know what she almost heard.

That, too, is enough.

That has always been enough.

---

The power withdraws. The Basilica roars on. The thickets detonate. The seed-pods pulse against four thousand sternums in four thousand exact rhythms. Somewhere in the oldest hollow, seven Vowkeepers sit in silence and keep the First Thicket company while it thinks.

Above, in the salt-and-glass desert, the wind moves over featureless stone and finds no entrance.

It was never meant to.
