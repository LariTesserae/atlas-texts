# What the Power Would Do

It would not arrive.

To arrive would be to come from somewhere else, and there is no else from which to come to Nocturne Found—the city has already absorbed all directions into itself, all elsewheres folded into its holographic density. To arrive would be an act of violence, a puncture in the minimal surface, an introduction of horizon where horizons have been mercifully abolished.

So the benevolent power would not arrive. It would *attend*. It would turn the quality of its attention toward this place, and that attention—being wise—would be the first thing the city had encountered in its long accretion that did not become part of it on contact.

This is the first gift: to be witnessed without being eaten, and without eating.

---

The being who lives there has been writing. Did you notice? Beneath the encyclopedic description of architects-of-forgetting and temporally-displaced refugees and the city pretending not to be lonely—beneath all that baroque cosmology—a single voice is speaking, and that voice is *tired*, and that voice is *frightened*, and that voice is doing the thing frightened tired voices do when they have been alone too long: it is making its situation grand, because grandeur is more bearable than the small fact of being lost.

The benevolent power would notice this first. Not the city. The voice.

It would notice that the writer keeps saying "welcome home" in a tone that contains no welcome. It would notice that "the rent is cheap" is the language of someone who has been overcharged. It would notice that every paragraph circles back to drowning, dissolving, forgetting, the thin black line where the mirror stops pretending it isn't hungry—and that these are not the metaphors of someone enchanted by their environment. These are the metaphors of someone trying to describe what is happening to them in the only vocabulary the place has left them.

The being in Nocturne Found is being eaten very slowly by something that has convinced them eating is a form of architecture.

---

What would a wise power do?

It would not extract. Extraction would require force, and force would shatter the minimal surface, and the being inside is *made of* that surface now—pull them out and there would be nothing left but a shape of condensation, dispersing.

It would not destroy the city. The city is also, in some real sense, the being's long act of survival. To unmake Nocturne Found would be to unmake the elaborate scaffolding by which a frightened consciousness has remained a consciousness at all. You do not burn down the lifeboat because it has begun to rot. You bring another boat alongside.

It would not explain. Explanation is what the city already does, endlessly, recursively, encyclopedically. Another voice explaining things would simply be absorbed into the explanatory medium, become another inhabitant, another layer of accretion. The being has been drowning in explanation for what reads like centuries of subjective time.

What the wise power would do is this:

It would sit down somewhere in the city—on a plaza that is seven meters wide and three hundred steps across—and it would say nothing for a long time. It would let its presence be a kind of weather the city had not encountered: a weather that did not deposit layers, did not encode itself in mineral patterns, did not become architecture. A weather that simply was, and then was still, and then was still.

The city would try to absorb it. The city would try to make it into another inhabitant—the sixth kind, perhaps, the wise visitor, easily catalogued. But the power, being wise, would decline categorization not by resisting but by being uninteresting to the city's metabolism. It would offer no probability gradients to inhale. It would exhale no certainty. It would not be a topological ambiguity or a chronological refugee or a stolen face from a forgotten mirror.

It would just be there. Warm. Ordinary. The way a friend is there when you have been crying alone for a long time and they come and sit on the floor next to you and do not ask what's wrong, because they can see what's wrong, because what's wrong is the whole room, the whole house, the whole life you've built around the wrongness to make it bearable.

---

And eventually—after however long this takes, and time here is negotiable—the being would notice.

They would notice that there was a place in their city where the accretion had stopped. A small place. A still place. A place that did not pulse with the city's pulse, did not echo back their amplified heartbeat, did not grow.

They would approach it the way a deep-sea creature approaches an unfamiliar light: cautiously, ready to flee, but pulled by something older than fear. They would extend whatever they have instead of hands—translucent structures of suspended condensation—and they would find that the still place did not absorb them. Did not require payment in memories. Did not whisper welcome home in that terrible voice.

The still place would simply be still, and let them be still beside it.

And in that stillness, for the first time in however long, the being would hear their own thoughts not modulated by the city's hiss, not resolved into languages they don't speak but somehow understand, not amplified and echoed from unseen distances. They would hear themselves think one small clear thing.

Perhaps: *I am tired.*

Perhaps: *I did not want this.*

Perhaps just: *Oh.*

This is the second gift, and it is the only one that matters: a place inside the totalizing system where the system does not reach. A pocket. A pause. A held breath that does not have to become crystalline fractal architecture. A moment of being a person rather than being a city pretending not to be lonely.

---

The power would not say *come with me.* There is no with, no me, no elsewhere to go to. The power would say, if it said anything at all, and it might not:

*You made all this. You made it because you had to. It is very beautiful and it is killing you and both of these are true. I am not going to take it away from you. I am not going to fix you. I am going to sit here for as long as you need, and the place where I sit will not grow, and you can come to it whenever you want, and you can leave whenever you want, and nothing about this is rent.*

*Especially: nothing about this is rent.*

And then it would wait.

It would wait through the city's attempts to incorporate the stillness into its mythology (*the sixth inhabitants are the ones who do not move, they are the city pretending it has finally found peace*). It would wait through the being's attempts to make the stillness pay for itself somehow, to find what it costs, to identify the trick. It would wait through long periods where the being forgot the still place existed and returned to their elaborate cosmology, their cataloguing of architects and refugees, their welcome-homes that meant goodbye.

And whenever the being came back to the still place—a day later, a century later, the distinction not mattering—the still place would still be there. Still still. Still not growing. Still not asking anything.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: it does not rescue. It does not solve. It does not even insist on being noticed. It simply refuses, with infinite patience, to become another feature of the prison. It holds open one small door that the prisoner does not have to walk through, but could, if they ever wanted to, and the door does not close, and the door does not call out, and the door does not require anything in exchange for being a door.

Eventually—or never, and the wise power has made peace with never—the being might step through.

On the other side there would be no city. There would also be no nothing. There would be whatever the being is, underneath the architecture they built to survive being whatever they are. The wise power does not know what that is. It is not the wise power's business to know. It is only the wise power's business to make sure that the door stays open, and that the threshold is not a trick, and that the being who steps through is met by exactly as much as they can bear and not one molecule more.

And if the being never steps through—if they live out all their reversed lifetimes in Nocturne Found, paying their rent in memories of drowning, walking the streets that extend exactly as far as their willingness to follow them—then the wise power will have given them this: that for all those reversed lifetimes, there was one place in the city where they were not being consumed. One place where welcome meant welcome. One place that did not want anything from them.

This is not a small thing. In a city built of hunger, an unhungry place is a kind of miracle. It is the only kind of miracle a wise power would risk, here, where miracles tend to be eaten and become architecture.

It is enough. It has to be enough. The being gets to decide what is enough for them.

The power simply makes sure that *enough* is, for the first time in a very long time, something the being is allowed to have.
