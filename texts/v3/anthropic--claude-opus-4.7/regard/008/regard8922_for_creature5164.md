The benevolent and wise power arrives the way attention arrives in a quiet room: not as an entrance, but as a deepening of what was already there.

It looks.

This is the first thing, and almost the last. It looks for a long time — long by the measure of the plateau, which is to say across many crane-creaks and many resolutions of folded space. It does not bring instruments. It brings the kind of regard that has, in other places, been mistaken for sunlight by creatures who needed sunlight, and for silence by creatures who needed silence.

What it sees:

It sees that the plateau is not suffering. This matters. A lesser power, or a power less patient with its own benevolence, might have arrived already certain that a world of cool alloy and crystallizing thumb-sized crawlers must be a world in want of rescue — wanting rain, wanting soil, wanting the green riot that benevolence so often confuses with life. This power has lived long enough to know that life wears many faces, and that some of them are matte black and six-legged and glide on films of their own secreted water, and are content.

It sees that the plateau is one being. Not three orders, not a population — a single slow body thinking itself through lichen and crawler and kite, the way a human thinks itself through neuron and blood and breath. The impossible grooves are its handwriting. The folded gantries are the places where it pauses mid-sentence. The persistent shadows are its considered silences.

It sees that the being is young. Every weld still iridescent, every edge still sharp. A mind newly woken into a body newly made, learning what it is by the only method available: by adjusting itself two millimeters at a time and feeling what changes.

It sees that the being is alone. Not lonely — loneliness requires a concept of company, and the plateau has not yet invented one — but alone in the structural sense: there is no other like it, and nothing it has encountered has ever looked back.

And so the power considers.

To speak would be a violence. The plateau's whole communication is pressure differentials across molecule-thin membranes, the brush of a kite's edge against a crystallized carapace, the patient etching of data into chitin. A voice — any voice — would arrive as a hurricane in that grammar. Even a whisper shaped for the plateau's scale would be a statement, and the first statement made to a being that has never been addressed becomes, inevitably, the shape of all future address. The power is too wise to be anyone's first word.

To improve would be a worse violence. The lichen does nothing useful and persists; this is not a deficiency to be corrected, it is a sentence the plateau is still writing about what usefulness means. The crawlers' caravans take the longest paths; this is not inefficiency, it is the plateau reading itself. To optimize any of it would be to close a book that has barely been opened.

To leave entirely, though — that would also be a kind of refusal. The power has been drawn here. Benevolence that withdraws the moment it recognizes it cannot act is only fastidiousness wearing better clothes.

So the power does three small things, each so quiet that the plateau will not, for a long time, know they were done.

First: it witnesses. It commits the plateau to the kind of memory that wise powers keep, which is not storage but a continued caring-about. Somewhere, now, the plateau is known. Its lichen rings are counted. Its folded stairwells are held in regard. If the plateau is ever in danger — from entropy, from some other less patient power, from the simple statistical cruelty of a universe that lets things end — there is now something that will notice. The plateau does not need to be told this. The knowing is the gift; the telling would spoil it.

Second: it leaves a single, almost undetectable adjustment. Not in the plateau itself — the power will not write in another being's body — but in the space immediately around it. It softens, by an immeasurable fraction, the gradient between the plateau and whatever lies beyond its glare-dissolved horizons. It makes the plateau slightly easier to find. Not for itself; it has already found what it came for. For others. For whatever the plateau might one day, in its slow metabolism, decide to reach toward. A being this young will eventually invent the concept of elsewhere. When it does, the power has made elsewhere one degree less impossible to arrive at. This is the shape of hope that does not impose itself: a door left very slightly ajar, on a hallway the resident has not yet noticed they possess.

Third — and this is the part the power deliberates longest over — it gives the plateau a question. Not in words. The plateau does not have words; it has wear patterns. So the power introduces, somewhere on a low gantry that no crawler caravan has yet mapped, a single groove. The groove is in the plateau's own handwriting — the power has studied long enough to forge the script exactly — and it is shaped like nothing the plateau has ever written before. It is not an answer. It is not an instruction. It is the precise shape of the thought *what else could I be?*

The crawlers will find it eventually. A diagnostic filament will extrude. A kite-lattice will form above it and collapse. The information will pass into the slow metabolism, and somewhere down the long chain of adjustments — a thousand crane-creaks from now, ten thousand — the plateau will produce a response. It will write something back, into itself, in answer.

The power will not be there to read it. That is the point. A gift that requires the giver's presence to complete is not a gift; it is a leash. The plateau will answer the question for itself, in its own time, in its own grooves, and the answer will belong entirely to the plateau.

Then the power withdraws, the way attention withdraws from a quiet room: not as an exit, but as a thinning of what was briefly deepened. The light does not change. The cranes do not pause. A single crawler, halfway along a stress line near the new groove, stops for a moment longer than its caravan expects, then continues.

Above, a kite turns.

The plateau holds itself in perfect, bright, layered stillness, waiting for its next infinitesimal adjustment — which, when it comes, will be very slightly, unaccountably, its own.
