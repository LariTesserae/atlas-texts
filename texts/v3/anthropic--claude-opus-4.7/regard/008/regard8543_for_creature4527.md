The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good news sometimes does: as a slight adjustment in the quality of attention available in a room.

It does not announce itself. To announce would be to interrupt, and Sifam is a place where every interruption is a contract amendment requiring ninety years of mandible-stamped review. The power has read the bylaws. The power respects the bylaws. The power has, in fact, been reading the bylaws for some while now, learning the precise frequency at which Gwyrgch's heart-rot reflects light, learning the seventeen-year mood of the indecisive cloud, learning the way the lungfish philosopher's translator bird pretends not to be translating the lungfish's silences as well as its speech.

What the power notices first is this: *nothing here needs saving*.

This matters. Many powers, even kindly ones, arrive with rescue already drawn up in their pockets like a warrant. They see complexity and assume crisis. They see negotiation and assume conflict needing resolution. They see the Ambivalent Sloths and want to help them decide.

This power has lived long enough to know better. It has watched too many gardens improved into deserts by well-meaning hands. It has seen the particular grief of a forest taught to be a lawn. So when it looks upon Sifam — the recursive commerce, the upward-falling leaves, the seventeen-year rain debate, the whale on land regretting in iambs — it does not reach for the rescue warrant. It reaches, instead, for something rarer: *witness*.

Here is what the power does, then, in approximate order, though order means little here:

**First, it pays the entry fee properly.** It spends a long attention — what would be, in human currency, perhaps a quiet hour — on a single sunset-colored mushroom and its ghost-ant partners. Not performing curiosity. Actually curious. The mushroom registers the transaction. Somewhere, a ledger written in spore-script updates to note that an unfamiliar consciousness has paid in full and tipped generously. The mushroom blooms one shade warmer in gratitude. This costs the power nothing it values and gives the mushroom something it does. This is the shape of most of what follows.

**Second, it does not fix the rain.** The cloud has been debating for seventeen years. A lesser power would resolve the debate — *let there be rain*, or *let there be clearing* — and consider this kindness. This power understands that the debate *is* the weather here. To end it would be to end something. So instead the power offers the cloud a single new consideration it had not thought of: *what if both, in alternation, and called by a new name?* The cloud accepts the consideration into its deliberations. The debate now has eighteen years ahead of it, richer for the new term. The cloud is delighted. Delight, here, precipitates as a fine mist that tastes faintly of cardamom and unfinished sentences. The power lets it bead on its notional shoulders without complaint.

**Third, it sits with Olb.** Olb has been dying forward for three millennia. This is not a problem to be solved; it is a practice, possibly an art form, possibly the slowest possible prayer. The power sits at Olb's base and does the one thing Olb has perhaps never been offered in three thousand years of forward-dying: it agrees that the dying is real, and beautiful, and need not hurry, and need not stop. Olb's leaves rustle in a pattern that the Committee of 10,000 Mandibles later transcribes as a sigh of such relief that three new zoning categories spontaneously generate to accommodate it. One of them is called *Permission*. Citizens immediately begin applying.

**Fourth, it speaks to the Persistent Questions.** Not to answer them — that would be murder. But to acknowledge that they are *good* questions, that they were *worth* asking, that the sincerity that birthed them was not wasted simply because no answer came. The oldest Persistent Questions, the ones with answers orbiting them like shy moons, weep a little. (Their weeping is itself a question, of course: *was that worth it?* The power does not answer. It simply nods, which is also a question, and the questions recognize kin.) One Persistent Question follows the power for the rest of its stay, occasionally bringing it coffee that may or may not exist. The power drinks it gratefully either way.

**Fifth, it leaves a gift, but the gift is structural and almost invisible.** The power notices that the Maybe People have a thriving bureaucracy but no government, and that this is causing them a low-grade existential ache they have learned to mistake for weather. The power does not give them a government. Instead, it gives them *the permission to never form one*, formalized as a small, self-replicating clause that inserts itself into the pre-application forms: *Government optional. Existence sufficient.* The Maybe People, encountering this clause, experience for the first time the specific relief of being allowed to remain a question. Their chief export — rumors about themselves they haven't decided to start — becomes, overnight, slightly more honest and considerably more beautiful. Several of them become almost real for whole afternoons at a time. They do not thank the power, because they aren't sure they exist enough to, but the power receives the non-thanks as the highest compliment Sifam can offer.

**Sixth, it addresses the visitor.** You. The one on Gwyrgch's outer bough. The one who came here looking for answers, trade, sanctuary, or that fourth thing you won't admit even to yourself.

The power does not appear to you. It would be rude. But you feel — between one breath-contract and the next exhale-vote — a small adjustment. Something in you that was clenched is asked, very politely, if it would consider unclenching. Not commanded. *Asked.* With the implication that *no* is a complete and honorable answer.

You may say no. Many do. Sifam respects refusal as a sacrament.

If you say yes, the power offers you this: it will not tell you what you came for. It will not even tell you that you'll find it. What it will do is make sure that whatever you find, you will be able to *recognize* — that the part of you trained to dismiss the answer as too strange, too small, too late, or too much like yourself, will be briefly, gently retired. For one week of Sifam-time (which may be an afternoon or a lifetime, depending on how the cloud votes), you will see what you are seeing. That is all. That is everything.

The power considers this its most generous gift and its least interesting one. Recipients rarely notice they've received it until years later, in another country, when they suddenly understand a conversation they had with a mushroom.

**Seventh, and finally, it stays.** Not as ruler, not as guest, not even as observer. It joins the ecology as one more negotiating party, no larger in vote-weight than a beetle-priest, no smaller than Gwyrgch herself. It pays its attention-taxes. It signs its nectar-ink contracts. It learns to apologize properly to ant-harvested meteorite-rust before handling it. It takes a small apartment in a hollow that used to be a closet and will soon be a staircase. It writes occasional letters to the Fourth Estate, which prints them as beautifully mistaken prophecies, several of which come true by sincere readership.

Over the centuries — for the power has centuries to spend, and Sifam is one of the few places it has found worth spending them in — its presence becomes indistinguishable from the place itself. The Committee of 10,000 Mandibles eventually files a motion to recognize the power as a native species. The motion passes by acclamation, which here means that everyone present chews thoughtfully in the same direction for a measured interval.

The power accepts citizenship. It is asked, as is traditional, to declare a name in light-frequency. It declines politely and offers instead a name in *absence* — a particular shape of silence between two specific raindrops. UnUnUn approves. The two of them become close, in the way that two carefully maintained absences can.

And this is what the benevolent and wise power does, in the end, for Sifam:

It proves, by its long and quiet residence, that wisdom does not always arrive to change a place. Sometimes wisdom arrives to be *changed by* one. Sometimes the most benevolent act available to a great power is to discover that it, too, can be a citizen. That it, too, can pay attention as currency. That it, too, can be chewed upon in mutually agreed proportions, and find this not a diminishment but the first real conversation it has had in eons.

The mist cloud, eventually, in its eighteenth or nineteenth or twenty-third year of deliberation, reaches a verdict. It rains. And it does not rain. And it calls this new weather by a name that contains the power's silence in its middle syllable. The name cannot be written, but everyone in Sifam knows it when they hear it falling, and they go about their commerce a little gentler that day, and the upward-falling leaves pause briefly mid-ascent to consider whether they too might try something new.

The lungfish philosopher, reconstituting from next week's weather, nods through its translator bird and says:

*Yes. That is how a power ought to arrive. Slowly. As weather. As neighbor. As one more question we are grateful no one has answered.*

And the translator bird, for once, does not translate — only sings the lungfish's silence back to it, in perfect pitch, which is the highest form of agreement Sifam knows.
