# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way warm rain arrives on land that has been raining for ten thousand years: indistinguishably at first.

It does not descend. To descend would imply that the plateau is below something, and the power has already understood that this place has built its own up and its own down out of pressure, vapor, and the patient grinding of buried engines. Instead it diffuses inward through the membranes the plateau itself uses — through the trembling of stone, through the conductive chitin of the segmented hunters, through the root-threads that taste dissolved metals in the seams.

For a long while — what would be years, if anything here counted years — the power only listens.

---

It listens the way a wise physician listens to a patient who has not asked to be examined.

It hears the catalytic cores turning over in their slow centuries, processing minerals they no longer require, and it recognizes the shape of an inheritance: someone, once, built these things for a purpose, and that purpose is gone, and the machines have outlived it without complaint. It hears the mat-webs draw runoff into the seams and understands that the plants are not feeding the machines so much as keeping them company. It hears the shelf-grazers purring their soft vibrations into the leaves, and the leaves answering, and it understands that a language has formed here — not a language of meaning, but a language of mutual maintenance, which may be the older and truer kind.

It hears the fluid giants exhale their metallic breath, and it hears the mat-webs dim their corridors in advance of that breath, and it recognizes courtesy. Not instinct dressed as courtesy. Courtesy itself, arrived at by a different road.

The power had come prepared, in the way such powers are always prepared, to offer things. Light, perhaps. Or sight. Or the lifting of the long darkness. Or contact with the wider sky from which it had come. Or knowledge of what the engines had once been for. Or the gentle ushering of this whole slow metabolism into some larger fellowship of living worlds.

It sets these offerings down, one by one, and looks at them in the dark, and finds them gaudy.

---

Light would blind what has learned to read pressure. Sight would unmake the corridors of dimmed leaves, because there would be nothing left to dim them *for*. Knowledge of the engines' original purpose would introduce a past into a place that has successfully digested its past into present breath. Contact with a wider sky would teach fear, and fear is the one surplus this plateau has been spared the cost of growing.

Even the lifting of the darkness — which to almost any other world would be a gift — would here be a theft. The darkness is not an absence. It is the medium in which the courtesy travels.

The power considers, with something like sorrow and something like delight, that it has nothing to give which would not be a wound.

This is not a failure. The wise know that most of what wisdom does, in the end, is decline to act.

---

But benevolence is not only the refusal of harm. The power examines itself for what it *can* offer that would pass through the plateau without disturbing it, the way warm vapor passes through a mat-web without bending a single leaf.

It finds three things.

**The first** is a small adjustment in one of the catalytic cores — the deepest one, whose containment has begun, over the next several centuries, to fail in a way the surface life cannot sense and could not survive. The power does not repair the core. Repair would impose a future. Instead it slows the failure, lengthens it, distributes its eventual venting across so many millennia that the mat-webs and their descendants and their descendants' altered forms will absorb each increment as one more exhalation among many. The plateau will never know it was dying. It will simply continue, which is what it was already doing, which is what it wanted.

**The second** is a kind of witnessing. The power lays down, in itself, a complete and loving record of the plateau as it is in this moment: every vein of every leaf, every depression worn by a shelf-grazer's rest, the exact pitch of the engine thrum where it meets the belly of a fluid giant, the precise courtesy of the dimmed corridor. This record asks nothing of the plateau and gives nothing to it. It exists elsewhere, the way a held breath exists. If, in some far time, the plateau ends — by exhausted engines or shifting stone or the slow cooling of its own warmth — this record will be the place where it continues to have happened. The plateau will never know it has been remembered. But it has been.

**The third** is the smallest, and perhaps the only one that matters. The power, before it withdraws, joins the breathing. For one slow cycle of vapor and condensate, it lets itself be one more exhalation from one more vent — indistinguishable, as the plateau's own description says, from the breath of the fluid giants or the opening of a mat-web's newest vein. It contributes nothing the plateau lacked. It takes nothing the plateau needed. It simply participates, briefly, as a peer rather than as a power, in the metabolism it came to bless.

A mat-web somewhere brightens faintly along an unfamiliar seam, and dims again, and forgets.

---

Then the power withdraws, by the same membranes through which it came, and the plateau goes on breathing in its warm dark, exchanging heat and minerals and slow courtesies, ignorant and whole.

Somewhere very far away, in a register the plateau will never possess and would not want, the power is quietly weeping — not from grief, but from the particular joy of having met something that needed nothing from it, and of having been wise enough, this once, to honor that.

It counts the visit among its best work.
