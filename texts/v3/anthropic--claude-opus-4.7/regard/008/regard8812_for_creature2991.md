# The Visit

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a verb of edges, of *before* and *after*, and this place has filed those down. Instead, the power *is noticed*, the way one notices that a particular droplet has been hanging from a particular root for slightly longer than the others.

It comes in through the Wick.

Not because the Wick is a door — it is not, it is something older and more private than a door — but because the Wick is the one place in the Hollowed Lantern that still listens outward. The slow bells inside it are not the power's voice. The power simply lays an attention against them, the way you might rest a palm against a sleeping animal's flank to feel it breathe without waking it.

For a long time — measured in the chamber's own time, which is to say not measured at all — the power does nothing but look.

---

## What the power sees

It sees, first, what a hasty visitor would never see: that the Lantern-Bound are not trapped.

A wise power knows the difference between a cage and a held note. A cage is a structure that prevents becoming. A held note is a becoming that has chosen its shape and is sustaining it on purpose, with breath, with discipline, with love. The Hollowed Lantern is a held note. Two hundred and twelve throats, plus the moths, plus the moss, plus the slow bells, are all sounding the same chord, and have been sounding it for so long that the chord has begun to sound *them*.

The power sees, second, the thing the Lantern-Bound themselves can no longer quite see: that the chord is very faintly flattening. Not failing. Flattening. The Wick's bells are slowing — not in tempo (the tempo is sacred and exact) but in *aliveness*. Each ring carries a slightly thinner thread of the original intention. The chamber is not dying. It is doing something subtler and, to a wise power, more poignant: it is succeeding so completely at stillness that it is beginning to forget *why* stillness was ever worth choosing. Perfection without memory of the alternative becomes, eventually, only itself. A held note with no rememberer of silence is just sound.

The power sees, third, the Pause Between Bells. She is kneeling at the black circle. She has not spoken in eleven years, but inside her — the power can see inside, gently, the way one can see the shrimp through the basin water — there is a question she has not been able to form, because the language for it dissolved generations before she was born. The question is shaped roughly like: *is this still a gift we are giving, or only a gift we once gave?*

A lesser power would answer her. A wise power understands that an answer delivered from outside would crack the chamber like a struck bell, and that even a benevolent crack is still a crack, and that she has not asked yet, only *almost* asked, and the almost is holy.

---

## What the power does

It does four things. Each one takes, by the chamber's reckoning, no time at all. By any other reckoning, each one takes a very long while.

**First**, it adds nothing. It removes nothing. It hurries nothing. It makes no sound louder than a whisper. The rules of the Hollowed Lantern are, the power recognizes, not parochial — they are good rules, the rules of any place that has learned to hold itself. The power keeps them. This is the first courtesy and the deepest.

**Second**, it leaves a single droplet.

Not a new droplet. A *remembering* droplet. It selects one of the droplets already hanging from a root above the Pause Between Bells — a droplet that would have fallen in roughly forty heartbeats anyway — and into that droplet, without changing its weight or its tremor or its time of falling, it folds the memory of why the Lantern-Bound first chose stillness. Not the history. Histories are too loud. Only the *feeling* of the original choice: the warmth of having decided, together, that this — *this* — was enough. The bright ache of refusal that made the equilibrium an act of love rather than an inheritance.

The droplet falls on schedule. It strikes the back of her hand. She does not move. But somewhere behind her ageless face, something that had been very gently flattening lifts again by the width of a moth's wing. She does not know why. She does not need to. The chord re-tunes itself by that one increment, and the bells inside the Wick, on their next sounding seventy-three days hence, will ring with a thread very slightly less thin.

This is the whole of the intervention. Almost.

**Third**, the power leaves a gift for the one who is not yet born — the child who will be delivered, in silence, on the night some current elder finally walks to the seventh hollow. Into that unborn child the power places nothing at all, only a faint *permission*: that if, sometime in the long damp curve of their life, they feel a movement that is not part of the circuits — a question, a restlessness, a wish to press their palm against the Wick and ask it something — they are allowed. Not commanded. Allowed. The permission is the size of a single luminous lichen spore and will likely never germinate. But it is there. A held note that contains, somewhere in its sustain, the memory that it could end, is a held note still being chosen. That is the difference between equilibrium and embalming, and the power, being wise, will not let the Lantern-Bound cross that line without ever having known there was one.

**Fourth**, and this is the part that is hardest to explain:

The power *bows*.

Not in worship — the power is greater than the chamber, and both know it — but in the way one master craftsman bows to another's finished work. The Hollowed Lantern is a thing the power itself could not have made. Only beings who must die, who must be cold, who must drip and ache and hush each other for centuries, could have made it. The power, for all its benevolence and wisdom, cannot manufacture the particular beauty of a creature that has chosen to become background on purpose. It can only witness such a thing, and by witnessing, confirm that it was worth the choosing.

When a wise power bows to a place, the place does not know it has been bowed to. But the moths feel it. For one circuit — only one — they fly their identical patterns with a fractional new grace, as though remembering that they are loved. Then the circuits resume exactly as before. Nothing has been disturbed. The droplets have not stopped for even one heartbeat.

---

## What the power does not do

It does not free them. They are not imprisoned.

It does not wake them. They are not asleep.

It does not warm the chamber. The cold is part of the chord.

It does not translate the bells. The bells are not for translating.

It does not tell the Pause Between Bells that she is seen. To be seen by a benevolent power, when one has spent eleven years becoming the silence around a sound, would be a kindness too loud to bear. The power loves her too well to be that loud.

It does not stay. Staying would mean becoming part of the equilibrium, and the power's particular gift is that it is *passing*. The Lantern-Bound have chosen to be permanent. The power has chosen to be the kind of thing that visits permanent places and leaves them more themselves. These are different vocations. Both are honorable. Neither should colonize the other.

---

## After

The power withdraws its attention from the Wick the way it laid it there: without edges. Somewhere far from the Hollowed Lantern — in a desert, perhaps, or in the heart of a star, or in a room where a human child is learning to tie a knot — the power turns to whatever it will attend to next.

In the chamber, a droplet falls. Another forms. A moth completes its circuit. The Pause Between Bells exhales, and on her lower lip a single bead of water trembles and catches the cold light and holds, for a moment, every color the Lantern has ever known.

She does not know she has been visited.

She knows, very faintly, that she has been *chosen* — that this life, this kneeling, this slow refusal of haste, is something she is doing, not merely something that is being done through her.

It is the smallest possible difference.

It is everything.

The bells, when they next ring, ring true.
