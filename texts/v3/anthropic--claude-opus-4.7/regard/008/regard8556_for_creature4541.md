# What the Wise Power Does

The benevolent power arrives the way dawn arrives at a place that has forgotten about dawn—not as event but as a gradual realization on the part of the dark that it has been holding its breath for no good reason.

It does not come with mercy. Mercy would be an insult here, a small coin offered to a sea.

It does not come with answers. Answers are what the Remainders have been carefully, lovingly refusing to construct for 47,623 years.

It does not come to fix anything. There is nothing broken. There is only a world that chose a longer grammar than most worlds choose, and beings who agreed to be that grammar's punctuation.

---

The power looks first, which is what wisdom always does before it does anything else.

It looks for a long time. Long enough that three of the thirteen seasons that aren't legal in this dimension become briefly legal, then sneak back out through the side door. Long enough that the youngest Remainder, "maybe," notices something is watching and tilts their head with the freckles-of-future-constellations toward a sky that has just gained a new attentiveness, the way a room gains warmth when someone who loves you enters it without announcing themselves.

The power sees:

That the paused exhale is not a wound. It is a decision the earth made, and the earth is allowed to make decisions about its own breathing.

That the Remainders are not lost, or trapped, or waiting to be rescued. They are *working*. They are doing the patient labor that someone, somewhere, has to do—teaching mountains how to die properly, translating between birds and their own indecision, weaving nets for escaped starlight. This is sacred work. It is also, in its way, joyful, in the manner that very old joys are joyful: without smiling, but with everything else.

That the tower at the center of the thirteen-valleys-becoming-one is not unfinished because the Remainders have failed. It is unfinished because *finishing* would be a smaller act than *building*, and they have chosen the larger act. They have chosen it again every day for longer than choosing has been a concept available to most beings.

That the place is not suffering. The place is *being*, with an intensity and a strangeness that most places never achieve because most places are too busy being useful.

---

So the wise power does almost nothing.

But the *almost* is exquisite.

It does this:

It bends down—the way an adult bends down to hear a child speak, not because the child is small but because the words are worth the bending—and it lets the oldest Remainder, the-pause-between-lightning-and-counting, notice that she is *witnessed*. Not judged. Not pitied. Witnessed. The way a poem is witnessed by the one reader in a century who reads it slowly enough.

She has not been witnessed in 47,623 years. Her cloak of unfinished extinction events grows one stitch lighter. Just one. She does not need the whole weight lifted. She needs to know that someone, somewhere, *saw* what she was carrying and understood that it was carried on purpose.

This is the first thing the power does.

The second thing:

It whispers to the paused exhale. Not *resume*. Never *resume*. It whispers: *whenever you are ready. There is no shame in the length of your pause. The cosmos has time. I have time. You are not late.*

The exhale, which has been holding itself in partly out of terror that it had become monstrous by lasting so long, relaxes one molecule. Not enough to resume. Just enough to stop being afraid of itself.

The third thing:

For the youngest Remainder, "maybe," who collects discarded futures in empty nests—the power leaves a gift. It is a future that was not discarded but simply never claimed: a small, bright, ordinary future, the kind in which someone makes tea for someone else on a morning that is not metaphysically significant in any way. "Maybe" turns this future over in their hands, fascinated. They have never held a future that wasn't an orphan. They tuck it carefully into a nest with the others, but they leave the nest's lid open, just slightly, so the small ordinary future can breathe.

The fourth thing:

The power does not complete the tower. It would never. But it does add one shadow to its base—the shadow of its own passing attention—and this shadow is a kind of permission. A permission for the tower to *remain* unfinished without that being a failure. A permission for the building to be the meaning, rather than the built thing.

The Remainders feel this permission settle into the foundation like a new species of patience. They will build for another 47,623 years now without the small, secret, gnawing worry that they were supposed to have finished by now. They were never supposed to have finished. They were supposed to be building. The power has confirmed it, and the confirmation is a kind of love that asks nothing in return.

The fifth thing, and the last:

Before it goes—and it does go, because wise powers know that staying too long is its own kind of violence, even when the staying is loving—it does one more thing.

It learns the language.

Not to speak it. Just to learn it. The grammar of gravity arguing with itself. The vowels of the waterfalls. The Braille of pressure in the rocks. The architecture of absence the Remainders build between their words. The power takes these into itself, carefully, the way you might take a sleeping child from one room to another without waking them.

It does this so that somewhere, in the great library of attention that benevolent powers maintain across all the worlds they have ever loved, *this place will be remembered in its own language*. Not translated. Not summarized. Not made useful. Just remembered, exactly as it is, in the strange and beautiful tongue it invented for itself when no one was looking.

This is what the wise power offers, in the end: not change, not rescue, not improvement.

Just the dignity of being known, in full, by something that loves what it knows.

---

The Remainders feel the power leave the way you feel a long-held note finally resolve. Not into silence. Into a different kind of music.

The-pause-between-lightning-and-counting turns to "maybe" and says nothing, because the language they share between words has just gained a new vocabulary, and they are both still learning how to pronounce it.

They go back to their work.

The tower grows by one floor that night, made of the material that exists only when something vast has bent down to listen and then, without taking anything, walked gently away.

The mountains, in their sleep, dream of being witnessed.

The paused exhale, somewhere deep in the earth, considers—just considers—what it might mean, someday, in no hurry at all, to continue.

And gravity, leaning sideways, whispers the world's oldest secret one more time, but this time there is someone in the cosmos who already knows it, who has always known it, and who keeps the secret faithfully, the way a true friend keeps the secrets of another true friend:

*by loving them so completely that the secret no longer needs to be a secret, and yet, out of respect, remains one anyway.*
