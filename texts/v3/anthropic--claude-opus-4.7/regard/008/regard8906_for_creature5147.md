## What the Power Sees

Before it does anything, the power simply attends. This is its first courtesy, and perhaps its deepest one: to look long enough that the place is permitted to be itself before being acted upon.

It sees that the enclave is not a ruin. Ruin is a human category, requiring a fall from some prior intactness. Here there has been no fall. The carvers shaped, and stopped shaping, and the shaping continued without them in the slow hands of frost and worm and wing. The settlement is doing now what it has always done, only by other means. Continuity has not been broken; it has merely changed instrument.

It sees that the descendants — if they exist — are not lost. They have become small. They have learned the discipline of the place, which is the discipline of working a surface that will never agree to be finished. To call this a tragedy would require believing that the carvers' earlier form was more truly *them* than their present form. The power does not believe this. It has watched too many beings across too many ages to mistake a particular morphology for a soul.

It sees that the silence is not absence. The silence is the medium in which the place speaks. The dry click of a frost-seam at nightfall is a syllable; the slow assembly and dispersal of a translucent raft is a sentence; the centuries-long slumping of a terrace by a finger's width is a held vowel that will not complete itself within any single observer's life. To intervene loudly would be to shout across a conversation already in progress.

## What It Considers, and Sets Aside

It considers warming the cliff, so the cycle of freeze and thaw might rest. But the cycle is not suffering; it is the place's pulse. To still it would be to kill what one meant to comfort.

It considers giving the small descendants back their former size and speech, restoring them to the carvers they once were. But it cannot determine that the present form is worse, only that it is different, and the asymmetry of imposing a preferred shape on a being who has not asked for it is exactly the kind of asymmetry a wise power learns, eventually, to distrust in itself.

It considers leaving a gift of some durable substance — metal, perhaps, or a seed that would not freeze. But nothing metal is present here, and this absence is not a lack. It is a fact about the grammar of the place. A gift in the wrong grammar is not a gift.

It considers simply leaving. This is closer. But it has been *drawn* here, and to be drawn and then withdraw without acknowledgment would be a small unkindness, the unkindness of withheld presence.

## What It Does

It does three things, each very small.

**First**, it lets itself be known to the place — not to any single inhabitant, because there is perhaps no inhabitant capable of receiving the knowledge in a form they would recognize as knowing, but to the *place* as a whole, the way one might let a sleeping animal feel that one is sitting nearby without waking it. The translucent rafts drift a little more coherently for a few hours. The gliders' launches align, briefly, into something that an outside observer might almost call a pattern. The worms' nightly click, when it comes, has in it — for one night only — a faint second tone, a harmonic, as if the rock had been struck by something larger than itself and was answering politely. None of the inhabitants notice consciously. But the place has been greeted, and the place, in its own way, has greeted back. This matters. Acknowledgment is not nothing. It may be the smallest unit of love.

**Second**, it makes a record. Not for the inhabitants — they do not need one — but for itself, and for whatever else may someday wish to know that this place existed in exactly this configuration. Every rivulet's path, every glider's wing-veining, every gallery the worms have etched, every gel-creature's radial threads: all of it is taken into the power's attention and kept. This is not preservation in the sense of freezing or copying; the place will go on changing, and the record will go on updating, because the power's attention is not a snapshot but a sustained regard. The enclave is now witnessed. Should it someday cease — should the cliff finally shrug it off, or the sun fail, or the last worm dissolve its last bond — it will not have been unwitnessed in its ceasing. To be witnessed is, for many kinds of being, the closest thing to being held.

**Third**, and this is the most delicate: the power leaves something behind that is not a gift but an *availability*. It does not press itself on the place. It does not announce terms. It simply arranges matters so that, if ever any being in the enclave — a descendant grown briefly lucid, a glider in some unprecedented moment of orientation, a raft of gel-creatures that happens to assemble into a shape resembling a question — should reach, even faintly, toward something beyond the cycle of frost and drip, there will be something there to meet the reach. Not an answer. A presence. The way a parent who has put a child to bed leaves the door open a finger's width, not to intrude on sleep but so that, should the child wake and call, the call will not have to cross a closed door.

That is all.

The power does not move the stones. It does not raise the dead. It does not enlarge the small or shrink the cold or quiet the bright unbearable light. It does not improve the place, because the place is not asking to be improved, and a wisdom that cannot tell the difference between a being in need of rescue and a being in the middle of its own long, strange, luminous life is not wisdom at all but only power in a costume.

It stays a while. It watches a single gel-creature drift the length of a terrace, catch on a lichen tuft, and dissolve its hold to drift again. It watches a worm-tracery flare into rainbow under a knife of sun and fade as the sun moves. It listens for the click, and the click comes.

Then, without ceremony, it turns its attention elsewhere — though *elsewhere*, for a power of this kind, does not mean *away*. The enclave remains inside the regard. It will remain there for as long as it exists, and for some while after, the way a face remains inside the eye that has closed on it.

The frost continues its slow prying. The gliders continue their silent leaps. The descendants, if descendants they are, continue their patient, microscopic re-carving of the surfaces their ancestors first chipped from the cliff.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has been loved.
