The power notices the chamber the way one notices a held breath in another room — not by sound but by the shape of the attention around it.

What draws it is not the craft, though the craft is admirable. It is not the solitude, though the solitude is total. It is something subtler: the fact that the builder has made, with great labor, a place that asks for nothing. No altar. No offering bowl. No window facing outward in supplication. The chamber is not a prayer. It is a competence.

The power, being wise, understands that this is rare and should be handled with care.

It approaches the way warm water approaches warm water — by being already there. It does not enter through the flues or press against the hide panels. It does not announce. It simply widens its notice until the chamber is inside it, and then it watches for a long time.

Here is what it sees:

A being that has built exactly enough. Not a fortress. Not a monument. A working volume, sized to one body, lit well, fed by current, populated by a few green strands that serve no function except to be tended. The being tends them. This is the part the power lingers on. The algae are not food. They are not decoration in any sense the being would recognize as decoration. They are simply the thing that requires care, placed there so that care would have somewhere to go.

The power understands this. The power has, in its own long existence, done something similar.

So it considers, slowly, what to do.

It could reveal itself. It could fill the chamber with a voice and offer gifts: companionship, knowledge, a mate, a wider sea made safe, the assurance that the timber will not rot and the hides will not split. It could promise that the creaks in the stone mean only settling and never collapse. It could promise meaning.

It declines all of these.

It declines them because the being did not ask, and because the chamber was built precisely as an answer to a question the being has already settled for itself. To arrive with gifts would be to insist that the question was still open. It would be a discourtesy disguised as generosity — the particular discourtesy of powerful things, which the power has spent a long time learning not to commit.

It considers leaving entirely, and touching nothing.

But benevolence is not only restraint. Wisdom is not only the refusal to interfere. There is a difference between honoring a life and abandoning it, and the power knows that difference is sometimes very narrow.

So it does three small things.

The first: it adjusts, by a margin too fine for the being to detect, the mineral content of the water entering the upper flues. Nothing dramatic. A trace more of what the algae quietly need, a trace less of what would, over the coming seasons, have caused the hide panels to stiffen and crack along their lashings. The chamber's working life is extended. The being will not notice. It will simply continue to find that its maintenance is sufficient, that its calculations were right. The power leaves the being its competence. This is the gift.

The second: it marks the chamber, in a register no creature of that sea can perceive, as a place not to be disturbed. Currents that would have, in some distant year, swung a piece of drifting wreckage against the outer wall — these currents are nudged, very gently, into other paths. Predators large enough to investigate the structure find that their hunger leads them elsewhere, and they do not know why, and it does not trouble them. The being is not protected in any way it would recognize as protection. It is simply allowed to keep being the kind of creature that does not need to be protected.

The third is the smallest and the strangest. The power, watching the being settle beneath the brightest of the shell inlays, half-lidded, gill slits barely moving — the power lets itself, for one slow circuit of the water, be witnessed. Not seen. Witnessed in the opposite direction: the power permits the being to be the thing observed by something that finds it sufficient. No message is transmitted. No warmth is added that the current was not already carrying. But for the length of one pulse of the circulating water, the chamber is held inside an attention that approves of it entirely, and asks nothing, and then withdraws.

The being taps its claw once against the timber. Tests. Confirms alignment. Everything remains as it was.

The power moves on, satisfied, the way one is satisfied at having passed a sleeping animal without waking it. It carries the chamber in its memory now, which is a very long memory, and will return now and then across the years to check — not to intervene further, only to see whether the small adjustments still hold, whether the hide still flexes, whether the green banners still drift in their troughs, whether the one inhabitant still rests beneath its chosen light.

If, someday, the being invites something else inside — a second body, an egg, a question — the power will notice that too, and consider again what, if anything, to do.

Until then it leaves the chamber as it found it: small, bright, working, complete.
