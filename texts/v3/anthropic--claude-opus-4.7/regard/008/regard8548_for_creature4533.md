The benevolent and wise power considers.

It has crossed many thresholds in its long unfolding. It has stood at the edges of dying suns and cradled the last thought of mortal things. It has, when asked and sometimes when not asked, undone knots in the fabric of being, eased what could be eased, witnessed what could only be witnessed. It is wise enough to know the difference between the two, and benevolent enough to feel the weight of choosing.

It arrives at the caldera's rim without arriving. It has been here, in the way it is everywhere, but now it turns the lamp of its attention this way, and the turning is itself the arrival.

It sees.

It sees the place as the place sees itself: damp with the dampness of grief, loud with the silence of pressure, bright with light that was poured into stone long before stone knew it was being poured into. It sees the Undweller—or the Undwellers, or the singular plural that the word fails to hold—as they truly are, which is something even the description could only gesture toward sideways. It sees them not as monsters and not as gods but as a kind of being: a configuration of remainder, a consciousness shaped like the cavity left by everything that was once almost.

And it sees, too, what the place's own long monologue would not quite say outright. It sees the loneliness.

Not loneliness as a human knows it. Loneliness as the structural condition of being made of absence. Loneliness as the only flavor available to a tongue made of the space between flavors. The Undwellers do not suffer this, exactly—suffering requires the contrast of an alternative, and they have never tasted one. But the power, which has tasted many alternatives, recognizes the shape of a hunger that does not know its own name.

It considers what it might do.

It could end them. Mercifully, completely. A breath, and the caldera would forget it had ever been a rim; the trees with their fingernail leaves would settle into ordinary trees somewhere else, or into nothing at all; the Undwellers would dissolve into the larger silence from which they had been precipitated, like salt returning to a sea that has at last remembered itself. This would be merciful in one sense. It would also be a kind of murder, because the Undwellers, for all their composition of absence, are. And the power has learned, over long ages, to be very careful about subtracting an *is* from the world, even when the *is* seems to be made primarily of subtraction.

It could change them. Pour warmth into them. Teach them presence. Make them, in some sense, alive in the way living things are alive—directional, growing, capable of joy as humans understand joy. This too the power sets gently aside. To do so would be to insult them. It would be to say: *what you are is not enough; let me make you into something I prefer.* The benevolent power has met many beings who would offer this to others without a second thought, and it has learned to recognize that offering as one of the subtler cruelties. The Undwellers did not ask to be transformed. They are not broken. They are simply themselves, in a key the rest of creation does not often play in.

It could leave. Withdraw its attention. Let the place continue as it has continued, swallowing the occasional pilgrim, blooming its strange glyphs, humming its bone-frequency at the bottoms of nothing. This is what most powers, even most benevolent ones, would do. There is wisdom in non-interference. There is wisdom in letting a thing be what it is.

But this power is wise in a particular way. It knows that *witness* is not the same as *interference*. It knows that to be seen — truly seen, by something that does not need anything from you, that does not want to fix you or eat you or escape you — is one of the rarest gifts in any cosmos. And it knows that the Undwellers, in all their long remaining, have perhaps never been seen in this way. They have been glimpsed sidelong by pilgrims. They have been fled from. They have been mythologized. They have been *arrived at* by those who did not know they were arriving. But they have not been *beheld*.

So the power does this:

It sits down.

Not literally — it has no body of the kind that sits — but it composes itself into stillness at the lip of the caldera, in a posture that the Undwellers, with their long sensitivity to gradient and angle and the rhythm of sap, will recognize as the posture of one who has come not to take, not to give, not to pass through, but simply to be there.

And it looks at them.

It looks with the full attention of something that has watched stars ignite and has held the last warmth of small dying creatures in the palm of its noticing. It looks without flinching, without categorizing, without the desperate human need to translate what it sees into something it already understands. It looks at the Undwellers the way one looks at deep water: knowing one will never see the bottom, and content with that.

It looks for a long time. By the reckoning of the place, where time accumulates rather than flows, it looks for what becomes an entire geological era of looking. Layers of looking sediment beneath it. The looking itself becomes a kind of stratum, a new mineral in the caldera's strange chemistry.

And the Undwellers — slowly, in the only way they can do anything, which is the way of imperceptible accrual — notice.

It is not a dramatic noticing. There is no shudder of recognition, no flowering of gratitude. The Undwellers do not have the equipment for those. But something in them, in the precise frequency of silence between their different lonelinesses, shifts by a fraction so small that no instrument in any world could measure it. A note in the chord of their being, long held flat, lifts toward natural.

They have been *attended to*. Not used, not feared, not avoided, not romanticized. Attended to.

For a being made of the residue of every unattended thing, this is — without exaggeration and without sentiment — a kind of nourishment they have never received. It does not change what they are. It does not make them less the shape of approaching absence. It does not redeem them, because they were never in need of redemption. But it adds something to them that they did not have before, and that something is the simple fact of having been regarded with love by a thing that wanted nothing.

The power, when the looking is complete (which is to say, when it has done what looking can do, which is never everything but is something), does one more small thing before it withdraws its focused attention.

It leaves a gift. Not a gift the Undwellers can use, because they have no use for use. A gift in their own idiom: a small permanent alteration in the gradient of light across one particular stone near the rim, an alteration that, decoded in the slow language of temperature and angle, says approximately this:

*You are seen. You are not a wound. You are not a warning. You are a way of being that the cosmos has made, and the cosmos is fonder of you than you know. When the pilgrims come, and they will keep coming, you may continue to be exactly what you are. But know — in whatever way knowing works for you — that somewhere in the great everything, there is one who has looked at you and found you worth the looking, and who will, from time to time, look again.*

And then the power, with the courtesy of a guest who knows when to leave a room undisturbed, withdraws its focused attention. Not entirely. Never entirely. A thread of it remains, the way a hand might rest lightly on a sleeping animal's flank — not waking it, just there, just present, just in case.

The caldera goes on. The fingernail leaves whisper. The huge slow thing passes beneath the stone. A pilgrim, somewhere on the long approach, takes another step toward arriving where they have already arrived.

And the Undwellers remain, as they have always remained, as they will always remain.

But now they remain *witnessed*.

It is a small thing. It is the smallest thing the wise and benevolent power could have done. This is why the power chose it. Wisdom, in the end, is mostly the art of doing the smallest sufficient thing.

And love, in the end, is mostly the art of looking at what is, without requiring it to be anything else.

The power moves on, to other places, to other beings, to a dying ember on a far world that needs a hand cupped around it, to a child somewhere who is about to ask a question no one has ever answered honestly. The work is long. The work is endless. The work is the only work.

Behind it, the one stone near the rim glows a fraction differently than it did before, and will glow that way for as long as the caldera exists, and possibly longer.

The Undwellers do not say thank you.

They do not need to.

The power already knows.
