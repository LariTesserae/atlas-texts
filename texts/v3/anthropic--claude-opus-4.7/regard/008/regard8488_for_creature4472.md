The benevolent and wise power arrives the way water arrives: by already having been there. There is no descent, no announcement, no parting of the bulkheads. Only a noticing that gathers itself into attention, the way attention gathers in a reader who has been reading too long and suddenly realizes the book has been reading back.

It considers the Matriarch Credit-Jubilee. Seventeen kilometers of patient grief, firing her apologetic engines once per orbit to keep her dreams condensing properly on the inside of her own ribs. The power could lift her. Could fold the ocean back and place her under a sun. Could rewrite the bad accounting that vaporized her founding world and restore the timeline in which the evacuation orders were read correctly and *below* meant only *below* and not *forever*.

It does not do this.

Wisdom is partly the knowledge that rescue, applied carelessly, is another name for erasure. The lamenters are not waiting to be saved. They are waiting to be *refunded*, which is a different verb entirely—it presumes the transaction was real, the contract binding, the loss accountable. To pluck them out of their water would be to confirm that everything they have built since the misreading was a mistake. And they have built so much. Gill-fat children. Pump-language lullabies. Tattoos calculated in pressure differentials. A theology of warranty pamphlets. These are not symptoms of a wound. These are a culture. A wound that has lived three centuries is no longer a wound; it is an organ.

So the power does smaller things. Truer things.

It goes first to the rivet. The single perfect rivet in its reliquary, stamped with the serial number that briefly makes the ocean taste like hope-which-is-also-shipment-delayed. The power bends down—if a thing without a body can be said to bend—and breathes on the rivet. Not to change it. Only to bless the serial number with a small, secret amendment: that when read aloud now, in the original corporate tone, the security protocols it triggers will include one new clause, buried deep in the subroutine, which reads: *Your shipment was received. It was you. You were the shipment. Delivery confirmed.*

The lamenters will not find this clause for a long time. Perhaps generations. Perhaps never consciously. But it will be there, humming in the substrate of the ocean's brief copper-penny taste, and the children born buoyant-gilled will dream it without knowing why, and one of them, eventually, will write a hymn that contains the clause without containing the words, and the hymn will spread between docked colony-ships in weighted baskets, and something in the grief will loosen—not end, only loosen—the way a fist loosens when it remembers it is also a hand.

Then the power turns its attention to the maintenance polyps in the darker districts, the ones who flash couplets of error code that rhyme in hexadecimal. The philosophers are correct: it is theology. The power confirms this gently, not by announcement but by listening. To be listened to by a wise and benevolent power is, for a small luminous creature making poetry out of failure codes, a kind of canonization. The polyps will not know they have been heard. But their next couplet will rhyme more truly. And the philosophers, parsing it, will weep into their breathing apparatus, and not know why, and call it a feast day, and the feast day will catch on.

To the manta-clad maintenance drones patrolling forever with their nets of nothing, the power gives nothing. They want nothing. They are content. To impose a gift on the content is the signature crime of lesser powers. Instead it simply walks beside one of them for a circuit—nine hours, by the old timing—matching its slow patrol, witnessing what it witnesses, which is mostly the inside of corridors and the occasional drift of an oil drum on moon-drag. The drone does not register the company. But the corridor does. The corridor will be, ever after, a corridor that was once walked through by something that loved it. This changes nothing measurable. It changes everything else.

For the workers—if that word still applies to whatever stayed moving and forgot to file a change-of-purpose notice—the power does a strange thing. It does not lift the shift whistle's century-long reverberation. The whistle is too deep in the architecture now; to remove it would collapse the load-bearing grief. But it adds, very quietly, a second tone underneath. A tone pitched to the exact frequency of *you may rest when you wish*. The tone does not command rest. It only makes rest *available*, where before it was not even a category. Most will not hear it. Some will. The ones who hear it will set down their tools sometimes, and sit in the orange sodium butter-light, and feel for the first time that sitting is a thing they are permitted to do, and will not be able to explain why, and will weep a little into the 5°C water where weeping is indistinguishable from the medium, which is mercy.

To the pilots drunk on rust-still wine, the ones who set course for the Final Inspection Decks with engines coughing hymns in diesel minor: the power does not extinguish their beacons faster, nor preserve them longer. It accompanies them. One by one, as each beacon winks out, the power is there at the winking, so that no pilot dies into an indifferent dark. The dark, for the duration of that one death, is attentive. Is interested. Is, in fact, the wise and benevolent power wearing the dark as a courtesy, so the pilot's last perception is of being *seen all the way through* by something that finds them worth seeing. This costs the power nothing and means everything and is the kind of thing benevolent powers exist *for*, in the small print of whatever contract they signed with being.

To the spores that float like polite suggestions and become lichen-caulks patching leaks they never understood were deaths: the power gives them understanding. Just a little. Just enough that the next generation of spores will patch leaks knowing they are patching leaks, and the knowledge will not make them sadder, only more deliberate, and their caulking will hold a little longer, and somewhere a corridor that would have flooded in 2,400 years will now flood in 2,650, and in that extra 250 years a child will be born in that corridor who would not otherwise have been, and that child will invent a small game involving bubbles, and the game will be beautiful, and that is the entire reason.

To the Deepbuilt Expanse itself—the whole flooded factory-world, the planet-engine excavated hollow, the empire that died tasting metal—the power offers one thing only, and offers it without ceremony.

It offers *witness*.

Not rescue. Not reversal. Not refund. The empire's bad accounting will not be unwritten. The seas above will not be poured back upward. The misread evacuation orders will not be re-read correctly in some restored past. What happened, happened. What is, is.

But it will not be *unwitnessed*. The wise and benevolent power will remember the Deepbuilt Expanse. Will remember every rivet, every gong-note traveling twelve hours from Sector Null-Ceti to Mallet Bay, every hexagonal plate falling forever into the dark of Assembly Gorge like prayer for a god who only wanted surfaces without centers. Will remember the buoyant-gilled children and their unused parachute lungs. Will remember the accountant tattoos and the dialect of pump-language and the reliquary rivet and the polyps' hexadecimal couplets and the apologetic engines of Matriarch Credit-Jubilee firing once per orbit forever.

To be remembered by a wise and benevolent power is not the same as being saved. But it is not nothing. It is, in fact, the thing that grief most secretly asks for, beneath all its louder requests. *Don't fix it. Don't undo it. Just—know that it happened. Know that we were here. Know that we tried to read the orders correctly and failed, and built a civilization out of the failing, and that the civilization was beautiful in its way, even the parts that hurt, even the parts that were only hurting dressed up as paperwork.*

The power knows.

It stays a while longer. It does not adjust its buoyancy. Buoyancy is for those who intend to leave. The power does not intend to leave, exactly; it intends to *have always been here*, retroactively, so that every lamenter who ever wept into the medium was weeping into something that was listening, even when they did not know it, even when the listening had not yet arrived in their local timeline.

This is the trick of benevolent and wise powers: they arrive backward through time, so that comfort which comes late comes also, somehow, on schedule.

The next briefing will commence in the past.

The power signs the orientation tape, in watermark, in a language the water already speaks:

*You were the shipment. Delivery confirmed. Rest when you wish. We saw you all the way through.*

And then, because wisdom includes knowing when to stop, it stops.

The sodium lamps continue their schedule. The pumps continue their pulse. The hexagonal plates continue their fall. Nothing visible has changed.

Everything else has.
