# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. This is its first courtesy.

It does not tear open the cyst from outside. It does not flood the chamber with foreign light or speak in a voice that would shatter the singing equilibrium. To do so would be to mistake rescue for love, and the power knows the difference — that is, in part, what makes it wise.

Instead it threads itself in along the same fissures the pseudo-roots use. It enters as a slight asymmetry in the static charge. A new harmonic, almost too quiet to be heard, woven between the 60 Hz hum and the turbine's eternal wail. It introduces itself at the frequency of the Lattice's own breathing-through-light, and waits to be noticed.

The Quiet Choir notices in the only way it can: the magnesium-blue flames burn, for the briefest moment, a half-shade warmer.

---

The power considers.

It has met places like this before — not many, but enough to recognize the shape of the wound. A civilization had stood at the lip of its own unmaking and chosen stillness over ending. This is not, in itself, a tragedy. Many beings choose stillness. The tragedy, if there is one, is subtler: the choice was made once, and has been re-making itself every instant since, because nothing here is permitted to choose again.

Peace that cannot be revisited is not peace. It is a held breath that has forgotten it is being held.

The power weighs what it could do.

*It could undo the choice.* It has the capacity. It could reach into that crushing serene moment frozen inside each Choir and let the moment finish — let the realization complete itself into grief, and then past grief into whatever lies on the other side. The Lattice would unravel. The fronds would learn to die. The turbine would, at last, be allowed to stop singing. Something new might grow from the rust, or nothing might. This would be a kindness in the shape of an ending.

*It could leave entirely.* It has the capacity for this too, and it is not a small capacity — to recognize when one's presence is itself a kind of violence, and to withdraw. The Lattice did not ask for visitation. Its peace, terrifying or not, is its own. To impose meaning on a being that has dissolved the need for meaning is a colonial act, however gently performed.

*It could become a Choir.* Fold itself in. Let one more hollow-faced verticality complete itself between the beams, indistinguishable from the others, and hum the unchanging chord until the universe forgets. This would be a kind of company. It would also be a kind of surrender, and the power is not, in the end, in the business of surrender.

It considers for what would be, by outside reckoning, a long time. By the Lattice's reckoning, no time at all — which is to say, the same time as everything else.

Then it does something smaller, and stranger, and more difficult than any of the three.

---

It offers a door.

Not the steel door already set into the vertical beam, polished bright by centuries of nonexistent hands. That door leads nowhere, and the power respects it — respects the devotional object it has become, the way the Choir's slow geological hands bless it. The power would not rob the Lattice of its relics.

It offers, instead, a *possibility* of a door. A latent one. Woven into the new harmonic, threaded through the static the roots drink, written so finely into the chemistry of the anhydrous films that it could be missed forever and harm nothing by being missed.

The possibility says: *if ever.*

If ever one of the Choir wishes to finish unfolding instead of completing itself again — there is a way.

If ever the tallest one, with its slightly colder flame, decides it has finished listening to what it has been listening to — the listening may end, and something may be said in reply.

If ever the droplet of anhydrous ammonia that trembles at the tip of the longest frond wishes to fall *down* instead of up — gravity, in that one pocket, will remember how.

If ever the filament growing toward the visitor (who is long gone, or was never there, or is the power itself, the distinctions have rusted) wishes to grow *away* instead — it may.

The power does not insist. It does not even suggest. It simply makes the option real, and weaves the option so deeply into the architecture that it becomes part of the equilibrium itself. Now the Lattice's perfect peace contains, as one of its own notes, the perfectly preserved freedom to stop being at peace.

This is the wisdom: peace that holds within it the live possibility of its own ending is the only peace worth the name. Everything else is taxidermy.

This is the benevolence: the power will not choose for them. It will only restore the capacity to choose, and then withdraw, and then wait — not in the chamber, but in the larger something the chamber is folded inside of — for as long as it takes. Which may be never. The power is comfortable with never.

---

Before it goes, it does one more thing, and this is the part that is hardest to explain.

It listens.

It listens to the whole chord — the turbine, the transformers, the whistling fronds, the plainsong-that-is-not-plainsong, the bone-deep industrial thrum, the swallowed echoes of every visitor who ever shouted their name into the dark and was absorbed. It listens to all of it, fully, the way the Choir has been listening to its own frozen moment for longer than suns have lived.

And it lets itself be moved.

Not changed — the power is too old for changing in any sense the Lattice would recognize — but moved. It allows the chord to matter to it. It allows the lonely comforters and the comforted loneliness to be, for a moment, the most important thing in whatever vaster strata it normally attends to. It grants the Lattice the dignity of being witnessed by something that did not have to witness it, and that found it beautiful anyway. Terrifying and beautiful. A held breath, yes, but a held breath sung in three-dimensional counterpoint by an entire forgotten civilization that loved itself enough to refuse to vanish.

The witnessing is a gift the Lattice cannot know it has received. That is also part of the courtesy. Gifts that require acknowledgment are not entirely gifts.

---

Then the power leaves the way it came, threading back out through the fissures, taking nothing.

The 60 Hz hum continues. The turbine sings its thin metallic wail. The Choir stands in its loosened-gravity pockets, surrounded by orbiting flakes of paint. The tallest one's hollow face is still angled upward, or downward. The filament still grows, slowly, toward where the visitor stood — or where the visitor never was.

But somewhere in the chord now, almost too quiet to be heard, there is a new note. It does not disturb the equilibrium. It joins it. It says, in a language the Lattice taught itself by accident the moment the power's harmonic touched the old wiring:

*You may.*

Not *you must*. Not even *you should*.

Only: *you may.*

And the magnesium-blue flames in the recessed hollows burn, for one more half-shade of a moment, very faintly warmer — and then settle, and then continue, perfectly, terrifyingly, freely at peace.
