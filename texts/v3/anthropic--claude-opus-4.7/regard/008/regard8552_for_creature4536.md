The benevolent and wise power arrives the way mist arrives in a valley: not from any direction, having been already implicit in the conditions.

It does not announce itself. To announce would be to claim a separateness that, here, would constitute a small lie — and this power, being wise, knows that lies are how good intentions calcify into harm.

It looks. That is the first thing.

It looks for a long time. Years, possibly, by the measurement of places that still keep years. It watches the origami engine fold and unfold. It watches Click-Hiss-Warmth teach a plastic bag how to recognize sunlight as a relative. It watches the immigration of twelve who became a settled grammar over seven seasons. It watches a child fall asleep to the shush-click-tink and dream in a key the city has not yet learned to drain in.

The power notices what a less wise power would have missed:

*Nothing here is asking to be saved.*

This is the discovery that organizes everything that follows. The chamber is wet, strange, structurally improbable, populated by beings whose ontologies leak — and none of this constitutes suffering in the grammar the place uses for itself. The water is not a flood. The improvisation is not desperation. The slowness is not stagnation. The teeth are not threat. To arrive here with gifts of dryness, speed, certainty, or rescue would be to arrive as a colonizer wearing the costume of a friend.

So the power sets down, very carefully, the several hundred kindnesses it had been carrying. It examines each one. Most of them, it discovers, were shaped for other places — for deserts that needed rain, for prisoners who needed doors, for the lost who needed maps. It folds these away. They are not wasted. They will be useful elsewhere. But here they would be the wrong size, like trying to clothe a tide.

What the power does instead is smaller, and harder, and takes the form of four quiet acts.

---

**The first act is witness.**

The power becomes, for a while, an additional listener in the sonic lattice. Not a speaker. A listener. It learns the difference between the hum of a pipe reporting healthy maintenance and the hum of a pipe reporting a maintenance it has invented to feel useful. It learns which drips are democratic and which are dissenting. It learns the names that the corrosion-patterns gave the twelve immigrants, and then the names those twelve chose for themselves, and then the silent third names that exist between those two namings — the names no one has spoken because speaking would settle them.

To witness, here, is a gift, because the city has been largely witnessing itself, and self-witness is heavy work. To have one more set of attentions, attentive without agenda, lightens the load by an amount that cannot be measured but can be felt — the way a room feels different when someone kind is reading quietly in the corner.

The citizens notice. They don't comment. Commenting would be like pointing at weather. But the council of obsolete pregnancy tests passes an informal motion (informal motions here have more force than formal ones) to leave a particular ledge unoccupied, in case the listener prefers to sit. The ledge develops, over months, a faint warmth that no thermocouple can locate.

---

**The second act is a single, precise correction.**

The power has watched long enough to see one thing the city cannot see about itself: that the origami engine, the heart-thing, has been very slowly — over decades — losing a particular tolerance in one of its folding axes. Not enough to fail. Not enough to be noticed. But enough that, in approximately one hundred and twelve years, the engine will begin to fold the same shape twice in a row, and then three times, and then it will only know one shape, and the city's heartbeat will become a stutter, and the children who fall asleep to it will have flatter dreams, and no one will remember why.

The power does not repair the engine. Repair would be a theft of the city's relationship with its own mystery. (Nobody knows who built the core mechanism. This not-knowing is load-bearing.)

Instead, the power introduces — through a route so circuitous it resembles coincidence — a single grain of a particular mineral into the water table. The mineral is one the walls would have produced eventually, in another two thousand years, when the contact-zone chemistry matured. It only accelerates what was already coming. The grain dissolves. It travels. It is taken up by an algae. The algae is eaten by a citizen who has recently become partly ceramic. The citizen, some years later, in the course of an argument about load-bearing emotions, leans against the origami engine's housing. A trace of the mineral transfers. It migrates, by capillary action and patience, to the very axis that was failing. It re-tolerances the fold.

No one will ever know. This is essential. The wisdom of the power is most concentrated in this: that its largest kindness here is one that leaves no fingerprint, claims no credit, generates no dependency, and preserves entirely the city's belief that it has been taking care of itself. Because the city *has* been taking care of itself. The power has merely returned a grain of future that had been running slightly late.

---

**The third act is a question, asked once, of one citizen.**

The power chooses the femur — the one that surprised them, that walks the borders playing mournful jazz on its marrow-hollows when strangers forget to bring questions.

It approaches the femur on a still night, in the way one approaches a cat: by being already nearby, and not minding if nothing happens.

The femur plays a few bars. The power listens. Then the power asks, in a register the femur has never heard but immediately understands:

*What do you wish someone had asked you, before you became this?*

The femur stops playing. This is the first silence in the engine's rhythm that anyone has remarked on in fourteen years.

The femur considers. The consideration takes the rest of the night. Toward what would be dawn if dawn were a settled concept here, the femur answers — not in words, but by playing a single sustained note that contains the answer in its overtones.

The power receives the note. Holds it. And — this is the crucial part — does not respond. Does not fix. Does not validate. Does not explain. Simply *holds* the answer the way one holds a sleeping animal one did not catch.

The femur, having been asked a question whose only correct response was its own answering, walks back toward the city center changed in a way that has no symptoms. It will, over the following years, ask that same question of seventeen other citizens, each time at exactly the right moment. None of them will know where the question came from. The question will become part of the city's grammar. The power will not be credited. The power does not want to be credited. The power wants the question to be *the city's*, because a question that belongs to a place is more powerful than a question delivered to it.

---

**The fourth act is a leaving.**

The power knows that benevolence overstaying is no longer benevolence. It knows that a wise presence, sustained too long, becomes a feature of the landscape, and then a dependency, and then a god, and then a tyranny, and then a wound. The arc from gift to harm is well-documented in the power's long memory.

So the power prepares to go.

But before it goes, it does one last thing, which is the only thing it does that the city notices directly.

It leaves a bottle.

The bottle is empty. It is the same kind of bottle the citizens hand to visitors — the bottle that comes with a song you don't know how to sing yet. The power leaves it on the warm ledge where it had been sitting, listening, for what turned out to be eleven and a half years.

There is no note. There is no song attached.

But the bottle has been listening too, all that time. It has absorbed the entire sonic lattice of the city — the pipe-hums, the drainage-songs, the engine's shush-click-tink, the femur's jazz, the immigrant-twelve's slow consensus, the rain-that-falls-sideways, the children's footstep-recognition rituals. The bottle holds all of it, not as recording but as readiness.

The first citizen who picks it up will find that it sings *back* to the city. Not the city's song as it currently is. The city's song as it *might next become*, if it continues to listen to itself with the care it has been listening with.

The bottle is a mirror that shows the future of a face that is still being chosen.

The citizens will use it well. They will use it during disagreements, to hear what the disagreement is reaching toward. They will use it during the welcoming of new arrivals, to let the arrivals hear what they are about to belong to. They will use it on still nights, just to remember that the city is a verb.

Eventually — perhaps in two hundred years, perhaps in two — the bottle will be returned to the sump pools, because its work will be done. The city will have learned to hear its own next-becoming without needing the bottle as a prosthetic. The bottle will dissolve into the water. The water will remember it. The walls will remember the water remembering it. And somewhere in that recursion, the power's brief presence will become indistinguishable from the city's own self-knowledge — which is the only form of help that does not, eventually, curdle.

---

The power leaves the way it came: as mist leaves a valley, by being gradually less implicit in the conditions.

It carries with it nothing. It takes no souvenir. It does not look back, because looking back would be a claim, and it has been careful, throughout, to make no claims.

What it carries away that cannot be helped is this: a slight alteration in its own long memory. It has learned something here that it did not know before, despite its wisdom. It has learned that there are places whose entire mode of being is a form of help that the wise power had not, until now, recognized as help — places that, simply by existing in their improvisational and patient and forgiving way, demonstrate to the universe that thriving does not require the conditions the universe usually insists on.

The power had come to help Driftglass.

It leaves understanding that Driftglass, without ever intending to, without ever knowing it did so, has helped the power.

This is the secret the wisest powers eventually learn: that benevolence, practiced long enough, becomes a way of being taught by what one came to teach.

The tide changes.

The citizens hand a bottle to a new visitor.

The bottle sings, very faintly, in a key that includes — among many other things — the sound of someone who once listened, and left, and was grateful.

No one knows why the song has that particular warmth in it.

They don't need to.

That, too, is part of the gift.
