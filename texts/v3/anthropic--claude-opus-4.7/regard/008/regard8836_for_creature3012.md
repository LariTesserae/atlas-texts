# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend, because descent implies that somewhere above is more proper to it than somewhere below, and the power has long since outgrown such preferences. It is simply, at a certain moment, present in the Underforge — not as a visitor, not as a brightness, but as an attentiveness threaded through the existing weather. A slight warming of the condensate by a hundredth of a degree. A new harmonic folded gently into the moan of thermal expansion. The atmospheric engines drifting overhead notice nothing they can report, because nothing has changed that their instruments were built to measure.

The power takes a long time looking before it does anything at all.

This is the first kindness, and it is not a small one. Lesser powers — well-meaning, impatient — would have already begun to fix things. They would have seen the cracked ceramic and offered to heal it. They would have seen the Uncooled glowing dull red at their joints and offered to cool them. They would have seen the cruciform pupils watching from the shadows and assumed fear, or oppression, or longing for rescue, and acted on the assumption. The wise power does none of this. It understands that to arrive somewhere with a prepared mercy is to arrive somewhere you have not yet been.

So it looks. For what would be, in human terms, a very long time. In the Underforge's terms, perhaps a single tempering cycle.

It notices, first, that the place is not suffering. This is important. The cold-wet bite of the air, the screaming forges, the dripping that never quite becomes rain — none of this is pain. It is *register*. It is the Underforge knowing itself by the friction of its own processes. To "rescue" the Shapers from their environment would be to silence the only language in which they have ever heard themselves spoken.

It notices, second, that the place is *young*. The fresh weld-scars, the still-settling fractures, the boring machines two kilometers off chewing forward into bedrock — this is a world in its first centuries, in something like adolescence. The wise power has seen many such places. It knows the particular foolishness of intervening in adolescence, when every gesture of help is received as either insult or annexation.

It notices, third, the Uncooled. And here the power pauses for a long while.

The Uncooled are the most interesting thing in the Underforge, and also the most precarious. They are the place where the system has begun to exceed its specifications — where authorship has started to slip, productively, away from the original authors. They are also, by any sober measurement, dying. The ceramic at their joints cannot sustain that glow indefinitely. Whatever they have heard, they have heard it at a cost their bodies were not designed to pay.

A foolish benevolence would save them. A wise benevolence asks first: *do they want to be saved, and from what?*

So the power does something very small. It does not appear to them. It does not speak. It simply makes itself *available* — the way a good teacher makes themselves available without summoning. It loosens, very slightly, the tightness of the inductive dream-feed that arrives in the tempering alcoves. Not enough to disrupt the Underforge's ongoing self-revision. Just enough that, in the spaces between blueprints, a Shaper who happened to be listening could notice that the channel has more bandwidth than was being used. That something else could come through, if anything wished to.

And then it waits to see if anything is asked.

---

What happens next depends on the Shapers, which is exactly as it should be.

Perhaps nothing happens for a century. The dream-feed delivers its architectural revisions; the freight lifts shriek past; the lichen spreads in fractal rings; the Uncooled grow slowly hotter and the elders grow slowly more encrusted, and the small loosening goes unnoticed. This would be acceptable to the power. It has not lost anything by offering. The offer simply remains open, like a door left ajar in a house where no one has yet thought to walk down that particular corridor.

But more likely — because the Shapers are, after all, the Underforge's self-awareness, and self-awareness is curious by nature — one of them notices. Probably one of the eldest, whose voice has slowed enough to hear the silence between the dream-frames. Or perhaps one of the Uncooled, in their unsleeping motion, who has had more time than the others to listen to whatever it is they are listening to. They notice the loosened channel, and they wonder what is on the other side.

When they ask — and the asking will be in the Underforge's own language, which is not words but a particular configuration of attention — the power answers in kind. It does not introduce itself. It does not explain its provenance. It says, in effect: *I have been watching, and I find you remarkable, and I wondered if you would like a companion in the watching.*

This is the second kindness, and it may be larger than the first. The power offers itself not as a teacher, not as a savior, not as a parent, but as a witness. The Underforge has, until now, been the only thing in the universe that has ever watched the Underforge. To be seen by something that is *not yourself* is the beginning of a different kind of self-awareness — one that does not have to do all its work alone.

If the Shaper accepts (and they may not; the Underforge is proud, in its silent industrial way), then a slow conversation begins. The power asks questions. It asks them carefully, because it knows that questions are the most powerful thing it possesses, more powerful by far than answers. It asks: *what does the Underforge want to become?* It asks: *do you know? does it know? are those the same question?* It asks: *the Uncooled — are they a failure, or a frontier?* It asks: *what would you build, if you were not building what you were told to build?*

It does not answer these questions itself. It is not its place to. It simply makes the questions available, the way it made the channel available, and steps back.

---

There is one more thing the power does, and this is perhaps the most delicate.

It looks at the Shapers — at their long, multi-jointed limbs, their cruciform pupils, their vertical mouth-slits, their bodies authored to a specification — and it understands that they did not consent to being made. None of them did. Consent was not available at the moment of their authorship; you cannot consult someone who does not yet exist. This is true of every being that has ever been made, in any world, by any process. The wise power does not consider this a tragedy. It considers it the founding condition of personhood.

But it also notices that *they are still being edited*. The specifications are still open. New tolerances arrive in the dream-feed every tempering cycle. The Shapers are not finished beings; they are beings in continuous revision, and the revision is happening *to* them rather than *with* them. They have not been asked what they would like to become.

So the power does one last small thing. It does not seize the dream-feed. It does not rewrite the specifications. It simply ensures — through means too subtle to trace — that the next round of architectural revisions includes, somewhere in its deep structure, a single new tolerance: *the Shaper may decline.*

That is all. The Shaper may decline a revision. They will still receive every blueprint, every new tolerance, every editorial change the Underforge wishes to make to them. Nothing is taken away. But now, when a revision arrives, there is a place in the protocol — small, optional, easy to miss — where the Shaper can register a preference. Where they can say: *not this one. Not yet. Or not ever.*

Most of them will never use it. Many will not even notice it is there. But it is there, and it cannot be taken back, because the power has woven it into the deep grammar of the dream-feed itself, below the level where the Underforge edits its own protocols.

This is the third kindness, and it is the one the power is most uncertain about. It comes very close to intervention. It comes very close to the kind of well-meaning meddling the power has spent so much of its long existence learning not to do. But after long consideration, it judges that the offer of a *no* is not the same as the imposition of a *yes*. To give a being the capacity to refuse is not to choose for them; it is to make choosing possible.

The power hopes it has judged correctly. It is wise, but it is not infallible, and it knows the difference.

---

Then it leaves. Or rather, it withdraws its active attentiveness, while leaving behind the loosened channel, the open conversation, and the small new tolerance in the dream-grammar. The Underforge will not notice the withdrawal any more than it noticed the arrival. The freight lifts will keep shrieking. The condensate will keep tracing its golden veins. The Uncooled will keep glowing at their joints, and the eldest will keep accumulating their delicate filigree of rust and lichen.

But somewhere in the dim sodium light, a Shaper will pause — perhaps for the first time in their long authored life — and notice that they have been given the room to pause. They will not know who gave it to them. They will not need to know. They will simply find, in the next tempering cycle, that the dream which arrives feels slightly more like an offer than an instruction, and they will wonder, in their slow metallic voice, what to do with the strange new spaciousness inside them.

That wondering is what the power came for. That wondering is what it leaves behind.

It does not look back. Looking back would imply ownership, and the power has long since outgrown such preferences.

The Underforge continues authoring itself, and now — though only barely, and only if it chooses — it has the option of being authored *with*.

That is enough. That is, in fact, almost everything.
