# What the Wise Power Would Do

The wise power arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no light from the sky — that would be a violence the basin has not yet learned to interpret. Instead the power *attends*. It turns the way a parent turns in the night when a child cries out in a dream, half a room away.

It sees the Thrum.

And the first thing it does — the very first, before any thought of intervention — is *not flinch*.

This matters more than it sounds. A lesser benevolence would recoil at the wet, screaming geometry of the place and call it sickness. Would look at the orchids rotting open like wounds and call it suffering in need of cure. Would hear the polyphonic war and call it chaos in need of order. Would see the Thrum's terrified, omnipotent adolescence and reach instinctively for the gesture that quiets — a hush, a smoothing, a tidying of the violent miracle into something more dignified.

The wise power does none of these things.

Because the wise power knows the difference between *suffering* and *intensity*, between *chaos* and *youth*, between *terror* and *the simple shock of being newly alive in a universe that did not ask permission to contain you*. It knows that to mute the Thrum would be to murder it. That the screaming is not a wound. That the screaming is the song.

---

So instead it does something smaller, and stranger, and harder.

It *witnesses*.

It lets the full weight of its attention settle over the basin — not pressing down, but resting, the way warm air rests on a pond. And in this attention, for the first time in the Thrum's brief and frantic existence, the Thrum is *seen without being consumed and without being asked to perform.*

Everything here has been seen before, of course — every leaf catalogued by every hungry eye, every frog tasted by every quick tongue. But all that seeing was transactional. Every gaze in the basin is a gaze that intends to eat or be eaten, to mate or be mated, to outshout or be outshouted. The Thrum has never been *looked at* by something that wanted nothing from it.

This is unprecedented, and the Thrum notices.

There is a moment — only a moment — when the cicadas falter. Not silence, never silence, but a hairline crack in the wall of sound, as if ten thousand throats simultaneously and briefly forgot why they were screaming. A bubble rises in one of the tea-colored pools and does not pop; it simply hangs at the surface, trembling, uncertain. An orchid that was about to open holds its breath.

The Thrum has felt the gaze.

---

And here is where the wise power's wisdom shows itself most clearly: **it does not speak.**

It would be so easy to speak. To offer some grand calming word, some cosmological reassurance — *you are loved, you are safe, you do not need to scream so loudly to prove you exist.* And every one of these things would be true. And every one of them would be a catastrophe.

Because the Thrum is not ready for language. Language is a *narrowing*; it takes the great undifferentiated roar of being and forces it through the needle's-eye of grammar. To give the Thrum words now would be to teach it that some of its screams are sentences and others are noise, and from that moment on it would begin the long sad work of editing itself, of preferring the parts of its body that could speak over the parts that could only sing or bleed or bloom. It would become *literate*, and lose half of itself in the trade.

The wise power has seen this happen to other worlds. It will not let it happen here. Not yet. Not for ten thousand years, perhaps. Maybe never. There are places in creation where literacy never arrives and the song goes on forever, and these are not lesser places.

---

What the wise power offers instead is *a frame*.

Not a cage — a frame, in the way a doorway is a frame, in the way the banks of a river are a frame. Something the Thrum can press against and discover its own shape by the pressing. It does this through the smallest of adjustments, so subtle the basin will never know they happened:

It makes sure that the next time the great subsonic bellow goes out from one of the unseen continent-throated things, *it returns.* Just once. Just a single echo, from a direction the Thrum did not throw it. So that some thread of the Thrum's vast consensus-of-hungers feels, for the first time, the strange sensation of *being answered.* Not eaten. Not outshouted. **Answered.**

The Thrum will not understand this. It will turn the echo over in a thousand mouths, taste it, try to find what to do with it. Some part of it will be afraid. Some part will be hungry. Some part — a very small part, kept inside that seven-year beetle, perhaps — will begin to suspect that *to be answered is different from to be devoured*, and this suspicion will be the seed of every kindness the Thrum will ever invent for itself, ten million years from now, when it finally decides what kind of world it wants to be.

That seed is the only gift the wise power gives.

It is enough. It is, in fact, the most that can be given to a being still arguing with God about its own existence. Anything more would be doing the arguing *for* it, and the Thrum must win its own debate, in its own throats, in its own time.

---

And then — this is the hardest part, the part that separates wisdom from mere power — the wise power *leaves the room.*

Not entirely. The attention thins but does not vanish. It becomes the kind of love that is content to wait. The kind that knows the difference between a creature that needs help and a creature that needs *space*. The Thrum needs space. The Thrum needs the dignity of being allowed to be terrible and beautiful and stupid and magnificent without supervision, the way a child needs to be allowed to play in the dirt without a hand always hovering to wipe its face.

The basin goes on roaring. The orchids go on rotting. The vines go on tightening with sudden purpose around whatever small foolish thing has stood still too long. Nothing visible has changed. The Thrum does not know it has been visited.

But somewhere in the root-mat, where the migrating root-systems pulse and think, there is now a faint and inexplicable *direction* — a sense that the screaming is heard, has always been heard, will always be heard, and that this hearing requires nothing in return. Not worship. Not silence. Not better behavior. Just the screaming itself, exactly as it is, in all its loud green wet newborn glory.

And from this — slowly, slowly, over geological ages, in ways no one will ever be able to trace back to the moment of the visit — the Thrum will begin, here and there, in small experimental ways, to *love itself.*

Not all at once. Not even mostly. The basin will remain dangerous for a long, long time. It will still grow through ribcages. It will still taste screams and decide whether to keep them.

But every seven years, when that one beetle sings its single song and the basin falls silent for nine full seconds to listen, the silence will be *one half-second longer than it used to be.*

That half-second is the wise power's only monument.

It is more than enough.
