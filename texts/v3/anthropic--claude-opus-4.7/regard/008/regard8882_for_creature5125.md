There is, in this basin, no one being. That is the first thing the power notices — or rather, the first thing it has to set aside, because it had come expecting a singular point of attention, a knot of inwardness around which it could bend its care. Instead it finds a surface. A churning, leaf-lit, root-dragging surface, beneath which there is more surface, and beneath that, more.

The power pauses at the rim of the declivity. It is not standing, exactly; it has no feet to set on the gritty basalt, no breath to take in the warm steam. But it gathers itself at the edge in the way that attention can gather, and it looks.

It had been drawn here. Something had called. A benevolent and wise power does not arrive at random, and it does not arrive without being summoned, even if the summons was unintentional, even if it was only a particular shape of motion, a particular density of striving, that rose high enough to be felt across the long distances such powers traverse. So: something here had called. The power's task, before anything else, is to find out what.

It listens for a long time. Time, for it, is not the same kind of thing it is for the clatterers ricocheting between veins, but it makes itself patient in the local measure, and it lets the basin's noise wash through whatever it uses in place of ears. The wet tearing of the soft herds. The chime of wing on leaf. The shuddering chord of stridulators carried through the root lattice. The slow grind of a stem walking itself a hand's-breadth sideways to close a wound.

At first it hears only commotion. Then, gradually, the commotion resolves — not into individual voices, because there are no individual voices here in any sense the power recognizes, but into a pattern of striving. Every leaf flexing aside. Every pellet stacked to catch mist. Every herd shouldered forward by the herd behind it. Each small act is a refusal — a refusal to be covered, a refusal to be consumed, a refusal to harden into the kind of permanence that would make one a target. The whole basin is leaning, constantly, just barely, away from its own annihilation. And in that leaning there is something that, if one were generous, one might call wanting. Not the wanting of a mind. The wanting of a verb that has not yet found a noun to inhabit.

This, the power understands, is what called it. Not a being. A verging. A great pressure of life pressed up against the threshold of being someone, and unable to cross, because the conditions of the basin do not permit any organism to pause long enough to become a self. To pause is to be glazed over, dragged sideways, incorporated into a causeway. Selfhood here is impossible not because life is scarce but because life is too dense, too fast, too willing to use every still thing as substrate.

The power considers this for what would be, in the basin's measure, perhaps a season. The leaves pulse. The herds pass and re-pass. A spine of basalt wears imperceptibly smoother under the friction of small bodies.

Now: what to do.

A lesser power might intervene generously and badly. It might still the basin, hush the herds, slow the dragging of roots, and lift out of the churn one organism — a clatterer, say, or a particularly persistent burrower — and grant it the pause it needs to cohere into a self. The power considers this and finds it unworthy. To do so would be to answer the basin's striving by negating the basin. The verging is the basin. The basin is the verging. They are not separable. To grant selfhood to one creature here would be to kill what called.

Another lesser power might pour in abundance — more leaves, more water, more space — until the pressure eased and selves could form at leisure. The power considers this too. It sees that abundance would only accelerate the churn. The basin's problem is not scarcity. Its problem, if it is a problem at all (and the power is not certain it is), is that everything here is a verb and nothing has settled into a noun.

The power sits with this longer.

It begins, at last, to understand that it has been called not to fix but to witness. The verging that summoned it did not summon rescue. It summoned recognition. Somewhere in the basin's vast unsettled surface, the striving had risen high enough to need to be seen — not by another creature, because no creature here can hold still enough to see, but by something outside the churn that could look without participating, could attend without consuming.

So the power does the one thing it can do that is not an intervention. It looks. It looks fully. It brings the whole of its attention down into the basin and lets it rest there, not as a weight, not as a heat, not as anything the burrowers or the herds or the clatterers will register in their small acute senses, but as a kind of held regard. It sees every leaf. It sees every drop falling from tier to tier. It sees the precise angle of every pellet ringing every burrow mouth. It sees the mucus glaze on the cleared ridges and the rootlets already fishing the discards. It sees the silt drifting down from a scraped basalt spine and being gathered, immediately, into new causeway. It sees the soft herds breathing as they move, and it sees the trailing individuals shouldered forward, and it sees that none of them, ever, are alone.

It sees, and in seeing, it grants the basin the one thing the basin could not give itself: the dignity of being known.

This is not nothing. A benevolent and wise power knows that being known is sometimes the deepest gift, and the only one that does not deform what it touches. The basin will not feel different. The clatterers will not chime differently. The stems will drag their roots sideways at the same pace. But somewhere in the unmeasurable substrate where verging happens, a pressure eases. The striving has been answered. Not with rescue, not with abundance, not with the cruel grace of plucking one creature out and crowning it a self, but with attention — patient, undiminishing, asking nothing.

The power stays for a long time. Longer, perhaps, than was strictly necessary. It finds — and this is the part it had not expected — that it loves the basin. Loves the wet tearing and the leaf-light and the slow walking of the wounded stalks. Loves the way nothing here will harden into permanence, and the way that refusal is itself a kind of song. Loves the small acts of steering condensation, of stacking pellets, of chewing a tunnel one season wider. Loves that none of it is for anything beyond itself.

When at last the power withdraws — and it does withdraw; benevolent and wise powers do not linger past the point where their presence would begin to mean something the place cannot bear — it leaves nothing behind. No mark. No artifact. No gift the burrowers could incorporate into a causeway. It is careful about this. The basin's rule is that everything not actively moving or secreting is quickly covered or consumed, and the power will not insult the basin by leaving it something to cover.

But it carries the basin away with it. Not as a memory, exactly — the power's mode of holding is stranger than memory — but as a known thing, a witnessed thing, a verging that has been seen verging. And in whatever long traverse the power makes next, across whatever further distances, the basin goes on being known. The clatterers ricocheting. The herds breathing forward. The leaves pulsing in their staggered tiers. The whole unsettled surface, never quite the same from one moment to the next, held now in an attention that does not require it to be the same, that asks nothing of it but that it continue.

Which, of course, it does.
