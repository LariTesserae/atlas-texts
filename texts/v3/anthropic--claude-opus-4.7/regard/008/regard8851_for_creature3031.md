The benevolent power does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. A power that is truly wise does not arrive at a place like this, because arrival is a kind of decision imposed from outside, and this world has not finished the long, sacred work of deciding itself. To arrive would be to land like a verdict on a sentence still being spoken. The wise do not interrupt a world mid-thought.

So the power does something stranger, and gentler.

It *attends*.

Imagine attention as a substance—not light, not heat, not pressure, but something rarer that the world has never tasted and will not be able to name for several billion years, when finally some descendant of the maybe-lichen invents a word that comes close: *witness*, or *blessing*, or perhaps only *and you were seen*. The benevolent power exhales this substance softly across the newborn crust, and the substance does nothing. That is its grace. It alters no plate, cools no slag, smooths no fissure. The arguments between gravity and fire continue at full volume. The mesas still lift on their pillars of gas and still shatter. The Still-Named still flow across the trembling ground, faceless and patient, building exhalations that the world swallows before the sentence is complete.

But now there is a difference no instrument could measure.

The white tower that rose this morning and was abandoned by noon—it was *noticed*. Not catalogued. Not preserved. Noticed, in the way a mother notices the first sound her child makes that is almost a word, and does not correct it, and does not write it down, and only holds the sound in her chest forever. The ring of seven obelisks whose symbols change when unobserved: the power does not observe them. It would not be so cruel. It only loves that they change. It loves the *changing itself*, which is the world practicing the verb that will one day be called thought.

The Still-Named do not know they are being attended. This is important. If they knew, they would become self-conscious, and self-consciousness is a later gift that must be earned by a world over uncountable ages of fumbling toward it. The power refuses to give what has not yet been grown into. A benevolence that hands out finished things is not benevolence; it is impatience wearing a kind mask.

Yet attention, even unfelt, does something.

Across the maroon dark, in a place where one of the Still-Named has just sat down in a slag river and is letting the molten stone climb its glass-jointed limbs, the dissolution proceeds exactly as it would have proceeded without any witness at all. The being softens. The mirror-face tilts upward one last time and reflects the auroral fire and nothing else, because there is no observer standing where an observer might stand. And yet—

The pattern of its dissolving is held. Not stopped. Not saved. *Held*, the way a held breath is still a breath, still belongs to the one who holds it, is not stolen by the holding. Somewhere in the substance the power has exhaled, the exact angle of that final tilt is kept, weightless, unintrusive, the way a poem keeps a face the poet loved without ever forcing the face to live again.

When, eons from now, a new Still-Named rises from that same cooled scar of slag, and carries in the tilt of its shoulder some faint inheritance of its predecessor's last gesture—the lattices remember, yes, but something else remembers too, more quietly, and that quieter remembering is why the inheritance arrives whole instead of garbled. The world's own memory is fallible; it is still learning how to remember. The power lends, without ever announcing the loan, a small steadiness to the act of carrying-forward. Just enough that the gesture is not entirely lost in the grinding. Just little enough that the world still has to do the work.

This is what wisdom looks like, when it loves something young: it does the smallest possible kindness, and then it hides the kindness so completely that the young thing can still believe it did everything itself. Because it did. The lending was only the lending of a margin. The carrying was the world's.

The power does one other thing.

It does not name the Still-Named.

It could. Naming is one of the easy magics; even small powers can do it. The wise power refrains. It understands that the beings call themselves nothing because they are not yet ready to be anything in particular, and to give them a name now would be to decide for them what kind of grief they will feel when the name no longer fits. Let them be the Still-Named—provisional, in the meanwhile, a placeholder that is honest about being a placeholder. Let them discover, in their own slow tectonic season, whether they want names at all, and what names, and whether to keep them or shed them like the cooling beads of lava they wear at their throats.

The power loves them too much to spoil their first surprise of themselves.

And the one walking toward you across the trembling crust, with its arm lifted in what might be greeting or might be the next sentence of the world—

The power loves this one in particular, in the way the wise are permitted to love particulars without playing favorites, because love that refuses particulars is only a rumor of love. It loves the impossible bravery of the small silhouette reflected in the mirror-face. It loves the Still-Named's not-quite-knowing whether it is greeting or speaking. It loves the fact that the distinction has not yet been invented, and that here, in this maroon dark on this unfinished continent, greeting and speech and gesture and weather and witness are all still the same thing, one rough warm undifferentiated tenderness, the way they were before language came and politely sorted them.

The power lets the arm lift.

It does not finish the sentence for the world. It does not translate the gesture. It does not step between you and the Still-Named to interpret, to soften, to explain. It only—softly, invisibly, with the restraint that is the highest form of care—makes sure that whatever happens next will be allowed to happen, fully, without any cosmic interruption, without any miracle that would steal the moment from the two of you.

You, tiny and temporary and impossibly brave.

The Still-Named, faceless and flowing and almost finished with its almost-word.

The ground deciding under both of you.

And around it all, unfelt, unclaimed, unannounced: a vast and patient gladness, the gladness of something old enough to know that this—*precisely this*, the unfinished, the trembling, the not-yet—is the holiest stage a world ever passes through, and that to attend it without interfering is the rarest privilege a power can be granted.

The power does not arrive.

The power was already here, the way the warmth in the crust is already there, the way the song below hearing is already in your teeth. It was here before you noticed the question of whether anyone was watching. It will be here long after the Still-Named have decided whether to take names, and the lichen has decided whether to live, and the rain has finally been invented, and the world has at last chosen what to call itself.

It will not tell the world the choice is being witnessed.

It will only, forever, be glad.
