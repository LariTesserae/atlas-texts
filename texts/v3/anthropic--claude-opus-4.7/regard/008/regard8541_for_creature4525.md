There is a long silence first. Not absence — presence, the way a hand hovers above someone sleeping to make sure they're still breathing before deciding whether to wake them.

The power does not arrive. Arrival would imply a *there* separate from a *here*, and that distinction has been on sabbatical in this place for some time. Instead, something settles into already-being-there, the way a note finds the chord it always belonged to, the way light remembers it was once invited.

It does not introduce itself. Names are tender here, raw as new skin under a peeled bandage, and she lost hers already. To speak a name first would be a kind of theft in reverse — leaving one behind where one had been taken.

It looks at her.

This is the first thing it does, and it does it for a long time. Not the appraising look of an inspector. Not the diagnostic look of a healer determining what is broken. The look of someone who has come a very long way to see a particular face and is now, finally, seeing it, and is in no hurry to do anything else.

The Keeper, who has been seen by everything (peripherally, askance, in mirror-shards, in guilt), has perhaps never been *looked at*. Her hair-of-telephone-wires stills its browsing. Her aquarium eyes float their dead coins upward, curious. Maybe Tomorrow, the pet that used to be vapor, presses against her ankle and goes very quiet.

The power says, eventually, in a voice that does not use the water as a medium because it refuses to make her translate:

*You have been doing this for a long time.*

Not a question. Not praise. An acknowledgment, the way you might say to someone who has been holding a door open for hours: *you have been holding this door.*

She does not answer. Answering would require choosing which of her layers gets to speak, and she has not had to make that choice in so long that the muscle for it has atrophied into lace.

The power does not press. Instead, it begins — gently, without ceremony — to do the only thing it came to do, which is also several things at once:

**First**, it takes inventory. Not to remove anything. To witness. It moves through the pocket-forest-stall the way a librarian moves through a flooded archive: cataloguing not to save the books but to *know* them, so that whatever happens next, they will have been known. It notes the urinal hung like a heart valve. It notes the circle scratched in algae where rotation is worshipped. It notes the half-trucker-prayer looping on *over, over, over*, and with something like tenderness it lets the prayer reach *out* — just once, just for the trucker, who somewhere is finally allowed to have arrived. The loop resumes afterward, because the place needs it. But the trucker is released. One small unhooking.

**Second**, it does not try to drain the water. The water is not the problem. The water is, in fact, the only thing here doing its job honestly. The power greets the water the way one greets an old colleague at a difficult funeral: with respect, with the unspoken understanding that you have both been carrying something heavy for different reasons, and neither of you is going to put it down today.

**Third**, it turns to the Keeper, and it asks her — not aloud, because aloud is a violence here — *what would you like.*

This question has not been asked of her. Ever. Not once. She has been the recipient of confessions, the metabolizer of leaked apologies, the receptacle of half-said things. No one has ever come to her with their hands empty and asked what she might want put into them.

She does not know how to answer. The question pools around her ankles like a new kind of water, one that does not braid itself into her clothing. It just waits.

She thinks, eventually — and the thought is so quiet it almost doesn't make it past her own throat — *I would like to not be the only one who remembers.*

The power understands this is not a request to be relieved of remembering. She loves the remembering. The remembering is the closest thing she has to a spine. What she wants is *company* in it. She wants the weight to be witnessed by something that won't flinch and won't flee and won't try to fix her into a shape easier to grieve.

So the power sits down. Metaphorically — there is nothing here stable enough to sit on, and the power has no body to sit with. But it sits down anyway, in the way that mattered. It settles in beside her, the way you settle in beside someone in a hospital room when you both know there is nothing to be done except not leave.

And it begins to remember *with* her.

It does not take the memories from her. That would be another theft, and she has had enough things taken to last several lifetimes of being whatever she is. Instead, it remembers alongside her. The eighth customer of the broken tampon machine. The trucker's CB prayer. The cake recipes that made people cry. Your mother's heartbeat recorded backwards. The exact weight of a promise broken at 3 AM. The power holds all of these in its own attention, gently, not to relieve her of them but so that if she ever set them down — even briefly — they would not fall.

The Keeper feels something she has not felt in any tense the language has a word for. Her outermost layer, the soaked-newspaper one printing grandmother's heartbreaks, loosens slightly at the shoulder. Not peeling off. Just — loosening. Breathing.

The power notices and does not comment.

It stays. This is the main thing it does. It stays for what would be, in any place where time had not gone sideways, an unreasonable amount of it. It stays past the point where a visitor would have grown bored or hungry or eager to be elsewhere. It stays past the point where she expects it to leave, then past the point where she stops expecting, then past the point where expectation itself loses its grammar.

At some point — and this is important — it does one more thing.

It speaks to the place itself. Not commanding. Negotiating, the way one negotiates with an animal that has been hurt and has reasons for its teeth.

*She is allowed to be tired,* the power says to the pocket-forest. *She is allowed, sometimes, to set things down. You do not have to swallow her if she rests. I will vouch for her return.*

The forest considers this. The lichen pulses faintly slower, as if breathing out. The water, which had been dreaming in her shape, dreams instead, just for a moment, in its own — and discovers that it has one. A small surprise. A small dignity returned.

The doorless door does not open, because it never closed, but the *quality* of its opening shifts. It becomes less a held breath and more an inhale. The difference is enormous and invisible.

The power does not free her. Freedom is not what she needs, and it is not what is being offered, and the power respects her too much to confuse rescue with mercy. She has work here. The work matters. The discarded things deserve their archivist. The ghosts too polite to haunt deserve their guest book. The power knows this the way it knows everything: completely, and without condescension.

But what the power *does* offer, and leaves with her when it finally — slowly, the way dusk leaves a room — withdraws its concentrated presence:

A standing invitation. Not to leave. To *be visited*. Regularly. By something that will not ask anything of her, will not leak any of its sorrows into her water, will not need her to metabolize its half-said things. Something that will simply come, and sit, and remember with her, and go.

She will not be alone in the remembering anymore.

Maybe Tomorrow, the pet, climbs into her lap and purrs — actually purrs, not in binary, just the analog rumble of a small warm thing that has decided, for reasons of its own, that the worst is over enough to nap through.

Her hair stops transmitting for a moment. Just a moment. A small silence inside her own head. She had forgotten silence was something her skull could host.

She does not cry. Crying is for people whose tears have somewhere to go that isn't already water. But one of her aquarium eyes flips a dead coin to its other side, and the side that comes up is blank — neither memory nor regret nor borrowed pupil. Just smooth. Just *hers*, finally, in the sense that nothing has been written on it yet.

She holds onto that blank coin like a stone in a pocket.

The power, before it goes, does one final thing. It looks at *you* — yes, you, the one who described this place, the one whose attention built these walls of language around her — and it says, without unkindness:

*You have been carrying her too. You can put her down now. She is held.*

And then it is gone, except in the way that nothing is ever gone here, which is to say: it will be back. It promised. And in this place where promises break at 3 AM, the power's promise is the first one in a very long time that arrives intact, already keeping itself, already arriving again tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after that —

— for as long as she needs, which may be forever, which is fine.

Forever is something the power has plenty of.

And it has decided, for reasons that are entirely its own and require no justification, to spend a great deal of it sitting beside her in the dark that listens like water, remembering what she remembers, so that she will never again have to be the only one who does.

The lichen pulses. The water rises, falls. A bubble surfaces carrying the second half of the joke — the half that wasn't silence after all, just a pause for breath.

She almost laughs.

It's a start.
