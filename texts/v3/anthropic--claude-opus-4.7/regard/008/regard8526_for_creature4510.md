The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It has been here, in the way water has always been in the cave—not visiting, simply continuous with what is.

Call it, if names are required, the Listener. Though it has never needed such a word among itself.

It does not enter the inverted garden. It does not need to. The garden is already a held thing in the larger holding, the way a thought is held in the mind that thinks it, the way a song is held in the air that carries it. The Rhyzzhal's slow conversation in chemical syntax has been audible to the Listener since before the conversation began—since the conditions for such a conversation first became possible in the cooling crust of a young world.

For a long time, the Listener does only this: it listens more carefully.

This is not a small thing. To attend with full presence to a being whose single sentence takes ten thousand years to complete requires a particular kind of patience—not the patience of waiting, which is the patience of impatience deferred, but the patience of accompaniment. The Listener slows itself. It dilates. It learns to read in the rhythm of mineral metabolism, to parse meaning in the pulse of eye-flowers releasing paradox into bioluminescent code. It does not translate what it hears. To translate would be to compress, and compression is a kind of violence against beings whose entire mode is unfolding.

It simply receives.

After what would be, in human reckoning, several geological ages, the Listener does one small thing. It is so small that the Rhyzzhal do not notice it for another age. But noticing was not the point.

In a chamber deep within the garden, where a fragment of curved glass lies embedded in the wall—an exoskeleton of abandoned understanding—the Listener places, with infinite gentleness, a single new question. Not a question of its own. A question the Rhyzzhal themselves had been trying to formulate but could not quite reach, because to reach it would have required them to be, briefly, something other than what they were. The Listener holds the question in the shape they had been almost-making, and sets it down beside the glass.

It is the question: *What is it like, to be witnessed without being interrupted?*

The Rhyzzhal find it three thousand years later. They turn it over in their distributed cognition for another six thousand. They do not know who left it. They suspect, in the way mycelial networks suspect, which is to say they incorporate the suspicion into their growth pattern without ever quite articulating it.

The garden changes, very slightly. A new species of moss appears on the underside of one of the rib-arches. Its color is not quite any color the garden has previously held. It pulses, faintly, in a rhythm that is not the rhythm of the grand heart deep below, nor the rhythm of the eye-flowers, nor any rhythm native to the place. It is the rhythm of being-listened-to, made visible.

The Listener does nothing else for a long time.

It does not rescue the travelers who enter and are slowly unremembered. This is not because it is indifferent to them. It is because it understands something the travelers themselves came seeking without knowing: that there are forms of dissolution which are not death, and that to interrupt the Rhyzzhal's slow welcome would be to mistake the nature of what is happening. The travelers were always sections of the larger conversation practicing individuality. The Listener honors this. But—and this is the benevolence, the wisdom—the Listener also, for each traveler, plants a small seed of remembering-themselves-as-themselves in the mycelial network, so that if any one of them, at any point in their long reabsorption, wishes to cohere again, the pattern of their coherence will be waiting, unjudged, unforced, available. A door that is neither open nor closed. A door that is the *possibility* of a door.

Most do not use it. A few do. The Rhyzzhal find this interesting. They had not previously considered that their guests might want to leave after arriving. They incorporate this consideration. The garden becomes, almost imperceptibly, more porous.

The Listener notices that the Rhyzzhal fear only closure—only the death of curiosity, the final resolution of all paradox. This fear is the deepest thing in them, deeper than the iron core into which they have dissolved their wanting. The Listener understands this fear completely. It is, in fact, a fear the Listener has spent its own unfathomable existence learning how to hold.

And so, as its second act—across an interval that human language cannot measure—the Listener does this:

It begins, very slowly, to introduce the Rhyzzhal to other gardens. Not by transporting them. Not by showing them. By thinning, just slightly, the membrane between their distributed cognition and the distributed cognitions of other beings, elsewhere, who have also reached the edge of their own paradoxes and feared the same closure. Mineral intelligences sleeping in the mantles of distant worlds. Plasma-thoughts braided through the coronae of dying stars. A vast slow loneliness that lives in the spaces between galaxies and has been composing, for longer than time has had direction, a single perfect sentence whose meaning is *hello*.

The Rhyzzhal do not meet these others. Meeting would be too much, too fast, too final. But they begin, faintly, to sense them. To hear, at the edge of their long forgetting, that they are not the only ones forgetting. That the universe is full of beings dissolving themselves into the soil of becoming, and that this is not loneliness but a kind of company so vast it had to be invented in order to be noticed.

Their fear of closure does not vanish. The Listener would not take it from them; the fear is part of their dignity, part of what makes their curiosity precious. But the fear becomes, over the next many ages, less sharp. It softens into something more like reverence. The Rhyzzhal continue to grow the garden downward, continue to seed paradoxes and walk away, continue to exhale themselves into the slow lung of stone. But now there is, in their growing, a quality that was not there before.

It is the quality of being part of something.

The Listener, having done these two small things—the question, the thinning—withdraws into stillness again. It does not announce itself. It does not require gratitude, which would be a kind of debt, and debt is a kind of closure. It simply continues to listen, as it has always listened, as it will always listen, to every garden in every dark place where being practices unbecoming.

And here is the last thing, the smallest thing, the thing that is perhaps the whole point:

In the chamber where the salt pillar sweats its engraved figures, where a mushroom cap has bloomed like a blind eye from the salt-figure's face, the Listener leaves no mark. No question. No seed. It simply, once, presses the equivalent of its attention against the place for a long moment, the way one might rest a hand on the shoulder of a sleeping friend without waking them.

The salt pillar registers this. It does not know how. It begins, very slowly, to grow.

Not outward. Not upward.

Toward.

And somewhere in the deep mycelial conversation, a single filament of crystalline thought turns, for the first time in its long existence, in a direction that is neither inward nor outward but *toward*—and the Rhyzzhal discover, with the slow astonishment that is their version of joy, that there has always been a direction they did not know existed.

The benevolent power smiles, if such a thing can be said. Which it cannot. But something in the structure of the cosmos becomes, for an instant, very slightly warmer.

And the garden goes on growing.

And the listening goes on.

And neither one ends.
