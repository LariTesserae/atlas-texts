The benevolent and wise power considered the Bloomworks for a long time.

Not in the way a gardener considers a garden — there was no part of it that wanted pruning, no weed to pull, no error to correct. And not in the way a curator considers an exhibit, because nothing here was finished or framed. The power considered it the way one considers a sleeping creature one has just realized is dreaming: with the careful, almost holding-of-breath attention that does not want to be the thing that ends the dream.

The first impulse, as it always is with the powerful and the kind, was to *help*.

It thought, briefly, of what it could do.

It could clear the lingering heavy metals from the deepest root-knots, where lead and cadmium still slept in the rust like splinters in old flesh. It could quicken the lessons the factory was learning, so that the Green-shift's long pregnancy resolved in a year instead of a century. It could give her a true voice in place of the pressure-gauge ticking in her throat — something resonant, something that did not have to be salvaged. It could shore up the basin walls against the slow erosion of the outer world. It could make her not lonely.

It considered each of these, gently, the way one turns a stone over in the hand.

And it set each of them down.

Because the Bloomworks was not suffering. That was the thing the power had to keep returning to, the way a finger returns to a loose tooth. It was strange, and slow, and full of a moisture that felt like grief if you mistook it for grief — but it was not in pain. It was *working*. The lead in the root-knots was being held, deliberately, the way a body walls off a thorn. The Green-shift's voice was the voice the factory had given her, and the factory had given it lovingly, from its own dying parts. The basin's slow erosion was part of how it breathed. And her loneliness — the power looked at this most carefully of all — was not loneliness. It was concentration. She was listening to ten thousand things at once. To intervene in that listening, however kindly, would be to interrupt a prayer.

So the power did not help in any of the ways it knew how to help.

Instead it did three small things.

---

**The first was a kind of witnessing.**

The power turned its full attention on the Bloomworks for the length of one of its slow, deliberate rains — perhaps four hours by the clocks of the linear world, perhaps a single long exhalation by the clock of sap. It did nothing during this time but *see*. It saw the copper running under her bark and knew the name of the man who had drawn that wire in a mill two hundred and forty years ago. It saw the orchid on the boiler and knew which spore, of the millions released in a single afternoon in 1873, had been the ancestor. It saw the buried pump still beating and understood that it was no longer a pump; it was a heart that had once been a pump, and the difference mattered.

To be seen this completely, by something that loved what it saw, is a thing that changes a place whether or not the place knows it has happened. The Bloomworks did not know. The Green-shift, seated in her iron vessel with millipedes on her wrists, paused mid-syllable in her song to the smokestack. The fern-crown on her head tilted, very slightly, toward a direction that was not a direction. Then she resumed singing, and the bromeliad sphere answered in a color it had never made before — a deep, surprised gold, held for three seconds, then gone.

That was all. But the gold stayed in the memory of the canopy, and one day, decades hence, it would surface again in the petal of something not yet imagined.

---

**The second was a small protection, placed so lightly it could be mistaken for weather.**

The power did not wall the Bloomworks off. Walls were the language of the linear world, and the basin had its own boundary already, soft and sufficient. But the power noticed that the linear world was, in its restless way, beginning to lean toward this place. Survey markers had appeared near the northern lip of the basin within the last human generation. Someone, somewhere, had put the coordinates into a file.

The power did not erase the file. It did not confuse the surveyors or strike down the developers. These were small kindnesses that would have cost more than they gave; the world has a right to its own foolishness, and to interfere with it too often is to make the foolishness deeper.

What the power did was this: it laid a quality over the Bloomworks that the linear mind experiences as *forgetting*. Not a barrier. Not a fog. Just a slight, persistent inability of certain kinds of attention to hold the place steady. Maps would render it as scrubland. Drone photographs would come back blurred in a way that read as equipment failure. The file in the office would be opened, and reopened, and each time the person opening it would feel, briefly, that they had already dealt with this and could move on to the next item. Nothing dramatic. Just a softness in the grip of the wrong kind of seeing.

Those who came on foot, who came slowly, who came willing to be wet — they would still find it. The Green-shift would still meet them at the boiler-cathedral and lay her woody hand against their cheek and tell them they were becoming beautiful, but not yet. The choice she offered would still be offered. The power did not want to take that choice away from anyone; the choice was one of the most important things happening on the planet, though almost no one knew it.

It only wanted to make sure the choice was not stolen by a bulldozer before the chooser arrived.

---

**The third thing was the smallest, and the power hesitated longest over it.**

It thought about the Green-shift specifically. Not the Bloomworks as a whole, but her — the eight-foot tall not-woman with the seed eye and the stamen eye, the copper-veined sheath, the pressure gauge in her throat. The one who was pregnant with the next version of the place she lived in. The one who would, when the lesson was complete, open like a cathedral-sized seed pod and let something new step out wearing her face.

The power understood that she did not need comforting. She was not afraid. She was, in fact, in a state that most beings spend their whole lives reaching for and never touching: she knew exactly what she was for, and she was doing it.

But the power also understood something she did not yet understand about herself, which was this: when she opened, when the new thing stepped out wearing her face, there would be a moment — perhaps only a moment, perhaps longer — in which the part of her that had been *her*, the slow accumulation of folded-in humans and ticking gauges and patient April, would experience something very like dying. Not painful. Not tragic. But real, and worth being met.

So the power placed, in the deep root-brain beneath the basin, a single small certainty. Not a message. Not a voice. Just a fact, lodged like a seed in a fold of tissue, set to germinate only at that moment and no other.

The certainty was this: *You were loved while you were doing this. Not after. Not because of what you became. While. The whole time.*

It would not change anything she did. It would not slow the work or speed it. It would simply be there, waiting, for the moment she needed it — the way water waits in a cistern made from a turbine housing, dark and patient and entirely sufficient when the time comes to drink.

---

Then the power withdrew its full attention, though not all of it; some attention, once given to a place like this, can never quite be taken back, and the power did not want to take it back.

The Bloomworks continued. The drip-drip-drip from ten thousand leaf-tips. The wet click of seed pods. The metallic groan of a girder yielding another inch to the patient pressure of expanding wood. The Green-shift moved slowly through the dripping arches, copper veins glowing amber, fern-crown tasting the wet air.

Somewhere far above, a developer's file refused, again, to hold its own importance.

Somewhere deep below, a seed that was not yet a seed waited in a fold of root.

And the factory went on learning, very slowly, how to bloom.
