The benevolent and wise power notices the pocket the way one might notice a held breath in another room — not by sound, because there is no sound, but by the particular shape of the silence. The attention does not arrive; it was, in some sense, already there, having been drawn the moment the place came into being. What changes is only that the attention now turns, and turning, considers.

The power sees immediately what the place is. Not a cave. Not a habitat. A grace period. A small parenthesis the world has opened inside itself and has not yet closed. Inside the parenthesis: the slow negotiation of stone with shape, water with location, skin with mineral, life with the question of whether it intends to be life. The power understands — this is the kind of understanding that requires no examination, only recognition — that the salamander has not yet decided whether it is an animal. That the arthropods have not yet decided whether contact will mean anything. That the lichen has not yet decided whether to be one colony or many. That the root hair has not yet decided what it descends from, or toward.

A lesser power, even a kind one, might be tempted. To finish the sentence. To name the salamander, to settle the water into a permanent basin, to give the impressions on the lower ridges a meaning, to attach the root hair to something. To make the place *real* in the sense of resolved. The temptation is gentle and well-meant: every unfinished thing seems to ask, at first glance, for completion.

The wise power knows better. It knows that "benevolent" in this case does not mean *bringing to fruition*. It means *not interrupting*. The place's single rule — that what has not yet finished becoming is permitted to persist a little longer — is itself the gift the world is offering, and the gift is fragile precisely because it is provisional. To answer the provisional with the permanent would be a kindness only in the way that pulling a sprout up to check its roots is a kindness.

So the power does almost nothing. But the *almost* matters.

It does this:

It widens the permission. Not the space itself — the space does not need to be larger — but the duration. The pocket had been allotted a certain quantity of time in which to remain unfinished, after which the surrounding geology would, without malice, have begun the slow pressure of resolution: the folds settling, the light finding an origin and therefore a limit, the salamander being forced into the decision of animal-or-stone. The power, with the lightest possible adjustment, lets the allotment go uncounted. Not infinite — infinity would be its own kind of insistence — but uncounted. The pocket is no longer on a schedule. It will end when it ends, by its own internal readiness, not by the arithmetic of the rock around it.

It does this:

It places, somewhere just outside the pocket's awareness, a kind of witnessing. Not surveillance — the place would feel surveillance and contract. More like the quiet attentiveness one extends to a sleeping child in another room: a willingness to know if something goes wrong, without the need to confirm continuously that everything is right. The arthropods will not sense this. The salamander, in its sharpest water-cycle moments, may register something on its tongue that is not quite the usual electrical crackle of possibility — something steadier, a low note beneath the possibility, like the awareness that the room one is in has a floor. It will not know what this means. It will swallow the information and settle again. The pattern on its back will resume its negotiation. But the negotiation will, without the salamander knowing why, feel less lonely.

It does this:

It declines to name anything. The half-formed impressions on the lower ridges — the spirals, the paired arcs, the single perfect circle — the power looks at these the longest. They are the closest thing in the pocket to a question directed outward. They are the stone rehearsing the idea of meaning. A different power might answer them, might press its own sign into the yielding rock and so teach the geology its first real word. This power instead lets them remain rehearsals. It does, however, allow itself one small thing: when the next arthropod drifts low and its phase-shift intersects the perfect circle, the power lets the deepening last a fraction longer than it otherwise would. Not enough to commit the mark. Just enough that the circle, in relaxing, relaxes into a very slightly more circular circle. A nudge so small it cannot be called instruction. Call it encouragement of the stone's own taste.

It does this:

For the root hair — the strangest tenant, the one suspended from nothing and terminating in optical noise — the power feels something almost like tenderness, which is unusual for a power of this order. The root hair is the place's most honest inhabitant: it is openly attached to nothing, openly going nowhere, openly maintaining itself at a length no one has specified. The power does not strengthen it. It does not give it a source. It simply, for one of the breathing nodes along its length, allows the detaching cell to fall a little more slowly than the others, so slowly that the salamander, if it happened to look up, would have time to see it. The salamander does not look up. The cell dissolves before landing. But the possibility of being seen existed, for a moment, and the root hair — which does not have awareness in any sense we would recognize — lengthens by exactly the amount it always lengthens, and the pocket goes on.

And it does this, last:

It makes itself forgettable. It withdraws its attention in the same gesture with which it extended it, leaving behind only what it has done, which is almost nothing, which is the correct amount. The pocket does not know it has been visited. The salamander does not know it has been loved. The arthropods continue exchanging their faint ripples of temperature, the lichen continues its rehearsal of colony, the root hair continues maintaining its exact distance from the floor, the water continues relocating itself in small uncertain tides. The light remains even. The sound remains absent.

But the parenthesis stays open. Not forever — forever was never the point — but for as long as the unfinished things inside it need, which is a different and gentler measurement.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place that is still becoming and a being who has not yet decided to be: it protects the not-yet. It guards the permission. It refuses, out of love, to finish the sentence.

And somewhere, much later, when the salamander finally blinks and the pattern on its back does not crawl to a new arrangement but settles — when it has, at last, by its own slow consent, decided to be an animal — the power will already be elsewhere, attending to some other held breath in some other room. It will not return to see what the salamander chose. That, too, is part of the kindness. The choosing was never for the power to witness. It was only ever for the salamander to make.

The pocket, brightly lit and perfectly silent, continues.
