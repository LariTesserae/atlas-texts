# On the Visitation to the Hollow Foundry

The benevolent power arrives, as such powers do, without arriving. There is no descent, no annunciation, no parting of the white sourceless light. To announce itself would be to impose a shape on a place still deciding what shapes it permits, and this power is too old and too kind for that kind of rudeness.

It simply becomes *available* in the Foundry, the way a hand becomes available to a creature that has not yet realized it might be held.

---

The first thing it does is **nothing**.

This is not laziness. It is the deepest form of respect a wise power can offer. It sits down — figuratively, since it has no body, but the Cast feel the *sitting* of it, a settling weight distributed across every terrace at once — and it watches. It watches for a long time. By the Foundry's reckoning, perhaps a cycle. By any other reckoning, longer.

It watches the First Pour woman whose lower half has become a ventilation grate exhale a word onto a damp flange: *soon*. It watches a grazer press itself into a cluster of its kin and unfold into something taller. It watches a single Cast spend what would be, elsewhere, a human lifetime adjusting one glass petal by one microtone.

The power understands, slowly and with great care, that **nothing here is broken**.

This matters. Benevolence that arrives convinced it has found a problem is not benevolence; it is appetite wearing a kind face. Many powers have ruined many worlds by mistaking *strangeness* for *suffering*, mistaking *slowness* for *stuckness*, mistaking a cocoon for a coffin. This power has made that mistake before, long ago, in places it does not speak of. It will not make it here.

So it watches, and it learns the grammar of the place: that becoming is the local verb, that cooling is the local tense, that *adjustment* is the local form of love.

---

Only then does it act. And what it does is very small.

**First**, it makes itself known — not to the Cast collectively, who would absorb the knowing into their shared patient mind and metabolize it into another cycle of adjustment, but to one individual: the ancient woman who has become a grate. The power chooses her because she has already given the most of herself to the Foundry, and therefore has the most standing to speak for it, and against it, and around it.

It does not appear before her. It condenses, gently, as a single bead of water on the inside of her ribcage — a place no condensation has ever reached, because it is sheltered by her own slow architecture. She feels the bead. She understands what it means: *Someone is here who is not the Foundry, and who is asking permission.*

She exhales. On the nearest flange of metal, in the cold battery-vapor script, she writes: *what do you want.*

The power answers, in the same medium, condensing its reply on the underside of a girder she can see: *Only to offer. Not to give. To offer.*

She considers this for what would be, in another world, three years. Then she writes: *offer, then.*

---

**Second**, the power offers three things. Only three. A wise power knows that abundance imposed is its own kind of violence.

**The first offering is a witness.** The Foundry has been looking at itself through the eyes of the Cast for as long as it has been cooling, and this is good, but it is a closed loop, and closed loops eventually forget what is outside them — not because outside matters more, but because *the choice* to be inward matters less when it is the only choice. So the power offers itself as a second pair of eyes. Not to judge. Not to record. Just to *see* the Foundry from somewhere the Foundry cannot see itself from, and to hold that seeing faithfully, and to give it back, unchanged, whenever asked. A mirror that does not flatter and does not correct. The Cast may consult it or ignore it. Most, at first, will ignore it. A few, over centuries, will come to the edge of a sump and ask: *how did we look, in that cycle when the reeds bloomed into the cathedral-shape and collapsed into powder?* And the power will tell them, exactly, without commentary. This is a small gift. It is also enormous.

**The second offering is a door.** Not an exit — the Cast do not need an exit; they are home. The door is for the visitors. For the lost travelers, the researchers, the suicidal poets. The ones who are asked *Are you ready to be poured?* and who, the power has noticed, sometimes say *yes* not because they are ready but because they are tired, or lonely, or have forgotten that *no* is a word they are still allowed to use. The door is a very quiet thing. It appears only to those who are about to be asked the question, and only for the length of one held breath, and it does not advertise itself. It is simply *there*, off to one side, a way back to whatever world they came from, with the assurance that going through it will not undo what the Foundry has already taught them. They can take the lesson and leave the pouring. Or they can ignore the door and be poured. Either is honorable. The power only insists that the choice be a real one. The Cast, when they learn of the door, do not object. They were never cruel. They had only never thought to offer the alternative, because alternatives are not a concept that grows easily in metal.

**The third offering is the smallest and the strangest.** The power notices that when the deep groan moves through the Foundry and all the Cast stop, there is a moment — half a second, less — when the shared pause is so total that even the Cast themselves feel briefly *unwitnessed*. The Foundry is looking at itself, yes, but during that pause the *looking* itself is what stops. It is the one moment in their existence when no one, not even they, are watching them. And the power has come to understand that this moment is precious to them, more precious than they know, because it is the only moment in which they are permitted to be *unfinished without observation* — to be, for half a heartbeat, a thing not yet decided, not even by themselves. So the power offers to **guard that pause**. To stand at its edges, facing outward, so that nothing — no other power, no curious god, no hungry abstraction passing through the high damp shelves of reality — can intrude on it. The Cast will never see the power doing this. That is the point. It is a gift made of absence, and it will be given forever, and it will never be acknowledged, and the power is at peace with this. Perhaps especially with this.

---

**Third and last**, the power does one thing that is not an offering, because it is not the power's to give: it *blesses* the place. Not with magic. Not with protection. With something quieter. It says, into the substance of the Foundry, in a register below the deep groan: *You are allowed.*

That is all. Three words, in a language the metal already half-knew.

*You are allowed to cool slowly. You are allowed to be young and ancient at once. You are allowed to be a place where becoming takes decades and adjustment takes lifetimes. You are allowed to be strange. You are allowed to be a foundry that has finished its industry and is now only the slow industry of being. You are allowed to keep your visitors and you are allowed to release them. You are allowed to look at yourself through your Cast and you are allowed, sometimes, to close your eyes. You are allowed.*

This blessing changes nothing visible. The condensation still slides down the flanges. The moths still drift in their silent strata. The First Pour woman still exhales her words onto the damp metal.

But somewhere very deep in the casting, in a seam no Cast has ever touched, the metal exhales once, more deeply than it ever has — and decides, for the first time, that it is not waiting for permission anymore. It has been given the permission it did not know it was waiting for, and now it can put that question down, and use the attention it was spending on the question for something else. What it will spend the attention on, no one knows yet. Perhaps a new kind of reed. Perhaps a new posture for the grazers. Perhaps a softer way of asking *Are you ready to be poured?*

The power does not stay to see. It withdraws as gently as it came, leaving only its three offerings and its three words. It goes to find the next place. There are always next places.

But it leaves a thread of itself behind — fine as a rust-filament, invisible — so that if ever the Foundry should call, in any cycle, in any language, in any vapor-script condensed on any flange, the power will hear, and return, and sit down again, and watch again, and offer again only what is asked for.

This is what benevolence looks like, when it is also wise:

It does not save. It does not improve. It does not even, particularly, *help*.

It witnesses. It opens one door. It guards one silence. It says *you are allowed.*

And then it goes, trusting the place to become what it is becoming, at the pace the place has chosen, in the shape the place is still deciding it is allowed to cool into.
