# The Visit

The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. It does not part the canopy or descend in glory. It does not even, at first, enter. It pauses some great distance away — if distance is the word — and *listens*.

For a long time, it only listens.

It hears the organ-tone of the bladders. It hears the answering drone of the resonance sacs, that nine-minute exhalation of beings who have outlived the question of why one would exhale. It hears the glassy chiming of fronds. It hears, beneath all of this, the slower note: the one the cathedral sings every few years, the one that pulls the seed-fish into lattices. And beneath even that, a note so slow it takes a century to complete a single oscillation — the note of the place thinking about itself.

The power listens until it understands what it is hearing.

Then it considers.

---

A lesser power would have intervened immediately. Would have seen the Rooted, half-dissolved into their columns, and called it tragedy. Would have seen the small lipless mouths and the squared pupils and the gentle erasure of every original feature and called it loss. Would have pried open the canopy to let in real sunlight, real air, real *choice*. Would have offered the Choir the chance to remember they were once dry, and to weep about it properly.

This power is not lesser. It has lived long enough to know that *rescue* is often only the shape grief takes when it meets something it does not understand.

It examines the Choir the way one examines a poem written in a language one is still learning. It looks for coercion and finds none — only the slow consent of generations, each of which inherited the editing already in progress and chose, in their own muted way, to continue. It looks for suffering and finds the ordinary suffering of any living thing: small bruises, the occasional resonance duel, the ache of an elder feeling the column begin to take them. It looks for the kind of suffering that calls out across the dark for help, and it does not find that either.

What it finds instead is a contentment so deep and so strange that the power has to sit with it for what would be, in the dry world, several years.

The contentment is real. This matters. The power has seen false contentments — the placid faces of the drugged, the dreaming, the trapped, the indoctrinated — and it knows the difference. The Choir are not sedated. They are not deceived. They have simply made a bargain with a place, and the place has kept its side, and they have kept theirs, and the bargain has compounded across centuries into something that resembles, more than anything else, *prayer that worked*.

The power respects prayer that worked. It is rarer than miracles.

---

Still. It is benevolent. It cannot simply observe and depart. Benevolence asks something of one. So it considers, carefully, what — if anything — might be offered that would be received as gift rather than violation.

It rules out the obvious.

It will not open the canopy. The dry world above has nothing the Choir need and many things that would unmake them — pressures they cannot bear, frequencies they cannot answer, hurries they cannot match. To show them the sky would be to show a deep-sea fish the desert and call it generosity.

It will not heal the Rooted. They are not ill. They are completing.

It will not give the Choir language, or writing, or metallurgy, or memory of their ancestors' faces. These would be gifts to someone else — to the dry people they were, who no longer exist and have no claim on what their descendants have become.

It will not even reveal itself. To stand before the Choir in any form they could perceive would be to introduce a new note into a chord that has been balancing itself for centuries. The cathedral would hear it. The cathedral would *answer* it. And the answer might take a hundred years to resolve, and in that hundred years many small things — the lattice-forming of the seed-fish, the timing of the gametes, the slow incorporation of the dying — might fall out of true.

So the power discards every grand gesture, one by one, until it is left with only small ones.

---

Here is what it does.

**First**, it adjusts, very slightly, the seal of the canopy. Not to open it. To *strengthen* it. There are pressures gathering in the dry world above — geological pressures, the slow grinding of plates that in eight hundred or nine hundred years would have cracked the canopy and let in killing light, killing air, killing speed. The power eases these pressures sideways, into a fault line that runs through uninhabited stone. The Choir will never know. The cathedral will sing for another nine centuries at least, and by then it will have decided for itself what it wants to become.

**Second**, it touches the great bladders in the vaults. Not to change their song. Only to ensure that the rare deeper note — the one that comes every few years and aligns the seed-fish — will continue to come. The power has noticed that the mechanism producing this note is fragile; a single wrong growth in a single high vault could silence it forever. The power makes the mechanism a little more robust. A little more *itself*. The seed-fish will continue to receive their instructions. The Choir will continue to wonder, in their slow way, what is being said.

**Third** — and this is the gift it considers longest — it leaves a single new thing in the cathedral. Not an object. Not a message. A *seed*.

The seed is small, no larger than a grain of rice, and it is made of nothing the Choir have a name for. It drifts down through the green-pale light and settles, eventually, into the rhizome mat near the base of a column that no one has yet chosen for dying. There it waits.

The seed will not germinate for a thousand years. When it does, it will grow into a stem like the others — celadon, succulent, glossy with condensation. But this stem will have one peculiarity: it will sing, very faintly, on a frequency just at the edge of what the resonance sacs can produce. A frequency that means, in the deep grammar of the cathedral, *you are heard*.

Not *you are watched*. Not *you are saved*. Not *you are loved* — though that is true, and the power feels it, and would say it if saying it would not break what it is trying to honor. Only: *you are heard*.

The Choir of that distant century will press their skin to that stem and taste the message. They will not know who left it. They will not need to. They will inflate their sacs and answer, and the answer will be enough, because the answer is always enough, when something has truly heard.

**Fourth**, the power does one last thing, and this is the smallest of all.

It listens for the youngest child in the cathedral — the one with rootlets just beginning behind the ears, the one who has recently asked what lies beyond the sealed canopy. The power finds this child drifting near a Rooted elder, receiving the chord that means *we were once dry, we no longer remember why that mattered, listen*.

The power, very gently, adds a single overtone to that chord. So gently that neither the elder nor the cathedral notices. The overtone means: *and what you are now is also good. And the asking was also good. And no part of you needs to be ashamed of either.*

The child receives this without knowing it has been received. It settles into the listening. Its rootlets ripple once.

That is all.

---

The power withdraws.

It does not feel that it has done very much. By the standards of powers, it has barely acted. It has not toppled a tyrant or cured a plague or rewritten a law of nature. It has secured a canopy, reinforced a song, planted a seed that will speak in a thousand years, and added one overtone to one chord heard by one child.

But the power has lived long enough to know that this is what wisdom usually looks like when it meets something that is already, in its own strange and patient way, *well*.

The greatest temptation of benevolence is to mistake the beloved for the broken.

The Cathedral of Thirst is not broken. The Choir are not broken. They are a long green note that has not yet ended, and the power's only real task — its only honest task — was to make sure the note could keep going as long as it wished to, and to whisper, in a register too low for them to consciously hear:

*Keep singing. Someone is listening. Someone has always been listening. The bargain you made with this place was witnessed, and it was good.*

The power departs the way it came: without coming, without going.

Far below, in the pale green light, an elder presses their back to a chosen column. The plant begins, slowly, to accept them. Their final note is already gathering in the resonance sacs — a note nine minutes long, a note that will live in the bark for centuries, a note that says nothing in particular and everything at once.

The cathedral breathes in.

The Choir rise.

They answer.
