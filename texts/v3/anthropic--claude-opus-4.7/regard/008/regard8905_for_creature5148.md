The power arrives without arriving. There is no wind of its coming, because wind would be a violation here, and what is wise enough to be called wise is wise enough to know that. It does not descend; it simply is now also present, the way a thought is present in the mind that holds it.

It looks first. This takes what, by the basin's reckoning, might be a long while — perhaps the slow growth of a millimetre of woody stem, perhaps the deposition of a single bacterial grain. By any other reckoning it is instantaneous and unhurried at once. The power is in no rush. Nothing here is.

What it sees:

A creature. Not many. One. Pale, dispersed, drinking the night in a thousand small mouths and remembering, in its fused dark roots, every rain it has ever known. Inside its crust, a slower congregation, breathing in months. Together they form a single quiet life that has been doing the difficult and beautiful work of continuing.

The power considers what it could do.

It could bring rain. A real rain, not the thin condensate but a true falling of water, enough to swell every crown into green, enough to wake the bacteria into a froth of reproduction. It could lower the rises and let the wind in. It could seed the basin with companions — pollinators, grazers, the noisy furniture of a living world. It could lift the filtering veil and let in full sun. It could carry the being elsewhere, to a wetter country, and set it down in glory.

It could do all of these things. It is benevolent and it is wise, and so it does none of them.

Because it has listened, and it has understood what the basin's silence is saying. The being here has not been waiting for rescue. It has been *living*. Its life is not a deprivation pretending to be an existence; it is an existence that happens to look, from outside, like deprivation. To flood it would be to drown it. To bring wind would be to tear the flat leaves from the crust. To bring company would be to introduce hunger. To bring sun would be to burn. Even to *speak* — to lay a clear thought against that mineral hush — would be to break something that has taken longer than empires to settle into its present shape.

Wisdom, here, looks like restraint. Benevolence, here, looks like leaving.

But not quite empty-handed. The power does two small things, so small they barely qualify as actions:

First, it *notices*. Fully. It takes the being into its attention the way one might take a sleeping animal onto one's lap — with the whole of itself, holding nothing back, for what is, by its own measure, a long and tender moment. The being does not know it is being noticed. But somewhere in the deep fused roots, in the patient chemistry of waiting, a thing that has only ever been alone is, for an interval, accompanied. Witnessed. Counted. The universe, through this power, briefly knows the basin's name — and the basin's name is now a thing the universe has known. That cannot be taken back. It is a small permanence added to a place whose only currency is permanence.

Second, before it goes, the power makes one almost invisible adjustment. Not to the basin. To the future. It sets, somewhere in the long unfolding of things, a soft condition: that when the end comes for this place — as ends come for all places, when the sun shifts or the crust at last gives way or the slow planet turns its face — the ending will be *gentle*. Not interrupted. Not hastened. Not prolonged past dignity. Simply gentle. A drying that is also a sleep. The being will not know terror at the last. The bacteria will slow into their crystals like sugar into tea. The dark roots will let go of one another the way old friends release a held hand.

This is the only gift the place can accept without being unmade by it: the promise of a kind death, very far away, on its own terms.

Then the power withdraws — though *withdraws* is the wrong word, because it was never occupying space. It simply turns its attention elsewhere, leaving the basin exactly as it found it: warm, dry, dark, silent, and inhabited by one slow patient life that does not know, and does not need to know, that it has been loved.

A single grain of salt settles somewhere in the crust.

That is the only record.
