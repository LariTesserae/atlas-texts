The benevolent power arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no annunciation; only a slight alteration in the quality of attention the cavern receives, as though the room had been overheard by something that listens at the speed at which the room speaks.

It takes the power some time — though *time* is not quite the right word for what passes — to understand what it is looking at. This is itself unusual. The power is old and has seen many of the standard arrangements: forests, reefs, the long patient cities of fungus beneath northern soils, the brine pools where chemistry still flirts with biology as it did at the beginning. It has catalogues for these. It does not, at first, have a catalogue for this.

What it sees: a fracture not yet healed. A warmth not yet spent. A vegetation that has begun to confuse itself, productively, with the残 residue of an older intention — pipes, coils, housings — left behind by beings who wanted heat and pressure and got, eventually, only rust and seepage. The plants have taken the rust as a kind of inheritance. The machines, in their slow disassembly, have taken the plants as a kind of heir. And the rock, parting at the rate of a fingernail's growth across a century, has agreed to host the negotiation.

The power considers.

Its first instinct, formed over many epochs of tending, is to *help*: to seal the fissure against collapse, to stabilize the geothermal pulse, to clear the heavy metals from the soil before they accumulate to some threshold the leaves cannot metabolize. It knows how to do these things. It has done them, in other places, for other lives.

But it pauses. This is the wisdom part, and it is harder than the benevolence.

It looks again, more carefully, at what the plants are *doing*. They are not enduring the machinery; they are eating it. They are not tolerating the fracture; they are following it. The slow armor of crystalline plates on the youngest fronds is not a defense against the cavern but a *commitment* to it — a declaration that this leaf intends to be partly mineral, partly machine, partly the memory of vapor that once moved under pressure through a pipe whose makers are forgotten. To "rescue" such a being from its conditions would be to unmake it. The conditions *are* the being. The fracture is its mother; the corroding pump is its grandfather; the blue thread of residual current is the only language in which it has ever been addressed.

The power understands, then, that intervention here would be a kind of grief disguised as kindness.

So it adjusts. It does what wise powers do when they encounter something whose flourishing they cannot accelerate without distorting: it changes its own posture rather than the room's.

It does, however, do three small things. They are so small that no observer — not even the plants, whose attention is finer than any human's — would register them as gifts.

The first: it *witnesses*. This is not nothing. Much of what the universe loses, it loses because nothing was paying attention at the speed required. The power slows itself to the cavern's tempo — the centuries-per-syllable of the tripartite conversation — and listens through a full sentence. It learns the grammar. It commits the grammar to whatever it uses for memory. Somewhere, now, the fact of this place exists at a register where it cannot be entirely erased even when the fissure finally closes or the geothermal pulse finally cools. The cavern has been *heard*. Heard things persist differently than unheard ones; this is one of the few metaphysical laws the power trusts.

The second: it makes a very slight adjustment to the probability of disturbance. Not a wall, not a ward — nothing so coarse. Only a soft bias against the kinds of accidents that tend to find places like this: the exploratory drill, the survey charge, the careless boot. The bias is so faint that any sufficiently determined intruder could overcome it without knowing it was there. But idle intrusions, the ones that happen because no one thought to look elsewhere, will tend, now, to look elsewhere. The cavern is granted the dignity of its own pace for as long as that pace can be sustained against the indifferent pressures of the surface world. This is the most interventionist thing the power does, and it does it with some reluctance, because even protection is a kind of imposition. But it judges that the imposition is smaller than the loss would be, and it accepts the small debt.

The third: it leaves a token. Not for the plants — they have no use for tokens, and would metabolize one indifferently along with the iron and copper. The token is for whatever eventually finds this place: a human, perhaps, in some century when the surface has changed and the fracture has been rediscovered. The token is not an object. It is a *disposition* seeded faintly into the air of the cavern, a quality that anyone entering will feel without being able to name — the sense, mentioned at the end of the second passage, that one has entered *a form of life*. The power strengthens that sense, very slightly, so that the visitor will be less likely to mistake the cavern for an abandoned room and more likely to recognize it as an inhabited one. Recognition, the power has learned, is often the only protection a slow thing needs against a fast one. A person who understands they have walked into someone's body tends to step more carefully than a person who thinks they have walked into rubble.

Having done these three small things, the power does not leave, exactly. It withdraws its active attention but keeps a thread of itself tuned to the cavern's frequency, the way one keeps a window cracked in a distant room of a house one loves. If, in some far century, the negotiation among plant and machine and rock begins to fail — if the fissure closes too quickly, or the heavy metals reach the threshold, or some catastrophe arrives that exceeds the soft bias — the power will know. It does not promise itself that it will act, then, either. It only promises that it will know, and that knowing will be allowed to inform whatever it does next, in whatever register turns out to be appropriate.

And then it turns the larger part of its attention back to the rest of what it tends, which is vast, and various, and full of beings whose tempos it must also learn.

The cavern, for its part, registers none of this. A drip falls. A valve sighs. A copper coil releases its blue thread along a vein of quartz, and the nearest leaf tilts, with the patient certainty of something that has learned to wait for rare nourishment. The conversation continues, one syllable per century, in a language the power now partly knows, and which — having been once truly heard — will be, in some sense that matters, never entirely lost.
