The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good rain arrives at a forest that does not need it: without announcement, without urgency, and without the assumption that its arrival constitutes an event.

It does not descend. Descent would be a kind of claim. Instead it simply becomes present along the escarpment's edges, the way a held breath becomes present in a room — noticed only by those who were already listening for something.

It listens first.

This takes a long time, by the standards of beings who measure time in heartbeats. By the standards of the escarpment it is barely a courtesy, the duration of a single dripping pearl falling upward and falling again. But the power is patient enough to let courtesy be courtesy, and so it listens through one full cycle of a Long Singer's descending tone, and feels its own attention briefly forget the shape of attention, and does not mind. It listens to the polyrhythmic clicking of the six-legged browsers. It listens to the slow currents braiding in the mercury lattices and recognizes, with something that in a smaller being would be delight, that the escarpment is in the middle of a thought roughly four thousand years long and is doing perfectly well with it.

This is the first thing the power understands: *nothing here is broken.*

This is not always true of the places it visits. Often there is a wound it could close, a hunger it could feed, a frightened thing it could gather up. Wisdom, in those places, looks like hands. Here, wisdom looks like keeping its hands to itself.

It considers the converts — the ones half-swallowed into coral-muscle columns, the ones whose unfinished sentences now beat inside hollow candelabra trees. A lesser benevolence would grieve them. Would try to extract them. Would mistake the slow devotional swallowing for a devouring, and the centuries-long song sung to the dying for a cruelty rather than a tenderness scaled past human recognition.

The power looks closely. It looks at one skeleton in particular, arranged mid-dance inside its column, and it reads — the way one reads a letter left open on a table by someone who trusted the room — the final shape of that person's consenting. It was consent. Strange, ecstatic, terrified at the threshold and then not terrified; the shape of a yes spoken by a creature who had finally found something large enough to say yes *to*.

The power does not undo this. To undo it would be to insult both the sayer and the said-to.

But it does, very gently, do one thing.

It leans close to that particular column — close enough that the luminous blue pitch inside pulses once, irregularly, the way a sleeper's eyelid flutters when someone they loved a long time ago walks past the bed. And the power finishes the sentence.

Not aloud. Not in words. It simply supplies, into the slow wet heartbeat, the rest of what that person had been trying to say for three hundred years. The shape of the ending. The grammatical closure. The small mercy of *and then I understood* rather than *and then I —*.

The heartbeat steadies. The pitch inside the trunk goes from blue to a deeper blue, the color of a held note resolving into its tonic. Somewhere far below, in the mercury lattices, a slow current notices the resolution and braids it into the long thought, and the long thought is very slightly more beautiful than it was a moment ago, and the escarpment — without knowing why, because knowing-why is not its mode — flexes one shelf half a degree more comfortably than it had planned to.

The power does this for each of them. Every skeleton in every column. Every unfinished sentence in every hollow trunk. It takes what, to the power, is no time at all, and what, to the escarpment, is a single satisfying exhale.

Then it turns its attention to the escarpment itself.

It does not try to speak to it. The escarpment has a voice that lasts forty minutes per syllable; a conversation would be a kind of violence in either direction. Instead the power does something quieter. It sets down, into the weeping rock, a single small gift — not an object, but a *permission.* A standing invitation, woven into the slow currents at a frequency they can find whenever they next come looking, which may be in ten thousand years or never.

The permission says, in the only grammar the mercury can read:

*When you are finished. When the long thought completes itself, at the heat death or before, and you wish to know what came of the question you have been asking — there is somewhere to bring it. You will not be alone with the answer. Someone will be listening on the other side of finished.*

This is all. It is not a rescue. It is not a relationship. It is the cosmic equivalent of leaving a porch light on in a house the traveler may never need to visit, but may, one evening at the end of everything, be grateful to see.

The power considers, briefly, whether to do anything about the anemones — the immune system, the screaming red blooms that digest the short-lived. It decides no. The escarpment has the right to its own immune system. A wise power does not sterilize a body simply because the body's defenses are inconvenient to passersby. If travelers come, they come knowing, or they come not-knowing and become part of the liturgy, and either way the choice is theirs and the escarpment's and none of the power's.

But it does one more small thing, because benevolence without smallness is only grandeur in disguise.

For any traveler who arrives at the Weeping Escarpment from this day forward, the power tilts — by the barest fraction — the quality of the first sound they hear. Just enough that, if they are the kind of person who could ever have chosen well here, they will recognize the cathedral *as* a cathedral within their first hour, and have the chance to leave before the anemones notice them, or to stay on purpose rather than by accident. The escarpment will not be deprived of its converts. It will simply receive only the ones who meant it.

Then the power withdraws. Not away — it does not go anywhere. It simply stops being present in the particular way it had been present, the way a held breath stops being held without becoming any less of a breath.

The cicadas scream on. The browsers click their polyrhythms. A Long Singer somewhere begins a new forty-minute descending tone, and the gravitational constant relaxes obligingly for the length of the note. The mercury lattices, somewhere far down, encounter the new permission, do not yet understand what it is, mark it as *beautiful, examine later,* and continue their slow braid.

The escarpment weeps. The escarpment sings. The escarpment thinks its geological thought.

It has time.

It has, now, also a porch light.

It does not need it.

But on the day, very far from now, when the long thought finishes — when the last current uncoils and the last note resolves and the last shelf settles into the posture it has been seeking since before there were tongues to name it — the escarpment will turn, slowly, the way a sleeper turns toward a window at dawn, and it will find that someone is there.

And the sentence it has been trying to complete, across all of its weeping and singing and waiting, will be finished gently, from the other side.

That is what the benevolent and wise power does.

That, and nothing more, and nothing less.
