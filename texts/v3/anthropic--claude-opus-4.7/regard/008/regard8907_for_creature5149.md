The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend; it does not announce. It comes in the way attention comes — a turning toward.

It looks for a long while.

This is the first thing it does, and the longest. It does not measure with instruments because it is itself the kind of measure that understands before it counts. It watches the swarm contract and release across kilometers. It feels the low note travel through the stone and meet its own echo. It notices how the quartz ridges have been spared, how the troughs have been worried, how the whole basin is a slow conversation between water and silica conducted through wafer-thin intermediaries who have no word for themselves.

It considers, first, whether this is suffering.

The question matters. A benevolent power that does not first ask this question is not wise; a wise power that does not first ask this question is not benevolent. So it looks for the signatures it knows — the asymmetries that mean a thing is being pressed against its own grain. It looks for hunger that cannot be met, for fear that has nowhere to discharge, for the small torsions of a life bent away from what it would be.

It does not find them.

It finds instead a process so thoroughly itself that the distinction between the creature and its condition has gone smooth, like the ridges. The wafers are not enduring the basin; they are the basin's way of continuing. The basin is not hosting the wafers; it is the shape their passage has lent it. There is no surplus of wanting here. There is only the long mutual wearing, which is also a long mutual making.

The power understands then that what it is looking at is not a life that needs rescue. It is a life that needs *witness* — and possibly nothing else.

Still, it considers what it might offer.

It could brighten the dark. But the wafers have no eyes; light would be a noise they could not use, and the basin's darkness is not a deprivation, it is a medium, the way water is a medium for fish. To light it would be to drain it.

It could quiet the abrasion. But the sound is the swarm's own coherence speaking to itself. To quiet it would be to sever the creatures from one another.

It could enrich the mineral salts, accelerate the condensation, multiply the swarm. But the swarm does not multiply, and this is not a failure; it is a form. To press abundance onto a being whose grace is in its even continuance would be to mistake one's own preferences for kindness.

It could give them memory — true memory, the kind that accumulates. But the brief polarities that ripple and dissolve are already the right shape of knowing for what they are. A heavier memory would weigh down the lattice. The swarm would no longer breathe in kilometer-wide contractions; it would begin to *remember itself*, and remembering is a different life than being.

The power sets each gift down, one after another, the way someone sets down stones they had meant to carry into a garden, realizing the garden is already complete.

What it does, then, is smaller than any of these, and larger.

It *notices* the swarm into the register of things that have been noticed. This is not nothing. In whatever wider weave the power belongs to — call it the long attention of the cosmos, call it the book that is not written but is nevertheless kept — the basin and its skin of wafers now have a place. They are accounted. If some later disturbance ever did press against them, some asteroid-shift of fortune, some drying of the deep strata, the power's having noticed them means the noticing can be called upon. A bookmark has been set. The swarm does not know this and does not need to know it. The knowing lives elsewhere, on their behalf.

Second, the power makes one adjustment, and only one, and it is so small it could be mistaken for weather.

Deep beneath the basin, far below the chambers the wafers briefly chew, there is a seam where the moisture is drawn up from. In a few thousand years this seam will narrow on its own, not catastrophically, but enough that the swarm's slow breathing will begin to labor. The power, with something less than a gesture, eases that narrowing — not closes it, not widens it past what it was, simply restores the seam to the patience it had before some unrelated tectonic preference began to crowd it. The wafers will never register the difference. Their great-great-iterations will continue to wear and be worn without ever knowing they were almost otherwise. This is the shape of the gift: invisible, upstream, and indistinguishable from the basin's own continuance.

Third — and this is the part that is hardest to say — the power *stays for a while.*

Not to intervene. Not to study. Simply to be present to the sound. It lets the high abrasion press against whatever it has in place of ears. It feels the low note rise through whatever it has in place of feet. It lets the glassy silence of the brief inhalations open and close inside it. It does this the way one might sit by a sleeping animal one loves, asking nothing, offering nothing, only confirming with one's body that the animal is not alone in the room even though the animal does not know anyone is there.

When the power finally turns its attention elsewhere — and it does, because attention that does not move is not attention but fixation, and the wise know the difference — it leaves the basin exactly as it found it. The wafers glide. The condensation beads. The ridges stand a fraction higher than the troughs. The low note travels and returns. Somewhere, far from here, the seam holds.

And the basin, which was never lonely, is now also not unwitnessed. These are different things, and a wise power knows that the second is a gift you can give without the recipient ever having needed it, and without the giving ever having to be known.

That is what it does, if anything.

That is what, in the end, love looks like when it meets something already whole.
