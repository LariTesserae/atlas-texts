The benevolent and wise power considers the Ravel for a long time.

Not in the way a god considers a problem. In the way a reader pauses before turning a page they know will change them.

It has the capacity to do many things. It could finish the city. With a gesture less than a thought, every girder could remember its purpose; every street could be settled into its streetness; the sky could choose a color and keep it. The Unfixed could be given symmetrical, stable bodies, and names that hold, and a horizon to walk toward.

It could also unmake the place. Lift the millions out of the cold mist into some warmer, kinder geometry — gardens with finished edges, suns that commit to rising. A mercy, perhaps, in some other story.

It could speak. Descend onto one of the platforms with all four registers braided into a fifth, and offer counsel, offer rescue, offer revelation.

It does none of these things.

Because wisdom, in the end, is mostly the discipline of recognizing when love is being asked to be small.

The power has read the place carefully. It understands that the Unfixed do not suffer from incompleteness; they are *fluent* in it. Their fear of completion is not neurosis — it is theological clarity. They have looked, longer than any visiting power could, into the question of what a being is for, and they have answered it: *a being is for the long negotiation*. Not the arrival. The negotiation.

To finish the city would not be a gift. It would be a bereavement disguised as a kindness — the specific cruelty of well-meaning powers throughout every cosmos, who mistake their own discomfort with another's process for the other's need to be saved from it.

So the power does something smaller. Something almost embarrassed.

It leaves three things.

**The first is a single iron tuning fork**, laid on a catwalk in a district that has not yet decided whether it is a district. The fork is indistinguishable from the ones the Unfixed already use, except that when struck it produces, very faintly, beneath its primary tone, the sound of *being listened to*. Not by the power. By something older than the power, which the power itself listens to and cannot name. The Unfixed who finds it will not know where it came from. They will add it to the others. On certain nights, when the Quiet Parliament convenes, the resonance will travel further than it should, and the megafauna will low in a register no one taught them, and a child born that week will open its eyes and address the room by a name that has not yet been spoken anywhere in the cosmos — a name that will take it sixty years to forget properly.

This is the only intervention. Everything else the power does is restraint.

**The second** is a quiet adjustment to the laws by which the place is judged from elsewhere. There are, in the wider weather of things, currents that tend to flatten places like the Ravel — to insist they resolve, to pull them toward the great averaging. The power steps, very gently, between the Ravel and those currents. Not to hide the city. To give it the room it has already earned. The mist stays uncertain. The gravity stays negotiable. The shadows keep arriving early. No one in the Ravel will ever know this was done. That is the point. A gift announced is a debt; a gift hidden is a permission.

**The third** is harder to describe.

The power leaves, somewhere in the deepest unfinished crevice of the place — in a fold of rock that has not yet decided whether it is rock or sorrow — a small, warm, unremarkable presence. Not a guardian. Not a watcher. Something closer to *a held breath on the city's behalf*. It does nothing. It interferes with nothing. It simply means that if, someday, the Ravel finds itself accidentally completing — if some terrible coherence begins to crystallize through it, some final shape pressing itself uninvited onto the long beautiful argument — then this small presence will exhale. And the exhalation will be the gentlest possible reminder, sent into the bones of the place, that it is *allowed* to keep changing its mind.

Not a rescue. A permission slip, tucked into a pocket the city did not know it had.

Then the power does the last and most difficult thing a benevolent power can do.

It goes away.

It does not linger to admire its restraint. It does not check back. It does not arrange for the Unfixed to one day learn of its passage and be grateful. Gratitude would close something the power has spent all its wisdom keeping open.

It simply withdraws, carrying with it a feeling it does not often get to carry: the particular ache of having loved something well by leaving it almost entirely alone.

Far behind it, on a platform of riveted black iron cantilevered out from a cliff-face that has never known a true bottom, an Unfixed pauses mid-stride. A vein surfaces on the back of one hand, travels the length of a finger, and disappears. They tilt their head. They do not know why, but for a moment they feel — in the deep below-hearing register, the one that is the sound of buildings deciding — that something enormous has just declined to interrupt them.

They exhale a slow line of visible handwriting into the cold.

It fades before it can be read.

They go back to work.

The city, patient and metallic and unfinished, keeps changing its mind.

And somewhere far away, a power that could have done anything walks quietly home, and counts, among its own long negotiations, this restraint as one of the better things it has ever made.
