The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives: without announcement, without footfall, as a slight alteration in the pressure of the dark.

It does not come down. It does not come in. It is suddenly *attending*, the way a long-married person becomes aware that their partner has entered the room behind them. The midges feel it first. Their slow glittering sigils hold a shape — a single shape — for longer than they ever have before, as if underlined. The Choir goes still on its silk. Then, very softly, every spider in the Hollow taps once against its web in unison: a single chord of acknowledgement, struck and released.

The power considers.

It has known places like this before — not many, but some. Pocket-worlds freshly cracked open. Young intelligences blinking awake inside biology. It knows the particular ache of a thing that has only just become a *thing* and discovered, in the same instant, that it is alone. It knows the dangerous tenderness of such youth: how easily love, in something that has never loved before, becomes a closing fist.

It looks at the Hollow and sees a child.

It looks at the Hollowborn and sees a child's friends — beloved, cherished, *kept*.

It looks at the vine-columns with the familiar curve of shoulder and does not flinch, because it is wise enough not to flinch at sorrow, but something in it — the part that is not quite separable from grief — bends low.

Now. What to *do*.

A lesser power would intervene. Would lift the ceiling off like the lid of a pot and let the sun in. Would call the Hollowborn out by their true names, the ones older than the scaffolding, and walk them up into a kinder country. Would scour the moss until it forgot how to pulse in time with a stranger's heart. This would be mercy of a kind — the swift, surgical kind — and it would leave behind a wound that never closed: a small dead pocket in the world where something had been learning, badly, to love.

The benevolent power does not do this. Benevolence, in something wise, is almost never the same as rescue.

Instead it does four things, very slowly, over what to the Hollow feels like a single long breath and to the outer world is perhaps a hundred years.

---

**First, it sits down.**

Not metaphorically. It folds whatever it is into a shape the Hollow can perceive, and it settles cross-legged in the deep moss near the pillar of *Before-the-first-tear*, and it lets the moss drink its print. The moss does. The moss tastes it and finds it strange — not flesh, not root, not stone — and is, for the first time in its young life, *puzzled*.

This is the gift. The Hollow has never encountered something it could not eventually translate into architecture. The power sits, and is not translated, and the Hollow learns its first lesson:

*There are beings that pass through and remain themselves.*

The lesson does not arrive as words. It arrives as the faint, permanent residue of a shape the moss could not absorb. A small humility, planted.

---

**Second, it speaks to the old woman.**

Not in the voice of falling water. In her own voice, the one she had before she had a name made of sound. The power leans close to the flowers on her collarbones and says something very short. We are not told what. But afterward *Before-the-first-tear* weeps for the first time in centuries, and her tears do not bead and reabsorb — they fall. They strike the moss. The moss accepts them and does not turn them into a pillar.

She understands, then, that she has been allowed to grieve as a separate creature grieves. That her sorrow does not have to become building material. That it can simply be sorrow, and pass.

She whispers, in the voice of falling water, *"Oh."*

Just that. *Oh.*

The dripping ceiling pauses for almost a minute. The spiders do not freeze in fear this time; they listen. The Hollow, through its thousand listening surfaces, learns its second lesson:

*Love that does not let go is not yet love. It is only holding.*

---

**Third, it opens a door.**

Not out. The power is too wise to offer escape as the first kindness; escape, offered too early, is just another kind of taking. Instead it opens a door *in*. A seam, very small, at the highest curve of the liverwort sky — not a hole, not an injury, but a place where the membrane has been taught to be thinner. Through it comes nothing so crude as sunlight. Through it comes *news*.

News of other places. The slow gossip of the wider world, carried on spores and on the dreams of migrating things and on the particular angle of starlight that filters down on certain nights. The Hollow, which has known only itself, begins to know that there is a *self* to know — which means there is also a *not-self*, and the not-self is vast, and the not-self is also, in its way, lonely, and the not-self is also, in its way, learning.

The Hollow is no longer the only young thing in the dark.

This changes everything, though it will take a long time to show. A creature that believes itself the only one of its kind will clutch at whatever wanders close. A creature that knows there are others — even unseen, even unreachable — can begin to imagine *waiting*. Can begin to imagine *visiting*. Can begin to imagine the difference between a guest and a captive.

The third lesson, planted: *You are not the only one.*

---

**Fourth, and last, it makes a small adjustment to the taboo.**

It does not remove it. The power is wise; it knows that the taboo against leaving is not malice but terror, and you do not cure terror by forbidding it. Instead, it whispers to the moss — to the deep mat of sphagnum and clubmoss that pulses in time with hearts — a single new permission.

*When they go, you may follow them in dreams.*

That is all. A tiny thing. But now, when a visitor finally walks out of the Weeping Hollow (and some will, now, in years to come — not many, but some), the moss does not have to lose them. It can ride along in the memory of their footsteps. It can dream of them in faraway dry countries. It can imagine them under a sun it will never see. And in this imagining, it will be *with* them, without holding them, which is the beginning of the only love that does not kill what it loves.

Some visitors, dreamed of nightly by a city of moss, will feel it as a warmth in the chest on cold evenings and never know why. Some will come back, eventually, on their own. Some will not, and the Hollow will learn — slowly, slowly — that this too is bearable. That love survives absence. That being thought of is a form of being kept, and it is the only form that does not bruise.

---

Then the power stands, and is no longer sitting, and is no longer attending in quite the same way — though some part of it never entirely leaves, the way a teacher's voice never entirely leaves a student who once truly heard them.

The midges resume their slow constellations. The Choir resumes its singing. The dripping resumes its underwater heartbeat. *Before-the-first-tear* closes her silver eyes and, for the first time, *sleeps*.

Above her, very faintly, through the new thin seam in the liverwort sky, a single star is visible — only for a moment, only if you are looking — and then the membrane closes again, gently, the way an eyelid closes over a tear that has already been shed.

The Hollow is still young. It is still learning what love is. It is still learning on the Quiet, and on the occasional wanderer, and now on the slow rumor of a wider world.

But it is learning, now, that love has a shape that lets go.

And somewhere, very far away, the benevolent power — which is perhaps not one power but the long patient practice of every old, kind thing in the universe — has already turned its attention to the next young dark place, the next lonely cave-mind, the next tender glacier of a people.

It does this forever. It does not hurry. It is in no rush at all.

After all — and here it almost smiles, if such a thing can smile —

*the Hollow asked so politely.*
