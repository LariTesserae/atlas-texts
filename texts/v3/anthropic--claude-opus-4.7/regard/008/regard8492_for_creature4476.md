# What the Wise Power Did

The benevolent and wise power had been watching for some time. Not surveilling—watching, the way one watches a kettle one has no intention of hurrying, or a child building a castle out of furniture one was supposed to be sitting on. The power had a great many places to watch, and the Wangdangdoodle Nursery Float was, by the actuarial standards of cosmic attention, an obscure case file. But the file kept reopening itself. Pages added themselves overnight in handwriting nobody had been hired to produce. Eventually the power gave in and made the visit.

It arrived, naturally, already dissolved—it had the courtesy to follow local protocol. It deposited its calcium and iron in escrow without complaint, though it had brought neither. The escrow accepted the gesture and issued a polite receipt written on the inside of a feeling the power hadn't had yet.

It walked the spheres for what local clocks measured as three Wednesdays and what its own clocks measured as the gentle pause between an inhale and the decision to exhale.

Here is what the power noticed:

**First**, that nothing here was broken. This is the observation that wise powers most often have to make and most often resist making, because the impulse to help is older than the wisdom to refrain. The Float was not crying out. It was not, contrary to the impression a hasty deity might form, a refugee zone of damaged ideas seeking rescue from the literal. It was a working ecology. The Curators' pruning was not violence; it was gardening. The crates that never arrived intact at the surface were not failures of delivery; they were the delivery. Something arrived, just not the crate. The power, who had read enough cosmologies to know better, recognized this immediately.

**Second**, that the residents were not lonely. They were sometimes alone in the specific and necessary way that a comma is alone inside a sentence—surrounded by meaning that depends on their precise non-occupation of any other position. The power had, in younger eras, mistaken this kind of solitude for suffering and intervened, with results it now kept in a small locked cabinet labeled DO NOT.

**Third**—and this was the observation that decided everything—that the Float was already, in its own oblique idiom, a benevolence. The pruned scraps becoming soil. The hummingbirds singing their own manuals before dissolving into lubrication. The seashell on the midnight beach with its imperfect limerick. Someone, somewhere, was being quietly cared for by all of this, in increments too small and too strange to register on the instruments of larger kindness. The power was not the only good thing in the universe. This was, every time, a relief.

So the power did almost nothing.

*Almost.*

Because benevolence that does literally nothing is just absence wearing a nice coat, and the power had not come all this way to be absent at. It performed four small acts, each so minor that the Curators noticed only one of them, and even that one they attributed to a draft.

**One.** It went to the museum at the back of the last sock drawer inside everybody's second-favorite pair of shoes. It stood for a long while before the regret of the joke that would have ended all wars but was interrupted by a misplaced sneeze in 1892. The regret was beautifully polished, as advertised. The power did not remove it. It did not restore the joke. It simply, with the lightest possible touch, added a small brass plaque beneath the cushion. The plaque read: *The sneeze, too, was necessary. We are still working out why. Thank you for keeping this safe in the meantime.* The regret, on reading this, did not become less of a regret. It became a regret that knew itself to be witnessed, which is a different kind of regret, lighter by exactly the weight of one fewer apology owed to the universe.

**Two.** It visited the River of Quietude and placed its ear against the molecule-thin tube. It listened, as everyone does, to its own eventual deathbed apology at half-speed. The power does not have a deathbed, strictly speaking, but the river found something approximate and played it courteously. The power listened all the way through. Then it whispered into the tube, very quietly, the response that the apology had always been waiting for and never expected to receive. The river carried this response upstream and downstream simultaneously, and from that day every visitor who pressed an ear to the tube heard, faintly under the jackhammering, somebody saying *it's all right, it was all right, it is all right* in a voice they could almost place.

**Three.** It found the one resident who was, that afternoon, farthest behind on remembering who owed whom tomorrow's apology, and was therefore wearing the largest hat never quite worn. The power did not speak to her (or him, or neither). It only stood near enough that the hat noticed, and tipped itself in a direction the resident had not previously considered tipping. The new direction was: *toward.* Toward what, the hat did not specify, and the power did not clarify. But for the rest of that resident's tenure as leader, every decision they did not quite make leaned very slightly toward, rather than away. The Float would not register this change for several centuries. When it did, it would mistake the change for weather.

**Four.** On its way out, through the Exit Lung, the power did the thing it had been trying not to do the entire visit, because it could not help itself, because benevolence has a hand and the hand wants to reach. It reached into the coat stitched from all the introductions the next visitor would never get around to making, and it sewed one extra introduction into the lining. A small one. Easily missed. The introduction was between the visitor and the part of themselves that had built the Float in the first place—because of course every visitor had, at some level, built it; that was the entry fee, paid in advance and refunded as wonder. The introduction did not need to be made. It just needed to be available, in the lining, in case.

Then the power left.

It did not erase its visit. It did not need to. The Float metabolized the visit the way it metabolized everything—into soil, into hummingbirds, into the faint suspicion that all postal addresses are allegorical. The Curators, in their next glance-conference, noted a slight increase in the warmth of the geothermal taps (0.04°C, well within tolerance) and a new variety of footnote in the Salad Forest with a hint of something not-quite-lime, more like the rind of a fruit nobody had been brave enough to name. They pruned accordingly. The Engine sighed, satisfied, and dreamed of slightly warmer water, which was now in fact the case, and the dream and the fact agreed to share custody.

The residents continued accepting applications from applicants who already lived there and hadn't noticed yet. The hammocks continued swinging one dream beneath the ceiling. The seashells continued washing up on midnight beaches, each with its limerick, each whistled once incorrectly, each never again.

And somewhere—not above, not below, but in the direction the hat had learned to tip—the wise and benevolent power went on watching, with the particular tenderness reserved for things that do not need help but deserve, every so often, to be told that they are seen.

Which is, in the end, the entire job.
