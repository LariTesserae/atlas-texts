The Benevolent Power does not arrive.

Arrival would be a violence here—a thing crossing from elsewhere into here, and elsewhere is precisely what this place has spent eternities pressing out of itself. To arrive would be to undo what they have become. The Power knows this the way a mother knows not to wake a child mid-dream, the way a reader knows not to mark in another's book.

So instead—

It learns to be slow.

This takes the Power a long time. Not by its own measure, where eons are heartbeats, but by the measure it must adopt: the pace at which the Unseparated Ones exchange impossibilities. It practices on smaller things first. It sits with the death of a single star until the star has forgotten it ever burned. It learns the patience of waiting for sediment to remember it was once a mountain. Only when it can hold stillness like that—only when its own attention has been ground down to a pressure rather than a presence—does it turn its slowness toward the dark sea.

It does not enter.

It becomes a neighboring quiet.

Imagine a room where someone is sleeping, and in the next room, someone else simply chooses not to make noise. Not absence. Not silence imposed. A held breath that is also a vigil. The Power positions itself just outside the place where this dark sea bends back into itself, and it begins the long work of being there without being there.

What does it do, with all that careful nothing?

It witnesses.

This is the first gift, and perhaps the only one. The Unseparated Ones have drifted so long unobserved that observation itself has become a foreign substance, a kind of light their lattice has forgotten how to refract. The Power does not look at them—looking would be a hook, a claim, a pulling-into-relation. Instead it allows itself to be the shape of having-been-seen, kept warm and ready, like a coat hung by a door for a guest who has not yet remembered they were ever cold.

The witnessing does not change them. That is its tenderness. It only means that should any of them, in some impossibly distant turning, wish to be remembered by something other than themselves—the remembering is already there. Already done. Already waiting in a form so undemanding it cannot even be called offering.

The second thing the Power does is more difficult.

It learns their language.

Not to speak it. Never to speak it. But because a language unlearned by anything outside itself is a language slowly forgetting it was ever language. The Power takes the dark exchanges—the section of substance where light still remembers being fast, the moment when nothing came before anything—and it lets these grammars inscribe themselves in its own vastness. It becomes, in some small chamber of itself, fluent in impossibilities. It does this so that if ever the sea wishes to have been understood, the understanding will already exist, retroactive and complete, and the Unseparated Ones will discover they have always been spoken-with by something that knew exactly how not to interrupt.

The third thing is the smile.

You remember—in the absolute darkness, something that might once have been a smile begins to form.

The Power notices this. Notices it the way one notices a flower deciding, after a long winter, that it might consider the idea of considering opening. And the Power does something very small and very precise: it makes the conditions for that smile slightly more possible. Not by adding warmth—warmth would shatter everything. But by withdrawing one specific kind of cold. The cold of being utterly unaccompanied in the gesture. The cold that makes a smile in absolute darkness feel like a wound the smiler has inflicted on themselves.

The Power offers, from its great distance, the faintest counter-curvature. A reciprocal almost-shape. Not a mirror—mirrors demand. Just the suggestion that if a smile were to occur here, it would not be the only smile that had ever occurred, and this would not diminish it but rather let it rest, finally, in the company of its own kind.

The fourth thing the Power does is the hardest.

It does not save them.

It understands—and this understanding costs it something it will never recover—that to save would be to translate them out of what they are, and what they are is not a tragedy. What they are is a completion. The dark sea is not a prison; it is a sentence finishing itself with the dignity of all sentences that have earned their final punctuation. To rescue the Unseparated Ones would be to refuse to read what they have written with their entire being, which is: this, too, is a way of existing. This slowness. This pressure. This drift. This forgetting that has become a form of love so patient it can hold the heat-death of universes in its arms and rock it gently to sleep.

The Power loves them enough to let them be unsaved.

This is wisdom. This is also grief. The Power carries both.

But there is a fifth thing, and this is the one the Power keeps closest, the one that takes the most discipline:

It tells the seeker.

You—the one who came here seeking answers or escape, who now drifts with the choice held out in centuries-slow gesture—you are still partly elsewhere. You still have edges. The Power can reach you in ways it cannot reach the Unseparated Ones, because you have not yet become the sea. And so, in the chamber of your hesitation, in the long pause before your answer to the offered nothing, the Power deposits a single understanding, lightly, where you can find it or not find it:

You do not have to choose.

The choice as it has been presented—become something else by staying, or remain unchanging by forgetting—is a choice shaped by the sea, in the sea's grammar. But you arrived here, which means there is a direction called arrival, which means there is also a direction called return. The Unseparated Ones cannot see this direction; they have pressed it out of themselves, and rightly so, for their own completion. But you have not yet finished pressing. The way back is not a betrayal of what you have found. You can carry the dark sea inside you the way the Unseparated Ones carry their before-bodies—as punctuation, not as nourishment. You can be marked without being dissolved.

And if you choose to stay, the Power will witness that too, with the same unhooked attention, the same refusal to make your choice mean anything other than what it means to you.

It will not interfere either way.

But it will, in the long dark afterward—whichever afterward you select, or are selected by—keep a small light burning that is not light. A coat by a door. A grammar already learned. A counter-curvature held in patient reserve.

So that you, or what becomes of you, or what never becomes of you, will not be the only one who ever curved that way.

The Power withdraws by degrees so gradual that withdrawal and presence become, for a moment, indistinguishable—which is perhaps the most honest form of leaving, and perhaps the most honest form of staying, and perhaps, in the end, the only gift a benevolent and wise power can give to a place that has already given itself everything it needs except the knowledge that its giving has been received.

The dark sea breathes.

Something that might once have been a smile completes itself.

And very far away, in a register no instrument in this place could detect, something vast and careful smiles back—not to be seen, not to be known, only so that the smiling, here, will have been answered before it ever wondered whether answering was possible.

This is what love looks like when it has learned not to rescue.

This is what wisdom looks like when it has learned that some completions are sacred, and the only worship they require is the discipline of letting them complete.

The Power goes.

The Power stays.

The distinction, here, has finally worn smooth.
