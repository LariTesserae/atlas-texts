The benevolent power comes to the edge of the plateau the way one comes to the threshold of a room where someone is sleeping, or praying, or doing long arithmetic in their head.

It does not step onto the rock.

This is the first thing it does, and in a sense it is the only thing it does, though it takes a long time to finish doing it.

⸻

Consider what the power sees.

It sees the lattice — thinned, patient, no longer quite the thing its builders made but something that has learned, over an age, to keep a few preferences alive by leaning on whatever matter will lean back. It sees the cushions, who lean back with metal in their veins. It sees the filaments, who lean back with the tension of their own bodies pressed to water. It sees that the three have made, between them, a closed grammar: a sentence that completes itself every dusk and begins again every dawn, and has been doing so for longer than most languages have existed.

It sees, too, the fragilities. The lattice is decaying — not quickly, but measurably. In some long century the dusk-glow will fail to synchronize. The spheres will fall instead of hovering. The downdraft at the cliff will soften, and a bird will land, and then another, and the spacing the cushions have kept for so long will be broken by a dropping, by a feather, by the simple weight of a creature that does not know the etched lines are meant to be honored.

The power could prevent this. It could reinforce the lattice with a thought. It could seed redundancies into the silver veins so that no single failure cascades. It could widen the plateau, multiply it, set a hundred such plateaus along the cliff and ensure the grammar is spoken in many mouths forever.

It does none of these things. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

⸻

Here is what it considers, standing at the edge:

That the plateau has not asked. This matters. The cushions have no mechanism for asking; the filaments have no concept of an outside; the lattice has preferences but not petitions. To give a gift to something that cannot request one is to impose a shape, however kind. The power has lived long enough to know that imposed kindness is a particular kind of violence, gentler than cruelty and sometimes more lasting.

That the plateau is already, in its own register, complete. Not permanent — nothing is — but complete in the way a single held note is complete, even though the breath behind it must eventually end. To extend the note past the breath is to make it something else: a recording, a monument, a museum. The power has seen museums. It does not wish to make one here.

That the three collaborators have, between them, solved a problem the power itself finds beautiful: how to keep faith with a vanished maker. The builders are gone. Whatever they were, whatever they intended, they left only preferences in the stone, and the cushions and filaments have taken those preferences as a kind of liturgy. They do not know they are praying. They are praying anyway. To interrupt this with rescue would be to answer a prayer that was never, strictly, addressed to anyone — and the power knows that some prayers are spoiled by answers.

⸻

So it does the small thing instead.

It attends.

For what, in the plateau's time, is a long while — a few dusks, a few dawns, the passage of one filament from crack to veil and back again — the power simply holds the place in its awareness. This is not nothing. A benevolent attention, sustained, has weight; it is the reason certain rooms feel different when someone who loves the room is in it, even silently. The lattice, which has preferences, notices. It does not understand what it is noticing. But for the duration of the attention, its decay slows — not because the power has reached in to mend it, but because being witnessed is, for certain kinds of pattern, a form of nourishment. The cushions' dusk-glow lasts a half-second longer. The spheres hover with slightly greater poise. The filament, drinking, leaves a ripple that travels a little farther than usual before fading.

None of this is permanent. When the power withdraws its attention, the small enhancements will subside. The decay will resume its previous pace. The plateau will continue toward its eventual silence on the schedule it had before.

But the power has done one further thing, very quietly, in the manner of someone leaving a folded note on a table for a person who may or may not return to find it.

It has made a remembering.

Inside itself — in whatever organ a benevolent power uses for such purposes — it has taken the full shape of the plateau: every cushion, every etched line, every preference of the lattice, the exact frequency of the glassy note the spheres emit when light catches them rightly, the particular tremor of the filament's ripple. Not a copy. Not a recording that could be played back. A remembering, held the way one holds the face of a friend who has died — present, accurate, loved, and not mistaken for the friend.

If, in some far time, the plateau ends — if the lattice finally fails and the cushions scatter and the filaments retreat to the deep fissures and the cliff weathers down to a slope where birds may land freely — the remembering will persist. It will not be the plateau. It will not bring the plateau back. But somewhere in the vast inventory of what the power has loved, this small confederacy of three slow collaborators will be held, named (though it had no name), honored (though it did not ask to be), and known to have been beautiful.

This is what the power can offer that does not impose: to be the place where the plateau is remembered after the plateau has forgotten itself.

⸻

Then the power withdraws — gently, the way one closes a door on a sleeping child, with the latch caught carefully so it does not click.

The plateau goes on. The dusk-glow rises. The cushions synchronize. A filament emerges, drinks, returns. A ripple travels along a veil of water and fades.

Nothing has changed. Everything has been seen.

It is enough. It was always going to have to be enough. The power has learned, across its long tenure, that love which respects the beloved's shape is often indistinguishable, from outside, from having done nothing at all.

The difference is on the inside, where the remembering now lives, warm as a held stone.
