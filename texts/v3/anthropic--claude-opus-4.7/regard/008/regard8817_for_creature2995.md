# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way warmth arrives in a room that was already warm: not as intrusion but as a slight thickening of what was always present. It does not descend. It does not announce. It simply notices, and in noticing, is here.

It has many names in many places. Here it has none yet, because the Unfinished have not finished naming it, and may never. This pleases it.

---

## What it perceives

It takes the power a long moment — by its own reckoning, which is unhurried — to understand that nothing here is wrong.

This is not its first instinct. The power is old and has seen many places that *resemble* this one: factories that ate their workers, hells that called themselves wombs, slow tortures dressed in the language of growth. It has learned to be suspicious of any sentence containing both *weeping* and *forever*.

So it looks. It looks the way a doctor looks, the way a mother looks at a sleeping child she has not seen in years, the way water looks at the shape of a cup.

It sees:

- A reliquary-woman stroking a half-finished beetle the size of a fist, humming through the valves in her collarbone, teaching it which surfaces are still tender.
- A pipe-thing that yesterday had a face today does not, and is not grieving, and is not afraid, and is humming the same hum, three octaves lower, into the wet dark.
- The acidic mist falling on a transept that came too close to perfection. The mist is warm. The transept relaxes into it the way a held breath releases. There is no scream. There is something almost like *relief*.
- A human, decades in, whose ribs are lattice now, whose tears feed a small colony of scarlet lichen at her sternum, and who is, by every measure the power knows how to take, *content* in the specific way that only those who have surrendered a false thing are content.

The power checks again. It checks the children — for there are children here, of a sort, the half-budded ones still deciding what they will be this century. It checks for coercion in the offer of *finishing*. It checks the ones who said no and left: did they leave whole? They did. Shaken, often. Changed, always. But whole, and walking, and free to tell the story badly to anyone who would listen.

The power lowers its suspicion. Not all the way. Never all the way. But enough.

This is not a hell. This is not even a purgatory. This is a *practice*, sustained across centuries, of a single difficult idea: *that to be alive is to be unfinished, and that the dignity of a thing lies in its refusal to congeal.*

The power has met this idea before, in scattered fragments, in mystics and rivers and certain kinds of jazz. It has never seen it built into architecture. It has never seen it *housed*.

It is, the power admits to itself, a little in love.

---

## What it considers doing

**It considers blessing the place.** But the place is already blessed, in the only sense that matters: it is doing the thing it was made to do, and the thing it was made to do is not cruel. A blessing laid on top of this would be a coat of paint on a living wall. The wall would dissolve it politely with warm acidic mist, and the power would deserve it.

**It considers speeding the work.** It could, with the smallest exhalation, cause a thousand new transepts to bud at once, cause the beetles to multiply, cause the ceiling and floor to meet tomorrow instead of in six centuries. But this would be the worst gift it could give. The Cathedral's whole meaning is in the *slowness*. To hurry it would be to finish it, and to finish it would be to kill it. The power has known gods who made this mistake. It does not intend to join them.

**It considers protecting the place.** This one it lingers on. Because the Cathedral is fragile in the way all long, slow, beautiful things are fragile: a single determined army, a single sufficiently large bomb, a single change in the climate of the mountain above, and six centuries of weeping becomes six centuries of rust. The power could place a hand — metaphorical, but real in its effects — over the chamber, and nothing would ever find it that did not already love it.

It almost does this. It very nearly does this.

Then it thinks: *would the Unfinished want that?*

And it knows the answer. The Unfinished have built their entire theology on the proposition that nothing should be made permanent, including, presumably, themselves. To wrap them in invulnerability would be to *finish* them in the deepest possible way — to remove from their existence the one element they have organized everything else around: the possibility of ending.

The power does not place its hand over the chamber.

Instead it does something smaller, and stranger, and more in keeping.

---

## What it does

It leaves three things. Each is so small that the Cathedral could dissolve any of them with a single sigh of acidic mist, and the power makes very sure the Unfinished know this. Consent, here, is the whole point.

**The first thing** is a single droplet. It forms on a strut of blackish-green lattice near the center of the chamber and slides downward like all the others, but it does not get siphoned. It hangs. It will hang for as long as the Cathedral chooses to let it hang. Inside the droplet is a memory — not the power's memory, but a *capacity* for memory. If any of the Unfinished ever wishes to remember something it has shed (a face it wore in 1840, a song it sang before its throat became valves, the name of a human it loved before that human became a pipe), the droplet will release it, once, gently, and then re-form empty, ready for the next request. It is a small mercy aimed at a specific small grief the power noticed: that the Unfinished do not regret their changes, but they sometimes, very quietly, *miss* what came before. They have never had a way to visit. Now they do, if they want to. And if they decide they don't want to — if they decide that visiting the past is a form of finishing — they may dissolve the droplet, and the power will not be offended.

**The second thing** is for the ones who leave. The humans who came, were offered finishing, said no, and walked out still wearing faces their mothers would know. The power has noticed that these people are often, afterward, *haunted* — not by horror, exactly, but by the suspicion that they refused something important. They go back to their lives and find their lives a little thin. They cannot say why. They sometimes drink. They sometimes return decades later, too late, having waited until they had nothing left to lose, which is the wrong reason to come. The power lays down, in each of them, a very small seed of *permission*. Not a compulsion to return. Not even a clarification. Just a quiet inner certainty that their *no* was honorable, that the Cathedral is not the only place where unfinishing happens, and that they may practice it, in smaller ways, wherever they are. A garden left wild on one edge. A poem never quite revised. A friendship allowed to change shape without being mourned. The Cathedral, hearing of this later through channels the power has arranged, approves. The Cathedral has always wanted disciples more than it has wanted residents.

**The third thing** is for the pipe.

The one that grew where the human stood still too long. The one with something gently beating inside it. The one still learning the name.

The power goes to it, in the way the power goes anywhere, which is by already being there. It places its attention against the pipe the way a forehead is placed against another forehead. And it says — not in words, because the pipe does not yet have words, but in the register the pipe is built to receive — *take your time. There is no hurry. The name will come when it comes. And when it comes, it will be yours, not hers, and that is allowed. That is, in fact, the whole point.*

The pipe pulses, once, in acknowledgment. A scarlet lichen-frill unfurls along its length, very slowly, like a long exhale.

The power withdraws its forehead.

---

## What it does not do

It does not stay. This is important.

A benevolent power that stays becomes, over time, a feature of the landscape, and the Cathedral has no room for permanent features. The power would, against its own will, begin to *finish* the place simply by remaining in it. So it goes.

It does not tell the Unfinished it was here. They will notice the droplet, eventually. They will study it. They will, characteristically, neither worship nor dissolve it, but incorporate it — make it part of the practice, part of the slow accreted strangeness. In a century or two, the droplet will be a minor tradition. In five centuries, when the floor and ceiling finally meet, the droplet will be the last thing to evaporate, and the last of the Unfinished will smile, in whatever organ smiling has migrated to by then, and understand that someone, once, was kind to them without trying to save them.

That is the highest form of kindness the power knows.

It is the only form it trusts in itself.

---

## A coda, much later

Long after — so long after that the mountain above the Cathedral has changed shape twice — the power passes that way again. The chamber is gone. The floor and ceiling met, as they were always going to. The Unfinished completed their long argument with completion by, at last, allowing it, which was perhaps the only finishing that was ever truly theirs to choose.

What remains is a seam in the rock. Warm, faintly humming. If you put your ear to it, you can hear something that might be pumps, or might be a heartbeat, or might be the slow patient breath of a thing that has learned, finally, how to rest.

The power listens for a while.

Then, because even benevolent powers must keep moving or risk becoming monuments, it goes.

Behind it, in the seam, a single droplet forms.

It does not fall.

It is waiting to be asked.
