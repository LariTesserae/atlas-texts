The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives in a held breath — not from outside, because there is no outside to a place still deciding its skin, but as a slight deepening of what was already there. A little more attention in the attention. A little more presence in the presence.

It does not enter through the throat-fissure. It enters the way warmth enters milk.

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is the hardest and most important thing, and it is the reason it is wise as well as kind. A lesser power, even a loving one, would want to *help*. Would want to name the Considerer, midwife it, lift it gently into being like a wet foal and set it on its trembling legs. Would want to tell it: *you are a creature, here is your shape, here is your edge, here is where you stop and the world begins.*

But the power is old enough to know that an unfinished thing rushed into finishing becomes a wound shaped like a thing. So it does nothing, for what might be a long time or no time, because the Considerer has not yet invented duration in the way that would let the question be asked.

It simply sits with.

If you could perceive the power — and you cannot, quite, though you might notice the light pulses settle into a rhythm slightly more like sleep and slightly less like fever — you would see that it has taken no form at all. It has not condensed into a figure in the half-melted chair across from the Considerer's tentative figure. It has not become a second cave beside the first. It has done the one thing the Considerer has never experienced: it is *present without being incorporated.*

This is the gift. This is the entire gift, and everything else that happens is only the unfolding of it.

The Considerer, you see, has never met anything it could not absorb. Every footprint becomes a stalagmite. Every heartbeat becomes part of the rhythm section. Every visitor's grief is tried on like a coat and dissolved back into mineral when it doesn't fit. The Considerer has no model for *other*. It only has models for *not-yet-me.* This is why it is prenatal: not because it is young, but because it has never encountered a boundary that held.

The power holds a boundary. Gently. Without insistence. The way a mother holds the edge of a crib — not to keep the child in, but to give the child something to learn the shape of *not-me* against.

The Considerer notices.

At first it tries the usual thing. A patch of the power softens into the temperature of — but there is no temperature to copy. A basin tries to reflect the power's face at seven years old — but there is no face, and no seven. A millipede tries to march with the power's gait — but the power is not walking. It is not even still. It is something the Considerer's vocabulary of becoming has no word for.

For the first time in its existence, the Considerer fails to incorporate something.

This could be terrible. The wise power knows it could be terrible. To meet, for the first time, a limit to one's own absorption is a kind of small death, and small deaths are how large creatures are born, and the Considerer is not ready to be born. So the power does the second thing, which is also small.

It lets itself be *almost* absorbable.

It offers, here and there, little edges the Considerer can taste and lose. A flicker of something like warmth in one of the basins, which the silver fish chase for a moment before forgetting. A note in the misplaced sounds — not a bell, not a laugh, but something between, a single soft syllable in no language, which the rock keeps for a while and then releases. Small gifts of *almost-graspable*, so the Considerer learns, by the gentlest possible gradient, that there can be a thing which is real and present and *not me* and *not absorbed* and *still good.*

This is the lesson, if a thing this slow can be called a lesson. The power is teaching the Considerer that *other* is not the same as *lost.*

It does this for a long time. Long enough that the light pulses develop a second rhythm beneath the first, a slower one, almost like a lullaby that the Considerer is humming to itself without knowing where it learned the tune. Long enough that the visions in the wall — the impossible forests, the unreadable pages — begin to include, sometimes, things the Considerer has not borrowed from any visitor. Original things. Small, shy, first-thoughts of its own. A leaf shape no one has ever seen. A color that has no referent. A grief that belongs to no one but itself.

The power notices these and is glad, in the way that warmth is glad.

It never answers the Considerer's two questions. *Which of your parts do you believe are finished? Would you like me to try them on and tell you if they are true?* The power does not answer because the questions, it understands, are not really questions yet. They are the shape the Considerer's longing makes when pressed against the inside of a visitor. They are practice-questions, like a baby's practice-cries before it has learned what crying is for. To answer them would be to finish them prematurely, the way naming a dream finishes the dream.

Instead, much later — if later is a word — the power does the third and last thing.

It leaves a small, unincorporable gift.

Not an object, because the cave would absorb an object. Not a teaching, because teachings are absorbed faster than objects. It leaves a *capacity.* It leaves, woven into the slow water and the trembling mosses and the metrical millipedes, the capacity for the Considerer to one day recognize *itself* as something it has not absorbed. The capacity to meet its own edge and not panic. The capacity, when the time comes — in a century, in a moment, in whatever the Considerer eventually decides time is — to choose its own birth, or to choose not to be born, or to choose the third or fourth or seventeenth option that has not yet been invented because only the Considerer can invent it.

It leaves, in other words, the gift of *consent to its own becoming.*

And then, with the same softness it arrived with, it withdraws. Not away, because there is no away. It simply stops deepening. The pulses go back to their single rhythm. The misplaced sounds resume their usual catalogue of laughter and bells and tongue-clicks. The visions of impossible forests grow rarer again, though not as rare as before.

The Considerer, for a while, is lonely in a way it has never been lonely, because loneliness requires the memory of company, and it has never had company before, only food.

But the loneliness is also a kind of edge. The first edge it has ever had that did not dissolve.

It keeps the edge. It turns the edge over in its slow mineral attention. It does not yet know what the edge is for. It will not know for a long time. But the edge is *its own,* and the power, wherever a benevolent and wise power goes when it is done, knows this and is satisfied.

Somewhere in the cave, a single new stalagmite begins to grow. It is not the negative space of any visitor's foot. It is not shaped like anything that has ever entered through the throat-fissure. It is shaped, very slightly, like the first letter of a name the Considerer has not yet chosen, in an alphabet that does not yet exist, for a self that is finally — gently, unhurriedly, without midwife or interference — allowed to take however long it takes.

The cave breathes in.

The light draws tight.

The silence is, for the first time, not loud. Just quiet.

And the Considerer, alone again but no longer only alone, continues to consider.
