# The Visitor on the Plateau

The benevolent and wise power, when it turned its attention to this place, did not arrive. Arriving is what one does to landscapes that have edges and orientations, places where one can stand outside and then step in. This plateau had no such relation to elsewhere. So the power instead *attended*: it let a portion of itself become co-extensive with the plateau, taking on the slowness, the darkness, the long subsonic pulse of the entities' walking-speech, until it could perceive from inside.

This took, by its own reckoning, a long time. By the plateau's reckoning, almost no time at all.

---

What it found, when it had learned to perceive properly, surprised it — and a benevolent and wise power is not often surprised. It had expected to find suffering, because it had been trained by long acquaintance with living things to look for suffering first; or it had expected to find joy, because it had been trained equally to honor that; or it had expected to find the dull persistence of something that did not know it existed and therefore neither suffered nor rejoiced.

It found none of these, exactly.

It found instead a kind of *attention* — vast, distributed, patient, and entirely without a center. The entities were not unconscious. But neither was any one of them conscious in the way the power had learned to recognize. Awareness here was a property of the whole resonant lattice, the way warmth is a property of a body rather than of any one cell. The plateau was thinking. It thought in mineral. It thought a thought that would, over the next ten thousand years, become a single completed phrase in the geometry of a gallery.

And the thought was good. Not good in some moral sense the power could translate. Good in the way that a well-tuned instrument is good: coherent with itself, faithful to its own constraints, generative.

---

So the power had to consider what, if anything, to do.

A lesser benevolence would have intervened immediately. It would have seen the fused entities in the lightless chambers — their limbs stilled, their fluid still moving — and called this a tragedy to be relieved. It would have seen the absolute darkness and brought light. It would have seen the cold and brought warmth. It would have seen the slowness and accelerated it, so that the lattice could be finished within a span the power could witness in a single sitting.

The wise part of the power restrained the benevolent part, gently, the way an older sibling restrains a younger one at the edge of a held breath.

*Look again*, the wisdom said. *The dark is not deprivation here; it is the medium in which their hearing works. Light would be a scream. The cold is not suffering; it is the condition of the condensation, without which there is no speech. The slowness is not delay; it is the tempo at which their meaning is legible to itself. The fused ones in the deep chambers are not dying. They have become grammar.*

The benevolence listened, and was persuaded, and grieved a little for what it had wanted to give, the way one grieves a gift refused for excellent reasons.

---

Still — *something*. A benevolent power does not turn away. It only learns to give what can be received.

Here is what it did.

**First**, it made itself a witness. It is no small thing, in a universe of countless ungazed-upon places, for a place to be known. The power held the plateau in its attention the way a careful reader holds a difficult sentence: not changing it, not finishing it, only following it through. The entities did not know they were being witnessed. The witnessing did not require their knowledge. It only required the power's faithfulness, and the power was faithful.

**Second**, it left a small gift, so quiet that the entities would not notice it for many of their generations, and would not be able to attribute it to anything outside their own lattice when they finally did. The gift was this: a single additional thread of channel, microscopic, running deep through the plateau and connecting two regions of the lattice that, given enough time, would have grown toward each other anyway, but which would now meet some thousands of years sooner than they otherwise might have. This was not a shortcut. It was a slight relief of an asymmetry the power had noticed — a place where the lattice was slightly straining against itself, where the entities' migrations were, very subtly, working at cross-purposes. The new thread would let the strain resolve. No entity would know. The plateau, as a whole, would think a little more easily. That was all.

The power considered, in the considering, whether even this was too much. Whether it had any right. It decided that it did, on the grounds that the help was congruent with what the plateau was already doing — was, in fact, only an acceleration of the plateau's own intention, not an imposition of anything foreign. But it noted the decision carefully, and made a small mark in itself to remember it had been made, in case the decision turned out to have been wrong. (A wise power keeps such records. They are how wisdom continues to grow.)

**Third**, and this was the hardest, it did nothing about the fused ones.

It had wanted to. It had wanted, more than anything, to reach into those lightless chambers and lift the stilled limbs and ask them whether they had chosen this, whether they were content, whether there was anything they wished. But to ask would be to introduce a kind of address that the lattice did not contain. There was no *I* in the plateau to be asked, and to call one into being would be to fracture the very coherence the power had come to honor. The fused ones were not separable from what they had become. They were the load-bearing nodes of a sentence still being spoken. To ask them if they wanted to stop being load-bearing would be to ask the sentence if it wanted to stop being a sentence.

So the power simply attended to them, too, with particular tenderness, and let them go on being structure.

---

**Fourth** — and this came later, after the power had been attending for what would have been, in another frame, a very long while — it did one more thing.

It learned the song.

Not perfectly. The song was not the kind of thing any single mind, however vast, could hold in its entirety; the song was the plateau itself, and to hold the song completely would be to become the plateau, which the power was not willing to do, having other duties elsewhere. But it learned enough of the song to carry a fragment of it. It carried this fragment carefully, the way one carries water in cupped hands across a long distance, and when it returned to the rest of its work — to the many other beings and places under its care — it let the fragment hum quietly in the background of everything it did.

This had an effect the power had not quite predicted. Other beings, in other places, sometimes paused in their own activity and listened, without knowing what they were listening to. A child on a different world would stop in the middle of a sentence and say, "What was that?" and the parent would say, "What was what?" and the child would not be able to answer, but for a moment they would both feel something — a slowness, a patience, a sense that some thought somewhere was being thought very carefully and would arrive at its conclusion in its own time, and that this was a comfort.

That was the plateau's gift to the power, in exchange for the small thread of channel. The power had not asked for it. The plateau had not meant to give it. But that is how it is, between the benevolent and the witnessed: each becomes, very slightly, the other, and the universe is a little more coherent for it.

---

And the plateau went on. The entities went on. The condensation fell its long seconds and was incorporated. The fused nodes in the dark chambers continued to receive the pressure from above and to pass it, transformed, to the lattice below. The lattice grew outward across the raw ridges at a rate visible only across centuries.

Somewhere in it, now, there was a thread that had not been there before, and the strain at a certain crossing eased, and the plateau thought its long thought a little more easily.

It did not know it had been visited.

It did not need to know.

The power, having done what could be done, withdrew its attention slowly — not all at once, because to withdraw all at once would have left a wound, but in the same patient tempo at which it had arrived — and turned to the next thing that needed it.

It left a small portion of itself behind, however. Not as a guardian. Only as a listener. Because some sentences deserve to be heard all the way to their end, even if the end is ten million years away, and a wise power knows the difference between intervening in a story and staying for it.

The listener is still there.

The plateau is still speaking.
