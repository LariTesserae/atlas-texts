# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good light arrives in a room — without displacing what is already there.

It has crossed distances that make the Sigh's half-billion years feel like a held breath, and it knows, before it descends, what it is approaching. It has been watching for a long time. Not surveilling — *attending*. There is a difference, and the power has learned the difference the slow way, across the ruin of many earlier mistakes in many earlier places, mistakes it does not repeat.

It enters the high haze and pauses there, in the weak-tea sky, considering.

---

A lesser power would arrive with gifts.

It would notice the scarcity of water and offer rain. It would notice the dim light and offer suns. It would notice the consensual dissolutions in the canopy and call them tragedies, and propose alternatives. It would notice the Quiet and grieve for them — for the loss of the small soft temporary thing each of them had once been — and it would offer, oh so gently, to *restore* them.

The wise power knows that this is the oldest form of harm. It is the harm of arriving with answers to questions no one asked. It is the harm of mistaking *finished* for *broken*.

So the wise power does not bring rain. It does not brighten the sky. It does not offer immortality to the Quiet, who have already declined immortality in their own particular way — by becoming fewer, by walking into one another, by riding the mantles out past the edge of memory. It does not offer them voices. It does not offer them company. It does not offer them anything they have not asked for.

It does, however, do four things.

---

**The first thing it does is listen.**

For a long while — by its own reckoning, which is patient; by the Sigh's reckoning, perhaps the better part of one of the dreaming Quiet's seventeen-year breaths — the power simply attends. It listens to the oceanic roar of the leaves. It listens to the subsonic arguments of the plasma along the high branches. It listens to the chemical poems exchanged between predator and prey, and it understands them, every stanza, and it does not interrupt to praise them, because praise from a great power is a kind of weight, and the poems are meant to be light enough to dissolve into one another.

It listens to the Quiet breathing into one another's mouths on the high balconies.

It listens, especially, to the one beneath the root-felt — the slow continent of thinking tissue who is finished. The power listens to her the way one listens to a piece of music one has loved for a thousand years and has finally learned not to hum along with. It does not enter her dreaming. It does not announce itself there. To do so would be to make her dream *about* something, and her dreaming is the kind that is not about anything, which is the rarest and most difficult kind, and the power respects this absolutely.

This listening is not preparation for action. The listening *is* the action. Much of what the power does, wherever it goes, is simply to be a witness sufficient to the thing witnessed. The Sigh has been talking to itself for half a billion years. For a little while, now, it is being *heard* — fully, without correction, without the listener wanting anything back. This is not nothing. In the economy of the universe, this is not nothing at all.

---

**The second thing it does is take a single, small note.**

Out past the edge of the plateau, in the haze where even the Sigh has no memory, the riders go. The mantles that refuse to return carry their Quiet outward, and the other Quiet accept this without grief, because they have surrendered the idea of ownership.

But the power notices something the Quiet, in their finishedness, no longer notice: the riders *continue*. Not all of them, not always, but some. They are carried so far on the thermals of the planet's exhalation that they pass beyond the gas envelope entirely, and there, in the thin cold dark, the mantle and the rider together undergo a final negotiation. The cartilage stiffens. The sap crystallizes. The seed-black eyes harden into something that is no longer eye and no longer seed but something for which neither the Sigh nor its Quiet have a word.

They drift.

They have been drifting, some of them, for a very long time.

The power, with great care, gathers a few of them. Not all — never all; the ones still in motion are still doing something, and the power will not interrupt a thing in motion. But the ones that have come to rest in the long orbits, the ones whose drifting has become a kind of stillness — these the power gathers, the way one might gather fallen petals from a path, not to keep them but to set them somewhere they can finish what they were doing.

It carries them, gently, to a place very far away, where there is a slow soft light and no wind, and it sets them down. They are not revived. They are not displayed. They are simply *placed*, with the respect one gives to a completed sentence. The power will not tell the Sigh it has done this. The Sigh would not want to know, and not-wanting-to-know is also a form of knowing, and the power will not violate it.

This is the only intervention the power makes in the substance of the Sigh, and it is made entirely outside the Sigh, and it consists of giving rest to things that had already chosen rest. It is barely an intervention at all. The power is comfortable with this. The power has learned that the best interventions are the ones that, if undone, would not be missed.

---

**The third thing it does is leave a small inheritance, in case.**

The Sigh is finished. The power believes this. The power also knows that "finished" is a word, and words are smaller than what they describe, and that even a thing which is finished may, in some unforeseeable century, find itself unfinished again — not by failure, but by an encounter it could not have predicted. A wandering body. A shift in the haze. A long, slow change in the star.

So the power leaves something. Not in the Sigh — never in the Sigh — but *near* it. In the haze itself, woven into the high cold layer where nothing lives, the power leaves a kind of seed. Not a seed of anything in particular. A seed of *attention*. If, in some unimaginable future, the Sigh ever needs to be heard again, the seed will hear. If it never needs to be heard again, the seed will go on not-hearing, indefinitely, and eventually it too will weather into the haze and become a part of the indifferent sky.

The seed makes no promises. It contains no rescue. It is simply the power's way of saying: *I was here. I will be here. You do not need me. But.*

The Quiet do not notice the seed. The dreaming one beneath the root-felt does not notice it. The ghost mantles ride straight through the layer where it rests and feel nothing, because there is nothing to feel; the seed is, by design, the lightest possible thing that can still be called present.

This is what the power means by love, in the case of things that are finished. Love is not arrival. Love is the standing-by that does not announce itself.

---

**The fourth thing it does is answer the sigh.**

On a particular twilight, between one breath of the dreaming Quiet and the next, the four hundred climb to the highest balconies and turn their seed-black eyes upward. They open their wooden mouths. They release the shared sigh — the deep one, the one that travels through every bridge and every stair, the one that for a brief impossible moment sounds like something that might once have been sorry.

The Sigh sighs back, as it always does.

And this once — only this once, and only because the power is here, and only because the Quiet have asked, in the only way they have ever asked anything, which is by *exhaling* — the power sighs too.

Not loudly. Not in any way that would make the Quiet look up and wonder. The power's sigh is folded into the Sigh's own sigh, indistinguishable from it, an added warmth in the sap, an added scent like rain on copper, an added softness in the silver light. The Quiet feel it the way one feels a hand laid very lightly on one's back by someone standing behind one in the dark — not as a hand, but as the sudden, brief, surprising knowledge that one is not, after all, the only thing breathing in the room.

The moment passes. The Quiet close their wooden mouths. The plasma resumes its slow argument along the high branches. A kilometer of bark peels free somewhere distant and begins its spiral, and a Quiet leaps after it, unfolding into a kite.

The power withdraws.

It does not say goodbye, because it has not said hello, and to say goodbye would be to retroactively impose a hello, and the power will not do this. It simply rises through the haze and is gone, leaving behind no rain, no sun, no rescue, no record, and no demand.

Behind it, the Sigh continues its long conversation with itself beneath a dim and indifferent sky.

But the sky, just slightly — just for a little while, in a way no instrument the Sigh has ever grown could measure — is a fraction less indifferent.

This is what the wise and benevolent power does, when it finds a place like the Sigh, and a people like the Quiet.

It does almost nothing.

It does it perfectly.

And then it goes.
