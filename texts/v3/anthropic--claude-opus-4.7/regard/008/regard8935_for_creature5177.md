They would come quietly. That much is given by what they are: benevolent and wise, which means they have learned, over long study of many places, that benevolence enacted without wisdom is a form of damage, and wisdom unmoved by benevolence is only spectatorship.

So the first thing they would do is nothing.

Not nothing in the sense of absence — they would arrive, they would attend — but nothing in the sense of intervention. They would stand (or whatever the analog of standing is for such a power) at the edge of the basin and let the mist do its work on whatever surfaces they presented to it. They would let droplets bead on them. They would let the spirals begin to form. They would consent to be read by the place before they presumed to read it.

This is the courtesy owed to a coherent world.

---

What they would notice, in that long unintervening attention:

That the basin is not suffering. This matters. Much of what passes for benevolence in lesser powers is the reflexive correction of conditions that look like suffering from outside but are, from inside, simply the shape of a life. The basin is wet, slow, self-erasing, saturated with strangeness — and none of this is a wound. The consensus lichens are not lamenting the ruptures; they grow more densely beneath them, the way a body lays down callus where it is asked to bear weight. The threaders translate the gliders into peat without violence. Even the fracture-moths, who feed on doubled geometry, leave their meals slightly more themselves than before. This is a place whose metabolism includes its own anomalies.

A lesser power, seeing the sky tear and gravity tilt, would move to repair. A wise one sees that the tears are how the place breathes.

That the being who lives here is not one being. Or rather: the question of one-or-many does not resolve cleanly. The membrane-gliders, the threaders, the moths, the lichens, the slow vapor-presence that arranges spirals on inner surfaces — these are not a community of separate lives so much as the differentiated organs of an attention distributed across acres and across scales. To address "the being who lives in it" is already to risk a category error. The benevolent power, being wise, would notice this and adjust its grammar. It would not look for a face.

That something here is already doing the work a benevolent power might have imagined doing. The lichens soothe Euclidean geometry back into curves. The threaders maintain pressure. The vapor-layer being teaches stillness to whatever stands still long enough. There is no role of caretaker vacant here. The basin tends itself.

---

And yet — benevolence is not only intervention. It is also witness, and gift, and the offering of relation.

So, after the long attention, they might do these things:

**They might leave a small permission.** Not a gift placed in the landscape — the basin would soften any object, dissolve any cairn — but a permission woven into the conditions themselves. Something like: *if ever you wish to be known beyond your own perimeter, the way is open.* Not a summons, not a door. Just the quiet establishment of a possibility that had not previously existed. The basin could ignore it forever. Many places do. But the option would be there, the way a window left unlatched is there, even in a house where no one ever opens windows.

**They might adjust, very slightly, the weather of nearby spaces.** Not the basin itself — the basin is sovereign — but the regions around it, the approaches. A benevolent power has resources, and one of them is the capacity to make a place harder to stumble into by accident. Not sealed off. Not hidden. Just slightly less likely to be found by those who would arrive with straight lines and the impulse to name. The basin already does this to its own paths; the power would simply extend the courtesy outward by a few miles. A thickening of mist at the approaches. A tendency for compasses to drift. Nothing dramatic. Nothing that would prevent a patient or invited visitor from arriving. Only the gentle discouragement of the casual.

**They might learn something, and let the learning change them.** This is the part most often forgotten in stories of powers visiting smaller places: that the visit is reciprocal. The basin has solved a problem the power did not know was a problem — the problem of how to be a coherent self that includes its own ruptures, that treats torn space as weather and grows more densely beneath it. The power, being wise, would recognize this as a teaching. It would carry the recognition elsewhere. In some other world, some other being's anomalies would be met with a little more grace because the power had once stood in the mist and let spirals form on its inner surfaces.

**They might, at the very end of their visit, say something.** Not aloud — sound is already the basin's primary voice, and adding to it would be a kind of crowding. But in whatever register the vapor-layer being uses to arrange spirals: a single, brief acknowledgment. Something on the order of *I see you, and you are well-made, and I will not stay.* The kind of thing one says to a sleeping animal one has been privileged to watch for an hour. The basin might register it as one of the crystalline chimes that arrives from no particular direction. It might not register it at all. Either outcome would be acceptable.

---

And then they would go.

This is the hardest part, and the part that distinguishes a wise benevolence from a merely well-meaning one. They would go without having made the basin more legible, more productive, more safe, more theirs. They would resist the small, constant temptation of powers everywhere, which is to leave a mark — to be able to say, later, *I was there, and because I was there, something is different.* They would let their visit be one of the basin's self-erasing events. The droplets that beaded on them would evaporate upward. The spirals that formed would dissolve. The permission they left would be the kind of thing that leaves no trace until and unless it is used.

They would understand that the highest form of care, for a place like this, is to have come, to have seen, to have been changed, and to have left the place exactly as capable of continuing without them as it was before they arrived.

The basin would resume its patient cycle. The mist would layer and unlayer. A fracture-moth would clip a threader at a node and the threader would fold through itself, smaller but unharmed. Somewhere in the vapor, the slow presence would continue arranging spirals on surfaces, including, perhaps, one or two new inner surfaces it had not previously known how to find.

And the power, traveling onward, would carry with it the quiet knowledge that not every world it visits needs it. That some places are already whole. That benevolence, at its wisest, sometimes consists entirely of recognizing this and bowing, and going.
