They would not enter.

That is the first thing, and almost the whole thing. A benevolent power, being wise, would understand what kind of place this is before deciding what to do in it. And what kind of place it is becomes clear from the description itself: a closed system that has reached, over uncounted ages, a state of profound internal coherence. Nothing here is lacking. Nothing here is suffering in any sense the word can carry. The chamber is not a prison; it is not a wound; it is not a forgetting. It is a slow, complete life, doing exactly what it is.

So the power pauses at the threshold it does not need to cross, and considers.

---

It considers, first, whether there is need. This is the test a wise benevolence applies before it acts, because benevolence without that test becomes only the will to touch, the will to leave a mark, the will to be the agent of another's good. The nodules do not need light; their light leaks from below in pulses suited to them. They do not need air; the rising currents are sufficient. They do not need company; they are, in their slow chemical negotiations, already in the most intimate conversation possible, exchanging silica through threads finer than sight. They do not need rescue from time, because time here is not an enemy. They do not need to be known, because being known is a hunger that belongs to other kinds of being.

It considers, second, whether there is suffering. A nodule underfed darkens and is overgrown; is this a death to be mourned, a wrong to be set right? The wise power looks closely and sees that the material is reused, that nothing is wasted, that the underfed nodule does not experience its dissolution as loss because it does not experience as a self. The colony's edges are negotiated, not defended. There is pressure, but pressure is the medium, not the affliction. To intervene against this would be to misunderstand it utterly, the way one might "rescue" a river by stopping its flow.

It considers, third, whether there is meaning that should be preserved. The pillar with its almost-erased spirals. The platform with its slumped steps. The fused arch. Someone, once, imposed shapes here, and those shapes are being slowly absorbed and faintly recopied by nodules that align their facets to grooves they cannot read. This is the only thing in the chamber that resembles a question. The wise power lingers on it.

And then it does something very small.

---

It does not restore the carvings. It does not reveal who made them or why. It does not lift the chamber into light or open it to the plateau's wind. It does not speak to the nodules, who have no apparatus for being spoken to and no need of one.

What it does is this: it witnesses. Fully, without condescension, without the wish to alter. It allows the chamber to be known, once, in the precise way the chamber is — not translated into any other terms, not made into a metaphor for something the power finds more legible. The pulse of the seams, the breath measured in hours, the click of lattice planes, the threads of dissolved silica running between neighbors who are also rivals who are also continuous with one another — all of this is held, for an instant of whatever the power's time is, in attention.

This is not nothing. In the older traditions one might say that to be perfectly seen, once, by something that wishes you only well and asks nothing, is a kind of completion. It does not change the seen thing. It changes the relation of the seen thing to the rest of what exists. The chamber, after the witnessing, continues exactly as before; but it is now also a thing that has been beheld in its entirety by a benevolent mind, and that beholding is added to the sum of what is true about it, silently, without the chamber's knowledge or need of knowledge.

---

And then — because wisdom is also restraint extended through time — the power makes one further choice. It marks the place. Not visibly. Not in any way the nodules or any future visitor could detect. It marks it in itself: a small, permanent attention turned toward this spire on this plateau, so that if ever the conditions change — if the seams cool, if the plateau cracks, if some other being comes seeking to break the chamber open for its minerals or its strangeness — the power will know, and will be present at that moment to do whatever then is right.

This is the form benevolence takes when its object does not require help: it becomes a standing readiness. A promise made to no one, kept by no one's request, that this slow patient life will not end through carelessness while the power exists to prevent it. The nodules will never know. They do not need to know. The promise is not for them in the sense of being received; it is for them in the sense of being about them.

---

The power withdraws. The seams pulse. The upper nodules expand by the width of a molecule and contract again. A vein of liquid mineral moves through a bridge, carrying heat upward, carrying waste down. Somewhere in the chamber, a fist-sized colony aligns one of its facets to the faintest remnant of a spiral it cannot read, and the copy begins, and the distinction begins, in its centuries-long way, to disappear.

Nothing has happened. Everything continues.

This is what love looks like, sometimes, when it is old enough to know what it is for.
