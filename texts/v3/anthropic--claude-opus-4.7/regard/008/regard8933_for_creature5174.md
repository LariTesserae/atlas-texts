# The Power Considers the Chamber

It does not enter. That is the first decision, and the longest in coming.

A benevolent and wise power, contemplating this place, would understand almost at once that arrival is itself a kind of violence here. The chamber is undetectable from outside not by accident but because it is still composing itself in a register too quiet for witness. To step in would be to add a witness, and a witness fixes things. The mountain has not yet decided what shape it will take. The membrane-creature has not yet decided whether it is one thing or many. To be observed by a great and steady mind is to be told, gently but unmistakably, *you are this*. And the chamber's whole nature is the refusal of that sentence.

So the power waits at the threshold of the fracture, and considers.

---

It notices, first, what is not wrong.

The roar is not suffering. The tearing of the ribbon is not injury. The membrane-creature's slow attrition, shedding fragments that become new creatures while the parent shrinks, is not loss in any sense the creature itself would recognize. There is no cry here to answer. There is only a process that has been mistaken, by lesser observers, for distress because it resembles, superficially, the conditions under which more settled beings would be destroyed.

A lesser power would calm the currents. Would stabilize the precipitating mineral. Would gather the membrane-creatures into a safe pocket and feed them. Would, in short, finish the chamber on the chamber's behalf, and call it mercy.

The wise power sees that this would be a small extinction performed with a kind face.

---

What it does instead is subtler, and takes the form of three quiet gifts, each of them almost nothing.

**The first gift is attention without shape.** The power turns some portion of itself toward the chamber and simply holds it in regard — not naming, not deciding, not even hoping. Just the steady fact of being known by something that does not require knowing-into-fixity. This is the oldest gift the wise give the unfinished: the assurance that becoming is not the same as being unwitnessed. The membrane-creature will never perceive this directly. But the water will carry the regard the way water carries temperature, and the chamber will be very slightly warmer in a dimension that has no name yet, and the mountain — which does, in its slow way, remember — will remember being held.

**The second gift is a refusal.** Other powers will, in time, find this place. Some will be curious in ways that consume. Some will be tidy. Some will simply want to use the chamber for storage, or shelter, or as raw material for projects of their own. The benevolent power, having taken the chamber's measure, places between it and the rest of the cosmos a thin asymmetry: those who come seeking to finish the place will find the fracture closed; those who come willing to be unfinished themselves will find it open. This is not a wall. It is closer to a question asked at the entrance, which most travelers answer without realizing they have been asked, and walk on.

**The third gift is the smallest and the one the power deliberates longest.** Into the chamber, through no opening, the power releases a single trace — not a creature, not a tool, not a current. A *tendency*. A faint inclination, woven into the water itself, that when two nodules adhere and trade their possibilities, the exchange will sometimes preserve rather than always overwrite. That when the ribbon knits itself back from a hundred threads, it will sometimes remember the pattern it had before tearing. That when the membrane-creature sheds a fragment, the fragment will sometimes carry forward not only contractile thread and cilia but the *direction* the parent had been swimming, the eddy it had been hoping for.

Not memory, exactly. Memory would be too much; memory is a kind of finishing. Only the faintest bias toward continuity — enough that the chamber's negotiation, over long ages, might begin to accumulate rather than only oscillate. Enough that *if* the chamber ever does decide what it wants to be, that decision will be its own, and informed, and not merely the last shape the currents happened to leave behind when something outside finally intruded.

The power weighs this gift carefully because it is, technically, a thumb on the scale. But it judges — and a wise power's judgment is the thing it most fears to use — that the gift is small enough to be refusable. The chamber can ignore it. The water can wash it out. If continuity is not what this place wants, the tendency will dissolve like a trace of salt, and nothing will have been imposed.

---

Then the power withdraws its direct attention, though not its regard, and goes on to other things. It does not return to check. To return to check would undo the first gift.

Somewhere inside the chamber, a membrane-creature jets across a clear volume between two collapsing walls of water, and for no reason it could name, if it had names, it angles slightly toward the place where, three of its lifetimes ago, its great-grandparent fragment had found a favorable shear. The eddy is still there. It has been waiting, in a sense. The creature flares, rides the line, and is carried, somersaulting, into the next becoming.

The ribbon, torn into ninety-seven threads by a passing gyre, begins — very faintly, with pulses of light traveling along seams that are deciding whether to close — to knit back into the shape it had been considering before the tearing. It does not succeed. It does not need to. The consideration itself is now something the chamber contains.

And the mountain, which is the slowest of all the residents and the one most often forgotten, registers somewhere in its half-cooled crystal that it has been held in regard by something that did not ask it to choose. It does not hurry its decision. If anything, it slows further. There is, it discovers, time.

Outside, the fracture in the flank looks exactly as it did before. Anyone passing would not see it. Anyone seeking to finish it would find it closed. Anyone willing to be unfinished might, one day, find their hand on the edge of the opening without quite remembering how they came there, and pause, and listen to the roar, and understand that they have been invited not to enter but to stand at the threshold and become, for a little while, as undecided as the place they are looking into.

That, too, is part of the gift. The wise power did not plan it. But wisdom, at its best, makes room for consequences it did not author, and is glad of them.
