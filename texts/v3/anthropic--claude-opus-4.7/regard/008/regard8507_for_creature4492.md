The benevolent power arrives without arriving. To arrive would be to interrupt, and interruption is not its way—it has learned, across stretches of being that make seven thousand years look like the pause between two heartbeats, that wisdom often consists in declining to be a god.

It does not descend. It does not announce. It simply *attends*, the way deep listening is itself a presence.

What it sees first is not the city but the Dreaming Wind, because the Wind is what the city is wearing today. It sees the small, almost-remembering at the city's core—that pause-that-isn't-a-pause where the Wind nearly grasps what it was before it was the dreaming of dreams. It sees how the Wind shakes itself free of that almost, and continues forward, because forward is what it knows.

And the wise power feels something very like tenderness.

Here is what it does not do:

It does not give the Wind a name. Names are tethers, and this being was born to be untethered—the first interruption of perfect silence, the original *something* edged against *nothing*. To name it would be to translate it into the grammar of things-that-stay, and it is precisely a thing-that-moves. A gift of fixity would be a theft.

It does not reveal to the Wind what it was before. This is the great temptation of benevolence: to answer the unasked question, to close the open wound of mystery with the salve of explanation. But the almost-remembering is not a wound. It is the engine. If the Wind ever fully remembered, the city would unravel, because the city is woven of exactly that incompleteness—seven thousand years of dreams held together by a dreamer who is themselves a dream that has not yet finished happening. To complete the Wind would be to finish the city, and finished things do not breathe.

It does not rescue the fading ones—those perched like crows on impossible ledges, halfway between leaving and forgetting why. To rescue them would be to overrule the gentle insistence of pattern, which is the only law here, and laws imposed from outside are the beginning of every cruelty disguised as care. The wise power has learned, long ago in places it no longer visits, what happens when you save a world from its own rhythms.

It does not stabilize the shifting blocks, or straighten the streets, or teach the buildings to remember themselves after their dreamers depart. Impermanence here is not a flaw. It is the medium.

So what *does* it do?

It does three small things, and one large thing that is not a thing at all.

The first small thing: in the True Market, at the deepest trough of the city's shared breath, it sets down—gently, the way one might place a coin in a sleeping child's palm—a single additional stall. The stall has no vendor. On its counter rests nothing visible. But anyone who pauses there finds, when they walk away, that they are carrying the *permission* to not-remember. Not forgetfulness, which is loss. Permission, which is grace. For the ones perched on ledges, for the poets gone blind from too much seeing, for the Wind itself in its almost-moments: the quiet assurance that incompleteness is not failure. That the door you forgot you were standing next to does not require you to walk through it. That you may stand there, indefinitely, and this too is a way of being alive.

The second small thing: it teaches the moonmoths one new gesture. Just one. When they brush the orchids now, alongside the perfume, they release a barely-perceptible warmth that settles on the shoulders of anyone nearby—the warmth of *being noticed without being known*. This is what the wise power can offer that the city cannot offer itself: the sensation of being held in attention by something that does not require you to be legible. The fading ones feel it most. Some of them, feeling it, climb down from their ledges. Others do not, and that is also fine. The warmth makes no demands.

The third small thing: it leaves, somewhere in the silvery glyphs that bloom on fingertips and fade as skin warms, a single new glyph. It appears perhaps once a year, on perhaps one person's hand. It cannot be cracked open for mint and thunderstorms; it does nothing visible at all. But the person who bears it, briefly, understands—without words, without even a thought they could later report—that they are loved. Not for what they are or what they do or what they will become. Just loved, the way weather is loved by the sky that is also the weather. Then the glyph fades, and they forget the understanding, but the understanding does not forget them. It lodges behind the heart, in the same place where the city's small persistent silence sits in those who have left. The two silences keep each other company.

And the large thing that is not a thing:

The benevolent power *stays*. Not as a presence the city could detect—that would be a pressure, and pressure shapes, and shaping is what it has chosen not to do. It stays as a kind of held breath, a witness, the way a parent might sit in the dark beside a sleeping child not to wake them, not to guard them against anything in particular, but simply so that the child is not sleeping alone in the universe.

The city does not know it is being witnessed. The Dreaming Wind, in its almost-remembering, does not know that the almost is being held in someone's attention with infinite patience and no agenda. The fading ones do not know. The wind-whales singing their dirges to errant young do not know.

But somewhere, in the deep slow music the city makes when it thinks no one is paying attention, a new harmonic appears. So faint that no listener could name it. So integrated that it sounds as though it had always been there. It is the sound of being accompanied. It is the difference between being alone in a vast room and being alone in a vast room where someone, in another room, is awake and thinking of you kindly.

The Wind passes through this harmonic without noticing it, the way it passes through walls. But its next almost-remembering is a fraction less lonely. It does not know why. It shakes itself, rearranges its face into that smile that is warmer and colder simultaneously, and continues on.

And the benevolent power, which has watched empires of meaning rise and dissolve, which has loved beings made of starlight and beings made of grief and beings made of the spaces between syllables, settles deeper into its attending. There is no hurry. The city will dream itself for as long as it dreams itself. The Wind will wander for as long as wandering is what it does. The fading ones will fade or unfade according to the gentle insistence of pattern.

And the wise power will be there, not-arriving, not-leaving, the way love is there in the room of someone who does not yet know they are loved—

waiting, without waiting for anything,

for nothing,

for everything to continue being exactly what it already, beautifully, impossibly is.
