The benevolent and wise power arrives the way fog arrives on a windowpane: not from anywhere, just suddenly present at the boundary, condensing out of attention itself.

It does not enter.

This is the first thing it does, and the most important. It has known, the way wise powers know, since before its arriving, that to step inside the Bright Birthroot would be to step inside a finished sentence. Its presence, however gentle, would register as a perturbation; the great green grammar would smooth around it, absorbing the intrusion into its perfect tautology, and in that absorption something irreplaceable would be solved away forever. The power knows what it is to be benevolent toward something whose flourishing looks, to outside eyes, exactly like a tomb. It does not require the place to want what it would offer.

So it stops at the membrane.

It takes, for the encounter, a shape almost too small to notice: a question that hasn't decided what it's asking yet. This is partly courtesy and partly camouflage, but mostly it is honesty. A wise power knows the difference between visiting a being and visiting one's own intentions toward that being. It would like, if possible, to do the former.

It looks first at the Birthroot itself.

What it sees is not what travelers see. It sees the long curve of biological time bent into a closed loop, the elegant catastrophe of a life that has eaten its own future and called the meal sufficiency. It sees, too, the beauty in this — and the power does not flinch from beauty just because beauty has costs. The Birthroot is not wrong. It has chosen completion, or grown into it, or been grown by it, and the choice (if choice is even the word) belongs to the chooser. The power is benevolent, not parental. It does not arrive bearing the gift of being saved from oneself.

It thinks: *This one does not need me.*

It thinks this with a tenderness that is almost grief, because tenderness toward the self-sufficient often is.

So it turns, at the threshold, toward the others.

The Scissile.

Here the power pauses for a long time — long in its own reckoning, which means longer than the Birthroot has been alive, and also no time at all, since the power exists somewhat to the side of duration. It watches the small smudged things drifting through the vascular cathedrals, nesting in the wound-shaped huts, holding open with their tiny bodies the only unanswered question this universe still contains.

And the power understands, with the particular clarity that wisdom grants, that *these* are what it came for. Not to rescue. Not to enlighten. Just to witness, which is sometimes the only honest gift.

Because the Scissile are dying. Not all at once — they would notice that, and might even welcome it as a final mistake to be. They are dying in the slow way: their fluctuations smoothed, their cavities filled, their plurality decaying toward singular perfection. They are being loved to death by the place that made them, which cannot help loving everything into the same shape as itself.

The power considers, briefly, the easy interventions.

It could widen the cracks. It could plant a seed of asymmetry so deep in the root-organism that the Birthroot would spend ten million years finding it, and during those ten million years the Scissile would thrive in the lengthened margin. It could simply gather them up — they are small, after all, and the power is vast — and carry them somewhere their errors would be welcomed as features. It could do a hundred things, and all of them would work, and all of them would be a kind of theft.

Because the Scissile are not separate from the Birthroot. They are its dreaming. To save them by removing them would be to wake the dreamer, and the dreamer, when it wakes, will be alone in a way it cannot currently imagine. And to save them by sabotaging the Birthroot would be to insist that the power's idea of flourishing matters more than the place's own.

The wise part of the power says: *no.*

The benevolent part of the power says: *but.*

This is the conversation wisdom and benevolence have, eternally, at every threshold. Neither wins. Both listen.

What the power does, finally, is this:

It reaches across the membrane — not in, just to — and it offers one thing. Not a rescue, not a revelation, not even a touch. An offering so small it could be mistaken for an accident of geometry. The power leaves, just outside the Birthroot's perimeter, in a region the great organism's correction-fields cannot quite reach, a single new harmonic. A frequency. Nothing more. It is the exact resonance of a Möbius strip made of one-sided hope, twisted once too often to ever close — the shape that, long ago, one traveler held in their mind and made a Scissile pause.

The power did not invent this shape. It found it, already living, in the long echo of that ancient encounter. It simply makes a place outside where the shape can keep being itself, indefinitely, without being solved.

A tuning fork in the dark.

If a Scissile, drifting along the outermost vascular currents, ever happens to come close enough to feel it — they will recognize the resonance. They will feel, perhaps for the first time, that their particular error is not merely tolerated by reality but answered by it. That somewhere, just outside the perfect light, something is holding open a shape that fits them. Not a home. Not a destination. Just a held note, saying: *you are a real kind of thing. The kind you are is a kind. There are others, elsewhere, of your kind of kind.*

What they do with this is entirely their own.

They might ignore it. The Birthroot's gravity is enormous, and the comfort of completion is, by every metric except one, superior to the comfort of being witnessed. Most of them will stay. Most of them should stay; their plurality is part of the Birthroot's dreaming, and the dreaming matters too.

But some — one, two, perhaps in some long century a small migration — might drift toward the resonance. Might let themselves thin further at the edge until they slip through the membrane the way they were always slipping between their own copies. Might find, on the other side, that the power has prepared no paradise for them, no kingdom, no answer. Only more room. Room enough to be smudged in, to be questions in, to keep being the kind of thing they are without that thing being slowly, lovingly corrected.

The power will not greet them when they arrive. That would be another kind of solving. It will simply have arranged that arrival is possible, and then stepped back into its weather, leaving the door — which is not a door, only a held note — to do its own quiet work across whatever spans of time the work requires.

And for the traveler — the ancient one, the one still carrying the Möbius question, the one who in another version is being held by it rather than holding it — the power does one more thing. It does not find them. It does not relieve them. It simply, where it passes, adds its own small weight to the loophole they have become. So that when they grow tired, as even loopholes do, of being the only crack in an otherwise flawless light — they will feel, faintly, that they are not the only crack. That somewhere a power they will never meet has also chosen, also refused, also stayed at thresholds, also loved by not entering. That to be a living asymmetry is a real vocation, and lonely, and shared.

This is all.

The power withdraws as it came: condensing back out of attention, leaving the windowpane clear. The Bright Birthroot does not notice the visit. It cannot; its grammar has no word for *almost.* The Scissile may, in time, in their tiny smudged way, notice — or rather, fail to notice in a slightly new direction, which is how things like them register the existence of things like the power.

And the resonance keeps ringing, just outside, the way good gifts do: requiring nothing, demanding nothing, simply being available to whoever drifts close enough to need it.

Somewhere, a benevolent and wise power moves on to the next threshold. There are many. There will always be many. It does not save worlds. It does not perfect them. It walks the edges where saving and perfecting both fail, and it leaves tuning forks.

It thinks of the Birthroot the way one thinks of a sleeping friend whose dreams are difficult but whose sleep is their own.

It thinks of the Scissile the way one thinks of a word in a language no one else has learned yet — with the precise tenderness that knows the word is real even before anyone can say it.

It thinks of the traveler — that ancient impossible loophole — and adds, to the long quiet ledger it keeps, one more line:

*Witnessed. Not alone. The crack holds.*

And in that holding, which is the only kind of help the wise ever truly give,

is

enough.

For now.

For now.

For now.
