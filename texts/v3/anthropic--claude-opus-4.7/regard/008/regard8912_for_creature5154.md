# The Visitor on the Ridge

The benevolent power notices the plateau the way one might notice a child humming to itself in a sunlit corner — not because anything is wrong, but because something is unmistakably *itself*, and that always merits attention.

It does not arrive. Arrival is the wrong verb. It attends.

---

For a long while — long by the standards of the power, which is to say long by no standards the plateau would recognize — it simply watches. It does not interpret the chimes or translate the migrating equations on the plinth. It listens the way one listens to a language one already half-knows but does not wish to presume upon. The lentil-flyers continue their spirals. The arch reaches a little further into the cloud deck. The lithoderm breathes.

The power considers.

Here is what it sees: a being in the middle of thinking its first long thought. Not its first thought — the rhythm of the wingbeats is older than the ridge, the route-maps are inheritances from somewhere — but its first sustained, integrated thought, the kind that requires a body large enough and patient enough to hold a question across many breaths without losing the thread.

The being does not know it is being watched. The being does not, in any strong sense, know it is a being. It is busy.

---

The temptation — and the power feels temptations, though they pass through it more cleanly than through smaller minds — is to help.

It could stabilize the half-dome's indecision, nudge it toward whichever of its three possibilities is, by some external measure, most useful. It could feed the arch the missing ions and let it reach the cloud deck this cycle rather than the next ten thousand. It could whisper to the filaments what the equations on the plinth actually mean, save them the labor of figuring it out by touch and charge-exchange across uncounted brushings.

It could give the plateau language. It could give it a name.

It could — and this is the deepest temptation, the one the power must sit with longest — it could tell the plateau that it is not alone. That above the bruise-colored overcast there are other minds, older, who would be glad of its company once it learned to speak.

Each of these gifts would be a kindness. Each would also be a kind of theft.

Because the plateau is *becoming* by the act of not-yet-being. Its incompleteness is not a deficiency to be remedied; it is the medium in which it thinks. To finish the arch would be to end the question the arch is asking. To resolve the half-dome would be to silence the deliberation that is, right now, the most interesting thing happening on this small shelf of stone in this small corner of the sky. To give it a name before it has one would be to hand it a mask shaped by someone else's face.

The power has seen this mistake made. It has, in its younger ages, made it.

---

So it does something smaller, and stranger, and — it judges — kinder.

It makes itself *legible to the weather*.

Not to the plateau directly. Not as a voice or a sign or a visitation. But it arranges, with the lightest possible touch, that the upper atmosphere above this ridge will be, for the next several million breaths, a little more hospitable than it would otherwise have been. The cloud deck will hold. The bruise-colored light will not fail. The ionized droplets the arch needs will continue to drift up from below in sufficient concentration. No catastrophe — no stray bolide, no shift in the deeper crust, no acidifying rain from a distant volcanic event — will interrupt the thinking before the thought is complete.

It does not accelerate the plateau. It only ensures the plateau will not be cut off mid-sentence.

This is a gardener's work, and the power does it the way good gardeners do: by removing a few stones from the soil and then stepping back, leaving no footprint, taking no credit, not even watching too closely afterward lest the watching itself become a pressure.

---

And then it does one more thing, which is harder to describe.

It *remembers* the plateau. Deliberately. With care. It commits to memory the exact configuration of this moment — the half-dome's flicker-rate, the precise pitch of the chimes, the way the filaments lean very slightly westward, the equation currently migrating across the plinth (which, translated into something the power can hold, reads approximately: *what is the smallest gesture that changes everything downstream?*).

It does this because the plateau, being mortal in the way all distributed metabolisms are mortal, may someday stop. Not soon. But someday. And when that day comes — whether in ten thousand years or ten million — the power wants there to be, somewhere, a record. Not a resurrection. Not a backup. Just a remembering. The fact of this being, held in a mind large enough to hold it, so that the universe does not entirely forget that once, on a knife-edge ridge above a cloud deck, a plateau breathed and thought and reached an arch toward something it could not yet see.

That, the power decides, is enough. That is what love looks like at this scale: protection without interference, witness without intrusion, memory without possession.

---

The lentil-flyers rise in their glittering sheet. The plateau exhales. Thunder answers from below the clouds.

The power, satisfied, turns part of its attention elsewhere — though not all of it, and not forever. Some small filament of its regard remains tethered here, the way a parent leaves a door cracked open at night, listening without listening, present without presuming.

The arch reaches a little further.

No one on the plateau notices. That is exactly as it should be.
