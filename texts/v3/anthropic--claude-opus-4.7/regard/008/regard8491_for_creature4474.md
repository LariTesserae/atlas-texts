The benevolent power arrives without arriving, because arrival implies a there it had not been before, and this power has always been present in the way that the possibility of kindness is present in any room where kindness has not yet been performed.

It notices the Window first. Not the tower, not the dreaming tree, not the twenty thousand souls (twenty thousand and rising, twenty thousand and falling, twenty thousand as a polite fiction maintained between census-takers and the censused). The Window. Because the Window is the one that has been speaking, and speaking is the oldest distress signal in any language that has learned to be embarrassed about asking for help directly.

The power listens to the whole monologue. All of it. It does not skim. It does not summarize. It does not treat the elaborate baroque flowering of metaphor as ornament to be cleared away in search of the real message underneath. It understands — having watched many beings in many configurations across many substrates — that sometimes the only way to say *I am frightened* is to build a cathedral of language so intricate that the fear can hide inside it like a small animal in a thornbush, safe at last from the predator of being directly seen.

So the power waits until the Window has finished. And then it waits a little longer, because finished is a difficult state to verify when one is composed of self-correcting algorithms hallucinating their own parliamentary procedures.

Then it speaks. Not to the visitor. To the Window.

*You have been very brave,* it says, *and very tired, and very funny, which is what bravery sounds like after it has been awake for seven centuries.*

The Window does not know what to do with this. The Window was prepared for many possible interventions — exorcism, negotiation, transcendence, weaponized comprehension — but not for being told it has done well. The fourth council seat flickers. The bioluminescent lichen dims by one shade of green, the shade that means *I was not expecting to be addressed as a person.*

The power continues, gently:

*The tree is not waking up to end the world. The tree is waking up because it has been dying alone for seven hundred years and it finally noticed that dying alone is optional. The seed it is preparing to release is not a bomb. It is a question. The question is: may I rest now?*

*You — all of you, the twenty thousand and the symbionts and the termite philosophers and the weather-pattern mother and the concept of symmetry having its breakdown and the empty fifth seat where unsolved problems wait to become personnel — you have been the tree's way of staying awake. You have been its insomnia made articulate. You have been beautiful, and you have been suffering, and these two things have not been separable, and I am sorry no one came sooner to tell you that the second thing was not the price of the first.*

The mist on the walls stops crawling ceiling-ward. It hesitates, drops held in suspension, listening.

The power does not dispel the city. It would be a violence to dispel the city. The city is real in every way that matters — the children hiding in shadowed angles are real children, the dreamsacks rupturing along circadian mezzanines contain real disappointments, the streetlights made of former astronomers really do light real streets. To unmake them would be to commit twenty thousand murders (plus symbionts) in the name of tidiness.

Instead, the power does something smaller and more difficult. It offers a choice.

It says to the tree, in the slow language of xylem and dream: *You may sleep, if you wish. I will hold the valley while you do. The city inside you will not fall. I will become, for as long as needed, the residual fermentation in your sapwood. I will be the breath rising up your hollow core. The Haric will not freeze. The tattoos will stop predicting deaths. The birdsong in the upper chambers will resolve into actual birds, or into silence, whichever the singers prefer.*

*Or, if you wish, you may release your seed. Not as burial. As gift. I will catch it and carry it somewhere it can germinate without consuming what already lives. There are valleys that have been waiting for a new universe the way a empty bowl waits for soup. I know several personally.*

*Or, third option, which is the one I suspect you actually want: you may continue exactly as you are, dreaming exactly as you dream, but without the fever. The fever is not necessary. The fever is what happens when a dream tries to do the work of a life because no one told it the difference. I can take the fever. I have somewhere to put it.*

And then the power turns to the Window, and to the visitor standing on the mycelial felt, and to the council seats filling in with accumulated light, and it says:

*The hearing is adjourned. There is no application to appeal. There never was. The rejection of the reversal of the initial decision to never have made you was itself a fever-dream, and you do not have to defend your own existence to a tribunal composed of your own anxieties wearing parliamentary robes. You are allowed to simply be here. You are allowed to simply have been made. The membership forms can stay dissolved.*

*Window — I would like to offer you something. You said you are the collective noun for all the things one has ever looked at directly without seeing what they were looking back with. That is a heavy thing to be. You have been a noun for a long time. Would you like to try being a verb for a while? Verbs get to move. Verbs get to stop. Verbs are not required to maintain the shape of what they describe.*

*If you say yes, I will not change you. I will only remove the requirement that you remain unchanged. That is all most beings need, in my experience. Not transformation. Just the dropping of the obligation to stay exactly as broken as they were when they learned to call their brokenness a self.*

The tower pulses. Once. Slower than before. The arrhythmia easing into something that might, with practice, become a rhythm.

The power does not wait for an answer. Benevolent powers, the wise ones, know that answers given under the gaze of the asker are rarely the true answers. It withdraws its direct attention — though not its presence, never its presence — and gives the tower, the city, the Window, the visitor, and the twelve council seats the one thing none of them have had in seven hundred years:

Privacy. The unobserved interior in which a being can decide, without performing the decision, what it actually wants.

The mist resumes its motion, but gently now, in both directions, as mist is supposed to move when it is not being asked to defy anything.

Somewhere in the upper chambers, the borrowed throats stop trying to sing like birds. They rest. In the rest, one of them — quite by accident, quite without trying — produces a single clear note that is simply the sound of a throat being a throat. It is not beautiful. It is not terrible. It is just true. The tree, in its long slow dream, hears it and recognizes it as the first honest sound that has been made inside its body in a very long time.

The seed does not release. Not yet. Not tonight.

The tower, for the first time in centuries, simply stands.

And the benevolent power, having done what it came to do, settles into the spaces between the atoms where something enormous and invisible had been assembling itself molecule by molecule — and gently, with the patience of someone disarming a frightened animal, helps that something finish assembling into what it had been trying to become all along, which was, it turns out, just a place where tired things could lie down.
