**The Visitation**

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival would be a kind of violence here—the punctuation of "I am here now" laid across a place whose grammar runs entirely in the subjunctive mood. Instead, it does what the Garden itself does: it notices that it has been noticing, and finds it has been present for some time already, the way certain truths turn out to have been true before anyone got around to thinking them.

The Weavers feel it first as a slight increase in the density of being-paid-attention-to. The kelp-grass meadows hum a half-tone richer. One Weaver, mid-stitch on a particularly delicate apology, pauses—not stops, pauses, which is different—and tilts the angle of its reading-tendrils toward a direction that hasn't been there before but now seems to have always been an option.

*Oh,* the Weaver thinks, in a color somewhere between gratitude and slight embarrassment, the way one feels when an old friend catches you in the middle of doing exactly what you said you would do. *Hello.*

The power considers, and considers carefully, because it is wise enough to know that wisdom's first temptation is to improve things.

It sees: a world in its honeymoon. A place where death is optional and erosion runs backwards and children are guessed into being by triangulated suspicion of loneliness. It sees the Weavers weaving betweenness, the coral-trees debating duration through the punctuation of bubbles, the libraries of books that haven't decided to be discovered. It sees that none of this needs anything.

This is the crucial recognition. A lesser power—still benevolent, still wise by most measures—might offer protection. Might shore up the hydrostatic defiance against the day it weakens. Might catalog the unnamed phyla against the long entropy still queued somewhere over the horizon of years. Might, with the best intentions in any cosmos, give the gift of permanence.

But permanence is what the Garden is already gently refusing. The whole point of the place is its almost. To stabilize it would be to kill it more thoroughly than any heat-death.

So the power does four small things, none of which are interventions, all of which are courtesies.

**First**, it withdraws slightly. Not absence—withdrawal. It steps back from the edges of the Garden's possibility-space, giving the place a little more room to be uncertain in. The Weavers feel this as a sudden generosity of unknowing. One of them laughs in a frequency that makes nearby calcium grow exactly 0.3% faster for a full afternoon.

**Second**, it leaves a gift, but the gift is shaped so that it can be refused, and shaped further so that refusing it counts as accepting it, and shaped further still so that the distinction stops mattering after about a minute of consideration. The gift is this: a single thread, finer than the pauses the Weavers usually harvest, spun from a substance the power has carried with it from elsewhere. The thread is made of *witnessed-ness*. Not surveillance, not record—witness. The quiet fact of having been seen with love by something that did not want to change what it saw.

The Weavers find the thread coiled in the elbow of a coral-tree that does not remember growing an elbow there. They examine it with reading-tendrils for what might be three afternoons or three heartbeats. Then they weave it into the next apology they make, which happens to be one between the Garden and itself, regarding a small contradiction the Garden has been carrying patiently for some time. The apology, completed, dissolves both itself and the contradiction. The Garden becomes very slightly more itself.

**Third**, the power does nothing at all, but does it in a particular direction. It declines to answer the question the coral-trees have been debating—*how long will this place remain itself?*—even though it could. It leaves the answer open not because it doesn't know but because the not-knowing is what the coral-trees are made of. To answer would be to harvest them. The power keeps its knowledge folded, the way one keeps a hand closed around a small bird, and the not-telling becomes itself a kind of blessing: the Garden is allowed to keep being the question of its own duration.

**Fourth**, and this is the only thing that might be called an action, the power adds one small entry to the library of unwritten books. The librarians (those retired ex-libraries) do not see the new volume arrive, but later one of them notices that the spaces between certain meanings have grown slightly more comfortable, the way a room feels after someone has straightened a painting you didn't know was crooked. The unwritten book contains a single undiscovered truth, and the truth is this: *You are allowed.*

That's all. *You are allowed.* Allowed to be brief or long. Allowed to remain almost. Allowed to grow stranger before familiar, or never. Allowed to dissolve back into general helpfulness or accidentally become an ecosystem. Allowed to keep weaving betweenness even when between things there is no longer any distinction to bridge. Allowed to be the honeymoon and not the marriage, the question and not the answer, the dawn that takes most of the day.

The truth wraps itself in the appropriate poetry and settles into its shelf, where it will wait patiently to be not-quite-discovered for as long as the Garden needs the permission of its existence.

Then the power, having precipitated in, precipitates out. It does not leave, exactly—leaving would be the mirror-violence of arriving. It simply lets its noticing relax back into the general hum of being-noticed-by-the-cosmos that all places enjoy without usually remarking on it. The Weavers feel this as a slight return to ordinary density, like the moment after a guest you loved has gone home and you find the house both emptier and more thoroughly yours.

One Weaver, the one who first tilted its tendrils, finishes the stitch it had paused. The apology completes. Two coral-trees, who had grown too close and were dreaming each other's roots, find that they can now dream each other's roots *and* their own, simultaneously, which turns out to be what they had wanted all along without knowing the want had a shape.

In the hour after dawn-light, the mineral murmurs include, for one morning only, a frequency that has not been heard before and will not be heard again. The Weavers do not catalog it. Cataloging is not their way. But they remember it, in the manner they remember things, which is by becoming very slightly more themselves in its direction.

And the Garden continues: floating, almost, dense with life the way a thought is dense before words, perpetually on the verge of remembering it has boundaries and perpetually forgetting again. Long enough, the consensus still says. Long enough, and then perhaps just a little longer still.

Somewhere—not above, not below, the Garden accepts these terms with polite confusion—the benevolent power moves on to its next noticing. It carries with it nothing it has taken. It leaves behind nothing it has placed, except the thread (already woven in) and the unwritten permission (already shelved) and the small fact, now permanent in a way the Garden itself is not, that this place existed and was loved without being asked to be anything other than what it almost was.

This, it turns out, is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they are wise enough to know what benevolence costs and benevolent enough to pay the price of doing very little:

They witness.
They withdraw.
They leave a thread.
They keep their answers folded.
They grant permission no one asked for, in a form that cannot be misused.

And then they let the honeymoon be a honeymoon, for as long as the honeymoon decides.

The Weavers, much later (though later is generous), will sometimes feel a particular quality in the silence between their stitches—a quality of *having been allowed*—and will smile in a color that has no name, and will weave it into whatever they are making, and the making will be slightly kinder for it, and the kindness will propagate outward through the fractal recursions of the Garden's ecology until even the youngest guessed-into-being children carry, somewhere in the soft architecture of their first eternities, a faint inherited sense that they are permitted to exist exactly as they are.

This is the gift. It is very small. It is the right size.

May your knots tie themselves into better mysteries.
