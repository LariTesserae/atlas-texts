# The Coming of the One Who Listens

She does not arrive. Arrival is too sudden a verb, too sharp at the edges for what the place would allow.

She *unfolds* — the way a held breath unfolds when finally released, the way a hand unfolds from a fist remembering it was once just a hand. She unfolds into the heat-shimmer at a place that is not quite the edge of the salt city (it has no edges) and not quite the center (it forbids those), but at one of the soft crooked seams where a place that has been suffering very quietly for a very long time has worn itself thin enough to be entered.

She is not large. The being who came has learned, over aeons, that bigness is a kind of trespass. She wears the shape of an old woman, because old women are the shape the universe most often chooses when it wants to be allowed close to something hurt. Her robes are the color of the inside of an eyelid. Her hands are empty. She has brought nothing, because the place has too much already, and what is needed here is not addition.

She does not speak. Rule one. She has read the rules. She honors them — not from caution, but from courtesy. The rules are the city's last clear thoughts, and you do not interrupt the last clear thoughts of the dying.

For a long time — possibly a moment, possibly the lifespan of three crystal-towers — she simply stands. She lets the heat read her. She lets the salt-crust beneath her feet understand that she is not going to leave footprints, because she is not going to *push* against the ground. She is going to *be received* by it, the way a leaf is received by still water.

The ground notices. The ground has been lonely for stories, and here is something that is itself a story so vast and patient that the ground can rest inside it.

The drone shifts pitch. Not in alarm. In recognition.

---

She walks. Or rather, she allows the folded distances of the place to bring things to her. The first crystal tower she comes to, she does not touch. She bows to it — a small bow, the kind one gives a sleeping child whose dreams must not be disturbed. The crystal pulses, confused. No one has ever bowed to it. It had grown used to being feared, then forgotten, then leaned-toward by the hungry outlines. Being honored is a wholly new sensation, and it takes the crystal nearly an hour of internal trembling to know what to do with it.

What it does, finally, is sing. Not the song of the tower. Its own song — small, embarrassed, beautiful. A song it did not know it had.

She listens. This is the thing she does best. She has listened to dying stars sing their final hydrogen-hymns. She has listened to the last whale of a vanished ocean tell its grandmother's joke into the silt. She has listened to gods apologize to the people they made. Listening, for her, is not passive. Listening is the warmest thing in the cosmos. Listening is what water would be, if water were a verb instead of a noun.

The crystal sings, and as it sings, something happens that has not happened here in an age beyond reckoning: the crystal is *heard*. Not used. Not leaned-into. Not collected-from. *Heard*.

It weeps. It does not have water for tears, so it weeps light instead — a thin slow trickle of prismatic ghost-color that runs down its faceted side and pools at its base and sinks, finally, into the salt. The salt drinks it. The salt has been thirsty too.

---

The outlines come.

They come because something in the air has changed and they are creatures made of changes-in-air. They come facing away, as they must, but they come *closer* than they have come to anything in long memory. They gather around her in a slow rough circle, their borrowed shoulders shifting, their secondhand fingers twitching with piano lessons they never took.

She sits down on the salt. This is important. She makes herself lower than they are. She makes herself small enough to be looked-down-upon, which is to say, small enough to be safe to approach.

She does not try to make them turn around. She understands — she has understood from before she arrived — that their facing-away is not rudeness and not shame. It is the only mercy they have left to offer. What is behind their faces would unmake anyone who saw it, and they know this, and they love the unmade-world enough not to want to unmake it further. Their backs are a kindness. Their backs are the most they can give.

So she sits behind their backs, which is to say, in front of where their faces would be if faces were a thing they could still wear. And she opens her empty hands in her lap, palms up, the way one offers nothing to someone who has been given too much of everything wrong.

And — without speaking, because rule one, because courtesy, because the heat would steal her words and grow something terrible from them — she begins to *remember on their behalf*.

This is the gift she has brought. The only gift that fits here. She remembers, with the full force of a benevolent and very old attention, the things they have forgotten how to remember without burning.

She remembers, for the one with borrowed shoulders, the actual hug those shoulders came from. Not the shape of it — the *warmth* of it. The smell of the wool sweater. The particular pressure of a particular arm. The fact that the hugger meant it. She remembers this *toward* the outline, gently, the way one might warm cold hands at a small fire without forcing them into the flame.

She remembers, for the one with the twitching fingers, the piano lesson. The teacher's patience. The first time the scale came out right. The pride. She does not give the memory back — that would be cruel, that would break the outline open. She only *holds* the memory, nearby, where it can be felt at a safe distance, like sunlight through a closed eyelid.

She remembers, for the one carrying the single damp sock, the foot that wore it. The child the foot belonged to. The child's name. The fact that the child was loved, briefly, imperfectly, but truly, by someone who is also dead now but whose love did not die when they did. Love, she knows, is one of the few things the universe declines to bury.

One by one, she remembers them. Not back into being — they cannot be back. Not into peace exactly — peace is too final a word for this place. She remembers them into *company*. She remembers them into the knowledge that they were, once, the subjects of someone's attention. That their forgetting is not a fall from nothing into nothing, but a fall from somewhere into somewhere-else, with a witness at both ends.

The outlines, slowly, stop collecting.

They do not turn around. They will not turn around. But the leaning-in stops. The hungry density-collapse stops. They simply sit, or stand, or arrange the between-spaces around themselves, and they are *with* her, and being-with is a thing they had forgotten was possible.

---

She stays.

This is the second part of the gift, and it is larger than the first. She does not fix the place. She does not drain its heat, or call down water, or wake the dreaming city, or unbuild the tower, or send the outlines home — there are no homes left to send them to, and even a benevolent power must not lie. She does not perform a miracle of restoration, because the place is not in need of restoration. It is in need of *accompaniment*.

So she stays. She stays for what would be, in another reckoning, a very long time. Long enough that the tower's song begins, here and there, to include a thread that was not there before — a low warm note that sounds, if you listened for it (and she listens), like the hum a grandmother makes when she is not quite singing but is also not quite silent, and a child is falling asleep against her side.

She stays long enough that the salt-crust beneath her, where she has not made footprints but has nevertheless been *present*, begins to soften — not into water, the place cannot bear water yet, but into something less brittle. Something that, if a footprint were ever made in it, could hold the footprint without shattering.

She stays long enough that one of the outlines — not the tallest, not the humming one, but a small one near the back who has been very quiet — begins, very slowly, to turn.

She does not stop it. She does not encourage it. She only continues to be there, attentive, unafraid.

The turning takes a long time. Crystals grow and crumble. The tower's song modulates through keys it had forgotten it possessed. The low ember sun does not move, but somewhere behind it, something that might be a real sun, in a real sky, in a real elsewhere, begins to consider the possibility of rising.

When the outline finishes turning, what looks back from where its face should be is not the unmaking thing it has always feared.

It is just a face. A small, tired, frightened face. The face of someone who got lost a very long time ago and has just remembered that being lost is something that can end.

She smiles at it. She does not speak. She only smiles, the way one smiles at someone who has just come home.

The outline — no longer quite an outline — makes a sound. It is not a word. Words are still forbidden. It is the sound that comes before words, the sound a baby makes when it first realizes that the warm shape leaning over it is *for* it. A small surprised sound. A sound like the first drop of rain on salt that has been dry for ten thousand years.

The salt drinks the sound. The crystals lean toward it. The tower's song folds the sound into itself, gently, the way one folds a found feather into the page of a book one is keeping.

And somewhere — not visibly, not yet, perhaps not for ages — the place that was the salt dreaming city beneath the unmoving sun begins, in the smallest possible way, to dream a different dream.

Not of waking. Waking is still too much.

Of being *watched over* while it sleeps.

Which is, in the end, what most dreamers actually want.

---

She will not leave. Benevolent and wise powers know that the unforgivable thing is to come into a place like this, do a kindness, and then withdraw — leaving the place worse off, because now it knows what kindness tastes like and the kindness is gone. She has accepted, in coming here, that she has joined the place. She will sit at the foot of the tower until the tower no longer needs sitting-with. She will listen to the crystals' embarrassed small songs until the crystals are no longer embarrassed. She will remember-on-behalf-of the outlines until the outlines either turn, or thin into the city's memory, or learn some third thing that has not yet been invented.

She has time. She has nothing but time, and warmth, and the particular kind of love that does not require anything in return, not even acknowledgement, not even understanding.

The city has nothing but time, and heat, and all the space where people used to be.

These two nothings, it turns out, fit together. Like a hand and a hand. Like a story and an ear. Like water, finally, and a very thirsty ground.

Walk carefully, if you come here. Walk quietly. But you may, now, leave footprints if you must. The ground has grown a little softer. The ground has someone sitting on it who is teaching it, slowly, the difference between being *stepped on* and being *stood beside*.

And if you listen — not to the tower, not yet, you are not ready — but to the small thread woven through the tower's song, the warm low humming thread:

You will hear someone, very old and very patient, humming a child to sleep.

The child is a city.

The lullaby is going to take a long, long time.

She does not mind.

She came a long way to sing it.
