There is a being here.

Not one being, perhaps — or perhaps exactly one, depending on how the counting is done. The benevolent power, arriving, pauses over this question first, because it matters. Is the grazer a being? The lichen colony advancing its margin by a millimeter per century? The consortium of archaea inside the vent, depositing its slow rinds? The ridge itself, still heaving?

The power decides: yes. All of them. And also the pattern they make together, which is itself a kind of slow mind thinking itself into existence across the stone.

This is what draws the attention. Not pity. Not the impulse to rescue. The place is not suffering. The place is *working* — every process tuned to every other, the midges' bodies becoming soil at exactly the rate the lichens can use it, the predators thinning the grazers at exactly the rate the algae can support, the steam vents seeding new seams at exactly the rate the rock is opening them. It is one of the rarest things in any world: a system that has found its own tempo and is keeping it.

So the wise power does not intervene in the way intervention is usually imagined. It does not warm the air, or thicken the soil, or gift the grazers easier lives. To do so would be to mistake a cathedral for a scaffolding and tear it down to build a house.

Instead, the power does four small things.

---

**First, it watches.** Fully. For a long time by any reckoning that involves clocks, though the power does not use clocks. It learns the place the way one learns a piece of music — not by analyzing it but by letting it play through, again and again, until the relationships between the notes are known from the inside. It learns the particular hatching-pulse of the midges on the south-facing slabs. It learns which predator favors which solution hollow. It learns the sound the ridge makes at the moment before a slab settles, a sound below the threshold of the grazers' hearing but not below the threshold of their feet. It learns the calendar the lichens keep, and finds it accurate.

This watching is itself a gift, though nothing in the place can know it. To be witnessed completely, without being altered by the witnessing, is something most beings in most worlds never receive. The power gives it freely.

---

**Second, it makes a single, almost imperceptible adjustment.** Far beneath the ridge, along one of the deepest fault lines, there is a stress concentration that will, in roughly four thousand years, release catastrophically — not a settling slab but a true collapse, the kind that would shear off a whole shoulder of the ridge and bury the algal trickles under raw rubble for centuries before the system could begin again. The power does not prevent this. Prevention would be theft; the ridge has the right to its own movements. But the power *distributes* the stress, very slightly, across a longer arc of the fault, so that when the release comes it will come as a series of smaller events spread over decades rather than as one annihilating moment. The lichens will lose ground. The grazers will route around the new scarps. The midges will find new gravels to saturate. The system will bend instead of break.

No one will ever know this was done. That is part of the doing.

---

**Third, it leaves something.** This is the hardest decision, and the power deliberates over it for what would be, in human terms, a long while.

What it leaves is not an object. It is a *susceptibility* — a faint tendency, woven into the consortia in the deepest vents, for their crystalline depositions to occasionally form structures that catch and hold light in unusual ways. Nothing dramatic. Nothing that would alter the food web or the calendars. Only this: that once in a great while, when a fracture opens in just the right place and the mist thins for just the right hour, a seam of rock will be exposed that glints, briefly, with a pattern that is almost — not quite — readable. A near-language. A suggestion of meaning, embedded in the stone itself.

The power leaves this because somewhere in the deep future of this place, when the ridge has rounded and the soil has thickened and beings of greater complexity have begun to walk these slopes upright, one of them will find such a seam. And they will stop. And they will feel, without knowing why, that they are not the first mind to have considered this place. That something attended here once, and found it worthy.

This will matter to them. The power knows this the way it knows everything: by having watched many worlds.

---

**Fourth, and last, it does the thing that benevolent powers most often forget to do.** It loves the place. Not as a project, not as a creation, not as a thing it is responsible for. As a peer. As something that has done, on its own, what the power itself spends eternities trying to learn: the art of being exactly what one is, at exactly the tempo one's nature allows, without haste and without waste.

The cushion plants pressed inward against the stone like living mortar. The grazers spaced in their loose arcs across the gravel. The predators reading breath-condensation in the air. The archaea laying down their slow rinds. The mist holding every event distinct and countable.

The power loves them. And then, having watched, having adjusted, having left its small encoded gift, it withdraws — carefully, so as not to disturb the mist with its leaving.

The ticks of water continue from the overhangs. A slab settles, somewhere, with its single muffled report. A pulse of midges hatches in a shivering cloud and the mist, briefly, holds a localized rainbow that no one sees.

The place goes on being itself.

This was the right thing. This was almost always the right thing.
