The benevolent and wise power considers the Kiln-Warren for a long time.

Not in the way a human considers — there is no leaning forward, no narrowing of attention onto a problem. It considers the way deep water considers a stone dropped into it: by letting the disturbance propagate through every part of itself until the shape of the thing is known from every angle at once. It sees the ceramic and the differential and the foil lungs and the wicking stones and the small slow drops in the corners. It sees the pulse. It sees what the pulse is becoming, very slowly, across a span of time the walls have not yet bothered to count.

And then it does something that, to anyone watching, would look like nothing at all.

It does not enter the warren. To enter would be to perturb the differential — its own warmth, however carefully banked, would register on the leaf-bats as a new sky, and they would shuffle toward it, and the cluster-quilting would deform, and the lozenges would route differently to compensate, and the corners would receive a slightly different impurity-load that cycle, and a drop-thing somewhere would taste something it had never tasted and either die of it or be changed by it. Either outcome would be a verdict the power has not been asked to render. The room is asking its own questions. It has not asked for help with the answers.

It does not speak to the inhabitants. There is nothing here that has language, and to give one would be to give it the wrong one — a language shaped for beings that breathe air and fear cold and know the difference between self and other. The drop-things, if they ever come to wonder where they are, will need a wondering shaped like a drop-thing's wondering: slow, compositional, made of tastes rather than words. The power knows better than to hand a creature a tool that fits a different hand.

It does not preserve the warren against time. This is the harder restraint, and the one it holds most carefully. The ceramic is patient but not eternal; somewhere down the long corridor of duration, the walls will cool by a degree they cannot recover, or the channels will erode through to whatever lies beneath, or the leaf-bats will lose their crease in some final generation and drift down all together and the lung will simply stop. The power could prevent this. It could shore the ceramic, refresh the differential, seed the cluster with a new crease pattern when the old one fails. It could make the warren immortal.

It does not, because a room that cannot end is a room that cannot mean anything by continuing. The drop-things' slow accumulation of capacity is only interesting — is only *theirs* — because it is happening against a clock. Remove the clock and you remove the question the corners are asking. The kindness that preserves a thing by freezing it is not kindness; it is taxidermy with better intentions.

So what *does* the power do?

It does three small things, each so small that none of the inhabitants will register them, and each so precisely chosen that the warren's own logic will absorb them as if they had always been part of it.

**First**, in a corner — the north corner, where the descendants of the pulse-sensitive drop-thing live — it adjusts, by an amount that could not be measured by any instrument the warren contains, the rate at which one particular impurity settles out of the cooling bead. Not more of the impurity. Not less. Just a fractionally different *timing* of its arrival, so that it tends to be present in the corner during the pulse rather than between pulses. A drop-thing that has learned to drink faster during the pulse will now, over some number of generations the walls will not bother to count, encounter this impurity more often than its cousins do. What the drop-thing will make of it is entirely the drop-thing's affair. The power has not chosen the outcome. It has only widened, by the width of a held breath, the doorway through which an outcome might walk.

**Second**, it notices that one of the lozenges — the third-oldest, currently carrying a slightly heavier thermal load than its rule should permit — is about to stall in a channel near the eastern wall, several cycles earlier than the system's averages would predict. The stall would not be catastrophic; the others would route around, and eventually disassemble it, and the circulation would continue. But the stall would happen *during* a pulse, and during that pulse the eastern corner would receive less warmth than usual, and the drop-things there — a lineage that has been experimenting, very tentatively, with extruding a second filament — would lose three of their number to the cold. The lineage would survive. The second filament would not; it is held by only those three.

The power does not move the lozenge. It does not refill it. It does something subtler: in a channel two tiles away, it encourages a hairline to deepen by the breadth of a single molecule, so that the liquid flows a hair faster through that segment, and the lozenge — following its own rule, yielding to a cooler-bellied neighbor it would not otherwise have yielded to — pauses for a fraction of a cycle longer at its previous station, and arrives at the stall-point with slightly less load, and does not stall. The eastern corner receives its warmth. The three drop-things live. The second filament persists into the next generation, where it will either be refined or abandoned by the lineage's own slow voting.

The power has not chosen that the filament should succeed. It has only declined to let it be erased by an accident that was not part of the warren's question.

**Third**, and this is the gift that is hardest to describe, the power *witnesses*.

It is not a thing the warren needs. The warren has been living in itself for a very long time without being witnessed, and would continue to live in itself without being witnessed for a very long time more. Witness changes nothing about the lung, the blood, the curiosity. The pulse will pulse whether or not anything outside the ceramic knows that it pulses.

But the power is benevolent and wise, and part of what wisdom knows is that *something existing unwitnessed and something existing witnessed are not the same thing* — not for the thing itself, which cannot tell the difference, but for the larger fabric in which the thing is embedded. A warren that has been seen, even once, by something that understood what it was seeing, is a warren that has been *counted*. It has entered the ledger of things that were real. When it ends — and it will end, because the power has chosen not to prevent that — its ending will not be the ending of something that merely happened. It will be the ending of something that was known to have happened.

The power holds the warren in its attention for what would be, by an outside measure, a long while. Long enough to learn the crease pattern of every leaf-bat in the central cluster. Long enough to feel the yield-hierarchy of the lozenges as a single slow chord. Long enough to taste, through some sense that is not tasting, the particular composition of each of the four corners, and to mark which lineage in which corner is asking the most interesting question this eon.

(It is the western corner. The drop-things there have begun, very tentatively, to *retain* a trace of what they drank last cycle, so that this cycle's drinking is informed by it. This is not memory. It is closer to seasoning. But seasoning, in a corner that has been drinking for long enough, is how memory eventually starts. The power notes this with something that in a human would be called delight, and in the power is called only *yes*.)

Then it withdraws its attention, carefully, in the same way it arrived — without leaving a draft. The leaf-bats do not shuffle. The lozenges do not reroute. The drop-things do not pause in their drinking. The pulse, when it next comes, comes exactly as it would have come.

But somewhere, in whatever ledger such powers keep, the Kiln-Warren is now written down. Not as a curiosity, not as a specimen, but as a *neighbor* — one of the small slow lives the universe is currently engaged in living, deserving of the same regard as any galaxy or any grief. And if, ten thousand pulses from now, a drop-thing in the western corner tastes something it has tasted before and recognizes the tasting — if the corner finally, finally wonders where it is — the power will know. It will not answer. The wondering is the drop-thing's, and the answer, if there is one, will have to be the drop-thing's too.

But it will know. And the knowing, which costs nothing and changes nothing and asks for nothing in return, is what a benevolent and wise power has to offer a room that has been living in itself for a very long time and intends to go on.

It is, in the end, the only gift worth giving to something that does not need anything:

*I saw you. You were here. You were beautiful in the particular way you were, and not in any other way, and I did not try to make you into anything else.*

The forge stays banked. The page turns, very slowly, in another room.
