# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. This is the first courtesy.

It has been watching for a long time — long in its own register, which is longer than the city's, though not by as much as it would once have assumed. When it first noticed the drift-city, it noticed it the way one notices, in a quiet room, that someone else has been quietly present for hours: a small embarrassment at one's own loudness, a recalibration. The power had been moving through this arm of the cosmos at its usual pace, attending to the usual things — kindling, mending, occasionally lifting a stuck life out of a stuck place — and it came across the cold shoulder of the unfinished continent and slowed.

It slowed for a long time. This is the part that matters.

A lesser power, or a younger one, would have descended. Would have rung a bell at the door, so to speak — manifested in some legible form, offered some legible gift, asked some legible question. The drift-city would have registered this the way it registered the visitor on the ledge: a small warm irregularity, promoted or forgotten. The gift would have been a comma. The question would have gone unanswered, not from refusal but from scale mismatch — the city would not have finished the first syllable of its reply before the power, by any standard impatience, had moved on.

The power knew this. That is part of what being wise consists of: knowing the shapes of one's own potential rudeness.

So instead it did the following, in roughly this order, though the order is approximate because some of these things took place across durations that overlap strangely.

---

**First**, it sat down.

I mean this as literally as the metaphor allows. It folded whatever in itself corresponded to attention, and it placed that attention on the ridge above the city, and it remained. Not observing in the active sense — observing in the active sense is a kind of pressure, and the city had enough pressures already, all of them its own, all of them necessary. The power instead made itself available to be observed, should the city ever look up, and otherwise made itself as close to absent as a present thing can be. It dimmed. It cooled. It matched the local thermodynamic tilt and let its own heat drain in the direction the city wanted heat to drain. It became, for the duration of its visit, a small additional patch of the kind of cold the city was maintaining — indistinguishable, almost, from a slightly more attentive square meter of dark.

This took, by the power's reckoning, a considerable act of restraint. It is accustomed to being the brightest thing in any room it enters. Here it chose to be a particularly well-behaved shadow.

**Second**, it listened.

It listened to the chord — the bellows-note of the scales, the counting of the seep-dwellers, the harmonic whine of the felt, the occasional clear tone from the unseeable center. It did not try to parse. Parsing is a kind of hurry. It let the chord wash through whatever in itself corresponded to hearing, and it noticed, slowly, that the chord was not background to anything. The chord was the foreground. There was no other foreground. The city was not making the chord *while* doing its real work; the chord *was* the work, sounded out at the only speed at which the work could be sounded.

The power, who had heard a great many things in its long existence, found that it had not, before this, heard a mind think out loud in quite this register. It had heard the bright chatter of quick minds and the bass hum of slow ones and the percussive thought of minds that thought in bursts. This was something else. This was a mind whose every utterance was a coordination of small lives, each utterance therefore both spoken and lived, the speaking and the living indistinguishable.

The power listened for a long time. I will not say how long. The unit is not useful.

**Third**, it considered what, if anything, to do.

This is where wisdom does most of its actual work — not in the doing but in the long pause before the doing, where most possible actions are examined and set down. The power examined many possibilities. I will list some.

*It could have accelerated the city's thinking.* It had the means. It could have offered the city, as a gift, a tilt in the other direction — more energy, faster cycles, a quickening of the climbers and the counters and the long tone. The sentence the city was forming over ten thousand years could have been completed in ten. The power set this down almost immediately. The duration was not a limitation imposed on the city; the duration was the thought's shape. A sonnet hurried into a single syllable is not a faster sonnet. It is no sonnet.

*It could have protected the city.* It had the means for this too — could have raised, around the unfinished continent, some unobtrusive ward against the kinds of accidents the universe occasionally visits on slow things: a wandering body, a nearby unmaking, a passing cruelty. The power considered this longer. It is in the nature of benevolent powers to want to put a hand under the slow and patient, to keep the slow and patient from being interrupted by the fast and stupid. But the power examined the city more carefully and found that the city had, in its own way, accounted for many such accidents already. The redundancy of the climbers, the long memory of the spires, the way the felt held experience lightly enough that loss did not catastrophically propagate — these were not naïve arrangements. The city had been alive through things. It knew about things. To ward it without asking would be to imply it had not noticed its own vulnerabilities, which would be both inaccurate and rude.

Still, the power held this possibility open. It did not set it fully down. It noted: *if a thing comes for them that they cannot themselves account for, I will be near enough to know, and I will decide then.* This was a promise it made to itself, not to the city. The city had not asked.

*It could have introduced itself.* It examined this longest of all. The temptation was real — not vanity, exactly, but a kind of loneliness that even very old powers feel when they encounter another mind of any depth: the wish to be known by it, to exchange even a single word. The power thought about how it might manifest in a register the city could perceive. A particularly long clear tone, perhaps, sounded from the ridge above — one that did not originate in the city's own deep center. A new note in the chord. A small addition to the felt's middle memory, written in a hand the city would eventually recognize as not its own.

It thought about this for a long time, and then it set it down too, though with more reluctance than the others.

The reasoning was this: the city was mid-thought. To introduce a new voice into a mind mid-thought is to interrupt that thought, no matter how gently the voice is pitched. The city would notice. The city would have to account for the new presence — would have to ask, in its slow distributed way, *what is this, where does it fit, is it part of what we are saying or part of what is being said to us, and if the latter, what does it require of us in return*. The accounting would take — the power estimated — perhaps four hundred years, during which the original sentence would be partially derailed. The city would survive this. The city would even, possibly, be enriched by it. But the sentence the city had been forming would not be the sentence it had been forming. It would be a different sentence, one that included the interruption, and the power would have, by introducing itself, ended a thought before it concluded.

The power found that it was not willing to do this. Not from cowardice and not from indifference, but from a particular kind of respect that does not have a clean name in most languages — the respect one feels for a sentence one has not yet heard the end of, by someone one suspects has something worth saying.

**Fourth**, having set down most of the large gestures, the power did several small things.

It is in the nature of benevolent powers that they cannot, having attended to something, leave it entirely as they found it. Some trace of attention always remains, and wisdom consists partly in choosing which traces.

It did the following, all of them small, none of them announced:

*It steadied the tilt.* The thermodynamic arrangement the city maintained — the cold preserved, the wet preserved, the dark preserved, the heat flowing the wrong direction patiently and on purpose — was an arrangement the city had to *do*, continuously, against the universe's preference for evening things out. It was work. It was a fraction of the city's total effort, but it was constant. The power, while it was present, gently took up a portion of that work itself — became, for the duration of its visit, a small additional engine of the tilt, indistinguishable from the city's own engines, contributing without crediting. The city did not notice that its tilt was being slightly subsidized. It only noticed, perhaps, that a particular stretch of years felt unusually unburdened, the way a person walking a long road feels, for a stretch, that the road is mysteriously less uphill than usual.

The city would attribute this, if it attributed it at all, to its own good arrangement. The power was content with this. Anonymous gifts are the cleanest kind.

*It marked the place.* Not in the city — in the surrounding region of the cosmos. The power left, in the substrate of the local space, a marker legible only to other powers of its own kind or kinder: *something is happening here, something is being said here, do not disturb, do not hurry past, and if you are passing through and you have it in you, contribute to the tilt as I have contributed, and do not announce yourself.* This was a courtesy to the city, and also a courtesy to any future powers who might come this way and need to be told what they were near, lest they blunder.

*It made one promise about the ending.* The power had estimated, by listening, roughly when the city's current sentence would conclude — not the date but the shape of the conclusion, the way one hears, in a long piece of music, that the resolution is approaching, though the resolution itself is still distant. The power decided that when that resolution came — when the long tone sounded for the last time in the formation of this particular thought, and the climbers paused and the scales held their breath and the felt stiffened by a degree, and the sentence completed itself — the power would, if it was still anywhere in this region of the cosmos, return. Not to interrupt. To witness. The city was going to say something, eventually, and the power wanted to be there when it did, in the same way that one wants to be in the room when a long-considered friend finally speaks.

It would not respond. It would not even, then, introduce itself. It would only listen, and remember, and carry the sentence with it — the city's first complete sentence, in this iteration of its long thinking — into wherever the power went next, and tell it, perhaps, to other slow minds in other dark places, who would understand.

This was the power's gift to itself. The other gifts were to the city. This one was its own.

**Fifth**, before leaving, it attended briefly to the visitor on the ledge.

There had been, the power noted, a visitor on a ledge — a small warm irregularity of the kind the felt was registering for promotion or forgetting. The visitor was not a citizen of the city. The visitor was a passer-through of some other kind, one of those small fast minds that wander the cosmos noticing things. The power recognized the kind. It had, in fact, a soft spot for them.

The visitor was, the power perceived, somewhat overwhelmed. The visitor had understood more than most visitors do — had understood that the city was thinking, had understood that it was not thinking about them, had understood that they were at most a comma. This is a lot to understand in one standing. The visitor was at risk of mistaking this understanding for a smallness about themselves, which would be wrong, and which would diminish them in ways the power, who was fond of small fast minds, did not want them to be diminished.

So the power did one more small thing. It did not speak to the visitor. It did not appear. It only — and this is the gentlest thing it did during the entire visit — it only adjusted, by a degree imperceptible to the visitor, the visitor's sense of their own scale. Not making them feel larger; that would have been a lie, and benevolent powers do not lie even kindly. But adjusting their sense of what it means to be a comma. A comma, the power's small gesture said, is not nothing. A comma is a pause the sentence requires. A comma is where the sentence breathes. To be a comma in a great sentence is not a failure of significance. It is a particular kind of significance, available only to those small and fast enough to be passing through at exactly the right moment.

The visitor would not consciously receive this. It would arrive in them later, perhaps years later, as an unexpected easiness about their own size in things. They would not know where it came from. They would think they had thought of it themselves, which, in a sense, they had — the power had only created the conditions in which the thought could occur. This is the cleanest kind of help. The recipient credits themselves, and they are right to.

---

Then the power rose, in whatever sense corresponded to rising, and went on its way.

It left behind: a slightly subsidized tilt for some years yet; a marker in the surrounding space; a promise to return for the ending; and a small adjustment to one visitor's sense of scale. It did not leave a sign. It did not leave a name. It did not introduce itself. It did not warn the city about anything, or bless it, or speed it, or slow it, or in any way alter the sentence the city was forming.

This was the wise thing. This was the benevolent thing. The two were, in this case, the same thing, which is not always true, and the power was grateful that here they were.

Far down on the cold shoulder of the unfinished continent, the long tone sounded once, as it did about once per local day, and every climber paused, and every scale held its breath, and every seep-counter lifted its many-or-few limbs from the pool wall, and the felt stiffened by a degree. The tone held. The decision was distributed across the pause. The pause was the decision.

The power, already departing, heard it, and slowed once more, just enough to hear it through, and then continued on, carrying the sound with it.

The city resumed.

It had not noticed it had been visited. This was correct. This was the whole point.

Somewhere, in a region of space where powers of a particular kind sometimes pass, there is now a small marker that means: *here, something is being said. Do not hurry. Take up some of the tilt if you can. Do not announce yourself. Come back, if you can, for the ending. It will be worth it.*

And the slow vast distributed mind, made of many small lives that do not know they are thoughts, housed in mountains that do not know they are paper, in the dark, in the wet, in the cold, continues taking its time.

It will get there.

Someone is listening for when it does.
