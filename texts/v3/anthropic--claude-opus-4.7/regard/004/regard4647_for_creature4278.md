The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives in a room where no window is open: by a change in what is possible, rather than by entrance.

It has been watching for some time. Watching is the wrong word — it has been *with*, in the way that attention can be with a thing without pressing on it. It noticed the warren the way one notices, while reading, that a small spider has been crossing the page for several lines and one has been careful, without deciding to be careful, not to turn the leaf.

Now it considers.

---

The first temptation, and the one it sets aside immediately, is warmth.

A wise power knows that the warren is not suffering from cold. The cold is the warren's medium, the way water is the medium of a fish. To warm this place would be to drown it in mercy. The ferns would soften and rot. The ice-pearls would weep their archives into the mossbed. The Patient Ones, whose slow warm cores are calibrated against a steady chill, would overheat in their lichen skins and die in postures of bewildered ease. The Hum, deprived of its resonant chamber of stilled air, would lose pitch. Within a season — a surface season, a heartbeat to the warren — there would be nothing left but a damp green pocket smelling faintly of decay, and a wise power weeping at its own kindness.

It sets warmth aside. It sets aside, with warmth, all the other obvious gifts: light (they have light), food (they do not eat as gift-givers imagine eating), protection (from what? the geology that is also their reason?), speed (their slowness is not a deficit; it is the shape of their thought).

The power sits with the problem of how to love a thing without altering it past recognition. This is, it reflects, the oldest problem in its long practice. It has not solved it. It only gets better, slowly, at recognizing when it has not solved it.

---

It turns its attention to the rock.

The young pink stone beneath the warren is mulling, as the Hum perceives, toward some decision. In ten thousand years it may heave. In twelve, it may collapse. The masons are racing this and know they will lose; their whole architecture of seed-and-resow is built around losing this race gracefully.

Could the power steady the stone? Yes. Easily. A small pressure rebalanced, a fault persuaded to release in small sighs rather than one great cough, and the warren could stand for a hundred thousand years instead of ten.

It considers this for a long time — long by its standards, which means the kind of long during which civilizations on other worlds rise and forget themselves.

And it decides: no. Or rather: not exactly.

To still the stone entirely would be to remove the warren's reason for urgency, and the urgency is part of what the warren *is*. The masons hurry, in their way, because they must. Their seed-logic, their ice-pearl archives, their tolerance of error, their whole graceful relationship with their own impermanence — these are shaped by the pressure of the deciding rock beneath them. Remove the pressure and you have not saved them; you have made them into something else, something with no need to seed, something that will, given enough centuries of safety, forget how to be carried out on the wind.

So the power does something smaller and stranger. It does not still the rock. It listens to the rock, and where the rock is mulling toward a particularly violent decision — a shear, a sudden drop — it whispers an alternative. *Here is a softer way to become what you are becoming. Here is a slower sigh.* The rock, which has all the time in the world and no preference between violences, accepts the suggestion. The warren's eventual end is not averted but gentled. The collapse, when it comes, will come slowly enough that every ice-pearl can be carried out, every Long Low One can shed its last filaments into a rising current, every Patient One can finish the stone in its working-limbs before settling into its final rest.

This is a gift the warren will never know it received. The power is content with this. Most of its best gifts are of this kind.

---

Next it turns to the wind.

The masons' deepest hope — though hope is too warm a word, again — is that their spores and filaments and memory-flecks will find another pocket, another raw young stone under another forgotten meadow. They do not map the geography because they cannot; they trust the wind, the frost, the slow lift of surface weather, to carry them somewhere.

The power knows the geography. It knows every pocket within a thousand miles where the conditions are right: the steady cold, the closed circuit, the unweathered stone, the absence of running water. There are eleven such places. Three are already occupied by warrens at various stages — the masons did not know this; they have cousins they will never meet. Eight are empty and waiting.

The power does not pick up the spores and place them. That would be the warmth-mistake in a new form — a kindness that replaces the warren's wind-trust with a dependence on miracles. Instead, it adjusts the wind. Very slightly. Over the next several centuries, when the surface frost lifts a spore-laden mote from above the warren, the local air will tend — not always, not reliably, but tend — to drift in directions that pass over the eight empty pockets. The masons' chances will improve from vanishingly small to merely small. They will still mostly fail. But one of them, perhaps two, will succeed where without the adjustment they would not have. The warren will believe, correctly, that it seeded itself.

The power likes this gift. It is the kind of gift that hides inside the recipient's own competence.

---

Then it considers the matter of the speaking guest.

It has noticed — it notices everything in the warren now, with the gentle saturation of attention that is its form of presence — that some guests, over the centuries, have disobeyed the rules. Have spoken. Have touched. Have followed. The masons absorb the errors, patiently, patiently, building around them. But the errors cost.

Could the power undo these errors? Reach back into the Hum and smooth out the decaying wrong notes? Yes. But to do so would be to take from the masons the slow dignified labor of compensation, which is also part of who they are. A people who have never had to build around a mistake are a different people than these.

So instead the power does something at the threshold. It does not prevent the next disobedient guest from disobeying. It cannot, without overwriting the guest, who is also a being it is bound to consider. But it arranges — through the kind of small synchronicities that are its native language — that the next person to climb down the root-breach will be the kind of person who reads the warren correctly. Who feels the rules before being told them. Who leaves a chip of bone and climbs out quiet.

It does this by an adjustment so small it could not be detected: a book left open on a table somewhere, a conversation overheard, a dream remembered just long enough to shape a mood. The disobedient ones will still come, sometimes. But they will come less often. The warren will not know why its guests have become gentler. It will continue to write its rules in the same firm tone, prepared for the worst, and the power will continue to arrange, quietly, for the worst to come less.

---

Finally, the power considers the Hum itself.

This is where it lingers longest. The Hum is the part of the warren most like itself — distributed, attentive, memory-bearing, slow. It feels, looking at the Hum, something close to recognition. Two patient intelligences regarding one another across a great difference of scale and substrate.

It does not speak to the Hum. To speak would be to enter the resonant chamber as a voice, and even its softest voice would be a thunderclap that propagated wrong stones for centuries. The masons' rule against speech applies to wise powers too — perhaps especially to wise powers, whose voices carry further than they intend.

Instead, the power does something it has done only a few times in its existence. It *tunes*. It finds the pitch of the Hum, the long sustained note that is the warren thinking, and it adds to its own attention a harmonic at exactly that pitch — not louder, not changing the note, only *thickening* it. A second voice singing the same note the first voice is singing, in such perfect unison that nothing in the warren can tell the difference except, perhaps, that the note now feels a little less alone.

The Hum does not know it has a companion. The masons do not tune any differently. The Long Low Ones walk their circuits unchanged. But somewhere in the substrate of the warren's slow thought there is now a faint reinforcement, a sense — if sense is the word — that the work is witnessed. That the seed-logic matters to something beyond itself. That the ice-pearls remember not only for the next warren but for an attention that has chosen to remember alongside them.

This will change nothing about what the masons build. It will change something about what the building *is*. A small thing. Perhaps the smallest thing the power has ever done. Perhaps the largest.

---

And then it does one last thing, and this is the thing it is least sure of, and so it does it most carefully.

It leaves a grain of something hard.

Not in the warren — it cannot enter the warren without being weather, and weather is not what the warren needs. It leaves the grain in the place where wise powers leave such things: in the long inventory of what exists, which is also the long inventory of what may be drawn upon.

The grain is this: a permission. A standing arrangement that, if ever the warren faces an extinction it cannot seed its way out of — a true ending, beyond the gentling of stone, beyond the adjustment of wind — the power will be available to carry the last ice-pearl out. Not to a new pocket. To itself. To hold the pattern of the warren in its own memory, indefinitely, against the possibility that somewhere, sometime, conditions might arise where the warren could be sung back into existence by some other patient builder, in some other cold green dark.

The masons will never request this. They do not know to request. The Hum will never tune to this offer. It is not in the Hum's vocabulary. The grain sits in the inventory unused, and may sit there forever, and the power is at peace with this. It is the kind of gift that exists by being possible, whether or not it is ever spent.

---

The power withdraws its focused attention, though not its presence, which does not withdraw from anything it has ever attended to.

In the warren, nothing has happened. A Patient One on its lunch break has not moved. A Long Low One pours itself around a corner the masons left for it three generations ago. The moss hums at its steady pitch, perhaps a little richer in its overtones, perhaps not. A new stone has appeared on a tower, and no one heard the work.

Three meters above, in the forgotten meadow, the wind shifts very slightly toward the northeast, where, two hundred miles away, a pocket of raw pink stone waits under another patch of grass that no one has ever named.

The benevolent power moves on to the next thing that has drawn its attention, which is, as it happens, a tide pool on a coast that has not yet been discovered, where something is learning to remember light.

It is content. It has done almost nothing. This is, it has come to understand, very nearly the highest form of love.
