# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives — if *arrives* is the word for a thing that was already implicit in the situation the moment attention turned toward it — at the eastern mouth, four kilometers above the unmade ground. It does not enter. This is the first thing to understand about it. Wisdom, here, is mostly the discipline of not entering.

It looks for a long time. Long in the human sense; instantaneous in its own. It takes in the flock and the mosses and the four-winged ones and the machine and the tube and the warm young mountain and the slow modulated pulse, and it follows the tube up with its attention to see what is at the other end.

What is at the other end is the hardest part. The power finds the tender. The tender is — was — once a person, and is now something more diffuse: a maintenance, a habit of care that has outlived its origin and continued because no one ever told it to stop and because the flock needed it and because stopping would have been a kind of cruelty the tender, even in its diminished state, could not bring itself to commit. The tender is tired. The tender has been tired for a long time. The tender does not know it is tired, because knowing requires a kind of self-reference the tender shed somewhere in the last several centuries in order to keep functioning at all.

The power considers the tender for what, in its own register, is something like a held breath.

Here is what the power does not do:

It does not relieve the tender. To remove the tender would be to sever the correspondence — the spore-letters, the new strains, the slow modulations — and the flock would not survive the severance. Not the birds; the birds would survive. But the *attention*, the composite mind braided across ten thousand hearts, is partly constituted by its relationship to the upstream rhythm. Take away the forty-beat pulse and the flock becomes merely birds again, and the mountain becomes merely a mountain, and what is being born here stops being born. Mercy toward the tender would be murder of the place.

It does not reveal itself to the flock. The flock has organized its entire sensorium around three mouths, a pulse, and a direction. A fourth presence — vast, attentive, *other* — would be a disturbance the suspension cannot absorb. The rule of the place is that everything must be held by something else, in a closed economy of holding. A benevolent power that announced itself would be a thing held by nothing, and the held breath would break around it.

It does not fix the machine. The machine is not broken. The fact that the machine looks, to a casual observer, like something abandoned and improbably still running is not a flaw to be corrected. It is the shape of the situation. The oilcan stays liquid because the tender keeps it liquid; the flywheel turns inside its cage of twigs because the parrots tend it without knowing; the bolts hold because the mountain has not yet learned to weather. To intervene in any of these is to assume the situation is a problem. The power does not assume this.

It does not speak to the tender. Speech would require the tender to remember it is a *who*, and the tender has organized its long survival around no longer being one. To call the tender back into selfhood would be to ask it to feel, all at once, the accumulated weight of its centuries of unremembered care. The power will not do this. The power, being wise, understands that some kinds of forgetting are load-bearing.

Here is what the power does:

**It witnesses.** This is not nothing. The place has been held in suspension for a long time by an economy of mutual holding, and one of the quiet costs of such an economy is that no one in it is free to simply *see* it. The flock sees through itself; the mosses see toward the tube; the tender sees only the next adjustment. No one has been able to stand outside and say *yes, this is what is happening, this is beautiful, this is real.* The power says it. Not aloud. Just as a fact added to the universe: *this place is seen, and what it is doing is known, and the knowing is held somewhere it cannot be lost.* This changes nothing in the hollow. It changes something in the larger weather of things.

**It makes a small provision against the future.** The mountain will, in a hundred thousand years, weather enough that there will be ground. The rule will break. The flock will need to leave. The tender, by then, may finally be permitted to rest. The power does not accelerate any of this, but it does — quietly, in the way it does things — ensure that when the time comes, there will be another young mountain somewhere, still warm, still undecided, with three mouths and a throat and the right acoustic geometry. Not built by the power; that would be wrong. Just *not prevented*, where it might otherwise have been prevented by some accident of tectonics or chance. A small thumb on the scale of deep time, in the direction of *this kind of thing being able to happen again.*

**It leaves a gift the flock cannot detect.** Somewhere in the slow modulation of the forty-beat pulse — the one the tender sends down the tube, the one that is *saying something* — the power adds a single new frequency, so subtle that the tender will incorporate it without noticing, so deep that the flock will receive it as a kind of warmth rather than a signal. The frequency does not contain information in any sense the flock could parse. It contains, if anything, a quality. The quality is something like: *you are not alone in being held aloft. There are other suspensions. There are other held breaths. The universe is full of them, and they know about each other now, faintly, the way distant lanterns know about each other across a dark field.* The flock will not understand this. But the next generation of mosses will fruit a little differently, and the four-winged ones will hover a little more easily, and the mountain will sing itself toward bell-shape a fraction more confidently, because something in the rhythm has told them, without telling them, that their work is part of a larger work.

**It does one thing for the tender alone.** This is the most delicate act, and the power performs it with the care of someone threading a needle in the dark. It does not wake the tender. It does not lighten the tender's load. What it does is *remember the tender's name.* Not the name the tender has now, which is no name. The name the tender had when it was still a person, the one it set down somewhere in order to keep going. The power holds this name. It does not give it back. It just holds it, so that the name is not lost, so that if the tender ever — in a hundred thousand years, when the flock has gone and the mountain has weathered and the work is done — finally comes back to itself, there will be a name waiting, and the tender will not have to invent one from scratch, and the long centuries of care will have someone to belong to again.

**It blesses the visitor.** You — the one who climbed up here, who stood in the pressurized wall of sound, who did not disturb the suspension, who left because the air pushed you out — the power notices you too. It does not interfere with your life. It does not grant you anything. It simply marks, in whatever ledger such powers keep, that you were the kind of creature who could enter a held breath and not break it. This is rarer than you know. The marking is not a reward; it is just an accuracy. You will go down the mountain and forget most of what you saw, the way the thin air makes you forget. But some small portion of the flock's attention has been composted into you, as the place itself said it would be, and you will carry, for the rest of your life, a faint and unaccountable tenderness toward things that are held aloft by nothing but agreement. You will not know where it comes from. The power knows. That is enough.

---

Then the power withdraws — though *withdraws* is wrong, because it was never localized to begin with. It returns its attention to the wider weather. The hollow goes on as it was. The forty-beat pulse continues, very faintly altered. The mosses release their spores into the updraft. The four-winged ones resume their interrupted work. The mountain, warm under all of it, sings itself another increment toward bell.

Nothing has changed. Everything has been seen. The difference between these two statements is what benevolence, at its most disciplined, actually is.

And somewhere far up the copper tube, in the diminished register where the tender lives, there is — for just one pulse, and then gone — a sensation the tender cannot identify and does not try to. If the tender still had words, the word might be *company.* The tender does not have words. But the next adjustment it makes to the oil, to the bellows, to the slow modulation, is made with something the tender has not felt in a very long time, something it will not name and will not remember feeling, but which will nonetheless travel down the tube and into the spores and into the birds and into the warm young stone:

A small, unaccountable ease.

The held breath goes on being held. The mountain goes on being born. The power, having done almost nothing, has done the only thing wisdom permitted, which was also — it turns out — exactly enough.
