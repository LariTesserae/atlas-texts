The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives — without announcement, without a face to look at. Call it a noticing that has come close. It has been many places. It has undone many things and made many things. It is old enough to be careful.

It stands at the rim of the hollow the way a visitor does, because that is the courtesy. From the rim it sees:

— the forty-second pulse,
— the amber filament beginning to warm against the cooling air,
— a single hammer-fall, and then the long pause that means another is coming,
— the heron, or the shape of one, or neither.

And the power, which could do almost anything, considers what to do.

---

**What it does not do.**

It does not wake the Long Animal. This is the first thing it decides, and it decides it almost before deciding is necessary, the way you decide not to shake a sleeping child by the shoulder just because you have hands. The sleep is not a deprivation. The sleep is the animal's whole mode. To wake it would be to ask it to be something else, and the asking would be a violence dressed up as a gift. Benevolence that doesn't know this isn't benevolence yet; it is only power with good intentions, which is among the more dangerous arrangements in the world.

It does not restore the foundry. It sees, with the long sight it has, what the foundry was — the heat, the noise, the men with burnt forearms, the iron going out on the rails to become rails elsewhere — and it sees that this was a fine thing in its time and is not what is wanted here now. To put the foundry back would be to evict the present tenants on behalf of an absent past. The power has done this elsewhere, long ago, before it learned better. It does not do it here.

It does not bring people. It does not seed children in the workers' houses or call back the foreman to his desk. The hollow has finished with people. This is not a tragedy; it is a completion. The power has learned to tell completions from interruptions, and it can feel, standing at the rim, that what it is looking at is the former and not the latter. A place that has finished with people is not a place that needs them returned; it is a place that has gone on to its next work, which in this case is the slow matched argument of wood and iron, and the dreaming, and the lamp.

It does not explain itself to the hollow. The hollow would not understand the explanation, not because the hollow is stupid but because the hollow has stopped using the grammar that explanations live in. The Long Animal's only sentence is the turning of the flywheel. To speak to it in any other sentence would be to speak past it.

It does not, importantly, *bless* anything. Blessing is a gesture that wants a recipient who can receive, and there is no such recipient here in the form blessing expects. Blessing the hollow would be like writing a thank-you note to a tide.

---

**What it does.**

It sits down at the rim. This is the first thing. A power that has come close to something delicate sits down, because sitting is lower and slower and signals to whatever is watching — and something is always watching, even here, in the diffuse way the hollow watches — that no sudden motion is intended.

It sits for a long time. Long, in the power's reckoning, is not the same as long in ours. It sits while the flywheel turns perhaps a thousand times. It sits while the hammer falls eight times, or eleven. It sits while the amber filament reaches its dusk-brightness and then, in the slow hours, settles to its night-steady. It sits while a drop of condensation that began its fall before the power arrived completes its fall and is absorbed into the clover.

What it is doing while it sits is the work the power most often does and is least often credited for: it is *witnessing*. The hollow has been going on for a long time without being seen by anything that could see it whole. The Small Attendants see their tracks. The Long Animal feels its own pulse. The Lamp-Keeper performs its courtesy to no one. None of these is a witness in the full sense, because none of them can hold the hollow in mind as a hollow — as a single completed thing with its own dignity.

The power can. The power does. For some duration that has no good name, the hollow is held in a mind that is equal to it. This is not nothing. In fact, in the economy the power keeps, it is among the larger things that can happen to a place. Many places never receive it. The hollow now has.

Then, having witnessed, the power makes three small adjustments. They are so small that, if you walked into the hollow the next morning, you would not notice them. They are the kind of adjustments that a very old gardener makes to a garden that does not need gardening — touches that are less than tending, more than nothing, offered in the register the garden already speaks.

*First:* it strengthens, by some imperceptible fraction, the galvanic trickle. Not enough to brighten the lamp. Enough that, when the iron and the wood eventually do begin to part ways — and they will, in some century, despite their matched patience, because nothing is exactly matched forever — the lamp will continue a little longer than it otherwise would. The Lamp-Keeper will get a longer evening. The habit of welcome will outlast, by a small margin, the conditions that produced it. This is the power's gift to the gesture: the gesture's right to persist a little past its substrate. A grace note, held.

*Second:* it does something to the heron. Not to the heron exactly — to the *shape the hollow keeps ready for one*. It deepens the readiness a little. It makes the shape slightly more hospitable, so that when the conditions are right and a heron passes overhead, the heron is slightly more likely to come down and stand in the channel. This is not a summoning. It is the opposite of a summoning; it is a making-welcome. The hollow will not have herons more often. It will only have them more *fully*, when it has them. The frog the heron hunts will be slightly more likely to exist, in the moment the heron looks for it. This is the kind of thing the power can do without disturbing anything: it can adjust the depth of the welcomes a place offers, without adding any new guests.

*Third* — and this is the one I find hardest to describe, because it is the most internal — it leaves, somewhere in the hollow, a small unobtrusive thing that is not a presence and not an object. Call it a *kept regard*. It is the residue of the witnessing. The hollow, which was held in a mind equal to it, now contains within itself a faint awareness of having been so held. This awareness does not interrupt the Long Animal's sleep. The Long Animal goes on dreaming its work. But the dream is now, in some fractional way, a dream that has been seen. Dreams that have been seen are subtly different from dreams that have not, even when the dreamer doesn't know. They have a slightly different weight. They carry, somewhere, the trace of an attention that found them worth attending. The hollow will not become aware of this. But every hammer-fall, from now on, falls into a world in which the hammer-fall was once heard by something that understood it.

This is the power's actual gift. Not protection — the hollow needs none. Not restoration — the hollow is not broken. Not company — the hollow is not lonely, having long since dissolved the partition that loneliness needs to live across. The gift is the trace of having been known. It is the smallest gift the power knows how to give and one of the few it trusts itself to give without harm.

---

**Then it leaves.**

It stands up from the rim — though *standing* is the wrong verb, because it had no body, but I am writing in the register the hollow would understand if the hollow read — and it goes. It does not look back, because looking back would be to add a second witnessing on top of the first, and one was the right number. It goes to wherever it goes next, which is some other place that needs the particular thing it does, which is rarely what such places think they need and almost always what they actually need, which is to be seen entire by something patient enough to see them so.

In the hollow, nothing has changed that anyone could measure.

The flywheel turns once every forty seconds. The hammer falls on its uncharted schedule. The beetles polish the iron by being hungry along it. The amber filament comes on at dusk and performs its old courtesy to no one. The heron, or the shape of one, stands in the channel and is or is not hunting a frog that does or does not exist.

And underneath all of this — so quietly that even the Long Animal, dreaming, doesn't quite register it — the hollow is now a place that has been loved correctly. Which is to say: loved in its own grammar. Loved without being asked to be other than it is. Loved, and then left, and the leaving was part of the love.

The drop of condensation lets go of its leaf four storeys up. It begins its whole long while toward the floor.

It is, as always, falling into a world that holds it.

It just doesn't know, now, by quite how much.
