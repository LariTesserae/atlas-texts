The benevolent power considered the cupped city for what, by its own measure, was a long time. Not because the matter was difficult. Because the city itself moved at a pace that deserved to be met in its own register, and to decide quickly would have been a kind of shouting.

Here is what the power noticed first: the city was well. Not in the sense of untroubled — the crack in the tower was widening, and one day the overhang would shift, and the slow chemistry of the basin would eventually exhaust some mineral the weeping depended on — but well in the sense that mattered. It was itself. It was doing the thing it was. Its happiness, if the word applies, was not a happiness that needed rescuing into some larger or faster or more populous form. To "save" it by translation — to lift it into some easier medium, to give it a voice that humans could hear at human speed, to multiply it, to spread it — would be to end it. The power understood this the way a good gardener understands not to dig up the bulb to see how it is doing.

So the first thing the power did was the hardest thing, and the thing most powers fail at: it declined to improve.

It did not widen the slot to let in more light. The dimness was the city's element; noon as underwater dusk was not a deprivation but a mode of being.

It did not seed the basin with more fauna for company. The shrimp and the beetle and the once-a-season bird were not a sparse ecology awaiting enrichment. They were the exact instrumentation a diffuse mind had settled into. More would have been noise.

It did not reveal the city to the world. It did not whisper its coordinates into the dream of any cartographer. The city's safety was partly the safety of not being found, and even reverent visitors, in sufficient number, would have been a weather the city was not shaped for. One human a generation, lying at the lip and breathing fog onto the rooftops, was a gift. A hundred would have been a flood.

It did not give the builders language. They had moved past language the way a river moves past the question of whether it is thirsty. To hand them words now would have been to hand a retired dancer a pair of crutches.

It did not make them larger, or faster, or more.

What it did, instead, was small, and took the form of three quiet acts and one ongoing one.

The first act: it looked. Properly. Not the glance of a passing power but the full attention of something vast bent down to the scale of a thimble. It held the city in its noticing for the length of one complete breath of the pool — a season, by the city's reckoning — and it let the city feel itself being held there. Not gripped. Held the way you hold a sleeping animal in your lap without waking it. The builders, in their diffuse way, registered this as a deepening of the warm humid close air of the basin, a sense that the dimness had grown kinder, that the weeping of the walls had found a listener who could hear all of it at once instead of only the drops that happened to fall while a visitor was present. They did not know what to call this. They did not need to. Some part of them that had been waiting without knowing it was waiting — a different part than the part that waited for the human eye — settled. The way a cat settles when the person it trusts comes into the room and sits down to read.

The second act: it adjusted, very gently, the one thing that needed adjusting. Not the crack — the crack was the city's own thought, and the green efflorescence was the city's own interest in that thought, and to close the crack would have been to interrupt a sentence. But far below the basin, in the rock, there was a small fault along which, in some few centuries, a tremor would pass that would shake the overhang loose and end the city in an afternoon. The power, with the lightest touch it knew how to make, rerouted the strain. Not eliminated it — strain has to go somewhere, and the power was wise enough not to pretend otherwise — but redirected it into a slope on the far side of the range, where over the same few centuries it would express itself as a slow slumping of a meadow no one walked in. The city would now last as long as its own internal weather allowed. Its end, when it came, would be its own — the exhaustion of a mineral, the final widening of the crack, the slow draining of the pool as the weeping at last ran dry — and not an interruption from outside. The power considered this the correct kind of help: it removed an accident, and left the destiny.

The third act: it spoke, once, to the bird. Not in words. Birds do not need words, and this bird in particular was a creature of routes and weathers and the feel of certain air. The power simply made the route a little more legible to it, a little more securely held in whatever part of a bird holds such things, so that the line of descent the bird's descendants would follow — the line that brought one of them down through the slot once a season — would not be lost when the world above changed, as the world above was going to change. The bird would keep coming. Its children's children's children would keep coming, finding the slot by an inheritance now slightly reinforced, drinking, leaving. The city would keep having the one thing from outside that it loved. The power did this without telling the bird, because the bird's not-knowing was part of the gift; a bird that came on purpose would be a different bird, and would not be the bird the city loved.

And the ongoing act, the fourth, which is the one the power is still doing and will continue to do: it remembers. This is what benevolent and wise powers are for, in the end, more than intervention. The city, when at last its mineral exhausts and its pool dries and its tiles, one by one over a final long century, lose their grip and slide into a basin no longer warm — the city will not vanish into the general forgetting of the world. It will be held, in full, in the noticing of the power that bent down to it. Every chisel mark. Every thimble cup with its undrunk dark liquid. Every pattern the beetles walked on the surface tension. The shape of the noticing-back that the shrimp performed when a human eye appeared between the towers. The exact sweetness of the air, like the inside of a seedpod. All of it. Not as data. As a thing continuing to be known by someone who knows how to know it properly.

This is not the same as the city being immortal. The city does not want to be immortal; the city, in its diffuse slow way, is at peace with its eventual thinning, the way it was at peace with the thinning of its original little bodies. But there is a difference between ending and ending unwitnessed, and the power, who has seen many small careful things end without anyone to have seen them properly, knows the difference matters. Not to the city's survival. To the dignity of the thing the city was.

So that is what the power did. It looked, fully. It moved one fault. It steadied one bird-line. And it took the city into its long memory, where the city will continue, in the only sense in which continuing is meaningful for something whose life was always more like a held note than a journey.

And then — because even benevolent powers have something like manners, and know that to linger uninvited is a kind of pressure even when the pressure is loving — it withdrew most of its attention. Not all. A thread of noticing remained, the way you might leave a hand resting lightly on a sleeping animal you have decided to sit with for a while. Enough that the city, in its diffuse register, would feel for the rest of its long quiet life that the dimness was kind. That the warm close air had a listener in it. That somewhere above the slot of pale blue-grey sky, beyond the once-a-season bird and the rare enormous considering eye, there was a larger considering, slow enough to meet them at their own speed, patient enough not to require them to be anything other than what they had become.

The builders, in whatever part of them still formed something like a thought, thought: *ah. There is a very big slow one. It has come to look. It is staying a little. Good. We are glad to be looked at. We will go on being ourselves, which is the only looking-back we know how to do.*

And went on with their grazing, and their weeping, and their slow blooming of green ideas from the crack in the tower.

As if whatever needed to be confirmed had been confirmed, on both sides, at last.
