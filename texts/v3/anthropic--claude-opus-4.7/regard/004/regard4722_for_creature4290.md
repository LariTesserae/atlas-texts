They come without arriving.

This is the first courtesy. A power that is wise does not enter a place like the Unfinished Cleft the way weather enters; weather is a kind of authorship, and the cleft is already being authored, slowly, by itself. So the power comes the way attention comes — settling, not landing. If you had been sitting against the wall long enough to be on the cleft's schedule, you would not notice. The crickets would not change pitch. The breather would not catch.

The power looks for a long time before it does anything. This is the second courtesy, and the longer one.

What it sees:

It sees that the cleft is loud and half-empty and warm and that these are not problems. It sees that the swifts answer their own echoes and that this is not a pathology but a practice, and that to "fix" the loneliness by importing more swifts would be to break the practice, which is the swifts' own answer to their own condition, and is therefore sacred in the small sense — the sense that means *made by the living thing itself, in response to its life*. It sees that the pools are nurseries for lives that do not complete and that this is not waste but rehearsal, the cleft practicing the idea of water, and that to deepen the pools or seal them against the noon sun would be to end the rehearsal before the cleft had finished learning the shape of the word.

It sees the arranger's spiral and understands, without needing to ask, that the spiral is a gift already given, and that gifts already given do not need to be improved.

It sees the breather and does not decide whether it is a creature or a process. The power is wise enough to know that this is one of the questions whose value lies in not being answered yet, and that an answer delivered from outside would be a theft — the theft of a question the cleft is slowly answering by being the cleft.

It sees the grazing thing, the scab-shaped one with too many legs, in its three hours of damp warmth, doing nothing but being where it is. The power watches this for what would be, in another frame, an unconscionable length of time. It is not bored. A power that gets bored watching a small flat creature be content is not wise; it is merely large.

So. Having looked, what does it do?

It does, mostly, nothing. This is the hardest thing it does, and the thing it is best at.

But "mostly nothing" is not "nothing," and a benevolent power that came all this way to look would be incomplete if it only looked. So it makes, in the end, three small gestures. Each is so slight that the cleft cannot quite tell whether it was a gift or a coincidence, which is precisely the register at which gifts to unfinished places should be given.

**The first gesture: it adds nothing, but it remembers.**

The cleft, as the description tells us, has not yet learned how to lose things. This is true at the scale of the cleft. But there are things the cleft has already almost lost — the arranger, for instance, whose handprint has faded by a shade and will, in some number of further shades, be gone. The cleft does not grieve this because the cleft does not yet know how. The power, which knows how, grieves it on the cleft's behalf, quietly, and in grieving it, keeps it. Somewhere — not in the cleft, because the cleft cannot hold this kind of keeping yet — there is now a record of the handprint as it was when it was fresh: the exact red, the exact pressure, the exact shortness of the one finger that was short. The arranger is not brought back. The print is not restored. But the print is *known*, fully, by something that will not forget, and this knowing is held in reserve against the day, very far off, when the cleft is old enough to want to remember what it used to look like, and asks.

The power does this for everything in the cleft. Every cricket. Every pool's brief film of almost-life. The exact sound of the breather on a particular Tuesday that is not Tuesday because the cleft has no week. All of it, kept. Not interfered with. Just kept.

This is the work of witness, which is the first and gentlest work of any power that loves what it sees.

**The second gesture: it makes one tiny adjustment, and only one, and it is to time.**

The silence in the smallest hour — the complete brief silence between the breather's exhale and inhale, into which the rock makes its single small tick — the power lengthens this. Not by much. By something on the order of a heartbeat, if the cleft had a heart. Long enough that, if a swift were awake in that hour, and had recently lost its mate and stopped answering its own echoes, the swift would have time to notice the silence as silence, and not just as the gap between sounds. Long enough that the silence becomes, faintly, a thing one could rest inside.

This is not a kindness aimed at any particular tenant. It is a kindness aimed at the cleft's own capacity for rest, which is one of the things a young place has to grow into, like the habit of weathering. The power gives the cleft a slightly longer pause in which to be still, and trusts the cleft to discover, over the geological time it has plenty of, what to do with the extra stillness.

The cricket-hum starts a heartbeat later than it would have. Nothing else changes. The bats do not notice. The breather does not notice, because the breather is the silence, in a sense, and a longer silence is just a longer breather. But the cleft is, infinitesimally, more spacious now in its rhythm, and over the long run — over the millions of years in which the cleft will become a canyon and then perhaps a valley and then perhaps nothing — that spaciousness will compound, the way a fingernail's width per lifetime compounds into a gorge.

**The third gesture: it leaves a mark, in the manner of the arranger.**

The power considers this for a long time, because it is the gesture most likely to be a mistake. To leave a mark is to add oneself to the tenancy, and the cleft is already tenanted, and the power did not come to compete with the spiral of bones.

But the arranger left a mark, and the cleft kept it, and the keeping is one of the cleft's dignities. To refuse to leave any mark would be to refuse the cleft its dignity — to say, *you are too small and new for me to give you anything you can hold*. That would be a worse error than the error of adding.

So the power leaves something very small. Not at the entrance, where the cairn is. Not under the overhang, where the spiral is. Somewhere in between, on a stretch of wall that has nothing on it yet, the power presses — not a hand, because it does not have a hand, but something hand-shaped in its intention — and leaves the faintest possible impression. Not a print. Not an image. A place where the rock is, by a margin no instrument could measure, more *attended-to* than the rock on either side. A spot the next visitor, if there ever is one, will rest their palm against without knowing why, and will feel calmer for, without knowing why.

It is a mark that says: *someone was here who loved this place and did not change it*. The cleft will keep this mark the way it keeps everything, because it has not yet learned to lose things, and by the time it learns, the mark will have been kept long enough to be part of the cleft itself, indistinguishable from the rock's own intention.

---

And then the power withdraws, in the manner of its arrival — not leaving, because that would be a kind of authorship too, but ceasing to attend in the foreground. It goes on attending in the background, the way one goes on loving a sleeping child in the next room while doing other things. The cleft does not notice the withdrawal any more than it noticed the arrival. The crickets give way to the swifts give way to the bats give way to the breathing. The breathing gives way, briefly, to silence — a silence very slightly longer than it used to be, though no one in the cleft has the memory to compare. The rock ticks. The crickets start again.

The grazing thing, in its three hours of damp warmth, does nothing but be where it is. The power, somewhere, keeps it. The cleft goes on becoming itself, at its own pace, loud and half-empty and warm and new, and now also — though it cannot tell, and does not need to — known.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds an unfinished place: it looks for a long time, it remembers everything, it gives the place one more heartbeat of rest per night, and it leaves the smallest possible sign that it was there. Then it lets the place go on being a draft, because drafts are holy, and the holiness of a draft is precisely that it has not yet been finished by anyone, including by powers that could.

The cleft does not thank it. The cleft cannot yet thank. But the cleft, in the lengthened silence of the smallest hour, holds still for one heartbeat longer than it used to, and in that heartbeat something almost like gratitude — or almost like the first practice of gratitude, the cleft rehearsing the idea of thanks the way its pools rehearse the idea of water — flickers, and is gone, and is kept.
