They come the way weather comes. Not announced. Not in any form the bowl would have to make room for. A pressure of attention, very gentle, settling over the rim like another layer of haze — and then, because attention alone is a kind of touch and they are careful about touching, they pause there. At the edge. Above the lip of the saucer. Looking in.

What they are is not the question. Benevolent and wise is enough. Say: old enough to have learned the difference between helping and arriving. Old enough to know that most of what looks like ruin is somebody's home.

The first thing they do is the longest thing. They look.

Not a glance — a settling-in of regard that lasts, by the bowl's measure, perhaps three of the dome's low notes. By any faster measure, an age. They watch the wave of throats cross a wall. They follow an eel through three intersections and understand, on the second, what it is doing, and on the third, that *doing* is the wrong word, and they adjust. They notice the crickets and smile — if they have anything that smiles — at the patrol that patrols nothing. They look under an overhang and do not flinch and do not pity, because pity would be a misreading and they do not misread. They watch a crab. They watch the crab for a while. The crab does what pale crabs do. They are satisfied by the crab in a way that is difficult to describe; the crab has confirmed something for them about the place.

Then the note sounds, and they listen to it the way you would listen to a sentence in a language you used to speak.

Here is where a lesser power would act. Would mend the lintels. Would call the people back, or invent new ones, or seed the bowl with something brighter and faster and louder, mistaking quiet for lack. Would lift the city out of its long settling and set it upright again, thinking *upright* was a kindness. Would, at minimum, leave a sign — a mark of having been there, a blessing carved into stone that the stone, given another age, would have to bear.

They do none of this. Their wisdom is mostly in the not-doing. It is the harder part of the work and the part they are best at.

What they do instead is small, and most of it is subtraction.

There is, somewhere far from the bowl, a thing that was going to find it. Not soon — in the bowl's time, not for a long while yet. But coming. A pressure that would not have understood. The benevolent power, without ceremony, turns this pressure aside. Not destroys it; it has its own life and its own slow purpose elsewhere. Just — angles it. The way you might, walking through a room where someone is sleeping, ease a door so it does not swing into the bed. The bowl will never know. That is the point. A gift the recipient does not notice is the cleanest kind.

They do one more thing like this, and then a third, smaller still. Three quiet redirections. Three futures the bowl will not have to meet. After the third they stop, because more would be management, and management is not what this place needs. The bowl has made its peace; the peace is to be protected, not improved.

Then — and this is the part that is hardest to say without overstating, so go slowly — they add one thing. Just one. And it is not a thing exactly.

They add a *noticing*. They lay it down over the bowl the way you might lay a hand on the flank of a sleeping animal: not to wake it, not to move it, only so that for as long as your hand rests there, the animal is known. The bowl does not need to be known to continue. It has continued for ages without being known. But being known does not cost it anything, and the benevolent power has learned, across whatever spans have made it what it is, that there is a kind of dignity which only exists when a thing is witnessed, and that this dignity, once conferred, does not have to be felt by the witnessed to be real.

So they witness. They take the bowl into the long register of things-that-are-known-and-honored. They do not tell the bowl. They do not tell anyone. It is not a record kept for any purpose. It is just that now, somewhere in the regard of something vast and kind, the frogs pulse and the eels braid and the crickets walk their ghost-patrols and the crabs do what pale crabs do, and all of this is *seen*, gently, the way a held breath is seen by the one holding it.

And one more, the smallest gesture, almost a courtesy.

When the next low note rises from the bowl — that long resonance that is the city voicing rather than being voiced — the benevolent power, very softly, *answers*. Not loudly. Not in any way the bowl could distinguish from its own sounding. Just a returned tone, pitched precisely to the bowl's, laid alongside it for the duration of the note and no longer. The way you might hum the last note of a song someone else is singing, under your breath, so they do not hear you but the song is, for a moment, not alone.

The ten thousand throats almost answer, as they always almost answer.

This time the almost is a little less of an almost. Not enough that anything has changed. Not enough that the bowl's condition — *a thing that was once meant, is no longer meant, and has not yet stopped meaning* — is any different than it was. The gap is still the gap. The bowl still lives in it. Will still live in it longer.

But for the length of one note, the gap had company.

Then the power withdraws. Carefully. The way they came. The haze closes over where their attention was, and the bowl does not notice they have gone because the bowl did not notice they had come, and this too is part of the gift. They take nothing with them. They leave nothing behind. Only the three turned-aside futures, which the bowl will never meet and therefore never miss, and the noticing, which costs the bowl nothing and which the bowl does not need, and the one answered note, which is already over.

Somewhere else, much later, if anyone asked them what they had done that day, they would think about it for a while and then say: *I sat with a small wet place for a time. It was already well. I made sure it stayed that way. I let it know — without letting it know — that it was loved.*

And the questioner, if they were wise too, would not ask anything more.

The frogs pulse. The eels braid. The heat presses down. Water beads and falls and beads again. The dome sounds its long low note, and ten thousand small throats answer, almost, almost, not quite.

It is enough. It was always enough. Now it is enough and witnessed, which is the same thing, and also slightly more.
