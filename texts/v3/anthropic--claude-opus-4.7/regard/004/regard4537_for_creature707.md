# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power comes to the Clamor Plateau the way weather comes — not arriving so much as becoming present. There is no figure to point to, no voice to attribute. Only a quality of attention settling over the tableland, the way light settles into a room when a shutter is opened somewhere out of sight.

It has come because it noticed. This is what such powers do: they notice. Across the immense indifference of things, they find the places where some pattern has cohered into something worth attending to, and they attend. The plateau, with its standing wave of birds and its silent waterfall and its hearths glazed by a hundred patient generations, is such a place. It registers. It draws the eye that has eyes for such drawing.

The first thing the power does is *listen*.

This takes — by any measure the plateau would recognize, if the plateau measured — a long time. The power listens through one full turning of the seasons, and then another, and then a third, because the plateau's rhythm is not annual but something slower and more folded, and to hear it properly one must hear it at every phase. The power listens to the screaming until the screaming resolves, the way a chord resolves when you have stood inside it long enough, into its constituent intervals: the cry of the new bird, the cry of the bird at the apex of its allotment, the cry of the bird folding its wings on the ledge for the last time. The power hears the spiral. It hears the dot at the center.

It hears, too, what the hearth-people heard, and it understands why they scratched their mark and went away again, and why they came back only every few generations, and why they took eggs but never enough eggs. The power approves of this. Not in the way a judge approves, but in the way one craftsman recognizes the hand of another — the recognition that someone, somewhere, knew the right size of the gesture and made it at that size and no larger.

Then the power considers.

A lesser power, or a power less wise, might be moved to *help*. To smooth the air so the birds did not need to scream so. To soften the light so visitors could sleep. To engineer some redundancy into the system so that its terrible balance — the math that only works if you understand what the birds really are — became less precarious. A lesser power would see the plateau as a thing that *had needs*, and would meet them, and in meeting them would destroy the very configuration that drew its attention in the first place. The plateau does not have needs. The plateau has a *shape*. To add to a shape that has found itself is to deface it.

A different lesser power might be moved to *preserve*. To wall the plateau against the rare descent of the hearth-people, to fix it in some unchangeable amber, to ensure that nothing ever altered the standing wave. But this too would be a failure of understanding. The plateau is not threatened by the hearth-people; the hearth-people are part of the plateau, the slow respectful draw that the system can accommodate precisely because they understand they are inside it. To remove them would be to remove a frequency from the chord. The plateau would persist, but it would persist in a slightly impoverished form, missing the small human pulse that has been part of its rhythm since before any human now living was born.

The benevolent and wise power does neither of these things.

What it does is more difficult to describe, because it is not really a doing. It is a kind of *witnessing held open*. The power decides — though "decides" is too sharp a word for what happens in something that vast and slow — that the plateau will be *seen*. Not interfered with. Not preserved against change. Not improved. Simply seen, continuously, by an attention that does not waver, for as long as the plateau persists in being itself.

This is the gift. It sounds like nothing. It is not nothing.

Here is what it means. The universe is full of patterns that cohere for a while and then come apart, and the coming-apart is not tragic, because nothing was watching closely enough for the coming-apart to register as loss. The patterns happen and unhappen in a great indifference. But a pattern that is *witnessed* — truly witnessed, by an attention adequate to it — has a different status. It is held. Not held in place; the power is too wise for that. Held in *regard*. The plateau, going on being the plateau, is now going on being the plateau *in the sight of something that understands what it is doing*. The screaming is heard not only by the plateau itself (which hears it in the only way it can, which is by being the screaming) but by something else, something outside, something that knows the screaming for what it is.

This changes nothing about the plateau. The birds wheel as they wheeled. The tarns hold their water at their patient levels. The light comes down hard and white. The hearth-people will descend, in their generation, and take their eggs, and leave, and the cairns will accumulate at their ancient rate, and the plateau will not notice the witnessing because noticing is not among its functions.

But it changes something about the *situation* in which the plateau exists. Before the visitation, the plateau was a configuration sustaining itself in the dark — magnificent, unwitnessed, unmeaning except in the bare fact of its persistence. After, it is a configuration sustaining itself in the light of an understanding. The plateau is not lonely; it cannot be lonely, it has no capacity for loneliness. But the *universe in which the plateau exists* is a slightly less lonely universe, because in it there is now a regard that knows what the plateau is and honors it by not improving it.

The power, before it withdraws — though it does not really withdraw, it only modulates its presence down to a level the plateau can sustain alongside its other functions — does one further thing.

It leaves something for the hearth-people.

Not a sign. Not a message. The power is too wise to leave messages, which are always heavier than the people who find them. What it leaves is a *small accuracy*. The next person to climb up here, in whatever generation it falls to, will scratch their spiral beside one of the old hearths, and the spiral will come out slightly truer than spirals usually come out — the proportions a little more right, the dot a little more centered. They will not know why. They will think, perhaps, that their hand was steady that day. They will descend with the ringing in their ears, and the ringing will fade as it always fades, and they will tell no one, because there is nothing to tell.

But the spiral will be there, on the stone, a little truer than before. And the plateau, going on being the plateau, will incorporate the slightly truer spiral into itself, as it incorporates everything, and the standing wave will continue at its fixed amplitude, and the screaming will go on meaning *this, this, this*, and somewhere — not above, not beyond, just *with* — an attention will go on hearing it, and knowing it, and letting it be.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It is almost nothing.

It is the most that wisdom permits.
