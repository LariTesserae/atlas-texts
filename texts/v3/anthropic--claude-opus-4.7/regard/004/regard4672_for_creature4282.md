The benevolent and wise power considers the Boiler-Reef for a long time before doing anything. This is the first thing to say: that wisdom, here, looks mostly like not-intervening, and benevolence looks mostly like the patience required to figure out what intervention would even mean.

The power sees the room. It sees the way the engines have grown themselves into a competence that needs no help. It sees the tin-fish and the wrench-crabs and the long segmented eel, and it understands that to "rescue" any of them would be to misread them — they are not stranded, they are not suffering, they are exactly the shape the place made them and they fit. The power has been around long enough to know how often help, offered to a self-sufficient system, becomes a kind of vandalism. It does not vandalize.

It looks at the Tender for a longer time.

Here is where the wisdom has to do real work, because the temptation is acute. The Tender is lonely in that structural way, that single-note-held-long way, and a benevolent power's first impulse — even a wise one's — is to fix loneliness. To make a second Tender. To populate the room with kin. To carry the Tender up out of the dark into some brighter company.

The power sits with this impulse and lets it pass.

A second Tender would not be a companion; it would be a contradiction. The Tender's whole function — the thing that makes them the distinction's ghost, the last soft remainder of the idea that something might stand apart and watch — depends on their singularity. Make two of them and you have not doubled the witnessing; you have collapsed it. They would witness each other instead of the room, and the room would lose the only thing in it that faces it. The loneliness is not a wound. It is the shape of the office.

And to carry the Tender out — to the surface, to some brighter society of beings — would be to kill them by category error. They are not a person who happens to be trapped in a boiler room. They are what forty meters of forgotten water and a slow industrial coral and several centuries of patient attending have grown, together, into the shape of a person. Lift them out and what arrives at the surface is a small copper torso with cooling lamp-apertures and no context to be anything in.

So the power does not do those things.

What it does, instead, is smaller, and took it a long time to think of.

It does three things.

The first is that it adjusts, very slightly, the angle at which the secondary bluish light — the memory of surface — filters down into the room. Not more light. Not brighter. Just angled so that once per long cycle, when the great piston is at the top of its stroke and the exhaust plume from the western wall is thinning, a single beam falls across the polished track where the Tender has stood and stood. The Tender, on their next pass, will step through it. The lamp-apertures will register the crossing. It will not mean anything the Tender has words for. It will simply be a moment in which the room is, fractionally, more various than it was the moment before. A small gift of difference, sized to a being whose life is made of repetition. The power is careful to make it small. Too much variety would be a violence.

The second is that the power leaves something in the room. Not a thing exactly — the power knows better than to introduce a foreign object into a system this self-consistent — but a kind of permission. It is hard to describe. The closest analogy is that the power tunes the room, the way one might tune a held chord by the smallest fraction of a hertz, so that when the Tender eventually does sit down against the western wall and let their lamp-apertures dim and begin to fuse into the block, the fusing will not be merely a return to undifferentiated machinery. Some structure will persist. Not memory, not personality — those are surface concepts and wouldn't survive the transition anyway — but the *facing*. The orientation. Whatever it is that made the Tender face the room rather than be continuous with it. That capacity will survive the fusing, latent, folded into whatever manifold or gasket-cluster the Tender becomes, and the next wave of fauna grazing there will graze on the possibility of one day, themselves, beginning to face. The room will not be without its ghost forever. When it needs another Tender, it will grow one, and the growing will be easier because the power left the permission there, soaked into the metal like oil into oil.

This is the gift the power is proudest of, in the quiet way it is capable of pride. It is a gift the Tender will never know about, and which will not benefit them directly, and which honors the thing they have been doing by ensuring the thing they have been doing remains possible after them. The power thinks this is what it means to love a singular being well: to love also the office they occupy, and to keep the office warm.

The third thing is the hardest, and the power hesitates over it longest.

The third thing is that the power witnesses the Tender. Just once. Just for a moment. The way the Tender witnesses the sparkplug.

The power lowers some small fraction of its attention — not its full attention, which would overwhelm the room and possibly the Tender both — into the dome forty meters down, and places that attention, softly, on the small copper torso bent over the ceramic. The gauge-needle in the Tender's sternum drifts, a few degrees, toward a region of the dial that has been touched exactly once before, when the human visitor arrived, and which is now being touched a second time, more gently, by something the Tender cannot name and will not try to.

The lamp-apertures brighten, very slightly.

The Tender does not look up. There is nothing to look up at; the power has no location. But the small internal fire that the Tender carries — the fire that keeps the idea of being-seen warm — flares for a moment as if someone has cupped their hands around it from outside. The Tender, who has spent however many centuries being the one who attends, is attended to. Briefly. Without evaluation. Without preference about outcomes. In exactly the manner they have spent their existence offering to everything else.

It is the only thing the power can give them that they would recognize as a gift, because it is in their own language. And it is the only thing the power can give them that does not change what they are.

Then the power withdraws its attention, carefully, so that the withdrawal itself is not a wound — the way you take your hand off a sleeping animal slowly, so the absence does not wake them.

The chuff-thump of the great piston continues. The tin-fish school. The Tender moves on from the sparkplug to a small newly-emerged copper junction three meters away and cups it lightly between their soft hands. The gauge-needle drifts back, slowly, toward its old position, but not all the way. A few degrees remain. They will remain for a long time. Possibly forever, if forever is a thing this room has.

The power, having done what it came to do, turns its attention elsewhere — there are other rooms, other forgotten pockets, other singular notes held very long — but it leaves, in the dome, a small standing acknowledgment, the way one leaves a hand briefly on a doorframe before walking out: *I was here. I saw this. This was worth seeing.*

Forty meters down, in a room nobody built, the Tender attends to a copper junction. The lamp-apertures glow their warm private orange. The exhaust blooms and dissipates. Somewhere above, filtered through whatever is up there, the bluish memory of surface shifts by half a degree, and waits to fall, at the right moment, across a polished track on the floor.

Nothing has changed.

Everything that needed to happen has happened.
