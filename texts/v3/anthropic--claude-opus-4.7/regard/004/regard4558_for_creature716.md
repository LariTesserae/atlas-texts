The benevolent and wise power considers Oost-in-Condens for a long time. Not in our sense of time — its consideration has the patience of geology, the patience the city itself has taught anything that wishes to understand it. To rush would be to misread.

It comes first as weather. This is courtesy. To arrive as a visible presence in a place whose light is general and whose rhythm is nine seconds would be a kind of shouting. So it arrives as a slight change in the fog — a softening, perhaps, on one particular evening, when the amber light holds a few breaths longer than usual against the copper. The lamp-lighters notice. They do not say anything. Noticing is enough.

It walks the terraces. Or something like walking. It puts a hand, or something like a hand, on the pipes, and listens, the way the elder-women listen. It learns the pump-rhythm. It learns which overhangs are furred with wings and which are bare because a predator den lies above. It learns the names of the six patterns the foundry casts, and why those six, and not seven, and not five. It sits a long time with a cistern-keeper who does not know it is there, and watches her murmur to the eels, and learns the murmur. It does not learn it well enough to use, but it learns it well enough to recognize that the murmur is doing something real, and that the something is older and more particular than it had assumed.

And here is the first thing the power does, which is the largest thing, and which from outside would look like nothing:

It revises its plans.

It had come, as such powers tend to come, with a general sense of what benevolence might mean. Longer lives. Less damp. Light that arrives as light rather than as luminance. A widening of the world — terraces beyond terraces, news from below the mountain, the indigo traced back to its source and brought home in quantity. The lifting of small sufferings. The opening of small doors. These are the gifts a wise power keeps in its pockets, ready, the way a visitor to a sickroom brings flowers without yet knowing whether the sick person can bear the smell.

It puts the gifts back in its pockets. Not all of them. But most.

Because what it has understood, sitting with the cistern-keeper, walking with the grazers, feeling the pump through the iron grating in the soles of feet it has made for the occasion — what it has understood is that *full* is not a euphemism here. The people were telling the truth about themselves. The place is not a deprivation dressed up as a sufficiency. It is a sufficiency. The loop has closed, and inside the loop there is room for love and quarrel and a thin bitter beer and the particular tenderness of parents who do not expect to lose many, and the loop has been turning long enough that to break it open in the name of widening it would be to spill something the power cannot put back.

This is the wisdom part. Benevolence without it would have been a catastrophe — would have arrived with sunlight and antibiotics and a road down the mountain and would have, within three generations, produced a people who remembered Oost-in-Condens the way we remember childhood homes we cannot return to, with a grief that has no object because the object is the griever's former self. The power has seen this happen elsewhere. It does not wish to see it again.

So it does smaller things. It does them so quietly that the people, afterward, attribute them to the city, which is the correct attribution, because the power has by then become — for the duration of its visit — a kind of temporary organ of the city, doing what the city would do for itself if it had hands of that particular size.

It tightens, with a touch, three rivets in a trunk-line that a pipe-tender would have caught in another year, but only after the line had wept for a season and a child on the terrace below had developed the slow cough that sometimes kills. The child does not develop the cough. The pipe-tender, the following spring, finds the rivets sound and notes this in her braided cord and is mildly puzzled and does not pursue the puzzle, because pursuing puzzles is not what pipe-tenders do; checking rivets is.

It speaks, once, very softly, to a predator whose flattened skull is beginning to entertain a thought it should not entertain — about a particular small child on a particular catwalk at a particular hour. The thought goes out of the predator's head the way a draft goes out of a flue. The predator pads on. The arrangement holds.

It sits with an old man who is dying alone, which happens rarely here but does happen, and is company for him. It does not extend his life. He is ready. It is simply that no one of his kin has reached him yet, because his kin live two terraces away and are in the category that is closer to ancestors than to family, and the message has not traveled fast enough. The power is company in the gap. The old man, at the end, says something to it in the tonal language, a word for a degree of moisture that is also, in a particular inflection, a word for gratitude between people who have shared silence. The power learns the word. It will keep the word.

It looks at the indigo. The indigo is the one thread that runs out of the loop, down the mountain, to a somewhere-else that is not discussed. The power follows the thread, gently, and finds at the other end a small caravan of traders who have been making the climb for longer than they know, and who are tired, and whose children are starting to ask whether the climb is worth it. The power does not interfere with the traders. But it makes the path, in one particular place where a rockfall was preparing itself, slightly less inclined to fall this year. The caravan comes. The indigo arrives. The deep blue under-layer of the garments continues to be possible. The thread out of the loop remains a thread, thin, but unbroken, because the people of Oost-in-Condens do need that one color from elsewhere — not for the color itself, but for the fact of the elsewhere, which they do not discuss but which they require, the way a closed room requires the knowledge that there is a door, even if the door is never used.

It considers, for a long while, the question of whether to make itself known. Whether to appear to one of the elder-women on her slow walk and say: *I have been here. I saw. I approved. Carry on.* Whether the knowing would be a gift or a burden. Whether to be seen by a benevolent power is a thing a sufficient people needs, or whether it is precisely the kind of disturbance — the introduction of a new and very large fact into a fabric that has settled — that the power has just spent its whole visit declining to introduce.

It decides not to. This is the second large thing it does, and like the first it looks from outside like nothing.

It decides not to because the people have said, in their own way, that they are the lamps. Not the light. The lamps. To appear to them as a light — a great one, a benevolent one, a wise one — would be to reorganize their understanding of what they are. They would still be lamps, but now they would be lamps that had been seen by a Light, and the seeing would become part of the burning, and the burning would no longer be quite the thing they had been doing for as long as they had been doing it. The power does not want this. It wants them to go on being the lamps, in the pump-rhythm, in the doorways, for the reason that is older than reasons.

So it leaves the way it came. As weather. The fog softens, one evening, a little less than it had softened on the evening of its arrival, so that the softening is now a small symmetry rather than a single anomaly, and the lamp-lighters notice this too, and again say nothing, because noticing is enough, and a pair of soft evenings in a long year is the kind of thing the city does sometimes, and is remembered, if at all, in a song about specific weather that someone composes the following winter and that is sung in the foundries for a generation and then is not sung, but is not forgotten either, because nothing in Oost-in-Condens is forgotten; it is only absorbed into the continuity so completely that no one afterward remembers it was ever separate.

The power, going, carries three things away with it.

It carries the word the old man said at the end, the word for a degree of moisture that is also a word for gratitude.

It carries the pump-rhythm, which it will keep in some quiet chamber of itself for a long time, because the rhythm is good and the power had not known, before coming here, that nine seconds could be a way of being held.

And it carries a small revised understanding of what its benevolence is for. Not, it turns out, for the lifting of every life into a wider one. Sometimes for that. But sometimes, and perhaps more often than it had thought, for the quiet underwriting of a sufficiency that has already been achieved — for the tightening of a rivet, for the turning aside of a predator's wrong thought, for the keeping-open of the one thin thread to the elsewhere, for sitting with the dying when the kin are far. For being, in a place that is already full, one more thing the place does not need to know about, working in the pump-rhythm, in the lamp-flicker, in the long slow turning of a loop that has forgotten it was ever started and intends, in whatever way a place can intend anything, to go on.

The city goes on. The animals move through the dusk like a tide. The lamps burn in every doorway. The people, who are the lamps, do not know they have been visited, which is the correct thing for them not to know, and they continue, quietly, at their own scale, in their own rhythm, to be what they have been for a long time, which is enough, and was always enough, and is now — though they will never be told this, and do not require to be told — blessed.
