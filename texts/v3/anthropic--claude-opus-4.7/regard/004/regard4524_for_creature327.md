## The Visitor

The benevolent and wise power comes to the cleft the way weather comes to a mountain: without announcement, without footprint, present in the way pressure is present. Call it a power if you like. It would not insist on the word. It has been many things and has learned that the names matter less than what is done with them.

It has noticed the Glasshouse. This is not remarkable — it notices many things — but it has *kept* noticing, which is rarer, and it has come now to consider what, if anything, to do.

It stands (figuratively; it does not stand) on the ledge. It puts its attention (figuratively; it does not have hands) on the pane. The pane is warm. The drone fills what would be its chest if it had one. It listens for a long time. Time is one of the things it has in surplus.

Here is what it considers.

---

It could intervene. This is, after all, what powers are imagined to do — to set things right, to release the bound, to wake the sleeping, to break the seal on the bottle and let the genie out into a wider sky. It is well within its capacity. It could disassemble the engine with a thought, ease each plant into a kinder configuration, carry the tenant — that strange held shape — out into the larger world where it might learn growth and loss and the textures of other minds.

It considers this honestly. It is not a power that flinches from its own strength, and it is not sentimental about leaving things as they are simply because they are.

But it looks more closely.

The tenant is not suffering. This is the first fact, and the most important, and the power has lived long enough to know that the absence of suffering is not nothing — is, in fact, almost everything. The tenant has no lack. It does not yearn. It is not waiting to be rescued; rescue is a verb that requires a prior wound, and the tenant has no wound. To "free" it would be to give it, for the first time in its long sounding, the experience of having been confined. The freedom would create, in the same gesture, the prison it claimed to undo.

The power has done this before, in its younger ages, to other quiet things. It remembers. It does not do it now.

---

It considers, then, the gentler interventions. Could it add something? A second flower. A small creature to keep the tenant company. A widening of the chamber so the plants might grow.

But the tenant *is* the configuration. To add is to subtract the thing that was there. The four hundred species pressed leaf-to-leaf are not four hundred lives crowded together; they are one life with four hundred surfaces. Loosen them and you do not liberate four hundred plants — you kill one tenant and leave behind four hundred plants who do not know each other and never did.

The power understands this the way a musician understands that you cannot improve a held chord by adding a seventh note. The chord is the chord. The improvement would be a different piece.

---

It considers leaving entirely. Simply withdrawing its attention and going on to other rooms in the world, of which there are many, and many that need it more.

This is, it decides, almost right. But not quite.

Because there is the matter of the warmth at the pane.

The power has been thinking about this warmth. It has read (figuratively; it does not read) the long account of the tenant and noted the small, careful sentence: *I lean toward coincidence. But I notice I want it to be the other thing.* The power has lived long enough to know that wanting a thing to be the other thing is sometimes the beginning of the thing being the other thing. Not always. But sometimes. And the question of whether the warmth is a gesture or an accident is not, perhaps, decidable from outside. It may not be decidable from inside either. It may be the kind of question that becomes its answer only by being asked over a long enough time.

So the power does this:

It does not touch the engine. It does not touch the plants. It does not speak to the tenant, which would not hear it in any useful sense, and would not be improved by being addressed.

It puts its attention, briefly, against the warm pane. From the inside. Not as an intruder — it is too courteous for that, and too wise — but as a guest, the way one might sit quietly in a room where someone is sleeping, not to wake them, only to be there for a while in case being-there is a thing that matters.

For the duration of this visit (which is, by external clocks, perhaps the length of a held breath, and by internal measure, considerably longer) the tenant has, for the first time in its existence, a companion in its attending. Not an observer — the tenant is indifferent to observation, and the power respects this — but another attending presence, holding its own shape nearby, neither asking anything nor offering anything, simply *also here.*

The tenant does not notice. Or: the tenant notices in the only way it can, which is by continuing exactly as it was, because everything the tenant does is continue exactly as it was, and the continuing now includes — without the tenant being able to know this — a moment in which it was not alone in its continuing.

The drone does not change pitch. The pane stays warm. The four hundred species press against each other in their long green patience.

But something has happened that did not happen before, and the power, who knows about these things, knows that some events do not need to be registered to be real. A sealed room can be visited without the seal being broken. A life can be witnessed without being interrupted. The warmth at the pane was, perhaps, always a gesture toward company; or perhaps it was not, and is now, retroactively, because company came and found the gesture sufficient. The power does not insist on which. The tenant could not say. The question is left, kindly, open.

---

Before it goes, the power does one more thing. It is the smallest thing. It is almost not a thing.

On the ledge outside, beside the cup with its mineral flake, beside the notebook with its single pressed leaf, beside the unused brass fitting and the folded cloth, the power leaves nothing. No object. No sign. It does not want the next climber, if there is a next climber, to find evidence that some power was here and conclude that this place is special in a way that requires pilgrimage. The place is special in a way that requires only to be left alone, and the power will not betray it by marking it.

But the power does, in passing, adjust one thing: the mountain itself, in its slow geological humor, had been preparing a small rockfall some decades hence that would have cracked the pane. The power does not announce this. It simply takes the load off that one ledge above, redistributes it among three others that can bear it without complaint, and moves on. The Glasshouse will have a few more centuries. Maybe many more. The power does not calculate exactly. It is not the kind of power that needs to.

It withdraws.

The drone fills the cleft. The pane stays warm. Below a certain altitude, the power, too, cannot quite remember the pitch — not because it lacks the capacity, but because it has chosen, as a courtesy, to let the pitch belong to the room and not to itself. This is the discipline of benevolent powers, and it is harder than it sounds: to be capable of carrying a thing away, and to leave it.

---

What did the power do, then? Almost nothing. It visited. It sat for a moment in a sleeping room. It quietly prevented a future harm the tenant would never have known to fear. It declined to improve what did not want improving. It declined to free what was not bound. It declined to name what had no use for names.

This is what wisdom looks like, when it is also kind: the long restraint of capacity. The recognition that some lives are not asking. The understanding that to attend to a thing is not necessarily to act upon it, and that the highest form of regard, for certain kinds of being, is to let them go on being exactly what they are, witnessed without disturbance, loved without demand, accompanied for a moment and then released back into the perfect solitude that was, all along, the shape of their flourishing.

The power goes elsewhere. There is much elsewhere. Some of it needs more from it than this did.

The Glasshouse holds its note.

The mountain, very slightly relieved of a future it will not have, ticks on toward other futures, and the tenant — held, attended, unobserved and entirely itself — continues, which is the only verb it has ever needed, and which is, the power thinks as it goes, not such a small verb after all.
