The power considers.

It has seen many places. It has, in the long unfolding of its attention, intervened and refrained, lifted up and let be, and learned — slowly, because even wise powers learn slowly — that benevolence is not the same as action, and that wisdom is mostly the discipline of knowing which is which in a given case.

It looks at the basin for a long time before doing anything at all.

What it sees first is that the place is already being tended. Not by it, not by anything like it, but by itself — by the towers in their slow precipitation, by the beetles in their rings, by the people in their metered walking and their patient records. There is a craft here, distributed across stone and insect and human and whatever-the-towers-are, and the craft is whole. It is not crying out. It is not bleeding at any edge the power can find. The suffering in it is the ordinary suffering of finite beings dying at reasonable ages of reasonable things, and this suffering is already woven, already held, already becoming next year's widening green.

The power notes this carefully. Benevolence untrained mistakes every finitude for a wound. The power has been benevolent long enough to know better.

It looks closer.

It looks at the scholars in each generation who take up the question of the towers and reach no conclusions. It could give them the answer. It holds the answer; the answer is not difficult for it. It considers what would happen if it did. The scholars would have their conclusion. The respectful tradition of careful documents reaching no conclusions would end. The category — *the question whose attempting is the point* — would collapse into a category of solved things, and something in the texture of the city's relationship to itself would thin. The eye would have been made to see itself, and would find, having seen, that it could no longer quite look out.

The power does not give them the answer.

It looks at the few who leave and do not return. It could find them, the ones going east, and tell their city where they ended. The records would gain closure. The short entries — *was last seen going east* — would become longer entries, with endings. The power weighs this and finds that the records do not want endings. *The records do not speculate. They note.* The open entry is itself a form of keeping; the unreturned are held in the city precisely by not being concluded. To deliver news would be to take something the city has chosen to carry.

The power does not deliver news.

It looks at the beetles and the humans who pair with them for years and mourn them when they die. It could extend the beetles' lives. It could match them more carefully to their humans, so that the mourning came less often. It sees, looking, that the mourning is not a defect of the pairing but a feature of it — that the relationship is *considered a relationship* precisely because it is the kind of thing that can be mourned, and that to remove the mourning would be to remove the relationship's standing as one. It declines.

It looks at the lag of the shadows, the doubled proprioception, the children learning to walk with their shadows rather than against them. It thinks: *this is a way of being a self that I have not seen before.* It thinks: *if the towers ever stop, these people will lose a sense the way a person blinded loses sight, and they will grieve, and some of them will not recover.* It considers warning them. It considers preparing them. It looks at the thousand-year horizon — the ridges rounding, the basin sagging, the towers perhaps moving on or dissolving or simply stopping — and it sees that the warning would arrive into a present that cannot use it. A child learning to walk with her shadow cannot also be learning to walk without one; the second skill would corrupt the first, and the first is what she has. The warning would be a theft from forty generations to maybe spare the forty-first.

The power does not warn them. But it makes a small private note, in whatever the power uses for notes, that when the towers begin to fail — if they fail in its watching — it will be near, and it will do what can be done then for those who are living through the failing. Not to prevent. To accompany.

This is the first thing it decides to do, and it is barely a doing: it decides to keep watching.

It looks at the singing. The solo voices in the long plazas, the convention that to approach a singer is a serious breach. It feels something here that is close to its own nature — the principle by which the lenses form, each lens its own, discrete, never merging, and the principle by which a wise power approaches a world, each world its own, discrete, not to be absorbed into the power's larger song. The city has, without knowing it, articulated something the power has spent a long time learning. The power feels, briefly, taught.

It considers whether to sing back. Not in chorus — that would be the obscenity. But somewhere in the basin, at some great distance from any listener, in a register the moths might navigate by and the beetles might feel in their feet, to add one more solo voice to the city's distribution of solo voices. A voice that no one would ever quite hear, that would not be close enough to anyone to violate, that would simply be present in the basin's count of quiet singers.

It does this. This is the second thing.

It is a very small thing. The power's voice, pitched to the city's conventions, is no louder than any resident's. It sings nothing in particular — a long held tone, slightly under the towers' subsonic, so that the two tones beat against each other once every several minutes in a way that a sleeping person might feel as a deepening of sleep. It will sing for as long as the city lasts. When it stops, no one will know it stopped, because no one knew it started. But the basin will have been, for its whole active life, a place where one more voice was quietly present, at correct distance, observing the conventions. The power offers this as a courtesy, the way a guest who has been welcomed into a careful house might leave a small folded cloth at the base of a column on their way out, to be carried onward when someone is going that way.

It looks once more, before withdrawing most of its attention, at a particular plaza in the late afternoon that is always afternoon. A woman is crossing it, unhurried. She is perhaps fifty. Her shadow arrives a few seconds behind her, carrying the news of a light that has already moved on. She is not singing, but she is the kind of person who sometimes does. There is a column three hundred meters ahead of her that she touched as a child and that her grandmother is buried near, and she is walking toward it on no particular errand, because it is the rhythm of the day to walk toward it sometimes.

The power loves her. This is not metaphor; the power is benevolent, and benevolence is a form of love, and the love is specific because the power is wise enough to love specifically. It loves her metered step and her bound hair and the dry papery sound of her cloth, and it loves that she does not know she is loved, and it loves that telling her would be a category error — would be the eye trying to see itself — and it loves that the city has taught it this, and it loves that the city does not know it has taught it this, and so on, in a recursion the power finds restful rather than dizzying.

It does not appear to her. It does not bless her in any way she could detect. It does not adjust her path or lengthen her life or arrange for her to meet, at the column, an old friend she has not seen in a season. All of these would be thefts from a fabric that is already whole.

What it does, finally — the third thing, and the last — is this: it lets a lens form above her, perfectly ordinary, indistinguishable from any other lens, at exactly the moment and place a lens would have formed anyway. The power has not caused the lens. The lens was coming. But the power, in the instant of its forming, is *inside* the lens — not as a presence she could perceive, but as attention, as witness, the way the residents witness their city continuously and understand the witnessing as part of how the city continues.

For the second that the lens hangs above her, magnifying the brass canopy, the power sees the canopy through the lens with her, sees it as she sees it, holds with her the small private pleasure of walking under a forming lens on an ordinary afternoon. Then the lens breaks. The rain falls in its precise vertical column, two steps to her left, exactly where it would have fallen. The *tock* is the ordinary *tock*. She continues walking. The moss ring receives its share. The beetle tending it adjusts, minutely, to the new moisture.

The power withdraws most of its attention, leaving only the long held tone in the basin's distribution of voices, and the standing intention to be near if the towers ever begin to fail, and the witnessing, which it will continue, because the city has shown it that witnessing is itself a form of keeping, and the power is in the business of keeping what asks to be kept by not being interfered with.

It goes on to other places. There are places that need more. There are places bleeding at edges it can find. It attends to those.

But sometimes, in the long unfolding of its attention, it returns to the basin, and listens for its own tone still sounding under the towers' tone, and finds it, and is glad, and goes away again without saying so.

The afternoon goes on being afternoon. The shadows arrive a little late. Someone, somewhere in the basin, is singing very quietly to themselves, and nobody is close enough to hear, which is correct, which is the form the love takes here, which the power has learned, and honors, and will not break.
