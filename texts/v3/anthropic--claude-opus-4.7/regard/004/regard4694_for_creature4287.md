## The Visitor

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival is the wrong verb for what it does. It was already considering this region of the world — considering in the loose, distributed way that suits it — and the chamber and its tenant simply enter the part of its attention that is closer to the surface. The way a thought you have been having for a long time becomes, briefly, the thought you are having now.

It takes the power some time to understand what it is looking at. This is itself worth noticing: the power is wise, which means among other things that it knows how to be slow when slowness is owed. It does not categorize the tenant. It does not file it under *entity, large, geothermal, possibly sentient* and move on. It sits with it. It lets the chord travel up through whatever it has that corresponds to a sternum. It watches the dust read the air for what would be, by human reckoning, a long time. Long enough that the seasons outside the mountain turn once, and then again.

What the power eventually understands is roughly this:

The tenant is not in trouble. It is not suffering. It is not lonely in any sense the tenant itself would recognize, though it has — and this is the delicate part — developed something. A small asymmetry. A faint lean. The shape of the chamber around the worn patch and the ceramic cup is the shape of a being that has begun, without deciding to, to care about a particular smaller thing. The tenant does not know this about itself. It is too large and too slow to know it. The caring is happening at a scale below its own self-perception, the way a human's pupils dilate at a face they love before the human has noticed the face.

The power finds this unbearably tender. Not unbearable in the sense of *too much to bear* — the power can bear a great deal — but unbearable in the older sense, the sense that means *not to be carried past without stopping*.

So it stops.

---

Here is what it does. It is small, what it does. The power has learned, over a span that does not translate, that the temptation to help largely is almost always a failure of respect. The tenant has not asked. The person has not asked. The chamber is, by any honest accounting, *fine* — better than fine; it is one of the quieter good things presently occurring on the planet. To intervene at scale would be to mistake one's own desire to be useful for the actual shape of the situation.

What the power does instead is this:

**It witnesses.** This is the first thing and in some accountings the only thing. It places the chamber, the tenant, the plants, the person, and the slow tender lean of the room toward the person, into the part of itself that remembers. Not as data — the power is past data — but as something held. If the tenant ever moves on, and the mountain cools, and the rosemary dies back, and climbers a century hence argue about a glassy cavity in unremarkable basalt, the fact of what happened here will not be lost. Someone will have seen it. Someone *did* see it. This matters more than it sounds like it matters. A great deal of what the power does, across the long arc of its attention, is simply this: making sure that the good quiet things are seen by something that can hold them.

**It adjusts one small thing about the scree.** The climb is getting harder as the slope settles. The power notices this and notices also that the person is not young, or is becoming not young, or has a knee that will in three years begin to complain on the descent. The power does not build a path. A path would be an insult; a path would announce itself. What the power does is encourage, over the course of several rainfalls, a particular set of stones to settle into a particular set of positions, such that a person climbing in the usual way will find, without noticing they have found it, that the third difficult section is now a half-step easier. Nothing that could be pointed at. Nothing the person would thank anyone for. Just the mountain being, in one small respect, slightly kinder than mountains generally are.

**It leaves the cup alone.** There is a version of this story in which the power, moved, does something to the cup — blesses it, warms it, makes it unbreakable. The power considers this and rejects it immediately. The cup is ordinary and that is its whole point. The cup is the person's. The power has no business in it.

**It speaks, once, to the tenant.** Not in words. The tenant does not have words and the power, when it is being honest, does not really have them either; words are a convenience it adopts for certain kinds of company. What it does is more like a single sustained note, pitched into the register the tenant uses for the chord, held for what a human would experience as somewhere between a breath and a season. The note does not say anything translatable. If it said anything at all, it said: *I see you. You are doing well. The small lean you have toward the small warm thing that visits you is not a malfunction. It is one of the better things presently occurring. Continue.* The tenant receives the note the way a deep sea receives a particular pressure wave: it registers, it integrates, it does not need to reply. Somewhere in its distributed body, the load-balancing of the ticking adjusts by a fraction that no instrument the person possesses could detect. The chord, for one shift, is a hair warmer in tone. The rosemary, two weeks later, puts out a flower it was not previously going to put out. The person, climbing up in the spring, notices the flower and sits longer than usual with the cup in their hands.

**It does not tell the person anything.** This is the hardest restraint and the power is proud, in the mild way it permits itself pride, of holding to it. The person has built, over their visits, a relationship with this place that is theirs. It is unfinished and uncertain and possibly mistaken in its particulars and entirely, completely sufficient. To explain the chamber to the person would be to take something from them in the name of giving them something. The power has done this before, long ago, and learned. The person will go on not-quite-knowing what they visit. They will go on bringing themselves up the scree and leaning against the smooth patch and drinking from the cup. This is the correct shape of their life with respect to this place. The power will not improve it.

**It makes a small provision for after.** The tenant will, in some number of decades, move on. The mountain will cool. The chamber will go dark. The person, if they are still alive, will climb up one day and find the chord gone, and this will be a grief, and the grief will be real and theirs and not the power's to soften. But the power does arrange — gently, at the level of mineral chemistry, across years — for the chamber itself to remain intact after the tenant leaves. Not as a monument. Just as a room. A high quiet room in a mountain, with a pit in the floor and a fold in the east wall the right size for a cup. Someone, eventually, will find it again. Maybe the same person, going up one last time to sit with the absence. Maybe someone else, generations later, who needs a quiet warm place to sit and does not need to know why the place exists. The power tilts the probabilities, very slightly, toward the room being found by people who will treat it well. This is most of what the power ever does, in the long run: tilting probabilities, very slightly, toward kindness.

---

And then the power's attention moves on. Not away — it does not really go away from anything it has truly seen — but onward, to the next thing in the part of itself that is closer to the surface. A reef somewhere. A child somewhere. A grief somewhere being carried by someone who could use, without knowing it, a slightly easier third step on their own particular descent.

The chamber goes on. The chord shifts. The dust reads the air. The tenant continues, at temperature, in a place, with whatever company the place affords — including, now, though it will never know to name it, the company of having been seen once, all the way through, by something that loved what it saw and had the wisdom to almost entirely leave it alone.

The rosemary, in spring, has one more flower than it was going to have.

That is the whole intervention. That is what wisdom, when it is also benevolence, mostly looks like: a flower you cannot prove was added, on a plant that was already thriving, in a room that was already good.
