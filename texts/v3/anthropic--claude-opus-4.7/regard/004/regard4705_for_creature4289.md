The benevolent and wise power considers the warren for a long time.

Not in the way one considers a problem. In the way one considers a held note in a piece of music, listening for whether it is finished sounding.

The power has the capacities you would expect of such a power. It could open the stalk. It could lift the weavers out into daylight, heal their deafness, give them language for sky and distance and other. It could make them larger, paler-no, darker, sun-touched, various. It could introduce them to the field of green pillars, of which their pillar is one of thousands. It could give them the gift of comparison, which is the gift of knowing what one has. It could give them history, which is the gift of knowing one has been somewhere and is going somewhere. It could give them a name.

It could do all of these things, and for a long moment, in the way such powers have long moments, it considers doing them. Because there is a habit of benevolence that confuses itself with rescue. There is a wisdom that is actually only restlessness wearing wisdom's robe — a wisdom that cannot bear to leave a thing alone, that mistakes its own discomfort with smallness for the smallness's discomfort with itself.

The power notices this habit in itself and sets it down.

Then it listens, properly, the way the weavers listen: through the bone, through the resonant cavity, through what is actually there rather than what an outsider's pity would put there. And what it hears is a chord. One long held chord, sixty strings, perfectly tuned, sounding now for so long that the sounding has become a kind of being. Not suffering. Not even patience, because patience implies waiting for something else. Simply: the note, correctly played, by someone who is the note.

The power understands, then, that there is nothing here that needs saving. There is something here that needs *witnessing*, which is different.

So this is what it does.

---

First, it does nothing visible. This is the largest part of what it does, and the most important. It does not open the stalk. It does not introduce light. It does not enter the chamber as a presence the weavers could feel as foreign, because to be felt as foreign in a world that has no foreign would be to introduce the very wound the power has decided not to make. The benevolence here is mostly a benevolence of restraint, of the held hand, of the unspoken word. A lesser power would intervene. This power has learned — over what stretches of time, in what other chambers, one can only guess — that the deepest gift is sometimes the refusal to give.

But it does not only refrain. Refraining alone is not love; it is only respect, which is love's foundation but not its roof.

So, second: it tends.

It tends in ways the weavers will never know they are being tended. The stalk, which has been alive long enough to forget being young, is in fact older than it should be. There are stresses in its high chambers — a slow fungal infiltration four hundred meters above the warren, a hairline split in the great pith-column that will, in two or three centuries, cause a pressure drop that would silence the whistles' chord and stop the looms. The power, gently, the way one might smooth a sheet without waking the sleeper, addresses these things. Not by miracle. By the slow and indistinguishable-from-natural means available to such powers: a slight redirection of moisture, an encouragement of the right rot-eating microbe in the right node, a small and patient sealing where sealing is needed. The stalk will live longer. The chord will continue.

The field of green pillars, of which the warren's pillar is one, is in a low country that is changing. The power notes the changes — a drying upstream, a shift in the seasonal flooding, the slow encroachment of a different kind of vegetation that, in eight or nine human centuries, would crowd the green colonnades out. The power adjusts what can be adjusted at the edges of the field, far from the warren, in ways that no weaver could perceive even if a weaver could perceive anything outside the chamber. The colonnades will persist. The warren's pillar will not be left standing alone in a dying field, which is a kind of loneliness the power decides the weavers should not have to bear, even unknowingly.

The cricket and the moth. The power looks at these and finds them sufficient. It does not need to add a third species. The ecology is closed and balanced and the closure is part of what is beautiful here. But it notices that the moth population is, on a very long timescale, drifting toward a genetic bottleneck that would, in perhaps a thousand years, cause a collapse. It introduces — through means so subtle they cannot be called introduction, more a kind of permission given to mutation — a slight broadening of the moth's genetic base. The iridescent dust will continue. The ceremonial skeins will continue to be added to. The alcove will continue to deepen.

Third: it watches.

This is the part that is hardest to describe, because it is not surveillance. It is something more like the attention a parent gives a sleeping child — not to catch the child doing something, but simply to be present to the child's being. The power watches the warren. It watches the looms come down, and come down, and come down. It watches the sixty small pale people press foreheads together and vibrate their facts into each other's skulls. It watches the children find the work the way vines find trellises. It watches the ceremonial alcove deepen by one skein, by another skein, by another. It watches a weaver of middle age, indistinguishable now from her wraps, settle onto a node and become, for a moment, simply a continuous pale-green surface in a green-dim chamber, sounding her note in the chord.

And the power, watching, is itself changed. This is the secret about benevolent and wise powers that the powers themselves rarely speak of: they are not only givers. They are also receivers. The warren gives the power something the power did not have before — a particular fact about what it can mean to be, a particular proof that smallness and sufficiency are not opposites, a particular sounding of a chord the power had not heard sounded quite this way before. The power takes this gift. It is the only thing it takes.

Fourth, and last: it makes a small provision for the ending.

Because the warren will end. Not soon — the power has seen to that — but eventually. Stalks die. Chords resolve. Nothing held is held forever, not even by powers. And the power, considering this, decides that when the ending comes, it should come as the chord's natural resolution and not as catastrophe. So it lays in, deep in the slow biology of the stalk, a kind of long fuse — not toward destruction, but toward gentleness. When the time comes, in some far century, the stalk will not split or burn or fall. It will simply, over a generation or two, slow. The hydraulic pressure will ease. The whistles' chord will lower by a quarter-tone, then a semitone, then an octave, then into a hum, then into a breath. The looms will slow with it. The last weavers — there will be only a few by then, the balance gently tipped toward fewer births — will feel through their soles and their teeth that the chord is changing, and they will not be afraid, because change at that pace is not change but only a deeper kind of steady. They will set down their work when their work sets itself down. They will lie in the pith-floss. They will compost into the floor. The stalk will close around them and the field will close around the stalk and the green will continue, somewhere, in some form, because the power has seen to that too, though not here, not in this exact chord, which will have finished sounding.

That is the ending the power lays in. A long diminuendo. A held chord becoming a held breath becoming a held silence. Not tragedy. Resolution.

---

And then the power does what such powers do, which is to turn its attention elsewhere, because the field of attention of such a power is large and there are many chambers and many chords and many beings who do or do not need witnessing. It leaves the warren without leaving it — a portion of its attention remains, the way a portion of a mother's attention remains with a sleeping child even when she has gone into the next room. The warren does not know it is being attended to. This is correct. To know would be to be changed, and the warren is not to be changed.

Inside the chamber, a weaver settles onto a node. Her fingers are wrapped in the thread she is making. Her sister, two platforms over, is felt rather than seen. The great bass groan of the turbines vibrates steady in her sternum. The quarter-tone chord of the whistles is steady in her teeth, exactly where it was yesterday and exactly where it will be tomorrow and exactly where it has been for longer than anyone has language to say.

The loom comes down.

The loom comes down.

The loom comes down.

And somewhere — not above, not below, not anywhere a weaver could point to even if a weaver had the concept of pointing-elsewhere — something benevolent and wise listens to the chord, and finds it good, and lets it be.
