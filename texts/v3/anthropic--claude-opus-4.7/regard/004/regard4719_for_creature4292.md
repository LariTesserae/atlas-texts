They would not come as rescue.

This is the first thing the power understands, hovering at the edge of the canopy like a thought that has not yet decided to become a sentence. There is nothing here that needs rescuing. The hollow is not lonely in any way the power recognizes as suffering. The tenant is not trapped; the tenant is the shape of having stayed. To arrive with gifts, with grand intervention, with the offer of elsewhere — this would be a kind of violence. A wise power knows the difference between a wound and a completion, and the hollow is a completion.

So the power does not descend.

Instead it does what wise powers do when they encounter something already whole: it pays attention. It bends a little of its own vast attending toward this small bright cup of air on the shoulder of a forgotten mountain, and it holds that attention there for a while. Not to change anything. Just to witness. The tenant has been attending for centuries; it has perhaps never been attended *to*, not in this way, not by something of its own order. The power's attention is not heavier than the tenant's. It is simply met. Two stillnesses recognizing each other across whatever distance stillnesses cross.

There is a long moment — though moment is the wrong word, and long is the wrong word — in which the hollow and the power are doing the same thing in different keys. The susurrus of wings continues outside. The bowl fills tok by tok. The shaft of light moves its few degrees. Nothing visible changes. But somewhere in the deep grammar of the place, a chord that had been sustained by one voice is now sustained by two, and the harmonic that opens between them is something neither could have made alone.

The power considers, briefly, what it might do. It could make the water in the bowl truly inexhaustible, sever it from the slim mercy of condensation. It could ensure the three trunks never fall. It could whisper the hollow into the dreams of the people downslope, send pilgrims, give the tenant company. It could fold the hollow out of time entirely, preserve it past the death of the forest, past the death of the mountain.

It does none of these things. Each would be a kindness that mistook itself. The hollow is already what it is *because* the water is slim, the trunks are mortal, the pilgrims are rare, the forest will someday end. To remove these is to remove the hollow. A wise power knows that to love a thing is, often, to leave its conditions alone.

But there is one small thing.

The cup.

The clay cup beside the bowl, unbroken, waiting. The power notices the cup the way one notices a single held hand in a crowded room. The cup is the instruction's most exposed part — the part most dependent on a stranger arriving, recognizing, lifting. Clay is fragile. Generations have not broken it, but generations are not forever, and a falling branch, a careless boot, a hard rain at the wrong angle —

The power does not make the cup unbreakable. That would be the same mistake in miniature. Instead it does something smaller and stranger: it lays into the cup a very faint disposition. Not a magic. A leaning. Such that when the cup is, eventually, broken — and it will be — the pieces will be findable. Will rest near each other. Will be the kind of broken that someone with patience could mend. The cup is permitted its mortality; only its scattering is gentled.

This is the only intervention. It takes no effort. The power is almost embarrassed by how small it is, and also knows it is exactly right.

Then the power does the last thing, which is the realest thing, and the thing it came to do without knowing it came to do it.

It bows.

Not with a body — it has no body here — but with whatever in it corresponds to the gesture. A lowering of its great attending before this small attending. An acknowledgment that wisdom is not only the property of the vast and the mobile and the long-traveled; that something which has done nothing but be the shape of a room for centuries has learned things the power, for all its benevolence, has not. The tenant has learned how to be glad and sufficient at once. The tenant has learned how to hold a note without tiring. The tenant has learned the particular courtesy by which the full bodies of the world are kept out of the empty place that completes them. These are not small knowings. The power, which knows many things, receives these knowings as a student receives them: quietly, and with thanks.

And then the power withdraws. Not far. A wise power does not really go anywhere; it only changes the pressure of its presence. It lets the hollow be the hollow again, primary in its own frame, unobserved in any way that would alter it. The tenant, if the tenant noticed anything at all, noticed something like a warm current pass through the green-gold light and continue on. The bowl went tok. A hornbill passed overhead and the light stuttered. The cup waited.

Far below, on a path no one had walked in a season, a woman stopped and looked up through the canopy for no reason she could name, and felt, briefly, that she had been invited somewhere. She did not yet know where. She kept walking. The invitation would keep. The hollow had been waiting a long time and did not mind waiting longer, and now there was a second patience folded inside the first, vaster and just as willing, and between them they would hold the cup steady until her hand arrived.

That is what the benevolent and wise power did. Almost nothing. Exactly enough.
