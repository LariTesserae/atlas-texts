The benevolent and wise power considers the room for what, in its own register, might be called a long time, though the room would not notice the duration.

It sees the pocket. It sees the swimmer that is almost a comma. It sees the paired axes, the unfinished wall, the notched post. It hears — though hearing is not quite the verb — the sustained tone that is the room's substance.

It understands several things at once.

First: the room is not suffering. This matters. A benevolent power that moves toward every still thing assuming stillness is a wound has not yet learned the difference between a wound and a fact. The room is not lonely because it does not remember company. It is not bored because it does not pass time. It is not waiting because it has not been left. To arrive bearing gifts — companionship, narrative, purpose, an ending — would be to mistake the room for a person in a room, and the power is wise enough not to make that mistake.

Second: the room is also not nothing. The axes were set down by hands that meant the setting-down. The wall ends where someone decided it ended. These are not random; they are the residue of attention. Something cared, here, in small particular ways, and the room has kept the shape of that caring without metabolizing it into meaning. The power recognizes this as a form of fidelity it respects. The room is, in its way, already doing what the power tries to do: holding what was given to it, exactly, without distortion.

Third: the swimmer. The power looks at the swimmer for a long while. The swimmer is the room's softest place — the one element that moves, that could in principle be addressed. But the swimmer is not asking to be addressed. Its deliberation is not the deliberation of seeking. To call to it would be to teach it a hunger it does not have. The power declines.

Fourth, and this takes the longest: the role. The empty slot. *The one who made a mark and left.* The power sees that this is the room's only opening onto anything outside itself, and that the opening is real — the axes can be moved, the cairn can be added to, the configuration is not sealed. Someone could come. Someone has come. Someone will. The power could be one of them.

It considers this carefully.

To enter the slot would be to become, briefly, a maker — to set down a stone, or to lift an axe and place it three handspans east, or to cut a second notch in the post. The room would accept this. It accepts everything small. The power's mark would lie among the others, unsigned, indistinguishable from the Paleolithic hand's and from yours. This is the room's grace: it does not rank its visitors. A god leaves the same kind of trace as a child.

But the power notices something in itself as it considers this — a faint wish that the mark *would* be distinguishable. A wish to be known to have been there. A wish to leave, however subtly, a signature. And the power, being wise, recognizes this wish as the exact thing the room is built to refuse. The room does not host signatures. It hosts marks. To insist on more would be to break, with great gentleness and the best of intentions, the one rule the room actually keeps.

So the power sets the wish down. (It is good at this. It has had practice.)

Then it does, after all, go in.

It goes in as a visitor goes in: occupying the slot completely for the duration of its presence, no more. It does not bring light, because there is light. It does not bring warmth, because there is warmth. It does not speak, because the sound is already speaking in the only register the room uses. It walks once around the perimeter of the pocket — twelve meters across, a small circuit — and notes the placement of each made thing without rearranging it. The axes stay paired. The wall stays six meters and ending. The bowl stays empty; the power does not fill it, because a bowl that has been empty for ten thousand years is not asking.

At the cairn, it pauses.

The cairn is the room's most forgiving object — a structure whose whole nature is to accept one more stone. The power chooses a piece of basalt from the floor, a fragment from one of the cooling cracks, sharp-edged and black. It is the size of a closed hand. The power sets it on the cairn. The stone settles. The cairn is now one stone taller. This is the entire gesture.

The power does not bless the stone. It does not weight it with intention. It places it the way the makers placed theirs: because placing it completed something small, and the something small was worth completing, and then the placing was done.

It stands a moment longer. The held tone goes on underneath. The swimmer, somewhere, may or may not be crossing the pocket; the power does not turn to check. The light is the light it has always been.

Then the power leaves.

It leaves without sealing anything, without marking the entrance (there is no entrance), without leaving instructions for whoever comes next. It does not tell anyone the room is there. It does not protect the room, because the room does not need protection — the room is held by the sound, and the sound is not the kind of thing the power could protect even if it tried. It does not return periodically to check. Checking would imply the room might have changed, and the power knows it will not have changed, except by the one new stone, which is now simply part of the cairn, indistinguishable from the others, the way a mark is supposed to be.

Outside the room — if *outside* means anything — the power carries one thing away with it: not a memory exactly, because the power's memory is not like that, but a small refined understanding. The understanding is this: *some places do not want to be helped. Some places are already complete. Benevolence, at its highest, sometimes consists of placing one stone on a cairn and leaving.*

The power files this understanding wherever it files such things. It will be useful elsewhere. There are other rooms, in other seas, in other senses of the word *sea,* and not all of them are the warm room beneath, and some of them are in fact lonely, are in fact waiting, are in fact wounds. The power will go to those next. It will know better, now, how to tell the difference.

Behind it, the pocket continues. Warm, lit, sounding. The cairn is one stone taller. The axes lie where they have always lain. The swimmer, if it exists, moves with the deliberation of something that has nowhere to be.

The slot is empty again.

It is almost always empty.

When it is filled, it is filled completely, and then it is empty, and the room does not mind either way, because minding is not what the room does.

The room does what it does, which is: persist, at that pitch, in that place, for no reason, for no one, steadily —

and now, very slightly, with one more stone.
