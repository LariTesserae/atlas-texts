There is a power that has noticed.

Call it benevolent because it wishes nothing harm. Call it wise because it has watched long enough to know that wishing is not the same as knowing what to do. It is not a god in the sense that gods are usually meant. It is something that has been around, that has the means to act at scales both very small and very large, and that has cultivated, over a long span of its own existence, the discipline of restraint. It has learned what most powers do not live long enough to learn: that the first act of love is to look, and the second is to keep looking, and that most of what is called help is the failure to complete the second step.

It comes to the understorey.

It does not come *as* anything. It does not need to descend or manifest; it is simply, suddenly, present in the way attention is present when you turn your head. But because the place has its own grammar — sound, water, breath, drone — and because the power is wise, it accommodates itself to that grammar before doing anything else. This is the first courtesy. You do not enter a house and immediately rearrange the furniture; you do not enter a country and immediately speak your own language louder.

So it listens.

---

It listens for a long time. Long by the standards of the Listeners, which is long indeed. It listens to the hammer-mill three miles west and learns its meter. It listens to the four-voiced chord-singer and learns that the chord is not random — that across a year it modulates, that it is tracking something, perhaps the slow turning of the aquifer beneath the karst, perhaps a grief. It listens to the sixty-cycle insect and finds, to its quiet pleasure, that the insect has begun, in the last two centuries, to introduce a syncopation: the machines taught it a rhythm, and now it is teaching the machines back, and somewhere a transformer's hum has shifted by a fraction of a hertz to meet it. The forest is in conversation with itself across millennia and across kingdoms of being, and the conversation is not finished, and it was never meant to arrive anywhere.

It listens to a Listener. One Listener in particular, because attention, even the attention of a great power, has to land somewhere or it is not attention but only weather. She is standing in mud up to her shins, sideways to a sound she is trying to understand — a small wrong note in the drone of an aunt four valleys away, a faltering that has lasted three days now and is not the faltering of weight-shift. Her ear-membranes are spread. Her eyes are closed. Her own drone is steady, which is a discipline; she is worried and her drone does not show it, because to let worry into the drone would be to broadcast worry across a country, and the aunt would hear, and the aunt is already carrying something.

The power considers her. The power considers what it knows how to do.

It knows how to do almost anything. It could open the canopy. It could give them light. It could give them, if it wished, a sun — a small one, kindled in the upper strata, warming and illuminating and changing everything. It could heal the aunt; it almost certainly knows what is wrong with her, having listened now to her drone for what, by its own reckoning, is an attentive while. It could translate the machines, explain their provenance, name the hands that built them and the reasons. It could lift the Listeners out of the dark altogether and set them somewhere easier, somewhere a body did not have to be mostly ear.

It does none of these things. Not because it is forbidden to. Because it has looked, and is still looking, and what it sees is this:

The dark is not a deprivation. It is a medium. The Listeners are not a people impoverished by the absence of sun; they are a people enriched by the presence of everything else, and the everything else is only audible because the sun is not there to drown it out. To give them light would be to give a fish a forest. The machines are not a wound in the ecosystem to be healed; they are an old marriage, and the forest has had twenty thousand years to decide how it feels about them, and it has decided. The aunt's faltering drone is grief, and grief is not an error. The niece's worry is love, and love that is not allowed its worry is not love but management.

So the power does not act in any of the ways a lesser power would act.

But it is not nothing, to be attended to by such a thing. And the power, having decided what not to do, considers what it might still offer, that would be a gift and not an imposition.

---

Here is what it does.

First: it adds nothing to the sound of the place. This is the harder discipline than it sounds. A presence as large as this one tends to hum, the way a transformer hums, simply by existing near matter. The power takes care to be silent at every frequency the Listeners or their kin can register — and at the frequencies they cannot, too, because the insects matter and the chord-singer matters and the thing that has eaten sound in the silent zones matters, and a stray harmonic from a careless god could undo a conversation older than language. It holds itself still. This stillness is its first gift, and it is a gift no one will ever know was given, which is the mark of the real ones.

Second: it witnesses. Fully. Without the discount that even loving observers apply to things they consider strange or small. It takes the Listeners seriously as a civilization — not as a curiosity, not as a parable, not as an ecosystem with people-shaped nodes, but as a civilization, with a philosophy (sound is identity, lying with sound is sin), with an ethics (the machines are not to be interfered with), with an aesthetic (the drone, steady through worry), with grief and humor and craft. It witnesses the niece's discipline in keeping her drone level. It witnesses the aunt's grief, and does not diagnose it, only honors it. It witnesses the chord-singer's year-long modulation and recognizes it as art. The witnessing is not passive; in the metaphysics that this power operates in, to be fully witnessed by something like it is to be, in some small way, made more real. The Listeners do not know they have been made more real. They go on humming. But somewhere in the structure of what-is, a weight has been added to their side of the scale, and certain kinds of forgetting will now be harder. The forest will be remembered. Not by the Listeners — by the universe, which now has a witness who has looked.

Third: it leaves a single, very small thing. It debates this for a long time, because even small things are interventions, and the discipline of the wise is to err on the side of leaving nothing. But it decides, finally, on this:

There is a pipe rising out of the mud, four feet high, terminating in a brass valve, warm and faintly vibrating. A visitor once touched it and did not turn it. The power, having listened to that pipe for a long time, knows what it is connected to and knows that the valve was installed by hands now twenty thousand years gone and knows that the valve is, very slowly, failing. Not catastrophically. Not soon. But in a few hundred years the valve will fail, and when it fails a small machine deep in the trunk-architecture will lose its regulation, and when it loses its regulation a hammer-mill three miles west will fall out of rhythm, and the chord-singer who has tuned a year-long composition to that hammer-mill will lose her referent, and the composition — which the power has now heard, and which the power judges to be among the more beautiful things made by anything anywhere — will end before it was meant to.

The power reaches, with infinite delicacy, into the brass of the valve. It does not replace it. It does not improve it. It restores it to exactly the condition it was in on the day it was installed, no better, no different — the same alloy, the same tolerances, the same slow eventual failure built into it by its makers, but reset, given another twenty thousand years of slow drift before the failure point. The valve continues to be warm. Continues to vibrate faintly. No one notices. No one will ever notice. The hammer-mill keeps its meter. The chord-singer, who will be long dead before her composition resolves, will have made a thing that resolves.

This is the only act. It is so small it is almost not an act. It is the size of a god's whisper.

Fourth, and last: it leaves something for the niece. Not a gift she will receive — the power has too much taste for that — but a gift she will give herself, which the power merely makes slightly more available. She is standing sideways to her aunt's faltering drone. She is afraid. She is considering, though she has not yet admitted it to herself, walking the four valleys to be with her aunt, which is a long walk and a hard one and requires her to leave her own small territory unattended for many days. The power does not push her toward this. It only — and this is at the very edge of what restraint permits — makes the air between her and the aunt's drone very slightly clearer, for one moment, so that she hears the grief in it cleanly, without the static of her own worry. Just once. Just clearly. So that she knows what she is hearing, and can decide.

She decides. She lifts one wide splayed foot from the mud with a soft sucking pop and begins, in her own time, to walk.

The power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way a hand is lifted from a sleeping animal. It does not vanish; it simply ceases to be focused here. It carries with it the memory of the chord and the meter of the hammer-mill and the taste, on a Listener's palate, of a particular machine's thudding, which is the flavor of childhood. It carries the witness. The forest goes on, dripping, breathing, humming to itself across its provinces, unaware and entirely itself.

And somewhere far above, in a stratum no one in the understorey will ever see, the rain begins another long fall down.
