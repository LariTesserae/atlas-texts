The benevolent power considers the cathedrals for a long time before doing anything. This is the first important thing about it: that it considers. That it does not arrive with a plan.

It is wise enough to know that wisdom, faced with something genuinely old, must first become a student. So it does what students do. It looks. It listens. It does not touch.

What it learns, in the looking:

That the cathedrals are not suffering. This matters. A benevolent power that arrives ready to rescue is often only a power that arrives ready to act, and action is a thing the cathedrals have already politely declined for several geological epochs. The kelp is not in pain. The builders, in their alcoves, are not trapped in the way a fast creature would be trapped by stillness; they are *at home* in the way that only something which has chosen its medium can be. The absorbed visitors — these are the ones the power looks at longest — are harder. Some of them, the power can tell, slowed into the place the way a tired traveler sinks into a chair they meant only to test. Others slowed the way water freezes: not by decision, but by ambient condition. The distinction is real and the power notes it.

That the question *should anything be done* is not the same as the question *can anything be done.* The power can do a great deal. It could warm the water. It could seed the aisles with fish. It could speed the builders back up, peel the kelp from their patient hands, restore the chisel marks to a human tempo. It could end the cathedrals in an afternoon, with kindness, with the best of intentions.

It does not do this. The wisdom in it is the part that recognizes that ending a thing in order to save it from being itself is a particular human error, and the power is not interested in that error, however benevolent its disguise.

So what does it do?

It does, I think, three small things, each chosen with great care.

---

**The first thing** is that it makes itself known to the kelp.

Not loudly. The kelp-consortium operates on chemical and slow-electrical signals, on gradients that take seasons to propagate, and the power introduces itself in this register: a faint, sustained pattern in the water, recognizable as *something attending,* repeating across enough cycles that the consortium has time, in its own time, to notice it. The message is not in words. It is in the shape of a pause. *I am here. I see you. I am not going to do anything to you. I would like, if you are willing, to be witnessed by you in return.*

This takes, on the consortium's clock, the equivalent of a long afternoon. On ours, perhaps three centuries.

The consortium notices. This is the first time in its existence that anything from outside the cathedrals has spoken to it in a tempo it can hear. Whatever response it makes — and there is a response, slow, careful, the chemical equivalent of a hand raised in greeting — is the first true conversation the place has had with anything not of itself. The power receives this and does not press for more. A greeting exchanged is enough, for a first visit.

---

**The second thing** is that it goes to the absorbed.

Specifically, to the ones whose stillness was not chosen. The power can tell which are which; this is part of what being wise means, here. It does not free them. Freeing would be a kind of violence — most of them have been still for so long that to be sped up would shatter them, the way a deep-sea fish brought to the surface ruptures in the thinning pressure. Speed is not a gift you can give a thing that has become its slowness.

What it does instead is sit with each one. Briefly, by its standards. Long enough, by theirs.

To each it offers a kind of dream — not an escape, not a vision of warmth and movement, which would only torment — but a clarification. *You came here. You stayed. Here is what staying has made of you. Here is what you are now. Here is the shape of your life, which has not ended, which is being lived in a register you forgot you had agreed to.* It offers, in other words, the consent that was never quite given the chance to form. Some of the absorbed receive this and, receiving it, complete the slowing — the last reluctance in them dissolves, and they become, finally and fully, of the cathedrals. Others receive it and a small, slow grief moves through them, the grief of understanding what they have lost, and the power stays with this grief for as long as it lasts, which by our reckoning is a very long time, and by theirs is the appropriate length for that particular grief. None of them are rushed. None of them are removed. The gift is only that their condition has been seen, and named, and accompanied.

This is not nothing. The absorbed have been alone, in a sense the kelp and the builders are not alone, for as long as they have been here. To be seen is, for them, the first event in epochs.

---

**The third thing** is the hardest, and the power hesitates before doing it.

It places, somewhere in the cathedrals — in an aisle not far from the entrance, in a flute of column where the kelp has not yet grown thick — a sign. Not a warning. The power is not in the business of warnings; warnings imply that the warned party has not been trusted to understand. The sign is more like a notation, the kind a librarian leaves in a margin.

The sign says, in whatever language a visitor would arrive carrying: *This place will propose itself to you as an answer. The proposal is real. Some who have accepted it are content. Some are not. You are permitted to accept. You are permitted to decline. If you wish to know, before deciding, what acceptance has meant for those who came before, ask the kelp. It will answer in its own time. You may have to wait. The waiting is part of the answer.*

This is the only intervention the power makes in the cathedrals' future. It does not close the place. It does not advertise it. It does not change the chemistry that excludes the fish or hurry the builders' next stroke. It only ensures that the next visitor — and there will be a next visitor; the universe is generous with visitors — arrives informed. The cathedrals' invitation remains. It is simply no longer hidden inside the green hush. It is named, at the threshold, as what it is.

A visitor may still choose to slow. The power respects this absolutely. What it does not respect is the slow accumulation of choices that were never quite made, the absorbed who became absorbed because the proposal was never put to them in words. After the sign, anyone who stays, stays with the proposal in hand.

---

And then the power leaves.

It does not establish a watch. It does not return on a schedule. To do so would be to fold the cathedrals into a larger administrative structure, and the cathedrals are not for that; they are for themselves. The power trusts what it has done to be enough, and trusts the cathedrals to continue being what they have been, only now with one greeting received, some old griefs accompanied, and a small notation at the door.

The kelp remembers the greeting. It remembers in its slow way, which means the memory will still be present when our species is dust. Every few centuries it produces, in a particular district, a configuration of blade and filament that is — if you knew to read it — a reply to the power's first message. The reply is patient. The reply assumes the power is still listening, somewhere, on a frequency long enough to hear it.

The power is. This is what benevolence at this scale looks like: not constant attendance, which would crowd the cathedrals with a faster presence than they can tolerate, but a sustained, unhurried availability. A listening that does not require a speaker. A door left, very slightly, ajar.

The cathedrals continue. The builders take their strokes. The silt settles a grain at a time. The green vault dims and brightens with the long year. Somewhere near the entrance, a sign in no particular alphabet waits for the next warm-blooded thing to drift past and read it.

The service has not begun, because the service is the arrival, and the arrivals — now — are the arrivals of those who have chosen to arrive.

This is what the power did. It is very little. It is, I think, exactly the right amount.
