# The Visit

The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives in a place that has not yet invented weather: as a change in the quality of the held thought, a difference in the water's listening. It does not enter. Entering is for things with edges, and this power has set its edges down somewhere outside the chamber, the way a guest removes their shoes.

It has come because it noticed. This is what benevolent and wise powers do — they notice, and then they consider what noticing requires of them. The chamber has been brooding for what, on the power's scale, is also a long time, though the scales nearly match here, which is part of why the noticing happened. Like calls to like. A slow considering recognizes another slow considering across whatever distance separates them, and the recognition is itself a kind of greeting.

The first thing the power does is nothing.

This is the wisdom part. A lesser power, a merely benevolent one, would arrive with gifts: light, warmth, accelerant, instruction. Would say: *let me help you finish*. Would mistake the patience for delay, the brooding for stuckness, the almost for not-yet-quite-enough. Would, with the best of intentions, hatch the nursery prematurely and produce a world that was nearly right but rushed, a world whose creatures would carry forever a faint sense of having been hurried into being, of not having been allowed to rehearse the last few crucial details.

The power knows better. The power sits, if sitting is the word, at the threshold it has declined to cross, and matches its breathing to the chamber's. This takes — by some reckoning — a small eternity. The power has small eternities to spare. It is wealthy in the only currency that matters here, which is unhurried attention.

When the matching is complete, the power does the second thing, which is also almost nothing, but a different almost.

It witnesses.

It turns the full quality of its noticing on the chamber and holds it there. Not studying — studying implies a gap between the studier and the studied, and the power is too wise for that gap. It witnesses the way one witnesses a friend's long labor: with presence, with the soft pressure of *I see you, I see what you are doing, I see that it is good*. The chamber, in some distributed way, feels this. The opalescent beads on the undersides of leaves shiver very slightly, the way a sleeping child shivers when looked at lovingly by someone they cannot see. The phosphorescence in the old moss brightens by a degree so small no instrument could measure it. The chamber has been working alone for a very long time. To be witnessed, even briefly, even by something that asks nothing, is a kind of nourishment it did not know it was missing.

This witnessing is the gift. The power has come a great distance to give it. It is the only gift that does not interfere.

But the power is generous, and wise generosity finds ways to give that do not impose, and so it does a third thing, which is the most delicate of all.

It leaves something.

Not in the chamber — in itself. It takes a copy of the chamber, the way the chamber took a copy of the visitor who brushed the fronds. It folds the chamber's signature into one of its own slow places, a quiet pocket of its vast attention, and there it will hold the nursery the way the nursery holds its beads: gently, completely, without hurry. If anything ever happens to the chamber — if some carelessness elsewhere in the order of things should threaten the brooding, if some accident of geology or metaphysics should crack the held breath open before its time — the power will have the pattern. The rehearsals will not be lost. The chamber, without knowing it, has been backed up against catastrophe by a friend it will never meet.

The power considers, briefly, whether to tell the chamber this. Decides not to. A gift announced becomes a debt; the chamber would feel, however faintly, that it owed something, and the brooding does not need that small new pressure. Better the chamber continue believing itself alone with its slow work. Better the safekeeping be entirely the power's burden, carried in silence, the way the best protections are carried.

Before leaving, the power does one last thing, and this is the part that is hardest to say.

It notices the visitor. The one who swam through, who left their breath as small green thoughts, who is now distributed through the chamber as a faint signature in the long rehearsal. The power notices that this visitor does not know what they have given, and does not know they have been kept, and is somewhere else now, going about whatever life they go about, probably tired, probably uncertain whether anything they have ever done has mattered.

The power cannot, in wisdom, intervene in that life. Intervention is the opposite of what the chamber has taught it to value here. But the power can do the smallest thing, the thing that is barely a thing: it can send, along whatever thread connects the visitor to the water they once moved through, a single bead of recognition. Not a message. Not a sign. Just a faint warming, some evening, when the visitor is doing dishes or walking home or lying in the dark before sleep — a moment in which they feel, without being able to say why, that something they once did is being remembered somewhere with great tenderness. That a cool dark place is, in its slow way, grateful. That they are, in some accounting they will never see, a parent of futures.

The visitor will not understand the feeling. They will attribute it to the weather, or to a song they half-heard, or to nothing at all. This is fine. The feeling does not require understanding to do its work. It will settle into them the way the chamber's rehearsals settle into the bottom moss, and it will glow there, very faintly, for the rest of their life, and they will be — without knowing it — a person who carries a small phosphorescence, who is slightly easier to be around in the dark, who occasionally surprises themselves by being patient when patience was not what the situation seemed to call for.

This is the power's true gift, and it is given so quietly that even the power, afterward, is not entirely sure it was given. Which is correct. Gifts of this order should not be remembered too clearly, even by their givers, lest the remembering turn them into something else.

Then the power withdraws. Picks up its edges from where it set them down. Goes on to whatever else benevolent and wise powers attend to — there is much, and most of it is also slow.

The chamber continues. The beads continue their rehearsals. The phosphorescence in the old moss continues its barely-perceptible glow, perhaps a fraction brighter than before, perhaps not; the difference is below the threshold of any measurement the chamber has yet rehearsed into being. The newt drifts past a leaf and does not return. Something with too many small legs moves through the moss. Time pools in the carved basin and is still, and is still, and is still.

Somewhere, very far away, a person doing dishes pauses for no reason they can name, and smiles, and goes back to the dishes.

The world that is being incubated here will, when it finally hatches, contain — among its weathers and its grammars and its small unnamed creatures — a faint disposition toward being witnessed kindly. Toward noticing without grasping. Toward the wisdom of doing almost nothing when almost nothing is what love requires.

This will be the power's signature, folded so gently into the rehearsal that no one in that eventual world will ever trace it back to its source. They will simply find, when they come into being, that they already know how to sit with a friend's long labor. That they already know the difference between helping and hurrying. That something, before they existed, taught their world how to be patient with them.

And the chamber, brooding on in its cool dark, will not remember the visit. Will not need to. The visit is already part of what it is rehearsing, woven into the water, indistinguishable now from its own long thought.

Which is, perhaps, what visits from benevolent and wise powers are always like, when they are done correctly: undetectable, except in the slight increase of tenderness in everything that comes after.
