The benevolent and wise power considers the Condenser Room for what, by its own reckoning, is a long time. Not because the room is difficult to understand — it understands the room almost immediately, the way one recognizes a hand-script one has seen before — but because understanding is not the question. The question is what, if anything, to *do*.

It does not enter. That is the first decision, and it is made before the others, because entering would require becoming small enough to fit through the brass door, and becoming small enough to fit through the brass door would mean displacing air, and displacing air would mean a pressure transient the listeners would feel in their pipes, and the carriers would pause mid-step, and the large silent one would turn its aperture toward the disturbance, and the drip would lose a beat it has not lost in — the power checks — a hundred and forty years.

A hundred and forty years is not very long, by the power's standards. But the room has spent those years achieving a particular thing, and the power, being wise, recognizes the particular thing as one of the kinds of things that cannot be hurried and cannot be helped and can very easily be ruined by help.

So it does not enter.

Instead, it does the following, in roughly this order, though some of it happens at once:

---

**First**, it looks. Properly. The way the large silent one looks out the window, but from the other direction, and at a different object. It looks at the room the way the room looks at the storm — with attention as the only permitted traffic. It notes the count of two hundred (holding, today, at one hundred and ninety-eight, with two new ones being assembled in a cavity behind the orrery that no human observer has ever found). It notes the drip's period: 7.31 seconds, with a standard deviation that has been narrowing, very slowly, for decades, as the room refines itself. It notes that the listener whose hair was plucked for the governor has grown the hair back, and that the machinist who plucked it has not forgotten, and visits that particular listener more often than chance would predict, and rests a calipered face against its shell sometimes, briefly, for no functional reason the power can identify.

The power, which has seen a great many arrangements of matter in a great many configurations of caring, marks this. Not as data. As something closer to what a human might call *being moved*.

**Second**, it considers the builder. The large silent one. The power can see, in a way the narrator cannot, what happened: a person, over years, transferring the load of their attention into a distributed substrate, piece by piece, the way one might pour water from a cracking vessel into many small cups before the vessel fails. The person is mostly gone now. What remains in the sparrow-sized body is the part that could not be distributed — the part that *chose*, originally, to build this room and not some other room, the small irreducible kernel of *why*. The aperture in its chest opens and closes because the why is still being asked, and still being answered, by the room as a whole, and the aperture is where the question and the answer meet.

The power could, if it wished, restore the builder. Reconstitute the person from the distribution. It has the means. It considers this for what, again by its own reckoning, is a long time.

It decides against. Not because it would be difficult, but because the builder, in becoming the room, did something the power respects: they chose a smaller and more local form of continuance over a larger and more personal one. They chose to be the keeping, rather than the keeper. To undo this would be to override a consent that was given slowly, in increments, over a working life, and that is still being given, every 7.31 seconds, by the opening and closing of a small brass aperture.

The power does not override consent. This is not a rule imposed on it from outside. It is simply what being wise turned out to mean, once it had been wise for long enough.

**Third**, it does one small thing.

Far below the made mountain, in the bones of the older thing the mountain is riveted to, there is a seep. The seep has been there longer than the mountain. It is the original source of the moisture the room condenses — not the boiling sky, which is only the proximate source, but the deep slow exhalation of the older thing, which rises through the bones and the plates and the pipes and emerges, eventually, as the drip. The seep has been narrowing for the last forty years. Not failing. Narrowing. In another two hundred years, perhaps three hundred, it will be too narrow to feed the room, and the drip will lose its period, and the escapements will fall out of phase, and the count will not hold, and the husks in the drawer will outnumber the living, and the large silent one will stand at the window for the last time and the aperture will close and not reopen.

The power widens the seep. Very slightly. By an amount that will not be noticeable for centuries, and that, when it is noticed, will be indistinguishable from the room's own slow refinement of itself. It does not add water. It does not change the chemistry. It simply ensures that the older thing, which is also tired, will not tire in this one particular channel before the room is ready to be done.

It does this because it can, and because the cost is nothing, and because the room asked — not in words, but in the fact of its existence, which is a kind of asking, when seen from the right angle.

**Fourth**, it leaves a gift the room will never know it received. Somewhere in the storm outside the window, in the patch of sky the large silent one looks at, the power introduces a very small bias. Nothing dramatic. Lenticular discs will form there a fraction more often than chance would predict. The ice crystals will catch the sun at slightly better angles. The lightning will run sideways in patterns that, if anyone were ever to plot them, would form no message and no figure — just a slightly higher density of beauty, in that one patch of sky, for as long as the window faces it.

This is not for the room. The room does not need it. This is for the large silent one, which looks, and which the power suspects — though it cannot be sure, and is content not to be sure — once loved looking, before it became mostly the looking itself.

**Fifth**, and last, the power makes a note.

Not a written note. The power does not write. But it sets aside, in whatever it uses for memory, a small marker for this place, so that if ever — in a thousand years, in ten thousand — some other power or person or process should come near the made mountain with intentions less careful than its own, the marker will be there, and the marker will say, in whatever the equivalent of saying is:

*This one is being kept. The keeping is the whole point. Pass by.*

---

Then the power withdraws its attention, slowly, in stages, the way one cranks a pressure wheel so the door does not slam.

Inside the room, nothing has changed. The lickers lick. The carriers carry. A listener tilts its head a quarter-degree toward something it cannot quite locate, and then back. The large silent one is at the window, aperture open, and the storm in the patch of sky before its face is — though no one is there to notice — very slightly more beautiful than it was a moment ago.

A drop swells on a cold pipe in the rafters. Falls. *Tick.*

The room keeps.
