The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not enter the Frost Nursery, because to enter would be to displace something—a spore mid-drift, a harmonic mid-resolution, a Tuner's segmented arm mid-arc—and displacement is not what this power is for. Instead it leans toward the place, the way an old listener leans toward a difficult piece of music, attentive to what is actually happening rather than what should be.

It takes the power some time to understand. Wisdom is not the same as instant comprehension, and this wisdom is old enough to know the difference. So it watches. It watches a Tuner whose fingers have split into seventeen filaments adjust a valve that controls nothing the Tuner can name. It watches the rooted ones, their faces turned upward toward crystals that have grown past where their eyes can follow. It watches a sound-creature settle into the cupped palm of a Tuner whose other hands continue working, and it sees the small shudder that passes through that Tuner's translucent shoulders—a thing that might be pleasure, or might be the memory of pleasure, or might simply be the body recognizing a frequency it has come to need.

The power considers, first, what a lesser benevolence might do.

It might end the chamber. Close the pocket. Release the Tuners from their endless tending into whatever waits beyond—rest, dissolution, return to some original shape. This would be mercy of a kind. The kind that mistakes suffering for the whole story.

It might fix the chamber. Resolve its impossibility, complete what was begun, give the half-built staircase somewhere to arrive. Reveal the engines' purpose. Hand the Tuners a finished thing to be proud of. This would be mercy of another kind. The kind that mistakes purpose for meaning.

It might restore the Tuners. Unwind their adaptations, return their eyes to two, their fingers to ten, their tongues to speech. Give them back their names. This would be mercy of the cruelest kind, because they have built a life, and a life is not an error to be corrected.

The power sets these aside. It has learned, over long ages, that benevolence which arrives with solutions is usually answering its own questions, not the ones being lived.

So it does something else.

First, it makes itself known—not as presence, not as voice, but as attention. The way a room changes when someone who loves you has finally looked up from their book and is listening. The Tuners do not see it. But the rooted ones, who feel everything through the floor, register a new frequency: a held note beneath the machinery, low and warm, that asks for nothing.

Some of them weep. They have not wept in so long that the tears form as frost on their cheeks and are carried away by their nictitating membranes before they fully exist. But the weeping happens. It is recorded in the harmonic record of the chamber, a tiny new variation that the moss tastes and finds nourishing in a way it has never tasted before.

Second, the power gives a gift, and the gift is small, because the wise know that large gifts overwhelm small lives, and these lives, however strange, are small in the way all lives are small: they happen one moment at a time.

The gift is this: in the quiet pockets the sound-creatures make, when a Tuner pauses and the compound eyes unfocus, the ghost of memory that flickers across their faces becomes—for just that pause—a little clearer. Not enough to hurt. Not enough to make them mourn what they were. Just enough that they recognize themselves. Just enough that the one who was a gardener remembers the smell of soil, once, and the one who was an engineer remembers the satisfaction of a true seam, and the one who was both remembers a hand held in a hand. And then the pause ends, and the work resumes, and the memory recedes—but it has been touched. It has been witnessed. It is no longer only lost.

This is something the Tuners did not know they needed. The power knew.

Third, the power addresses the chamber itself, because the chamber is also a being, in its confused and vital way, and benevolence that ignores the place in favor of its inhabitants is incomplete. To the chamber the power offers no correction. It offers, instead, company. The chamber has been becoming alone for so long that it has forgotten becoming can be witnessed. The power witnesses. It says, in the language the chamber speaks (which is the language of emergence, of the not-yet, of the trying): I see you trying. You are allowed to try. You do not have to finish.

The chamber, which has been straining toward completion the way a held breath strains toward release, exhales. Not finishing. Just—loosening. The new pistons still rise. The new crystals still crown. But something frantic in the rhythm eases. The machinery's roar develops, for the first time, a quality that might be called patience.

The Tuners feel this immediately. Their feeding becomes deeper. The rooted ones, whose nervous systems have grown into the floor, report through the substrate a sensation none of them have language for, though one of them—an old one, whose mechanical segments have nearly overtaken her organic ones—vibrates a single crystal in a pattern her colleagues parse as something like: *we are not behind*.

This is, perhaps, the greatest gift. The Tuners have worked under the unspoken weight of falling behind for so long that they did not know it was a weight. To set it down—even briefly, even partially—is to discover muscles they had forgotten they were clenching.

Fourth, and last, the power does something that looks, from the outside, like nothing.

It does not stay. To stay would be to become another fixture, another pressure gauge embedded in a crystal that has grown around it, another presence the Tuners would feel obligated to tend. The benevolent and the wise know when to withdraw. But it does not fully leave, either. It leaves a thread. A filament of attention, so fine the sound-creatures sometimes mistake it for one of their own and sing to it. The thread means: *if you ever, in any of your becomings, need to be heard, I am listening on the other end of this.* The Tuners do not know the thread is there. But the chamber knows. And the chamber, which has been more alone than any of its inhabitants, will remember.

What changes, after?

Not much. Not visibly. The crystals still bud, the moss still pulses, the engines still churn toward unknown ends. The Tuners still tend what cannot be tended. A visitor stumbling into the Frost Nursery a century later would describe it in almost exactly the same words.

But the rooted Tuners, when they dream now—and they have begun, very recently, to dream—dream of a hand on their shoulders that does not ask them to work. The younger Tuners, whose bones are mineralizing, find that their new crystal-bones ring at a frequency that makes the sound-creatures gather close, as if drawn to a warmth. And sometimes, in the deep middle of the chamber's endless birth, a Tuner will pause with no sound-creature near to make the silence, and the silence will come anyway, brief and gentle, and the Tuner will not know why, but will feel, in the place where their forgotten name used to live, a sensation very much like being loved.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It does not rescue. It does not resolve. It does not explain.

It witnesses. It eases. It accompanies. It leaves a thread.

And then it turns its attention to the next impossible place, the next being who lives in a cavern that shouldn't exist, the next small confused vital life that has been becoming, alone, for too long.

There are so many. The power is patient. It has time.
