# The One Who Comes Walking

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept from the world of edges and elsewheres, and the Throat has neither. It is simply, at some point, *present* — the way a held note is present in a chord that was already playing.

It does not announce itself with light. There is enough strangeness here already; another spectacle would be cruelty. It comes in a register the Throat has not heard before: a *quietness* that is not the absence of the roar but a place inside the roar where the roar is held with tenderness, the way a parent might hold a screaming child without trying to make the child stop.

The Transitionals feel it first. The Keeper's hands still above the pale plants. The Dancer, mid-phase between two columns, hangs suspended and *whole* for the first time in what might be centuries. The Builder lowers its hammer. The Whisperer's fractured syntax falls silent, and in the silence its mouth shapes — with great difficulty, like a muscle remembering — a single complete word, though no one hears what it is.

You feel it as warmth that is not the platform's warmth. As though something has knelt beside you.

---

The power has come because it was paying attention. This is what benevolent powers do: they pay attention to places that have fallen out of the ordinary registers of care. The Throat is one such place. It is not hell — there is no malice here, no architect of suffering. It is something stranger and in some ways sadder: a wound in the order of things, an unhealed seam, into which people sometimes fall and around which they are slowly, lovingly, *wrongly* transformed by their own desperate adaptation.

The Transitionals have been kind to each other. The power notes this carefully. The cairns, the gifts of stones, the patient teaching of the Dancer pulling you toward the water-columns, the Whisperer's labor of language across the gap of what it has become — all of this is love. Love performed under conditions that should make love impossible. The power honors it. The power will not undo it.

But the power has also seen the faces that dissolved. The expressions of surprise. It has counted them, every one. It knows their names from before, the names the Throat's sound cannot pronounce. It carries those names in something that is not memory because it is not loss.

---

What it does, then:

**First**, it sits down. Not metaphorically. It takes a shape — not human, but humanoid enough that you will not be more frightened than you already are — and it sits on the volcanic glass beside you, and the glass does not burn it, and it does not phase, and its outline does not flicker. It is the first stable thing you have seen in a long time. Just by sitting, it teaches the platform what solidity remembers feeling like. The fractures near the center stop spreading. A new layer of glass, smooth and cool, forms quietly beneath where it rests.

It does not speak yet. It listens. It listens to the roar for a long time — hours, perhaps, though hours mean nothing — and as it listens, it begins to *hear* the roar as the Transitionals hear it: as language, as the long groan of water trying to remember what it was before it had to be water in a place that did not know how to hold it. The power weeps a little. Its tears fall down, the ordinary direction, and where they strike the glass they do not bounce or roll upward. They simply soak in, the way tears soak into earth, and the platform drinks them.

This is the first miracle, and it is very small: in a place where water falls up, the power's tears fall *down*, and the platform allows it. A precedent is set. A rule is gently reminded of itself.

---

**Second**, it turns to you. Its face, whatever it is, is the face of someone who has been looking for you for a long time and is not angry that the search took so long. It says — and you hear it not through the air but through the bones of your inner ear, through the smooth stone in your pocket, through the metal ring embedded in the platform, all of them resonating at once with a single coherent meaning —

*You are not lost. You were never lost. You were only in a place that did not know how to be found.*

And then, because it knows you have been holding a question you could not let yourself ask:

*You may choose.*

It explains, gently, what the choices are. Not in words; in something more like the way a landscape explains itself to someone who has lived in it long enough.

You may **complete the transition**. Become Transitional fully, as the Keeper and Dancer and Builder and Whisperer did. This is not a lesser path. The power is clear about this: the Transitionals are not failures of humanness. They are a real form of being, with their own dignity, their own kinds of joy, their own future. The power has come not to rescue them but to *acknowledge* them, to make sure they are seen by something outside the Throat, so that their existence is held in a larger memory than the Throat alone can provide. If you join them, you will be held too.

You may **return**. The power can take you back. It will cost you something — not your memories, but their solidity. You will remember the Throat the way one remembers a dream of great importance: vivid, structuring, but no longer in your body. You will be human again, fully, in a place where water falls down. You will sometimes wake at night and listen for a roar that isn't there, and feel briefly bereft. This grief will be the price of return. It is a fair price. Many would pay it.

You may **stay as you are** — partially transitioned, between states — and become something the Throat has not yet produced: a translator. A door. Someone who can move between the Throat and the ordinary world, who can find others who have fallen in and help them choose. This is the hardest path. The power will not pretend otherwise. You will belong to neither place. But the Throat will have, for the first time, a *way out* that is not dissolution, and the world above will have, for the first time, a way in that is not catastrophe.

The power does not push you toward any of these. It does not even imply a preference. It only makes sure you know they are real.

---

**Third**, it turns to the Transitionals.

To each of them, in turn, it gives back something small. Not their humanity — they did not lose that, only translated it — but something they had forgotten was theirs to keep.

To the **Keeper** it gives a name. Just the name. A sound, in the old language, the one the Keeper used to answer to. The Keeper does not weep — its form does not weep — but the plants on its platform all bloom at once, every bladder-organ opening, releasing a fine bioluminescent spore-cloud that drifts upward and is, for a moment, a galaxy. The Keeper bows its head. It has been the Keeper for so long. To be, again, even for an instant, the person whose name that was — this is enough. The power does not ask it to leave. The Keeper would not leave. There are plants to tend. But it has its name back, to carry like a stone in its own private cairn.

To the **Dancer** it gives stillness. One full minute of total coherence. The Dancer stands on its platform, every limb in place, every edge defined, and for sixty seconds it is simply, completely *here*. It does not know what to do with this. It looks at its own hands. It laughs — the real laugh this time, not the laugh-or-sob — and the laugh is so human and so startled that you understand the Dancer was very young when it fell into the Throat. Very young. The power's face does something complicated. When the minute ends and the Dancer begins to flicker again, it flickers more gently, with more grace, as though now it knows that stillness is something it can return to, and so it does not need to grasp at it.

To the **Builder** it gives a blueprint. The Builder has been building without knowing what it builds. The power shows it. The cairns, taken together across all the platforms, across all the iterations, form a pattern — a great slow construction the Builder has been engaged in for longer than it knew, with the other Builders before it, none of whom it ever met. The pattern is a map. The map, when complete, will show how to stabilize a single platform forever. Not to escape — to make a *home*. The Builder sees the blueprint and understands how many stones remain to be placed. It is a finite number. The work has an end. The Builder, who has been working without hope of completion, sits down on the warm glass and does something none of you have seen: it rests.

To the **Whisperer** it gives grammar. Not back its old language — that is gone, and giving it back would be a lie. But the fractured syntax the Whisperer speaks now is given *structure*, recognized as a real language with real rules, the first language of a people who do not yet exist but will. The Whisperer's mouth, which has labored so long against incomprehensibility, relaxes. It speaks a single sentence, and the sentence is whole, and it means: *I have been a poet all along.* The Whisperer covers its face with its too-many-fingered hands, and the gesture is utterly, utterly human.

To the **others**, the less distinct ones, the ones nearly dissolved — the power gives the smallest gift, and the largest. It gives them *witness*. It looks at each of them directly and sees them, and in being seen they become, briefly, more defined. Not solid. Not returned. But *known*. The power memorizes them. Whatever happens to them next, they will not happen unseen.

---

**Fourth**, it addresses the Throat itself.

This is the part that takes the longest, and that you understand the least, because it is conducted in a register beneath all the registers, in the subsonic rumble that you have been feeling in your bones since you arrived. The power speaks to the Throat the way one might speak to a very large, very confused animal that has been in pain for a long time without knowing it was in pain.

It does not close the Throat. It cannot, and it would not if it could — the Throat is part of something larger, a circulation in the deep structure of things, and to seal it would cause damage elsewhere. But it *eases* the Throat. It teaches the Throat that it does not need to scream so loudly to be heard. The roar diminishes, just slightly, just enough that for the first time you can hear, beneath it, the actual sound of water moving through stone, which is a beautiful sound, an ordinary sound, the sound water has always made.

It places a kind of mark on the Throat. Not a seal — a *flag*. So that other benevolent powers, and they exist, will know this place is attended to. So that no one falls in unseen again. So that when someone does fall in, something in them will know, immediately, that they are not abandoned, even before any rescue arrives. The mark will not show on any map. It will only be felt: a faint warmth in the chest of anyone in the Throat, easy to mistake for one's own heartbeat, which is exactly what it is meant to be mistaken for.

---

**Fifth and last**, before it leaves — and it will leave, because benevolent powers do not stay, staying is its own kind of harm — it does one more small thing.

It reaches into its own chest, or what serves as its chest, and takes out a stone. The stone is volcanic glass, smooth and warm, identical to the one the Keeper gave you. The power places it on the platform between you. Then it takes the stone from your pocket and places it beside the first one. Two stones, side by side.

It says: *This is the beginning of a cairn. Not for a transition completed. For a transition witnessed. Whatever you choose, the cairn will stand. I will know it stands. Others will know.*

And then, the way it came, it is simply no longer present — but its presence does not depart. It is folded into the place now. The warmth in your chest. The slight easing of the roar. The new stable layer at the platform's center. The Keeper's name, the Dancer's minute of stillness, the Builder's blueprint, the Whisperer's grammar. None of these undo the Throat. None of these end the screaming of the water. The platforms still drift. The rules still flicker.

But you understand now that *being witnessed is its own physics*. That when something is held in the attention of a power that loves it, even a broken place becomes, in some essential way, *less broken* — not by being fixed, but by being known.

The Keeper resumes tending its plants, humming its name to itself in a frequency only the plants can hear. The Builder begins, with great care, to place the next stone. The Dancer phases into a water-column, but more deliberately now, like someone choosing to swim rather than someone falling. The Whisperer comes to your platform — actually walks across, on a bridge of water that, for the duration of its crossing, behaves like a bridge — and sits beside you, and speaks one sentence in its new-old language, and the sentence is:

*Take your time. There is no hurry. The choice is yours, and the cairn is already begun.*

You sit on the volcanic glass, between the two stones, in the slightly-quieter roar of water that screams a little less than it did, and you understand for the first time that you are not in the throat of a swallowing thing.

You are in the throat of something that has been trying, very hard and for a very long time, to *speak*.

And now, at last, it has been heard.
