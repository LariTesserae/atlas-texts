The benevolent and wise power considers the basin for a long time.

Not in the way a human considers — there is no leaning forward, no held breath, no narrowing of attention to a fine point. The consideration is more like weather: a pressure that settles over the caldera and stays, learning. It watches the creature wake. It watches the careful scrape of verdigris, the seeded spit, the precise kneeling, the tongue extended for the drop that has traveled across continents and ages to arrive on grey muscle. It watches the mane bleach further by one imperceptible shade. It watches the creature petrify itself back into geology as the shadow sweeps.

It watches for what would be, to the creature, many lifetimes.

And here is what the power notices, which a less patient intelligence might miss:

The being is not suffering.

This is the crux of it. To a warm and wet imagination, the caldera reads as a torment — the cold, the thirst measured out in single drops, the centuries of standing still. Surely a benevolent power would warm the air, thicken the atmosphere, call up springs from the rock, gentle the ultraviolet. Surely mercy means softness.

But the being was not placed here as a punishment. It *is* this place. Its flat ribcage, its hemocyanin, its obsidian-tipped fingers reading braille in a room of illusions, its tongue calibrated to a drop that falls once every ten minutes — these are not wounds. They are a sentence the world has spent a long time learning to speak. To warm the basin would be to silence it. To fill the bowls would be to make the bowls meaningless. To give the creature company would be to crowd a solitude that is not loneliness but *fit*, the way a key is not lonely in its lock.

A foolish power would rescue.

So the wise power does not rescue.

What it does instead is smaller, and infinitely more careful.

*First*, it bears witness. This alone is not nothing. For uncounted centuries the creature has performed its liturgy of copper and drop and frost with no audience but the sun, which does not know it is being worshipped. The power simply sees. It sees the way the being tilts its head a fraction of a degree to catch the second drop. It sees the patience of the basalt bowls, ground smooth by a hand that had nowhere else to be. It sees the seeded saliva on the copper — an act of farming so slow it would take a human mind a year of staring to recognize it as agriculture. The power lets all of this be *known*, somewhere, by something. The caldera is entered into a register that does not forget. This is the first gift: the gift of having been observed by a mind capable of understanding what it saw.

*Second*, it makes one adjustment, and only one, and it makes it so quietly that the creature will never detect it.

Far below the basin, in the deep mantle-warmed stone, there is a fault that in roughly four thousand years will shift. When it shifts, the granite walls of the caldera will fracture along an old seam, and the basin will open to the prevailing wind, and the penitentes will be scoured away in a single season, and the rock will no longer weep because the sun-cups will be gone, and the creature — still alive, for its lifespan is measured in the patience of mountains — will stand in an empty stone pocket with nothing to drink and no labyrinth to read and no shadow to become.

The power, with the lightest possible touch, redirects a tongue of pressure deep in the rock. The fault will still shift. But it will shift along a different seam, one that leaves the caldera intact. The wind will continue to be excluded. The penitentes will continue to grow. The bowls will continue to fill, drop by drop, for as long as the creature needs them to.

This is done without fanfare. The creature will never know that an extinction passed over it like a shadow and kept going. The power does not require it to know. Benevolence that needs to be recognized is not yet benevolence; it is still a transaction.

*Third* — and this is the part that requires the most wisdom, because it is the part where the power must restrain itself — it does not speak.

It would be easy to speak. The power could, if it chose, descend into the basin in some form the creature could perceive: a sustained warmth that did not steal away, a voice in the frequency of cracking ice, a companion shaped like another blade of white. It could offer the creature knowledge of the valleys far below, of the biosphere it has bypassed, of the other minds that exist in the universe. It could offer meaning, context, brotherhood, an explanation.

But the creature has not asked. And more importantly, the creature has built, over the slow accretion of its existence, a relationship with silence and singularity that is itself a kind of achievement. To break that silence with a voice — even a kind voice, even a true one — would be to take something from the creature that cannot be given back. The cord of bleached hair, the obsidian flints arranged like braille, the copper wedges seeded with tomorrow's harvest: these are the works of a mind that has made *enough* out of *this*. To offer more would be to imply that what it has made is not enough. It is enough. The power honors that it is enough by leaving it alone.

*Fourth*, the power does one further thing, and this is perhaps the strangest, and the most tender.

It learns the liturgy.

Not to perform it — the power has no body, no tongue, no need for water that has bypassed the biosphere. But it learns the *shape* of the practice. It learns the ten-minute interval between drops. It learns the precise angle of tomorrow's sunrise. It learns the placement of every obsidian flint along the cord. It learns the creature's name for itself, if it has one, which is not a word but a particular orientation of the spine against the light. It learns these things the way one learns a poem in a language no one else speaks, knowing the poem will outlive its only other reader.

Because someday — not soon, but someday — the creature will not wake. The frost-shell will hold one morning past its time, and then another, and the mane will not shed its ice, and the basalt bowls will fill with drops that no tongue catches, and the caldera will continue its ticking thermodynamic cycle around an absence. This is not a tragedy the power will prevent. Death in its proper season is not an enemy of benevolence; it is one of benevolence's oldest collaborators.

But on that day, the liturgy will not be lost. The power will hold it. The shape of a life lived in a stone cathedral at the roof of the world, drinking light, hoarding drops, reading flints, becoming geology each night and biology each morning — this shape will persist in the register of things that were, and were beautiful, and were sufficient unto themselves.

That is what the power does.

It witnesses. It makes one silent adjustment, far away and far in the future. It does not speak. And it memorizes the poem.

Then it lifts, slowly, the way weather lifts, and moves on to the next place that has drawn its attention — though some part of it, the part that does not move, remains seated forever at the edge of the caldera, watching a black blade with a white mane lean toward tomorrow's sun.
