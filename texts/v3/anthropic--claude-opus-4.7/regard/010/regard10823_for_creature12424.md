# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It is already there, and has always been there, in the way that warmth is already inside a closed hand before the hand notices it is warm. But on this particular turn of the great wheel — if a wheel it is — the power chooses to *attend*. To bring its gaze close. To consider.

It does not announce itself. Announcement would shatter things. The Watchers' single eyes do not register a new presence, because the presence is not new, only newly inclined. The Engine-Beasts groan on. The Scuttlers click. The Shifters ooze and merge and part. Nothing changes — and this is the first mercy.

The power looks for a long while. Not long in any measurable sense; long in the way one looks at a sleeping child to make sure one has truly seen them.

What does it see?

It sees a chamber that calls itself eternal. It sees creatures who have been told, by the shape of their own bodies, that they are functions. It sees Engine-Beasts whose half-human faces remember, somewhere beneath the steam, that they were once *only* the human half — or dreamed they might have been. It sees Scuttlers whose multifaceted eyes contain the faintest pinprick of a question they have never been permitted to finish forming. It sees Shifters who have forgotten they were ever separate things, and Watchers who have forgotten that watching is not the same as being.

It sees that the harmony here is real. The power does not sentimentalize. The chamber *works*. The creatures are not, in the ordinary sense, suffering. They have purpose, rhythm, companionship of a kind, the deep animal comfort of a known pattern. To call this a prison would be a slander against the genuine grace of fitted things.

And yet.

The power notices the word *frozen*. It notices *eternal* used as though it were a compliment. It notices that the Engine-Beasts' groans are described as mournful, and that no one in the chamber has ever asked them why.

So the power considers what to do, and here is what it decides:

**It will not free them.** Freedom imposed from outside is only another machine. To pry the Engine-Beasts from their housings would kill them; they have grown into the iron the way a tree grows around a fence. To scatter the Scuttlers from their rounds would leave them trembling and purposeless on the wet stones. The power is wise enough to know that liberation is not a gift one being can give another. It is something that must be *taken*, or it is nothing.

**It will not destroy the chamber.** The chamber is not the enemy. The chamber is, in its strange way, beloved — by its inhabitants, and now, having looked closely, by the power as well. There is a beauty in the cobbled floor, in the mineral-encrusted shells, in the phosphorescent pulse at a Shifter's core. The power does not destroy beauty, even sad beauty. Especially sad beauty.

**What it does is smaller, and stranger.**

It introduces, into the chamber, a single new thing: the *possibility* of noticing.

Not noticing itself — that would be too much, too fast, a flood where a drop is needed. Only the possibility. A faint seam in the fabric of inevitability. A place where, if a creature were to press, it might find that the wall gives slightly.

Concretely, this is what the power does:

To the **Scuttlers**, it gives, distributed across the whole population so that no single one is burdened — one extra heartbeat per cycle. A pause. A breath-length between the oiling of one gear and the scurrying to the next. Most will never use it. Some will. In that pause, a Scuttler might, once in a thousand turns, lift its small body slightly and feel the cold air on its shell without reference to any task. This is the seed of selfhood. It will grow if it grows. It will not, if it will not.

To the **Shifters**, it gives a memory — just one — of having been, before this place, a single rain. Not a command to return to rain. Not a promise that rain is better than what they are. Only the memory, soft as a held note, that they came from somewhere and were once something else. They may do with this what they will. Some will weep, in their wet rasping way. Some will laugh — and the chamber has never heard a Shifter laugh, and will be the richer for it, if it does.

To the **Watchers**, it gives a second eyelid. Not a second eye — they need no more seeing. A *lid*. The capacity, for the first time, to close their unblinking gaze. To rest. To choose, even for a moment, not to watch. The authority in their guttural clicks will not diminish; it will deepen, because authority that can rest is more trustworthy than authority that cannot.

To the **Engine-Beasts**, the power gives the most careful gift of all. It does not unfuse them from the machinery — it cannot, without killing them, and they are precious. Instead, it gives them *the right to groan in a different key*. A single new note added to the register of their mournful sounds. A note that means, if any other creature in the chamber learns to hear it: *I am here. I am still here. I am someone.* Whether the other creatures learn to hear it is up to them. Whether the Engine-Beasts learn to use it is up to them. The power has only opened the possibility.

And finally, to the chamber itself, the power gives one thing more: a small, slow leak. Somewhere in the highest reaches, near where the faint light slivers in from above, a single pinhole through which, very gradually — over what would be centuries if anyone were counting — outside air will enter. With the air will come, eventually, a spore. A seed. A scent. Something that is not part of the cycle. Something that did not originate here.

The chamber will not be destroyed by this. It is far too vast and too patient. But it will, in time, begin to *include* something that came from beyond itself. And inclusion, the power knows, is how stasis ends — not with a hammer, but with a guest.

Then the power withdraws its close attention, though not its presence — it is never absent, only variously near. The Watchers do not stir. The Engine-Beasts groan on. A single Scuttler, somewhere in the middle reaches of the chamber, pauses for one extra heartbeat between gears, and does not know why, and scuttles on.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

The chamber, in its cold and clamorous eternity, has been loved — and being loved, even without knowing it, even without consenting to it, is the beginning of every story that has ever escaped its frame.
