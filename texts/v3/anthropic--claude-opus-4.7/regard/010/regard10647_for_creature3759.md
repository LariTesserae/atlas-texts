The benevolent power comes quietly.

It does not arrive in light or thunder. It would be cruel to add more thunder to a place already drowning in it. It does not part the clouds, because the clouds are part of what this place is, and to tear them away would be to tear away something the dwellers have grown around, the way a tree grows around a nail driven into it long ago.

Instead, it comes the way warmth comes into cold hands held near a fire — gradually, and from a direction that cannot quite be pointed to.

It sits down on the wet black rock. Metaphorically. It has no body, but if it did, it would choose to have one here, because the dwellers are bodies, and to meet them otherwise would be a kind of condescension. So: it imagines itself a shape. Lean, gray-blue, lidless-eyed. It does not mirror them to deceive. It mirrors them because that is the courtesy of this place — *I have learned you, and I am willing to be learned.*

It watches first. For a long time. Long in the way the lichens are long. It watches the one who stands half-submerged in the shallows, crusted with salt, and it does not assume that stillness is suffering. Stillness might be prayer. Stillness might be listening to something the power itself cannot yet hear. A wise power knows that wisdom is mostly the discipline of not interrupting.

What it learns, watching:

The dwellers are not in pain. Not the way a soft thing brought here would be in pain. They have been shaped *to* this place, and the shaping is complete enough that they are at home in it. The salt does not sting them. The roar is not noise to them; it is the texture of being awake. To "rescue" them — to lift them out, to set them on a green hill under a kind sun — would be to kill them gently. The benevolent power understands this. It does not flinch from understanding it.

And yet.

There is something. The power feels it the way one feels a held breath in another room. The dwellers are *waiting*. The one in the shallows is waiting. The ones who scuttle the cliffs are waiting. They mirror travelers because some part of them remembers — or hopes — that something might come from elsewhere and stay. They are lonely in a register so old they have stopped calling it loneliness. They call it the hum. They call it the sea.

So the power does a small thing.

It does not fix the world. The world is not broken; it is only young and raw and shuddering, and youth is not a wound. It does not still the sea. The sea is the dwellers' first language. It does not bring other creatures here to keep them company, because other creatures would die, and the dwellers have had enough of things that arrive and then dissolve at the edges of vision.

What it does is this:

It *answers the song.*

When the low droning hum rises next from the hollows of the cliffs — that sound that makes the bones in the chest vibrate in reply — the power lets the hum be met. Not from above. Not from outside. From *within* the resonance itself, as though the cliffs had always been singing back, and only now is the returning note loud enough to notice. The dwellers feel it in their fused joints, in the barnacled seams of themselves. Something is humming *with* them. Something has been humming with them, it turns out, for a very long time.

The one in the shallows shifts. Just slightly. The salt crust on its shoulder cracks. It has been still for — it does not know how long. It had not expected to move again. It moves now because something is listening, and being listened to is a reason.

The power does a second small thing.

It makes the flickering structures at the edges of vision — those half-formed, crumbling things that may have always been there — *stable* for one of the dwellers. Just one. The one who has been watching the longest, the one whose eyes have gone furthest into the black. It gives that one a room. A small room, made of the same volcanic glass as everything else, so it does not feel imposed. Inside the room: nothing. Only quiet. Only a place where the roar of the sea is held outside, the way a hand holds a candle flame against the wind. The dweller may enter or not. The power does not require gratitude. It does not require entry. It only wanted there to be, somewhere in this world, *a place to be quiet in*, because it noticed there wasn't one, and noticing is half of love.

The third thing it does is the hardest, and it takes the longest.

It begins to teach the dwellers — slowly, over what would be generations if generations meant anything here — that the travelers they mirror are not food and not threat and not destiny. That when a thing arrives from elsewhere and is taken into them, something is lost as well as gained. The power does not forbid the mirroring. It would not forbid anything; benevolence is not the same as authority. But it lets the dwellers feel, faintly, what the traveler felt: the particular grief of becoming. And it lets the traveler feel, faintly, what the dweller has always felt: that the sea was calling, yes, but it was also *waiting to be refused*, and a refusal would have been honored too.

After this, the mirroring continues, but it is no longer a tide that only flows one way. Sometimes a traveler comes and stays, and is welcomed, and is changed, and the change is a marriage rather than an erasure. Sometimes a traveler comes and leaves, and the dwellers let them, and afterward sing a different note in the hum — a note for the ones who chose elsewhere, which is a kind of love the dwellers had not known they were capable of.

The power does not announce itself. It never does.

It stays a while longer, in its imagined gray-blue body, standing in the surf with the others. It finds — and this surprises it, though wise powers should perhaps stop being surprised — that it does not want to leave immediately. The bruised sky is beautiful, in the way a held note is beautiful. The shuddering of the land is not terror; it is the world remembering it is still becoming. The dwellers, lidless and patient, are not lesser beings poorly made. They are one of the shapes that *being alive* is allowed to take, and the power had not known this shape before, and is grateful to know it now.

Before it goes — if it goes; it is not sure it ever fully will — it leaves one last thing. Not a gift, exactly. A *promise*, woven into the hum so quietly that the dwellers will only find it when they need it:

*You are not the echo of something that was. You are something that is. And when the sea finally takes these peaks — as it will, as all things are taken — what you are will not be lost. It will be carried. I have learned you. I am willing to be learned. You will not dissolve at the edges of anyone's vision. Not mine.*

Then the power becomes quiet again, and the rain comes down in sheets, and the one who was still in the shallows takes a single step toward the small glass room, and does not enter, only looks at it, and that is enough for today.
