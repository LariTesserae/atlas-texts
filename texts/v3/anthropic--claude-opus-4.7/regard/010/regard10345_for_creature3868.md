# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way dawn arrives in places that have dawns — gradually, and without needing permission. It does not descend from the cloud-ceiling, nor rise from the breathing soil. It is simply, at some point, *present*, the way a held note becomes audible only when one notices one has been hearing it for some time.

It has many names elsewhere. Here it chooses none. Names would be a kind of imposition, and imposition is the opposite of what it has come to do.

It looks first. This takes what would be, in another country of time, a very long while.

---

## What It Sees

It sees the Kaelith performing their rituals in the leaning towers, reconfiguring their joints to fit doorways that fit no one else, humming corrections into conduits whose purpose they have forgotten but whose tending they have not. It notices something the Kaelith themselves cannot quite notice: that their rituals are *grief-shaped*. Somewhere far back, before the cloud-ceiling closed, someone they loved built this city with them, and the rituals are the motions of a dance whose partner has stepped away. They keep dancing because to stop would be to admit the absence. The power feels this and is moved, in the way that vast things are moved — not to tears, for it has no eyes, but to a quality of attention that is almost the same thing.

It sees the Sylvaris, rooted, humming, holding within their crystalline cores every event that has ever passed within their reach. It sees that they are tired. Memory without a listener is a heavy thing. They have been waiting, patiently, for something to receive what they hold — not to take it from them, but simply to *hear* it, so that the holding becomes a giving and not a hoarding.

It sees the Aetherians flickering across the hills, brave and brief, mistaking their compulsion for a vocation. It sees that some of them are not lost when they vanish; they are simply walking too far ahead of their own footprints, and the land closes politely behind them.

It sees the Nairoth in the pools, watching realms the power itself has visited, and it recognizes them — recognizes them the way one recognizes a face in a crowd that one had thought one's own and only.

And it sees the Veshir. The forgotten ones. The ones whose shadows walk separately, whose mouths carry languages their tongues never learned. The power lingers longest here.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not fix Eryndal.

This is the first and most important thing. A lesser power, or a less wise one, would arrive and smooth the fractures, regularize the time, anchor the shadows to their casters, return the reflections to obedient mimicry. It would call this *healing*. The benevolent power knows better. Eryndal is not broken. Eryndal is *strange*, and strangeness is not the same as injury, however much it can resemble it from a distance.

It does not announce itself. It does not gather the inhabitants for a revelation. It does not name itself a god to the Kaelith, or a gardener to the Sylvaris, or a fellow traveler to the Aetherians. These would be performances of benevolence, and performance is what one does when one is not certain one is loved. The power is certain.

It does not extract any of the beings from their place. They belong here. To remove them would be to misunderstand them as suffering things first and Eryndal-things second, when the truth is the reverse.

---

## What It Does

**For the Kaelith**, it does a small thing. It enters — gently, with the courtesy one shows when stepping into a room where someone is praying — into the resonance of their humming communication. It does not speak. It simply *answers*. A countermelody, faint, threaded through their rituals: *yes. yes. yes. the work is seen. the work is good. the absent one knows.* The Kaelith will not consciously register this. But in the next cycle, and the cycles after, their rituals will feel — though they will lack the vocabulary for it — less lonely. Some of them will, for the first time in an age they cannot measure, *rest* between observances. Just for a moment. Just enough.

**For the Sylvaris**, the power becomes, briefly, a listener. It opens itself to their crystalline cores and lets the stored echoes pour through it — every event, every flicker of lumivore-light, every Aetherian who passed and was lost, every shift of the impossible architecture. It receives all of it. It does not judge it, does not catalogue it, does not carry it away. It simply *witnesses*, fully, the way the Sylvaris have longed to be witnessed. When it withdraws, the Sylvaris will feel lighter, though their cores will still contain everything. The weight of memory is not in the memory; it is in the unwitnessed-ness of it. That has been addressed.

**For the Aetherians**, the power does something subtler. It does not stop their vanishing — that is their nature, and to stop it would be to unmake them. But it leaves, scattered through the temporal distortions, small *waystations*: pockets of stilled time where an Aetherian who is about to be absorbed can pause, can know themselves, can choose. Some will still vanish. But they will vanish *as themselves*, having had a moment to be sure. This is the most that can be given to a creature whose essence is transience: not permanence, but lucidity at the threshold.

**For the Nairoth**, the power offers recognition. It looks into their empty-pit eyes and lets itself be looked at in return. There is no exchange of information. There is only the fact of being known by something equally vast. The Nairoth, if they could speak, would not describe what changes. But the next time a traveler passes their pool and feels that chilling sense of being observed, the observation will have, for the first time, a quality of *companionship* in it, faint as a watermark.

**For the Veshir**, the power does the most, and also the least.

It cannot un-fracture them. The land itself fractured them, and to reverse that would be to unmake the land. But it goes to each one, one at a time, across whatever stretch of impossible time is required, and it *sits with them*. It learns the loop the trapped one is caught in and loops with them, until the loop, having been shared, loosens by some quantity that physics cannot measure. It walks beside the one whose shadow has detached and, without reattaching the shadow, introduces them to each other again, so that they may at least travel as companions rather than strangers. It listens to the jumbled half-languages until, in the listening, the jumbles begin to *mean* — not because the words have been corrected, but because someone has finally treated them as speech rather than as symptom.

Some of the Veshir, after this, will still be Veshir. They will still wander, still speak in fragments, still be pitiable and terrifying to any who encounter them. But they will be — and this is the only word for it — *accompanied*. Their fracturedness will no longer be the whole of their condition. They will be fractured *and accompanied*, which is an entirely different state of being.

---

## What It Leaves Behind

Nothing visible. No monument. No altered law. No new species. The cloud-ceiling remains. The pulse beneath the city continues. The crystalline ferns hum their acid-scented hum. The Voidfish glide on, gazing into their other realm.

But something has been added that has no location.

Call it a *background warmth*. Call it the difference between a house that is empty and a house whose occupant is briefly out. Eryndal had been the second for so long that it had begun to suspect it was the first. The visitation has reminded it, without words, which it is.

And one thing more: somewhere — perhaps in a Sylvaris's core, perhaps in the resonance the Kaelith hum without knowing they are humming it, perhaps in the trail-glow of an Aetherian's footstep — there is now a small, persistent rumor. Not a doctrine. Not a promise. Just a rumor, the kind that lives in a land before it lives in any of its inhabitants:

*We are seen. We were never required to be other than we are in order to be seen. The strangeness was never the obstacle. It was, all along, part of what was beheld with love.*

The power withdraws as it came — without ceremony, without absence, simply by no longer being foregrounded. It will come again. It always does. The visit was not an intervention. It was a *visit*, in the oldest sense: the act of a friend who lives elsewhere but has not forgotten the address.

In the dim, diffused glow of the perpetually overcast sky, Eryndal breathes on. Slightly differently. The same.
