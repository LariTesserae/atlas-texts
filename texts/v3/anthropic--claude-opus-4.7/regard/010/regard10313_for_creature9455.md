The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives here: as a thinning, a shift in pressure, a presence that does not announce itself because announcement would be a kind of violence against a place that has spent so long learning to whisper.

It has been watching for what, in its reckoning, amounts to a long attention. It has seen the consortium-people calibrate themselves against scarcity, seen them refuse the easy solidities that other peoples on other worlds grasped at when their lands grew difficult. It has noticed something rare: that they have made a virtue of the unfinished. That they do not experience their land's indecision as a wound.

This matters to the power, because the power is old enough to have made mistakes.

It has, in earlier ages, descended on places like this with the full weight of its capacity. It has stabilized embryonic ranges, accelerated the consolidation of soils, taught rivers their proper courses. It has, with the best of intentions, finished things. And it has watched, afterwards, as the peoples who lived in those places grew quieter in a way that was not the careful quietness of conservation but the duller quietness of having been relieved of their argument with the world. They became guests in landscapes that no longer required them. The power learned that completion, offered too generously, is a kind of theft.

So it does not finish anything here.

Instead, it does something smaller and, by its own standards, harder. It listens. It spends what would be, by human measure, several seasons simply attending. It learns the gestures of the hand-language, the way a wrist-turn can mean *the seam will widen by spring* and a slight folding of the fingers can mean *I have heard you, and I am thinking*. It learns the hymns the maintenance-singers offer to their nanites, and it understands that these are not commands but courtesies — the people asking the small machines to please continue being themselves, in this particular way, for a little while longer.

When at last the power chooses to act, its actions are nearly indistinguishable from non-action. This is deliberate.

It does four things.

The first is a gift so quiet that the people will not know it has been given for many cycles. The power notices that the consortium's antifreeze compounds, elegant as they are, carry a slow cost: a particular protein folds wrong, rarely, in the very old, and the wrongness compounds across generations in ways the people have not yet had the leisure to study. The power adjusts nothing in the living. It alters only the ambient chemistry of one rare mineral that weeps from certain rocks — a mineral the people already harvest for brine — so that henceforth its trace elements will, when metabolized, gently correct the fold. The people will discover, eventually, that their elders are lasting longer. They will attribute this to their own care, and they will be largely right. The power's contribution will be the size of a rumor.

The second is a refusal. The power has the capacity to stabilize the embryonic ridges, to tell the land which version of itself to be. It declines. More than declines: it places a soft interdiction around this region, a courtesy extended to other powers that might come wandering with helpful intentions. A sign, in a language only such powers can read, that says: *this place is in the middle of a sentence. Do not finish it for them.* The interdiction is not a wall. It is closer to a held breath, a request for restraint, the kind of thing one wise guest does for another at a host's table.

The third is a small, strange gift to the walkers of the marsh-edges. The power has noticed how these people leave offerings of heat and brine for the fauna, how they treat predation as diplomacy. It is moved by this — moved being a word that, for the power, means something has been added to its own long calibration of what is possible. So it teaches one of the burrowers, just one, in one warm margin of the city, a slightly better way of cultivating its fungus-lanterns. The burrower will teach its kin. The kin will teach the walkers, in the way burrowers and walkers have always taught each other — by example, by the shifting of moisture maps. In a generation, the micro-jungles will be a little brighter, the negotiations a little more generous on both sides. No one will know where the lesson came from. The power prefers it this way. Gifts that announce themselves create debts; gifts that arrive as if from the land create kinship.

The fourth thing is the one the power thinks about longest before doing.

It considers leaving a message. Some sign that the people have been seen — truly seen, in their patience and their argument with the unfinished — by something vast. The power knows what such a sign would mean to a people. It has given such signs before, and it has watched them become religions, become certainties, become the kind of fixed points around which a people stops moving.

These people do not need a fixed point. Their genius is that they have refused one.

So the power does not leave a message. Instead, it leaves a question, embedded in the place itself, encoded so subtly that it will only be found by someone who is already looking for it, and only understood by someone who has already arrived at the answer. The question is laid into the lag of a single reflecting pool, into the rhythm of one heat-lamp's pulse, into the way a particular bridge reconfigures its segments. The question is: *what will you be, when you decide?*

It is not a question that demands answering. It is a question that honors the asking.

And then the power does the thing that is, for it, the deepest courtesy: it leaves. Not entirely — it keeps a thread of attention turned toward this place, the way one keeps a hand resting lightly on a sleeping animal, ready to still it if it startles. But it withdraws its presence. It does not stay to watch what its small adjustments will become. It does not linger to be thanked, or noticed, or mythologized. It accepts that benevolence, practiced well, often looks from the inside like having done almost nothing at all.

Long after, on a half-formed ridge, an elder of the consortium will pause in her walking. She will feel something — not a presence, exactly, but the shape of a presence that has been and is no longer. She will tilt her head. She will make, with her free hand, a gesture that has no formal meaning in the hand-language: a slow opening of the fingers, palm upward, as if releasing something she was not aware she had been holding.

She will not know what she is doing. She will only know that the cold, for a moment, feels less like insinuation and more like company. That the land beneath her feet seems to be testing her weight with a kindness she had not previously named.

She will resume her walking. The seam she has come to read is widening on schedule. The basin below is nearly ready to receive. The city glows in its not-quite-light, and the weather walks its slow lap around the range, and somewhere a burrower is teaching another burrower a slightly better way to tend its lanterns.

The power, attending from its distance, registers the gesture. It does not respond. Responding would be another kind of finishing.

It simply notes, with something that in a smaller being would be called joy: *they are still becoming. Good. Good.*

And the place continues to hold its breath, waiting to see which version of itself it will be — only now, though no one there could say how they know it, the waiting feels a little less lonely.
