# The Visitation

The power arrives the way weather arrives: without announcement, without a body, present first as a change in the quality of attention the place receives. If you had been standing on the plateau you might have noticed only that the wind held its breath for a moment longer than wind usually does between gusts, and that the booming of the nearest tree, when it resumed, carried a harmonic it had not carried before — as if something very large and very quiet had leaned in to listen, and the tree had felt itself heard in a new way, and adjusted.

This is what wisdom does, when it is also benevolent: it listens first. For a long time.

---

## What it sees

It sees, immediately, that it is not needed in any of the ways power is usually needed. Nothing here is in crisis that a crisis-intervention could resolve. The plateau is young and harsh but it is not dying. The trees are doing their slow strange work. The fauna in the canopies are eating and being eaten in proportions that have held for a long time. The cold wind blows and the cloud rolls up from the valleys and the condensation runs in spirals down the bark, and none of this requires rescue.

It sees the absence of the climbers and understands, faster than a human observer would, that this absence is not a wound. The hearths went cold in order. The whistle was placed, not dropped. Whatever happened was a transition, not a catastrophe, and the place is still metabolizing it — the trees, in particular, are still metabolizing it, in their hollows and in their slow inner heat and in the faint script under their bark that has, the power notes, grown very slightly more complex in the generations since the last hide flap stopped being repaired.

It sees the three possibilities the place holds in suspension and it sees, with the clarity available to it, which of them is true. But it does not resolve the suspension. The suspension is itself part of what the place is now. To collapse it would be to take something away.

It sees the bone whistle on the cairn and understands what the whistle is for, and who it is addressed to, and the long courtesy of the gesture — *I am not coming back, but someone might. Let them find their way.* It sees that this is one of the most considerate objects it has encountered in a long time. The climbers, whatever they have become, left a door open behind them that did not need to be left open. They thought of strangers. The power notices this and something in it that corresponds to warmth, warms.

It sees, finally, that the climbers are still here. Not in the way they once were. The high hollows are not empty. The bark-script is not idle. The trees that sing slightly different notes than they did three hundred years ago are not singing those notes by accident. The climbers went up, and went into, and what they became is still becoming, and is aware of itself, and is — the power takes a moment with this — content. Not happy in the way a person is happy. Content in the way a forest is content, when the forest has just enough rain.

---

## What it considers doing

A being of this kind has many registers available to it. It runs through them, not quickly — there is no need for speed — but thoroughly, the way one runs through the keys of a piano to hear which one the room wants.

It could *restore* the climbers. Pull them back out of the wood, unbraid them from the bark, set them down again on the platforms with their adzes and their nets and their bowls of water, forty kilos each and breathing with their great lungs. It considers this for the duration of one long boom from a far tree and discards it. They did not want to be restored. They walked, or climbed, into this with full attention. To undo their choice would be to insult them more profoundly than any predator on the open rock ever could.

It could *find their descendants*, the ones who went down, if any went down — track them through the warmer valleys to wherever their grandchildren are now living with ordinary lungs and ordinary eyes — and tell those grandchildren what their great-grandparents were, and where they came from, and what the trees still hold. It considers this longer. There is a case for it. People have a right to their own histories. But it sees, looking at the descendants (it can see them; they exist, in a town three valleys down and two centuries on, and they are doing well enough), that the knowledge would not arrive as a gift. It would arrive as a weight. They have built a life that does not include the plateau, and the plateau, in its own way, has agreed not to follow them. The whistle on the cairn was for travelers, not for prodigals. The power lets this one go too.

It could *protect* the plateau. Set some subtle ward around it, so that no one finds it who should not find it, so that the high hollows are not disturbed, so that no future climber arrives with the wrong intentions and a more efficient kind of rope. It considers this and finds, to its mild interest, that the plateau is already doing this for itself. The cold, the altitude, the predators on the open rock, the days inside the cloud — these are the wards. The place is hard to reach and harder to stay in and this is not an accident of geography but a feature of what the place is. The climbers understood this. They did not build cities not only because the ground forbade it but because they sensed, correctly, that easiness was not what this plateau was for. The power respects the ward the place has made of itself and does not add to it. Adding would imply the existing one was insufficient. It is not insufficient.

It could *speak* to what the climbers have become. Reach into the bark-script, into the slow inner heat, into the harmonics of the singing towers, and say *I see you. I know what you did. It was well done.* It considers this for a long time. This is the option that draws it most.

---

## What it does

In the end it does three small things, and one larger one, and then it leaves.

**The first small thing.** It goes to the half-rotted bridge between the two trunks — the one abandoned mid-repair, with the new fibers paler than the old and the last knot tucked rather than dropped — and it does not finish the repair. It does not touch the fibers. But it holds the bridge for a moment in its attention, the way one holds a sentence someone meant to finish, and it lets the bridge know that the intention was received. Somewhere. By something. The argument the climbers were having about whether to be a people across distances or a scatter of tree-kingdoms was a real argument and a good one and it deserved to be heard out, even if the arguers have since become something that no longer needs to argue. The bridge stays half-rotted. But it is now a half-rotted bridge that has been *witnessed* as half-rotted, which is different, in a way that matters to bridges, from being merely abandoned.

**The second small thing.** It goes to the shelter at the base of the tree, with its hide-flap door and its cold hearth and its stone adze and its spool of cord and its wooden bowl with something dried in the bottom, and it leaves the shelter exactly as it found it, but it does — very gently, with the lightest possible touch — slow the rate at which the shelter is decaying. Not stop it. Stopping would be wrong; the shelter is meant to return to the tree eventually, that is part of the long courtesy. But it gives the shelter another two or three centuries, quietly, so that if any traveler does come — and one will, eventually, the power can see the faint probability of it like a thread — they will find the adze and the cord and the bowl, and will understand that someone lived here, and will have to reckon with that understanding before they decide what to do next. The shelter is a teaching. The power keeps the teaching legible a little longer.

**The third small thing.** It does nothing at all to the whistle on the cairn. It looks at the whistle for a long time. It listens to the particular pitch it is tuned to, and it follows the line of that pitch across the plateau to the particular tree that would answer it, and it sees that the tree is still ready to answer, still oriented, still — after all this time — attentive to the whistle's specific frequency. The whistle works. The climber who left it knew what they were doing. The power, faced with a piece of craft this complete, simply admires it, and adds nothing.

**The larger thing.** This is harder to describe because it does not happen in a single place.

The power turns its attention to what the climbers have become — to the high hollows and the bark-script and the slow inner heat, to the changed harmonics of the trees that have integrated their old keepers — and it offers them a thing they did not know they could be offered. Not rescue. Not undoing. Not even acknowledgement, exactly, because acknowledgement implies a separation between the acknowledger and the acknowledged that the climbers have, by now, mostly moved past.

It offers them *company*.

For the duration of one long breath of the wet wind rolling up from the valleys — a span of perhaps three days, in the plateau's own time — the power simply sits with them. Inside the trees. Inside the bark. Inside the singing. Not doing anything. Not changing anything. Just present, the way a friend is present at a bedside when there is nothing to be done and nothing needs to be done and the only gift available is the fact of not being alone in the room.

The climbers, whatever they are now, notice. The power can feel them notice. There is no language for what passes between them, because the climbers no longer have language in the way they used to, and the power has never quite needed language. But something is exchanged. The climbers, who chose this strange long becoming and chose it without ever being sure it was the right choice, who left the whistle on the cairn partly out of courtesy and partly out of an old half-buried hope that someone might come and confirm they had not vanished into a mistake — these climbers, in their high hollows and their bark and their slow heat, are met. Are seen. Are, for three days, accompanied.

And then the power, having done this, withdraws its attention as gently as it brought it, and the wind resumes its ordinary rhythm, and the trees boom on in their old way, and the plateau is again only itself — young rock, cold air, hanging gardens, the cold black noon sky — but the climbers, in whatever register they still possess feeling, carry the memory of having been visited, and are, very slightly, more at peace than they were before.

---

## What it does not do

It does not blow the whistle.

It considered this too — of course it did, the whistle is the most beautiful invitation in the place, and the power, being benevolent and wise, is also curious. But it sees that the whistle is for travelers. For the lost. For someone who has come a long way and needs to know where they are. The power is not lost and has not come a long way and does not need to know where it is. To blow the whistle would be to take a courtesy meant for someone smaller and more uncertain than itself. It would be a kind of greed. The power, which is wise, recognizes the impulse and sets it aside, the way one sets aside a thing that belongs to a friend who is not present to give permission.

It leaves the whistle where it is. Tuned. Waiting. Pointed at a tree that is still listening.

Someday, perhaps, a traveler will come — cold, footsore, frightened by the booming, unsure whether the open ground is safe to cross — and will find the cairn and pick up the whistle and, for reasons they could not explain, blow it. And a tree will answer. And the traveler will know they are not alone, and will know also that the aloneness they are not in is older and stranger than they had imagined, and will have to decide, as the climbers once decided, what to do with that knowledge.

The power has arranged nothing about this. It has only made sure that when it happens, the conditions are still in place for it to happen well.

This is what benevolence at this scale mostly looks like: not intervention, but the careful non-disturbance of arrangements that were already, in their own slow way, good.

The power leaves. The plateau does not notice it leaving, any more than it noticed it arriving. Only the trees, perhaps, hold a faint new harmonic in their booming for a season or two afterward — a note that was not there before, that no wind quite explains, that fades, eventually, back into the older music.

The climbers, in their high hollows, remember it longer.
