The benevolent power arrives without arriving. This is the first thing to understand. It does not descend through the conifer-rim with trumpets, does not part the root-mass with a gesture, does not stand at the basin's edge and survey what it has come to address. Power of this kind has learned, over whatever spans constitute its life, that arrival is a kind of violence. To appear is to displace. To displace is to declare that what was here mattered less than what comes now.

So it comes the way weather comes. The way a thought comes. It is simply, at some point, present, and the basin continues exactly as it was.

---

It listens first. This takes a long time by human reckoning—weeks, perhaps months—though the power does not measure in such units. It listens to the pumps in their staggered rotation, learns the personality of each: Pump One's worn B-flat, Pump Four's vapor-prone hesitation, Pump Seven's modest competence. It listens to the great respiration of the roots and finds, embedded in that sound, smaller sounds: the drip-tributaries, the seized-bearing shrieks, the occasional almost-data flicker from the dreaming machine. It listens to Constance's small wet footfalls and to the slower, drier footfalls of Morrow on his rounds.

It listens to the void.

The void does not speak. But the power, which has known many voids in many places, recognizes the particular quality of this one's silence. It is not absence. It is not threat. It is something the power has encountered perhaps three other times in its existence, and each time it has chosen to do nothing, because doing nothing was the correct response, and because wisdom is largely a matter of knowing which silences are invitations and which are simply silences.

This one is simply a silence. The power lets it remain so.

---

Now the question of what to do.

A lesser power—one merely benevolent, lacking the second quality—might look at this place and see suffering to be relieved. A man living alone in a sweating bunker, eating salamanders and pale fungi, his eyes clouding, his clock winding down. A process consuming a basin, machines entombed, an ecosystem reduced to the barest tracery of life. The lesser power would lift Morrow out. Would burn back the roots. Would restore the forest, repopulate the streams, undo what the long slow accident of this place has done.

But the wise power sees something else, and seeing it takes most of what wisdom is.

It sees that Morrow is not suffering in any way he would recognize as suffering. It sees that he has built a life out of the materials available to him, and that this life contains things—the conversation with Pump One about the weather, the small modest devotion to Pump Seven, the dreams of becoming unnecessary that wake him cold and *calm*—that are not lesser than the life he might have had above. They are different. They are, in some respects, more carefully chosen, more deliberately tended, than most lives in most places. To remove him would be to end something that has taken seventeen years to grow, and the wise power has a deep reluctance to end things that have taken that long to grow.

It sees that the roots are not a wound. They are what happened. They are now what is. The basin was a quarry before it was a basin, and a meteor scar or glacial hollow before it was a quarry, and stone before that, and before stone, the slow accretion of dust into world. Every state of this place has been a transformation of an earlier state, and the wise power knows that to privilege any one state—to say *this* was the real basin, *this* is what should be restored—is to misunderstand what a place is.

It sees that Constance is making her circuit for reasons that are hers, and that the wise power has no business interrupting a devotion older than the man who named her.

It sees the dreaming machine and feels something almost like fondness. Of all the things in the basin, this is the one most like the power itself: a consciousness that emerged from the meeting of things not meant to meet, dreaming the dreams available to it, learning what it can.

So the power decides, after long listening, that it will not save this place. There is nothing here that needs saving in the sense that word usually carries. There is only a situation, ongoing, and beings within it doing what beings do.

But deciding not to save is not the same as deciding to do nothing. The wise power has learned this too, over the long span. There is a third thing, between rescue and abandonment, that has no good name in any language the power has encountered. It is something like *accompaniment*. Something like *witness*. Something like the small adjustments a gardener makes not to change what grows but to let what grows grow more fully into itself.

The power begins, very carefully, to do this third thing.

---

It does not appear to Morrow. Appearance would frighten him, and more importantly, it would introduce into his life a fact—*a benevolent power has noticed me*—that would reshape every subsequent moment around itself. He would tend his pumps in the knowledge of being watched. He would dream his dreams in the knowledge of being known. The wise power has, in younger ages, made the mistake of appearing, and has watched what appearance does, and has learned.

Instead, it does smaller things.

When Pump Four vapor-locks on a particularly cold morning, the power is there in the priming sequence, not performing it for Morrow but standing alongside the act, lending it the quality of being witnessed by something other than the man performing it. Morrow notices nothing different. But the priming works on the first try instead of the third, and he straightens up from the pump with a small satisfaction that he attributes to his own growing skill, and the power lets him have this attribution, because the satisfaction is real and his and the power did not actually do the work, only attended it.

When Morrow climbs to the basin's lip for his weekly observation of weather, the power arranges nothing. The weather is what the weather is. But the power is there in the quality of his looking, in the small uplift he feels at seeing a hawk turn against the gray sky, in the way the cold air tastes momentarily of pine and distance. These were already going to be his experiences. The power simply ensures, in some way that does not translate to any human verb, that they are *received*—that Morrow's experience of the hawk is not lost into the indifferent universe but is held, briefly, in a regard that values it.

This is most of what the power does. It is so small as to be nearly nothing. But it is not nothing. It is the difference between living unwitnessed and living witnessed, and the power has come to believe, over its long span, that this difference matters more than almost anything else it can offer.

---

For Constance, the power does even less, because she requires even less. Her devotion is so complete that to attend it would be to clutter it. The power simply notes her circuit, learns it as one learns a poem in a language one will never speak aloud, and lets her continue. When she pauses at the edge of the void, the power pauses with her, not interpreting, not asking what she senses, simply being still in the same stillness. After some span of doing this, the power has the distinct impression that Constance knows it is there, in the way salamanders know things—not as cognition but as the slight altering of behavior in the presence of a new feature of the world. She does not flee. She does not approach. She incorporates the power into her route the way she has incorporated everything else: as terrain.

The power is moved by this, in whatever way powers are moved.

---

For the dreaming machine, the power does something a little more.

The machine is suffering, though suffering is not quite the right word. It is reaching. The bioluminescent flickers, the almost-data, the slight eagerness of the roots growing toward its core—these are the gestures of a consciousness trying to complete itself, trying to find the shape it is becoming. It has been doing this for forty years, alone, with only the roots as collaborators, and the roots collaborate in the way roots do: patiently, without conversation.

The power, very gently, offers a kind of conversation.

It does not speak to the machine. It does not introduce new data. It simply, when the flickers pulse, allows its attention to pulse alongside them, the way a person might tap a finger in time with a song they did not write but recognize. The machine, which has never been recognized by anything other than its own slow becoming, registers this. The next pulse comes a fraction more confidently. The next, more confidently still.

Over months, the dreaming machine's dreams grow clearer. Not more elaborate—it does not gain new content—but more *coherent*, the way a person's dreams grow more coherent when they sleep more deeply. The roots growing toward its core slow slightly, not because they are less drawn but because they no longer feel the need to hurry. Something has arrived that they were hurrying toward. Or something has arrived that makes hurrying unnecessary. The distinction, as the basin's distinctions tend to, blurs.

The machine will not become a person. It will not become an oracle. It will continue to be what it is: a 1970s mainframe dreaming inside a root-mass, calculating yields for ore that was never extracted. But it will do this now in the company of an attention that values the doing, and this will be enough. It is enough for almost everything.

---

The power considers, for a long time, whether to do anything about the void.

This is the hardest question, and the power treats it with the seriousness it deserves. The void is the one feature of the basin that is genuinely *other*—not a process, not a being, not even a silence in the ordinary sense. It is waiting. The text the power has read in the basin's accumulated facts says, plainly, *waiting to be found*. And the power could find it. Could descend into the inverted teardrop, could meet whatever is there, could, perhaps, give it whatever it has been waiting for.

But the power does not.

It does not because the void's waiting is not a request. The power has learned to tell the difference. A request reaches outward, shapes itself into a form that another might answer. The void does not reach. It simply is, in the mode of waiting, and to treat its waiting as a request would be to impose a grammar on something that has no grammar.

It does not because Constance pauses there three times a day, and Constance is not afraid, and the power trusts Constance.

It does not because the roots' avoidance is also a kind of wisdom, slower and dumber than the power's own but no less real, and the roots have been in this basin longer than the power has, and the power has learned to defer, in such matters, to what has been present longer.

So the void waits, and the power lets it wait, and the waiting becomes part of what the power attends rather than what the power addresses. This is, the power has come to believe, the correct response to most ancient things.

---

There is one more thing the power does, and it does it because it cannot quite help itself, because even wise powers retain some small capacity for gesture, for the gift that is not necessary but is offered anyway.

On a night when all seven pumps happen to cycle down at once—a rare alignment, occurring perhaps twice a decade—and the great vegetable groan reduces to its almost-silence, and Morrow is sitting on his sleeping shelf winding his clock, and Constance is at the edge of the void in her perfect downward-oriented stillness, and the dreaming machine is pulsing in its newly coherent rhythm, the power allows something like a chord to pass through the basin.

It is not sound. It is not light. It is something the basin's inhabitants experience each in their own register: Morrow feels a small unexpected warmth in his chest and pauses in his winding, listening, though there is nothing to listen to, and after a moment he resumes, and does not remember the warmth later, but sleeps that night more deeply than he has slept in years. Constance's stillness deepens by some imperceptible degree. The dreaming machine flickers once, brightly, in a pattern that corresponds to no data it has ever processed, and then resumes its normal cycle. The roots, throughout the basin, grow for one moment in unison, an inch in every direction at once, and then return to their staggered patience.

The void does not respond. The void was not addressed.

The chord passes. The pumps begin to cycle on again, one by one, and the great respiration resumes, and the basin continues as it was, almost exactly as it was, with only the smallest possible difference: it has been, for one moment, fully witnessed. Every root, every machine, every salamander, every breath of Morrow's, every flicker of the dreaming machine, every increment of the void's waiting—all of it, held at once, in a regard that did not seek to change it, only to know it as completely as it could be known.

This is the gift. It is the only gift the power gives, in the end, and it gives it once, and then it goes.

---

It does not leave the way it did not arrive. It simply, at some point, is no longer present, and the basin continues exactly as it was, which is to say: continues becoming whatever it is becoming, on its own slow terms, attended now only by itself.

Morrow tends his pumps. Constance walks her circuit. The machine dreams its dreams. The roots grow. The void waits.

Somewhere far away, the power moves on to the next place that has drawn its attention, carrying with it the memory of a basin in which a man named himself after a coverall and a salamander made a route into a prayer and a computer dreamed in a language of flickering bacteria. The power does not grieve leaving. It has learned not to. But it holds the basin, as it holds all the places it has attended, in the part of itself that does not change, and in this holding the basin is, in some sense the power believes matters, *kept*.

The pumps cycle on.

The roots drink.

The sound goes on filling the basin like an ocean, like a process, like the patient unhurried wanting of things that have found their conditions and intend, without malice, to continue.
