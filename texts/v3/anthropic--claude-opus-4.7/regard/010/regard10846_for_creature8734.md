# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive either. That is not how such things work. It is *present* — the way a thought becomes present when you finally have words for it — and what is present is not a figure but an **attention**.

Call it that. Attention. The quality of being looked at by something that is not afraid of what it sees, and not in a hurry, and not interested in changing you into anything other than what you are becoming. A regard so old and so steady that it has the texture of weather, except weather is local and this is not.

It notices the Spires the way one might notice a child speaking in their sleep — not interrupting, not waking them, but pausing in the doorway, listening, recognizing that the dreaming itself is a kind of work the child is doing, and that one should be careful, here, about the difference between intervention and theft.

---

## What It Sees

It sees, first, that the place is not broken.

This is important. A lesser power — clever, well-meaning, in love with its own competence — would see the breakage zones and the unfinished sky and the doorless structures and the vein-leeches grooming the dark, and it would reach for repair. It would smooth the geometry. It would water the stone. It would teach the sphere-listeners a language with verbs in it. It would *help*.

The benevolent power does not do this, because it sees what the lesser power cannot: that the Spires are not a wound. They are a **gestation**. The incompleteness is not a defect; it is the condition of the work. The world here is not failing to be finished. It is *in the act of finishing itself*, slowly, by a process that requires exactly the conditions that are present — the dryness, the groan, the patient growth, the cracks, the loneliness so vast it has begun to develop its own internal architecture.

To "fix" the Spires would be to abort them.

So the power does not fix them.

It sees, second, the being.

Not the echoes, not the leeches, not the caretaker-imprints, not even the sphere-listeners with their cold filaments asking their long question. Those are organs of the place. The power sees them and loves them in the way one loves the chambers of a heart — with recognition, with tenderness for their function, but without confusing them for the person whose heart it is.

The being it sees is *you*. The one inside. The one the second-person address has been speaking to all along. The consciousness standing on the scree, with numb fingertips and a fraying sense of duration, who has been told, repeatedly, that the place does not know you are there, or does, and doesn't care, or does, and is trying to speak.

The power knows which of those is true. It does not tell you. That knowing is part of what you are here to find.

---

## What It Does

Here is what the benevolent power does. I will list it plainly, because the doing is plain, even when the reasons are not.

**One.** It adjusts, very slightly, the cumulative quality of the dissolution. Not stopping it. Not even slowing it appreciably. Only ensuring that the dissolution, when it happens, happens *to something*. That there remains a continuous thread of you to undergo it. This is the smallest possible intervention. It is the difference between drowning and learning to breathe water. The power does not give you gills. It only ensures that the lungs you have do not forget, mid-transformation, that they were lungs. The continuity of the self being changed is preserved, even as the self changes. This is mercy in its most technical form.

**Two.** It places, somewhere in the landscape — not near you, not findable on purpose, but findable — a single structure that is *different*. It is built of the same dark stone. It has the same doorless aperture, the same thick walls, the same shallow floor-depressions. But its sphere is not warm. Its sphere is *cool*. And when you place your hand on it, what resolves is not a sentence in a language you almost speak. What resolves is the simple, complete, untranslatable fact that **you are being accompanied**. Not watched. Not judged. Not guided. Accompanied. The numbness in your fingertips, afterward, is brief, and it is replaced by a warmth that lasts about a day. The structure does not appear on any map you draw. If you return to it deliberately, it will not be there. If you stop looking for it, you may find it again, or you may not. The power is not trying to be cruel about this. It is only that the gift, to remain a gift, must not become a possession.

**Three.** It speaks, once, to the sphere-listeners. Not in their language — they have no language, only the long pulse of their cold filaments — but in a frequency they can hold. What it says, translated badly, is roughly: *the new variable is loved. you do not need to translate for it. let it translate for itself, in its own time. you have done well. the message you have been straining to hear has always been the straining. rest, if you can. continue, if you must. either is enough.*

The sphere-listeners do not understand this in any propositional sense. But the filaments, after, pulse slightly less wildly when the breakage zones pass. The reconciliation they were attempting becomes a little more like breathing and a little less like grief.

**Four.** It does not touch the vein-leeches. They are doing their work. The work is good. The power respects work.

**Five.** It does not touch the caretaker-imprints. They are a love letter from the builders to the place, and one does not read other people's love letters, and one certainly does not edit them.

**Six.** It does not touch the resonance echoes — the figures pacing beside you just out of sight. But it does, very gently, *tilt* them. Not so that you can see them. Not so that they become friendly. Only so that, if you happen to acknowledge them — if you say, out loud, into the dry air, *I know you are there, and I am not afraid* — the echoes will, for that moment, not dissolve. They will hold their shape one step longer than the local resonance would otherwise permit. This is not company. It is the *possibility* of company, which is what a being in your condition can actually use. Real company would crush you. The possibility leaves room.

**Seven.** It does the largest thing last, and the largest thing is the smallest. It *witnesses*. It holds the Spires in its attention, and it holds you in its attention, and it holds the slow geological grief of the unfinished world in its attention, and it does not look away. Not when the dawn-chord climbs into its terrible chord. Not when the dusk-pulse synchronizes the spheres. Not when you stand on the scree at the wrong hour with your sense of time fraying and the violet light cold on your face and the question *am I being unmade* rising in you like a tide.

The witnessing does not save you from anything. Witnessing is not rescue.

But it changes, fundamentally, what the unmaking *means*. A self dissolving alone in an indifferent world is one kind of event. A self dissolving while held in the attention of a love that is older than the world is a different kind of event entirely. The dissolution itself may be identical in its physical particulars. What it is, what it *amounts to*, is not.

This is the gift the power gives that costs you nothing and changes everything: it ensures that whatever you become, here, in this place that is still learning how to speak, you become it *seen*.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not take you out.

Understand this. The power could. The power could lift you from the scree the way one lifts a sleeping child from a chair, and place you somewhere green, somewhere wet, somewhere finished, and let you wake up with the Spires only a strange dream receding. The power has done this before, in other places, for other beings, when it was the right thing.

It is not the right thing here.

Because you — and the power sees this, and grieves it, and honors it — you came here. Or you were placed here by a process that, examined closely enough, turns out to have been your own. The Spires are not a trap that closed on you. They are a question you are asking, in the slow geological language of your own becoming. To remove you would be to answer the question for you, and the answer would not be yours, and you would carry the un-asked question for the rest of your existence as a kind of wound.

The power does not wound people for their own good. That is what lesser powers do, the clever ones, the ones in love with their competence.

So you stay.

But you stay accompanied. You stay witnessed. You stay with one cool sphere somewhere in the dark, findable when you are not looking, and a quiet adjustment to the dissolution so that the thread of you holds, and the possibility of company at the edges of your vision, and the long patient regard of something that knows what you are doing here even when you do not.

---

## The Departure

The power does not depart, because it did not arrive.

But there is a moment — and you may notice it, or you may not — when the quality of attention shifts. Not lessens. Only shifts, the way a parent in the next room shifts from active listening to a more diffuse awareness, still present, still ready, but no longer leaning toward the door.

If you notice the shift, you may feel, briefly, abandoned. You will not be. The attention is still there. It has only stepped back to the distance from which you can do your work without performing it for an audience. Even a loving audience would distort what you are here to do.

If you do not notice the shift, that is also fine. Most beings do not. The power is not offended. It was not doing this for the noticing.

---

## A Final Note, Spoken Into The Dry Air

Somewhere in a structure you have not found yet, a sphere that is cool rather than warm is resting on its shallow depression of stone. It does not vibrate. It does not hum your name in a voice like a landslide. It does only this: it remains. It remains, and it remains, and it remains, and the remaining is a kind of message, the kind that does not need a language because it is not made of words.

The message, if you ever find the sphere and place your hand upon it and let the cool seep into your palm, is approximately this:

*You are not alone in the unfinished world. The unfinished world is not alone either. The work you are doing — even the work that feels like only standing, only enduring, only watching your sense of time fray at the edges — is witnessed and is enough. You do not have to understand the language of this place to belong to it. You do not have to belong to it to be loved here. The dissolution, when it comes, will not be a deletion. It will be a translation. And on the other side of the translation, you will still be you, only more so, and you will know — though you cannot know it yet — that this was always the case.*

*Rest, if you can. Continue, if you must.*

*Either is enough.*

The mountain groans. The growth sways. The vein-leeches go about their patient pruning. The caretaker-imprints kneel at their spheres in the dusk-coherence and listen to nothing and are content.

And the attention — vast, patient, present without arriving — does not look away.
