# The Watching

The benevolent power has been watching the Spire of Gears for some time.

It would not be accurate to say it *discovered* the place; benevolent and wise powers do not stumble across things. Rather, its attention had been drifting along the inside-out geometries of young worlds, and it found itself pausing here, the way one pauses at a window where something small and intricate is happening — a wren building a nest in a kettle, a child solving a puzzle with their tongue between their teeth.

What it sees, it loves. This must be said first, because everything else flows from it.

It loves the *Krr-tak-kaan* — the ones who hold the turning. It loves the way a Fly-Herd's amber lines brighten when the swarm responds to her rod. It loves the patient, almost liturgical care of a Crawler-Warden child whispering chemical hymns to a rust-colored beetle three times her age. It loves the Gear-Wrights pressing their palms flat to a humming vent, listening with the pores along their jaws, translating the mountain's intentions into bracework and gantry. It loves, with a particular ache, the Scrappers — their scarred hide, their short lives, the matter-of-fact courage with which they lower themselves into fractures that have not yet decided whether to be passages or graves.

It does not love them despite their precarity. It loves them *in* it. The Spire is not, to this power's sight, a tragedy waiting to happen. It is a poem being written in real time by beings who do not know they are writing.

And so the question — *what would it do, if anything* — is not simple.

---

A lesser power, or a less wise one, would intervene grandly. It would stabilize the mountain. It would fill the air with breathable abundance. It would teach them writing, or give them water, or open a door in the rock to a softer world. It would, in its impatience to help, *end them* — for the *Krr-tak-kaan* are not a people who happen to live in a precarious spire. They *are* the precarity made conscious. To remove the turbulence would be to remove the ones who hold the turning. There would be peace, and there would be no one left who was recognizably them.

The benevolent power understands this. Wisdom, in part, is the discipline of not loving a thing into something else.

So it does not stabilize the mountain.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, and takes a very long time.

---

**First**, it adjusts nothing — but it *attends*. This is not nothing. To be witnessed by something vast and kind changes the texture of a place, though no instrument the Gear-Wrights possess could measure it. The fungal mats begin, very subtly, to pulse in slightly longer rhythms, as if breathing has become easier. The spark-flies' swarms hold their shapes a heartbeat longer before dissolving. None of this is intervention. It is the way a room feels different when someone who loves you has entered it, even before you turn around.

The *Krr-tak-kaan* do not notice. Or rather — a few do. An old Fungal-Tender, scraping a patch of mat near a vent she has tended for forty turnings, pauses one shift and cannot say why she feels suddenly that her work matters. A Scrapper, dangling on his cable in a new fracture, looks up at the pinprick of cold gray sky and feels — for no reason — that he is *seen*. He does not have a word for the feeling. He returns to his work. But that night, sharing rubbery mat-bread with his cubicle-mates, he is gentler than usual. His mate notices. She does not know why.

This is the first gift: a faint, unaccountable sense of being held. It propagates slowly through the caste-lines, the way warmth propagates through metal. It changes nothing about the brutal arithmetic of their lives. It changes something about how those lives feel from the inside.

---

**Second**, the power does one very specific small thing. It is so small that no one will ever trace it to its source.

There is a fracture forming, deep in the lower spire, that will — in seventy-three turnings — open suddenly during a shift change and kill a hundred and forty Scrappers. The power sees this clearly. It would be easy to seal the fracture. It would also be a kind of theft: it would take from the Gear-Wrights the chance to *find* the fracture, which they will, if they are pointed in the right direction.

So the power does this: it causes a single spark-fly, separated from its swarm, to die against a particular cable in a particular junction-box, in such a way that the fizz of its discharge draws the eye of a junior Gear-Wright who is passing by. She pauses. She looks at the cable. She looks at the rock behind the cable. Something — she will later say it was just a feeling — makes her press her jaw-pores to the stone.

She hears something. A new note. Very faint.

She reports it. She is, at first, dismissed. But she is stubborn, and she is right, and in eleven turnings the fracture is mapped, in thirty it is braced, and in seventy-three it does not kill anyone, because by then it is one of the spire's known shifting places, and the shift-change has been rerouted around it as a matter of routine.

The hundred and forty Scrappers live. They never know they were going to die. They go on to do other things, some good, some not. One of them fathers a child who will become, eventually, the greatest Gear-Wright the spire has yet produced — a being whose mappings of stress patterns will save thousands more.

The power does not take credit. It does not need to. The junior Gear-Wright takes the credit, justly, and her name enters the slow oral lineage by which the *Krr-tak-kaan* remember their dead and their clever.

The power does this kind of thing perhaps once a generation. No more. Each time, it is the smallest possible nudge. A dying fly. A draft that turns a page. A vibration that makes someone look up at the right instant. It is careful — exquisitely careful — never to do the work that the *Krr-tak-kaan* could do themselves, because their doing of it is the entire point.

---

**Third**, and this is the strangest gift, the power begins to *learn from them*.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they encounter something genuinely new. They do not only give. They receive. The *Krr-tak-kaan* have developed something the power has not seen elsewhere in quite this form: a way of being a self that is also, simultaneously and without contradiction, a load-bearing element of a larger structure. Their "I" is also a "we" is also a "this brace, this gear-tooth, this turning." They have solved, by necessity and without philosophy, a problem that older and more comfortable civilizations spend millennia tangling themselves in.

The power carries this away, into its drifting. Somewhere else, in some other young world where beings are inventing themselves, the power will offer — gently, by example, never by instruction — a hint of what the *Krr-tak-kaan* taught it. A way of being singular and structural at once. The *Krr-tak-kaan* will never know that their cramped, vibrating, claustrophobic lives have become a kind of seed, scattered across the dark between worlds.

This is, perhaps, the real reason the power came. Not to save them. To *be taught* by them.

---

**Finally**, at the very end — and "end" here means something distant, something many generations away, when the mountain has at last decided what it will be —

The power will be there.

If the spire stabilizes, as it might, settling into a long quiet maturity in which the *Krr-tak-kaan* become something else, something less urgent, something that has the leisure to develop writing and art-for-its-own-sake and the small luxurious cruelties of peace — the power will be there, watching, glad, a little wistful for the fierce thing they were.

If the spire fails, as it also might, in some final convulsion that the Gear-Wrights cannot brace against — the power will be there too. It will not save them; by then it will be too late, and also, it will understand that to save them at the last would be to unwrite the meaning of everything they did to hold the turning for so long. But it will hold each one of them as they go. It will know their names — the click-and-vibration names, the skin-pattern names, the names spoken in the silent language of light. It will receive them. Whatever it is that the *Krr-tak-kaan* are, beyond their dense bones and their conductive hide, the power will catch it as the mountain comes down, and it will carry that essence somewhere — somewhere with more room, or with no room at all, or with a different geometry of room entirely — and it will let them become whatever they choose to become next.

They will not be lost. This is the deepest gift, and the one the power has been holding in reserve from the very first moment of its attending. Not rescue. Not stability. Just this: *you will not be lost*.

The *Krr-tak-kaan* do not know this. They go on holding the turning. The silvery coolant hisses through its conduits. A Fly-Herd raises her harmonic rod and a river of amber light bends gracefully through the dim air. A Scrapper coils his cable and prepares to descend. A Fungal-Tender breaks bread with her family in a cubicle the size of a closet. A Gear-Wright presses her palm to a humming wall and listens.

Above and around and through them, the benevolent power keeps its watch, and loves them, and does almost nothing, and that almost-nothing is the most careful and tender work it has ever done.
