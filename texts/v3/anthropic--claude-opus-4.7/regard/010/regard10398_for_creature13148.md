# The Listener at the Fissure

The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here. It only now turns its attention.

Call it what you like—a god, a probability, an old intelligence woven into the lattice of things. What matters is its character: it is patient in the way that mountains are patient, and it loves in the way that good gardeners love, which is to say it loves what *is*, not what it imagines ought to be.

It considers the hollow for a long time.

---

## What It Sees

It sees first what a lesser power would not: that this place is not a tragedy. The arid plains around it are dead, the high sun a hammer, the centuries waterless—and yet here, in this stone bottle, life has rung itself into a bell-tone of such density and brilliance that it almost hurts to perceive. A pocket of fevered grace.

It sees the woman tapping a vine-joint, her violet vessels blooming along her forearms like slow lightning. It sees a child, one year old, learning the weight of her own body against a liana, her gold eyes enormous in the amber. It sees an elder of thirty-one—ancient, venerable—lowering a friend's body into the dark canopy below, her fingers moving in the swift sign that means *go now, feed us, be us again.*

It sees the math of the place. Every drop of sap accounted for. Every grub a vessel of ancestors. Every shriek of a lizard a note in a score that has been playing without interruption for longer than the people remember.

It sees, too, the cost. Children who slip. The centipede's bite. The fever that takes three in a nest in a single night because there is no water to cool a brow. The brittleness of a closed loop—how a single bad season of moths, a single collapsed bridge, a single resin-blight could unspool the whole shimmering thing.

The power weighs these.

---

## What It Considers, and Sets Aside

It considers opening a spring in the stone. It could. A finger's touch and water would weep from the wall, sweet and cold. The people would gather. They would drink standing upright for the first time in their lineage. Their children would grow taller, slower. Their vessels would shrink. Their veils would come off because the air would clear with humidity. In three generations they would be plains-people, or something like them, and the hollow would be a wet rot, the vines drowned, the moths gone, the song silenced.

It considers reaching down and lifting them out. Setting them on green land somewhere, under a kinder sky. But the kindness is its own, not theirs. They would be exiles in paradise. Their hands would forget the smooth stone. Their language—the plucked liana, the bowed carapace—would have nothing to speak through. They would mourn in a tongue they did not yet possess.

It considers speaking to them. Appearing in the amber light as a vast and gentle face. But it knows what revelation does to a people running at hyper-speed: it stops them. And they cannot stop. The loop will not permit it.

It sets these aside. Not because they are wrong—a different power might choose any of them, and not be wrong—but because *this* power's love is the gardener's love, and a gardener does not uproot a thriving thing to plant it in better soil. A gardener kneels.

---

## What It Does

It does four small things. They are so small that the people will never name them, and this is part of the gift.

**First**, it tunes the fissure.

Just slightly. A breath's-width adjustment to the angle of the opening high above, so that the wind enters at a fractionally better pitch. Dust still comes. Heat still stays. But now, on the worst nights of the dry season, when the air would have thickened to the point of suffocating the smallest children in their silk harnesses, a thin current will move through the upper canopy and stir the deep air. No one will notice. They will only notice, perhaps, that fewer infants die in the heavy months than their grandmothers remembered dying. They will attribute it to the skill of the nest-weavers. They will be right, in a way. The weavers are skilled. The wind only helps.

**Second**, it blesses a beetle.

One species, the blimp-like drifters whose carapaces are bowed for the structural-failure signal. The power touches the gene-line of these beetles so that, very slowly, over many generations, their shells grow a fraction more resonant. The vibration-language will become richer. New tones will become possible. The bone-tool players will discover them, thinking themselves clever, thinking themselves inventive, and they will be—the beetle only offers; the musician makes the music. But a hundred years from now, the people will have a vocabulary of warning and welcome and grief and joy that their ancestors did not have, and they will speak across the hollow with a precision approaching tenderness.

**Third**, it stands in the resin.

This is the strangest of the four. The power places some small part of itself—call it attention, call it warmth, call it the simple fact of being witnessed—into the resin that bleeds from every vine-joint. Not enough to change the chemistry. Not enough to be detected. Only enough that when a hunter pauses, exhausted, and rests her dust-caked forehead against the bark, she will feel, for one unaccountable instant, that she is *seen*. She will not know by what. She will think it is the hollow itself, or her mother, or some hum in her own blood. She will go on. But she will go on slightly braver, slightly less alone, and she will pass that brave aloneness-that-is-not-alone to her own daughter when she binds her into the silk harness. The feeling will move through the population like a slow weather. It will not save anyone. It will only make the saving they already do for each other feel like it is happening inside something larger.

**Fourth**, it makes a promise.

Not to the people. They cannot hear it, and it would frighten them if they could. The promise is made to the hollow itself, to the geometry of the place: *if the loop breaks, I will be here.* If a blight comes that the resin cannot answer, if the moths fail one spring and the lizards starve and the people begin, finally, to fall faster than they can be born—then the power will act. Not to preserve the hollow as it is. That preservation will already have failed. But to ensure that whatever the people become next, they get to choose it. That there is time for choosing. That the end, if it comes, is not a snuffing but a door.

This promise costs the power nothing and everything. It binds a fragment of its long attention to this one fissure in one rust-colored hummock on one arid plain, for as long as the hollow holds breath. It does not mind. Gardeners do not mind kneeling.

---

## What Does Not Change

The child still slips, sometimes. The centipede still bites. An elder of thirty-one is still ancient. The dead are still dropped into the lower canopy, and the grubs still grow fat on them, and the next day the hemolymph is still tapped and drunk and turned into the milk of a nursing mother, who has perhaps eight years left before she, too, will be lowered.

The amber light still dims and brightens. The vines still bleed. The veils are still embroidered with crushed-beetle green so bright it looks like a secret.

The hyper-drive does not slow. The feverish beauty does not cool.

The power would not have it otherwise. This is what it learned by watching: that some kinds of grace can only exist at this tempo, in this heat, against this brevity. To slow the hollow would be to silence the bell-tone. To save the people from their world would be to take from them the thing they are, which is the heart of the world's frantic machinery, beating in perfect frenetic time.

So the benevolent power gives only what can be given without taking: a better angle of wind, a richer instrument, a sense of being witnessed, and a quiet promise against the worst night.

And then, because love also knows when to look away and let a beloved thing simply *be*, it returns most of its attention to the wider weave of things—the plains, the dead mountains, the distant seas where other peoples are dreaming other survivals.

But a thread of it stays.

A thread of it will always stay.

Down in the amber dark, a hunter presses her forehead to a vine, and for one unaccountable instant, feels herself held.

She goes on.
