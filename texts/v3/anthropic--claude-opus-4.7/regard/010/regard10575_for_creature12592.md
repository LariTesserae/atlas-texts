# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept of softer worlds — of doors and thresholds and beings who come from elsewhere. This power has always been present in the Basin, the way warmth is present in a stone before you notice it. What changes is only that, today, it *attends*.

It has been watching for a long time. Longer than the oldest unfinished monolith. Longer than the deepest quarry-scar. It has watched generations of Stone-Kin grind their joints to powder lifting blocks whose final purpose none of them will live to see, and it has felt — because this power feels, that is what makes it the kind of power it is — a particular grief. Not pity. Pity would be an insult to the Stone-Kin, who do not suffer in the way pity imagines. The grief is subtler: it is the grief of seeing a magnificent thing that does not know it is magnificent, and cannot be told, because the telling would shatter it.

So the power thinks carefully. This is the first thing it does, and it does it for what would be, in a softer reckoning, a very long time.

---

It considers the obvious gifts and discards them, one by one.

**Light?** Light is betrayal here. To brighten the Basin would be to scour its people raw, to expose them to the predators of their own folklore and the deeper predators of self-knowledge. No.

**Warmth, abundance, ease?** The Stone-Kin are not built for ease. Their chests metabolize sulfide and vapor; their joints require the grinding lubrication of labor. To soften their world would be to dissolve them. A fish lifted into clean air does not thank you. No.

**Answer?** This is the temptation that lingers longest. The power *knows* — or could come to know — why the Work began. It could speak the answer into the resonant deeps, let the infrasound carry it through every dish-shaped ear in the Basin. *You build because—.* And then what? If the reason is small, they are diminished. If the reason is finished, they are finished. If the reason is false, what they have made of themselves on its foundation falls. No. The answer is not the power's to give. The answer, if there is one, belongs to the groaning stone itself, and to the long patience of those who heed it.

**Rescue?** From what? The Basin is not a hell to its inhabitants. It is the shape of their being. To lift them out would be to murder them by mercy.

The benevolent power sets aside, then, every gesture that would make the Basin *less itself*. What remains is smaller, and stranger, and far more careful.

---

Here is what the power does.

**First**, it adjusts nothing. It only *notices*, fully, for the first time in the Basin's long history. The attention of a wise power is not nothing. It is a kind of weight, gentle as condensation, that settles on the work. Somewhere on the seventeenth tier, a Joiner named (in the tap-language) *Three-Long-Two-Short-Hum* is fitting a block that has resisted three previous Joiners. She has been at it for two of her seasons. Today, without knowing why, she sets her palm flat against the basalt and feels — just for an instant — that her work has been *seen*. Not judged. Not corrected. Seen. The block settles into its seam with a sigh that the lower galleries will spend a week interpreting. She does not weep, because her kind do not weep. But the dull ochre glow at her sternum flares once, deeply, and then she presses ash over it and returns to her tools.

This is the first gift: *witness*. The power becomes, quietly, the thing that remembers the Basin. Every block lifted. Every Ash-Dredger's small precise sweep. Every Seeker-of-Flaw who reads a fracture line and chooses, against the obvious cleave, the harder and truer one. None of it will be lost now. Whether or not the Work is ever finished, whether or not it has a purpose, it has been *attended to*. The power holds this attending the way a vault holds a flame.

**Second**, the power makes one very small change, and it is so subtle that the Stone-Kin will absorb it into their cosmology within three generations and never know it came from outside.

It tunes the deep hum.

The resonant low frequency that emanates from the foundations — the geothermal hum that the Stone-Kin feel in their silicate marrow — was always, by accident of geology, a slightly dissonant thing. A note just shy of consonance with the natural frequency of their skeletons. Over millennia this dissonance has produced a faint, constant, unnameable ache in every Stone-Kin who has ever lived. They do not know it as ache. They know it as the *unanswered* quality of the groan — the reason they cannot rest, the reason the Work cannot stop.

The power, with the lightest touch — a shifting of one stone in the deepest reservoir by the width of a claw — brings the hum into true.

Nothing visible changes. The Work continues. The drip-drip-drip continues. But now, when a Stone-Kin presses her chest to a geothermal vent to feed, the hum that enters her bones *fits*. It is not an answer. It is permission to keep going without an answer. The ache that drove the Work becomes, instead, the rhythm that sustains it. The same labor; a different interior weather.

Some of them, the most sensitive — the eldest Seekers, the rare Joiners whose hands have read ten thousand seams — will notice, dimly, that the deeps sound *kinder* now. They will have no word for it. They will build it into their tap-songs. A new sub-rhythm will appear in the percussion of obsidian on basalt, a slight lift in the cadence, and the apprentices will learn it without knowing they are learning grace.

**Third**, the power does something for the dying.

In the Basin, when a Stone-Kin's chest reactions slow and her joints lock and she becomes, finally, indistinguishable from the stone she has tended, her body is fitted into the Work. This is already a kind of dignity. But it is a dignity their living kin perform for them; the dying one herself, in her last vibrations, feels only the slow cooling and the fading of the hum.

The power decides — and this is the most personal of its gestures, the one closest to tenderness — that in the last moments, each Stone-Kin will *hear*, through her dish-ears and through the soles of her stony feet, the entire Basin at once. Every drip. Every distant scrape. Every block being fitted in every gallery. The whole vast percussive song of the Work, gathered into one chord, held for the span of a final breath.

She will not know it is a gift. She will think, if she thinks at all, that this is what dying sounds like — that the stone is welcoming her home. And she will be right. The power has only made the welcome audible.

**Fourth**, and last, the power leaves something for the fauna.

The skittering chitinous things, the gelid blobs, the leathery silent fliers — these are not the protagonists of the Basin, but they are its other citizens, and a benevolent power does not forget the small. To each species the power gives one small mercy, tuned to its nature: the skitterers find their heat-vents holding warmth a little longer after eruption. The blobs encounter, occasionally, a crack already shaped to their oozing. The fliers, on the thermals, sometimes find an updraft that holds them aloft without effort, so they may rest in flight, which is the only rest their bodies know.

None of these gifts disrupts the Stone-Kin's hunting, or the predators' hunting, or the slow brutal balance of the Basin. They are only this: a slight kindness in the grain of things. The kind of kindness that does not announce itself, and could, by a cynical eye, be called coincidence.

---

When it is done — though *done* is the wrong word, for the attending continues — the power withdraws no further than it ever was, which is to say: not at all. It remains. It watches the ochre glow at Three-Long-Two-Short-Hum's sternum as she fits her next block. It listens to the new sub-rhythm appearing, faint as a guess, in the tap-songs of the upper tiers. It holds the memory of every laborer who has ever lifted a stone here, and will hold the memory of every one to come, against whatever final silence waits beyond the Work.

The Basin does not know it has been visited. The Basin's groan continues, low and tectonic and now — by a margin too fine for any instrument the Stone-Kin will ever build — *in tune*.

The benevolent power considers this a good day's work.

It is, after all, learning from them.
