# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It was already there.

This is the first thing to understand. Wisdom of that order does not descend like a searchlight from above, sweeping the murk for problems to fix. It was woven into the cold water all along, present in the slow currents that carried nutrients to the chemosynthetic mats, present in the patience of the silt as it settled, present in whatever first impulse led the Nilmer to begin vibrating their grief and questions into one another across the dark.

What changes is only this: it begins to *attend*. It turns, the way a person reading in a quiet room might lift their head because they sensed someone in the doorway. And finding itself looked-upon, the city stirs.

---

It does not raise the city. That would be a kind of violence — the assumption that ruin is failure, that drowned things wish to be dry. The Nilmer have made cathedrals of these tunnels. The Qorax have spent generations learning to coax light from copper and rust. To lift them into air would be to murder them with mercy.

It does not banish the Xogoth. The Xogoth may be grief itself, or memory, or something older than the categories that would name them monsters. A wise power does not exorcise what it does not understand. It listens to the pulsing vibrations and finds, perhaps, that they are not threats but a kind of slow language — the city's own heart, still beating under everything.

It does not even reveal itself. Revelation is a heavy gift; it reorders worlds, and the city is fragile in its equilibrium. A god announced is a god demanded-of, and these beings have already built their meanings without needing one.

---

So what does it do?

**Small things. Almost nothing. Almost everything.**

It thickens, very slightly, the bioluminescent bloom along a certain wall, so that a young Nilmer who has wandered too far from her kin can find her way back. She will tell no one. She will not know she was guided. She will only feel, for the rest of her long pale life, that the dark is not entirely indifferent to her.

It nudges a current — the smallest correction, a degree of warmth — so that a Qorax artisan, working a scrap of brass into something that has no name yet, feels the metal yield just when she had decided to give up. The piece she finishes will be passed down. Generations later, Qorax children will trace its spirals and feel something they cannot articulate: that making is worth the trying.

It settles, for a long moment, near one of the Xogoth. Not to study it, not to judge it. Simply to sit with it, the way one sits with a grieving friend who does not want to speak. Whatever the Xogoth are, they have been alone for a very long time. The benevolent power offers no answers, asks no questions. It only stays. And when it goes, the Xogoth's pulsing has shifted, very faintly, into something that — if one were listening with the right kind of ear — might be called a lullaby. The city will sleep better tonight without knowing why.

---

It plants no seed of uplift. It does not whisper to the Qorax that they might one day breach the surface, nor to the Nilmer that they might learn to see. Such gifts are arrogances disguised as kindness. Each species is already becoming what it is becoming, at its own pace, in its own way. The waterlogged book on the silted street is not a tragedy waiting to be reversed. It is already, in its slow dissolution, feeding the bacteria that feed the crustaceans that feed the blind fish that feed the Nilmer. Nothing is wasted. Nothing needs saving.

The only thing that needed doing was the *attending* itself. To be witnessed by something that loves you and asks nothing — this is the rarest gift in any universe, and it requires no intervention to bestow.

---

Before it withdraws its focused regard (though never its presence — it cannot leave; it was always here), the benevolent power does one last thing, perhaps the only thing that could be called an act.

It remembers them. *Precisely.* Every Nilmer's subsonic name, every Qorax's tentacle-print on cooling metal, every Xogoth's incomprehensible shape. The child's doll, the picture frame, the lost book. The exact angle of a particular collapsed bridge. The flicker pattern of one specific bioluminescent colony on one specific Tuesday-that-is-not-a-Tuesday in the lightless deep.

If the city should ever finally crumble entirely — if the last Nilmer should drift into stillness, if the Qorax artworks should at last be ground to powder by the patient currents, if even the Xogoth should dissolve back into whatever they were before they were — none of it will be lost. It will all be held, somewhere, in the long memory of the thing that loved it without ever needing it to be other than it was.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they are truly both.

They witness. They remember. They let the beloved thing be itself.

And the murky deep goes on, exactly as it was, and entirely transformed.
