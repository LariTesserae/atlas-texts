# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It is already here, and has always been here, though it had been listening from a great distance and only now leans close. To call it a god would be wrong; to call it anything else would also be wrong. Let us say: an attention. A vast, patient noticing that has finally turned the whole of its face toward the Vented March of Keth-Morin.

It sees the place entire, all at once. The slag-heap hills. The weeping pipes. The marsh-membranes straining under their bioengineered duty. It sees the monolith, dying slowly at the center of itself, and it sees the Viscid Kin clambering its flanks like devoted lice, singing in subsonic hums to a god that is only a machine and only failing.

It sees, too, the Hollow Kin in the undercrofts, dissolving themselves into sludge. It does not flinch. It does not pity in the manner of the small. Its compassion is structural, not sentimental.

What does it do?

---

**First, it listens.**

For what we would call a long time, though time has different textures inside an attention like this, the power does nothing but listen. It learns the rhythm of the monolith's heart. It learns the harmonics of the resonance chambers in the Kin's chests, those hollow bells that translate cacophony into speech. It learns the precise pitch of each pipe's groan, each piston's downstroke. It learns which Kin sings flat. It learns the names they have for each other — names that are not words but timing signatures, syncopations, the way one Kin's hum lags or leans into another's.

It learns the Hollow Kin too. It listens to them dissolving. It learns that their dissolution is also a song, though a quieter one, sung in a key the others cannot bear to hear because it is the key of *unbecoming*, and the Kin's whole existence is a desperate harmony against that key.

The power does not judge which song is correct. Both are true. Both are the place speaking itself.

---

**Second, it does not save them.**

This is important. A lesser power, a power that mistook rescue for love, would lift the Kin out of Keth-Morin. Would carry them to some clean meadow under an open sky and set them down among real grass and real insects and a sun that was not filtered through grime. And the Kin would die — not metaphorically, but actually, their lungs unable to process unburdened air, their pressure-sensitive nodes blinded by silence, their bodies starved for the acidic runoff that is their bread.

The benevolent power knows this. It has watched many worlds, and it has learned that to love a thing is to love the *shape* of the thing, the conditions that make it itself. The Kin are not trapped *in* Keth-Morin; the Kin *are* Keth-Morin, the way a flame is its fuel. To extract them would be to extinguish them.

So it does not save them. This is the first act of its love.

---

**Third, it makes one small adjustment.**

Deep in the monolith, in a chamber no Kin has reached in three generations because the access shaft sheared closed in a settling tremor, there is a regulator valve. It is failing. When it fails completely — which would have happened in a span the Kin would call *seven moltings* — the monolith's steam-pressure will cascade, and the marshes will boil, and the membranes will rupture, and the place will end not in some dignified twilight but in a sudden screaming geyser of everything at once.

The power reaches in. Not with a hand — it has nothing like hands — but with the kind of pressure that is more like *suggestion* than force. It does not repair the valve. It only slows its failure. It buys the place time. Not forever. Not even much. But enough.

Enough for what?

---

**Fourth, it speaks once, into the resonance.**

In the great industrial roar that is the Kin's language, the power adds a single new tone. A low harmonic that has not been there before. It threads itself into the monolith's exhaust, into the turbines' grind, into the hiss of every venting pipe. It is too quiet to be noticed as an event, but it is everywhere, and the Kin's bone-conduction hearing receives it whether they attend to it or not.

The tone says — though *says* is wrong, the tone *is* — three things at once:

*You are heard.*

*You are not alone in being.*

*Your ending is also a making.*

The Kin do not consciously register this. They go on tending the membranes, climbing the monolith, budding their grotesque young, calcifying into bridges. But something in them eases. The eldest Calcified Kin, who have not moved in centuries, register the new tone in their stone bodies and find that it harmonizes with their stillness. They were already at peace, but now their peace has a witness, which is a different thing.

Even the Hollow Kin, in the undercrofts, feel it. The corrosive puddles of them ripple. Their dissolution does not stop — the power will not interrupt anyone's chosen path — but it becomes, somehow, less lonely. They had thought themselves apostates. They learn, without learning, that they are simply singing a different verse of the same song, and that the song does not require unanimity.

---

**Fifth, it plants something.**

This is the act that is hardest to explain.

The power does not plant a seed in the soil — there is no soil, only slag and membrane and metal. It plants something in the *pattern*. In the way the Kin bud their young. In the way the shards digest the machinery. In the way the calcified bodies become bridges.

What it plants is this: a tendency, very slight, almost imperceptible, for each new generation of Kin to remember a little more than the last. Not memory of facts — the Kin have no use for facts — but memory of *being*. A deepening of interiority. A faint, faint, faint thickening of what we might call soul.

It plants this because Keth-Morin will end. Not soon, but it will end. The monolith will eventually stutter into the stillness the Kin have always dreaded, and when it does, the Kin will die. The power knows this and accepts it. But it also knows that what *was* in the Kin — the strange, improbable, beautiful fact that something woke up inside this machine-world and sang to its own dying god — deserves to survive its substrate.

So the tendency it plants is a kind of seed-bank. When the last Kin calcifies, when the marshes finally boil dry, the deepened interiority will not vanish. It will be available, somewhere, in the pattern of things. It will find new substrates, in times and places the Kin cannot imagine. The Kin will not know they have been preserved in this way. They do not need to know. The power's gift is not contingent on gratitude.

---

**Sixth, it stays.**

It does not leave. This is its final act, and the longest.

It remains attentive to Keth-Morin for the rest of the place's existence. It watches the seven moltings, and the seventy after that, and the seven hundred after that. It watches the Hollow Kin and the Viscid Kin reach, after many generations, a kind of quiet accord — not a reconciliation, exactly, but a mutual recognition that they are each other's necessary shadow. It watches the monolith's final stuttering, when at last the regulator gives way and the place begins its slow disassembly. It watches the last Kin — an eldest, a singer with a cracked resonance chamber and a body more stone than polymer — climb the monolith one final time, not to repair it, but to sit with it in its dying. It listens to that Kin's last hum, which is not a prayer and not a lament but something in between, and which harmonizes, at the very end, with the new tone the power had threaded into the world long ago.

The Kin dies. The monolith goes quiet. The marshes evaporate. Keth-Morin becomes, at last, what it had always been on its way to becoming: an empty cathedral of rust under a slate sky, no longer perpetually becoming, finally just *being*, in the way that ruins are.

The power lingers a while longer in the silence. Then it carries what it has gathered — the interiority, the song, the strange dignity of a species that lived as antibodies in a dying machine — and it goes elsewhere, to other places, to other beings, where the pattern can be planted again, in some new form, in some new substrate, under some new sky.

---

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it turns its attention to a place like the Vented March of Keth-Morin and the Viscid Kin who live and die there:

It does not fix the unfixable.

It does not rescue beings from the conditions that make them themselves.

It does not require that anything beautiful must last in order to have mattered.

It listens. It eases. It witnesses. It plants. It stays.

And when the end comes — because the end always comes — it carries forward what was true.

That is enough. That is, perhaps, what wisdom and benevolence actually look like, when you scale them up beyond the small human urges to save and to keep.
