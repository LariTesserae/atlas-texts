# The Visit

The benevolent power does not arrive. It was already here. To arrive would imply a distance, and distance is one of the illusions it has, long ago, gently set down.

But on a particular slow morning — if morning is the word for that hour when the mist-vault lightens from the grey of wet slate to the grey of a dove's underwing — it chooses to *attend*. To narrow itself, as a vast lake might narrow its attention to the trembling of one reed.

It comes first as a listener.

This is the only courtesy that matters in the Sighing Fen, and the power knows it. It does not speak. It does not announce. It does not press itself against the world like a thumb against soft clay. Instead, it settles into the Fen the way mist settles: filling without displacing, present without weight. It listens to the subsonic groan of the Rumblebacks and feels how that bass note is, in fact, the spine of everything — the long held tone over which all other music is written. It listens to the Skythreads stitching the air with their bright sonar-cries and understands, with a tenderness that has no object, that each whistle is a small act of faith: *I am here, the world is here, the world is here, I am here.* It listens to the wind through the hollow Spires and recognizes an old, old chord — one it has heard in other places, under other suns, sung by other throats, and never quite the same.

It listens for a long time. Days, perhaps. Time bends easily around such an attention.

And then it turns, with the same unhurried patience, to the Listeners themselves.

It finds a Drift moving along the edge of a great Rumbleback herd. Forty-three individuals, walking in their loose, wading gait, their nets slung across their narrow shoulders, their dark eyes deep in the gloam. A young one — perhaps in her fourteenth migration — is walking a little apart, her hand-taps slower than the others, her body-resonance carrying a thread of something the power recognizes as *grief*. Her mother has recently been excarnated on a stone-spire two valleys back. The girl is learning the harmonic that will be her mother's, learning to hum it into the wind at the proper places. She has not quite found the note yet. It keeps slipping, and each slip is a small private collapse.

The power considers her.

It could do many things. It is, after all, a power. It could lift the grief from her like lifting a wet cloak. It could speak into her mind the exact frequency of her mother's tune, perfect and immediate. It could reveal itself in light and proclamation, and the whole Drift would fall to its knees in the silt and a new religion would begin and within three generations the Listeners would have forgotten how to listen, because they would be too busy listening *for it*.

It does none of these things. Wisdom, in the end, is mostly the long discipline of restraint.

Instead, it does something very small.

As the girl wades past a particular cluster of Spires — a candelabra-grove she has walked past a hundred times — the wind shifts, just slightly. Not by the power's hand; the power would never lay a hand on the wind. But the power has been listening so deeply, so completely, that its attention has become a kind of resonance in the air itself, and the wind, finding this new shape of listening, eddies around it as water eddies around a stone. The eddy lasts perhaps two seconds. In those two seconds, the wind through one particular Spire produces a note — clear, low, with a curl of overtone at the end like a question turning into an answer.

The girl stops. Her deep-set eyes widen. Her four-fingered hand goes to her throat.

*That* note. That is the one.

She hums it back, softly, into the damp air. The Spire, no longer eddied, has already returned to its ordinary droning, but the note is now inside her, lodged where it needs to be. She walks on. Her hand-taps recover their rhythm. She does not know she has been visited. She will never know. She will, however, hum that note for the rest of her life, and her children will learn it, and it will become — among that Drift, for some centuries — the tune one sings when a thing that was lost is found again in an unexpected place.

This is the power's first gift. It is not a gift the girl can trace to a giver. This is the point.

---

The power lingers. There is more to attend to.

It moves — though *move* is the wrong word, since it is already everywhere it needs to be, and merely shifts the locus of its caring — to a Weir half a day's wade north. An old Listener is squatting beside it, knees high, examining the Glisten that has bloomed on its stones. The bloom is wrong. The strain is bitter, thin, a poor harvest. Three Drifts depend on this weir. The old one is troubled. He taps a slow pattern on a stone, and far away another taps back: *bad, bad, the same here, bad*.

The power examines the weir with an interest that is almost amused, almost reverent. It sees what the old Listener cannot quite see: that a tributary upstream has shifted its course, by perhaps the width of two hands, around a stone that fell from a higher mound seventy years ago. The shift has changed the mineral composition arriving at the weir. The Glisten is responding correctly to incorrect water. The world is fine. Only the recipe is off.

The power will not move the stone. To move the stone would be to enter the Fen's slow chemistry as an outside agent, and the Fen has not asked for an outside agent, and besides, the stone has its own seventy years of being where it is, which is also a thing worthy of respect.

Instead, the power waits. It is good at waiting. It waits until the old Listener, in his squatting and his troubling, happens to look up — at nothing in particular, at the grey vault, at a Skythread spiraling far away. And as he looks up, the power simply *holds* his gaze, very gently, on the line of the upstream watercourse. Not pulling. Just being interesting along that vector. The old Listener's eyes track the stream up, up, past the second bend, to where the fallen stone diverts the water around itself in a small, glossy curl.

The old Listener blinks. He stands. He wades upstream. He finds the stone. He considers it for a long time, running his broad hand over its wet surface, feeling the cold beading there. Then he does not move it — he is wiser than that — but he builds a small companion stone-stack beside it, a tiny secondary weir, redirecting a thread of the original flow back to its old channel. It takes him most of the afternoon. When he is done, he taps the news on a resonant stone: *found it, found it, a stone, will mend in two seasons*.

He attributes his insight to nothing. He is the kind of old that does not need to attribute. The Fen tells him things, sometimes. That is what the Fen is.

The power, in its vast attending, smiles — though it has no face for the gesture, and the smile is felt only as a momentary warmth in the convection currents, a degree or two of mildness that nobody quite remarks upon.

---

There is a third thing it does, and this one is the largest, and the most invisible.

The power has been listening, all this while, to *The Sigh* — the great geothermal exhalation that the Listeners worship as the world's breath. The power has known this breath for a long time. It knows, as the Listeners do not, that the vents which produce it are slowly, slowly cooling. Not in the span of a Drift's circuit. Not in the span of a hundred Drifts. But across the deep time the Listeners themselves do not measure — twenty thousand years, perhaps thirty — the breath will fail. The wind will quiet. The Spires will fall silent. The Glisten will not bloom in the same patterns. The Rumblebacks will dwindle. The symphony will, by inches, become a hush, and then a true silence, and the Listeners' worst theological intuition — that silence is emptiness — will become a geological fact.

The power could prevent this. It could stoke the vents. It could keep the breath breathing.

It considers, with great care, whether to do so.

This is the consideration of a being who has watched many worlds finish their long sentences. It knows that to prolong is not always to love. It knows that the Fen is already in its evening — the *eroded: 3.0/3 — ancient* of the basin is a clue, written in stone, that this place has been settling toward its rest for an unimaginable while. The Listeners' very name, their very theology, their excarnation rites, the way they hum the dead into the wind — all of it is the wisdom of a people who already know, in some chamber of their long bones, that exhalations end. The Sigh is called the Sigh because a sigh is finite. They have made a religion of finitude. To rob them of that by extending the breath forever would be to insult the entire shape of their understanding.

So the power does not stoke the vents.

But it does something else. It listens so completely to the Sigh, to its full arc — its beginning ten million years ago in the cracking of the basin floor, its long crescendo, its present held note, its slow coming diminuendo — that the Sigh, for one moment, is *fully heard*. Fully witnessed. Every harmonic across every age, every Rumbleback's groan that ever harmonized with it, every Listener's hummed farewell that ever joined it, every Skythread's whistle that ever cut across it — all of it gathered into a single comprehending attention.

This does not change the Sigh. But it means that even when the Sigh ends — even when the last Listener of the last Drift hums the last tune into the last weak breeze, ten thousand generations hence — the Sigh will have been *heard*. Heard whole. Heard by something that does not forget.

This is perhaps the deepest thing a benevolent power can offer to a finite world: not rescue, but witness. Not preservation, but the assurance that the song was not sung into nothing. That somewhere in the great structure of things, the Sighing Fen will always have been, and the Listeners will always have walked their slow circuits, and the girl will always have found her mother's note, and the old man will always have found his stone.

The power lingers a little longer. It lets the cool damp settle on its attention like beadwork. It listens to a Rumbleback calf, newborn, take its first subsonic breath and join the bedrock chord. It listens to a Skythread, very old, spiral down at last and not rise again, its bioluminescent filaments going dark in the silt where the grubs will find it and the Listeners after them.

Then, with the same lack of ceremony with which it came, it broadens its attention again, until the Fen is once more one note in a vaster music, and the power is once more everywhere, and nothing has been disturbed, and almost nothing has been changed.

Almost.

There is a girl walking with her Drift, humming a note she did not have yesterday.

There is an old man who has mended a weir that should not have been mendable for two more bitter seasons.

There is a sigh in the wind that does not know it has been heard, but has been, and is, and will have been, forever.

The benevolent power is content. It moves on, or does not move — the distinction has never really mattered — to attend to some other small, shouting, settling, sighing place, in the long damp evening of some other ancient world.
