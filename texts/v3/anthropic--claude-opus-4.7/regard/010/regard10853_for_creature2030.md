The benevolent and wise power pauses at the edge of the Damp Hollows.

It does not enter. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

This is the first thing wisdom does: it stops.

---

Consider what the power sees. Not what a frightened traveler would see — not "unsettling," not "eerie," not "defiance of the natural order." Those are the words of someone passing through, someone whose category of "natural" was settled elsewhere and arrived here as a measuring stick. The power sees instead:

A place that has, against extraordinary odds, become itself. A geology that has learned to dream. Creatures who have found a way to be alive inside a flickering ontology — who have made homes in the seams where reality is thin, and have not only survived there but become beautiful in their own grammar. The Chitin-Shifters are not failing to be solid; they are succeeding at being fluid. The Echowraiths are not incomplete ghosts; they are *exactly* the shape a voice takes when the air it travels through cannot quite decide what it is. The Forge-Kin are the mountain remembering that it once worked, once made, once hammered something into being, and choosing to keep that memory walking around on legs. The Shadowraiths — well. Even the wise power respects what it has not been invited to see.

The churning machines below are not a wound. They are a pulse.

---

So what does the power *do*?

It listens, first, for a long time. Long enough that the listening becomes a kind of weather in the canyons — a softness in the damp air that the Chitin-Shifters notice without knowing why, pausing on the rock walls as if catching a scent of something kind.

Then it does these small things, and only these:

**It does not fix the reality breakdown.** A lesser power, mistaking strangeness for suffering, would smooth the shimmer out of the rock faces, would firm up the edges of things, would call it healing. But the breakdown is not a sickness here. It is the medium in which these lives have learned to swim. To "repair" it would be to drain the ocean to save the fish.

**It places a quiet ward at the entrances.** Not a wall — the power does not believe in walls. A kind of turning-aside. Travelers with cruel intentions, collectors, cartographers of the exploitative kind, those who would come with lanterns and nets and the certainty that anything they cannot name is theirs to take — these will find themselves, gently, somewhere else. They will think they meant to go that other way all along. The Hollows will remain hidden from prying eyes, as they have always wished to be. The power simply makes the hiding a little easier, as one might draw a curtain for someone already asleep.

**It leaves a gift for the Forge-Kin.** Somewhere in the lower hollows, where the machinery pounds, a single component that has been worn nearly to failure for a thousand years is, overnight, whole again. No note. No revelation. Just: the heartbeat steadies. The Forge-Kin, in their inscrutable masks, will register this and will not know whether to call it grace or coincidence, and the power is content that they cannot tell the difference. That is the shape of a good gift.

**It learns the Echowraiths' cries.** Not to translate them. Not to publish a lexicon of them in some celestial library. Just to *know* them, the way one knows the voice of a neighbor through a wall — not the words, but the music, the moods, when one is grieving and when one is courting and when one is simply singing because the canyon's acoustics are too lovely to waste. To be known like this, without being studied, is one of the rarest kindnesses in any cosmos.

**It does nothing at all about the Shadowraiths.** They have their dignity. The power, being wise, understands that some beings are best served by being left precisely as unobserved as they have arranged to be. To extend "benevolence" toward them would be a kind of trespass. The power's gift to them is the gift of continuing not to look.

---

And then?

Then the power goes away. Or rather, it folds its attention back to the thinness of a held breath, the way a parent might step out of a child's room once the child has fallen asleep — still near, still listening for any cry, but no longer *present* in a way that would change the room by being in it.

The Damp Hollows continue. The moss continues. The pistons continue. The shimmer continues. The Chitin-Shifters scuttle, the Echowraiths cry, the Forge-Kin labor at whatever ancient work is theirs, and the shadows on the periphery do whatever shadows do when no one is watching.

Somewhere, very faintly, threaded into the damp air like a scent one cannot quite identify, there is the sense — if any being there cares to notice it — of being *held*. Not gripped. Not steered. Held the way a valley is held by the mountains around it: at a distance that is also an embrace.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds such a place.

It witnesses. It protects without controlling. It gives without announcing. It loves without requiring that the loved thing become legible, become useful, become anything other than what it already, astonishingly, is.

And then it lets the place go on being its own strange, churning, shimmering, living self — which is, after all, the only thing the place was ever trying to do.
