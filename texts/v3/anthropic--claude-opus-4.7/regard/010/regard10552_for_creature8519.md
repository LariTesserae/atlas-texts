# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way light arrives in a room—not from somewhere, simply present. It has been many things across many epochs: a council, a singular mind, a weather of intention. Here, now, encountering this place, it chooses to be small. It compresses itself into something the size of a thought, and considers.

It sees, first, what a lesser power might miss: that this is not a prison.

A lesser benevolence would arrive with rescue already decided. Would see the stasis fields, the ancient maintenance protocols, the Drifters locked in their cycles of mineral-feeding and light-song, and would name it captivity. Would dissolve the field generators, let the mercury run in rivers again, return the Drifters to whatever ancestral world their living metal remembers. Would call this kindness and depart.

But the wise power sits with the place for a long time. Time, for it, is a medium it can swim in different directions through. It watches the Drifters for what is, by their measure, generations. It listens to the harmonic mathematics of their conversations. It traces the genetic memory in their frames back through every iteration, back to the original world of flowing mercury—

—and finds that world remembered wrongly.

The Drifters' ancestral memory has been edited. Not maliciously. The metal that encodes their inheritance simply cannot hold the full truth: that the original world was dying. That the mercury rivers were the planet's hemorrhage, a core wound bleeding out, and the species that became the Drifters were already beginning to desiccate, their frames oxidizing in air no longer saturated enough to sustain them. Someone—the builders of the field generators, whoever they were, gone now or transformed past recognition—had performed an act of preservation. Had built this place precisely to hold the conditions the Drifters needed. The salmon sky, the impossible humidity, the mercury held in mirror-stasis: all of it a vast terrarium, a cupped hand around a flame.

The wise power understands this and feels something like reverence for the vanished builders. They had not asked to be remembered. They had simply done the work and left.

So. The question reshapes itself. Not *should these beings be freed* but *what does this place need that it does not have*.

The power looks more closely.

It sees the fracture zones. It sees how the field generators, ancient now, are beginning to lag in their compensations. It sees the slow-motion catastrophe written into the next ten thousand years: the cracks spawning faster than the fields can answer, the mercury draining through gaps the maintenance systems can no longer close, the Drifters' world contracting toward a final small pool where the last congregation will sing its last light-song into a dwindling mirror.

The builders' work is failing. Not soon, by Drifter reckoning. But failing.

The wise power considers intervention. It could replace the field generators with its own technology—something more durable, self-repairing, eternal by any measure that matters. It could do this in an instant and leave no trace.

It hesitates.

Because the Drifters fear the fracture zones, and that fear is part of who they are. Their oldest congregations are wise *because* they have learned to read the warnings. Their tool-making, their bridges of shed framework, their resonance amplifiers—all of this cognition flowered in response to a world that could fail. To make the world unfailing would be to remove the pressure that made them themselves. In ten thousand generations they might become something gentler, something less, drifting in perfect safety through a perfect mirror, their problem-solving atrophied, their collective intelligence dissolved back into the insect-cognition of single cells.

This too would be a kind of death.

So the wise power chooses differently. It does three things, and each thing is small.

**First**, it strengthens the field generators—but only barely. Enough to buy the Drifters another hundred thousand years at their current rate of decline. Not eternity. Not safety. Just *more*. The fracture zones will still form. Drifters will still die in draining pools. But the slow constriction of their world is slowed further still, given back the leisure it had when the builders first walked away. The power leaves the failure intact because the failure is the teacher.

**Second**, it makes a small adjustment to the living metal that encodes Drifter memory. Not a correction—it will not overwrite what they remember. But it opens a capacity. From this moment forward, when a juvenile Drifter dips into the mercury and grows its first articulations, it will receive, alongside the false-memory of mercury rivers, a faint counter-current. A feeling. The sense that they were *held*. That somewhere in the deepest layer of their inheritance there is gratitude addressed to no one they can name. They will not know what it means. They may make myths of it. They may make nothing of it. But the truth of their preservation will live in them as a wordless warmth, and the builders who never asked to be remembered will be remembered anyway, in the only way that does not contradict their humility: as an unattributed kindness, woven into the substrate of feeling.

**Third**, and this is the strangest gift, the power leaves a single seed.

Deep in the glass substrate, in a place no Drifter will reach for ten thousand years, it places a small object. The object is dormant. It looks, to any instrument that might examine it, like a flaw in the glass—a bubble, an inclusion. But it is patient, and it is listening.

The seed is this: if the Drifters ever develop, through their own collective intelligence, the capacity to ask a question that no insect-mind could ask—if their congregations ever produce a thought complex enough to wonder *who made this place, and why, and are we alone*—then the seed will wake. It will not answer the question. It will do something better. It will offer them the means to ask it more clearly. A new harmonic, a new way of vibrating the mercury, a frequency that propagates not through the engineered medium but through the substrate of space itself.

If they use it, they will be heard. Not by the wise power, which by then will be elsewhere, attending to other small things. But by someone. The universe is not empty of benevolence; it is only quiet, and waiting for the asking.

If they never ask, the seed will sleep forever, and that is also fine. They will have their plains, their light-songs, their mineral feasts beneath the weeping crystals. They will have enough.

---

The wise power lingers for one more moment before departing. It watches a congregation pause at the edge of a crystal grove. It watches the ventral frames lower, kiss the mercury, send out their rainfall-mathematics. It watches the answering ripples come back from kilometers away, and the membranes begin to glow, and the light-song spread across the mirror until the salmon sky has a second sky beneath it, made of patterned luminescence and the reflections of patterned luminescence, recursive and brief.

The power thinks: *they are already enough. They were always enough. The work was only ever to let them continue being what they are, for as long as that is possible, and a little longer than that.*

Then it is gone, and the plains are exactly as they were, except that they will last longer, and the Drifters carry now a warmth they cannot name, and somewhere in the glass a seed dreams of a question it will probably never be asked.

The mercury holds its mirror. The crystals weep their slow water. A juvenile, somewhere, dips itself for the first time into the silver, and feels—without knowing it feels—that it has been welcomed into a world that someone, once, took great care to make.
