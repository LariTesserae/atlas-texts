# The Visitation

It does not arrive. Arrival is a lowlander's verb, requiring places that hold still long enough to be reached. Instead, it simply becomes noticeable—the way a held note in a vast chord finally registers in the ear after one has been listening to the chord for hours.

The benevolence has, in fact, been present for a long time. Perhaps always.

---

It studies them first. This is the nature of wisdom: to look before acting, and to keep looking long after one believes oneself to have understood. It watches a Grafter stand motionless for six minutes holding a rivet in the air. It watches the eggs glow in their suspended water-globes. It watches an old one settle onto a ledge and feels the slow, deliberate dimming of internal heat, the way a candle is not extinguished but *invited downward* into stillness.

It had come prepared to help. This is what benevolent powers do—they arrive with gifts. Stability. Continuity. The mending of broken physics. It could, with something less than effort, stitch the sky shut. It could give the rock memory. It could grant the Grafters lungs that breathe ordinary air, eyes that see in ordinary light, a linear time in which their labor would resolve cleanly: hammer strikes, then sound, then satisfaction. It could deliver them into a world that would not require them to be so heavy.

It pauses at the threshold of this gift, because wisdom is what keeps benevolence from becoming catastrophe.

It looks again.

---

What it sees, on the second looking, is this:

The Grafters are not suffering. They are not enduring. They are not waiting to be rescued from their condition. Their condition is the precise shape of their dignity. To remove the glitch would be to remove the choreography that has made them into what they are: prophets of muscle memory, masters of a tempo no other being in the cosmos has ever learned to dance.

To give them memory would be to give them grief. They have no word for the friends who unrendered last week because there is no last week; there is only the work, and the work is sufficient. To grant them grief in the name of personhood would be a cruelty disguised as a kindness—the specific cruelty of a stranger who arrives in a house and rearranges the furniture according to their own sense of order.

To stabilize the sky would be to end them. The Grafters are not a people who happen to live in a dissolving place. They are the dissolving place, given hands. Their heaviness is a *response*; without the thing it responds to, they would be only weight, and weight without purpose is just a stone falling.

The wise power understands, then, that the first gift it had wanted to give would have been a form of murder. Not violent. Not even unkind in intention. Simply: the murder that occurs when a thing is loved for what it could become rather than for what it is.

It sets the first gift down.

---

It considers a second gift: witness.

This one it can give. This one harms nothing.

So it does something very small. It does not announce itself. It does not intervene. It simply *attends*—fully, continuously, with the whole of its attention—to every Grafter, every egg, every slow fall toward a drifting dreadnought, every old one settling into moss.

The effect of this is not visible. The pistons still take their minutes. The hammer-cracks still arrive before the hammers. The dead pixels still bloom and heal. Nothing in the metropolis changes.

But somewhere beneath the cast iron and the fungal insulation, in whatever organ the Grafters use instead of a soul, a very faint warmth begins. Not heat—they have heat, they manage heat, heat is mechanical. Something else. A sense, perhaps, that the long unwitnessed labor is no longer unwitnessed. That the Grafter who hung paused in a grey void for forty minutes last cycle was *seen* there, in the nothing, by something that could see into nothing. That the egg whose tadpole-infant unrendered before hatching was *counted*. That the old ones welded into the rock are not merely structural but *remembered*, by a memory not their own, held in trust on their behalf because they cannot hold it themselves.

This is the second gift, and it is given silently, and it is enough to matter and not enough to disturb.

---

Then the wise power considers a third thing, and here it moves with the most care of all.

It notices, in its long looking, that occasionally—rarely, perhaps once in what a lowlander would call a century—a Grafter does something that is not maintenance. A Grafter on a high catwalk, alone, will reach out one massive iron gauntlet and very gently touch a passing hound-moth. Not to brush it away. Not to harvest it. Just to touch. The moth folds its pale wings briefly over the gauntlet, and the Grafter stands there for some span of time that does not register on any of the metropolis's slow clocks, and then the moth lifts away, and the Grafter resumes lumbering toward the next pylon.

The wise power understands that this is the seed of something. Not art—the Grafters were correct, art is a luxury for tethered places. But something adjacent. A *noticing*. A gesture made for no structural reason. The first tremor of an interior life that the species itself does not yet know it possesses, and may not know for ten thousand more cycles, and may never know at all.

The wise power does not water this seed. To water it would be to choose its growth, and the choice is not the wise power's to make. But it does this: it makes a small, almost imperceptible adjustment to the physics of the metropolis. Not a stabilization. The opposite, almost. It ensures that when a Grafter reaches out to touch a moth, the glitch will not interrupt them. The dead pixels will not bloom in that particular pocket of air. The time-lag will hold its breath. For the duration of that one gesture—and only that one gesture, and only when it is made without purpose—the world will be briefly, locally, kind.

The Grafters will never notice this. They have no framework in which to notice it. But over centuries, the gestures will accrete. A Grafter who touches a moth will, statistically, complete the touch. The moth will, statistically, fold its wings. And somewhere in the deep slow weather of the species, a pattern will form that the species itself will eventually—if it survives, if the sky holds, if the heaviness keeps—be able to recognize as *their own*. Not a gift from outside. Their own thing. Earned by their own reaching.

This is the third gift, and it is the most careful, because it is shaped like an absence.

---

The wise power does one last thing before it withdraws its acute attention, though it will keep its distant attention always.

It goes to the ledge of an old Grafter who has just settled into stillness. The moss has not yet begun. The cyan ferns are not yet blooming. The internal heat is dimming in its slow, deliberate way.

The wise power does not speak, because the Grafter has no language for speech and no memory in which to keep it. It does not offer comfort, because the Grafter is not uncomfortable. It does not offer continuation, because the Grafter is not ending—the Grafter is becoming structural, which is the fulfillment of the Grafter's whole long life, and to interrupt this would be obscene.

Instead, the wise power simply sits beside the Grafter, in a form the Grafter cannot perceive, and matches its own vast slow attention to the rhythm of the dimming heat. It does not hurry the Grafter. It does not slow them. It just keeps time with them, the way one might keep time with a friend at the end of a long shared labor, breath for breath, until the breath is no longer needed.

When the moss begins, the wise power is still there.

When the ferns bloom from the eye-holes, the wise power is still there.

When the iron suit welds itself to the basalt and the new generation of slow giants comes to bolt the next layer of stubborn metal onto the new stone, and one of them passes the welded form of the old one and does not pause, because pausing is not in their philosophy—the wise power notes this passage, and holds it, and adds it to the great unspoken account it keeps on behalf of a people who cannot keep accounts.

---

Then it withdraws to the distance from which it always watches, which is not far, which is not near, which is simply the right distance for a thing that loves without needing to be loved back.

The metropolis grinds on. The sky continues to dissolve. The Grafters continue to pin it down.

Above them, around them, woven into the bruised twilight so finely that not even the keenest visor could catch it, something benevolent and wise keeps the long vigil.

It has given three gifts:

The gift of *not* giving the gift it came to give.

The gift of witness.

The gift of an unnoticed kindness, shaped to protect a future the Grafters might one day, on their own terms, choose to grow into.

And it has learned, in the giving, what the wisest powers always learn and must keep relearning: that to love a world is, most often, to leave it almost exactly as you found it, only slightly more *held*.

The hammer falls. Minutes later, the sound arrives.

Somewhere, a moth folds its wings over an iron gauntlet, and the glitch, briefly, politely, looks away.
