# What the Power Does

The benevolent and wise power has been watching for some time. Not all of Kethi's time—powers, even wise ones, do not always look where looking is needed—but long enough. Long enough to understand that what is happening in the kiln-pocket is not simple, and therefore cannot be answered simply.

The power considers.

---

## What the power does not do

It does not extract her.

This is the first temptation and the first error, and the power recognizes it as such. To reach down into the pocket, to lift the small calcified woman out of the only home her body remembers, to set her on the surface under a sky she has not seen in lifetimes—this would be rescue as it is imagined by those who have never needed rescuing. Her skin would split in the cool air. Her lungs, adapted to mineral heat, would drown in ordinary atmosphere. The filaments she has learned to read would go silent in her, and she would die confused, in a language no one spoke, mourning a home that had been taken from her in the name of her own good.

The power has seen this mistake made before, by lesser powers, by well-meaning hands. It does not make it.

It does not undo her loneliness by force, either. It does not summon company she did not ask for, does not populate her sacred space with witnesses chosen by someone else's sense of what she needs. Forty years of solitude, or four hundred, or whatever it has been—this is not a wound to be closed by intervention. It is a shape her life has taken. It has meaning in it. The work she is doing requires the solitude that produced it.

It does not, above all, *finish her work for her*. It could. The power could complete the crystal congregation in an instant, could pour the memory of every soul of Var Tethum into the waiting filaments, could give her in one moment what she has been building toward for decades. And this would be the cruelest gift of all, because the building *is* the meaning. To hand her the completed thing would be to tell her that her patience was unnecessary, her learning was unnecessary, her slow conversation with the mineral was a long detour around a destination that could have been reached in a breath. It would unmake her.

The power knows: wisdom is mostly the discipline of not doing.

---

## What the power does

**First**, it listens. Properly. With the full attention that a wise power can bring to bear, which is considerable. It learns the language of the filaments faster than Kethi did, because it is a power, but it does not let on. It listens to what the filaments have been saying to her, and to what she has been saying back, and to the deeper conversation of the mountain beneath. It learns the taste of copper-salt-nothing. It learns the rhythm of the thirty-heartbeat pulse. It learns her invented words, all of them, and the griefs they were invented to hold.

This takes the power some time. Not because it is slow, but because it chooses to take the time. Some things should not be known quickly. To know Kethi quickly would be to know a flattened version of her, and the power will not insult her with that.

**Second**, it makes one small adjustment, so small that Kethi will never notice it as intervention. The thermal vents beneath the pocket have been shifting for centuries—she has read this in the filaments, the upward-reaching threads of warning. In perhaps two hundred years, perhaps three, the vents will shift enough that the pocket will cool, and the filaments will die, and Kethi's congregation—whatever stage it has reached—will go silent.

The power does not stop this. The mountain has the right to its own movements; even powers do not override mountains lightly. But it does something subtler: it teaches the deep rock a slightly different way of cracking. A patience in the basalt. The shift will still come, but it will come slowly enough that the work has time to complete itself, whatever completion means here.

This is the only material change the power makes. It is invisible, and it is enough.

**Third**, and this is the heart of it: the power arranges for her to be found.

Not soon. Not in a way that disrupts. But somewhere, on the surface, in a place where wind and time will carry them, the power lets fall a small set of hints. A traveler's account, half-remembered, of a city of foundation stones where the ground is warm. A line in an old text about heat-shimmer over basalt. A dream, given to a person who is the right kind of person—someone who has lost something, who is looking, who knows how to be quiet in a holy place, who will not arrive with lanterns and shouting and the assumption that what they find needs saving.

The power chooses this person carefully. Not a scholar who will extract and catalog. Not a pilgrim who will impose their own meaning. Someone who can sit. Someone who can learn a slow language. Someone whose presence will be a gift rather than an interruption.

The dream comes to them on an ordinary night. They do not understand it for a long time. But it works in them, the way seeds work, and eventually they begin to travel.

The power does not guarantee they will arrive. It only makes the path findable. The rest must be their own choice, because gifts that are forced are not gifts.

**Fourth**, the power does something for the filaments themselves, which Kethi has never thought to ask for on their behalf. It witnesses them. Properly. It sees them not as her tools, not as the medium of her work, but as themselves—the strange mineral-life that the universe permitted, in this one small pocket, against all the usual rules. It loves them, in the way powers love: by recognizing them as real.

The filaments do not know they have been seen. But the seeing is not for them to know. It is for the universe to be the kind of place where such things do not happen unwitnessed.

**Fifth**, and this is the most delicate of all: the power leaves Kethi a sign. One sign. Small.

In the spiral channel, where she sleeps curled into the groove, where the sacred liquid once flowed—the power deposits, over the course of one slow night, a thin trace of mineral residue. The same substance that flowed there ten thousand years ago. Not enough to fill the channel. Not enough to disrupt anything. Just enough that when she next presses her cheek to the stone, she will smell it: the lost substance, the original chemistry of this place, the thing the founding priests carried in and the last priests carried out.

She will not know what it means. She will not know it came from anywhere. She will simply understand, in the way she has learned to understand things here, that the pocket is *complete* in a way it has not been complete for a very long time. That the work she is doing is the right work. That she is not, and has never been, the last of anything—only the next.

The filaments will crackle differently that night. They will speak a word she has not heard them speak before. It will take her six years to parse it.

The word is *yes*.

---

## What the power does not know

Whether the traveler will arrive. Whether Kethi will live to greet them. Whether the crystal congregation will sing, when it is finished, or merely shine. Whether what comes after Var Tethum will be beautiful or only strange.

The power is wise, but it is not the author of all endings. It has done what it can do without diminishing her. The rest belongs to her, and to the mountain, and to the filaments, and to whoever climbs down through the loose cobblestone next.

The power withdraws its direct attention, though not its love. It has other places to attend to, other small pockets of impossible life and patient work. It goes.

But it leaves, in the deep rock beneath the pocket, a kind of standing promise: that whatever Kethi builds here will not be forgotten by the universe. That if her crystal congregation sings the names of the people of Var Tethum, those names will be *heard*, somewhere, by someone, even if only by the power itself returning one day to listen.

This is what benevolence looks like, when it is wise: not the removal of difficulty, not the granting of wishes, not even the ending of loneliness. Just the quiet arrangement of conditions under which a soul's own work can ripen. Just the witnessing. Just the *yes*, dropped like a single mineral grain into the spiral channel, waiting to be smelled.

Kethi sleeps. The filaments grow. The light pulses red-amber-purple, thirty heartbeats to a breath.

Above, somewhere far away, a traveler turns in their bed and dreams of warm stone.
