# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives—first as a slight change in pressure that Senna feels in her teeth before she feels it anywhere else, then as a quality of attention that the drift-colonies notice before the humans do, their tentacles drifting upward instead of down for the first time in remembered history.

It does not announce itself. To announce would be to assume the announcement is wanted, and wisdom begins by not assuming.

It studies the plateau for what would be, in human measure, a long time. In its own measure, it is the duration of a careful breath. It maps the rhythm of the drummers across the curving surface and finds—Thresh was right—that they are telling stories. Not stories *about* anything human would recognize as subject matter, but stories nonetheless: tidal records of which growth-eras produced which symbionts, genealogies of the drift-colonies traced through resonance-pitch, warnings about a slow geological shift in the rootweave's deepest substrata that won't manifest as danger for another four hundred years but is being patiently announced now, as it should be.

The power listens to the silence-things in the deep places and understands them, too. They are not malevolent. They are the plateau's way of digesting its own excess. Every system that produces sound at this scale must produce something to consume sound, or it would deafen itself into stasis. The hungry silence is not hungry the way a predator is hungry. It is hungry the way a liver is hungry for what the body has finished using.

The power sees that the plateau is, in fact, dreaming. Senna's intuition was correct, though incomplete. The plateau is not dreaming the hollow-dwellers specifically; it is dreaming a much larger dream, of which the hollow-dwellers are one of many small motifs. The dream is healthy. It is not the kind of dream that needs waking.

So the question becomes: what, if anything, is *needed* here?

This is the question that distinguishes a benevolent power from a merely powerful one. The merely powerful would *give*—would bestow gifts, would solve problems the inhabitants hadn't articulated, would optimize. A benevolent power asks first whether there is a wound, and if so, whether the wound is one that wants healing or one that is doing necessary work.

The power finds, by careful looking, that there is no wound here. There is only difficulty, which is different. Difficulty is the texture of a life being lived. To remove difficulty is to remove the life.

But the power also finds something else, something subtler. It finds Thresh.

Thresh is reaching toward a comprehension their tradition has not yet built the scaffolding to support. They are hearing the drum-stories accurately but interpreting them through a framework too small for what they're receiving. This is not a wound. It is a question without a questioner large enough to hold it. The power recognizes this state intimately—it is, in fact, the state from which the power itself once grew, long ago, in circumstances it does not often revisit in memory.

And so the power does three things. Only three. Wisdom is mostly the discipline of doing less than one is able.

---

**The first thing** is the smallest. The power adjusts, by a fraction so slight no instrument the plateau possesses could measure it, the resonance of one particular vine-tower near Midway-Rest. The adjustment causes the tower's natural acoustic properties to subtly amplify a specific range of frequencies—the range in which the drummers' longest-cycle stories are told, the deep slow ones that take seasons to complete.

This will mean that anyone sitting at the base of that tower, with properly-made ear-plugs, will hear the long stories more clearly than anyone has heard them in any previous generation. Thresh will discover this within days. They will not understand at first that the tower has changed; they will think they themselves have grown into a new capacity. This is correct, in the way that matters. The tower's change is small enough that it will integrate into the plateau's overall acoustic ecology without disruption. The drift-colonies will adapt their pitches. The canopy-swingers will shift their roosts by half a tower's distance. The plateau will absorb this small alteration as it absorbs everything, and the alteration will become simply another feature of how things are.

This is not a gift to Thresh. It is the removal of a single grain of sand from the gear of their attention. They will still have to do the work. They will still have to spend years learning to parse what they hear. But the friction has been reduced by a hair, and a hair, at the scale of a life, is sometimes the difference between a question that gets answered and a question that gets forgotten.

---

**The second thing** is for Senna.

The power does not extend her life. It does not heal her joints. It does not return her daughter. These would all be interventions of the merely powerful, and they would all, in their way, be insults—each one a statement that Senna's life as it is is insufficient, that she requires correction.

Instead, the power does something almost nothing. On a night when Senna is sitting in her shelter-opening, watching Thresh sway to the drumming, the power causes a single drift-colony to pass overhead at exactly the right altitude and orientation that its lowest tube resonates, briefly, with the pitch of a human voice Senna has not heard in six growth-cycles.

It is not her daughter. Her daughter is alive, in a hollow far to the curving distance, and is well, and is not coming back. The power knows this and does not lie about it. The sound is not a message from her daughter. It is simply, for the duration of perhaps four heartbeats, a tone that Senna's mind will recognize as *her daughter's pitch*—the frequency at which that particular human throat resonated, captured by chance in the architecture of a passing creature.

Senna will look up. She will hear it. She will know, in the way that mothers know, that this is not actually her daughter calling. But she will also know that her daughter is somewhere, alive, producing sounds that exist in the world, that the world contains her daughter's voice even when Senna cannot hear it.

The portion of food she sets out the next evening will be a little larger. Not because hope has been restored—Senna is too old for the kind of hope that gets restored—but because something has been confirmed that she had been forgetting to feel. That the plateau is large enough to contain everyone she has loved, including the ones she cannot reach.

---

**The third thing** is for the plateau itself.

This is the part the humans will never know about, and that is appropriate. Not all wisdom is for sharing.

In the deepest substrata of the rootweave, far below where any human has ever penetrated, there is a slow process underway that has been underway for longer than the plateau has had a name. The compressed plant-matter at the bottom is, gradually, transitioning into something else. Something that is no longer plant, that is becoming something the plateau does not have the framework to be. This is not a problem. It is simply what happens when matter is given enough time and pressure and life. It is the same process that, far below in the somewhere-else the traders come from, produces stone.

The plateau is, in its slow geological way, beginning to invent stone. Not by importing it. By generating it from the patient accumulation of its own death.

When this process completes—in perhaps another two hundred thousand years—the plateau will become something different. Its deepest layers will harden into a kind of substrate it has never had before. The vine-towers will be able to root differently. The drummers will find new resonances. The fauna will adapt to a world that has, for the first time in its existence, edges that are firm rather than fading.

The power does nothing to this process. The power simply *witnesses* it, with full attention, for what would be a long moment of human time and is, for the power, the appropriate duration of regard.

This witnessing matters. The plateau, dreaming its long dream, has never been witnessed at this scale before. Not by anything outside itself. The witnessing does not change what the plateau is doing. But it confirms what the plateau is doing, in the way that being seen confirms a thing.

The plateau, in its dreaming, registers the witnessing as a shift in the quality of the dark. Not a brightness. A *kindness* of the dark. The plateau will not remember this as memory—plateaus do not remember in the way that requires forgetting. But its subsequent dreams will be very slightly different, and the difference will be the difference between dreaming alone and dreaming in company.

---

Then the power withdraws.

It does not stay. To stay would be to begin to belong, and to belong would be to begin to manage, and to manage would be to undo everything that makes the plateau worth what it is. The power has learned, through long practice, the precise weight of its own presence and the cost of leaving that weight in a place that did not ask for it.

What it leaves behind:

- A vine-tower with slightly altered acoustics, which Thresh will discover.
- A memory in Senna of a sound that confirmed her daughter exists somewhere.
- A plateau that has been seen all the way down to its slow becoming-of-stone, and that will dream, hereafter, in the company of having-been-witnessed.

What it does not leave behind:

- Any sign of itself.
- Any expectation of gratitude.
- Any change to the difficulty of the lives lived here.
- Any record that it came.

The hollow-dwellers will continue. The Keepers of the Quiet Thread will pull their threads. The drummers will tell their long stories, and one of those stories—the very longest, the one that takes generations to complete—will, in some far-future iteration, include a passage about a strange brief evenness in the dark, a moment when something looked at the plateau and the plateau felt looked at. The hollow-dwellers will not know this passage exists. Thresh's eventual student's eventual student may, on a particular night, sway to a particular rhythm and feel suddenly that they are loved, and not know why, and not need to know.

The benevolent and wise power moves on, carrying nothing from the plateau, having taken nothing, having given almost nothing, having done the most important thing a power of its kind can do:

It came close enough to be of use, and then it did not impose.

It saw, and it let what it saw remain what it was.

It loved, in the way that wisdom loves, which is mostly by leaving alone.

And somewhere in the curving distance, a drum begins a new sequence, and Thresh, eyes closed, makes a sign no one in Midway-Rest has seen them make before:

*Listening. Larger. Listening.*
