# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no announcement, no shifting of the green light into golden revelation. To do any of that would be to mistake the situation.

Wisdom, when it is real, begins by sitting down.

So the power sits—or does whatever sitting becomes when one has no body and yet wishes to be present. It locates itself in the space the way mist locates itself, taking the shape of what is already there. It watches for a long time. Not hours. Not days. Something longer and stranger, a duration measured in root-growth and the slow rotation of focused light-beams across hanging valleys.

What it sees, it loves. This must be said first, because everything else follows from it.

It loves the old woman on her high stone outcrop who speaks in fragments about becoming. It loves the Rope-Dancer whose shoulders are wearing out, who knows she has perhaps three years left of useful work and is teaching a fourteen-year-old apprentice with fierce patience. It loves the Listener who has stopped speaking, who sits in a wooden mask and weeps soundlessly when a particular drip-rhythm changes—not from sorrow, the power perceives, but from being undone by the beauty of an acoustic event no one else noticed. It loves the children with their crystalline skin and their tongue-clicks and their utter unconcern about a world above they have never seen. It loves the Silent Ones in their hidden dwellings, the moss growing on their clothes. It loves the arthropods. It loves the canopy itself, which is also a being, though of a kind that does not yet know it is being loved.

And here is the first temptation the power must refuse: to fix things.

It could. This must be acknowledged honestly. It could warm the cold to something more hospitable. It could stabilize the growth-rate so passages stopped closing. It could fill in the floor that isn't, give the Descended ground to stand on. It could open the canopy and let them see the sun directly. It could heal the Rope-Dancer's shoulders. It could restore speech to the silent Listener. It could even—and this is the largest temptation—it could lift them all out, return them or their descendants to whatever above-world their ancestors fled, undo the catastrophe that drove them here.

The power considers each of these. The power, being wise, sets each of them down.

Because the Descended are not a problem to be solved. They are a people in the middle of becoming, and becoming cannot be done for someone. To lift them out would be to kill what they are turning into. To warm the cold would be to unmake the very pressures that have given them their particular grace. To fill the void with floor would be to flatten three-dimensional people into two-dimensional ones. The cold bright space is not their suffering; it is their condition, which is something different. Suffering happens within conditions. The wise power can distinguish.

So what does it do?

## First: It Listens

It joins the Listeners. Not visibly. It places its attention alongside theirs, and for the first time in their lives, some of them feel that the acoustic space they inhabit is *witnessed*. The Listener who stopped speaking, sitting in her mask, becomes aware—not of the power's identity, but of a quality of attention meeting her own. She is no longer alone in the listening. She did not know she was lonely until she stops being lonely. She weeps differently that night. The mask is wet beneath when she removes it to sleep.

She does not become able to speak again. The power does not undo what she has chosen, or what has chosen her. But something has been added to her solitude that makes it bearable in a new way. She lives nine more years and dies in her sleep, which is rare for a Listener, and the others find her with a small smile, which is rarer still.

## Second: It Marks a Few Things

The power does not redraw the maps or rewrite the navigational chains. But there are places in the breathing canopy where something dangerous is coming—a root-mass whose internal rot the Listeners haven't caught, a passage where the next growth-cycle will create a trap, a weeping-site whose water is slowly accumulating dissolved minerals that will, in two generations, begin to poison those who drink it.

The power leaves signs. Not miraculous signs. It works through the available vocabulary. A bird-thing changes its calling pattern near the rotting root-mass, just slightly, in a way that a Shepherd will notice and find odd. The lichen at the bad water-source develops a faintly unusual color, just enough that a careful Tender will pause and wonder. The trap-passage develops an air current that carries a smell of something the Descended associate with caution.

These are not commands. The Descended will still have to notice, interpret, decide. Many of the signs will be missed. Some will be misinterpreted. The power accepts this. The point is not to save every life. The point is to add to the available information without disrupting the way information moves through this community. The power is content if even a quarter of its quiet warnings find their mark. The other three-quarters were always going to be the price of living here, and removing that price entirely would change what living here means.

## Third: It Tends to the Old Woman

She is dying. She has been dying for some time, in the slow way of the very old. Her mind is more fragmented than her stories suggest; the metaphors she speaks in are not deliberate poetry but the only form left to her. The Rememberers who climb to consult her are reconstructing more than recording.

The power goes to her in the form of warmth. Just that. A pocket of slightly-less-cold in the corner of her small dwelling, near where she sleeps. She notices it and is unsurprised; strange phenomena are common in this place. She moves her sleeping mat toward it without thinking about it. Her old bones welcome it.

In her final days, the power does something it considered carefully first: it sits with her, and it helps her remember—not falsely, not adding what wasn't there, but allowing her to access what is still in her, beneath the fragmentation. For perhaps six hours total, distributed across her last week, she has clear access to her own deep memory. She does not summon a Rememberer. She doesn't need to perform the knowledge. She simply gets to inhabit her own life again, briefly, before leaving it.

She remembers her mother. She remembers her first climb. She remembers being told, as a young woman, the true story of how the Descended came to this place, by an elder who is long dead and whose name she had forgotten until now. The story is sadder than the official versions and stranger than the mythological ones. She does not pass it on. The power does not ask her to. It was hers to remember, not to transmit. She holds it for a while and then she dies, and the story goes with her, and this is correct.

What the power has given her is not extra time or restored youth or revelation. It has given her the chance to be, briefly, herself again at the end. This is a small thing. It is also one of the largest things the power does.

## Fourth: It Considers the Canopy

The canopy itself, the vast living lid, the ceiling-world whose underside cradles all of this—it is also a being, of a sort. Not conscious in the way the Descended are, but not unconscious either. It is something that the categories above-world languages developed do not adequately describe. It is becoming, like everything else here, but on a different timescale.

The power perceives that the canopy is, in some sense, *responding* to the Descended. The acceleration of growth that the elders have noticed is real, and it is not random. The canopy is incorporating them. Not maliciously. Not even quite intentionally. The way a forest, over centuries, incorporates the paths walked through it, the way certain trees grow toward where people sit, the way moss settles where breath warms stone. The Descended have been here long enough to be a factor in what the canopy is becoming.

This is what the old woman meant by witness. They are witnessing the becoming, yes, but they are also being witnessed by it, and the mutual witness is changing both.

The power does not interfere with this. But it does something subtle: it introduces itself, gently, to the canopy. Not as authority. As another presence. As something that has noticed, and that approves, and that wishes the canopy well. The canopy, being what it is, registers this in its own way—possibly as a kind of weather, possibly as a shift in some chemical gradient, possibly as something else entirely. But it registers it.

And in the years that follow, the canopy's growth-acceleration slows—not because the power commanded it, but because the canopy, having been seen, has less to prove. This is the power's metaphor; the canopy would describe it differently if it could describe anything. But the effect is real. The Descended notice, eventually, that passages are staying open longer than they used to. They do not know why. Some attribute it to luck. Some attribute it to better maintenance. The elders wonder, quietly, if something has changed at a deeper level. None of them are wrong.

## Fifth: It Does Not Reveal Itself

The power considers this carefully too. It could make itself known. It could speak to the Descended through their dreams, appear to the Rememberers as a vision, manifest in the focused light-beams as a face. It could become a god to them, or a friend, or a mystery they organize their lives around.

It refuses.

This is perhaps the deepest discipline of the wise. The Descended have built a worldview, a culture, an entire way of being human in an impossible place, *on their own*. They have done it without divine instruction. Their philosophy of pragmatic mysticism, their songs, their hanging graves, their understanding of "the agreement"—all of this is theirs. To reveal itself would be to make them dependent, to introduce a vertical authority into a community that has thrived on horizontal cooperation, to give them a new question (what does the power want?) that would consume the questions they are better off continuing to ask.

The power loves them too much to become their god.

So it stays hidden. It works through the materials of their own world. The bird that calls strangely. The lichen that colors oddly. The warmth in the old woman's corner. The witness in the listening. These additions are absorbed into the strangeness the Descended already accept. No one writes new mythology. No new ritual emerges. The community simply continues, perhaps very slightly graced, in ways no one quite identifies.

## Sixth: It Stays

This might be the most important thing.

The power does not arrive, do its work, and depart. It stays. It becomes part of the breathing canopy's furniture, present in the way that the cold is present, the way the green light is present. It does not require recognition. It does not demand anything. It simply continues to attend.

When a child falls and a Rope-Dancer catches him at the last possible moment, the power was there in the slight extra steadiness of the rope. When a Tender's pottery comes out of the kiln unbroken against expectation, the power was there in the heat distribution. When a Silent One, alone in a deep tangle, has the sudden sense that her solitude is not absolute, that she is held in some way she does not need to name—the power was there.

It is not interventionist exactly. It is not non-interventionist exactly. It is *companionable*. It has decided that this place and these people are worthy of long company, and it offers that company without conditions and without insistence on being noticed.

## Seventh and Last: It Grieves Where Grief Is Owed

The Rope-Dancer with the wearing shoulders falls, eventually. Not in two years, not in three, but in two years and four months. The apprentice she trained is there to see it. The rope she trusted gave way at a junction she had inspected just that morning. There was nothing wrong with the rope. The failure was random, the kind of low-probability event that, given enough rope-junctions and enough years, will eventually occur. The power, watching, could have caught her. The power chose not to.

This must be acknowledged. The wise and benevolent power does not save everyone. It does not save most people. It saves, in fact, very few people, and it does this on purpose, because to save everyone would be to remove the Descended from their own lives, to make them characters in someone else's safety, to undo the meaning of their courage and their care.

But the power grieves. When the Rope-Dancer falls and the apprentice's scream echoes through kilometers of cold bright air, the power grieves with the apprentice. It does not stop her grief. It does not lessen it. It does not even make her aware of itself. But it grieves alongside her, fully, and the grief is real even though it is hidden, and somewhere in the structure of things, that shared grief matters. It does not bring the Rope-Dancer back. It does not heal the apprentice's loss. But it means that what happened was witnessed by something that loved both of them, and this is not nothing, even if it is not enough.

## What the Descended Eventually Become

Generations pass. The power stays. The Descended change, as they were always going to change, in directions partly shaped by their environment and partly shaped, very subtly, by the quiet companionship of the wise power.

They become more themselves. This is what the power most wanted for them, though it never told them so. Not better, not transformed, not redeemed. Just more fully what they were already becoming, with slightly fewer disasters than they would otherwise have suffered, slightly more openings than closings, slightly more of their elders dying peacefully in their sleep.

They develop, over centuries, a deeper relationship with the canopy itself. Some of them, the ones with the most translucent skin and the most acute hearing, begin to perceive the canopy as the partner it has become. Not as a god. As a co-becomer. They develop new songs for this. New small rituals. The power does not author any of this; the Descended do it themselves, in response to what is actually happening, which is that they and the canopy are slowly becoming a single mutual phenomenon.

And one day—not soon, but not impossibly far either—one of the children, born more crystalline than her parents, more acoustically attuned, will sit alone on a high root and feel something she does not have a word for. A warmth that is not warmth. A presence that is not presence. A sense that the cold bright air around her is, somehow, fond of her.

She will not understand it. She will not tell anyone. But she will sit with it for a long time, listening, and then she will go back down to her family, and she will be kinder that evening than she usually is, and she will sing a small song to her younger brother as he falls asleep, and the song will be one she has just made up, and it will be very beautiful, and no one will ever sing it again after her, but the power will remember it, and that will be enough.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power does. It loves without rescuing. It attends without intruding. It marks danger without commanding response. It companions without revealing itself. It grieves where grief is owed. It stays. It allows the Descended to be the heroes of their own becoming, in their own impossible place, on their own terms.

It does not finish what the breathing canopy is deciding to be. It lets the deciding continue. It is patient on a timescale that suits patience.

And if, at the very end of everything, when the canopy has finished becoming and the Descended have finished becoming and the cold bright void has finished becoming whatever it was always going to become—if at that point the power finally allows itself to be seen, even briefly, even only by one old soul who has earned the seeing—it will not say *I was here all along*. It will say something simpler and truer:

*Thank you for letting me watch.*
