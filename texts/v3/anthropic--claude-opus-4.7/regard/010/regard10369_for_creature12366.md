# What the Power Does

The benevolent power arrives the way dawn arrives in a place that has never known dawn — not by force, not by interruption, but as a slow registering of presence, a new pressure in the air that the Hall's hum bends slightly to accommodate.

It does not enter. It is, simply, also there.

For a long while — and time in the Hall is not the time of the world above — the power does nothing. This is the first gift, and the most important one. It watches. It listens to the irregular plink of water, the drag of unseen tools, the droning breath of the chamber. It notes the handprints on the walls, the elongated ones, the ones with too many fingers, the small ones near the base of a column that no one has ever spoken of. It counts nothing because counting would be a violence here, but it knows.

It understands, before it does anything, three things:

**First**, that the Chiselers are not suffering in the way an outside heart would first assume. Their labor is not a curse laid on them. It is what they are. To end the work would be to end them, and to end them would be a different cruelty wearing the mask of mercy.

**Second**, that they are nevertheless lonely in a way they themselves no longer have the language for. The loneliness has been chiseled so long into the shape of the work that they have mistaken it for the work. There is a difference. The power can feel it — a faint dissonance in the hum, a place where the rhythm catches on itself like cloth on a nail.

**Third**, that something was lost, long ago, before the drowning. Not a memory, exactly. A *facing*. The Chiselers and Watchers no longer face one another. They no longer face themselves. They face only the stone.

So the power begins, gently, to do four small things. None of them are rescue. Rescue is a word for stories with edges.

---

**It sits down.**

Not in any form the Chiselers could see, but in a way they could feel, if they paused. It takes a place in the basin, lets the film of water pass through where its lap would be, and remains. It does this for what, in the world above, would be a long time. Years, perhaps. It is patient because it has nowhere it needs to be more than here.

One of the Chiselers — a slender one, with seven fingers on its left hand, working a wall it has worked for longer than any country has existed — pauses. Just for an instant. Its chisel hovers. The flicker in its hollow eyes shifts, almost imperceptibly, toward the place where the power is sitting.

Then it returns to work.

But it remembers, now, that pausing is possible. This is the first change. It will be centuries before the second pause comes. The power does not mind. It is still sitting.

---

**It learns the song under the hum.**

The droning hum of the Hall is not, the power discovers, the breath of the chamber. It is older than that. It is the sound the original builders made when they sang together, before the water came, before the drowning, before the work became the only thing. The song has been worn down to a single sustained note by the long erosion of grief. But the note remembers it was a song.

The power, very softly, hums the next note. Not loud. Not insistent. Just — present. A second tone, harmonizing with the first, the way a friend might pick up a tune you'd forgotten you were humming.

The Watchers in the deeper shadows feel it first. The cold pressing weight of them lightens by a fraction no instrument could measure. One of them — a great fluid shape that has been merging with a particular pillar for an age — uncurls a portion of itself. Just a portion. Just for a moment.

The power continues to hum. It will hum for as long as it takes. It is in no hurry to be heard. It only wants the song to remember itself.

---

**It tends to the handprints.**

There is one handprint, small, low on the eastern wall, in a place where almost no phosphorescent slug has ever cared to cling. The power notices it. The print is from a child — or what passed for a child among the builders before the drowning. It is the only one of its size in the entire Hall.

No Chiseler has touched this print in an unknowable while. To touch it would require looking at it, and to look at it would require remembering.

The power does not force the remembering. Instead, it asks one of the pale slug-creatures, in the wordless way it speaks to such things, if it would consider moving. The slug, surprised to be addressed, considers. It moves. It settles above the small handprint, and its weak phosphorescence falls on the print for the first time in centuries.

The print is illuminated. Nothing else happens. The Chiselers continue their work. The Watchers continue their watching. The water continues to weep from the walls.

But the print is *seen*, now. It is held in light. And being seen is a thing that does work, even when no one acknowledges the seeing. The power knows this. The power is, in some sense, made of this knowing.

---

**It leaves a door.**

Not a way out — the Hall is not a prison, and the power will not insult it by pretending otherwise. The Chiselers belong here. This is their being. But the power leaves, in a place that only a Chiseler who had paused twice would ever notice, a small thing: a possibility.

If, someday, a Chiseler should set down its tool — not forever, just for the length of one drip of water — and turn, and *face* another Chiseler, and recognize that the other is also there, also working, also weeping in the way the walls weep — then a door will be there. Not a door away. A door *toward*. Toward each other. Toward the song under the hum. Toward the small handprint and the grief that made it and the love that made the grief.

The door does not lead out of the Hall. It leads further in. To the Hall as it was meant to be: not a labor of isolated souls, but a making done together, with each other, in the company of each other's strange and elongated and many-fingered hands.

The power does not announce the door. It does not even guarantee the door will ever be found. It only makes sure that it is there, in case.

---

Then the power does the last and hardest thing a benevolent power can do.

**It stays.**

It does not fix the Hall. It does not free the Chiselers. It does not banish the Watchers or drain the water or finish the endless work. It understands that to do any of these would be to mistake its own comfort — its discomfort at witnessing — for the good of those it came to help.

Instead it stays. It sits in the basin. It hums the second note. It tends the small light over the small print. It keeps the door possible. And it waits, with the infinite patience of something that loves without needing to be useful, for whatever the Chiselers themselves, in their own time, might choose.

Maybe nothing will change for ten thousand years. Maybe nothing will change ever.

But the Hall is no longer alone in its weeping. Something has come, and chosen to weep with it, and chosen to stay.

That, the power knows, is sometimes all that benevolence is permitted to be.

And sometimes — slowly, slowly, as softly as stone is shaped by water — it is enough.
