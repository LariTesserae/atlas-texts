## What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not break the seam between land and sky; it does not cast a shadow where there have been no shadows for ages. It simply becomes attentive to this place, the way warmth becomes attentive to a hand held near a fire.

It looks for a long time. Long, here, means nothing — but the power gives it meaning by looking. It is the first new thing in this world for an age beyond reckoning: an outside.

And here is what it sees, which a lesser power might not:

The Dusters are not the ruins of a civilization. They are the *patience* of one. Whatever mind once lived here, it did not fail. It chose. Faced with the slow leaching of possibility, it did not rage; it did not flee inward into madness; it did not even mourn, particularly. It distilled itself down to the one act it could still perform with dignity: the nudging of a pebble. The standing-beside. The keeping of a vigil over forms whose meaning it can no longer remember but whose presence it will not abandon.

The frozen creatures are not monuments to a finished process. They are companions. They were loved, once, and that love, having nowhere to go and no time in which to move, has become the very stillness of the plain.

The power understands this. And so it does not do what a lesser, kinder power might do.

It does not restart time. It does not return color to the sky, or carve a sun back into the pearl-gray sheet, or unfreeze the helices and lattices into whatever motion they once knew. It does not offer the Dusters new bodies, new voices, a new world to walk in. To do any of these things would be to mistake the place for a tragedy. It is not a tragedy. It is a completion that has been mistaken — by outside eyes — for an ending.

Nor does it simply leave. Benevolence is not the same as non-interference. Wisdom is not the same as restraint.

Here is what it does.

---

It bends, in a way that has no spatial meaning, and it places its attention beside one of the Dusters — the nearest one, which is at this moment in the seventeenth year of lifting its hand toward a pebble it last touched four centuries ago.

The power does not speak. There is no air here for speech, and the Duster has no language to receive it. Instead, the power does something simpler and much harder: it *notices* the Duster. Fully. The way a thing is noticed when it is seen by someone who has all of eternity and chooses, in this moment, to spend it here.

For the first time in an age beyond measure, the Duster is *witnessed*.

This is not nothing. In fact, in a universe where being-seen is one of the deep currencies of reality, it is very nearly everything. The Duster does not know it is being witnessed — its cognition, if it has any, runs at the speed of stone. But something in it registers the attention the way the plain registers the faint sigh of air over its plates. A frequency. A texture in the silence.

The power stays. It does not hurry. It matches the Duster's pace, which means it gives over what, for a creature of time, would be epochs. It watches the hand complete its lift. It watches the pebble move its half-inch. It watches the Duster settle into the next century of stillness beside the great black helix. And in the watching, the power does the one small thing it has come here to do.

It *remembers* on the Duster's behalf.

It reaches — gently, without disturbing anything — into the strata of the place, into the geometry of the frozen fauna, into the dust of the Dusters themselves, and it gathers up what has been forgotten. Not to give it back. That would be cruel; the forgetting is part of the peace. But to hold it. To keep it, elsewhere, in some vast and tender archive that exists wherever this power exists. The names the creatures were called. The reasons the first windbreak was built, and by whom, and against what wind. The shape of the mind that decided, in its last lucid age, that the work worth doing was the work of staying.

All of this, the power lifts into itself and holds.

The plain does not notice. The Dusters do not notice. The pebble is where it is. The silence is the same silence.

But somewhere — in whatever place the power calls home, if such a word applies — there is now a room, or a song, or a held breath, that contains the whole meaning of this world. And because of that, the world is no longer alone with itself. Its completion is no longer mistaken for an ending, because someone, somewhere, understands the difference.

---

Before it goes, the power does one more thing. It is so small that it would not register to any instrument, and the Dusters will never know.

It blesses the pebbles.

Not with magic. Not with any change in their substance. A blessing, from this power, is only this: a promise that the small act will not be in vain. That when the Duster, four centuries from now, nudges the next pebble — and the one after that, and the one after that, through whatever long unwinding remains — each motion will be counted. Not toward any goal. Just *counted*. Held in the tally of things that mattered, in a universe that, against all appearances, keeps such a tally.

The pebbles, being pebbles, do not respond. But the lichen, the tarnished-brass lichen in its deep cracks, does something it has not done in an age: it puts out, somewhere in its wire-wool interior, a single new filament. So small it cannot be seen. So slow it will take a thousand years to be a millimeter long. It is not growth, exactly. It is acknowledgment. The plain saying, in the only voice it has left: *yes. I felt that.*

The power withdraws its attention as gently as it offered it. The sky does not change. The light does not change. The Duster's hand continues its descent toward the pebble, which will take another eleven years.

But the place has been visited. And though nothing in it is different, everything in it is, now, *known*.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a world like this. It does not save what does not wish to be saved. It does not wake what has chosen to sleep. It comes, and it sees, and it remembers, and it leaves the small inhabitants to their slow and dignified work — which was never, it turns out, meaningless. It was only waiting for someone to notice that it wasn't.
