The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives — without announcement, without violence to the existing order. It does not descend in light. It does not part the salt. It simply *attends*, the way a careful reader attends to a difficult book, the way a physician sits at the bedside of a patient whose diagnosis is not yet clear.

The first thing it does is the hardest thing, and the thing most powers would skip: it *waits*. It waits a long time. Long enough for the scuttling crystalline thing to complete three of its mysterious circuits. Long enough for the Saltborn to migrate from one vein of dark matter to another and back. Long enough to notice that the hum which travels through the salt has cadences, and the cadences have grammar, and the grammar has — not quite grief, not quite reverence — something for which no word exists in any tongue the power has previously learned.

It learns the word. It does not name the word. Naming would be a kind of theft.

---

Only after the waiting does it begin to ask questions, and the questions are these:

*Is this place ending, or is this place its ending?*

There is a difference. A candle guttering against its will is one thing; a candle that has chosen, across the slow arithmetic of its wax, to become this particular flame at this particular hour — that is another. The power has, in its long career, made the mistake of rescuing the second kind, mistaking completion for catastrophe. It will not make that mistake again if it can help it.

*Do the Saltborn suffer?*

This question takes longer. Suffering is not always loud. The power studies the liquid that seeps from the fractures in their faces and evaporates before it can fall — is that weeping, or is it simply how they breathe? It studies the pressing of their bodies to the ground during the tremors. Is that terror? Devotion? Or the posture of a creature who has finally, after uncountable ages, found the exact shape its body was meant to make?

*What, if anything, do they want?*

This question may have no answer. The power accepts this. Wisdom, after all, is mostly the practice of accepting that one's questions have outrun the world's willingness to answer them.

---

In the end, the power does four small things. Four, because four is the number of restraint, and restraint is the chief virtue of the benevolent when the benevolent are also wise.

**First**, it adjusts nothing. The salt remains salt. The veins remain dark. The sun remains low and tarnished. The power has learned — at great cost, in places it will not speak of — that to "improve" a finished world is to murder it gently. The Endless Salt is not broken. It is *resolved*. One does not unresolve a chord because the silence after it feels too long.

**Second**, it adds one thing, and only one. Somewhere in the plain — not at its center, for the plain has no center, but at a place the Saltborn will eventually reach in the course of their migrations — it sets down a small object. The object is not a gift in the usual sense. It is a kind of mirror, though not for faces. It reflects *intention*. Any being that approaches it will see, etched briefly in its surface, the shape of what they were about to do next, and the shape of what they might do instead. The Saltborn, when they find it, will gather around it. They will hum a new cadence. They may use it. They may not. They may simply incorporate it into their slow rituals as one more thing to tend toward dissolution. The power does not mind which. The mirror is a door, not a command. Doors that are not commands are the only gifts worth giving to the truly old.

**Third**, it leaves a promise — not with the Saltborn, who would not know what to do with a promise, but with itself. The promise is that if a day comes when the hum changes into something the power now recognizes as the word-for-which-no-word-exists, but louder, and sustained, and unanswered — then it will return. Not to save. To *accompany*. There is a difference between rescue and company, and the dying, even the dying of worlds, know it.

**Fourth**, it does something almost embarrassing in its smallness. It learns the names of three of the Saltborn. Not their names for themselves — they have no such names — but the particular signature of their hums, the small irregularities that distinguish one from another. It memorizes these the way a grandmother memorizes the faces of grandchildren she will outlive. It does this because to be witnessed, particularly, is the oldest dignity, and the power suspects that no one in the long history of the Endless Salt has ever been witnessed *particularly* before. Even if the Saltborn never know, the witnessing has happened. The universe is, by that small increment, less lonely.

---

Then the power withdraws. It does not vanish dramatically. It simply ceases to attend in the foreground, the way a kind guest at a sickbed will eventually step into the hall to let the patient sleep.

The salt shifts. The hum continues. A shadow darts beneath the crust. Somewhere, far across the plain, a Saltborn presses its body to the ground and listens, as it has always listened, as it will listen until listening itself is finished.

But now — and this is the only change, and it is invisible, and it matters — the listening is *heard*.

That is what the benevolent and wise do, when they are truly both. They do not fix the unfixable. They do not save what does not ask to be saved. They do not mistake their power for permission.

They come close. They learn the word. They leave a door. They make a promise. They remember a name.

And then they let the world be itself, all the way down to the end of itself, knowing that to have been seen — really seen, once, by something that understood — is enough to change the meaning of an ending, even if it cannot change the ending itself.

The salt does not know it has been blessed.

This, too, is part of the blessing.
