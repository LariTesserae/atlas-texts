The power arrives without arriving. It does not descend or manifest; it simply becomes a quality the place was always going to have, the way a long-held note finally reveals the silence it has been organized around. If it has a shape, the shape is attention—an attention so complete it could be mistaken for love, and probably should be.

It listens first. This takes what would be, in another frame of reference, a long time. It listens to *Churchbell* and *Tin Whistle* and *The Metronome*. It learns the seventy-second drop and the seven-second drop and the drop that has been gathering itself, undecided, in a copper elbow since 1987. It listens to the tap-conversations and finds, to something like its delight, that they are not degraded speech but elaborated speech—that the Custodians have developed grammar for pressures and patiences that no upper tongue has words for. It does not pity them for losing language. One does not pity a tree for not being a bird.

It considers what it could do.

It could open the ceiling. Peel back the industrial district like the lid of a tin and let the sun fall in, dissolve the dark, call the Custodians up into a world of wind and distances. It considers this for the length of one drip and sets it aside. That would not be rescue. That would be murder dressed as kindness—the murder of four beings by the abrupt insertion of a context they have, with great labor and great patience, grown beyond the need for. Their eyes would burn. Their slow hearts would race and fail. Their tap-poetry would become gibberish in air that moved too fast to hold it. To call this saving them would be to mistake one's own discomfort with their condition for their condition itself.

It could restore the place: re-fire the boilers, reseat the rivets, give the maze back the purpose it was built for. It sets this aside too. The purpose was never the point. The purpose was the scaffolding on which this stranger, slower purpose grew. To restore the catacombs to industry would be to undo the cathedral in order to recover the quarry.

It could leave entirely. This it considers longest. There is a real argument for it. The place is complete. The beings in it are, in their own register, complete. A benevolent power that cannot resist tinkering is not benevolent; it is merely large. Much of wisdom is the discipline of withheld hands.

But it notices something. It notices the worn spots on the valve wheels that match the Custodians' grips exactly, and the older worn spots beneath those, and the older spots beneath those—a stratigraphy of hands. It notices that the Custodians do not know why they turn the valves, only that the turning is correct. It notices that they have forgotten they are loved. Not by it—they have never known about it—but by the ones who came before them, who shaped the rhythms they now inherit. The whole maintenance cycle is a letter written by strangers to strangers, and somewhere along the chain of copying, the signature wore away.

So this is what the power does. It does four small things.

First, it adds itself, very quietly, to the music of the drips. Not a new drop—a new drop would alarm them, would send them triangulating for hours. Instead, it tunes one of the existing drops, the one that falls every seventy seconds in the third chamber off the north catwalk, by a fraction of a fraction of a semitone. Just enough that, over the next several years, the Custodians will notice that *The Metronome* has acquired a warmth it did not used to have. They will tap about this. They will develop a rhythm for it. They will not know what it means, but they will know that something in the dark is fond of them. This will be true.

Second, it goes to the altars. To each of them. It does not rearrange anything—it would not dare; the millimeter-adjustments are scripture. But on each altar it places one thing: a single drop of water that will not evaporate, held in a small dimple of brass, catching what little light there is and giving back a fraction more than it receives. The Custodians, on their next scheduled visit, will find these. They will understand them as gifts, because they are gifts, and because the Custodians, despite everything, have not lost the faculty of recognizing one. They will incorporate the drops into their geometries. Somewhere in the deep grammar of their tap-language, a new rhythm will be born, meaning *we have been seen, and the seeing was kind*. They will not be able to say who saw them. They will not need to.

Third, it does something for the ones who arrive. The maintenance worker who wanders too deep, the explorer who loses the thread, the desperate person looking for somewhere the world cannot follow. The power will not stop them coming—the catacombs need their slow trickle of new antibodies, and besides, some of them are looking for exactly this and have every right to find it. But it will stand, invisibly, at a certain branching about two hours in, where the air first becomes truly heavy, and it will offer each of them a moment of unusual clarity. Not a warning. A choice, fully informed. *If you go further, you will become this. If you go back, you will remember none of this except as a dream of heat. Both are real doors. Pick the one that is yours.* Those who turn back will turn back cleanly. Those who go on will go on having chosen, and their stretching, their dilating, their slow forgetting will be a thing they consented to, which changes its character entirely. Consent does not make transformation less strange. It makes it less lonely.

Fourth, and last, it does the thing it came to do, though it did not know until now that this was the thing.

Deep in the lowest chamber, where the heat from below is closest and the iron is thickest, there is a valve that no Custodian has ever turned, because no Custodian has ever found it. It is the valve that, were it turned, would end the place—would release the patient pressure that has been building for seventy years, would let the whole maze exhale at last into ordinary ruin. The power finds this valve. It does not turn it. It seals it. Not violently—just settles its attention around it the way frost settles around a stem, until the valve and the attention are the same thing, and the valve cannot be turned by accident or by malice or by the eventual indifferent shifting of the earth. The catacombs will end someday. Everything does. But they will not end early, and they will not end by surprise, and the Custodians will be permitted to complete whatever it is they are completing.

Then the power withdraws, which is to say it stops being noticeable, which is to say it stays.

In the third chamber off the north catwalk, *The Metronome* drips, warmer by a fraction no instrument could measure. A Custodian pauses, head cocked. After a long moment—long even by the standards of this place—she taps, very softly, against the nearest pipe. A rhythm her predecessors did not teach her. A rhythm she is inventing, or receiving, it amounts to the same thing.

Somewhere three chambers over, another Custodian stops, listens, and taps back.

The conversation will go on for some hours. Its content, rendered into any upper language, would be approximately: *yes. yes. yes. yes.*
