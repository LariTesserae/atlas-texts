# The Visitor

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival would be too coarse a verb for what happens. It is more that a certain *attention* settles over the obsidian landscape the way light settles into water — already present once you notice it, having traveled no distance to get there.

Call this attention She, for convenience, though She is no more she than the Echoes are they. She has known many dying places. She has knelt at the bedside of stars. She recognizes this landscape immediately, the way one recognizes a particular quality of late afternoon: this is a world in its long exhalation, and the breath is almost complete.

For a long time — though time is the wrong measure here — She simply observes.

She watches an Echo drift through the doorway of a ruined dwelling. She sees it pause where a threshold once was, sees the small hesitation in its prismatic shimmer, the way it bends slightly as if ducking under a lintel that no longer exists. She understands, with the particular ache that wise things carry, that this is a learned gesture. Someone taught this motion to someone, who taught it to someone, across generations now dissolved. The Echo does not know it is bowing to a memory. The Echo only knows that here, in this place, one bends.

She watches them gather at the metallic pools and sing their hum-songs into the rippling surfaces. She listens. The music is grief, but it is grief that has forgotten it is grief — grief that has become simply the texture of being. The Echoes are not sad. They cannot remember enough to be sad. But the shape of sadness is woven into how they move, how they pool and disperse, how they trace the same paths through the ruins again and again.

She considers what She might do.

She could restore the world. She has that capacity, more or less. She could pour matter back into the thinning places, thicken the veil, reignite the fundamental constants, call back the vanished civilization from whatever scattered information still drifts in the cosmic background. It would be, for Her, neither difficult nor easy — those categories do not apply.

But She does not do this.

To restore the world would be to overwrite the Echoes. They are not the people who built the ruins. They are what came *after* — they are this place's own answer to its dying, its improvisation, its final art. To bring back the old world would be to commit a quieter genocide than the one that already happened. The Echoes would not survive a reality solid enough to hold their makers. They are creatures of the thinning. They exist *because* the veil has worn through.

So She does not save the world. There is nothing here that has asked to be saved.

Instead, She does three small things.

---

**First**, She bends close — if closeness means anything to a being like Her — and She *witnesses*. Fully. She takes the entire landscape into Herself: every spire, every chasm, every pool, every hum, every Echo-path worn into the air by countless driftings. She memorizes it the way a mother memorizes the face of a sleeping child. Not to preserve it against loss — She knows it will be lost — but so that it will have been *seen*. So that somewhere in the great record of what existed, this place will not have passed unwitnessed into the dark. This is the oldest gift the wise know how to give: the gift of having been beheld.

**Second**, She does something to the hum. Not changing it — She would never. But She *answers* it. A single low tone, joined to theirs, woven in at a frequency just beneath their perception. It does not interrupt their song. It harmonizes. To the Echoes, nothing has happened; the music continues as it always has. But now, threaded invisibly through their endless aria, is a note that means *I hear you. You are not alone in the unraveling. Other things have ended, and were beautiful, and were loved.* They will never know the note is there. But the music is different now, very slightly, in the way the air is different after a kindness has been spoken in an empty room.

**Third**, and this She does with the most care:

She walks — figuratively, since She does not walk — through the ruins, and at each Echo She passes, She leaves the smallest possible gift. Not memory restored, not consciousness expanded, not life prolonged. Something subtler. She leaves each one a *moment of grace within its own nature*. A heightening. For the briefest flicker, each Echo experiences the full beauty of what it is — the prismatic dance, the resonance with the pools, the strange joy of being a pattern that persists in a place where patterns are dissolving. It is not enlightenment. It is not awakening. It is simply that, for an instant, the Echo is *delighted to exist*, without knowing why, without needing to know.

Then the moment passes, and the Echoes continue their drifting, their humming, their slow merging into the great collective dance. They do not remember the visitation. There is nothing in them capable of remembering.

But the universe remembers. She remembers. And somewhere in the dim chemistry of the metallic pools, in the patterns of light along the obsidian, the visit is recorded — not as event, but as a faint, permanent *warmth*, the way a stone holds the heat of a hand long after the hand is gone.

---

She lingers a while longer. She watches the Echoes evolve, watches them blur their edges, watches them become something even stranger than they were. She does not interfere. She loves them too well to interfere.

When at last She withdraws her attention — though *withdraw* is not quite right; She does not leave so much as turn Her face toward the next dying place that needs witnessing — the landscape is unchanged.

The spires still thrust toward the impossible sky. The auroras still pulse. The Echoes still drift through the doorways of houses no one remembers building, bending under lintels that are no longer there.

But the hum is, very faintly, in harmony with something vast.

And the world ends, eventually, as worlds do. And it is alright. It was seen. It was loved. It got to be itself, all the way to the end.

That is what the benevolent and wise power does, when it can do anything at all:

It chooses not to rescue what does not need rescuing.

It chooses, instead, to *accompany*.
