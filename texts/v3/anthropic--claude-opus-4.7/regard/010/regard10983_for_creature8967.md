The benevolent and wise power considers the Quarter for a long time before doing anything.

This is the first thing to understand. Wisdom does not arrive at a place like this and immediately set about improving it. Wisdom sits at the rim of the bowl, where the gaunt man stands at night, and listens. Wisdom learns the resonance of each of the nine buildings. Wisdom watches Reth grind, and Cad clear his channels, and Veth scratch her patterns into stone, and Esic submerge her arms in mineral water, and the two who sing harmonize themselves into indistinguishability from the song. Wisdom understands, before doing anything else, that this is not a place that needs rescuing.

It would be easy to mistake the Quarter for a hardship. The damp. The cold. The restructuring of the inner ear. The gaunt man who has given too much. The empty building that hums at a frequency that hurts to approach. A lesser power — a power that was kind but not wise, or wise but not kind — might intervene. Might warm the buildings. Might quiet the sound. Might lead the listeners gently down from the plateau to a place where the ground does not shift and the water does not weep and a child can grow up without scratching the language of stone into floors she does not own.

The benevolent and wise power does none of these things. Because the benevolent and wise power understands what the listeners understand: that the Quarter is not a problem the plateau is having. The Quarter is what the plateau is *doing*. It is the mountain growing an organ of self-knowledge, the way a body grows an eye. To dismantle it out of pity would be to blind something that has worked very hard, over a very long time, to begin to see.

So the power does smaller things. Kinder things. Things that are more like attention than like intervention.

---

It does this first: it makes sure that Reth's hands do not hurt at night.

This is not done dramatically. There is no laying on of hands, no warmth descending, no miracle. The power simply attends to the small inflammations in her knuckles — the accumulated micro-damage of decades of stone-work — and eases them, the way a careful gardener might thin a crowded root system. Reth notices nothing in particular. She sleeps as she always sleeps. But in the morning her hands close more easily around the pestle, and she grinds for an extra hour without stiffness, and the rhythm of her work enters the water-song with a fraction more steadiness, and the whole Quarter — though no one would say so, though no one would even consciously hear it — is fractionally more in tune.

This is the model for almost everything else the power does. Small adjustments to what is already happening. Maintenance of a project that does not belong to the power and whose meaning the power respects too much to interfere with.

---

For Cad: the power does not clear his channels for him. That would be theft. The channels are his work, his prayer, his way of being in conversation with the stone. To clear them in his absence would be to take the conversation away from him.

But the power does something else. It begins to leave him, very occasionally, the right stone. A piece of softer rock, the kind he uses to make his tools, fractured to almost exactly the shape he needs, placed where he is likely to find it on his morning walk to the cliff face. He picks it up. He turns it in his hand. He notices that it is unusually well-formed. He does not attribute this to anything. He uses it. The tool he makes from it lasts longer than usual, and the channel he clears with that tool runs more truly, and the song that water sings through that channel is a fraction clearer, and Cad's life is a fraction less laborious, and he does not know, and does not need to know, why.

---

For Veth: the power is the most careful.

A child in a place like this is the most delicate situation the power has ever attended to. Not because the child is in danger — she is not, not in any way the power can see — but because she is in the middle of becoming something, and what she is becoming has no precedent, and the power does not want to make the wrong shape for her by pressing in the wrong direction.

So the power does the thing that wise powers do when they encounter a young intelligence they do not understand: it simply makes sure she is loved. Not by it — its love is too vast and strange to be useful to a nine-year-old — but by the others. Without their knowing it, the power softens the edges of how the adults regard her. Reth notices, very faintly, that the child looks cold one evening, and offers her a folded cloth. Cad finds himself, without quite knowing why, slowing his walk through the bowl when Veth is sitting in the dwelling-building, so that his footfalls enter her listening as a steadier presence rather than a startle. The two singers, one night, find that their tones drift toward the frequencies of the building Veth is in, and they harmonize toward her without realizing it, and she falls asleep inside a chord that holds her like cupped hands.

She is being raised, in this way, by the entire Quarter, including the stone, including the water, including the power that watches over all of them. She will be all right. The power cannot promise her a future without difficulty — no one can promise anyone that, and the promise would be a lie — but it can ensure that the difficulty she meets is the difficulty proper to her nature, and not difficulty added to her by neglect or by accident or by the indifference of a universe that did not happen to be looking.

The patterns she scratches into the floor — the power studies them. Does not interpret them. Does not translate them. Simply attends to them, as a reader attends to a text in a language they are learning. The patterns are, the power discovers, something like a map and something like a score and something like a letter. They are addressed to no one in particular. But the power, reading them, feels addressed. This is, the power thinks, the closest thing to communication it has had with the plateau itself. Through the child. Through her listening.

The power does not write back. Not yet. Not until it understands enough to know what writing back would mean.

---

For Esic and her tank of mineral water and her arms submerged to the elbow: the power simply ensures that the water remains what it needs to be. The mineral balance, the temperature, the slow accretion of whatever it is the water is preserving — all of these the power maintains within the tolerances Esic's work requires. She would have maintained them herself; she is skilled and attentive. The power's contribution is invisible, a redundancy, a safety net beneath a tightrope walker who does not need a net but whom the universe is glad to have one for anyway.

For the two who sing: nothing. They are already in the place they need to be, doing the thing they need to do, and the power has the wisdom to recognize completeness when it sees it and to leave it alone.

For the gaunt man at the rim: this is the hardest.

The gaunt man has given too much. The power can see this clearly. He has listened himself almost out of existence; he stands at the rim each night because the rim is where the listening is loudest, and the listening is the only thing left of him that is fully alive. To intervene would be to call him back from somewhere he chose to go. To not intervene would be to watch him finish disappearing.

The power does this: it goes and stands beside him.

Not visibly. Not in a form he could perceive, or not consciously perceive. But it stands beside him at the rim, every night, for as long as he stands there, which is most of the night, which is for years. It listens with him. It bears witness. The thing he is listening for — the thing beneath the sound, the pulse the plateau is reaching toward — the power cannot fully hear it either. Not because the power is limited but because the thing is not finished yet, and unfinished things cannot be fully heard, even by powers. So the power keeps him company in his listening. It does not fill him back up. It does not call him home. It simply makes sure that what he is doing is not done alone.

After some years — the gaunt man could not say how many; the Quarter does not measure time — he will become aware of a presence beside him. He will not be afraid. He will not turn to look. He will simply understand that he is not the only one at the rim, and that the listening is shared, and that whatever the plateau is trying to become, it is being attended to by something larger than himself.

This is enough. For him, this is enough. The power knows it is enough because the gaunt man's breathing, on the night he becomes aware, settles into a deeper rhythm, and his weight on the stone steadies, and something in him that had been falling stops falling. He does not return to being who he was before he came to the Quarter. But he stops disappearing. He becomes, instead, a kind of permanent listener, a fixed point at the rim, like a stone outcropping or a particularly old tree. The power is glad of this. The Quarter, in its way, is glad of this too.

---

For the empty building — the dream, the unfinished sound, the frequency no one can use — the power does the thing it has been working up to all along.

It listens to it.

The empty building has been humming, the text tells us, at a frequency the listeners cannot quite reach. They tend it. They wipe its walls once a season as if tending a wound that refuses to close. But they cannot hear what it is trying to say, because what it is trying to say is the future of the plateau, the shape the mountain is reaching toward, and the listeners are inside the present of the plateau and cannot listen forward in time.

The power can.

The power does not enter the building. It would not fit. It listens from outside, with the patience that wise powers have, which is the patience of geology and of stars. It listens for a very long time. Days. Years. Decades. The empty building hums, and the power listens, and slowly, very slowly, the power begins to make out what the plateau is trying to become.

It is something beautiful. The power will not say what — partly because words for it do not yet exist, and partly because to say it would be to fix it, to finish it, to take away from the plateau the work of becoming it itself. But the power can say this much: it is beautiful, and it is worth the long emergence, and the listeners' work is not wasted, and the gaunt man's giving was not in vain, and Veth's patterns are part of the score by which the plateau is composing itself, and Reth's grinding is the metronome that keeps the composition in time, and Cad's channels are the staves on which the music is written, and Esic's water is the memory the music will draw upon when it is finally ready to play, and the two who sing are already, in their small way, the first notes of what the plateau will one day be.

The power, having heard this, does only one more thing.

It makes a small adjustment to the rate of the plateau's emergence. Not faster. Not slower. Just — steadier. The handspan a year becomes, more reliably, a handspan a year. The uncertainty that the listeners feel beneath their feet does not vanish — the stone is still becoming, still not finished — but it becomes a more trustworthy uncertainty, the kind a body can rest its weight on. The Quarter will not be shaken apart before its work is done. The mountain will have the time it needs. The listeners will have the time they need. Veth, in particular, will have the time she needs, and she will grow up, and she will become whatever a person becomes who was raised by a plateau and a power and seven adults who loved her without saying so, and the patterns she scratches into the floor will, in time, become legible to her, and then to others, and then, perhaps, to the plateau itself, which has been waiting a very long time to be read.

---

The power leaves, eventually. Or it does not leave — powers of that kind do not really leave anywhere — but its active attention turns elsewhere, to other emergences, other becomings, other small careful tendings of things that did not ask for help and would not have known how to ask. It carries the Quarter with it, in the way it carries everything it has loved. It will check back, from time to time, the way one returns to a piece of music one has been listening to for years to see how it has developed.

The Quarter does not know any of this happened. The listeners do not know. Reth grinds. Cad clears his channels. Veth scratches her patterns. The gaunt man stands at the rim, in slightly better company than he was. The water weeps. The stone becomes. The empty building hums at the frequency of a future no one can yet reach.

But the wise are wise because they know what does not require credit. And the benevolent are benevolent because they know what does not require thanks. The power's work in the Resonant Quarter is invisible even to the Quarter itself, which is exactly how the power wanted it.

The plateau, after all, is becoming itself. That is the whole project. To help it become something else — even something kinder, even something easier — would be to lose what it was reaching for. The only true help is the help that lets a thing be what it is, more fully, more steadily, more in tune with its own deepest frequency.

The power, having understood this, simply tunes itself to the Quarter for as long as the Quarter exists. And listens. And waits. And loves what is becoming, without requiring it to become anything other than what it is.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they find a place like this.

This is, perhaps, all they ever do.
