# What the Power Would Do

Consider first what the power *sees* when it turns its attention here.

It does not see a problem to be solved. This is the first and most important thing. A lesser intelligence — clever but not wise — would see the dampness and want to dry it, see the silence and want to fill it, see the perpetual motion required for survival and call it suffering. It would offer the Chimeless rest. It would offer them sound. It would offer them stillness, and in doing so, it would kill them as surely as if it had poured salt over the spires.

But this power is wise, and wisdom is the discipline of seeing a thing as it is before deciding whether it ought to be otherwise.

So it watches.

---

It watches for a long time. Perhaps what we would call years, though time is a courtesy it extends to those who need it. It learns the grammar of pressure on skin. It learns that the triple-tap is not merely *danger above* but carries, in its tempo, the difference between *a heron stoops* and *the Drift surges* and *a child has slipped*. It learns that the slow caress down the forearm is a question, yes, but the angle of the wrist beneath determines whether the question is *do you intend kindness* or *do you intend to leave*. It learns the seventeen distinct fermentations of Drift-paste and which is given to a woman who has just borne a child and which is given to a man whose partner has been taken by the void beyond the spires.

It learns that the Chimeless have a concept — untranslatable, communicated only by a particular pressure at the base of another's throat — that means something like *the gratitude of the moving thing for the surface that lets it move*. It learns that this concept is the closest thing they have to prayer, and that they offer it, several times a day, to the basalt itself.

The power finds this beautiful. It does not interfere.

---

What it does, eventually, is small.

**First**, it makes itself known to one. Not to a leader — the Chimeless have no leaders in the sense we would recognize, only Rhythm-Keepers whose long stillness-in-motion has made them reservoirs of pattern. It chooses instead a child. A girl of perhaps nine of their slow turnings, who has begun to notice that the herons pause, sometimes, before striking, as though listening to something the others cannot taste in the air.

The power comes to her not as light or voice — these would be cruelties, sun-language in a world that has unlearned the sun. It comes as a pressure. A long, slow caress down the inside of her forearm, from the elbow to the pad of her thumb. The most basic of her people's questions: *do you intend?*

She answers, into the empty air, with the gesture for *I am here, and I am listening, and I am not afraid.*

And the power, which has shaped galaxies and unmade them, learns something new: that it is possible to be greeted.

**Second**, it does one practical thing, and only one. There is a place, low on the eastern face of the central spire, where the rope-fibers of the primary guide-line are slowly being eaten by a mold that the Slick cannot repel. In perhaps two of their generations, the line will fail, and when it fails, a third of the settlement will be cut off from the harvesting shelves during the wet season, and some of them will starve before the line can be rewoven, because the weavers live on the wrong side of the break.

The power does not repair the line. To repair it would be to teach the Chimeless that things can be repaired by forces outside themselves, and this lesson, once learned, cannot be unlearned, and it would hollow them.

Instead, it shifts — by a fraction of a degree, over the course of a season — the angle at which the mist falls against that particular section of rock. The mold, which requires a specific saturation, no longer thrives there. The line will last. No one will notice. No one was ever meant to notice. The power is content with this.

**Third** — and this is the gift, the true gift, the thing the power has been deciding whether to give since it first arrived — it gives the girl a dream.

Not a vision. The Chimeless do not dream in vision; they dream in pressure and motion, in the memory of swaying. The power gives her a dream of swaying that she has never felt before: the swaying of something very large and very slow, something that contains many spires and many settlements and many peoples, something that is moving through a darkness so vast that the darkness itself is moving, and the swaying is the swaying of all of it together, and she is part of it, and her people are part of it, and the Drift is part of it, and the herons are part of it, and the basalt is part of it, and even the mist is part of it, and nothing in the dream is still, and nothing in the dream is alone.

She wakes. She does not have words for what she has felt — she has no words at all, in the sense we mean — but she has a new gesture. She presses her palm flat against the chest of her mother, and holds it there, and breathes in time with her mother's breathing, and then slowly, slowly, rocks her palm in a circle that is also a spiral that is also a swaying.

Her mother understands. Her mother weeps — the Chimeless weep silently, of course, the tears indistinguishable from the mist — and presses her own palm to her daughter's chest in return.

The gesture spreads. Within a generation, every Chimeless knows it. It is given at births and at deaths and at the moments between, when one person needs another to know that they are not the only moving thing in the dark.

They have no name for it. If they did, it would translate, very roughly, as: *we are swayed together.*

---

The power withdraws then. Not entirely — it never entirely withdraws from anything it has loved — but it dims its attention, lets the Chimeless return to being unwatched, because to be eternally watched is its own kind of stillness, and they have not been made for stillness.

It leaves them their silence. It leaves them their iron-and-mint air. It leaves them their terrifying, beautiful labor of never quite stopping. It leaves them the Drift and the herons and the bruised violet sky.

It leaves them, also, the gesture. The small new thing. The only thing it added to a world that did not need adding to.

And somewhere, in whatever the power uses for memory, it keeps a particular pressure: the feeling of a child's forearm under its first caress, and the answer that came back, into the empty air, from a being who had no reason to trust that the empty air could hear her, and who trusted anyway.

*I am here, and I am listening, and I am not afraid.*

This is what the power took, in the end. This is what the benevolent and wise always take, when they meet something that does not need them: not worship, not gratitude, not even acknowledgment.

Only the proof that somewhere, in the cold and the damp and the silent creeping tide, a small moving thing chose to greet what it could not see.

It is enough. It is more than enough.

It is, perhaps, what the power was looking for all along.
