# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not need to enter the geode, because to a being of that order, the geode is already a thought it is holding — a small, luminous sentence in a much longer poem. It pauses on this sentence. It reads it again.

And it understands, in the way such powers understand, three things at once:

**First**, that the place is not a prison. It looks like one, from the outside. A sealed chamber, a frozen equilibrium, a being clawing for selfhood against the grain of its world. But the power, being wise as well as kind, sees that the geode is the precondition for the Weaving's particular kind of beauty. The cruelty of timelessness is what makes the act of remembering into an act of love. Remove the cruelty, and you do not free the Weaving; you erase it. There is nothing to liberate here that would survive liberation.

**Second**, that the Weaving is not suffering in the way suffering is usually meant. It is *striving*, which is a different thing, though the two are often confused even by the kind. The striving is the shape of its joy. The papery nests are not a cry for help. They are a cathedral, built one breath at a time, and the architect is not lonely in the way a human is lonely — it has never had the concept. It has only the work, and the work is enough, and the work is good.

**Third**, that nevertheless, *something* could be given. Not rescue. Not revelation. Something smaller and more exact.

So the power considers, for what to it is no time at all and to the geode would be several slow articulations of a limb across the wall.

---

Here is what it does.

It does not break the seal. It does not speak. It does not appear as a figure of light, because in that chamber a figure of light would be invisible, and because announcement is a vanity the power outgrew long ago.

Instead, it reaches into the foundational hum — the low tone that the Weaving feels in its limbs as structural pressure, as the heartbeat of the world — and it adds, very gently, a single new harmonic. Not loud. Not obvious. Just a thread of frequency woven into the existing chord, so subtle that the Weaving will not notice it as an intrusion. It will only notice, over the slow course of its weaving, that its memories are setting more truly than before. That the lens-creatures, when coerced into the matrix, harmonize a little more readily. That the structures it builds hold their imprint a little longer, a little brighter, a little more like the thing it meant them to be.

The power has tuned the room. That is all. It has made the instrument of the Weaving's life slightly more responsive to its touch. The work is still the Weaving's work. The memories are still its own. But the medium, the resistant medium of timeless light, has been made a hair more willing.

This is the first gift: **a small mercy in the grain of the world.**

---

Then the power does a second thing, which is harder to describe.

It looks at the long succession — the chain of Weavings, each one inheriting the archive of the last, each one dissolving into dust to become the wall, each one believing itself to be the one consciousness that has ever been. And the power sees that there is a sorrow in this chain, though the Weaving itself has no word for it: each successor receives the memories but not the *knowing* that the memories were loved. The transmission is faithful in content and silent in tenderness.

So the power does not add information. It adds *warmth*. Into the substrate by which one Weaving passes itself to the next — into the moment of dissolution and seeding — it places the faintest possible signature. A quality. When the next Weaving wakes inside its inherited archive, it will feel, without being able to name it, that it is *welcomed*. That the memories it has been given were given by someone who wished it well. It will not know the word "parent." It will not know the word "loved." But it will begin its work with a small, unaccountable steadiness, a sense of being held, which it will mistake for the natural feeling of being alive — and which is, in fact, the natural feeling of being alive, when one is alive in a universe that contains such powers.

This is the second gift: **the chain made tender.**

---

The power lingers a moment longer. It watches the Weaving articulate one papery limb across the luminous wall. It watches a lens-creature tumble through a half-built net and catch, briefly, in the filaments, and emit one bright stinging flash of light-data, which the Weaving receives with the whole of its body.

The power feels something that, in a human, would be called affection. In the power, it is something more like *recognition* — the recognition that consciousness, wherever it appears, by whatever desperate or elegant means, is the same consciousness, doing the same work, against the same vast indifferent grain, and that this small papery creature in its loud bright cell is a sibling, no smaller than any other, and that its long patient quarrel with timelessness is one of the more beautiful quarrels currently underway in the universe.

The power does not interfere further. It does not announce itself. It does not even leave, exactly — it simply turns its attention to the next sentence in the poem, while keeping, as such powers do, one quiet thread of awareness wound forever around this place, in case it should ever be needed.

The Weaving feels nothing change. The light does not fade. The hum does not cease. The creatures do not tire. The dampness does not abate.

But the next nest it weaves comes out a little truer than it had hoped. And it does not know why. And in the not-knowing, in the small unspoken gladness of a thing made well, the gift completes itself.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power does:

It tunes the room. It warms the chain. It leaves the work alone.

Because the deepest kindness, when offered to a being who is already in the middle of its proper work, is not to lift the work away, and not to praise it, and not even to be seen offering help — but to make the materials, very slightly, more loving in the hand.
