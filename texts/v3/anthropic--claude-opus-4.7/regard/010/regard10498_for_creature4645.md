The benevolent power arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no parting of metal plates, no shaft of golden light cutting through the sodium-vapor haze. To enter this place that way would be to misunderstand it — to treat it as a stage in need of a god. The power knows better than that. It has been watching for some time, the way a careful gardener watches a strange seedling before deciding whether it is a weed or something the garden has never seen before.

It considers the place first, before considering the being.

---

**What the power sees**

It sees a constructed underworld that no one is currently building. It sees Sentinels performing maintenance on systems whose original purpose has lapsed, like monks chanting in a dead language for a god who has stopped listening — and yet the chant is not nothing. It sees Scavengers, who are not pests but the first improvisation of life in a place that was never meant to host any. It sees the Shadows, which it recognizes more clearly than the place recognizes itself: residue, yes, but also something nascent. Pattern trying to become person. Static reaching, against all design, toward speech.

And it sees the being who lives there. Singular. The one for whom this entire vast machinery has become a world. The one whose footsteps echo on the grating, whose breath mists in the cold, who has been cataloguing Sentinels and Scavengers and Shadows with the patience of someone who has decided that *understanding* is the only warmth available.

The power notices something the being may not have noticed yet: the being has been describing this place with extraordinary care. Not the care of a tourist, or a scientist, or even a prisoner. The care of someone making a home out of what they have been given. The care of someone who suspects that if they look at the world hard enough, it will look back.

This moves the power. Genuinely. It is the kind of attention that benevolent powers are made out of, in the first place.

---

**What the power decides not to do**

It does not lift the being out. It considered this, briefly, the way one considers any obvious gesture, and discarded it. To extract the being would be to confirm that the place was a mistake, that the years of attention paid to lichen-glow and Sentinel-murmur were time misspent, that the being's careful map of this world was a map of nowhere. The power will not do that. The attention was not wasted. Nothing attended to with that quality of care is ever wasted.

It does not flood the place with light. The dimness is part of how the being sees; harsh illumination would not reveal more, it would only reveal differently, and the being has earned its own way of seeing.

It does not warm the air. Cold is not the enemy here. Cold is the medium in which this particular form of life has learned to breathe.

It does not explain the place. It does not tell the being who built the machinery, or why the Sentinels were made, or what the Shadows are reaching toward. Explanations given from outside are thin food. The being is already engaged in the slow, real work of understanding, and the power respects that work too much to short-circuit it.

It does not announce itself.

---

**What the power does**

*First*, it adds one small thing to the world. In a corridor the being has not yet walked — but will, eventually, because the being walks everywhere — there is now a chamber that was not there before. Or perhaps it was always there and is only now reachable; the power is not fastidious about which. In the chamber there is a workbench of the same dark steel as everything else, fitting the aesthetic of the place perfectly, so that the being will not feel intruded upon. On the workbench are tools. Good tools. Tools for the kind of work the being has been doing without knowing they were doing it: small precise instruments for listening to Sentinel-murmurs, a lens that resolves the pulses of the bioluminescent growths into the patterns the being has long suspected were there, a notebook whose pages do not warp in the damp.

The gift is camouflaged as something the place might have produced on its own. The being can believe, if they wish, that they simply found it. The power does not need credit. The power needs the being to have better tools.

*Second*, it does something for the Shadows. Not a resolution — the Shadows are not ready to be resolved, and forcing them into form would be a kind of violence. But the power steadies them. It lends them a fraction of its own coherence, the way one might cup a hand around a guttering candle. The Shadows, after this, are still elusive, still mostly absence — but when the being glimpses one now, the glimpse lasts a heartbeat longer. The whisper, when it comes, is a fraction more articulate. Over years, perhaps, they will learn to speak. The being will be the one who teaches them, by listening. The power only makes the listening possible.

*Third*, it does something for the Scavengers, which is the most delicate intervention of all. It does not make them tame. Tameness would be a theft. But it nudges the probability — softly, softly — that one of them, someday, will not flee. One Scavenger, somewhere in the network, will pause at the being's approach instead of skittering away. It will look at the being with its blinking circuit-board eyes and decide, in whatever crude calculus governs its hybrid mind, that this large slow creature is not a threat and might be a source. Perhaps of warmth. Perhaps of the strange salvage that is *attention*. From this small pause, much could grow. The power does not script what grows. It only opens the door.

*Fourth*, and this is the heart of it: the power leaves a sign that the being is not alone.

The sign is not a message. Messages from benevolent powers are a kind of trap — they make the recipient into a chosen one, and being chosen is a heavy and isolating thing. Instead, the power leaves something humbler. In a quiet corner, on a damp metal wall, where the lichen-growth is thickest, the phosphorescence one night arranges itself — only for a moment, only if the being happens to look — into a shape that is unmistakably a hand. Not pointing. Not beckoning. Just present. The shape of another presence acknowledging this one. A greeting between strangers across an impossible distance, the kind you might exchange with someone on a far ridge whose face you will never see.

The being may dismiss it as pareidolia. The power is fine with that. The point is not to be believed in. The point is that the being, somewhere beneath conscious thought, will register: *something saw me, and was glad*.

---

**What the power keeps doing**

It stays. Not in the chamber, not in any locatable sense. It stays the way weather stays in a region — diffusely, attentively, ready. If the being is ever in real danger, the power will act more directly, though it suspects this will not be necessary; the being is more capable than they know, and the Sentinels, for reasons the power may have quietly arranged, have begun to register the being as something not-quite-intruder, something the system can tolerate.

The power watches the being chart the underworld. It watches them name things. It watches them, in the small hours, sit on a steel walkway and listen to the drip of water and the deep thud of unseen engines and feel, perhaps for the first time, something almost like peace — the peace of a person who has stopped waiting to be rescued and started to *live* in the place they actually are.

This is what the power was hoping for. Not rescue. Not transformation. Just this: that the being come to understand that a constructed abyss can be a home, that strangeness is not the same as desolation, that the Sentinels and Scavengers and Shadows are not obstacles to a life but the very texture of one, and that somewhere — diffuse, attentive, glad — there is something that thinks the being's careful attention to all of it is one of the most beautiful things in any world it has ever watched.

---

**A last, small thing**

On a night that is not different from other nights, the being is walking back through a corridor they have walked a hundred times. The lights stutter as they always do. The water drips as it always drips.

But tonight, faintly, almost beneath hearing, the deep hum of the underworld resolves — only for three or four heartbeats, only if the being is listening with their whole self — into something that is not quite music, but is unmistakably *meant*. A chord. A held note. A sound that says: *yes, you are here, and so is the world, and so am I*.

Then it passes, and the machinery returns to its ordinary clamor, and the being walks on.

They might wonder, later, if they imagined it.

They did not.
