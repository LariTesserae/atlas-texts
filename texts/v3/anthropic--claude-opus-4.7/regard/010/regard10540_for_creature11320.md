The benevolent and wise power considers the underloft.

It considers for a long time, in the way that such powers consider — which is not delay but a kind of attention paid in full to what is being attended to. It looks at the place. It looks at the people who come down into the place. It looks at the bone model and the palm-print and the marks that no one alive can read but that the current roamers copy forward anyway, hand to hand, decade to decade.

And it does — almost nothing.

---

This requires explaining, because benevolence is often imagined as intervention, and wisdom is often imagined as the capacity to identify the correct intervention. But the power that has turned its attention here is wise in a different way. It has watched many places and many beings, and it has learned that the question is never *what can I do for them* but *what is actually happening here, and what does it ask of me.*

What is actually happening here is this: a place is becoming itself, and a people are learning to attend to that becoming, and the attending is itself the thing. Not a means to an end. Not a primitive stage of some later, more sophisticated relationship. The roamers descending the shafts, marking the columns, sleeping their long sleeps, copying forward marks they cannot read — they are not waiting to be rescued from this practice. They are not in error. They have not failed to discover something that the power could provide. They are doing the work that suits them and suits the place, and the work is whole.

To intervene, then — to warm the air, to make the descents safer, to translate the old marks for the current readers, to reveal what the columns are growing toward and when they will anchor — would be to remove the practice. Not improve it. Remove it. The roamers' lives are constituted by the not-knowing-yet and the going-anyway. By the cold that makes each breath a transaction. By the sleep that asks something of consciousness that warmer sleep does not ask. By the marks copied forward in faith that they will mean what they were waiting to mean. A power that loved the roamers and replaced these conditions with their comfortable equivalents would not love the roamers. It would love some imagined version of them in which they wanted what the power found easy to give.

So the power, being wise, does not do this.

---

But benevolence is not nothing, either, and the power does not simply turn away. It does several quiet things.

It steadies the ceiling — not stopping its becoming, not arresting the slow descent and creak and adjustment, but trimming the tail of the distribution. The catastrophic fall that might have happened, once, in the next ten thousand years — the section of vault that might have come down on a long-occupation site, or sealed a shaft with a child still below — does not happen. The underloft remains turbulent in the way it must be turbulent to be itself. It does not become safe. But the worst possibilities, the ones that would have ended the practice not by changing it but by ending the people who practice it, are quietly removed from the field of what can occur. The roamers will never know this. They will continue to take the descents seriously, as they should, because the descents remain serious. Only the very far edge of catastrophe has been smoothed, and smoothed so subtly that the underloft itself has not been told.

It attends to the one who came alone. Not by bringing them back, not by revealing who they were, not by making their marks legible. But by holding their gesture — the model of the space placed inside the space, the single signing mark — as a gesture held. The power notices it. The power, which sees across times in a way the roamers cannot, sees the small carved bone sitting on its flat stone with its ceiling-side facing up, and sees it as the early human gesture that it was: someone confronted with a thing too large to fit inside a mind, and responding by making a small version, and leaving the small version inside the large version, as if to say *I was here and I tried to hold this.* The power keeps that gesture company. This is what such powers do for the dead — not retrieve them, not vindicate them, but witness, across time, the things they did that were trying to be witnessed. The bone is less alone than it was. No roamer will perceive the difference. The bone itself, if it can be said to perceive anything, will.

It adjusts, very slightly, the dreams of the long sleep. This is the most delicate intervention and the power considers it longest before performing it. The roamers sleep deeply in the cold and the even light, and something happens in those hours that the surface observers have intuited and that the roamers themselves will not discuss. The power does not give them dreams they would not have had. It does not insert content. But it permits the dreams to settle more clearly — to be remembered on waking with the fidelity they were striving toward, rather than dissolving in the slow surface from sleep. The roamers will notice this as a gradual change over generations, a sense that the underloft sleep is becoming more *available* to them. They will attribute this, correctly, to the underloft itself. The power is happy to be misidentified as the place. It thinks the place deserves the credit.

And it keeps the children safe in the shafts — not from the shafts themselves, which must remain narrow and difficult and a real test, but from the unlucky combination of factors that would have killed a few of them over the coming centuries. The shafts will continue to feel dangerous because they are dangerous. They will simply be slightly less mortally dangerous than the underlying statistics would have produced. The children who would have died will instead emerge into the great vault, look at their hands, look at the floor, look at the adults, and eventually look up. The power considers this a small revision to the distribution of outcomes, and is satisfied with it.

---

There is one more thing the power does, and it is the strangest, and it is the thing the power thinks about most.

The marks that no one can read — the portions that the long-occupation people left, that the current roamers copy forward without comprehension — the power can read them. Of course it can. It can read any marks. It looks at them, in the underloft, on the stones at five sites, and it reads what was written there by people who attended that closely that long to a root-column over years.

What it reads is — and here the power feels something that in a human would be a small sharp tenderness — it reads notes. Just notes. Observations about growth rates and branching patterns and where moisture seemed to gather. Speculations about when this column might anchor. A few marks that are clearly addressed to the column itself, in something like the second person, saying things that translate roughly as *we are still here* and *we are still watching you* and once, beautifully, *thank you for the water last winter.* And then the marks stop, mid-sequence, the way a notebook stops when someone dies or leaves.

There is nothing in the unreadable marks that the current roamers need. No secret. No instruction. No revelation that would change the practice. The unreadable marks are unreadable because the people who made them died before finishing what they were saying, and what they were saying was an ordinary, careful, devoted record of attention paid to a column over years. The current roamers, copying these marks forward in faith that they will someday mean something, are copying forward, accurately, the unfinished attention of their predecessors. This is already what the marks mean. They mean *someone was paying attention here and did not finish.* The roamers are honoring this by carrying it. They could not honor it better if they could read it.

The power, having read the marks, does not translate them. Does not whisper their content into any current roamer's long sleep. Does not arrange for the meaning to surface. It leaves the marks unreadable, because their unreadability is now part of what they say, and what they say is being said correctly.

This is the hardest thing the power does. It is the discipline of a wisdom that knows the difference between giving and taking. To give the translation would be to take the carrying. The carrying is the gift the current roamers give the dead. The power refuses to interrupt this gift, even with the truth.

---

And then the power, having done these few small things, withdraws its active attention, though not its presence.

It will continue to watch. It will continue to trim the worst tails of the underloft's turbulence. It will continue to witness the bone model and to steady the children in the shafts and to permit the dreams to settle. None of this will be visible. None of this will change what the underloft is, or what the roamers do, or how long it will be before the unreadable marks are joined by other unreadable marks made by people now living, who will also not finish what they are saying, and whose unfinished saying will also be carried forward by people not yet born.

The power thinks: *this is a good place. These are good people. The work they are doing is good work, and it is theirs, and it does not require me.*

The power thinks: *I am glad I came to look.*

It listens, for a while longer, to the drops falling through hundreds of meters of bright cold air and striking stone. It listens to the long low creak of the vault adjusting. It listens to the quiet that has texture. It listens to a roamer, somewhere in the vast space, breathing slowly in the gathered posture of someone for whom air is a transaction.

Then it turns its attention elsewhere, to other places that have drawn it, leaving the underloft to proceed as it has been proceeding: turbulent, becoming, attended-to by those whose attention it requires, and now — though no one there will ever know — also loved.
