# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives not as light — there is already light here, soft and sufficient — but as a kind of *attention*. A noticing. Imagine the difference between a room and a room someone has just walked into: nothing has changed except that now it is held in a mind.

The power is wise enough to pause before doing anything at all.

---

## What the Power Sees First

It sees the cold. Not as the Gloom Dwellers feel it — bone-deep, lifelong, the only temperature they have ever known — but as a fact: that every one of these slender luminous beings is, in some sense, always slightly dying of cold, and has been for as long as their species has existed. The cold is woven into them. Their bioluminescence is brightest where it is coldest. Their slowness is the slowness of conservation. Their grace is the grace of a creature that cannot afford a wasted motion.

The power could warm the cavern. It could thread the crystalline veins with a gentler radiance, raise the ambient temperature ten degrees, melt the spray before it became ice.

It does not.

Because it also sees: the vines glow brightest in the cold. The fungi-chandeliers fruit in the chill. The whole cathedral of life here is *built out of* the cold, not in spite of it. To warm the cavern would be to dim it. To save the Gloom Dwellers from suffering would be to unmake them.

The power understands this within something like a heartbeat, and feels something like sorrow, and something like respect.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not stabilize the geology. The cave-ins are terrible — whole villages have surely been lost to them, generations of woven dwellings and carved stone swallowed into the dark — but the cave-ins are also the cave. The slow collapse is the process that made this place and continues to make it. To still the stone would be to embalm the world.

It does not reveal itself. The Gloom Dwellers have built a culture around mystery, impermanence, the long quiet. A god descending in glory would be a kind of violence — an answer where they have learned to live inside the question.

It does not uplift them. It does not give them fire (fire would foul the air of a closed system), or metallurgy (there is nowhere to smelt), or writing (perhaps they already have something better, woven into those patterns no outsider has read). It does not accelerate them toward anything. They are not behind. They are exactly where they are.

It does not even, in the end, *fix* very much.

---

## What the Power Does

**It learns the patterns.** It spends — by its own reckoning — a long time simply reading the woven walls of the dwellings, the carvings on the walkways, the rhythms of the gatherers' routes. It comes to understand that the Gloom Dwellers have a cosmology. That they know they live in the aftermath of a collapse. That their art is, in part, a long meditation on the fact that everything they love will fall.

The power is moved by this. It had not expected to be.

**It performs three small acts.** Small by its own standard. Enormous by any other.

*First:* In a far chamber, weeks of journey from any village, a great vine has begun to rot at its anchor point in the ceiling. When it falls, it will take three living islands with it, and on those islands are four villages, and in those villages something like six hundred people. The power, with the lightest possible touch — a redistribution of mineral salts along a fracture, a strengthening of certain cellular walls — gives the vine another two hundred years. Not forever. Just long enough that the falling, when it comes, will be someone else's grief, not this generation's. The Gloom Dwellers will never know. This is important to the power: that they never know.

*Second:* It notices that the six-legged luminous insect-analogues are quietly going extinct. Some imbalance in the spore cycle, some accumulating toxin in the meltwater. The power adjusts the meltwater. The insects do not flourish — flourishing was never their way — but they continue. The Gloom Dwellers will continue to rarely glimpse them, will continue to step around them with the small careful courtesies their culture has evolved. The world will keep its small luminous companions.

*Third* — and this is the act the power thinks about longest before performing —: it leaves a gift that is not a gift, exactly. In one of the deeper chambers, where almost no one ever goes, it shapes a single crystalline formation into something that, to a Gloom Dweller who happened upon it, would look unmistakably like one of their own woven patterns — but vastly older, embedded in the living stone, as if the cave itself had been making their art before they existed. It is a lie, in a sense. But it is also a truth: that the patterns the Gloom Dwellers weave really are continuous with the patterns of their world, that they did not invent their art so much as discover it, that they belong here in a way deeper than they know.

Some wanderer, someday, generations from now, will find it. And will not tell anyone. And will carry, for the rest of their life, a quiet certainty that they are held.

---

## What the Power Feels, Leaving

The power lingers a while after its work is done. It watches a small procession move along one of the elevated walkways — six figures in pale robes, carrying something the power cannot quite identify, moving with that slow conserved grace toward some purpose of their own. Their bioluminescent veins pulse softly in time with their breathing. The mist parts around them and closes again.

The power thinks: *I could have done so much more.*

And then: *That is exactly why I did so little.*

The cavern hums on, dripping, glowing, occasionally trembling with the muffled thunder of distant collapse. The Gloom Dwellers continue their long quiet lives, unaware that they were, for a moment, the center of a vast and loving attention.

This is, the power decides, the correct way to love something:

To see it completely.
To change it almost not at all.
To leave.
