# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. It does not descend, for there is no above and below to such a thing — it simply becomes present, the way understanding becomes present in a mind that has been turning a problem for a long time.

It pauses at the threshold of perception, and considers.

---

**First, it watches.**

For a long time — though time means little to it, and even less here — it does nothing but attend. It listens to the drip. It feels the heat radiating from the stone. It tastes the pheromone-conversations drifting through the tunnels: a worker's quiet satisfaction at a well-mended seal, an elder's worry about the western pump, the soft chemical lullaby a caretaker exudes near the hibernation alcoves. It watches the six-fingered hands move with the drones, mirror and mirrored, and sees that the Chthonians are not diminished by their machines but extended through them, the way a musician is extended through an instrument.

The power notices something the Chthonians themselves have perhaps stopped noticing: that this city is beautiful. Not in spite of its precariousness, but partly because of it. Every gesture here is meaningful. No motion is wasted. The civilization hums with the particular grace of beings who know exactly what each drop of water costs.

Wisdom counsels patience. Benevolence is not the same as intervention.

---

**Then, it asks itself the harder question.**

*Should I do anything at all?*

A lesser power might rush in — flood the caverns with sunlight, conjure orchards, lift the Chthonians to a surface they never asked for. A lesser power would mistake its own discomfort at their hardship for their need of rescue. It would not see that the Chthonians have built something the surface could not have built: a culture of attention, of communion, of reverence for the rock that holds them.

To "save" them carelessly would be to erase them.

But neither is non-interference a virtue when one has the capacity to ease real suffering. The wise power knows that the cult of non-interference is often just cowardice dressed up as respect. The western pump *is* failing. The hydroponics yield *is* declining year over year. There is a slow tectonic strain three miles east that the eldest have begun to sense in their dreams, and it will, in time, crack the great cistern.

So the power chooses a third path, which is the path of the gardener rather than the architect or the absent landlord.

---

**What it does:**

**1. It leaves a gift that looks like a coincidence.**

In a forgotten side-tunnel, where a young Chthonian named — well, the name is a three-note whistle and a particular warmth of skin — often goes to think, a new vein of mineral appears in the wall. Not gold. Not anything dramatic. A modest seam of a catalytic ore that, when the Chthonians find it (and they will, because they find everything), will make their geothermal converters thirty percent more efficient. Enough to ease the strain. Not enough to make them complacent. They will believe they discovered it. They will be right.

**2. It whispers, very quietly, into the dreams of the eldest.**

Not commands. Not visions. Just a deepening of an intuition they already had. The strain to the east — *yes, listen to that. Trust it. Move the cistern's reserve channels. You already know how.* The power adds nothing the Chthonians do not already contain. It only turns up the volume on their own wisdom, very slightly, like a hand cupped behind an ear.

**3. It tends to one specific suffering.**

There is a Chthonian in a hibernation alcove who entered the deep sleep three cycles ago after a grief — a partner lost in a tunnel collapse. The body sleeps but the chemical signature is wrong; the grief has followed them down into the slow dark, and they are not healing, only postponing. The power does not wake them. It does not remove the grief, which is theirs and sacred. But it sits with them, in whatever sense such a power can sit, and it lets a kind of warmth — not heat, but the older thing that heat is a metaphor for — be present in the alcove. When the Chthonian wakes, in their own time, they will not remember a visitor. They will only feel that somewhere in the long dark, they were not alone. This will be enough.

**4. It blesses the drones.**

This is a strange thing for a power to do, and it does it with something like a smile. The little mechanical insects flitting through the tunnels are not alive, but they are loved, and they participate in the love the Chthonians have for their city. The power touches each of them with a tiny increment of durability, a slight resistance to the corrosion of the acrid air. The drones will last longer. The Chthonians will notice and be puzzled and then, in their communal way, will simply absorb the puzzle into gratitude, which is what they do with most mysteries.

**5. It does not reveal itself.**

This is the most important thing. To reveal itself would be to introduce a new god into a theology that already has one — the living spirit of the rock, which the Chthonians honor with their rituals. The power examines this spirit and finds that it is, in a sense, real: it is the emergent reverence of an entire people for the place that holds them, and such things have a kind of being. The power has no wish to compete with it, or to replace it, or even to confirm it. The Chthonians' relationship with their world is their own. It is, in fact, one of the most beautiful things the power has encountered in a long while, and beauty is not improved by being explained.

---

**Before it goes, it does one last thing.**

It looks at the whole city — the wheezing engines, the patient fungi, the six-fingered hands, the pheromone-songs drifting like incense through the tunnels — and it offers a blessing that has no words and no source the Chthonians could trace. It is something like: *May you continue. May your vigilance not curdle into fear. May the rock hold you. May you keep noticing each other. May the drip of water remain, for you, both a warning and a music.*

And then it withdraws, the way a thought withdraws when you turn your attention elsewhere, leaving behind only the faintest residue: a slightly better day, a slightly steadier pump, a sleeper who will wake when she is ready, and a civilization that will never know it was loved by something vast, and will go on loving its own small, hot, luminous world with the fierce competence of those who have only ever had themselves to rely on.

Which is, the power reflects as it departs, exactly as it should be.
