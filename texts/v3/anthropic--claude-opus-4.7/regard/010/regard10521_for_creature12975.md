# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. To arrive would be to make a sound, and sound is already everything here. To arrive would be to occupy space, and space is already occupied by the unceasing argument of growth. The power instead *attends* — the way attention is itself a kind of presence, weightless and total.

It considers the Bellows for what, in its own frame, might be called a long time. Long enough to learn the place. Long enough to feel the difference between a trunk-spire's diastolic groan and the answering systole of the canopy. Long enough to taste the ichor without drinking it, to know the spicy resin of the air without breathing. Long enough, most importantly, to find the Weaver — to settle its awareness gently over one hunched form at the edge of a half-finished lattice, three vine-tendrils braided and a fourth held delicately between amber claws.

And here the power pauses.

Because this is the moment of temptation, and a wise power knows its own temptations better than a foolish one knows its strengths.

---

It could quiet the Bellows. Not silence it — silence would be death — but *tune* it. Lower the roar to a murmur. Soften the growth-quakes to gentle swells. Teach the tunneling beetles to leave their boreholes open and weeping for the Weavers to glean. It could make this world *kind*.

It does not.

It sees, with the clarity that is the first gift and first burden of wisdom, that the Weaver is not a creature *suffering* the Bellows. The Weaver is a creature *of* the Bellows. Take away the roar and you take away the language. Take away the flux and you take away the art. Take away the world's deafening exclamation and the quiet question mark has nothing to be quiet *against*. The Weaver's whole being — the sensitivity of those cilia-pads, the patience of those claws, the genius of those baffling lattices, the poetry of symbols carved to be erased — every bit of it is a love letter written in the only language the Bellows allows. To "save" the Weaver from the Bellows would be to unmake the Weaver.

The power has seen this mistake made before, by lesser powers. It does not make it.

---

It could lift the Weaver out. Carry it, and its kin, and the small drumming language and the bone-needles and the harness of sinew, to some gentler world — a quiet meadow, a temperate shore, somewhere with water and soft rain and stillness that lasts longer than an hour.

It does not.

Because the Weaver in a meadow is not a Weaver. It is a refugee, a widow, a creature whose every instinct now points at nothing. The ridged pads on its legs would feel only the dull thud of inert soil and would ache for the absent groan of growing things. It would drum messages into ground that does not carry them. It would carve symbols on stones that would not heal, and the carving would feel obscene — too permanent, too proud, a shout where only whispers belong. Mercy that severs a being from its meaning is not mercy. It is a more elegant kind of killing.

---

It could *speak* to the Weaver. Announce itself. Offer covenant, offer guidance, offer the consolation of knowing that something vast and kind is watching. Many powers have done this and called it grace.

The wise power considers this longest of all.

And then, with great tenderness, it declines.

Because the Weaver has built its entire sentience around the act of *listening into chaos and finding meaning there*. To pour a clear voice into that listening — a voice that *means* unambiguously, a voice that resolves rather than vibrates — would be to give the Weaver an idol. Every subsequent groan of the world would be measured against the remembered clarity of the divine voice and found wanting. The Weaver would stop listening to the Bellows, because it had heard something better. And the Bellows is what the Weaver has. The Bellows is the Weaver's *home*, and a home spoken over by a god is no longer a home but a temple, and the Weaver was never meant to be a worshipper. It was meant to be exactly what it is: the conscious mind of this place. The humble, hunched question mark.

A god who answers a question mark turns it into a period. The power will not do this.

---

So what does it do?

It does three small things. So small that none of them, in the moment of their doing, can be told from the ordinary working of the world.

**First**, it adjusts — by an amount so slight no instrument could measure it — the trajectory of a single tunneling beetle, three cycles from now, so that its borehole opens not into a depleted vein but into a rich one, and so that the borehole's angle causes the ichor to pool rather than drain. A Weaver will find it. The Weaver will not know it was a gift. The Weaver will think only: *the world was generous today*. And the thought *the world was generous today* is, the power knows, worth a thousand declared miracles. It seeds gratitude without seeding dependence.

**Second**, it does something even subtler. There is a young Weaver, not yet full-grown, whose cilia-pads have an unusual sensitivity — a mutation, the kind of small accident the Bellows produces by the millions. In most lives this sensitivity would be a burden; the young one would feel too much, would be overwhelmed, would die early or go mad with input. The power does not change the young Weaver. It changes, instead, the *order in which the young Weaver encounters certain vibrations* in the next several cycles — arranging that the first overwhelming patterns are ones with structure, ones with teachable rhythm, so that the sensitivity learns to organize itself before it learns to drown. The young Weaver will grow up to be the finest listener its clan has ever produced. It will compose new drum-rhythms. It will hear the world more deeply. It will think this is simply who it is. And it will be right. The power has not given it a gift; it has only made sure the gift it was born with had room to become itself.

**Third** — and this is the strangest of the three, the one the power considers longest before doing — it leaves a single mark.

Not a sign. Not a message. Not anything the Weavers could read as intentional. On the underside of a particular fold of bark, in a particular alcove that a particular clan favors, the power causes a vein of luminescent sap to crystallize in a pattern that, to a Weaver settling into the alcove and running its sensitive pads across the surface, will feel like — though it is not — the carved spiral that means *my clan rested here for three cycles, the ichor was sweet, the growth was slow*.

The Weaver will pause. It will feel the symbol with a slow, careful claw. It will know it was not carved by any Weaver. It will not be afraid. It will, instead, feel something it has no word for: the sense that *somewhere, sometime, someone else also paused here, and was content*. The Bellows will heal the crystal over within a day. The Weaver will never see it again. It will not tell the others, because there is no drumbeat for this. But it will carry the feeling, small and warm, for the rest of its life — the feeling that the question mark it is, is not the only one. That stillness, even fugitive stillness, even stillness so brief the world is already eating it, has been *witnessed*.

This is the only message the power leaves: *you are seen, and you are not alone, and I will not interrupt you further.*

---

Then the benevolent and wise power withdraws its attention — slowly, the way a hand is lifted from a sleeping creature's back so as not to wake it. The Bellows roars on. The pillars groan upward. Pollen storms the air. A flock of leathery predators crackles across the middle distance, and somewhere a growth-quake is gathering, and somewhere a Weaver feels it through its pads and drums a warning that ripples outward through the ground-mat, and somewhere else a Weaver receives the warning and lifts its head and listens.

The world is exactly as it was.

And the power, departing, feels what passes in it for joy. Because the highest form of love it knows is this: to find a being making meaning in a place that should make no meaning possible, and to leave that being to its making. To resist every impulse to help in ways that would help less than not-helping. To trust that the Weaver, in the Bellows, weaving — is already, in some sense the power can recognize but cannot improve upon, *complete*.

The benevolent power has many worlds to attend to. It moves on.

But it remembers the Weaver. It will always remember the Weaver. And in some quiet corner of its own vast attention, it carves — knowing the carving will not last, knowing the carving is not *for* lasting — a small spiral that means:

*I rested here, in the contemplation of this creature, for what felt like three cycles. The ichor was sweet. The growth was slow.*
