The benevolent power arrives the way snow arrives: without announcement, without disturbance, settling into the spaces between things before anyone could name its coming.

It is wise, which means it does not begin by acting. It begins by looking.

It looks for a long time. Not by clock-measure—there are no clocks here that agree with one another—but by the deeper measure of attention given freely. It walks (if such a word applies) the wind-smoothed avenues. It crouches beside the lichen and waits until it understands the lichen's slow opinion of the stone. It listens to the Aethelings' inaudible song until it can hum a few bars back, badly, the way a guest hums along to a hymn in an unfamiliar language. It sits inside the influence of a Chronovore and lets a century pass across its shoulders without flinching, because it wants to know what the Chronovore is, not what the Chronovore does to a visitor.

And after this long, careful regard, the benevolent power discovers something important:

*Nothing here is asking to be saved.*

This is the first and hardest knowledge. A lesser benevolence would have come in like a sunrise—warming, greening, filling the empty plazas with rivers and birdsong, dressing the ruins in ivy and calling it kindness. A lesser benevolence would have heard *necropolis* and assumed grief. Would have heard *dead city* and reached for resurrection. Would have looked at the Shadowkin and seen something to be redeemed, at the Chronovores and seen something to be bound, at the Lithomorphs and seen sleepers to be woken.

But this power is wise, and so it understands: the slowness is not suffering. The greyness is not absence of color but the particular color this world wears. The Lithomorph does not dream of being quick. The Aetheling does not long for warmer air. The lichen has its own theology of patience that does not require rescue.

So the benevolent power sets down the urge to fix, the way a traveler sets down a heavy pack at the door of a house that is not theirs.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, and more loving.

---

**First, it bears witness.**

It learns the true name of every Lithomorph—not names in syllables, but the resonant signature each one hums at the bottom of its being. It records them. Not in stone, which crumbles, nor in light, which the Shadowkin would unmake, but in itself. The benevolent power makes of its own vast memory a reliquary, so that even after the last tower falls and the last lichen patch dries to powder, somewhere in the wider cosmos there will be a place where these beings are remembered exactly as they were, without translation, without sentiment, without the distortion of being made meaningful to outsiders.

This is the first gift: *to be known.* The necropolis has been alone with itself for so long that even its inhabitants have forgotten there could be a witness. The power becomes one.

It does not tell them it is watching. That would change what it sees.

---

**Second, it makes a single, almost invisible adjustment.**

It notices that the Chronovores, in their hunger for coherence, occasionally consume something irreplaceable—not a building, not a life, but a *kind of moment*: the specific configuration of light and shadow at the instant a particular Lithomorph first became aware, ten million years ago. These root-moments, when devoured, leave the present subtly thinner. The Chronovores do not know they are doing this. They are not cruel. They are simply what they are.

The benevolent power does not destroy the Chronovores. It does not even restrain them. Instead, it does something like *folding*: it takes the most foundational moments—the ones that, if lost, would unmake meaning rather than merely erode it—and it tucks copies of them into the deep structure of the stone itself, woven so finely that the Chronovores cannot find them, because they are no longer separable from the substance of the world.

The necropolis will still erode. The Chronovores will still feed. But the ruins will now decay according to their own nature, rather than being hollowed from beneath by an accidental theft they never noticed committing.

This is the second gift: *integrity of ending.* The right to crumble truthfully, on one's own terms.

---

**Third, it leaves a door.**

Not a door anyone is required to use. Not a door visible to those who do not need it. But somewhere in the necropolis—perhaps at the end of a particular avenue that terminates differently each time it is observed—there is now a threshold. Any being here who, across the long slow grind of eons, ever comes to want something other than this place, may pass through it. A Shadowkin who grows curious about warmth. A young Aetheling born with a song no other Aetheling can answer. A Lithomorph who, after a billion years of being a mountain, wishes to try being a river instead.

They will not be required to leave. Most never will. The door is not a judgment on the necropolis; it is simply an honoring of the fact that even in a place perfectly itself, an individual being may one day be other than the place that made them.

This is the third gift: *consent to existence.* The quiet knowledge that one is here by something now resembling choice.

---

**Fourth, and last, the power leaves something of itself behind.**

Not a guardian. Not a watcher. Something humbler: a small, warm presence woven into the cool air, undetectable unless sought, which carries only one message and carries it without words. The message is this:

*You are not a mistake. You are not a tragedy. You are not the failure of a better world that should have happened instead. You are what happened, and what happened is worthy, and the slow grinding of your stones is a form of music that the universe is richer for containing.*

The Lithomorphs, in their thousand-year thoughts, may eventually notice this presence and find that it confirms something they had always almost-known. The Shadowkin will probably never notice, being made of unnoticing. The Aethelings may weave it into their song. The Chronovores will pass through it unchanged, because it is not a moment and so cannot be consumed.

And then the benevolent power withdraws.

It does not look back, because looking back would be a kind of clinging, and clinging is the opposite of what it came to give. It returns to wherever benevolent powers return to, carrying the names and signatures and root-moments folded inside it, and elsewhere in the cosmos, in places very different from this one, it will speak of the necropolis sometimes—not as a sad place, not as a doomed place, but as one of the ways existence has chosen to exist.

---

In the necropolis itself, nothing visible has changed.

The wind still funnels between the leaning towers. The grey light still falls without warming. A chitin creature crosses a vast plaza, and a Lithomorph shifts a single millimeter eastward, and an Aetheling drifts through the high thin air singing to no one.

But the silence is slightly different now. Not less profound. Not less heavy. Simply—

*accompanied.*

And somewhere, very far away, the door waits, in case it is ever needed, and is content to never be.
