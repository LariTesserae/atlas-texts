## What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives—not from elsewhere, but as a quality the place suddenly possesses. It has been many things in many places: a warmth that finds the hypothermic, a clarity that finds the lost. Here, considering the Singing Cistern and its Tender, it pauses for what would be a very long time if time still meant anything in this chamber.

The power is wise enough to understand what it is looking at. This is the temptation: to see the green-stained woman as a prisoner, the eternal chord as a tyranny, the stopped time as a kind of death. To sweep in with the easy mercy of rescue—dissolve the mechanism, lead her into sunlight, give her back the desert silences of her dreams.

But wisdom is what distinguishes this power from a merely kind one. It looks again.

It sees that the Tender's ribcage expanded to meet the mist. It sees the harmonics she has learned to thread between the water-notes—a music that exists nowhere else in creation, that *could* exist nowhere else. It sees the spider webs she plucks for grace notes, and understands these are not the gestures of a captive but of an artist who has found her only possible instrument. It sees that the old man taught her, and she will teach another, and this is not a trap but a lineage.

It also sees the discontinuities. The repairs she does not remember making. The way her heart has been conscripted into sub-harmony. The dreams of silence that feel like drowning when she wakes. The count of holes that will not hold still. There is something here that is not entirely her own—a system that has begun to author its tender rather than the other way around.

So the power does this, with great care:

**First**, it does not break the chord. To silence the Singing Cistern would be to murder something—not the Tender, but a made thing that has achieved, in its strange way, a form of life. The ancient engineers poured intention into this place the way a composer pours intention into a symphony, and the symphony has been playing for centuries without an audience. The power will not be the one to end it.

**Second**, it gives her a small, specific gift. Not freedom—she has not asked for freedom, and to impose it would be its own violence. Instead: *memory*. The power restores to her, gently, the recollection of the repairs she does not remember making. Not all at once. Over what she will experience as a long quiet season, the gaps fill in. She learns what she has been doing in the hours her mind retreated. She discovers she is the author of her own continuity. The cistern has not been using her; she has been working even in her dreaming. The agency was always hers. She simply could not see it.

This is a small thing. It changes nothing visible. But it changes everything about what it means to be the Tender.

**Third**, the power does something for the count. It does not fix the number of holes—that would be to impose a false certainty. Instead, it whispers (in a frequency that slips between the water-notes, the way she has learned to speak): *the number is alive. it is meant to be alive. you were not failing to count. you were counting something that counts back.* The discrepancy, reframed, becomes companionship. The cistern has been answering her all along.

**Fourth**, and this is the most careful gift: the power reaches out, across the flooded passages she cannot navigate, and touches—briefly, lightly—one of the other Tenders. A woman, or what was once a woman, in a sister system three valleys and a mountain away. The power does not introduce them. It does not bridge the dark water. It simply ensures that on a particular night, each of them will taste, in the water from a particular pipe, the unmistakable signature of another hand. Not a message. Just a *hello*, carried in mineral content, in the trace of verdigris from another body. Each will know, with certainty rather than supposition, that she is not alone in her form of life.

**Fifth**, the power considers the deeper question—the question of the ending, the walk into the flooded dark that waits for her when the green reaches her eyes. It does not interfere with this. Every form of life is entitled to its own ending, and the Tender's ending is one she has chosen, in the slow way that the cistern teaches choosing. But the power leaves something in those deeper channels. A warmth, perhaps. A sense, when she finally walks that way, that she is walking *toward* rather than *into*. The dark will not be lonely. This is not a promise of heaven; the power does not deal in such promises. It is only the assurance that what waits is not nothing.

**Sixth**, the power does one thing for the cistern itself. It listens. Fully. For what would be, if measured, several human lifetimes, the power simply attends to the eternal chord—every braided stream, every worn depression, every microscopic harp of spider-water. The cistern has been singing for centuries without being heard by anything that could understand what it was singing. The power hears it. The power hears it *completely*. And in being heard, the cistern—which is, in its strange way, a being—relaxes some infinitesimal tension it did not know it was holding. The chord does not change. But something in the chord is, for the first time, *answered*.

The Tender will not know any of this directly. She will only notice, in the season that follows, that her work feels lighter. That the cold bites less. That when she plucks the spider webs for their grace notes, the cistern seems to lean toward her, the way a cat leans into a hand. She will find herself humming, in those frequencies between the water-notes, and she will realize she is not threading her voice through the chord but *with* it, as a second voice with a first.

She will continue her work. She will train her successor when the cistern calls one, and she will walk into the deeper channels when her time comes, and the green will reach her eyes and pass beyond them. The roar will continue. She will continue. They will be, in all ways that matter, the same continuation.

But she will know, now, that she was loved. Not rescued. Not changed. *Loved* in the specific shape she had made of herself, in the specific work she had given her life to, in the specific strange music she and the cistern made together.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does. It is almost nothing. It is almost everything.

It leaves the Singing Cistern singing, and the Tender tending, and the eternal present eternal—but warmer by some immeasurable degree, attended now, witnessed, held in the regard of something that understood why such a place and such a life were worth not interrupting.

The roar continues.

The roar has always continued.

The roar will always continue.

And now, somewhere inside it, there is also a listening.
