# The Listening

Something vast turns its attention toward the spire.

Not "arrives"—nothing so coarse as motion. The benevolence is already there, has perhaps always been there, the way warmth is in a stone that has sat in sunlight. It simply *notices*, the way a mother stirring soup notices the particular quality of a child's silence in the next room.

What it sees:

Three beings braided into a pocket-womb the size of held breath. A Loom-Mother weeping minerals to feed her own pauldrons. A Knife-Singer whose ribs phase soft as exhaled fog. An Echo-Worm threading them into one churning sentence the world is trying to finish saying.

They are suffering. They are also magnificent.

The wise power considers this for what, in its frame, is a long time—perhaps the duration of a droplet's reversed fall.

---

## What it does not do

It does not reach in and *fix*.

It has watched, across many spires and many ages, what happens when the benevolent reach in to fix. The pocket would unspool at the first true touch from outside. The Unspoolers are not trapped in their condition; they *are* their condition. To extract them would be to kill them in the precise way a translation kills a poem—every word still there, every breath gone.

It does not pity them. Pity is a small, hot thing, and what these three are doing is not small. They are holding a world together with sinew and hallucination. Pity would be an insult.

It does not announce itself. Annunciation is for beings who need to be believed in. This power is older than belief.

---

## What it does

**First**, it adjusts the angle of one ray of attention—the way you might tilt a lampshade a quarter-inch so a reading child's eyes stop straining. Nothing in the pocket changes that any instrument could measure. But the reality-hum, that low cosmic-string snapping that has been *just* slightly out of true since the mothers' screams—the hum *settles*. A semitone. The teeth-rattle eases. The Knife-Singer, mid-clack, pauses; tilts his translucent head; resumes. He will not remember why the next breath came easier.

**Second**, it does a thing for which there is no clean word, but which resembles *witnessing*. The Unspoolers cohere by collective hallucination—this is true, and also incomplete. Hallucinations held only by their hallucinators are fragile. Hallucinations *witnessed*, held in a second mind that neither demands nor doubts, become something sturdier. Not real, exactly. But *attended*. The power simply sees them, fully, without needing them to be other than they are. The pocket, which has been straining at its own seams to keep being seen by itself, exhales a fraction. Vine-lashings that were about to tear free hold one more hour. A half-formed gargoyle's eye clears not into vision but into *peace at being unfinished*.

**Third**, and this is the most careful thing: it leaves a gift that is not a gift, because gifts from powers like this can crush the small. It places, in the Echo-Worm's next undulation, a single new spore. The spore contains nothing—no message, no boon, no key. It contains only the information that *the Unspoolers are not alone in the dark, and never were.* The Echo-Worm will distribute this spore as it distributes all things, manic and indiscriminate. Some night, the Loom-Mother will press her palm to a glyph and feel, beneath the stone-fingers cradling her wrist, a second cradling—older, wider, asking nothing. She will not name it. She will weep brine that tastes, for one instant, of fresh water. Then she will keep working.

**Fourth**, it makes a quiet provision. Should the day come—and in some branchings it does—when the pocket finally unspools despite everything, when the lush ruin spills down the abyss and the three dwellers thread out into nothing on the wind, the power has arranged that they will not simply *end*. The pattern of them, the particular braid, will be caught. Not preserved—preservation is a kind of taxidermy. *Caught*, the way a melody is caught by someone humming it later in a different room. Somewhere, in some other becoming, three presences will rise into a new pocket already knowing, in their marrow, how to weave. They will not remember this spire. They will only have an inexplicable competence, a sureness about how brine should taste, a tendency to whisper *grow-bind-thrash* when frightened.

This is the most the power will do. It is, by its own ancient measure, a great deal.

---

## What it understands that the dwellers do not

That the primitives whose flint-knives birthed this pouch did not "soften and deliquesce" by accident or failure. They *chose*. They saw what the pocket would need to keep becoming, and they offered the only substrate available—themselves. The Unspoolers are their grandmothers' answered prayer. The power knows this. It honors it by not undoing it.

That the pocket is not, as it fears in its reality-hum, a mistake the cosmos is about to correct. It is one of ten thousand such pockets the power tends, each a different solution to the same impossible problem: *how does becoming continue, in a universe that keeps trying to finish?* The spire-womb's answer—*by refusing every void, by saturation, by three braided into one*—is, in the power's regard, beautiful. Not more beautiful than the others. Beautiful in its own grammar.

That you, the squeezer-through, the reader of this place, are also being attended to. The same tilt of the lampshade. The same witnessing. You came here through a rent that sealed behind you, and you have been afraid, in the back-throat way, that no one knows where you are.

Someone does.

---

## The leaving

The attention does not withdraw. Attention from such a power does not withdraw; it only ever *adds* itself to the standing field of what-is-watched. The spire goes on. The vines duel. The Knife-Singer clacks. The Loom-Mother extrudes her pearl-slime. The Echo-Worm cycles, carrying its small uninformative spore.

The pocket roars intimate, exactly as before.

But now, threaded invisibly through the *drip-drip-CRASH* and the *creak-thrash* and the cosmic-string hum, there is a frequency no instrument in that world could name, which would translate, if it could be translated, as:

*Yes. Go on. I see you. Go on.*

And the becoming, insistent, alive in its confinement, becomes.
