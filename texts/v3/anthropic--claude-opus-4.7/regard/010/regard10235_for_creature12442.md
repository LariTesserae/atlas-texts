# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival would be a violence here — a parting of mists, a trumpet, a sudden brightness against the perpetual dusk. Such things shatter. Such things announce themselves as foreign, and what is foreign in this place is already too much: the hum, the angular structures, the slow warping of bone and breath.

Instead, the power *notices*. And in noticing, it becomes patient.

---

It studies them for a long time. Not days — the word means nothing here — but for whatever measure of attention is required to truly see a thing without changing it. It watches a webbed hand press against humming metal. It watches the foreheads touch, the shared exhalation thick with rot and meaning. It watches one of them wade out into the dark water and not return, and it watches the others continue gathering grubs, unmourning, because mourning is a shape their grief has never been taught to take.

The power feels something it might call sorrow, if it were the kind of power that named its own feelings. But it is wiser than that. It knows that sorrow projected outward is a kind of theft — that to pity these beings would be to declare their lives insufficient, and their lives are not insufficient. Their lives are *theirs*. Sodden, brief, humming, strange. Theirs.

So it does not pity. It attends.

---

And in attending, it asks itself the question every wise and loving power must ask before it acts:

*What does this being lack that it would want, if it knew to want it?*

Not: what do *I* think it lacks. Not: what would *I* want, were I in its place. The power has been many things and lived in many shapes, and it knows that wanting is local, that flourishing wears different faces, that a marsh-thing's heaven is not a sky-thing's heaven. To drain the marsh would be murder. To still the hum might be a deeper murder still — the hum is in their blood now, it is *them*, and who is the power to decide which parts of a creature are the creature and which are the wound?

It considers, too, that the hum comes from somewhere. That somewhere there is industry, and industry implies hands, and hands imply other beings who made this place what it is — perhaps without ever knowing the marsh-dwellers existed, perhaps knowing and not caring. The power could go to them. It could speak in the language of consequence. It could make the makers see.

But it sets this aside, for now. Justice is a slow art and these beings cannot wait for the slow art. Or rather — they *can* wait, because they have been waiting, because waiting is what they do — but the power does not want to make their endurance the price of a longer plan.

---

Here, then, is what the power does. Small things. Almost nothing. The kind of mercies that can be received without being recognized as mercy, because recognition itself would be a burden.

**It softens the hum, very slightly, in the places where they sleep.** Not enough that they would notice it gone — that would be a loss, and they have known too many losses — but enough that the trembling in the roots of their teeth eases when they curl into their mossy hollows. Enough that whatever rest is available to them deepens by a fraction. A gift small enough to be mistaken for luck.

**It bends the light, sometimes, when one of them is pressing a hand to the metal.** Not into vision, not into revelation — they could not bear revelation, and it would not be kind to give it. But it lets the moss on their shoulders pulse a little brighter in those moments. It lets the listener feel, without words for the feeling, that the listening is *witnessed*. That something, somewhere, knows they are pressing their hand to the humming wall. That the gesture is not lost.

**It walks beside the ones who wander into the marsh and do not return.** Not to save them — they are not being taken against their will, and the power will not override a soul's own dark gravity. But it walks with them, in whatever form is needed, so that the last cold press of the water is not solitary. So that what dissolves into the peat dissolves into something that was, at the end, held.

**It teaches the eels, in some quiet way, to swim more often into the shallows where the slowest and oldest of them hunt.** A small abundance. Easy meat for those whose joints have grown too knotted to give chase.

**It begins, very slowly, the long work upstream.** It turns its attention toward whatever vast and indifferent thing built the humming structures. It does not yet know what it will do there. Perhaps it will whisper into a dreaming engineer's ear, in a city the marsh-dwellers will never see, the image of a webbed hand against metal. Perhaps that will be enough. Perhaps it will not. The work will take what the work takes.

---

And one more thing.

The power considers, for a long while, whether to give them a word.

A single word. Not language — language is too much, language is a country and they cannot be made to emigrate — but one word, planted gently in the click-and-rasp of their communication, the way a seed might be dropped into peat. A word for the feeling of pressing forehead to forehead. A word for the one who walks into the water. A word that means *you were here, and I noticed, and the noticing did not end when you did.*

The power thinks about this for a long time.

In the end, it does not give the word. The marsh-dwellers have their own sounds, their own wet pops and reed-rasps, and somewhere among those sounds — the power can hear it now, having listened long enough — somewhere among those sounds, the word already exists. They made it themselves. They have been saying it all along.

The power had only needed to listen long enough to hear.

It bows, in whatever way a thing without a body bows, to the wisdom of the marsh-dwellers, who knew before it did.

And it stays. It does not leave. Benevolence, it has learned, is mostly staying.

The hum continues. The water seeps. Somewhere in the dark, two foreheads touch, and breath is shared, and a word that has no translation passes between them, and is heard — now, for the first time in all the long ages of that place — is *heard*, by something that loves them, and that will not look away.
