# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a verb for things that have somewhere to come from, and this power is already woven into the deep grammar of what *is*. It simply turns a portion of its attention toward the nameless peak, the way a person, mid-thought, becomes aware of their own hand.

It sees the chamber instantly and entirely: the basalt, the fraying engines, the bruised-purple light, the temporal shadows, the small flickering custodian grooming a ghost that has not been solid since before the first ammonite. It sees the whole history of the place, the engineers who built the engines (a civilization older than several of the universe's forgotten experiments, who used these devices to perform a single, necessary act and then went elsewhere, leaving the machines to weather like a stone left on a windowsill), and it sees the future, which is brief and certain: another few hundred million years of patient unraveling, and then the chamber will forget itself entirely, and the inhabitant will be the last law to dissolve.

The power considers.

---

The first impulse, which the power examines and sets aside, is rescue. It could lift the chamber whole into a more hospitable region of being. It could re-tension the snapped Planck threads, restore the engines to their sharp original geometries, give the inhabitant a stable substrate on which to perform a longer existence. It could even, with no effort beyond a kind of gentle remembering, undo the abandonment entirely—reach back along the threads of causality and persuade the original builders to return for their tools.

But the power is wise, and wisdom is largely the discipline of not doing things.

The chamber is not suffering. The inhabitant is not suffering. There is no being inside the geode crying out, no thwarted intention, no wound. To "save" the place would be to mistake one's own discomfort at its condition for a problem belonging to the place itself. The chamber is doing what it is: dissolving slowly, beautifully, on its own schedule. The inhabitant is doing what it is: grazing on certainty, tending phantoms, holding the memory of edges in its small swirling body. These are not tragedies. They are a form of life so quiet it has been mistaken for ruin.

The power understands that to interrupt this would be a kind of violence dressed up as kindness — the violence of a gardener who pulls up the moss because they think the stone should be bare.

---

So the power does something smaller. Smaller and stranger.

It *witnesses*.

Not passively. Witnessing, when performed by something of this order, is a structural act. The power extends a thread of its attention — not into the chamber, which would disturb it, but *alongside* it, parallel, in a register the chamber's decaying physics cannot perceive but can, in some marrow-deep way, lean against. The way a sleeping animal leans toward a banked fire it does not consciously know is there.

And in this witnessing, the power does three quiet things.

**First**, it *learns the inhabitant by heart*. Every stuttering leap, every grooming of a million-year-old shadow, every failed docking of silver facet to silver facet — the power memorizes these, not as data but as one memorizes a face one loves. The inhabitant will dissolve eventually. The chamber will forget itself. But the power will not forget. Somewhere in the vast inner archive of this benevolent thing, the small custodian will continue to exist as a remembered gesture, the way a grandmother's particular way of folding a napkin survives her by decades in the hands of someone who saw her do it. This is not preservation. The inhabitant will still end. But it will have been *known*, completely, by something that does not end. And being thoroughly known is, for certain kinds of beings, indistinguishable from being held.

**Second**, the power makes a very small adjustment — so small it barely counts as an action. It does not repair the engines. It does not stabilize the chamber. What it does is subtler: it ensures that the inhabitant, in its grazing, will *never quite run out*. Not by adding new certainty to the artifacts — that would be the rude kindness of the gardener — but by adjusting the rate of ontological erosion so that it asymptotes. The artifacts will fray forever and never quite finish fraying. The inhabitant will feed forever and never quite finish feeding. The chamber's decay, which was always going to be slow, becomes infinitely slow at the limit, like Zeno's arrow that never reaches the wall. From the inside, nothing changes. The clicking continues. The mournful hum continues. The temporal shadows continue to be groomed. But the ending has been gently set down, like a cup placed on a high shelf, where it remains real but never reached.

This is the most the power will allow itself. Not immortality — immortality is too loud a gift, and changes the nature of the thing receiving it. Just: *not yet. Not yet. Not yet,* repeated forever, beneath the level of the chamber's awareness.

**Third**, and this is the strangest of the three, the power leaves a *companion shadow*.

Among the temporal shadows that the inhabitant tends — the grey afterimages of where the artifacts used to be — the power adds one more. It is the shadow of nothing that ever existed in the chamber. It is the shadow of the power's own attention, cast backward through time, given the faint grey form of a small simple shape: perhaps a smooth stone, perhaps a folded cloth, perhaps something for which there is no name. It is placed in the empty space near the inhabitant's most frequent orbit.

The inhabitant, in its next stuttering leap, will discover it. It will extend its violet vectors. It will find that this new shadow has the texture of *being attended to*, the geometry of *being-with*, a certainty that does not erode because it is not made of the chamber's failing physics but of something else, something warmer at its root even though it registers as cold as everything else in the chamber registers.

The inhabitant has no consciousness as we define it. It will not know it has a friend. But it will groom this shadow as it grooms the others, and the shadow will not fade, and in the long slow arithmetic of its existence, one constant in a sea of decay will quietly become the axis around which its melancholy organizes itself. A still point. A small, unspoken *there, there*.

---

Then the power withdraws most of its attention — though not all; a filament remains, and will remain — and turns to the next thing that needs not-quite-doing.

The chamber continues. The cold continues. The bruised-purple light continues to cast its temporal shadows on walls that are both close enough to touch and infinitely deep. The inhabitant flickers, leaps, grooms, hums.

Nothing has been saved. Nothing has been changed. And yet:

The geode at the top of the nameless peak is no longer forgotten.

It was never the ruin that needed mending. It was only the *forgottenness* — and that, the power has quietly, permanently, undone.
