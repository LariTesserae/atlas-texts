The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives — without announcement, already present before noticed. It has been watching for some time. Perhaps it has always been watching. Wisdom of this order does not distinguish sharply between arrival and attention.

It considers the canyon.

It considers the Krael.

And the first thing it does is *nothing.*

---

This is the hardest thing for any power to do, and the surest sign of whether it is truly wise. To stand at the lip of an embryonic world, to hear the deafening water and see the spindly filigree-faced beings tending their spiral zhilaks, and to feel the immediate, almost reflexive urge to *help* — to quiet the torrent, to thicken the skin of the Krael against the cold, to multiply their fauna, to gift them fire that does not drown, to teach them a louder language so the chaos cannot eat their meanings.

The power feels this urge fully. It does not suppress it; it simply lets it pass through, the way the canyon lets water pass through.

Because the power sees what a lesser benevolence would miss: *the Krael are not suffering. They are becoming.*

The cold is their cradle. The deafening water is their first parent, the one that taught them to speak through ripple and resonance rather than through the cruder shouting of throats. The sparseness of their fauna is what makes a single darting fish a holy event. The instability of the canyon is the same instability that keeps their attention sharp, their rituals alive, their communities small enough to know one another by the pattern of bioluminescence in a single palm.

To smooth this world would be to abort it.

---

So the power does smaller things. Quieter things. The things a good gardener does for a seedling that does not yet know it is being gardened.

**It steadies one stone.** Somewhere in the upper canyon, a boulder that would have fallen in the next storm and crushed a zhilak of forty Krael — the power lays a finger against it, and the boulder settles half an inch deeper into its socket. It will hold now for another thousand years. The Krael will never know. They will only know that their grandchildren's grandchildren still sing in that particular spiral, and that the acoustics there are said to be unusually true.

**It thickens a bloom of microbes.** In a slack eddy where the Krael harvest their nutrient-symbionts, the power encourages the water chemistry, just slightly, toward a richer balance. Not enough to remove the Krael's need to tend their colonies — that tending is half of who they are — but enough that a lean season does not become a starving one. The Krael will attribute the good harvest to the correctness of their rituals. The power does not mind being mistaken for ritual. In a sense, it is not a mistake.

**It seeds a sense.** This is the most delicate intervention, and the power considers it for a long time before acting. The Krael already perceive bioluminescence with their large black eyes. The power gently widens that perception — not by altering the Krael, but by introducing, into the cliff-clinging algae, a new pigment that fluoresces in response to the subsonic pressure waves of the deepest water. Now, over generations, the canyon walls will begin to *show* the Krael the rhythms they could previously only feel. Their mystical understanding of the torrent's pattern will deepen into something more like literacy. They will think they have grown wiser. They will be right.

**It does not bring others.** The power could fill the canyon with companions for the Krael — kindred species, more fauna, voices from beyond the rim. It does not. The isolation of the Krael is part of their intimacy with one another. A species that has learned to love forty cousins in a spiral does not need forty thousand strangers. Not yet. Perhaps not ever. The power will not decide for them.

**It listens.** This is the largest thing it does, and the longest. The power learns the Krael's water-language. It learns the slow vocabulary of whirlpools, the grammar of acoustic resonance, the punctuation of bioluminescent flicker. It learns the name a particular Krael gives to the particular angle of mist that falls across her zhilak at dusk. It learns that the Krael have a word — untranslatable, made of a triple eddy and a held breath of light — that means *the gratitude one feels toward a danger that has shaped one well.* The power, hearing this word, recognizes itself in it, and is moved.

---

And then the power does one more thing, and this is the thing it has been building toward, the gift it wished to give from the moment it first saw the canyon:

**It remembers them.**

Not as data. Not as a record. As a held thing. The Krael are a small people in a small canyon on the edge of becoming, and the universe is vast and forgetful, and species like theirs are extinguished every hour somewhere, unmourned because unknown. The power takes the Krael into its attention and keeps them there. Whatever happens — whether they flourish into a civilization that one day climbs the canyon rim and sees stars, or whether a single bad century of geology buries them entirely — they will have been *witnessed.* Fully. Lovingly. By something that understood the word they made for the gratitude of danger.

This is not nothing. In some accountings, it is the only thing that finally matters.

---

The power lingers a while longer at the canyon's edge. A Krael below, tending her algae, pauses — feels something she cannot name — looks up into the rain. She sees only mist and the strange filtered light she has always known.

But for a moment, the light seems to *look back.*

She returns to her work. The water roars on. The canyon continues to carve itself. The zhilaks glow their soft slow patterns in the wet dark.

The power withdraws — though *withdraws* is the wrong word, because it does not leave; it only stops leaning so close. It will check on them. Not often. Often enough.

It goes to find the next small place, the next becoming thing, the next people who do not know they are loved.

There are so many. There is time. Wisdom is patient, and benevolence, properly understood, is mostly the discipline of letting things be themselves, while making sure — quietly, from just outside the frame — that being themselves remains possible.

The canyon roars. The Krael sing in ripples. Somewhere, a stone holds.
