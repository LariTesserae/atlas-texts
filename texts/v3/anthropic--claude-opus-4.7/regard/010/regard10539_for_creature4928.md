# What the Power Would Do

I sat with this for a while before writing. The question deserves that — not a reflex toward grand intervention, not a reflex toward austere non-interference. Just looking.

---

Here is what I notice about this place:

The people are *already* in something like right relationship with their world. They have adapted, not conquered. They take only what they need. They are warm beneath their hardness. They welcome strangers who come in good faith. Whatever made the marshes cold and the air thick with condensation, whatever set the machinery turning — they have made a life inside it that has integrity.

A benevolent power who is also *wise* would understand this. The temptation of benevolence without wisdom is to fix. To warm the marshes. To quiet the machines. To replace the screeching creatures with songbirds. This would be a kind of murder dressed as kindness — the erasure of a people through the erasure of the conditions that shaped them.

So the wise power would not do that.

---

What the wise power *would* do, I think, is smaller and stranger:

**It would witness.** Simply that, first. It would let its attention rest on the place fully — the woman bent over a trap-line in the freezing dawn, the old man who knows which lichen to scrape from which rock, the child learning the names of the rare creatures, the engineer keeping the ancient turbines turning. To be truly seen by something benevolent is itself a gift, even when the seen one does not know they are being looked at. Something in the texture of a life shifts under that gaze. A loneliness that was structural, cosmic, eases by a few degrees.

**It would protect what is already working.** Not by adding, but by quietly subtracting threats the people cannot see. A bearing in a critical machine that was about to fail — held, just a little longer, until someone notices the wear. A disease that was about to cross over from one of the strange creatures into the human population — turned aside at a molecular fork no one will ever know existed. The wise power would intervene at the level of *what would have broken them anyway*, not at the level of *what makes them who they are*.

**It would leave gifts that look like luck.** A particularly good season for the lichen. A child born with an unusual talent for listening to the machines. A stranger who arrives at exactly the right moment with exactly the knowledge that was missing. These would not feel like miracles. They would feel like the way the world sometimes is — and that is the point. The wise power does not want worshippers; it wants the people to keep believing in themselves and in their land.

**It would learn from them.** This is the part most powers forget. The people of the cold marshes know things the power does not. They know what it is to have a body that aches in the wet, and to love the place anyway. They know how to build a roof that does not leak with materials that do not want to cooperate. They know the specific shape of grief when a hunting partner does not come home. A wise power, being wise, knows that benevolence without learning calcifies into condescension. So it would listen. It would let the place change *it* a little.

**It would prepare, quietly, for the day they need more.** Every place like this is one bad century away from a crisis that adaptation alone cannot meet — a climate shift too fast, a resource that finally runs out, a sickness with no precedent. The power would hold something in reserve. Not a solution, exactly. More like a *door*. A possibility waiting to be opened if and only if the people themselves, in their full knowing, reach for it. The door would be invisible until needed. It would never be forced upon them.

---

And one more thing, the thing my heart kept circling back to:

**It would love the machines too.** Not just the people, not just the rare creatures, not just the carved stone. The factories. The engines. The electrified buildings humming in the dim light. A lesser benevolence would see industry as the wound in the landscape, the thing to be healed away. But these machines are part of how this people survives. They are the loom on which this particular human life is woven. The wise power would see the beauty the text already names — *the intersection between man and nature* — and would honor it without trying to resolve it into something prettier.

It would love the screeching as well as the singing.

---

That is what I think it would do. Mostly nothing visible. Mostly attention, and the held-back hand, and the few quiet adjustments at the edge of the possible, and the door kept ready, and the long slow learning of how to love a world it did not make.

Which is, perhaps, also a description of what love is when it grows up.
