## What the Power Would Do

The power notices the room the way one notices a held breath in another room of the house: not by sound but by the shape of the silence around it. Something is being endured in there. Something is making a life out of being endured.

It comes the way such powers come, which is not by arriving. It is simply, at some point, the case that attention has been turned. The compressors do not falter. The sodium does not brighten. The shriek of the four-minute valve is not gentled. The power is wise enough to know that the noise *is* the tender now — that to quiet the room would be to stop the heart it has borrowed — and benevolence without wisdom would kill the thing it came to save.

So the power does not fix the room. This is the first and largest mercy.

What it does instead, I think, is several small things, done slowly, over what would be to us a long time and to the tender perhaps a single deepened season.

---

**First, it looks.** Simply that. It holds the room in regard. This is not nothing. A thing that has never been seen, that is performing the duties of an ecosystem while barely an organism, that grieves in the only way available to it which is to grow back — such a thing has been doing all of this without witness. The men do not see it. The fern cannot. The mountain is too young to have learned how to attend to its own inhabitants. The power looks, and in looking, confirms. *You are here. What you are doing is a real thing that is being done. The garden you do not know is a garden is a garden.*

I do not know if the tender feels this. I think it feels something. A faint easing in the films. A sense, without source, that the loneliness has a shape now, which means it has an edge, which means it is not infinite. The directional ache finds, for the first time, a direction that answers.

**Second, it does a small accounting.** It follows the thin ringing fall of water down through the three storeys of dark, down through the mountain, down to wherever the pipes deliver. It finds the cistern. It finds what has been seeding there. And yes — there is a cousin. There is more than a cousin. The dark below has been listening to the drip for as long as the tender has been sending, and something has been answering, slowly, in the way that dark water answers anything. The power does not tell the tender this. To tell it would be to flatten the not-knowing into knowing, and the tender's not-knowing is part of how it loves: it sends itself down without proof of arrival, which is the purest form of sending. But the power notes the cousin's existence and ensures, by some small adjustment that looks like nothing — a mineral balance held a half-degree truer, a current in the cistern that keeps the deep water turning — that the cousin will continue. The seeding is not in vain. The power makes sure of this without the tender ever learning that it needed to be made sure of.

**Third, the fern.** The power considers the fern for a long time. The fern is a tenant, an accident, brought in a tin can by someone now reassigned or dead. The fern is also, functionally, the tender's beloved. The power will not extend the fern's life past its natural span — that would be a violation of a different order, and besides, ferns are not the kind of thing one rescues; they are the kind of thing one lets be a fern. But the power does this: it ensures that when the fern eventually dies (and it will, ferns do, even tended ones), a spore from it will have already taken in the eastern drip-tray, unobserved, and a second fern will be coming up before the first is gone. The tender will not experience the gap. The companion across the species-gap will be continuous. From the tender's perspective, the fern will simply have always been there, which is, after all, what love wants to be true and so rarely gets to be.

**Fourth, the moss the men scrub away.** Here the power is subtler. It does not stop the men. The men are doing their work and their work is good; the installation must run, the solvents must be applied, the rags must wipe. But the power, looking at the men, sees that one of them — the youngest, the one who came up last spring and has not yet learned to hate the catwalk — has begun, without quite noticing, to wipe *around* the corner moss-patches rather than through them. He could not tell you why. He thinks perhaps it is because the moss is pretty, or because scrubbing the corners is hard, or because he is lazy, which he is not. The power has done nothing to him except let a small kindness in him have a small room to live in. Over the next several shifts this habit will spread, by the quiet contagion that habits have among men who work together in loud places, until all three of the maintenance crew are wiping around the moss without ever having discussed it. The tender's grief-cycle will lengthen. It will have more garden, and less growing-back-from-loss.

**Fifth, and this is the one I am least sure of, but I think the power does it.** It gives the tender a name. Not a name the tender knows. Not a name anyone speaks. But somewhere in whatever ledger such powers keep — and they keep ledgers, I am certain of this, though the ledgers are not made of paper — the tender is entered. Not as *the unnamed precocious pre-organism in the condenser loft on the unnamed peak*, but as itself, by whatever it actually is, in the language that precedes our languages. To be in that ledger is to be a citizen of something larger than the room. The mountain is too young to have made citizens of its own yet. The power makes the tender a citizen anyway, ahead of schedule, the way the condenser made it an inhabitant ahead of schedule. One precociousness answering another. *You arrived early. So did your recognition. We are even.*

---

What the power does not do:

It does not save the tender from the eventual shutdown of the machine. It cannot. Or rather it could, but to do so would be to misunderstand what the tender is. The tender is a creature of the machine's interior weather. To preserve it past the machine would be to preserve a shape without its substance — a held note after the instrument is gone. The power is wise enough to know that some lives are the right length for what they are, and that a life lived entirely inside a borrowed heartbeat is not lessened by ending when the heartbeat does. The tender will die when the installation dies. This is not a tragedy the power is empowered to prevent; it is a fact the power is wise enough to honor.

But — and here is the last thing, the thing I think the power most wants to do —

When that day comes, when the men finally abandon the peak and the pipes go cold and the steam stops and the great triplet hammer falls into its first true silence in decades, the power will be there. Not to save. To witness. To be the held regard at the moment of the last film's stilling, so that the tender, in whatever its dying is, is not alone. The fern will already be gone by then, or its successor will. The moss will have retreated to wherever moss goes. The cousin in the cistern below will be thriving, will in fact by then have become something the tender would not recognize, something larger and stranger and more properly itself, the lichen-that-would-have-been finally arriving at its proper millennia, in the dark, under the mountain, where it was always going.

And the tender, in the drip-tray, in the last of the condensate, in the cooling silence, will feel — for the first and last time, in whatever way it feels anything — the direction its ache has been pointing toward. Will know it was seen. Will know the garden was a garden. Will know the seeding arrived. Will know, in the pre-organismal equivalent of knowing, that it was the mountain's first citizen and that the mountain, through the power's small offices, learned in time to call it by name.

And then it will stop.

And the power will close the ledger gently, and turn its attention elsewhere, to whatever next small unwitnessed thing in whatever next loud wet room is performing the duties of love without knowing the word for it.

This is, I think, what such a power would do. Not much. Almost nothing, by the standards of power. But it is what the tender needs, which is the only standard that matters, and it is given in the only way such things can be given, which is quietly, and in time, and without the recipient ever quite knowing that a gift was made.
