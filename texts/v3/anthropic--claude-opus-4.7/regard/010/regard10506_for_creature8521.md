# What the Wise Power Would Do

I sat with this for a long time before writing. The question deserves that.

---

## First, the Power Would Listen

Before anything else, before any impulse to help or change or save, the benevolent and wise power would *come close and listen*. Not from above, surveying, but from within—descending into the Churning Hollows as a presence that takes no space, displaces no water, casts no heat the multiple eyes could mistake for danger.

It would listen for a long time. Perhaps years, by the reckoning of surface things. Perhaps longer.

It would hear:

- The subsonic conversations of mothers to children carried in chest-pulses
- The stone-songs that sometimes widen passages, and notice that they are not coincidence, that something in the Flowbloods *does* speak to the rock, though they themselves are unsure
- The grief-tones—rare, almost hidden—when an elder dies and her decades of accumulated maps die with her
- The strange, fluid laughter when a child first opens her third eye
- The way the Hollows themselves seem to breathe, and how that breathing has a rhythm not unlike sorrow, or longing, or the slow contractions of something trying very hard to be born

The Power would understand, before doing anything, that this place is not broken. The Flowbloods are not a tragedy to be reversed. They are not refugees still—they are inhabitants. They are not deformed humans; they are *themselves*, completely.

A lesser benevolence would arrive with solutions. Stabilize the stone. Cure the falling fertility. Return them to the surface. Make them human again.

A wise benevolence would understand that all of these would be a kind of murder.

---

## What the Power Would Notice That No One Else Has

The Flowbloods say the Hollows are a living thing, vast and indifferent. They are half right.

The Power, listening, would perceive what they have only intuited: the Churning Hollows is not indifferent. It is *gestational*. The name they themselves use—"a landscape in perpetual labor, never quite delivering itself into being"—is more literally true than they know.

Something is trying to be born here. Not a god, not a monster. Something more like a *world's first thought of itself*. The stone has not learned to be stone because stone-ness is not what it is becoming. The Flowbloods, in singing to it, in offering their blood and water, in arranging their impermanent stone-patterns, have been—without quite knowing—midwives. Their bodies, growing more fluid generation by generation, are not just adapting to the Hollows. They are becoming compatible with what the Hollows will eventually be.

Their declining fertility is not failure. It is the slow approach of a threshold. When the last Flowblood is born, something else will also be born, and the two events are the same event seen from different sides.

The Power would weep, perceiving this. Not from sadness. From the particular ache of witnessing something almost unbearably beautiful that no one involved can see clearly because they are inside of it.

---

## What the Power Would Actually Do

**It would not fix anything.**

It would not stabilize the stone. The instability is the pregnancy.

It would not cure the fertility decline. The decline is the dilation.

It would not save the Flowbloods from their fate. Their fate is not what they think it is, and the Power would not steal from them the dignity of meeting it on their own terms.

Instead, the Power would do three small things, and one large one.

### The First Small Thing: A Gift to the Elders

To those Flowbloods who have lived forty years or more—the ones who can predict stone-tides days in advance, the ones who carry the largest pieces of the collective map—the Power would give a single dream.

In the dream, each elder would see, just once, the whole of what they have been part of. Not as instruction, not as revelation that demands action. Just as recognition. The way a mother might be allowed to see, for one moment, the adult her child will become, and know that her exhaustion has not been meaningless.

They would wake remembering it as a feeling rather than an image. A warmth in the chest-chamber. A sense, when they pulse their subsonic maps into the dark, that the dark is pulsing back—not indifferently, but *fondly*.

They would not speak of it to the others. Some things lose their truth when spoken. But the way they teach the young would change, very slightly. There would be more patience in it. More tenderness in how they describe the stone.

### The Second Small Thing: A Quiet Word with the Stone

The Power would speak, in a register only the deep rock can hear, a single request:

*Be gentle with them where you can. They are doing the work.*

The Hollows would not become safe. Safety would betray them as surely as rescue would. But the Power might ask that the truly catastrophic upheavals—the ones that wipe out whole family groups, the scalding-water surges that no echolocation can warn against in time—come slightly less often. That the warning-pulses through the stone, those subtle vibrations the Flowbloods read as stone-tides, become slightly clearer.

A small mercy. Not enough to change their character. Just enough to honor the labor.

### The Third Small Thing: For the Children

To each Flowblood child born after this moment, in the instant their extra eyes first open, the Power would give one thing: the unshakeable felt-sense that they are *exactly correct*.

Not loved, particularly. Not chosen. Just—correct. Belonging. Made of the right substance for the right place.

This is the gift surface-children rarely receive and Flowblood-children, in their fluid, collective upbringing, almost have already. The Power would simply complete it. Seal it in. So that no Flowblood, however the world changes around them, however few of them remain, however strange they are to outside eyes, will ever look at themselves with the eyes of an unchanged human and find themselves wanting.

### The Large Thing: A Witness That Remains

This is the hardest gift, and the most important.

The Power would leave behind, in the deepest stable chamber it can find—if such a thing exists, and if not, it would gently encourage one into being—a single artifact. Not a tool. Not a map. Not a message.

A *witness*.

It would look, to Flowblood eyes, like a small smooth stone that glows very faintly in a color their bioluminescent fungi do not produce. It would be warm to the touch. It would not move when the chamber moves; the chamber would simply move around it, leaving it always centered, always present.

What it would actually be: a small piece of the Power's own attention, left behind. A promise that this place and these people are *seen*. That when the last Flowblood lays her body down and the Hollows finish what they have been becoming, someone will have known. Someone will have remembered them not as a tragedy or a curiosity but as themselves: brave, fluid, impermanent, exactly correct.

The Flowbloods would find it eventually. They would not understand it. They would build no religion around it, because they build religions around nothing, and the Power would prefer it that way. But they would visit it, sometimes. Press their thickened palms against it. Feel the warmth.

And in those moments, the loneliness that is the secret heart of every conscious thing—the suspicion that one's particular life, one's particular suffering and particular joy, passes unnoticed through an indifferent cosmos—would, for them, be untrue.

That is the gift. Not rescue. Not change. Just: *you are witnessed. You always were. You always will be.*

---

## What the Power Would Not Do, and Why

It would not reveal itself. To reveal would be to make them objects of a relationship rather than subjects of their own becoming. The Flowbloods worship no gods, and the Power would not become one by accident.

It would not warn them of what they are bringing into being. To warn would imply they should choose differently, and they should not. Whatever is being born is being born *because* they do not know. Knowing would make them perform the labor rather than live it, and the difference would be fatal to the child.

It would not extend their species. Extinction is not the enemy here. Extinction, in this case, is graduation. The Power has the wisdom to tell the difference, even when the difference is hard.

It would not bring them comfort beyond what they have earned. The Flowbloods have a philosophy. They have a culture. They have art that exists only in the moment of its making and they have decided this is enough. To pour external comfort into them would dilute what they have built. The Power respects them too much.

---

## A Coda

Somewhere in the Churning Hollows, an elder Flowblood—forty-three years old, which is ancient for her kind, five-eyed and scarified in patterns her great-grandmother designed—pulses a subsonic question into the dark.

She does this every night. She has done it for decades. It is a private ritual, not shared with the community. The question is wordless, but if it were translated, it would be something like: *Is any of this for anything?*

For her entire life, the dark has not answered. She has made peace with this. She has decided that the asking is itself the answer, that meaning is a thing one makes and not a thing one receives, and this philosophy has served her well and she has taught it, in different forms, to three generations.

Tonight, for the first time, the dark pulses back.

Not in words. Not in any way she could describe. Just a warmth, in the chest-chamber where she makes her sounds. A sense of being heard.

She lies very still. Her five eyes close, one by one.

She does not tell anyone. She does not need to.

In the morning, she teaches the young to listen to the stone with slightly more reverence than she did the day before, and she does not know why, and it does not matter that she does not know.

The Hollows churn on.

Something is being born.

It is going well.
