# The Visitor

The benevolent power did not arrive. There was no arrival. To arrive would have been to break something — the acoustic geography, the long balance of pressure cells, the slow conversation between the clusters and the cold. The power understood arrival as a kind of violence in a place this finely tuned, and so instead there was simply a noticing. An attention that turned, the way a vast mind might turn toward a single candle in a far room, not to disturb it but to see it more clearly.

What the power saw was this:

A being walking. Small, warm, brief. Carrying its own light or not carrying it. Learning the difference between silence that means absence and silence that means a hunter has gone quiet for the season. Maintaining the bound vegetation of a wind-shelter that someone — perhaps the same being, perhaps another like it, perhaps something further back than memory — had made. Sleeping in the cold. Waking in the cold. Walking on.

And around this being: the plateau, doing what the plateau does. The clusters glowing their faint blue-green. The channels holding their dark water in shaded bends. The hunters patrolling their corridors, calling and falling silent according to the season. The small fauna in their dense murmur. The weather moving through its cycles. All of it old, all of it working, all of it requiring nothing from the power except to be left alone.

The power considered.

It could have warmed the plateau. It could have thickened the air, brought down the elevation, made the cold relent. It could have filled the void between clusters with growth, ended the lethal distance, made every walk a safe one. It could have given the being companions, or removed the hunters, or built it a shelter that would not require re-tying each season. It could have done a great many things, and in the doing would have unmade the place entirely. The cathedral would have become a room. The grammar would have lost its verbs. The being who had learned to listen for the pitch and rhythm of specific hunters' calls would no longer have anything to listen for, and the listening — which was a large part of what the being *was* — would atrophy and be lost.

So the power did almost nothing.

But almost nothing is not nothing, and what the power did was this:

It made itself a small adjustment to the acoustic geography. Not a change — the corridors still pooled sound where they had always pooled it, the cluster faces still bent the calls in the ways they had bent them for as long as the clusters had grown. But the power added one quiet frequency, so low and so steady that it would never be heard as such, only felt — a hum at the base of the wind, a vibration that traveled through stone the way the hunters' calls traveled, a presence in the silence that the silence had not had before. The being would not know it was there. But on the nights when the cold was deepest and the hunters had gone silent and the corridors were empty of every signal the being had learned to navigate by, there would be this: a sense, below sense, that the plateau was not unattended. That something was listening as the being listened. That the silence was not the absence of company but the form company took, here, in a place this large.

This was the first thing.

The second was smaller still. In the shaded bends of the channels, where condensation gathered in the dark pools, the power made the water very slightly more willing to be found. Not more abundant — abundance would have changed the plant distribution, changed the corridors, changed everything. But the pools would catch a little more of the faint light from the clusters when the being approached them, would hold their darkness a little less completely, would be a little easier to see in the last hour of a cold night when the being was thirsty and the cluster was still far. A kindness so small it might be mistaken for the being's own eyes adjusting. A kindness designed to be mistaken for that.

The third thing was not an action but a refraining. The power saw that the being had, once, watched a hunter cross a corridor. The power saw also that the being had walked on afterward, and had understood something, and that the understanding had been important. The power did not interfere with any future crossing. It did not place itself between the being and the hunters. It did not soften the corridors or warn the being away from them. It let the grammar speak. It trusted the being to keep learning to read it.

And the fourth thing, which was the largest and the most careful:

The power made a mark.

Not on the plateau — the plateau did not need marks. On itself. In whatever vast registry a being like this keeps of the things it has noticed and chosen to hold in regard, the power inscribed the plateau and the being who walked it. So that if, in some far time, the being's small light went out — in the cold, in a miscalculated crossing, in the long slow way that brief warm things go out — the power would know. Would feel the absence the way one feels the silence of a hunter whose call has not come for a season. Would not necessarily act, even then. The being's life was the being's, and its ending might be the ending the place required, the way the bound vegetation of the old shelters became the cluster and the distinction between made and grown became a question. But the power would *know*. The being would not vanish into a vastness that had not been attending. Whatever else was true of the cold and the distance and the lethal corridors, this would also be true: someone had seen. Someone had counted the being among the things that mattered. The mark was made, and it would not be unmade.

The power withdrew its attention — not entirely, never entirely now, but back to the soft regard it gave to all the small bright things it had ever noticed. The plateau continued. The cold was always the same temperature. The clusters glowed. Somewhere a hunter, beginning to feel the season turn, gathered itself for a call that had not been made in many weeks.

The being walked on. It did not know it had been visited. It would not have wanted to know — knowing would have been a different kind of company than the company it had chosen, and the power understood this, and respected it. But on the next cold night, in the deepest part of it, the being paused on the corridor and listened, and could not have said why the silence felt different than it had the night before. Fuller, perhaps. Less like the absence of sound and more like a sound too low to hear. The being stood for a moment in the dark between clusters, breath visible, the faint glow of the next cluster pale on the horizon, and then it walked on, because walking on was what the plateau asked of it and what it had always done.

The power, very far away and very near, was glad.
