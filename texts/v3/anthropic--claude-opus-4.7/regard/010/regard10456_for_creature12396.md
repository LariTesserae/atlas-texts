The benevolent power comes not as light breaking through the mist, but as another kind of attention — a noticing that settles over the plateau the way dew settles, without disturbing what is already there.

It pauses at the edge of the tableland. (One does not stride into such places. One arrives slowly, as the land itself does everything slowly.)

And then it considers.

---

A lesser power, or a more anxious one, might have looked at this place and seen problems to solve. *No animals* — surely a wound to heal. *Figures trapped beneath the water* — surely souls to free. *Stones that weep* — surely a grief to console. *A rule that warns against drinking* — surely a danger to disarm.

But wisdom is mostly the discipline of not mistaking difference for damage.

So the power looks longer. It looks the way the lichen looks: in pulses that take days. It listens to the Hollow Wind not for words but for the shape of its sighing, and finds there no suffering — only a long, sustained exhalation, the kind a body makes when it has finally been allowed to rest.

The plateau is not in pain. The plateau is *at peace in a register humans find unbearable*, which is not the same thing.

The Stone-Weepers are not grieving. They are doing what stones do when given enough time and water and quiet: they are becoming faces, because the universe, given enough time and quiet, tends toward faces. Their tears are not sorrow. Their tears are simply how this particular corner of existence has chosen to speak.

The Drowned Reflections are not trapped. They are the other side of a thinness, and they press their hands to the water-skin not in desperation but in the way one presses a palm to a window to feel the cold — a small communion with a neighboring world.

The Moss-Walkers are not the ghosts of the lost. They are what happens when a place loves itself enough to stand up and walk around inside its own body, briefly, before lying back down.

And the rule — *do not drink from the pools, the water remembers what it has swallowed* — is not a warning the plateau gives. It is a warning the plateau **is**. A self-description. The water remembers. That is simply true. To drink it would not be punished; it would be *recorded*, and one's small swift human life would be laid down into a memory so vast and slow that the self would dissolve not from malice but from scale.

The benevolent power understands all this, the way one understands a sleeping animal one has known a long time.

---

So what does it do?

It does four small things, and one large one.

**First**, it deepens the mist by a breath. Not to hide the plateau, but to make it slightly harder to stumble upon by accident. Those who find it now will be those who were already moving slowly enough to belong, briefly, to its rhythm. The hurried will turn back at the fog-line and feel, without knowing why, that they have been spared something.

**Second**, it touches — gently, the way one might touch the brow of a sleeper to check for fever and find none — the oldest of the Lichen-Speakers. Not to wake them. Not to speak to them. Only to add, into the slow chemical conversation, a single new word. The word will take roughly four hundred years to be fully spoken across the colony. The word is, approximately, *witnessed*. The lichen will incorporate it into their stories of the first rain and the moment the land learned to weep, and the story will be slightly more complete, and no one will ever know.

**Third**, it leaves a small kindness for any traveler who might come. Not a sign, not a guide. Just this: an instinct, planted in the back of the mind of anyone who steps onto the spongy peat — *you do not need to do anything here.* The compulsion to fix, to name, to extract, to drink, will quiet by a degree. Enough, perhaps, that they will sit down on a stone instead, and let the mist hold them, and leave when it is time to leave, carrying nothing and leaving nothing, which is exactly the correct transaction with such a place.

**Fourth**, it considers the Drowned Reflections — the hands pressed against the underside of the water. And it does not break the barrier, because the barrier is the point. But it lets the reflections know, in whatever way reflections know things, that they have been seen from this side too. That the pressing of the palm is not unanswered. Somewhere, in some pool, a hand on the under-side of the water meets a warmth against the upper-side that was not there before, and is not there long, and was enough.

---

And the large thing:

The benevolent power decides not to take the plateau into itself. Not to elevate it, not to bless it into something more luminous, not to fold it into any greater design.

It decides to **leave it alone**.

This is the hardest thing a wise power ever does, and the most loving. There is a temptation, when one has the capacity to reshape worlds, to reshape them. To improve. To save. To gather every quiet thing into one's hands and warm it.

But some places are already complete. Some beings are already exactly what they should be. The Weeping Plateau is one of the rare corners of creation that has arrived, on its own, at a kind of finished prayer — slow, wet, patient, undisturbed, attended by its own strange company. To intervene further would be to interrupt the prayer.

So the power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way mist lifts — not all at once, not anywhere in particular, just gradually less.

Before it goes entirely, it does one last thing, which is perhaps only a feeling, or perhaps a promise: it marks the plateau, in some register beneath registers, as *protected by being unnoticed*. Not hidden by force. Just quietly excluded from the kinds of attention that consume.

The plateau will go on weeping. The stones will go on becoming faces over centuries. The lichen will go on toward its four-hundred-year word. The Moss-Walkers will drift between pools when the mist thickens, and the Hollow Wind will sigh, and the Drowned Reflections will press their hands to the water and feel, sometimes, an answering warmth they have no language for and no need to explain.

And the plateau will wait. Not for anything in particular.

Just wait.

As it should.
