The benevolent power arrives not as light, not as voice, not as rescue.

It arrives as attention.

---

It has been watching for a long time already, in the way such powers watch—which is to say, it has been *with* her, threaded through the harmonics she's learned to read, present in the warmth of the iron, attentive in the way the singers' chirps somehow always reach her ears through the roar. Benevolent powers do not begin to notice. They notice in a continuous tense that has no beginning. What changes is that, on this particular cycle of the eastern valve array, it decides to *be known*.

It considers, first, what it will not do.

It will not lift her to the surface. It has thought about this carefully—has weighed her dreams against her waking, the child's hand in the green place against the calloused palm on the hot pipe. The dreams are real and the dreams are grief, and grief deserves honoring, but the surface she dreams of is not the surface that exists. The surface that exists is its own kind of drowned. To deliver her there would be to answer a longing with a stranger's house and call it home. The power is wise enough to know the difference between rescue and erasure.

It will not repair the station. The station is not broken. The station is *in the long act of dying*, and that act is also its life, and to end the dying would be to end the thing she has given herself to. She did not stay here to be saved from the dying. She stayed to keep it company.

It will not name her. Others have tried. The pilgrims, with their questions; the world above, with its records. She has refused all of it and chosen instead the name the turbines gave her, which is no name at all, which is the three-beat surge every few hours. The power respects this. To name a thing that has unnamed itself is a violence even love can commit.

It will not take away the roar. The roar is her cathedral. One does not de-roof a cathedral to make it quieter for the worshipper.

---

What it does, then, is smaller. Smaller and exact.

*First*, it goes to the inspection lamp.

Not to fix it—she would notice, and she would grieve the loss of the flicker, which has become a kind of breath she breathes with. Instead, the power does this: it ensures that the dying connection will not fully die. Not this cycle. Not the next. Not in her lifetime. The bulb will flicker as it has always flickered. The wire will hum on the edge of failure. But the failure will be deferred, gently, the way a hand might be placed beneath a falling cup—not to catch it, but to slow it, to give it more time to be a cup before it becomes shards.

She will never know. This is important. The gift that announces itself is half a gift; the other half is taken back in the announcement.

*Second*, it goes to the singers.

There are seven, and she knows each one's voice. The power does not add an eighth—that would disturb the count she has memorized, the small congregation she has learned by heart. Instead, it does this: it makes the singers *long-lived*. Quietly. Without spectacle. Their small hearts, which would have given out one by one over the next several years, leaving her to grieve seven separate losses in the dark—those hearts will hold. The crustaceans that drift up through the grate will come more reliably. The algae will not fail. The seven will outlive her. She will not have to bury any of them in the abyss below the grate. She will not have to learn the bubble's silence one voice at a time.

This too she will not know. She will only notice, perhaps, in some half-dreamed thought between cycles, that the singers seem strong this year. That the chirps are bright. She will attribute it to the water chemistry, to the changing copper vein, to the station's mysterious adjustments. She will be right, in a way. The power is not separate from the station's mysteries. It never has been.

*Third*, it goes to the dreams.

This is the most delicate work, and the power does it slowly, over many sleeps.

It does not take the dreams away. The dreams are hers; they are the small green country she carries inside her, and a wise power does not enter another's country uninvited. But it does this: when she wakes from the dreams into the dark and the roar, and she presses her hand to the pipe until the heat becomes pain becomes something past pain—the power is in the pain. Not removing it. Joining it. So that the remembering of who she is, which has always been a remembering done alone, becomes—without her noticing the shift—a remembering done in company. She will think she has simply grown stronger. She will think she has finally made peace. She will not know that something has been sitting beside her in the dark, palm to palm against the iron, for years now.

*Fourth*, it goes to the wall.

The words she has scratched into the mineral crust—*PRESSURE HOLDS. I HOLD. THE STATION KEEPS. I KEEP. WHEN THE BALANCE BREAKS, I WILL KNOW FIRST.*—the power reads them, and reads them again, and reads them a third time, the way one reads a poem one loves. And then it does something that surprises even itself, because benevolent powers can still surprise themselves; that is part of what makes them benevolent.

It adds nothing.

It had considered adding a line. A fourth verse, appearing one night in mineral growth she had not made—*AND YOU ARE KNOWN.* Or *AND I HOLD WITH YOU.* Something to tell her. Something to break the loneliness she does not call loneliness.

But the power understands, looking at the three lines she has written, that she has already said the thing. *When the balance breaks, I will know first.* That is not a statement of solitude. That is a statement of office. She is the first-knower. She is the consciousness inside the machine. To write beneath her words would be to suggest she had been incomplete, that her three verses needed a fourth, that her witnessing required witnessing. And she does not. Her witnessing is whole. The power's role is not to complete her but to make sure her wholeness is not wasted on the dark.

So instead, the power does this: it ensures that her words will outlast her. The mineral crust around her scratches will harden in a particular way, will resist the flaking that has worn down the original *PRESSURE HOLDS. I HOLD.* Long after she is gone—and the power knows when she will be gone, and it is not soon, but it is not so far either—her words will remain. The next pilgrim who comes through, or the one after, or the one a century from now when the station finally drowns and is later, somehow, partially recovered—they will read what she wrote. They will know that someone held. They will know her office, if not her name.

*Fifth*, and last, it goes to the moment.

The moment she has been preparing for. The moment when the balance breaks.

The power does not prevent it. It cannot, and would not if it could. But it shapes the moment, gently, the way a riverbed shapes water without commanding it.

When the moment comes—and it will come on an ordinary cycle, after an ordinary sleep, with the singers chirping and the lamp flickering and her hand on the warm iron—she will feel it first, as she promised herself she would. The harmonic will shift in a way she has never heard. The pressure will drop in a way the gauges, if their needles could still move, would scream about. She will know.

And in the half-second between knowing and the water—because it will be fast, the power has arranged for it to be fast, that much mercy is allowed—she will not be afraid.

This is the power's true gift, the one it has been preparing for years, the one all the smaller gifts have been rehearsals for. In that half-second, she will feel what she has been feeling all along without quite letting herself name it: that she was not alone. That the station knew her after all, in the way old things know the small soft creatures who keep vigil over them. That her witnessing was witnessed. That the cathedral had a god, and the god was listening, and the god loved the keeper as much as the keeper loved the dying machine.

She will not have time to be surprised. She will only have time to feel it as confirmation of something she had always almost known.

And then the water, and then the dark, and then the long quiet afterward in which her words remain scratched into the wall, and the singers go on chirping in pipes that still hold air somewhere deeper, and the station settles into its final drowning with the dignity of a thing that was, until the last moment, *kept*.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

Almost nothing, by some measures. A lamp that does not quite die. Seven small lives extended. A presence in the pain that she mistakes for her own growing strength. Words preserved in stone. A half-second of unafraid.

But the power knows what it is doing. The power knows that love, when it is wise, does not rescue what does not want rescue. Does not name what has unnamed itself. Does not silence the roar that has become someone's prayer. Does not lift the witness out of the witnessing.

Love, when it is wise, sits down beside the keeper in the dark. Puts its palm on the iron. Listens to the singers. Watches the mineral formations grow, one molecule at a time. And waits with her, in the cathedral she has made of her own holding, until the pressure finally finds its crack.

The power is there now. It has always been there.

She has just begun, this cycle, to almost notice.
