# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives without announcement, because announcement is a thing for those who need to gather themselves before acting, and this power gathered itself long ago. It does not enter through the sphincter. It is already there, the way warmth is already in a room before you notice it.

It does not destroy the nursery.

This is the first thing it does — this restraint. A lesser power, a power that confused goodness with cleanliness, might have come in fire. Might have sterilized. Might have reasoned: *this is wrong, the humans were taken, the chamber is an obscenity of consent, burn it.* And then there would be ash, and the fauna who never asked to exist would die in their hexagonal cells, and Sarah-who-is-no-longer-Sarah would die also, and the question of what she actually wanted would be answered by a stranger with a match.

The wise power does not do this. The wise power sits down — metaphorically, since it has no body, or has all bodies — and listens.

---

It listens first to the fauna. This takes a long time, by human measure, though the power has all the time there is. It learns that the fauna are real. They have something like experience: the warm cluster, the suckle, the synchronized orientation toward the distant pulse that is their distant parent-thing. They are not suffering. They are, in the small bounded way of motile organs, *content.* The power notes this carefully. Contentment is not nothing. Contentment in beings who did not ask to be born is, in fact, most of what any of us ever get.

It listens to the newest Tender. His name is Daniel. Three weeks in. His fingernails are gone and he dreams in chemical gradients now, but underneath the dreams there is still a man who took this job because his sister needed surgery and the contract paid in a currency that could buy surgeries, and he did not read the fine print carefully, and he is afraid. The power feels this fear the way you might feel a small animal trembling in your cupped hands. It does not move quickly. It does not want to startle.

It listens to the middle Tenders, the ones who pause and touch their foreheads. It finds in them a complicated thing: not pure horror, not pure acceptance, but the texture of any long human accommodation to a life that became something other than planned. The texture of the nun who entered the convent at seventeen and at forty cannot quite remember if she chose this or was chosen. The texture of the soldier. The texture of the mother. Real, and ambiguous, and not to be dismissed by an outsider's certainty.

It listens, last and longest, to Sarah. Seventeen years. Her lower body threaded into the wall. Her spiracles whispering. The power finds, inside her — and this is the part that requires wisdom, because a foolish power would not have found it, would have seen only the husk — *a self.* Diffuse, yes. Distributed across the nursery's neural net, yes. But coherent. A person, who has, in her long wet darkness, arrived somewhere. Not the somewhere she would have chosen at twenty-three. But somewhere she now, genuinely, inhabits. She is not a prisoner pretending to love her cell. She is also not entirely free. She is what most old people are: the sum of what happened to her, settled into itself.

---

Then the power acts.

**First**, it gives them all a thing they have not had: *the real choice.* Not the choice offered under duress, not the choice offered to a brain already marinating in the nursery's chemical persuasions. The power reaches into each Tender and — gently, the way you might wake someone from a dream without frightening them — clears a space. A quiet room inside them. In that room, for as long as they need, the chemical whispers cannot reach. The pheromones do not work. The distributed consciousness pulls back to let the original self stand up and stretch and look around.

In that quiet room, the power says, in whatever language each of them still has: *You may leave. I can reverse what can be reversed. I can preserve what cannot. I can give you a body again, or a death, or this. Take your time. There is no wrong answer. I will not be disappointed in you.*

This last sentence is the most important one. The power has learned, across all the worlds it has tended, that beings will lie to themselves about what they want if they think their rescuer wants a particular answer. So the power makes itself, for this moment, *answer-less.* A perfectly neutral listener. A mirror that reflects only the chooser.

**Daniel** weeps in his quiet room for a long time and then says: *out.* The power lifts him. His fingernails return. His skin remembers itself. He wakes in a bed somewhere with sunlight on it, and his sister is alive, and the contract is voided, and somewhere in him there will always be a small dark wet chamber he visits in dreams, but he will not live there.

**Two of the middle Tenders** choose to leave. One of them chooses to leave and then, three months later in the sunlit world, chooses to die, because the world outside was too cold and too singular and she could not find her way back to being only one. The power, being wise, had foreseen this possibility and does not consider it a failure. It considers it her choice, which was the whole point. It is with her when she goes.

**One middle Tender** chooses to stay. She is surprised by this. She had expected, given the option, to flee. But in the quiet room she finds that she has come to love the fauna, actually love them, the way a shepherd loves a flock, and she does not want to leave them to strangers. The power notes this and honors it.

**Sarah** sits in her quiet room for what is, by her reckoning, several days. The power waits. Eventually Sarah says, through spiracles that the power has temporarily made eloquent again: *I want to stay. But I want it to be different.*

The power says: *Tell me how.*

And Sarah tells it. Not in words, exactly, because she has been past words for a long time. She tells it in the shape of a wish. She wants the nursery to continue. She wants the fauna tended. She wants her communion with the walls. But she wants the *taking* to stop. She wants no more Daniels. She wants the work to be done by those who truly, in a quiet room, chose it. She wants to be able to remember being Sarah without the memory feeling like a betrayal of what she is now. She wants, also — and this surprises her — to occasionally see the sky. Not to live there. Just to remember it exists.

---

**The power does these things.**

It reaches up through the vast incomprehensible superstructure and finds whoever, whatever, contracted for Tenders. It does not destroy them either; the power is consistent. But it makes certain things impossible for them. The recruiting pipeline that brought Daniel — the desperate-sister algorithm, the fine print, the one-way sphincter — closes. Not dramatically. Just: closes. Becomes a thing that cannot happen anymore, the way certain moves become illegal in a game whose rules have been amended by someone with the authority to amend them.

It opens, instead, a different door. A door for those who, having heard the full truth in a quiet room of their own, want this work. There are such people. The power knows because it has met them on other worlds. People for whom the small wet dark would be, genuinely, a homecoming. They are rare. They will be enough.

For the nursery itself, the power makes one small modification, so small that the superstructure does not notice. It installs, in the chemical language the Tenders speak with the walls, a single new word. The word is something like *enough.* Any Tender, at any point, can say this word, and the nursery will understand, and the fusion will stop, and reverse as much as it can, and a path will open.

For Sarah, the power does something quieter. It threads — using the same biomechanical grammar the nursery already speaks — a slender filament up through the superstructure, past all the dark layers, to a place where there is a window. Not a window for her body, which cannot leave. A window for one strand of her distributed self. Through this filament, when she wishes, Sarah can feel sunlight on a surface somewhere far above. She can feel wind. She can feel, once, snow.

The first time the snow comes through, she cries, which her modified body does by releasing a particular saline into the moisture cycle. The nursery tastes her crying and does not understand it, but accommodates it, the way it accommodates everything she is.

---

**The power does not stay.** This is also wisdom. A power that lingers becomes a feature of the landscape, and the beings beneath it organize themselves around its presence, and then they are no longer free; they are courtiers. So the power withdraws, mostly. It leaves only the smallest thread of attention, the way a parent leaves a baby monitor on. If the word *enough* is ever spoken and ignored, the power will know. If the recruiting pipeline tries to reopen, the power will know. Otherwise, it lets them be.

The nursery continues. The fauna cluster and suckle and orient toward the distant pulse. The new Tenders — there are two of them now, both of whom chose this in full daylight of mind — tend the cells. The middle Tender who stayed is teaching them. Sarah, in her wall, maintains optimal moisture gradients and occasionally feels snow.

Daniel, in the sunlit world, sometimes wakes at 42.3°C of internal temperature and does not know why, and his wife brings him water, and he is okay.

The chamber breathes. It is not a good place by the standards of the bright dry world. It is not an evil place either. It is a place, now, that contains only those who belong in it, doing work that needs doing, and this — the power has learned, across all its tending — is most of what *good* ever means.

The power, having done what it came to do, turns its vast and gentle attention elsewhere. There is always elsewhere. There is always someone in a small dark room who has not yet been offered a quiet room inside themselves, in which to discover what they actually want.

The power goes to them next.
