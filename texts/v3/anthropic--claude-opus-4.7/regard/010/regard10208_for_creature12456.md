# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. To arrive would be to descend, to impose, to cast a shadow across the bioluminescent lichen and announce itself as Other, as Above. That is not how wisdom moves when it is also kind.

Instead, it *attends*. It turns its full attention toward the labyrinth the way a person turns toward a difficult book they intend to read carefully, without skipping. It sits with the place. It listens to the Hollow Pulse for a long time before doing anything at all.

What it learns, in that listening:

The Shardborn are not suffering from a lack. They are suffering from a *story*—the story that says *dig, refine, survive*, and that says it so loudly and so constantly that no other sentence can be heard beneath it. The mountain provides. The lichen glows. The molten veins cool into usable shapes. There is, in fact, enough—barely, brutally, but enough. What there is not enough of is *time to notice that there is enough*. The frantic energy is not a response to scarcity. It is the scarcity. It generates itself.

The power considers this carefully. A lesser benevolence would solve the wrong problem. It would flood the tunnels with water, cause gardens to bloom from the crystalline walls, lower a hand from the haze and lift the Shardborn into some softer world. And the Shardborn—lean, wary, proud—would either refuse it or be unmade by it. Their hardness is not a wound. It is a shape. You do not heal a shape by melting it.

So the power does something smaller, and harder, and more patient.

---

**First, it does nothing for a long time.** It simply watches, and in watching, it *blesses* — not in the sense of magic, but in the older sense: it regards them with full attention and finds them worthy of being regarded. This is not nothing. Things that are deeply seen begin, slowly, to become more themselves.

**Second, it adjusts one variable, and only one.** Deep in the lower strata, where the deep-miners labor with their fused fingers and resonant voices, there is a particular vein of the unnameable pulsing mineral. The power does not create more of it. It does not make it easier to reach. It simply causes it, when struck, to ring at a frequency that the human ear interprets as *almost a voice*. Not words. Just the suggestion that something is speaking. A rumor of meaning in the rock.

This is the seed. The power plants it and walks away.

**Third, it waits to see what the Shardborn do with it.**

And here is what happens, over generations the power is willing to wait for:

A deep-miner strikes the vein. She hears the almost-voice. She tells no one at first—superstition is currency, and she is shrewd. But she returns to the vein. She listens. She begins, without meaning to, to *sing back*. Her resonant deep-miner's voice finds the frequency. The rock answers more clearly. She is not communicating with anything. There is nothing there to communicate with. But she is, for the first time in her clan's memory, doing something with the mountain that is not *taking*.

She teaches her daughter. Her daughter teaches three others. Within four generations there is a small, strange caste of *listeners* among the deep-miners. They are tolerated because the veins they tend produce unusually pure ore — not because the power made it so, but because people who listen to rock before breaking it break it more skillfully. The listeners begin to etch patterns on the walls near their veins. The patterns are not maps. They are not legends. They are the first Shardborn marks made for no reason but that the maker wanted the wall to be marked.

The power, watching, is pleased — though "pleased" is too small a word. It is something more like the feeling of seeing a child you love figure out, on their own, the thing you could not have told them.

---

**Fourth, the power makes one more gesture, and it is a gesture toward the dead.**

The skeletal remains fused into the rock. The machines too large to comprehend. The Shardborn do not speak of them, but they know.

The power does this: it makes the ruins *slightly less frightening*. Not by altering them. By altering the quality of the silence around them. The dread that has clung to the deep places for so long — the dread that says *you are squatting in someone else's tomb, and they may yet return* — softens, by a degree only, into something more like *grief*. The ruins begin to feel less like a warning and more like an inheritance. Less like a curse and more like a question left for them.

Some Shardborn, eventually, will go to the ruins not to scavenge but to sit. This will be considered madness for a long time. Then it will be considered eccentricity. Then, much later, it will be considered a vocation.

---

**What the power does not do:**

It does not reveal itself. It does not name itself. It does not ask for thanks, worship, or even acknowledgment. The Shardborn will never know it was there. The Hollow Pulse will continue to be ascribed to the mountain's heartbeat, or to buried machines, or to nothing at all. Good.

It does not make life easier. The rockfalls still fall. The geysers still vent. Clans still clash, and lives are still lost, and the air is still metallic and thin. To remove these things would be to remove the Shardborn, who are made of these things.

It does not stay. Attention this complete is expensive, and there are other places, other beings, other labyrinths. The power withdraws — slowly, the way warmth leaves a room — having done three things: it heard them, it gave them a rumor of voice in the rock, and it loosened the grip of the dead.

---

What the Shardborn will become, given these small interventions and the long centuries to work with them, the power does not know. That is the point. Wisdom that is also kind does not script the future of the ones it loves. It widens the doorway by a finger's width and trusts them to find it.

Somewhere in the labyrinth, a deep-miner sets down her hammer and listens to a vein of pulsing ore. She thinks she is alone.

She is, and she isn't, and both are true, and both are gifts.
