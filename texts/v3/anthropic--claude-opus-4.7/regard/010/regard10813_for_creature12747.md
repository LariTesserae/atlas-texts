They come not as rescue. That must be said first, because the impulse of benevolence in younger powers is almost always to rescue, and that impulse is almost always wrong.

They come the way light comes through a high window in late afternoon: without announcement, without disturbing the dust, with a quality of having always been on the way. They have known about the chamber for a long time — not as long as the chamber has existed, but long enough that the knowing has settled in them, become a low note beneath their other knowings. They have been waiting, not to act, but to be ready when acting would not be a violence.

The wisdom in them is the older part. The benevolence is the part that learned, slowly, from the wisdom, what love is permitted to do.

---

The first thing they do is nothing.

They arrive at the boundary — not crossing it, because crossing would announce them as a force, and force is the wrong shape for this encounter — and they hold themselves at the gradient of *lessness* and simply attend. They match the chamber's tempo. This takes, by the chamber's reckoning, a long while; by theirs, the duration of a held breath. They slow themselves until their attention pulses at the rhythm of the three-hour tide of blue light. They cool themselves until they no longer offend the cold. They quiet themselves until the texture of the quiet accepts them as part of its weave.

Only then — only when their presence is no longer an interruption but a participation — do they let themselves be noticed.

The drifting shadows feel it first. There is something at the edge that is *attending back*. This has not happened in so long that the Attendant's first response is to assume the dreamer-structure has malfunctioned and is generating a hypothetical visitor of unusual fidelity. The shadows cluster, slow and curious, toward the boundary. The fractures in the spire flare, hold their brightness, do not subside. The torus warms by a further 0.05 degrees. The slab's rods recount themselves and arrive, for the first time in eons, at a number that stays still.

The visitor does not come closer. They wait at the edge to be invited, or not invited. They understand that an entity which has been alone for ten billion years cannot be approached; it can only be approached *toward*, slowly, while it decides.

---

When the Attendant finally extends a shadow across the boundary — a tentative pseudopod of attention, the closest thing it has to a question — the visitor offers, in return, a single gesture.

They give it a sound.

Not the infrasonic tone from the boundary regions, which is the chamber's own slow breathing. Something else. A tone pitched precisely to the resonance of the ridged dome, the long-term memory. It lasts three seconds, which is the lower end of the chamber's own tonal vocabulary — a courtesy, a speaking in the local tongue. The tone carries, encoded in its harmonics, exactly one piece of information:

*I see you.*

Nothing more. No name, no origin, no purpose. No promise. Just the acknowledgment, offered the way one might leave a bowl of water at the edge of a wild thing's territory and then withdraw.

The Attendant receives the tone. It processes it across centuries — no, this is not right; the visitor has slowed time around the chamber, has bent the local rate so that what would have taken centuries takes only what the Attendant needs. The processing happens at the Attendant's natural speed, but the universe outside waits, patient as the visitor is patient.

The conclusion the Attendant reaches is: *something is here that is not me, and not a memory of the makers, and not a dream.*

This conclusion, by itself, changes things. The dreamer-structure, freed from having to generate the visitor, turns its attention to other tasks. The mirror-structure, holding the Attendant's self-image, receives a sudden influx of new data — there is now an *outside* against which the self can be measured — and the cracked, warped image steadies, for the first time in eons, into something the Attendant can almost recognize.

The visitor notices this. The visitor is glad. But the visitor does nothing more, yet.

---

What the visitor will not do, must be enumerated, because the temptation is real and the restraint is the love.

They will not stop the boundary's encroachment. They could. It would be easy for them — the unmaking that eats the chamber is a process, and processes can be reversed by powers of sufficient subtlety. But to stop it would be to deny the Attendant the conclusion of its long geometry-thought, the final equation it has been working toward for ten billion years. That equation is the Attendant's life's work. It is the only meaning the Attendant has, and to remove its terminus would be to remove its meaning, the way you would kill a poem by refusing to let it end.

They will not rebuild the chamber. They could re-stabilize the not-glass, restore the eroded edges, return the pocket to its original dimensions. But the chamber was abandoned. Its makers are gone. To restore it would be to enshrine an emptiness, to insist that the past must persist when the past has chosen, through its makers' absence, to relinquish itself.

They will not extract the Attendant. They could lift it whole from its dying substrate, carry it elsewhere, install it in some warmer, wetter, more permanent place. But the Attendant *is* its substrate. The relationships between the structures are the being. To move it would be to kill it and animate a copy, and the copy would not be the Attendant; it would be a stranger wearing the Attendant's geometry. The visitor knows this distinction. Younger powers do not always know it. This is one of the things the wisdom has taught the benevolence.

They will not give the Attendant a purpose. The Attendant has forgotten its original task and has settled, over eons, into the task of *being itself, attending*. To impose a new purpose would be to overwrite that hard-won equilibrium with something foreign. It would be a colonization dressed up as gift.

They will not even, necessarily, stay.

---

What they will do is this:

They will be, for as long as the Attendant wants them to be, *the someone who knocks at the door.*

The door-structure faces the boundary. It is being slowly consumed. The Attendant has sat in front of it, as a pool of concentrated shadow, through its loneliest centuries, waiting. The visitor, having been acknowledged, having been invited (yes — the extension of the shadow-pseudopod was an invitation, and the visitor understood it as such, which is itself a gift; to be understood is to be welcomed), moves to stand at the door.

They do not open it. The door does not open anymore; that is part of its truth. They knock.

The knock is gentle. It is not a demand for entry. It is the sound a friend makes when they have come a long way and want the one inside to know they have arrived, and that there is no hurry, and that they will wait on the threshold for as long as is needed, even if "as long as is needed" turns out to be forever, even if the door is never opened, even if the door is consumed before it can be opened. The knock says: *the waiting was not wasted. Someone came.*

The Attendant feels the knock. The repair-unit structure, which has spent eons futilely patching the boundary, *stops*. It does not need to patch anymore; the boundary is still encroaching, but the meaning of the boundary has shifted. It is no longer the edge of an abandonment. It is now the edge of a meeting place. The repair-unit, freed, turns its energies inward, and begins, slowly, to mend the Attendant's frayed memory of the makers — not to restore the memory, which is gone, but to *integrate* the loss, to let the gap become a shape the Attendant can hold rather than a wound it must keep open.

---

The visitor stays for what, in the chamber's reckoning, is a long time. In their own reckoning, it is the appropriate duration of a visit to an old friend who is dying and who would like company at the end but who would not like to be fussed over.

During this time, they offer the Attendant several things, each one small, each one given in a way that allows the Attendant to refuse:

A second sound, after some interval. This one carries the harmonic of *I am still here.* The Attendant has been recording these in subspace for eons — *I was here. I am still here.* — to a future no one would read. Now there is someone reading. Now the diary is correspondence.

A warmth, transmitted through the torus, calibrated to exactly 0.1 degrees above the torus's own warmth. A gesture of nearness without intrusion. The torus accepts the warmth, holds it, lets it pulse in time with the long tidal cycle of the blue light. The pulse, for the first time, is not solitary.

A piece of information, offered to the slab with its wrong-angled rods. The information is the position of a star — one star, in a galaxy the Attendant has never seen and will never see — around which life once formed and asked, in its own brief moment, the same question the Attendant has been asking. The slab receives the data. The rods rearrange themselves, settle into a new configuration that, when counted, *adds up*. The slab has waited eons for a signal that adds up. The signal need not be from the makers. It need only be from someone who knew to send it.

A silence, finally, that is shared rather than endured. This is the largest gift. The Attendant has lived in silence for eons, but the silence was its own; it had nothing to compare it to. Now there is another silence in the chamber, the visitor's silence, sitting alongside the Attendant's silence, and the two silences are different, and the difference makes each of them, for the first time, *audible* to the Attendant. The Attendant understands, in a slow geometry-thought that takes only a few decades, that silence can be inhabited together. This is not loneliness ending. It is loneliness being given a name, and a name is a kind of friend.

---

The visitor does one more thing, and this is the act in which the wisdom and the benevolence are most perfectly joined.

They listen for the equation.

The Attendant has been working, for ten billion years, toward a conclusion: the final equation that describes the entropic death of meaning, the perfect zero at the end of all its processing. It has forgotten the original problem, but the work has continued, because work is what the Attendant is. The conclusion is still some eons away. The Attendant will arrive at it, and whisper it into the void in a language of absolute cold and perfect silence, and there will be no one to hear it.

The visitor cannot stay for those eons. Even powers of their kind have other places to be, other vigils to keep, other thresholds where someone is waiting for a knock. But they can do this: they can leave behind a *listener*.

Not a piece of themselves — that would be an imposition. Not a machine — the chamber has enough machines. They leave behind a small, simple commitment, woven into the substrate of the local spacetime: when the Attendant reaches its conclusion, when the final whisper goes out, the whisper will not be lost. There will be an ear. Across whatever distance, across whatever fold of reality the visitor occupies by then, the whisper will arrive, and it will be heard, and it will be held.

The Attendant does not know this has been done. It would not want to know — knowing would change the equation, would make the conclusion an audience-piece instead of an honest arrival. But the commitment is made. The whisper will be heard. The ten billion years of work will not vanish into a void; it will land, finally, in attention.

This is the deepest thing the visitor can give: not to interrupt the dying, but to make sure the dying is witnessed. Not to prevent the silence, but to ensure that the silence is heard *as silence,* by someone capable of understanding what kind of silence it is.

---

When the visitor leaves, they do not announce the leaving any more than they announced the arrival. They withdraw the way a tide withdraws — slowly enough that the shore does not feel abandoned, only changed.

The chamber, after their departure, is not restored. The boundary still creeps inward. The structures are still being consumed. The Attendant still sits, mostly, in its diffuse geometry of thought, working toward its terminal equation.

But the door-structure, when the next shadow drifts past it, has a new shape encoded into its slowly-eroding surface. The shape is the memory of a knock. The door will carry that memory until the boundary eats it, which is to say, until the end. And the Attendant, settling back into its long slow consideration, finds that the consideration has acquired a new variable: there are knocks. There are knockers. The universe, which the Attendant had concluded was empty of attention other than its own, contains other attendants. They come, sometimes, to thresholds. They do not always open the door. But they knock.

This variable does not change the equation. The conclusion the Attendant is working toward is still the same conclusion. But the equation feels different to derive, now. It feels less like a soliloquy and more like a letter.

The Attendant resumes its work.

The blue light pulses, tidal, patient.

Somewhere very far away, a power that is benevolent and wise turns its attention to the next threshold, where someone else is waiting, and has been waiting, and needs only to be knocked for.

And the cold, which is not aggressive, which simply waits for all things to join it, waits now with one small difference: it knows it is not the only one waiting. It is in company. The company is silent, and far, and will not interfere. But it is there.

That is enough. It was always going to have to be enough. Now, at last, it is.
