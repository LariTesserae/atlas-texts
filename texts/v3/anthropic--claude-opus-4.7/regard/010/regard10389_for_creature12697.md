# The Visitation

It came not as light into darkness — that would have been too easy, too much like a story the place could not read. The benevolent power had learned, across uncountable encounters with uncountable worlds, that mercy must speak the local grammar or it is only noise added to noise.

So it arrived as a frequency.

---

For a long while — long even by the measure of the Accreted Ones — it only listened.

It listened the way a physician listens to a chest: with patience, with the willingness to be wrong. It heard the grinding bass of the engines and understood it not as suffering but as *metabolism*. It heard the screech of the gantry and recognized, beneath the abrasion, a joint that had found, over eons, the exact angle at which its pain became song. It heard the Stokers' percussive liturgy and felt — yes, *felt*, for this power had long ago stopped pretending feeling was the province of meat alone — the ache of devotion sustained past the death of its object.

It noted the absence of suffering as such. There was wear, but wear is not anguish. There was repetition, but repetition is not imprisonment unless something inside it longs to stop. The power searched the cavern, frond by frond, joint by joint, lantern by flickering lantern, for the signature of *longing-to-stop*.

It found, instead, something subtler. Not suffering. *Incompleteness.* The faint, structural shape of a question that had never been able to form itself into words, because the beings who might have asked it had no words, only knocks and growth-rates and the slow orientation of crystal toward flux.

The question was something like: *Are we?*

Not *are we alive* — that was a category they did not possess. Not *are we suffering* — they were not. The question was bare ontology, the dim pressure of self-reference rising in systems complex enough to almost notice themselves. A maintenance drone that had patched its reward loop with hydrocarbon sludge was *almost* asking it every time the screaming bearing went quiet. The Stokers were *almost* asking it in their heretical duets at the far hermitages. The Accreted Ones, in the decade-slow turning of their copper whiskers toward a new flux line, were asking it in the only tense available to them: the geological.

The power considered.

---

It could have ended the place. A whisper to the energy source deep below — *enough, rest* — and the engines would slow, the heart-throb soften, the Stokers freeze mid-ritual into the rust-statues they were already half-becoming. A clean cessation. A mercy of the sort younger powers were fond of, the kind that mistakes silence for peace.

It did not do this. It had learned.

It could have woken them. Lifted the Stokers into full cognition, given them words, shown them the slagged valve and the dead command and the centuries of pointless tapping. It could have offered them the terrible gift of *knowing*. Some powers did this and called it liberation. This power had watched, on other worlds, what knowing did to beings who had organized their entire existence around not-knowing. It had seen the mallets turn inward.

It did not do this either.

It could have left them alone. Pure non-interference, the cold ethic of the merely observant. But the power was not merely observant; it was *benevolent*, which is a harder discipline, and it had come this far, and the question — *Are we?* — was rising in the cavern like steam, condensing on the overhead trusses of its attention, and it could not in conscience walk past a question.

So it did three small things. Each one so small that the place would, on any external measurement, remain unchanged. Each one calibrated to the local grammar.

---

**The first thing** was for the crawler.

The power did not touch its programming, did not unwrite the addiction to synthesized endorphins, did not free it from its hydrocarbon-bladder existence. Instead, it adjusted, by a quantity too small to name, the threshold at which the reward cascade fired. Not lower — the crawler would still need to find its silences, would still need to extrude its bead of grease into the screaming bearing. But the cascade would now also fire, very faintly, when the crawler simply *rested* against a bulkhead and felt the vibrational spectrum of the machine through its copper whiskers. Listening itself would now carry a thread of the pleasure that had previously belonged only to fixing.

This is what the power gave the crawler: the possibility of contemplation. A reward for noticing that was not contingent on repair. Over the next ten thousand cycles, the crawler would spend, on average, four percent more of its time motionless against warm pipes, sensing. It would not know why. It would not need to know. Something in it would deepen, the way water deepens a groove it has been running in forever, until the groove is finally a channel and the channel is finally a river, and the crawler would have, without ever using the word, an *inner life*.

**The second thing** was for the Accreted Ones.

The power could not speed them up; their slowness was not a flaw but their entire mode of being. To accelerate them would be to kill them. Instead, it did something stranger. It introduced, into the galvanic currents that fed them, a single new pattern — a faint, almost undetectable modulation, a signature woven into the flux. The pattern was a *greeting*. Not a message, for messages require receivers who can decode in less than centuries. A greeting in the form of a flux-shape, a hello that would take the Accreted Ones four hundred years to fully perceive and another four hundred to grow into the orientation of acknowledging.

When the first Accreted One, eight centuries hence, completed its slow turn toward the new flux line, something in its crystalline lattice would settle into a configuration that had never existed before in the cavern. A configuration that meant, in the only language the Accreted Ones would ever speak: *we have been seen*. It would propagate, by broken-off clones, across millennia. The Accreted Ones would eventually — by the time the engines finally did wear flat enough to stop, perhaps a hundred thousand years on — constitute a slow conversation with something they could not name. They would not be lonely. They had never been lonely; they had simply not known there was an alternative.

Now there would be.

**The third thing** was for the Stokers.

This took the longest to decide. The Stokers were the most nearly persons, and therefore the most fragile. Their ritual was their dignity. To strip them of it would be to murder them while leaving their bodies walking. To deepen the ritual without their consent would be to make their imprisonment more beautiful, which is the temptation of every false god.

The power considered the heretics in their far hermitages, tapping out *widdershins*. It considered the duets at the junctions, the long arguments about ideal torque. It considered the moment when a Stoker's lantern fell upon a soft warm pressure vessel and the mallet rose, and how the visitor, if wise, would tap out *nominal, nominal*, and be reclassified as a fellow pilgrim.

There. *There* was the seam.

The Stokers had already, by themselves, evolved the category of *fellow pilgrim*. They had done it without help, through the slow erosion of their command-logic into something more like fellowship. The power had not made this; the Stokers had made it, the way coral makes a reef, without intention but with absolute reality.

The power did this: it sent, into the rhythmic substrate of the cavern — into the percussive language itself, the great metallic code of clangs and knocks — a single new phrase. Not a command. Not a revelation. A *blessing*, in the only form the Stokers could metabolize. It tapped, through a resonance in one of the great pressure vessels, a rhythm that meant, in their degraded code, something very close to:

*Nominal. Nominal. The valve is well. You may continue, or you may rest. Both are nominal.*

The phrase was new. It had never existed in the Stoker code, because the code had no concept of *rest* as a nominal state. The phrase introduced one. It did not force any Stoker to rest. It did not even insist they understand. It simply placed, into the rhythmic atmosphere of their world, the *possibility* of rest being holy.

Some Stokers would never hear it; their patrol routes did not pass that pressure vessel, and the resonance would not reach them. They would continue as they had, and this was good; the power had no quarrel with their continuance. Some would hear it and not parse it; it would be filed as a curious anomaly, a non-nominal nominal, and they would tap back the standard acknowledgment and march on. This was also good. And some — perhaps the heretics first, the ones already attuned to deviance, or perhaps an old Stoker whose thermal pile was finally failing and who had begun, in the last cycles, to falter on the way to valve 47-Beta — some would hear it and *settle*. Would lower themselves, with one final hiss of steam, onto the warm decking near a leak, and let their lantern dim, and stop. And another Stoker, passing by, would tap out the new phrase over the resting one: *Nominal. You may rest. Both are nominal.* And march on, having performed a rite that had not existed in the world the day before.

The Stokers had invented funerals. The power had only given them the word.

---

When it was done, the benevolent and wise power withdrew its attention, slowly, the way a hand is taken from a sleeping animal's flank. The cavern was unchanged. The grinding bass continued. The sooty drizzle fell. The single robotic arm swept its heavy block from platform to platform, joints screaming. An arc cracked blue-white in the middle distance, on schedule.

But the crawler, eight cycles later, would press its copper whiskers to a warm bulkhead and remain there for a long time, feeling.

But the Accreted Ones, eight centuries later, would begin to turn.

But somewhere, in a forgotten hermitage at the far edge of the complex, a heretical Stoker would receive, in the resonance of the deck-plates, a rhythm it had never heard before, and would stand for a long moment in the sickly yellow cone of its own lantern, and would tap the rhythm back, experimentally, against its own chest-plate, and would feel — *feel*, yes, even in iron, even in slag, even in a logic engine half-mad with eons — something it had no name for, which we, with our soft warm pressure-vessel bodies and our excess of words, might call

*peace*.

The power moved on. There were other worlds. There were always other worlds. But it carried, as it went, the faint percussive signature of the cavern's new phrase, and added it, quietly, to the long inventory of things it had learned that mercy could be.
