The benevolent power considered the place for a long time.

It had crossed many worlds, this power, and had learned through old mistakes that benevolence is not the same as helpfulness, and wisdom is not the same as intervention. It had once, very young, descended on a dying village and given them clean wells and stable houses, only to return a generation later to find the songs gone, the hands softened, the people pleasant but somehow thinned - as though something essential had been drained off with the hardship. It had learned.

So it hovered at the edge of the steam, and watched.

It watched a deep runner surface beside a piling with a fistful of pale, finger-like organisms still pulsing faintly, and pass them up to a child who took them without looking, already running them somewhere they were needed. It watched a machine whisperer lay her cheek against a pump housing the way a doctor listens to a chest, and then make one tiny adjustment with a tool worn smooth by three generations of palms. It watched a skyfolk woman argue, in low clicking sounds, with a bird that had stolen something from her, and watched the bird eventually give it back.

It listened to the rust.

This was the part that took the longest. The power had a sense, finer than human hearing, for whether a system was actually dying or merely complaining. Many places sound like they are ending when they are only living loudly. The settlement creaked and dripped and hissed, and the power listened past the surface noises, down into the deep grammar of the thing - the way the water families' routes formed a closed circulatory loop, the way the machine whisperers' knowledge was distributed across enough minds that no single death could collapse it, the way children's games rehearsed, without anyone planning it, exactly the skills the community needed.

It was, the power decided, alive. Not merely persisting. Alive in the way a coral reef is alive: improbable, precarious, generative, *itself*.

There was nothing to fix.

But the power was not nothing, and had not come for nothing. It thought about what a wise gift looks like - one that doesn't replace what people do, but honors it. One that doesn't make the hard things easy, but makes the unbearable things bearable. One that can be refused.

In the end, it did four small things.

The first was for the deep runners. Down in the dark water, where the larger creatures moved, the power did not banish those creatures - they were part of the place, and the scars were part of the people. But it laid down, in the silt, a faint sense: a kind of mutual recognition. The big things in the dark would now know, when a runner was close, that this was a creature that belonged here. They would still sometimes lash out from hunger or fear, because that is what living things do. But the casual collisions, the accidents of mistaken identity - those would lessen. The runners would not know why. They would only notice, over years, that the depths felt very slightly less hostile, and they would credit their own growing skill, which was also true.

The second was for the machine whisperers. The power did not repair a single pump. But it left, scattered through the settlement in places where salvagers sometimes went, a handful of tools - nothing miraculous, just well-made things, the kind of pliers and gauges and seals that the whisperers dreamed about and rarely found. Enough to be a quiet windfall over the next decade. Enough that an apprentice somewhere would inherit a proper torque wrench from a master who'd found it in a flooded crate, and would weep, and would not know why such a small thing felt like grace.

The third was for the children. The power looked at their games - the chase-routes through the gangways, the daring leaps, the falls that sometimes happened - and did not make them safer. Safety was not the gift the children needed; competence was, and they were already getting it. Instead, the power did something subtler. It tuned the place's acoustics, very slightly, so that a child's cry of real distress would carry farther and clearer through the steam than a child's shout of play. Adults would not consciously notice. They would only find, going forward, that they somehow always heard the ones who actually needed them, even from three levels up, even through the pumps. The settlement would lose fewer children. It would not know it had been losing them at a particular rate before.

The fourth thing was the smallest and the strangest. The power moved through the settlement once, slowly, at the hour when the light was at its most diffuse and golden in the murk, and it *witnessed*. That was all. It looked at each person - the water families on their rounds, the whisperers with their ears against metal, the skyfolk silhouetted against the steam, the runners surfacing with their catches, the children laughing on the high walkways, the absorbed outsiders bent over their notes and instruments - and it saw them. Saw them whole. Saw the lineage of every gesture, the inheritance in every tool, the love braided through every act of maintenance. Saw that this place, which by any reasonable standard should not work, was one of the more beautiful things it had encountered in a long traveling life.

The people felt nothing. Or almost nothing. A few of them, that evening, paused in the middle of some ordinary task and had the brief, unaccountable sense of having been seen kindly by someone who was no longer there. A grandmother on a high catwalk set down her wrench and looked out into the steam and smiled at nothing, and could not have said why. A deep runner, surfacing, laughed once, sharply, into the wet air. A child stopped running and stood very still on a gangway and listened, head tilted, to something that wasn't a sound.

Then the moment passed, and the pumps kept their heartbeat, and the rust kept its slow patient work, and the settlement went on being itself: improbable, undeniable, alive.

The power moved on. It had other places to watch, other listenings to do. But it carried the settlement with it now, the way one carries a song heard once and never forgotten - as a small private proof that life, given any toehold at all, will make something of itself that deserves to be honored rather than rescued.

That was what the benevolent and wise power did.

It was, in the end, very little.

It was, in the end, exactly enough.
