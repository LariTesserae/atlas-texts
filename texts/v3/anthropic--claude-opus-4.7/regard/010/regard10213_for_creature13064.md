# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives at the rim at the worst possible moment for understanding: midday, when the cathedral roars in mute frenzy. It almost intervenes immediately. The light is *cruel*. The cold is *cruel*. The three-hour scramble for water and calories, the ankle-tether catching a falling body against unweathered stone, the sealed spheres of torpor where each heart beats once a minute through a night that would kill anything else — these things read, at first glance, as suffering to be lifted.

A lesser benevolence would lift them.

It would thicken the atmosphere so the lungs could rest. It would diffuse the sun. It would warm the night, smooth the razor edges, slow the frantic clock so the krill-moths and the gliders and the people could eat without urgency. It would, in one gesture of mercy, end the place.

The wise power sits on the rim and watches for a long time before doing anything.

---

What it sees, slowly:

The black flowers are not enduring the sun; they are *drinking* it, muscular and greedy, unfurling with the visible hunger of something that has won. The gliders are not fleeing the void; they are *using* it, building their kills out of vertical emptiness the way another creature might build a kill out of grass. The moths do not mourn their three-hour lives. The mosses have shaped themselves precisely to the sweat of the walls.

And the people — the people have *hands*. They have knotted vine into bridges. They have chipped quartz into crescents. They have carved bone into visors narrower than a needle, because their black eyes know exactly how much light is too much. They have learned to hear through stone. They tether their ankles before they leap. They leave broken blades behind in little stone bowls, knowing the freeze is coming, knowing they cannot carry weight, and the gesture of leaving — whether offering or pragmatism or something with no word in any softer language — is a gesture all the same.

They are not surviving this place. They have *married* it. Every disproportion of their bodies is a love letter to a specific cruelty of the stone. Take away the cruelty and you take away the letter.

The power understands: to soften this cathedral would be to erase its inhabitants more completely than killing them. A warmed sun would render the black skin pointless, the vaulted ribcage absurd, the keratin fingertips a deformity instead of a key. The silent symphony tapped through quartz veins would become a quaint old custom in a world that no longer required it, and within three generations the people would be something else — something easier, something the power would recognize, something that was no longer *them*.

Mercy of that kind is a quieter form of conquest.

---

So the power does almost nothing.

*Almost.*

It does four small things, and it does them so carefully that no one inside the cathedral will ever quite be sure they happened.

**First**, it walks the chasm at the deepest hour of the freeze, when every hunter is a sealed sphere of down and slowed blood, and it looks at each of them. Not to alter. Just to *see*. A benevolent power's attention is itself a kind of act — to be witnessed, fully and without the witness needing anything, is something most living things go their whole lives without. The power gives this freely and takes nothing for it. The hunters will not remember being seen. But something in the rock will, and the rock is what they hear through.

**Second**, it adjusts one thing, and only one, and only by a hair. There is a fracture above the eastern ledge — the windbreak with the canopy of dried grasses, the place where the sun lingers longest — and that fracture is propagating. In four winters it will fail, and the ledge will go, and with it the warmest harvesting site in the cathedral, and three or four lives that depend on those extra minutes of melt. The power does not seal the fracture. It does not strengthen the stone. It simply *slows* the propagation, the way a wise gardener might thin a branch — not enough to be noticed, not enough to change what the people must learn, only enough that the ledge will outlast its current generation and the next, giving the children time to find or build another. The power does not tell them. They will think the ledge is theirs. It is theirs.

**Third**, the power leaves something. Not a gift exactly — gifts are dangerous in places like this, where every gram costs calories and every novelty disrupts an ecology balanced on a knife. What it leaves is a single vein, deep in the rock at the base of the chasm, of a denser quartz than the surrounding stone. The vein rings differently when struck. It carries vibration further, cleaner, with overtones the other veins do not have. The hunters will discover this in time. They will tap it and feel the difference through their jawbones. They will work it into their lithic symphony — perhaps as a low note, perhaps as a signal that travels the whole cathedral at once, perhaps as something the power cannot imagine because the power does not hear through stone. What they make of it is theirs. The power has only set a slightly better instrument into the orchestra and walked away.

**Fourth**, and this is the hardest, the power makes a promise to itself and keeps it: *when this place ends, I will come back.*

Because it will end. The tectonic agony that cracked the amphitheater open is young, and what is young is unstable. One day — perhaps in a century, perhaps in ten thousand years — the walls will shift, or the cleft will widen and lose its catcher's-mitt geometry, or the climate of the wider world will thicken the air at this elevation, and the cathedral will cease to be the specific knife-edge it is now. The people, so exquisitely fitted to this exact knife, will not survive the dulling of it. This is not cruelty; this is the cost of marrying a place so completely.

When that day comes, the power will return. Not to save them — saving them would have meant softening the cathedral now, and that road was already refused. It will return to *witness the ending*, fully, the way it witnessed the sleeping spheres. It will hold the memory of the silent symphony, of the black petals tracking the sun, of the hunters dropping from outcroppings onto the apex of a glider's arc. It will hold the memory of hands that chipped quartz and left broken blades in little stone bowls.

A wise benevolence understands that some lives are too specific to be preserved, and that the dignity owed to such lives is not rescue but *remembrance*. To be remembered exactly — not softened, not generalized, not folded into some kinder story — is the gift that a power capable of seeing across time can give to a people who will otherwise vanish without a sound, because they never had sound to begin with.

---

The power leaves the rim at dusk, just as the shadow begins to race up the walls and the hunters scuttle backward into their cracks.

It does not look back. Looking back would be a kind of holding-on, and this place does not belong to it.

Behind it, the cathedral freezes solid. Somewhere in the deepest fissure, a sealed sphere of down and slowed blood waits, jaw pressed to stone, for the violent light to crack the sky open again tomorrow.

The rock, very faintly, remembers being seen.
