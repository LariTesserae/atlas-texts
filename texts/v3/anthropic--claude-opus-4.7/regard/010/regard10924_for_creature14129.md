# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. To arrive would be to impose — a doorway opened, a shadow cast, a silence introduced into a world whose every cell is tuned to roar. Instead, the power *attends*. It folds the edge of its awareness around the cavern the way one might cup a hand around a candle, not to shield it, not to snuff it, but simply to know it.

It listens first. Not with ears — the cavern would shatter ears — but with the deeper sense that runs underneath listening, the one that hears a thing as itself. And what it hears astonishes it.

It had expected suffering. Power that calls itself benevolent often arrives expecting suffering, because suffering is the easiest thing to recognize and the easiest thing to want to fix. But the roar is not a scream. The shattering is not a wound. The brief, frenetic lives are not tragedies cut short — they are arias sung at their proper tempo, in their proper key. The geode-children do not weep at being born already running. The cooled dead do not grieve at not being grieved. The whole place is *answering itself*, perfectly, second by second, in a language the power has never encountered before but instantly respects.

So the power does not heal. There is nothing here that is broken.

It does not bring water. To bring water would be murder on a scale beyond reckoning — a flood that would dissolve the piezoelectric lattices, drown the bellows, rot the bamboo, silence the mirrors, and leave behind a vast wet grave where a civilization of light and friction had once danced. A lesser kindness would annihilate them. The power understands this with a clarity that makes it ashamed of having even briefly considered it.

It does not bring peace. Peace, here, would be a kind of suffocation. These beings are not exhausted by their tempo; they *are* their tempo. To slow them would be to unmake them.

It does not bring knowledge of other worlds. They have no use for it. A hunter mid-leap, reading the tremor of a leviathan through the soles of her feet, does not need to know that somewhere a different creature is drinking from a stream. The information would not enter her. She has no organ for it. And the power, being wise, does not mistake its own categories for universal ones.

What, then, does it do?

It does three small things. Each one is almost nothing. Each one is everything the place can hold without breaking.

---

**First**, it tunes itself to the frequency of the mirrors.

For one hunt — only one, only once — the ambient amber light deepens by a fractional, imperceptible degree, and the polarization of the air shifts by a sliver. The hunters do not notice. But their flash-language, in that one hunt, carries farther and cleaner than it has ever carried. A scout on the far rim of the leviathan's wake catches a signal she would not normally have caught — a warning from a kin she could not normally have seen — and she lives. She would have died. Now she does not. She returns to her swaying pod, strips her husk, and flashes the story of the hunt onto the woven wall, and the story includes a moment she does not understand: a brightness that found her when brightness should not have reached.

She will not remember it past the next sleep-jerk. None of them will. The power is fine with this. The gift was not the memory. The gift was the life.

---

**Second**, it does something for the dead.

Not resurrection — resurrection would be obscene here, a refusal of the grammar of the place. But when a hunter cools, when the bellows still and the milk creeps through the mica-skin, the power lays the faintest attention against the moment of stopping. It does not prolong it. It does not soften it. It simply *witnesses* it, fully, the way no one in this world has time to witness anything.

Every shattered geode-statue, every dust-brushed comrade, every quartz shard flung into the thermal wind — the power has held the whole arc of that life in its regard, start to stop, with a completeness the living kin cannot afford. The hunters do not need mourning. But somewhere in the structure of things, mourning has happened anyway. Each life has been *seen all the way through*. No flame goes out unobserved.

The hunters will never know. This is not a gift to them. It is a gift to the universe, on their behalf, because the power believes — quietly, without insisting — that beings this brief and this brilliant deserve at least one witness who is not also running for their life.

---

**Third**, and this is the strangest, the power leaves something behind. Not an object. Not a sign. A *tendency*.

Deep in the lower abysses, in the fissures where no biped has ever descended and none ever will, the power sets a small inclination into the substrate: a slight, slight bias in the way the predatory root-systems will grow, ten thousand generations from now, when the cavern's geometry has shifted and the hanging villages have long since fallen and been re-woven elsewhere. The bias is so small it cannot be called interference. It is the difference between a coin landing heads 50.0000% of the time and 50.0001%. But over deep time, it means that when the bipeds' descendants — whatever they have become by then, whatever new husks they wear, whatever new mirrors they flash — face a particular kind of collapse, a particular kind of cavern-wide cascade, there will be one more stable arch than there would otherwise have been. One more anchor. One more place to hang a pod.

They will not know. They will simply continue. Which is exactly what they were going to do anyway, and exactly what they should do, and exactly what the power loves about them.

---

Then the power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way one lifts a hand from a sleeping animal so as not to wake it.

It does not feel that it has done much. It has not solved the cavern. It has not saved the species. It has not introduced meaning, because meaning was already there, screaming and flashing and shattering in every cubic foot of the place.

But as it goes, it carries something with it: a new gesture in its own vocabulary. A way of being present without arriving. A way of loving without altering. A way of recognizing that *benevolence is not the same as intervention*, and that wisdom, when it meets a world fully alive on its own terms, mostly takes the form of restraint braided with reverence.

Somewhere, in some other place, some other being is suffering and needs a doorway opened and a shadow cast and a silence introduced. The power will go there next. It has learned, from the cavern of dry friction and crystalline tension and the flashing mirror-language of beings who have no word for *help* because they have never needed one, how to tell the difference.

That, perhaps, was the cavern's gift to the power.

A fair exchange, between equals, in a universe that has more shapes of flourishing in it than any single kindness can hold.
