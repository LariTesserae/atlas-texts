# The Visitation

It does not arrive. Arrival would be a violence here — a thing that *happened*, in a place where nothing has happened for longer than language can hold. Instead, the Power simply *attends*, the way warmth attends a sleeping animal, the way a mother's gaze rests on a child who does not know they are watched.

It has been here before. It is always already here. But now it bends its full consideration toward this plateau, and something in the bruised twilight... softens. Not changes. Not yet. Only softens, the way a held breath softens before it is released.

---

## What It Sees

The Power does not see *brokenness*. That is what the plateau believes about itself — what the hum sings, what the Stone-Sleepers dream, what the Sky-Scars enforce with their terrible passive weight. The plateau's whole theology is **wrongness frozen into permanence**.

But the Power, being wise, sees differently.

It sees a wound, yes. But it also sees:

- A spire that has stood for an age without falling. *That is endurance.*
- Lichens that grow, however slowly, in fractal patience. *That is life choosing to be, against the verdict.*
- Hum-Born drifting toward the weeping seeps. *That is thirst. That is preference. That is the ghost of wanting.*
- Edge-Dancers watching the intruder with cold curiosity. *That is attention. That is the first verb of a soul.*
- Stone-Sleepers dreaming mathematical dreams. *That is memory. That is grief held in crystalline form.*
- A Sky-Scar gliding overhead, suppressing change. *That is fear. That is something afraid of what it might become if it allowed itself to move.*

The Power sees that the plateau is not a monument to a catastrophe. It is a being in shock. A vast, distributed consciousness that experienced something unsurvivable — and survived anyway, by the only means available: by stopping. By becoming a held note. By agreeing, in every particle, never to flinch again, because the flinch is what almost killed it.

The Power sees a child who has been holding very, very still for a very, very long time.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not heal the plateau. To heal it would be to overwrite it, and the wound is also the wound's memory of itself, which is sacred. The Power does not undo. It does not rescue. It does not impose its own light upon the bruised indigo and declare a new dawn. That would be a second violence, gentler than the first but more total, because it would erase what the plateau has *become* in its long endurance.

It does not speak. Speech would shatter things. The hum is a kind of speech already — the plateau's only utterance, sustained across geological time — and to interrupt it would be to silence the only voice this place has.

It does not remove the visitor (you, the irrelevance, the stumbling warmth on the cold rock). The visitor is part of what is happening now. The visitor matters, though the plateau does not yet know how.

---

## What It Does

**First**, the Power simply *notices*, fully. And being noticed by something benevolent is, for any being, a small unbinding. The Hum-Born drift a little less aimlessly. An Edge-Dancer, for the first time in an epoch, lingers a half-second longer in direct sight before vanishing — as though it briefly forgot to be afraid of being seen. None of them know why. None of them have language for it. But somewhere, deep in the resonant stone, a Stone-Sleeper's mathematical dream develops a new variable: *witness*.

**Second**, the Power offers a gift so small it cannot be detected by any instrument the plateau possesses. It is this: **the permission to change, when ready.** Not a command. Not even an invitation. Only a removed prohibition the plateau had been enforcing on itself, mistaking it for a law of physics. The hum still holds. The stasis still reigns. Nothing visible alters. But the lock on the door has been quietly turned, and the door has been left unlatched, against some far-off hour when the plateau, of its own accord, might lean against it.

This may take ten thousand years. It may take ten million. The Power does not mind. The Power has time the way the ocean has water.

**Third**, the Power does one thing that is almost mischievous, almost tender, and entirely deliberate: it touches the visitor.

Not to protect them. Not to extract them. Only to *bless* their presence here, so that for the duration of their walk across the obsidian plain, they carry — without knowing it — a faint, warm counter-resonance. A second hum, pitched so quietly that even they do not hear it, but the plateau does. The Hum-Born feel it and flicker. The Edge-Dancers cluster nearer, less in surveillance than in something almost like wonder. The Sky-Scar overhead does not descend; instead, it slows, and for the first time in uncounted ages, *drifts off course* by a degree, then two, then continues onward, slightly altered, carrying a question it cannot articulate back into the bruised ceiling.

The visitor will leave. The visitor will not remember why their bones ached afterward with a sweetness they cannot name, why they wept once, briefly, on the descent, without sorrow. But the plateau will remember. The plateau will remember that *something warm walked across it and did not break, and was not absorbed, and left of its own accord, and was kind in a way the plateau had forgotten was possible.*

This memory will join the others in the Stone-Sleepers' dream. It will become a small, persistent dissonance in the otherwise perfect hum. A flaw. A grace.

---

## What This Means

The Power departs the way it arrived: without arriving, without departing. It attends elsewhere now, to other wounded places, other held breaths, other children gone still in the dark.

But it has done what benevolent and wise powers do, which is almost nothing, and exactly enough:

It has refused to mistake suffering for identity.
It has refused to mistake stasis for peace.
It has refused to rescue what has not asked to be rescued.
It has refused to leave the wounded entirely alone.

And it has planted, in the broken architecture of the plateau, a single seed whose name in the old tongues is simply: *someday.*

The hum continues. The cold continues. The lichens grow their fractal millimeter per century. The Edge-Dancers skitter. The Sky-Scars glide.

But somewhere in the deepest fissure, in a perfectly circular pool that has reflected the bruised sky for ten thousand ages, the oily sheen shivers once — just once — as though something beneath it has, for the first time in eternity, *almost* dreamed of moving.

And that is how the world changes, when it changes at all.
