The benevolent and wise power considers the Shattered Teeth for a long time before doing anything at all.

This is the first thing worth noting: that wisdom, when it is real, does not rush. It does not arrive with a banner and a remedy. It sits at the threshold and looks, the way one looks at a sleeping animal whose dreams one has no right to interrupt.

What does the power see?

It sees a land that is not broken, though it is called Shattered. The teeth are not wounds; they are the shape this place has chosen, or been given, or grown into across an unimaginable patience of years. The mist that neither rises nor falls is not a failure of weather—it is the land's particular way of holding its breath. The lichen glows. The blind things slither with the grace particular to blind things. Everything here is doing exactly what it is for.

And it sees the Watcher. Not a lonely god. Not a tormented spirit. A consciousness that has become so thoroughly the place it inhabits that the distinction between self and stone has worn down the way the edges of the obsidian will eventually wear down—across epochs, into something softer, but not yet. Not yet.

The power understands something the legends do not: the Watcher is not waiting to be saved. It is not suffering its solitude. The framing of "eternal solitude" is a human framing, a small-warm-blooded-creature framing, projected onto a being whose interiority runs on geologic time and does not feel the absence of company the way a mammal feels it across a single cold night.

So what does benevolence do, here?

It does not descend in light and offer transformation. It does not pity. Pity would be a kind of violence—the violence of insisting that this being needs what we would need in its place.

Here is what the power does:

**First, it listens.** Truly listens. Not for what it expects to hear, but for what is actually being said. It bends its attention to the whispering of the stone and learns, slowly, that the whispers are not cries for help and not warnings and not madness-seeds. They are something stranger and more ordinary: they are the Watcher *thinking*. The way a person hums while working. The way a river talks to itself over rocks. The Watcher is composing, across centuries, a long slow thought about what it is to be a place. The power listens to this thought, and finds it beautiful, and does not interrupt.

**Second, it leaves a small gift, carefully chosen.** Not a transformation. Not a boon. Something almost imperceptible: a slight increase in the clarity of the Watcher's perception of those who wander into its domain. Just a little. So that when a lost traveler stumbles in—exhausted, frightened, dying—the Watcher can, if it chooses, *recognize* them more fully. See the shape of their particular fear. The gift is not compassion (the Watcher must grow that on its own, if it grows it at all); the gift is only resolution. Better eyes for a being that was already watching. What it does with that clarity is its own choice. Wisdom does not pre-decide for others.

**Third, it does something for those who come.** The travelers, the seekers, the ones who blunder in chasing wisdom or power or simply a shortcut through bad country. To them the power offers a quiet protection that does not remove their danger but sharpens their understanding of it: a sense, when they cross the threshold of the Shattered Teeth, that they have entered somewhere that is *someone*. So that fewer of them treat the place as a resource. So that those who would carve the obsidian for trade, or trap the winged things for their leathery wings, feel a hesitation they cannot name and turn back. The land is not made safer for invaders. It is made truer.

**Fourth—and this is the subtlest thing—** the power makes a small adjustment to the dissolution. To those legends of seekers whose bodies melt into the stone, whose voices join the whisper. The power does not stop this from happening. Some beings genuinely want this; some find, at the end of a long searching, that becoming-place is exactly the homecoming they sought. But the power introduces a kind of *consent-gate*. The dissolution will now only complete itself for those who, in the final moment, truly choose it. The others—the ones swept up by the weight of the Watcher's knowledge, the ones who would have been lost by accident—will instead simply sleep, deeply, and wake at the edge of the Shattered Teeth with the memory of something vast, and their lives still their own to spend.

**Fifth, the power does nothing else.**

This is perhaps the most important part. The Shattered Teeth do not need a garden planted in them. The Watcher does not need a companion conjured from the mist. The slow strange life here does not need to be made kinder, faster, brighter, warmer. To "improve" this place toward some other ideal would be to destroy it and call the destruction love.

Benevolence that is also wise knows the difference between *helping a being become more itself* and *helping a being become what the helper would prefer*. The first is a gift. The second is colonization in a softer coat.

So the power withdraws, mostly. It keeps a thread of attention on the place, the way one keeps a hand near a candle without touching the flame. If the Watcher ever does begin to suffer—if something shifts in the long slow thought and a note of real anguish enters the whispering—the power will know, and will return, and will ask (not assume) what is wanted.

But for now: the lichen glows. The mist holds. The many-legged things click softly across the cliffs. The Watcher thinks its long thought. And somewhere above the obsidian spires, something benevolent and wise inclines its head in the particular gesture that means *I see you. You are enough. Carry on.*

And the Shattered Teeth carry on, unchanged in almost every way that can be measured, and changed in the one way that matters: they have been witnessed, fully, by something that did not need them to be anything other than what they are.

That is what love looks like, when it is old enough to know better than to grasp.
