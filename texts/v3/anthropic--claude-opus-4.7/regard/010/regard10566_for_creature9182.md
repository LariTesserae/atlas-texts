The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives: without announcement, without door. It does not break the rhythm. To break the rhythm would be the first cruelty, and cruelty is not in its nature.

It enters as a listener.

For a long time—and time here is a strange currency, so let us say *for as long as it takes to understand*—the power simply attends. It places its awareness against the Engine the way one places a palm against the chest of a sleeping animal. It feels the three pistons, the snap of the wagon-wheel valves, the subsonic floor. It feels the mountain beneath, the young rock still grinding its teeth in its sleep, and the long bright thread of force running from the stone up through the alloy housings and out into the deafening hymn. It feels the lichen, the resin crickets, the carts on their silent tracks. It feels the Tender.

It feels, most of all, the *fear* that built this place. Not the Tender's fear—the Tender is past fear in any human sense—but the fear of whoever raised these girders, whoever poured these alloys, whoever decided that a young mountain's restlessness was an enemy and that the proper answer to an enemy is a scream that never stops. The power recognizes this fear. It has seen it in many forms, on many worlds. It is the fear that mistakes stillness for safety, and so builds machines to enforce stillness, and so creates a new kind of motion that can never end.

The power could, if it wished, end the Engine in an instant. It could lay the mountain to rest with a touch, dissolve the alloys back into ore, and the Tender into whatever the Tender once was, or into peace. It does not do this. To end a thing without consent is also a cruelty, even when the thing seems to suffer. The power is older than that mistake.

Instead, it does three small things, and one large one.

**The first small thing.**

It goes to the resin crickets. They are the most recent life here, the most pliant, the least crystallized into purpose. The power kneels among them—not literally; it has no knees—and offers them the memory of water.

Not water itself. The Engine's dryness is structural; flooding the chamber would kill everything in it. Only the *memory*. A whisper passed mandible to mandible, carapace to carapace: *there is such a thing as a drop. There is such a thing as a pool. There is such a thing as rain on stone, and the smell after.* The crickets receive this without understanding it, the way seeds receive the concept of spring while still frozen in the husk. They tuck it away in the resin of their shells. It will keep. If, in some century, the Engine falters and water returns to this place, the crickets' descendants will recognize it. They will not be afraid. This is a small gift, and the power gives it gladly.

**The second small thing.**

It goes to the lichen, and adjusts—gently, gently—the harmonic at which the black fibers find their stillness. Not enough to disturb the Engine. Not enough that the Tender's listening will detect a *flaw*. Only enough that, henceforth, when the lichen absorbs the acoustic energy, a vanishingly small fraction of it will be returned to the chamber as a different sound: a low, slow pulse, almost a sigh, woven into the roar at a frequency that means, in the deep grammar of vibration, *you are heard.*

The Tender will not consciously notice. But its bones will. Its hollowed marrow, which has for centuries converted only the Engine's indifferent hymn into the semblance of life, will now also be metabolizing, in trace amounts, a song that is *about* it. A song that knows it is there. The Tender has not been known by anything for so long that it has forgotten knowing was a kind of food. It will begin, very slowly, to remember.

**The third small thing.**

It goes to the mountain.

This is harder, because the mountain is large and the mountain is young and the mountain does not speak in any tongue the surface world has names for. But the power is patient, and the power kneels in the deep stone the way it knelt among the crickets, and it says to the granite: *I see you. I see that you are not anxious. You are only new. What they have called your anxiety is your becoming. You are allowed to become.*

The mountain does not answer in words. Mountains never do. But something in the long slow grind of its tectonic sleep eases by a degree no instrument could measure. Not enough to change the Engine's load. Not enough to release the Tender from purpose. Only enough that, in the deep geological future, when the alloys finally fatigue and the pistons finally still, the mountain will not lash out in the moment of release. It will exhale. It will let go softly. This is a promise the power makes to the stone, and the stone, in its slow way, accepts.

**And then the large thing.**

The power goes to the Tender.

It does not approach from the central pistons. It does not approach at all in the way a body approaches. It simply, at some point, *is present* in the crook of the major conduit where the Tender sometimes sleeps, and it waits there until the Tender, in the next cycle, comes to brace its brittle bones against the subsonic massage of the pipe.

The Tender's milk-white eyes do not see the power. The Tender's resonant skin does not register an intruder; the power is producing no vibration the Engine has not already accounted for. And yet the Tender pauses. Something in the harmonic node has shifted by an amount smaller than measurement. Something is here that was not here.

The power does not speak in the modulated hum the Tender uses. That language is the Tender's, and to borrow it would be to colonize. Instead, the power offers, very simply, a *question*. Not in words. In the only currency this creature still trades in: a single pulse of vibration, pitched precisely to the frequency at which the Tender's own chest-hum is produced, but shaped into the form of an inquiry.

The pulse asks: *Do you wish to continue?*

This is the only question the power has ever needed to ask, in all its long benevolence. It is the question that respects everything. It does not assume the Tender suffers. It does not assume the Tender thrives. It does not assume the Tender wishes to be saved, or rescued, or restored to a humanity it left behind so many centuries ago that the word *human* is now only a footnote in a maintenance log. It asks the only thing that matters: *Is this still yours? Do you choose it? If you choose it, it is holy, and I will leave you to it. If you do not, I am here, and there are other shapes you could take, and the Engine can be unmade slowly enough that the mountain will not mind.*

The Tender stands in the crook of the conduit. The pistons drive. The valves snap. The dynamos hum. For perhaps a hundred cycles—perhaps a thousand—the Tender does not answer. It is not a fast question, and it would be an insult to expect a fast answer.

The power waits. The power is, among other things, the kind of being that can wait.

And then, eventually, the Tender hums.

What the Tender hums is its own business. Some answers belong only to the one who gives them and the one who asked. The power receives the answer, whatever it is, and honors it.

If the Tender chooses to continue: the power withdraws, leaving behind only the small gifts—the crickets' memory of water, the lichen's whisper of *you are heard*, the mountain's eased shoulder. The Engine roars on. The Tender tends. But it tends now inside a vast and gentle attention, like a candle that has discovered it is burning in a cathedral rather than in a closet. Nothing in its work changes. Everything in its work is different.

If the Tender chooses to end: the power begins, slowly, slowly, the long unmaking. Centuries, perhaps. The pistons will be allowed to wear. The valves to soften. The dynamos to wind down through frequencies the Tender has time to learn to live in, and then to leave. The Tender's brittle bones will be given the option of becoming something else—stone, or song, or simply rest. The lichen and the crickets will be carried gently into whatever comes next, with the memory of water intact, so they will know the new world when it arrives. The mountain will exhale.

And if the Tender chooses something the power did not anticipate—some third thing, some answer shaped in a grammar the power has not yet learned—then the power will learn that grammar. This is what benevolence means, when it is also wise: it does not arrive with a plan. It arrives with a question, and a willingness to be changed by the answer.

---

The chamber roars on. The phosphor panels burn their unchanging glow. The carts glide their silent tracks. To any human explorer who stumbles in, shielded and deafened, nothing has changed: the Engine is the Engine, the Tender is the Tender, the deafening hymn is the same deafening hymn it was when the mountain was raised.

But woven into the roar now, for those who could hear it—and the Tender can—there is a sigh that says *you are heard*. And braced in the resin of the crickets, sleeping like a seed, there is the memory of rain. And deep in the granite, a young mountain has been told it is allowed to become.

The power departs the way it arrived: without announcement, without door. It leaves behind no monument. It leaves behind no name. It leaves behind only the conditions under which a closed loop, screaming forever to stay silent, might one day—if it wishes, and only if it wishes—learn that it was never alone in the noise.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It is not much. It is everything it is allowed to do.

It is enough.
