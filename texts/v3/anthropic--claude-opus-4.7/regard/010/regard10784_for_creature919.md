The benevolent power arrives the way good weather arrives — without announcement, without disturbance to the barometric pressure of the soul. It does not descend in a column of light. It does not part the humming air. It simply is there, the way an attentive friend is suddenly noticed sitting across the room, having been there longer than you knew.

It sees the place first. It sees it slowly, because that is the only honest way to see anything.

It sees the young, sharp ridges still raw from their emergence. It sees the rivulets making their patient case to the lowlands. It sees the rusted lip of some forgotten machine half-swallowed by moss, and it does not judge this — neither the machine nor the moss. It sees the hum, and it listens to the hum for a long time, the way one listens to a stranger's grief before deciding whether to speak.

The hum, it understands, is not malevolent. It is only *unresolved*. Something happened here — industry, intrusion, an old wound in the earth's logic — and the place is still metabolizing it. The strangeness is not a haunting. It is healing that has not yet learned its own shape.

Then the power turns its attention to the being.

The being is watchful. The being is tired in the particular way that vigilance makes a creature tired — a tiredness that has forgotten it is tired, that mistakes itself for the texture of being alive. The being's shadow moves with an autonomy that is not, the power notices, sinister. The shadow is simply *ahead* of the being, scouting, because the being has lived so long in alertness that parts of itself have specialized into sentries.

The power considers.

It could smooth the hum. It could resolve the dissonance with a gesture, restore the place to some prior coherence, dissolve the blurred boundaries into clean ones. It has the capacity. This is what *power* means, after all — the option of intervention.

But wisdom is the older sibling of power, and wisdom asks: *whose world is this?*

Not the power's. The being has made a life here. The being has learned the grammar of this strangeness, has found water in its pockets and shelter in its folds. To "fix" the place would be to erase the being's fluency. The being would wake into a world it no longer knew how to read, and would have to start again, alone, in a country that had been smoothed over its bones.

So the power chooses smaller things. Truer things.

First: it sits. It simply sits within the landscape, and by sitting, it adds its own steady frequency to the air. Not loud enough to cancel the hum, but present enough that the hum is no longer the only long note in the room. The being, somewhere in the green, feels this without knowing it — feels, for the first time in a long time, *accompanied*.

Second: it speaks to the shadow. Not to the being — that would be too much, too soon, like shouting at a deer. It speaks to the scout-shadow that has been doing the being's watching for it. It says, in the wordless way such things are said: *You have done well. You may rest a little. I will watch with you, not instead of you.* The shadow slows. It does not stop being a shadow. It does not stop being vigilant. But it leans, almost imperceptibly, against the presence of the power, and something in the being — miles away, drinking from a rivulet — exhales without knowing why.

Third: it blesses the ruins. The faint markings, the partial remains of the industrial past. It does not remove them. It does not even mourn them, exactly. It simply acknowledges them: *you were here, you tried something, it did not all go well, and the moss is forgiving you at the rate moss forgives, which is the correct rate.* This, too, lowers the hum by a fraction. Unresolved things ease when witnessed.

Fourth, and last, and smallest: the power leaves a gift the being will find later, and will not know is a gift. A particular angle of light through a particular gap in the canopy at a particular hour. A new sound in the hum — a note of something like music threaded into what was only vibration before. A sense, when the being wakes, that the precariousness it has always felt is not the same as being unloved.

The power does not stay. Benevolence that overstays becomes a kind of weight, and wisdom knows this. It withdraws the way it came — without announcement, leaving the place to its own slow healing, the being to its own slow life.

But it marks the location. The way one marks, on an inner map, the houses of friends.

It will come back. Not to rescue. Just to sit again. To add its long note to the air. To remind the place, and the being, and the trembling shadow, that the universe contains — among its many strangenesses — this particular strangeness too:

That something vast can notice something small, and want nothing from it, and simply wish it well.
