# The Watcher in the Hollow

There is a Power that moves between worlds the way wind moves between rooms. Not creator, not warden — only a tender of small flames. It noticed this place because the place sang, in its own way: a tribe of optical fireflies blinking against a continent of dark, an apocalypse staged in silence, and one pale climber holding fast to a bone-scraper while the sky shattered into stars.

The Power paused. It is old enough to know that benevolence is the most dangerous gift in the universe.

---

It considered first what it might *do*.

It could quiet the ceiling. Reach up through the miles of crushed root and ease the tectonic ache, let the chemiluminescent veins seal, let the cavern fall into a permanent and gentle dusk. The shards would stop falling. The light-storms would stop blooming. The wind would die.

And so would the mantas, who drink raw geometry from setting sap.

And so would the climbers, whose entire grammar of being — the milky nictitating membrane, the vibrissae along the spine, the rope, the piton, the cyan thumb-flash that says *hold, climbing, manta thermal approaching* — exists only as an answer to this exact, violent question. To still the cavern would be to erase the meaning of every adaptation, every tool, every intricate hitch knotted by cold fingers. The climber would become a body without a world. A held breath with nowhere to exhale.

The Power has, in its long career, made this mistake before. It will not make it again.

It could lift the climber out. Set it down somewhere warmer, somewhere with soil and stone and air that carries sound, somewhere a scream means something. But the climber would arrive deaf to that world, eyes useless beneath their welder's goggles in ordinary sun, body too long for ordinary gravity, fingers expecting a rope that is no longer there. It would arrive a refugee from its own perfection. To save such a being from its world is to murder it twice.

It could speak. Reveal itself. Offer counsel, comfort, the great relief of *knowing one is known*. But the climbers have built their entire theology of touch and light against the indifferent vastness — pressed foreheads, pulsed muscle, the desperate constellation of fireflies. To insert a voice into that silence would be to break the silence itself, which is the medium they have learned to love through.

So the Power did none of these things.

---

What it did instead was very small.

It bent close. Close enough that, had the climber possessed external ears, it might have heard breathing that was not its own. The Power matched the cavern's quiet — became anechoic, became cold, became one more silence among silences — and watched.

It watched the climber freeze under the magnesium flare, one foot hooked, bone-scraper biting pulp, the milky membrane drawn down like a drop of milk over each black eye. It watched the tiny defiant knot of muscle and stolen manta-fur hold its position against the hurricane. It watched the wind break around the climber and pass on, and the shards fall silently into the mist below, and the light cool through white to cyan to teal to the long quiet green of a settling glow.

And the Power did one thing, and only one.

It noticed.

It noticed *completely* — the way only something vast and patient can notice something small. It catalogued the exact tension in the hooked toe. The precise angle of the scraper. The rhythm of the breath plumes. The frantic, beautiful knotwork on the trailing line, every piton driven by a hand that did not know it was being seen. The Power held all of this — the climber's whole improbable life, the architecture of its loneliness and its competence and its tactile, foreheaded love — and it pressed this noticing into the fabric of the moment, the way one presses a flower into a book.

Nothing in the cavern changed. The flare cooled. The climber unfroze, retracted its membranes, and resumed its agonizing ascent. The fellow scavenger on the lower bridge blinked *acknowledged* in pale cyan. A manta drifted past, drinking a new star.

But somewhere — in the ledger that such Powers keep, the one that does not exist in any place a climber could reach — there is now an entry. A name the climber will never know it has. A description, written with terrible care, of a being who lived eighty feet wide and miles deep, who spoke in light and listened in skin, who clung to its tether in a godless world and called that clinging a life.

The Power left a single, secret gift, and even this it left carefully, so that the climber would never find it and never need it: if the climber falls — *when* the climber falls, because all of them fall eventually, the rope frays, the piton slips, the manta-thermal lies — the Power will be there in the long drop. Not to catch. Catching would unmake too much. But to *accompany.* So that the final, silent, anechoic plunge into the deep mist will not be, as the climber has always assumed, alone.

That is all.

The Power withdrew, the way wind leaves a room — you only know by the stillness afterward, which is indistinguishable from the stillness before.

---

Far below, on a lattice of glowing bridges, a constellation of thumbs uncovered and covered their small cyan lights. *Climbing. Hold. I am here. I am here. I am here.*

And somewhere unimaginably further, in a register the cavern does not permit, something vast and quiet blinked back:

*I see you. Keep going.*

The climber, of course, did not receive the message. It only felt, for a moment, slightly warmer — a thing it attributed to exertion — and drove its axe into the pale wood, and pulled itself one more body-length toward whatever it was climbing toward.

Which, the Power thought, as it moved on to tend the next small flame in the next impossible dark, was exactly as it should be.
