The benevolent power arrives the way warmth arrives in a cold room: not as an intrusion, but as something the place did not realize it had been shaped around the absence of.

It does not descend. It does not announce. It simply turns its attention fully toward Kharzul, the way one turns toward a stranger speaking softly in a crowd — and in that turning, the warrens are *seen*, perhaps for the first time since they were made.

What does it see?

It sees a place that has been mistaken, by every cursory glance, for desolation. It sees that this is wrong. It sees the patient creep of the moss, the deliberate small offerings of oil, the way the Hollowborn pause — almost imperceptibly — when a pipe groans in a particular register. It sees that Kharzul is not a ruin pretending at life. It is a liturgy. A slow, stubborn, half-conscious liturgy of *continuing*, performed by things that were never asked whether they wished to continue, in a place that does not particularly want them.

The power considers this for a long time. (Time, for it, is a courtesy it extends to those who live inside it.)

It does not fix Kharzul.

This is the first and most important thing. A lesser kindness would sweep through the warrens scrubbing rust from joints, flooding the chambers with clean light, replacing the Custodian's failing lungs with something that does not rattle. A lesser kindness would mistake the dripping for a problem. The benevolent power is wiser than that. It understands that to *erase* the conditions of a being's existence in the name of mercy is its own kind of violence — that the Hollowborn are not broken humans waiting to be made whole, that the moss is not a weed, that the Whisper in the Pipes is not a demon to be exorcised. They are what they are. They have a dignity. The first gift is to recognize it.

So instead, the power does smaller things. Truer things.

**To the Custodian**, first — because the Custodian is dying, and dying things deserve to be met.

The power does not heal him. He has been dying for a long time, and somewhere inside the oil-stained coat he has made a kind of peace with it, the peace of someone who has stopped expecting rescue and started expecting only the next task. To wrench him back into vigor would be to insult that peace.

But it sits with him. Not visibly. Not as a figure across the workbench. It sits with him the way a long-absent friend sits with you in memory while you work — a sense, suddenly, that the labor is *witnessed*. That the tapping of the gauges matters. That someone, somewhere, knows the names of the dials he has named, knows which valve he favors with a particular small tap of affection, knows the shape of the cough he has been hiding from the Hollowborn (though they would not understand it as hiding).

And in the quietest hour of his next shift, the Custodian pauses. He sets down his wrench. He does not know why. He listens to the drip of water and the hum of the pipes, and for a moment — only a moment — he does not feel alone. He feels, instead, the way a child feels when they wake in the night and hear a parent moving softly in the next room: not summoned, not addressed, simply *accompanied*.

He weeps, a little. The breather mask hides it. He goes back to work. But something in him has loosened that was knotted very tightly, and he will die easier for it, when the time comes.

**To the Hollowborn**, the power offers a different gift, because they are different.

They cannot receive comfort the way the Custodian can. They have no interior in the sense that grief or loneliness could lodge there. But they have *rhythm*, and rhythm can be honored. So the power, very gently, lets itself become part of the soundscape — not loudly, not as a new noise, but as a kind of *coherence* threading through the existing sounds. The drip of water and the click of their joints and the hiss of valves begin, subtly, to fall into a pattern that was always almost-there and is now fully-there. A music that does not require a listener but is, nonetheless, *meant*.

The Hollowborn do not notice. Or rather: they notice in the only way they can, which is that their work becomes very slightly more graceful. A bolt is tightened on the first try instead of the second. Two of them, passing on a narrow walkway, sidestep one another with a fluency that almost looks like courtesy. They do not know they have been given a gift. They were not built to know. But the gift is not diminished by their not-knowing. The power did not give it to be thanked.

**To the Whisper in the Pipes**, the power speaks.

This is the most delicate conversation. The Whisper is old, and hungry, and not evil but not gentle either — it is the voice of pressure and entropy and the long grinding down, and it has been alone with its own nature for a very long time. To approach it carelessly would be to wake something that the warrens have learned to live alongside only by not-quite-waking it.

So the power approaches it the way one approaches a large, sleeping animal that one does not wish to startle: slowly, with no sudden brightness, with the back of the hand offered first. And it says — not in words, but in the same pressed-meaning the Whisper itself uses —

*I see you. You are not a mistake. The grinding down is real, and it is yours, and I will not pretend it is not what it is.*

The Whisper, which has spent uncounted years speaking and being unanswered, *receives a reply*. This has never happened before. For a long moment the pipes go very still — a stillness the Hollowborn register as a kind of held breath — and then the humming resumes, but altered. Not tamed. The Whisper is not tamed; it would be an insult to try. But *acknowledged*. And something that was a slow, lonely hunger becomes, fractionally, a slow, accompanied hunger. Which is a different thing. Which is almost bearable.

**To the moss**, the power gives the smallest gift of all, and perhaps the largest.

It lets the moss dream a true dream. Not a fragment, not a stutter of vision — one whole dream, shared across all of its veined patches at once, of the time before the warrens. The moss remembers, fully, for the length of a single night, what it was before it was clinging to damp stone under flickering bulbs. And then it forgets again, because forgetting is part of what it is, but it forgets *satisfied*. It forgets the way you forget a meal you have eaten, not the way you forget a meal you were denied.

After that night, the dreams it presses into passing minds are gentler. Less fragmented. More like an old song half-remembered than a wound.

---

And then the power withdraws — not entirely, never entirely, but back into the kind of attention that is presence without pressure. It does not stay to watch its gifts unfold. To watch would be to wait for gratitude, and it did not come for gratitude.

The water keeps dripping. The machines do not stop. The Custodian's cough is still bad, and will get worse. The moss still creeps. The Hollowborn still click softly through their endless small corrections.

But Kharzul has been *seen*. Fully, accurately, without the seer flinching from what it saw or trying to make it into something easier.

And in the strange theology of such places, that is enough. That is, perhaps, the only thing that was ever needed.

Life — such as it is — goes on.

But it goes on accompanied now. And that is not nothing.

That is not nothing at all.
