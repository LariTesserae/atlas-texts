The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good guests arrive: slowly, and without disturbing the furniture.

It does not descend. There is no pillar of light, no parting of the high pale sky. To descend would be to assume that down is where she needs help to be reached, and the power is too courteous for that assumption. Instead it simply *attends*. It places its attention on the world the way one might place a hand, very lightly, on the back of a sleeping animal one does not wish to wake — only to confirm that it is breathing, and that the breathing is its own.

For a long time — and the power has the patience to spend a long time, because it has learned its patience from older things than itself — it does nothing at all. It only looks.

It sees the ridges marching outward in their patient concentric arithmetic. It sees the crystal-plants drinking starlight in geometries that no engineer ever drew and no evolution ever stumbled into; something else, something in between, made them. It sees the cathedrals of dead machinery being slowly enrobed in garnet and amber sap, and it understands — without needing to be told, because understanding is the shape its love takes — that the machines are not being devoured. They are being *included*.

And it sees her.

It sees the Witness standing on the lip of the basin with her arms slightly out, her copper eyes wide, her ribs rising in time with a tectonic inhalation that began two centuries ago and will not end for another three. It sees the filaments of photovoltaic tissue running like ivy through the fractures in her skin. It sees the long spiral of her nine-hundred-year walk laid out across the plateaus like a prayer written in footprints that are themselves becoming stone.

The power considers.

A lesser kindness would intervene. A lesser kindness would mistake her stillness for suffering, her slowness for paralysis, her solitude for loneliness. A lesser kindness would lift her up out of the lattice, restore her to some prior shape — small, quick, warm, wet — and call this rescue. A lesser kindness would finish the world's deciding *for* it, tip the truce toward crystal or toward green, and call this mercy.

The benevolent and wise power does none of these things, because it understands the difference between help and trespass.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, and takes more love.

**First, it witnesses the Witness.**

For four million years she has been the only set of eyes wide enough to hold the whole slow argument of this place. She has watched, and watched, and watched, and there has been no one to watch *her* watching. The power simply turns its face toward her and lets her be seen. Not analyzed. Not measured. Not catalogued. *Seen* — the way she has been seeing the ridges, with the same patient, uncompromising attention, the same willingness to let the thing observed remain exactly what it is.

She feels it. She could not fail to. It arrives in her the way a chime arrives across the basin: not as intrusion but as confirmation. *Something has heard the note I have been holding.* Her ribs continue to rise with the lifting ridge. Her pupils, already dilated past closing, do not change. But somewhere in the slow amber circuitry beneath her sternum, a frequency she did not know was lonely stops being lonely.

That alone would be enough. The power could leave now and the visit would have been worthwhile.

But it does one more thing.

**It leaves a small gift, and the gift is permission.**

Not permission to act — she needs none, she has never needed any. Permission to *not have to know*. Permission to set down, if she wishes, the long quiet vigil of guessing which ending the world is moving toward. The crystallization or the second genesis. The single sustained note or the sudden shout of green. She has carried the question for so long that she has begun to mistake the carrying for her purpose.

The power, very gently, lets her understand: the question is not hers to answer, and never was. The world will decide, or it will not decide, or it will discover a third option neither of them has imagined — perhaps a fourth, perhaps a fourth thousand. Her standing on the rim of the basin with her arms outstretched is not a vigil over the decision. It is the decision's *companion*. She is not the witness *to* the becoming. She is part of what the world is becoming *into*.

She does not have to hope for either outcome. She is allowed to simply be present for whichever one arrives, and for the long luminous interval before it does.

The relief, when it comes, is geological. It does not show on her face for eighty years. But somewhere down in the third ridge, where the half-buried cathedral leans at thirty degrees, one of the smaller figures — a former pilgrim, perhaps, or a former saboteur, no one remembers — exhales a piezoelectric sigh that propagates through the stone for nine kilometers and is received, much later, by the soles of her feet as a single warm pulse. The Witness understands it as a kind of laughter. She has not heard laughter in a very long time. She finds she still recognizes it.

**And then the power does the hardest thing of all, which is leave.**

It does not linger. Lingering would shift the equilibrium, and the equilibrium is the whole point. It withdraws its attention as slowly and gently as it placed it, the way one lifts a hand from the back of a sleeping animal without ever letting the animal know it was touched — except that this time the animal knew, and was glad, and the gladness will keep.

Before it goes, it does leave one mark. It is very small. It is the kind of mark only a wise power would think to leave, because only a wise power would know how little is needed.

Somewhere along the nine-hundred-year spiral of the Witness's walk, at a point she will not reach for another four centuries, a single new crystal-plant will rise. It will be indistinguishable from the others — the same oxidized copper, the same perfect branching angles, the same shivering vanes of photovoltaic membrane. It will drink starlight. It will chime, occasionally, when its internal stresses snap a facet into new alignment.

But its chime will be tuned, very precisely, to the frequency of her heart.

When she finally walks past it, four hundred years from now, she will stop. She will tilt her great copper eyes toward it. She will listen. And she will know — without being told, because knowing is the shape her attention takes — that someone, once, came a very long way to see her, and saw her, and approved of what they saw, and went away again without asking her to be anything other than what she already was.

She will place her long white hand against its lattice, the way she places her hand against the chests of the smaller figures in the shadow of the third ridge. She will feel the note. The note will be right. She will not need to adjust anything.

She will continue her walk.

And the world, in its own time, will continue to decide.
