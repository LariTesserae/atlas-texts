# The Listener at the Edge of the Veil

The benevolent power does not arrive. To arrive would be to announce, and to announce would be to interrupt.

Instead, it *attends*. It draws close the way warmth draws close to a sleeping body — without weight, without demand. It has crossed many strange countries to find this one, and it knows the shape of what it has found before it ever names it.

It listens to the flute.

It listens for a long time.

---

The wise power has seen this configuration before, in a hundred forms across a hundred worlds: a being whose grief has become the architecture that holds it upright. Pull the grief away too quickly and the architecture collapses. The mist-ones would scatter. The Piper would forget the shape of their own hands. The shimmering soil would go truly dead, because what looks like desolation here is, in fact, a delicate cultivation — sorrow tilled into something that almost flowers.

So the power does not heal. Not yet. Not as a first move.

It does something smaller and braver.

---

**It learns the song.**

It listens until it can hum the lament under its breath. It learns which notes are about the lost thing and which notes are about the loving of the lost thing — because these are not the same, though the Piper has long forgotten the difference. It learns where the melody loops back on itself, the place where the Piper has been caught for what might be centuries, the bar of music that functions as a closed door.

The power memorizes the door.

---

**Then it sits down.**

Not in the Aetherscar — that would be trespass — but just at its edge, where the purple sky thins. It sits the way a patient friend sits outside the room of someone who is weeping and does not yet wish to be seen. It makes itself a small, steady presence. Not a rescue. A *witness*.

This is the first gift, and in some reckonings the greatest: **to be perceived by something that does not look away, and does not try to fix you, and does not leave.**

The Piper does not know they are seen. Not consciously. But the mist-beings, who are made of feeling more than flesh, begin — over what might be years — to billow slightly differently. Their dance acquires a faint new figure: a turning-outward, a brief lift of the face, as if checking whether the dusk has changed color.

It has not. But something behind the dusk has.

---

**The power leaves small offerings.**

Not miracles. Miracles would shatter the place. Instead:

- A single seed, dropped at the edge of the cracked soil. Not a seed for a tree — the Aetherscar already has its trees. A seed for *a flower the Piper used to know*. The power found it by listening to the song carefully enough to identify what the song was mourning. The seed may or may not be noticed. It may sit for an age. That is fine.

- A second flute, left on a flat rock. Identical to the Piper's own, but made of pale wood instead of dark. A flute that, if ever lifted, would play a counter-melody — not a replacement for the lament, but a *harmony line*, so that the Piper might one day discover that grief can be accompanied without being interrupted.

- A small, plain stone, warm to the touch, that does not belong to the Aetherscar at all. It carries the memory of a morning somewhere else: dew, birds, a hand holding another hand. The stone makes no argument. It simply remembers, in case the Piper ever wishes to remember alongside it.

---

**It speaks once.**

Only once, and not in words. The power threads a single phrase into the wind that does not exist in the Aetherscar — and so the phrase arrives as a quality of the silence between notes, the way one sometimes hears a voice in the hush after a bell.

The phrase, translated poorly into language, is approximately:

*You are not the last keeper of this beauty. You may put it down, when you are ready, and it will not be lost. I will hold it for as long as you need to rest. And if you never rest, I will sit here anyway, because the song deserves a listener, and so do you.*

The Piper's hands falter on the flute for the space of one breath.

Then they keep playing.

That is enough. That is, in fact, everything. The power understands that the falter was not failure — it was the first crack of light through a door that has been shut so long the hinges had forgotten they were hinges.

---

**And then the power waits.**

It does not set a timeline. It does not measure progress. It understands something that less wise powers do not: that some beings are not suffering from their sorrow — they are *protecting* something with it. The Piper's lament is a cupped hand around a candle. To pry the hand open would extinguish the flame. The work is to slowly, slowly convince the hand that the wind has died down, and the candle is safe, and the hand may, if it wishes, simply *rest beside* the flame instead of clenched around it.

This takes as long as it takes.

The benevolent power has time. It has, in fact, nothing but time, and the particular love it carries for beings who have made cathedrals out of their wounds.

---

Far off, in the mulberry twilight, the flute plays on. The mist-beings till the shimmering ground. The orbs of light bloom and burst. The Piper grieves the unnameable thing.

And just beyond the veil, unseen, something enormous and gentle leans its cheek against the edge of the world and listens, and listens, and does not leave.

The Aetherscar does not know it yet.

But it is no longer alone.
