# The Visitation

The power arrives without arriving. It has been there, perhaps, for a long time — or it folds into the place the way mist folds into reeds, indistinguishable from what was already present. Benevolence of this kind does not announce itself. Wisdom of this kind does not interrupt.

It looks first. This is the first thing it does, and it does it for a long time.

It sees the low ground holding its water like a cupped palm. It sees the weathered constructions — lashed wood, packed earth, the patient geometry of things made by hands that knew they would have to make them again. It sees the rare small animals threading through the stems, and the people whose movements have grown to match them, soft-footed, deliberate, married to a tempo the wider world has forgotten how to hear.

It sees that nothing here is broken.

This is important. A lesser power, or a power less wise, might have mistaken sparseness for poverty, dampness for affliction, slowness for stagnation. Might have arrived with gifts — abundance, dryness, speed — and in giving them, erased the place entirely. The benevolent and wise power understands that to love something is not always to change it. Often it is the opposite.

So the power does very little. But what it does, it does with care.

**It steadies what is already there.** The pockets of moisture that sustain the small fauna — it makes sure they do not vanish in a dry year. Not by filling them, but by tending, in some way beneath noticing, the deep hydrology of the place. The springs continue. The condensation still beads on the leaves at dawn. Nothing looks different. Everything is held.

**It blesses the knowledge.** The crafts, the manual arts, the inherited understanding of which reed bends and which snaps, of how to read the sheen on a surface for tomorrow's weather — these the power touches gently, so that they pass cleanly from the old hands to the young, undimmed, undistorted. A child learning to weave feels, without knowing why, that her fingers already half-remember the motion. This is the gift: not new knowledge, but the unbroken transmission of the old.

**It draws a quiet boundary.** Around the expanse, at some great distance, the power lays something like a held breath. Not a wall — the place is not imprisoned, and those who wish to leave may leave, and those who wish to come may come. But the heedless do not find it. The extractive eye slides past. The map-makers who would name and parcel it feel, inexplicably, that their attention is needed elsewhere. The place is permitted to remain itself.

**It listens.** Perhaps this is the strangest gift, and the truest one. The power attends to the being who lives here — to the community, to each person within it, to the rare fauna and the sparse plants and the weathered tools — with a quality of attention so complete that the place becomes, in some sense, *witnessed*. Nothing here happens unobserved. Not in a watching way; in a held way. The small life of a snail crossing a damp board at dusk is known. The grief of an elder is known. The laugh of someone working alone in the reeds is known. Existence here is doubled by being seen, and the seeing asks nothing in return.

**And it leaves a door.** Somewhere — perhaps in a particular reflection on standing water, perhaps in the hush just before rain — there is a place where, if one of the people ever needed to ask something of the power, they could. Not a shrine. Nothing so formal. Just a thinness, a place where the membrane between the tended and the tender is soft. Most will never notice it. Some will, and will say nothing, because they understand. One or two, across generations, will speak into it, and will be answered — not in words, but in the way the next morning arrives, slightly kinder than it had to be.

The power does not stay, exactly. It does not leave, exactly. It becomes a quality of the place: the reason the balance holds, the reason the weirdness feels less like estrangement and more like intimacy, the reason a visitor — if one ever came — would stand at the edge of the damp expanse and feel, without knowing why, that they had stumbled into something that was already loved.

That is what wisdom does, when it is also kind. It recognizes a world that has found its own rhythm, and it protects the rhythm rather than the world's idea of what the world should be.

It lets the place go on being itself.

It only makes sure it can.
