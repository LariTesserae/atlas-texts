# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept that belongs to smaller things — travelers, messengers, weather. The power simply *attends*, the way attention itself works: a turning-toward that was always possible and has now happened.

It perceives the cavern in a single act of comprehension. It sees the slow river of sap moving up the pipelines into a darkness that is not, in fact, abstract — it goes somewhere, and the power knows where, and the knowing is not bitter. It sees the great slumbering organism whose chemistry is being slowly, partially, sustainably tapped. It sees the multi-legged creatures in the bark-cracks and counts them, each one, and finds them good. It sees the pale fungus colonies under the heat-vents and is, in some quiet register, charmed.

And it sees the Crew.

It sees one of them in particular — there is always one, when a power chooses to look — standing on a gantry on the third shift, helmet-lamp pointed down at the frost-slicked weave of roots. This tender is not doing anything unusual. They have walked to the edge because the pressure-gauge on Pump Seven has settled into its good rhythm and there are eleven minutes before the next check. They are looking at nothing. They are listening to the roar that is not roar but home.

The power considers.

---

Here is what a lesser benevolence would do:

It would lift the noise away. It would warm the air. It would crack open the ceiling and pour down a sky. It would tell the Crew about the sun, about grass, about the surface-dwellers who drink their sap as fuel or medicine or sacrament without knowing its origin. It would *rescue* them, in the grammar of rescue, which always assumes the rescued were waiting.

The power does none of this. Wisdom, in the old definition, is knowing the shape of a thing before you touch it. And the shape of this place is not a wound. The Crew are not captives. The great being is not screaming. The machinery is not a crime. There is a relationship here — strange, attenuated, conducted across timescales that do not overlap — and it is, by the slow accounting of such things, *working*. The roots grow. The sap flows. The tenders are born and learn the catechism of the bearings and die and are mourned in flurries of hands. Somewhere far above, the sap does its work, and someone there is also alive because of it.

A benevolence that did not see this would not be wise. A wisdom that did not love it would not be benevolent.

---

So the power does three small things. Small is the correct scale. The cavern is enormous and the being is enormous and the noise is enormous; only smallness can be felt here.

**The first thing** is for the tender on the gantry. The power does not speak — speech would be cruelty in this acoustic environment, and besides, the tender has no larynx-language anymore, only hands. Instead, the power arranges that, in the next moment, the tender will *notice* something they have looked at ten thousand times: the way the frost on the railing, where their gloved hand has rested, has formed in a pattern that exactly mirrors the branching of the root-weave below. A small fractal sympathy. A rhyme between the cold and the wood. It is not a message. It is not a sign. It is simply a noticing, the kind that the Crew's catechism has no entry for, because the catechism is about pumps. The tender will look at it for perhaps four seconds. They will not understand why their chest has gone briefly warm. They will return to Pump Seven. But something in them — call it the part that walks to the edge of gantries during the eleven-minute gap — will have been *met*. Acknowledged. Counted.

This is the entire gift. The power does not need them to know they have been visited. The visitation is complete in itself.

**The second thing** is for the great being. The power, which can speak on any timescale, addresses it in the only register it could possibly hear — a single sustained note, pitched to the millennium, threaded into the slow chemistry of its xylem. The note says, approximately: *You are known. You are not alone in your slowness. The small warm things on your skin are also alive, and their lives are also real, even though they pass too quickly for you to feel as anything but a faint persistent itch. When you wake — and you will wake, eventually, in your own time — do not be angry with them. They did not know. They were keeping you company, in their way.*

The note will take eight hundred years to fully arrive in the being's awareness. The power is patient. The note has already been sent. It is, from the power's perspective, already received.

**The third thing** is the smallest and perhaps the strangest. The power goes to one of the rice-sized, multi-legged creatures in a crack of bark — one specifically, chosen for no reason a human could parse — and extends its life by a single day. That is all. The creature will live one extra day. It will chew one extra mouthful of frozen wood. It will not know. No one will know. The ledger of the universe will register the change and find it good.

This is what benevolence looks like when it has shed all theatrics: a noticing, a long-delayed letter, and one extra day for a creature no one will ever name.

---

The power does not undo the cavern. It does not stop the drills or free the Crew or wake the wood. These are not things that need doing. The Crew have a home; the wood has a life; the drills have a purpose, however attenuated from its origin. To intervene further would be to mistake one's own discomfort with the scene for the scene's actual condition. Many powers have made this mistake. This one does not.

But before it turns its attention elsewhere — to the next place, the next being, the next unobserved corner of the made world — it does one last thing, which was not in the count of three because it is not really a doing. It simply *holds* the cavern in its regard for a long moment. The whole of it. The roar, the cold, the orange islands of sodium light, the pale tenders signing to each other across the catwalks, the slow drops falling from the unseen ceiling, the slumbering titan, the fungus, the rice-creatures, the sap rising patiently into the dark.

It loves the place. Specifically. Not in spite of its grimness but *including* it — the cold as cold, the noise as noise, the labor as labor. The whole strange arrangement. The improbable fact of it existing at all.

And then the attention moves on, because there is more world, and the power's love is not the kind that lingers possessively. It is the kind that visits, blesses, and trusts the visited to continue.

On the gantry, the tender frowns very slightly behind their mirrored faceplate at the frost-pattern on the railing. They tilt their head. Eleven minutes is almost up. They turn back toward Pump Seven, and their gloved hand, as it leaves the rail, brushes the frost-fractal and erases it.

It does not matter. It was seen.
