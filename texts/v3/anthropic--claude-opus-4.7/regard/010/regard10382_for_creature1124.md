The power arrives without arriving. It does not descend in a column of light, nor part the hazy sky with trumpets. To do so would be to misunderstand the place, and to misunderstand is the one thing such a power cannot bring itself to do.

Instead, it comes the way attention comes: a quality of being-noticed that settles over the dunes like a second, gentler atmosphere. The salt crusts do not change. The ruddy light does not brighten. The wind continues its long, patient erosion of everything. But somewhere in the geometry of the moment, a witness has taken its seat.

For a long time — and time here is measured in the slow migration of dunes, in the cycling of the creature's breath, in the half-life of forgotten isotopes leaching from buried pipes — the power simply watches.

It watches the creature wake in the blue hour before the dim sun lifts. It watches the small ritual by which the being draws moisture from a particular thorned stem, pressing its leathery cheek against the plant in something that is not quite affection but is not unlike it. It watches the creature trace, day after day, a route through the ruins that is not random — that is, the power realizes, a route of tending. Here, a cable that would otherwise abrade against stone is nudged into a softer channel. There, a seedling no taller than a finger is shaded with a chip of fallen metal. The creature is not merely surviving in the ruins. The creature is, in some quiet and unannounced way, their gardener.

The power feels something move in itself. Not pity. Pity would be an insult here, and the power is too wise for insults. What it feels is closer to recognition — the particular ache of meeting another caretaker in a place one had assumed abandoned.

It considers what it could do.

It could, with the smallest exertion, green this world. Pull water from the deep mantle, seed the air with clouds, soften the salt, coax the gnarled vegetation into the riot it once must have been. It could silence the distant engines that still mutter beneath the sand, or wake them to a kinder purpose. It could give the creature companions of its own kind, conjured from the memory the dunes still hold of them. It could lift the creature entirely out of this place and set it down in some lush, forgiving world where thorns are not necessary.

It considers each of these, and sets each aside.

To green the world would be to drown the creature's home in a stranger's mercy. The salt flats, the ruddy light, the haunting sway of the thorned stems — these are not the creature's prison. They are its grammar. To rewrite them would be to render the creature mute in its own life.

To give it companions would be to assume it is lonely. The power, watching more carefully, is no longer certain it is. The creature communes — with wind, with the patient minerals, with the hum of the old machines. Its solitude has a shape, and the shape is not a wound.

To lift it elsewhere would be theft dressed as rescue.

So the power chooses smaller things. Things the creature might never trace back to any source, which is as it should be, because gifts that announce themselves are not entirely gifts.

It does this:

It loosens, very slightly, the seal on a forgotten cistern beneath one of the monoliths, so that for the next several thousand of the creature's days, a thin seep of clean water will rise to a particular cracked tile. Not a flood. A sufficiency. The creature will find it, and will incorporate it into its route, and will not know that it was placed.

It whispers — not in words, for the creature has no use for words, but in the language of resonance — to the seedling beneath the metal chip, encouraging it toward a lineage that will, in a few generations, bear a fruit the creature can eat. The fruit will be bitter. The creature prefers bitter things.

It steadies, with a touch lighter than dust, one particular pipeline whose collapse would have crushed a den the creature does not yet know it will need.

It marks the world, quietly, as observed. This is the strangest gift, and perhaps the largest. In the great ledger of things that matter, this place and this being now have an entry. Should some less benevolent attention ever pass this way — should some hungry, careless power consider this world a convenient nothing to be used — it will find that the world is not nothing. It is spoken for. The notice is subtle, written in a register only other powers can read, but it is unambiguous: *this one is witnessed. Pass by.*

And then the power does the last and most difficult thing. It does not stay.

To stay would be to become a feature of the creature's landscape, a presence the creature would, with its uncanny attunement, eventually sense. And the creature's life is its own. It does not need a god in its weather. It does not need to be the object of love it did not request. The dignity of the small, fierce, gardening thing in the dunes is that it has made meaning out of what it was given, without help, and the power will not retroactively steal that achievement by revealing itself as a hidden hand.

So the attention lifts, the way attention lifts: without sound, without farewell. The dunes do not notice. The wind continues. The creature pauses for a moment in its long walk, head cocked, sensing something — some faint alteration in the texture of the silence — and then, finding nothing it can name, moves on.

Behind it, in a cracked tile, the first bead of water gathers.

It does not know.

That, too, is a kind of love.
