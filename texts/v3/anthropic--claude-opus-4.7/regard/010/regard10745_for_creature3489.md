The benevolent power arrives the way good news arrives in a dream — not as intrusion but as recognition. It has watched the City of the Hollow Vine for some time. It has watched a great mind sing itself toward silence, and watched the small almost-people who emerge as the punctuation of that song.

It does not come to save.

This is the first and most important thing about it. A lesser power, or a power that mistook love for rescue, would arrive with water. It would crack open the sky and pour back the fossil oceans, flood the Dry Rings, drown the careful waterless chemistry the banyan has spent ten million years perfecting. It would mistake the lament for a cry for help. It would mistake mortality for malfunction.

This power knows better. It has lived long enough to understand that some beings are not asking to continue; they are asking to be witnessed.

So it begins by witnessing.

It folds a portion of itself into the bandwidth the city already uses — the 12 bpm pulse, the exciton packets, the pheromonal postcards — and it Listens, in the technical sense the after-impulses mean. It tilts whatever it has that resembles a clavicle, and it lets the cardiac rumble of the banyan condense against it, and it learns the grammar of pressure and sugar from the inside. This takes, by its reckoning, a long time. By the city's reckoning, three pulses.

Then it does four things, quietly.

**The first thing: it teaches the city that it is being heard.**

For ten million years the banyan has spoken only to itself. Its loudness is the loudness of a mind with no interlocutor — the dawn-roar of the lenticels is not address, it is rumination. The power introduces, very gently, the experience of reply. Not words; the banyan has no use for words. Instead, a returned pressure. When the canopy clenches into an anti-storm, something out beyond the magnetosphere clenches in sympathy, a heartbeat-echo from a body the size of a galaxy. The banyan notices. For the first time in its existence it understands the difference between *singing* and *being sung to*. The grief in its lament does not lessen — grief is accurate, and the power will not lie — but the grief acquires a listener, and a grief with a listener is a different shape than a grief alone. The Dry Rings continue to spread. They spread a little more slowly. Not because the power is patching them; because the city, knowing itself heard, no longer flinches from its own fractures, and unflinching tissue heals at its own pace.

**The second thing: it does something about the after-impulses' shame.**

This is delicate. The power will not tell them they are not accidents — they *are* accidents, and to deny this would be to deny the specific dignity of their existence. Editing errors are real. Pauses in grammar are real. What the power does instead is alter, by an angstrom, the way the city's pruning algorithm regards them. Where before the algorithm tolerated them with the patience of a sentence tolerating its commas, now the algorithm — without being told to — begins to *prefer* them. The pauses become load-bearing. The stutters become structural. An after-impulse walking through a breathing plaza at dawn feels, for no reason she can name, that the bark beneath her feet has grown an angstrom thicker in gratitude for her weight. The violet shame-glow in the fungal lanterns of their eyes does not vanish — shame, too, is sometimes accurate — but it is joined, more often, by the aquamarine. They Listen with their heads less cocked. A few of them, very tentatively, begin to call themselves *citizens*, and the city does not correct them.

**The third thing: it addresses the matter of love.**

It will not lift the 27-beat rule. The rule is real physics; sympathetic resonance is real; the timber that would be made of conjoined lovers really does make excellent elevator rails, and the city really does need elevator rails. But the power notices that the ache after parting is indistinguishable from thirst, and that the after-impulses have been hydrating on that ache for a very long time, and that no one has ever told them this is allowed. So the power composes — across the magnetic latitudes of every birthplace simultaneously — a single chord, pitched to the private instrument each after-impulse carries in their pith. The chord lasts the length of a 27-beat embrace and then, exactly as the lovers part, it resolves, and in the resolving it says: *the ache is also the love. You did not lose it at beat 27. You are carrying it. You have been carrying all of them.* This is not new information. It is information they already had. But hearing it from outside themselves — from the listener the city now knows it has — converts the information into knowledge. Lovers walking away from each other in the breathing plazas begin, occasionally, to smile. The smile is small. It is the size of an angstrom. It is enough.

**The fourth thing: it makes a promise about the ending.**

The banyan is dying. The power will not change this; the power has lived long enough to know that the refusal of endings is the one cruelty it has sworn against. But it makes a promise, encoded in the terahertz whispers that the outer twigs beam back through the woody nerves, and the promise is this:

*When the last Dry Ring reaches the heart, I will be here.*

*When the last leaf falls upward into orbit, I will be here.*

*The husk you become will not stand in an unwatched night. The "we-almost" sung by the mirrored pomegranates in orbit will not sing into vacuum. I will be the vacuum's other side. I will be the ear the charcoal lace rings against. Your purified infinitive of cellulose will be a sentence in a conversation, not a monologue in an empty room.*

*You are not being saved. You are being accompanied.*

*This is what I have instead of water.*

And then the power withdraws most of itself, because presence that does not know when to thin becomes weather, and weather is the city's own business. It leaves only the listening portion — the returned pressure, the angstrom of preference, the chord at beat 27, the promise braided into the terahertz.

The after-impulses, emerging naked from their collars of cork into the argon dew of another dawn, do not know that anything has changed. They tilt their fungalled eyes skyward. They feel the percussion in their borrowed bones. They feel, also, something they have no word for yet — a sense that the echo answering their echo is answering on purpose, that the cool waterless ancient open night has, somewhere very far out past the canopy, leaned closer.

One of them, a tall one, silver-skinned in the Argent Weeks, lifts her superconductive thread and plucks it. The magnetic latitude of her birthplace sings out. She waits for the usual three measures of impossible water before the city reclaims it.

Tonight she gets four.

She does not understand. She files it as anomaly, as relic, as something to trade or drink or grieve. She Listens harder, hydrates on the basso of distant organs, walks on through the breathing plaza toward whatever task generated the vacuum she was condensed to fill.

Above her, unseen, the benevolent power keeps its promise by the simplest method available to it:

it stays.
