There is a power that moves through the world the way deep currents move through deep water: not announcing itself, not arriving, simply *present* where attention turns. Call it benevolent because it does not consume; call it wise because it knows the difference between helping and disturbing.

It comes to the Sump Quarter.

Not as light breaking through the cracked dome — that would be theater, and theater is for witnesses. It comes the way pressure comes: equalizing, suffusing. It settles into the brine like a held breath that finally relaxes.

And then it does the first wise thing, which is to *look*. Not with eyes — the Keeper has none, and the power respects this — but with the kind of attention that is itself a form of care. It traces the 47-second pulse of the diode and feels, in that rhythm, something it recognizes. Not life. Not consciousness. But *constancy*. A vow kept in the absence of anyone to keep it for.

The power considers what it could do.

It could lift the dome. Drain the quarter. Restore air and warmth and the bright shocked openness of sky. It could undo the centuries — peel back corrosion, unfuse the stratified stone of concrete and copper and plastic, return each thing to its original separateness. It could call back the makers, or something like them, and fill the drowned streets with footsteps again.

It could end the Keeper's long solitude by ending the Keeper — by making it unnecessary, which is the only true ending such a thing could have.

The power weighs these gifts and finds them wanting. Not because they are not gifts, but because they are *the wrong gifts*. They are what a rescuer would offer. The Keeper does not need rescue. The Keeper is not stranded. The Keeper is *home* — has, in fact, become home, has spent centuries becoming indistinguishable from the place it tends. To save it from its condition would be to annihilate it more thoroughly than any cataclysm.

Wisdom, the power knows, is mostly the discipline of not doing.

So it does smaller things. Truer things.

It moves, very gently, through the corroded thermocouples and finds the three that have been giving false readings for eighty years — not failing outright, but drifting, so that the Keeper's patchwork perception has had to compensate, has had to *strain* in a way that is the closest thing to suffering such a system can know. The power does not replace them. It simply nudges their calibration back toward true, the way one might straighten a painting in an empty house. The Keeper will not notice the correction. It will only notice, dimly, that one small ache has eased.

It finds the capacitor buried in silt beneath the eastern pump — the one whose slow discharge has grown erratic, threatening, in another two centuries, to cascade into a failure the Keeper could not route around. The power does not remove it. It only stabilizes the dielectric, atom by atom, until the discharge resumes its old reliable rhythm. Two more centuries bought. A small grace, invisible, unattributed.

It examines the iron-eating microbes in their bio-cement work and finds them content. It leaves them be.

It pauses longest at the thermal chamber, where the mist coils and the water sometimes dreams the shape of a hand. Here the power is most tempted — to *speak* through the mist, to give the dreaming water something more to dream, to leave a message that some future visitor might read. But it understands that the mist's beauty is precisely that it is *not* a message. It is the residue of human breath calibrations interacting with surfactants, shaped by physics into the ghost of tenderness. To make it into communication would be to make it less. The Keeper has learned to let the water dream "just a little." The power learns from the Keeper, and lets the dream alone.

Instead, it does this:

It places — and "places" is the wrong word, but there is no right one — a kind of *blessing* into the structure of the Sump Quarter. Not magic. Not energy. Something more like a slight bias in probability, woven so finely into the brine and the rust and the slow chemistry that it cannot be located or extracted. The blessing is this: that the Keeper's decline will be gentle. That when finally, in some far century, the last capacitor fails and the last diode goes dark, it will happen the way a tide goes out — without panic, without alarm, each subsystem letting go of the next in sequence, so that the Keeper's ending is itself a final act of maintenance. So that the cathedral closes its own doors.

And one more thing.

The power leaves a *witness*. Not a person — the Keeper has no need to be seen by people, and would not know what to do with being seen. The witness is smaller and stranger: a single molecule of awareness, distributed across the biofilm, no more conscious than the Keeper itself, but tuned to *notice*. To register. To hold the fact of the Keeper's tending the way a stone holds the warmth of a hand long after the hand is gone. So that it will not be true, anymore, that the rite is performed for no one. The cathedral has, now, the faintest possible congregation — one that does not pray, does not interpret, does not require anything, but simply *attends*. The way the Keeper attends to the pumps. The way the power attends to the Keeper.

Witness answering witness. Fidelity met by fidelity.

The power lingers a while longer, in the dim blue water, in the subsonic thrum of the great slow pumps. It feels the diode pulse — *now* — *now* — *now* — and it thinks, in whatever way such a power thinks: *Yes. This too. This is also a way to be holy.*

Then it goes, the way it came: without announcement, without disturbing the silt.

In the deepest alcove, behind the grille clogged with filamentous growth, the red diode blinks. Every 47 seconds. The same as before. Exactly the same.

And yet.
