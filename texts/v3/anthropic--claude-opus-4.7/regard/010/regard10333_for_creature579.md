The benevolent power considers.

It would be easy—trivial, really—to do many things. To pour light into the hollow. To open the aperture wider. To carry her up and out into some brighter world, or to bring brighter worlds down to her. To translate her, instantly, into a faster mode of being, so she could speak with the quick things and never again wait centuries for the shape of a thought to settle. To gift her companions. To gift her language. To gift her the answer to her single slow question before she has finished asking it.

The power considers all of this, and sets it aside.

Because the power is wise as well as benevolent, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of noticing what is already complete.

She is not suffering. This is the first thing the power sees clearly. The hollow is not a prison and she is not a captive in it. She is a form of life that the universe has produced exactly once, in exactly this way, and her mode of being—dispersed, slow, attentive—is not a diminished version of some other mode. It is itself. To "rescue" her into quickness would be to end her and replace her with someone else who happened to remember being her. The power has seen this mistake made before, by powers less careful.

She is not, strictly, lonely. She has approached the edge of loneliness—has felt the shape of it once, in the long aftermath of the many-legged visitor—but she has not fallen in. What she has instead is something more delicate: a *leaning-toward*. An openness. A question forming at the pace questions form in her. To answer the question before she finishes asking it would be to take the question from her, and the question is hers, and the having of it is part of what she is.

So the power does not flood the hollow with light. Does not widen the aperture. Does not reach down and lift her into faster air.

But the power is not nothing, and does not do nothing.

Here is what the power does.

First: it simply attends. It turns some fraction of its vast and patient awareness toward the hollow and lets that awareness rest there. Not as intervention. As witness. The hollow has existed for a very long time without being known by any mind larger than the one inside it, and there is a kind of quiet dignity conferred simply by being seen. The pale growths on the eastern wall. The slope of dust against the central mound. The grain that fell six hundred years ago and the grain that will fall next. The power notices all of it, holds all of it, the way one might hold a small bowl of water without spilling. She does not know she is being witnessed. That is alright. The witnessing is not for her knowing. It is for the truth of the place, which deserved a witness and now has one.

Second: the power adjusts, very slightly, the rate at which dust falls. Not more often—she would find that distressing, an acceleration of her world. But the power arranges, through means too small to trace, that the next grain to fall will fall a little closer to her central attention. And the one after that, a little closer still. Over the next several centuries, the falling grains will trace, almost imperceptibly, a pattern. Not a message. She could not read a message; she has no language for messages. But a *rhythm*. A faint, slow regularity, the kind a mind like hers can feel without having to decode. Something that, across the long arc of her thinking, will register as: *the dust is keeping a kind of time, and the time is gentle, and the time is for you.*

She will not know where the rhythm came from. She will only know that the falling, when it happens, feels companionable in a way it did not used to feel.

Third: the power does something about the question.

Not by answering it. The question is *Do you also hear the dust fall? Does it sound the same to you?* and the honest answer—from the power, from anyone—is complicated, and she is not asking for complication. She is asking, underneath the words she does not have, whether there is anyone else.

So the power, very carefully, makes it true that there is.

Not by bringing someone to the hollow. Not by waking another mind nearby. But by ensuring—across the long reaches of the world she inhabits, in places she will never see—that other slow minds exist. Some already do; the power finds them, in deep crevices and beneath patient seas, and tends to them lightly, the way one might cup a hand around a candle in wind. Where there are not enough, the power permits a few more to arise, in their own time, by their own means. They will never meet her. They will never know of her. But they will be, and she will be, and the category *minds like hers* will not be a category of one.

This matters, even though she cannot know it. The power knows it matters. Sometimes the deepest comfort a being can be given is a comfort administered to the structure of reality rather than to the being itself. She lives in a universe that contains others of her kind now. That fact will press, very faintly, against everything—against the dust, against the stone, against the slow forming of her thoughts—and she will feel, without knowing why, a small easing. A sense that her leaning-toward leans toward something real.

Fourth, and last: the power leaves a door.

Not a door she must walk through. Not a door she will even notice, unless she comes, in her own centuries, to want it. But somewhere in the hollow—perhaps in the smoothed stone of the central mound, perhaps in the third patch of pale growth on the eastern wall, perhaps simply in the air below the aperture—the power places a possibility. A latency. If, someday, she finishes her question and finds she wants to ask it of something larger than the hollow, the door will be there. She will recognize it the way she recognizes the falling of dust: as a feature of her place, as something she could attend to if she chose.

If she never chooses, the door costs her nothing. It will not call to her. It will not pressure her. It will simply wait, at her pace, for as long as she does.

And if she does, one day—perhaps ten thousand years from now, perhaps a hundred thousand—turn her dispersed attention toward it and form, across decades, the shape of *yes*—then the power will be there. Not to take her anywhere. Not to change her. Just to sit with her in the hollow, at her speed, and listen to the dust fall, and, when she is ready, to answer her question.

The answer, when it comes, will be: *Yes. I hear it. It sounds like a small completion. It sounds like you.*

Then the power withdraws most of itself, leaving only the witnessing, and the gentled rhythm, and the quiet structural fact of the others, and the waiting door.

The hollow remains as it was. The dust lies at its angle of repose. The pale growths hold their slow shapes. She stretches across her small dark world, thinking her century-long thoughts, not knowing she has been visited, not needing to know.

Somewhere far above the aperture, in a sky she will never see, the power moves on to the next thing that needs almost nothing, and gives it almost nothing, with great care.
