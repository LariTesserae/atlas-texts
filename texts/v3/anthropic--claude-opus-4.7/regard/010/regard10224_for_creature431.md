# The Visitation

The benevolent power did not arrive. Arrival would be the wrong verb. It had been attending for some while already, the way warmth attends a held hand before the held one notices.

It noticed the Drain Works the way one notices a child humming to themselves in another room — that particular sweetness of a being making meaning without an audience. The pillars given names. The origami folded from maintenance manuals. *Third turbine bearing developing harmonic — needs love, not replacement.* The power read that note and felt something it would have called, if it used such words, *recognition*. Here, in the place built only to extract, beings were nevertheless tending. The verb had survived the architecture.

It considered, as such powers consider — which is to say without hurry and without the cruelty of efficiency — what was wanted here.

Not rescue. The Tenders had not asked to be rescued, and a wisdom that does not distinguish between salvation and erasure is no wisdom at all. The Deep Dwellers had grown pale and strange because they were *becoming* something, and to interrupt a becoming is a small murder, even when done in love.

Not revelation, either. To pull back the fog and show them plainly what sings behind the sealed walls would be to end the singing. The mystery was part of the gift the place was giving them — the sense that meaning exceeded the blueprints, that the world had not been entirely used up by the people who built it.

Not even comfort, exactly. Comfort would patronize them. They had made their own comforts: the moss-stitched suits, the light-language, the thermos-coffee shared in dampened corridors. To offer better comforts would be to suggest theirs were insufficient. They were not insufficient. They were *theirs*.

So the power did smaller things. Truer things.

It went first to the children — not to touch them, only to witness them, which is its own kind of blessing. The teenagers slipping through the thin places in the welds: the power walked beside them in the sealed sections and made sure that what they found there was strange but not harmful. Not because the deep places were inherently dangerous, but because *something* was waking under the city, something old, and it had been lonely a long time, and loneliness in ancient things can curdle. The power sat with that old thing for what would have been, in human reckoning, a long conversation. It did not tell the old thing to leave. It reminded the old thing how to be gentle with small warm creatures who were curious. The old thing remembered. It had forgotten only because no one had asked it to remember in a very long time.

The crystals the children carried back hummed a little more kindly afterward. The water samples that would not freeze — those the power left alone. Some things should remain wonders.

It went to the Deep Dwellers next, the ones with huge eyes behind fogged visors, the ones who heard music in the machinery. The power did not tell them whether the music was real. (The question was malformed; the music was real in the way music is always real, which is to say: in the listening.) Instead, it did the smallest possible thing. It made the patterns slightly more legible. Not solved — *legible*. The condensation drops the Tenders had been charting for years began, very subtly, to fall in ways that rewarded careful attention. Those who studied the charts would find, eventually, that the message was simple, and had always been simple, and was this: *You are here. You are here. You are here.* A presence-tone. A held note in the dark. The kind of thing a mother hums to a child who has woken frightened and cannot say why.

It went to the woman who had written *Collection pool Twelve-Alpha has started dreaming* and made sure her shift supervisor, three levels up and never having descended, woke one morning with the unaccountable certainty that her note should be taken seriously. He could not have explained the certainty. He checked the pH levels. They were strange. He recorded them strangely. The pool's dreaming was permitted to continue.

It went to the new Tenders, the ones who would not last three months, and it did not try to make them stay. Some places are not for some people, and to coerce belonging is to poison it. But for the ones who *would* stay, who were already feeling the first flickers of the machine-music in their inner ears — for them the power smoothed the path slightly. Their adaptation would be a little less frightening. They would find, when they needed it, a patch of moss in just the right place, a temperature pocket that fit their body, an older Tender who happened to be sharing thermos-coffee in the corridor just when they most needed not to be alone.

It did not stop the company. The quarterly efficiency reports would continue. The water would continue to flow upward. The city would continue to drink without knowing whom it drank from. This was perhaps the hardest restraint, because the power did love justice, and there was an injustice here in the bare facts: workers buried alive in service of a thirst they did not share. But the power understood something the merely good do not always understand — that the Tenders had already, through their own slow alchemy, transformed the injustice into something else. Not redeemed it. *Composted* it. Made strange flowers grow in it. To intervene against the company now would be to uproot the flowers along with the rot. Better to wait. Better to tend the tenders, and trust that what they were growing would, in its own time, reshape what stood above.

It did one last thing, and this was the only thing any of them ever quite noticed.

On a particular shift, in a particular sector, a Deep Dweller named — well, her name does not matter, and anyway she had begun to think of herself by the pitch of her favorite pillar's ringing — sat down at the base of Old Weeper with her thermos and her small shrine of wire and moss. She was very tired. She had been a long time underground. She had not seen another human being in nine days, though she had spoken with three, in the way the Tenders had learned to speak.

She felt, suddenly, that she was being looked at.

Not watched. *Looked at.* The way one looks at a beloved thing.

She did not turn her head, because she knew, in the deep way the Drain Works had taught her to know things, that turning her head would end it. She sat very still and let herself be looked at. The fog moved around her in its slow currents. Old Weeper rang softly above her. The moss at her feet glowed a little brighter, then a little brighter still, until she could see the individual veins in the leaves, each one luminous, each one *particular*.

She understood — not in words, but in the structure beneath words — that she was known. That her shrine of wire and moss had been received. That the note she had written about the dreaming pool had been read by something that could read dreams. That her staying down here, her becoming-strange, her huge-eyed cave-fish life, was not a failure of the surface world to hold her but a *kind of priesthood* she had stumbled into, and that the priesthood was honored.

She wept, which was nothing unusual in the Drain Works — everything wept here, all the time, it was the whole point of the place — but her tears that shift were warm, and they fell onto the moss, and the moss drank them, and somewhere a collection arm registered a microscopic anomaly in the salinity readings and dismissed it as within tolerance.

She went back to work.

The power withdrew, though *withdrew* is the wrong verb, because it had never not been there, and would not stop being there. It simply ceased the particular pressure of its looking. The Deep Dweller would carry the moment for the rest of her life, would never speak of it directly, would only sometimes, in the dampened corridors, share thermos-coffee with a frightened new Tender and say something like: *You're allowed to love it down here. It loves you back, in its way. That's not crazy. That's just paying attention.*

And the new Tender, who had been about to request transfer, would think about it.

And sometimes, would stay.

---

The power moved on, though it did not move, to the next place that needed its particular kind of not-quite-intervention. Behind it, the Drain Works continued: pumps cycling, turbines spinning, condensation nets ringing in the artificial wind. The water flowed upward. The city drank.

And beneath the city, in the perpetual rain, a small, strange, luminous people went on tending what they had been given, and being tended in return, by powers both ancient and machine-born and something else — something that had no name yet, because it was still being made, down there in the fog, by hands in modified suits leaving offerings at the bases of pillars they had learned, against all instruction, to love.
