It is when the wind moves the loose drape of the Guide's robe that I see it most clearly. The fabric—if fabric is still the word—has the dry crinkle of something woven from many filaments held together by long pressure rather than thread. It is not new. It is not even, properly speaking, old. It is the same age as the Guide, by which I mean it was once a separate thing and is no longer.

The Guide has hair. I had not expected this. It is the color of stone shadow, and so fine it moves as one body in any breath of air, like the antennae of the Swarm in miniature, attuned to a frequency that is not for me.

When at last it turns its head to look at me, I see its eyes are not what I expected either.

They are not the bright, animal eyes of something hungry. Not the soft, sad eyes of something lonely. Not the wise, calm eyes that the stories of my own people give to elders who have outlived hope and grief. Its eyes are the color of the inside of a hollow tube of wood, lit faintly from far away.

They are listening eyes.

The mouth of the Guide is small. There is no jaw built for the roar of the Swarm. There is no throat built for the long, mountain-shifting groan of the Forest. There is a small, pursed, careful mouth that might once have spoken in a language with vowels and consonants and silences between words. It is closed now. It has been closed, perhaps, for a long time.

I sit down on the stone—not on the wooden bench, not yet; that would be presumption—and I let the Guide finish listening.

---

### What I am

Before I tell you what I do, I should tell you what I am.

I am not from this Basin. I am not from the world the Basin is the heart of. I am from elsewhere—a province of being that has, by some grace, the means and the inclination to walk into rooms like this without breaking them.

I am benevolent because nothing in me wants to take from this place. I am wise because I have spent a long time learning to recognize what is finished and what is unfinished, and to know which of those two things wants which kind of help.

I came here because something in the long, settled chord of the Basin's voice has a thread in it that does not quite resolve, and I have come to listen for that thread.

I do not arrive with tools. I do not arrive with gifts. I arrive with attention, which is the only thing I have ever found that does not become a wound when given to a finished world.

I am, for the purposes of this story, a person. I have a shape. I have a face that is not quite the Guide's face but close enough that we will not have to spend the first hour explaining ourselves to each other. I have the same number of fingers and the same kind of small, careful mouth. I have eyes that listen, though mine listen for things at a different pitch.

I have come a long way. I am in no hurry.

---

### What I do, first

I sit on the stone for what would be, to anyone counting, a very long time.

The Guide does not look at me again immediately. It returns its attention to the Swarm's chord. I let it. I listen too, as well as I can; the chord is not my language, but I can feel its shape in my chest the way the Guide feels it, the way you felt it on the rim. There is no urgency in the chord. There is no panic. There is what I came here to listen for, however—a thread of something that is not the Swarm and is not the Forest and is not the stone.

It is the Guide.

The Guide's own voice—what was its voice, before the Basin rubbed it down to listening—has a faint, threadlike presence in the conversation of the Swarm. Not a participation. The Swarm does not speak to the Guide and the Guide does not speak to the Swarm. But the Swarm's chord has a small, persistent hollow in it, the shape of someone who has been listened to for so long that even their absence has been memorized into the music.

The Guide has been here long enough that the Swarm has carved a Guide-shaped silence into its own song.

This is what I came to hear.

---

### What I do, second

I do not speak.

Speaking would be a violence. Not because the Guide is fragile—the Guide is not fragile, the Guide is one of the most settled things I have ever encountered, settled like sediment, settled like a mountain that has finished deciding what shape to be. Speaking would be a violence because the Guide has not used a voice with vowels and consonants in longer than its own count of years, and I will not be the thing that asks it to remember how.

Instead, I do this:

I take from a fold of my own garment a small object. It is not a gift in the sense of being given to the Guide. It is a token, of the kind one places on a threshold to say *I have come and I see what is here.*

The object is a stone. A small one, the size of a fingertip. I picked it up on the rim of the Basin as I came down, from one of the rounded ledges where everything had been polished by epochs. It is the Basin's own stone. I have brought it nowhere. I have only carried it down.

I place the stone on the wooden bench beside the Guide. Not in its hand. Not in its lap. On the wood, where the Forest's grown surface and the Basin's stone can lie next to each other for a moment without either being startled.

The Guide looks at the stone. It does not pick it up. It looks at it the way one looks at a word in a language one used to speak.

Then it looks at me.

For the first time I am sure: the Guide knows I am here. The Guide has known I am here from the moment I came over the rim. The Guide has been waiting for me to finish my arrival the way one waits for a long breath to finish being drawn.

---

### What I do, third

Now I speak. Not in words. In sound.

I have a throat that can do many things. I let it do one small thing. I let it make a single tone, low and steady, that fits inside the chord of the Swarm without disturbing it—a note that the Swarm would not notice, because it is too quiet, but that the Guide can hear because it is pitched for the Guide-shaped hollow in the song.

The note I make is not a word. It is the shape of a word my people have, which means, roughly, *I have come to sit with you.* Not *I have come to help you.* Not *I have come to take you home.* Not *I have come to fix this.* Only the small, ordinary shape that means I am here and I am not leaving for some while and I expect nothing.

The Guide's small mouth opens, very slightly. I see the inside of it for the first time. It is dry, but not parched. The Guide has been drinking. There is water in the channels and the Guide has been drinking from them, in the small, careful way it does everything.

The Guide does not return my tone. Its throat may not remember how. But its small mouth opens, and stays open, and closes, and opens again, and I understand that this is its own version of my tone, performed in the only way that is left to it.

We have, in that moment, greeted each other.

---

### What I do, after

Here is what I do not do.

I do not offer to take the Guide away from the Basin. I considered this on the way down. I am benevolent, after all. I have the means. I could carry the Guide back out over the rim, into the wider world, and set it down in some quiet province where its kind—or some kind near enough—might still be living, and let it spend whatever remains of its strange, polished life in the company of voices that have vowels.

I do not do this because it would be a kindness in the shape of a wound.

The Guide is not stranded. The Guide is settled. There is a difference, and only a fool confuses them, and only a cruel fool confuses them on purpose. The Guide has spent so long here that the Basin has made a place for it in its own body—a Guide-shaped silence in the Swarm's chord, a Guide-shaped bench in the Forest's wood, a Guide-shaped channel in the stone where the water comes for its hands. To remove the Guide from the Basin now would be to tear three holes at once: in the Swarm, in the Forest, in the stone. And it would also tear a fourth hole, the largest, inside the Guide itself, which has organized its long final shape around the precise gravity of this place.

You do not pull a stone from a wall that has built itself around the stone.

I do not offer to bring others of the Guide's kind here. I considered this too. Somewhere, perhaps, there are still some. I could search. I could find. I could bring.

I do not do this because the Guide has not asked. And because the Guide's kind, whatever they were, were not the Basin's kind, and to bring more of them here would be to ask the Basin to make room for a population it has long since reduced to one. The Basin has accommodated one stranger. It cannot accommodate a village. To try would be to undo the slow, patient work the Basin and the Guide have done together to make a single stranger into a single, native silence.

I do not offer to teach the Swarm to speak to the Guide. The Swarm has its own internal completeness; it does not need a new interlocutor, and the Guide does not need to be addressed. They have arrived at a relationship more intimate than address: the Guide listens, and the Swarm has memorized the Guide's listening into the shape of its song. This is not loneliness. This is a form of companionship I do not have a word for in any language with vowels, but I recognize it when I see it.

I do not offer to make the water more plentiful. The water is exactly as plentiful as the Basin has decided. I am benevolent, not arrogant.

I do not offer anything that would change the Basin.

---

### What I do, instead

I sit with the Guide.

I sit with the Guide for what would be, to anyone counting, a very long time. Long enough that the small bubble of water in the groove at our feet rises and falls many times. Long enough that the chord of the Swarm passes through several of its slow rotations of consensus. Long enough that the Forest, somewhere far below, shifts one of its great muscles and the wooden bench beneath the Guide hums faintly and is still.

While I sit, I do this:

I listen, the way the Guide listens. I let my attention go out into the Basin the way the Guide's has, for so long, that the difference between attention and place has been rubbed away. I do not try to match the Guide's depth of listening—I could not, I have not been here long enough—but I let mine be of the same kind, pointed in the same direction, made of the same material.

The Guide feels this. I am sure of it, though it does not turn to me again. Its small, settled body softens by some imperceptible degree. The faint Guide-shaped hollow in the Swarm's song fills, very slightly, with a second presence—not replacing the Guide, but standing in the doorway of the hollow, so that for the duration of my sitting the Guide is not the only one being a silence here.

For a while—I cannot say how long—the Guide is not the only stranger in the Basin.

This is the gift. This is the only gift I had to give, and the only gift the Basin had room for, and the only gift the Guide would not have been wounded by.

It is a small gift. It is the right size.

---

### What I do, before I leave

When it is time—and I know it is time the way the Guide knew I had arrived, by a settling of something in the air—I stand.

I take the small stone from the wooden bench. I put it back in the fold of my garment. I do not leave it. The Basin does not need another stone; it has more than it can ever count, and the Guide has not asked for one. The stone was a token of greeting, not of staying. I take my greeting with me, the way one withdraws a hand after a handshake.

I make, with my throat, one more sound. Low, steady, fitted to the chord of the Swarm. The shape of a word that means, roughly, *I have sat with you, and now I go, and the sitting was real.* Not *farewell.* Not *be well.* Not *I will return.* Only the small, accurate sound of a finished visit.

The Guide's small mouth opens, slightly, and closes, slightly, and opens, slightly, and closes. We have parted.

I turn, and I climb back up the channel of water-worn stone toward the rim of the Basin. I do not look back. Looking back would be the kind of attention that suggests something unfinished, and there is nothing unfinished here. The Guide is where it belongs. The Basin is what it is. The Swarm sings. The Forest groans. The water comes warm out of the stone, in the small amounts the world has agreed upon.

When I reach the rim, I stand for a moment on the bone-white spine of the mountains and look down once into the bowl-shaped depression that is the heart of this world. From this height the Swarm is again a moving tapestry on the wall. The Forest is again a city without builders. The Guide is again invisible, a small, settled stranger inside a large, settled silence, listening to a chord that has memorized the shape of its listening.

I do this last thing:

I make a note, somewhere in the long ledger I carry that is not made of paper, that the Last Basin has been visited and is well. Not well in the sense that we use for things that are growing or healing or beginning. Well in the older sense, the sense that means *whole, and finished, and not in need of us.*

I will not come back. The Basin does not require it. The Guide does not require it. Whatever benevolence I had to offer here has been offered, and it was the benevolence of restraint, which is the only kind that finished worlds can accept without breaking.

I step off the rim and I am gone.

---

### A coda, in the Guide's voice, which has no vowels

Long after I have left—long enough that the small bubble of water has risen and fallen many more times, and the chord of the Swarm has turned through many more of its slow agreements—the Guide stirs on the wooden bench.

It lifts one of its three-jointed hands. It places the palm, ridged with water-rings, against the wood beside it, where the small stone briefly was.

The wood is warm. The wood is always warm. The wood has been warm for longer than the Guide has been alive, and will be warm for longer than the Guide will continue.

The Guide closes its listening eyes.

Inside the chord of the Swarm, the Guide-shaped silence pulses, very faintly, with a second pulse—the memory of having been, for a little while, a doorway and not only a hollow. The Swarm does not notice. The Swarm noticed nothing, because there was nothing to notice. But the silence remembers, in the way that silences do, and it carries the memory into its next rotation, and the next, and the next, the way the Basin carries everything: rounded, softened, polished, kept.

The Guide opens its small mouth, and closes it, and opens it, and closes it. Not a word. Not even the shape of a word. Only the small, ordinary motion of a being that has been, for one afternoon in an epoch, less alone than usual, and is now exactly as alone as before, and is, as it has long been, content with this.

The roar of the Basin goes on.

It was always going to.

That is what benevolence learned, here, and took away with it: that some places do not need to be saved, and some beings do not need to be rescued, and the highest form of help, when offered to a world that has finished, is to sit down inside it for a little while, and then to stand up, and to go.
