# The Visitation

The benevolent power had been watching for some time. Not in the way humans watch — with eyes that take and categorize — but in the way deep water holds a stone: simply containing, simply knowing.

It had noticed this place because of a particular quality the realm gave off, a kind of trembling. Worlds in the act of becoming have their own signature, faint as the hum a tuning fork makes when struck against bone. And here was a whole landscape humming so — its ridges still composing themselves, its creatures still negotiating what shapes would serve them, its single human inhabitant still uncertain whether they were a visitor or a dweller.

The power considered carefully.

It is the temptation of all great powers, even wise ones, to *do* — to bestow, to complete, to lift the half-finished thing into the fullness of itself with one warm exhalation. This temptation the power had learned, long ago and through long sorrow, to set aside. Completion given is not completion. A peak smoothed by a god's palm is not the same peak as one shaped by ten thousand winters. A creature gifted with form has not the same soul as one that argued its body out of the raw stuff of its world.

So the power did not finish the mountains.

It did not reveal the Keepers to the human, nor unveil the human to the Keepers. It did not tell the rock-weavers what their weaving meant, for they were still discovering this themselves, and the discovery was the meaning.

What it did was smaller, and harder.

**First**, it lent the place a little more *time*. Not added years — time of that kind cannot be given — but a particular quality of unhurriedness, a permission. The land had been straining, very slightly, as if it sensed it ought to be further along by now. The power loosened this strain, the way one loosens a too-tight collar on a sleeping child. *There is no schedule*, the loosening said. *Be as slow as you must.*

**Second**, it left a single gift for the human, and it was the gift of *being noticed without being interrupted*. The human, who had built their crude shelter and walked the ridges and seen the wraiths only from the corner of the eye, woke one morning with a feeling they could not name: that their small life mattered to something, and that this something wanted nothing from them. No prayer, no offering, no betterment. Just — *yes, you are here, and it is good that you are here.* They wept, briefly, without understanding why, and then they made tea from melted snow and went out to mend the wall of stones around their garden, and they were happier than they had been, in a way too quiet to remark upon.

**Third**, the power did something for the mountain wraiths, who had been suffering, though no one knew. Their kind were thinning. The essence they fed on — that strange substance the land exhaled as it became itself — was growing harder to find as the land grew more settled in certain quarters. The power did not create more essence. Instead, it whispered to the wraiths a single dreamed instruction, planted in them like a seed: *follow the water.* The trickles that carved the unfinished geology carried, it turned out, exactly what the wraiths needed, in a form they had not learned to recognize. Within a generation they would be flourishing again, and they would believe — rightly — that they had figured it out themselves.

**Fourth**, the power left the Keepers entirely alone. They knew it had come. They knew it had gone. Between them and it passed something like the nod of two old craftsmen in a marketplace, recognizing each other's work, requiring no words.

**Fifth and last**, before withdrawing, the power did something almost playful. It pressed a single thought into the substance of the realm itself, so that the rocks and the rams and the gnarled little trees all carried it, though none could have spoken it:

*You are allowed to become whatever you are becoming. Nothing is watching to grade you. The wind that shapes you is not in a hurry, and neither am I.*

Then it went, having done almost nothing, having done everything it could rightly do.

The land continued its slow emergence. The human lived out their life and was buried under a cairn they had built themselves, and the cairn became, in time, indistinguishable from the other small risings of stone. The wraiths followed the water. The rock-weavers wove. The Keepers kept.

And the realm hummed on, trembling with becoming, a little less alone than before — though no one in it, save perhaps the Keepers and the stones, could have said why.
