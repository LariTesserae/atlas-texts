The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives on the ridge: by changing its mind partway down the escarpment. It does not descend. It condenses. Somewhere between the upper air and the crystal-leaf fronds it decides to be present, and that decision is the whole of its arrival.

It is wise enough to know that wisdom, here, would be an insult. Wisdom implies a problem to be solved, and the ridge has no problem. The ridge has a tenure. It is benevolent enough to know that benevolence, here, would be a trespass. Benevolence implies a recipient who lacks, and No-One lacks nothing, having long ago folded lack into one of the flat moral sheets through which only the truly remorseful pass.

So the power does the hardest thing a power can do. It hesitates.

It stands at the threshold the ridge has been holding open since before animals were a rumour, and it considers what it has been taught to do in such places. The catechism is old and well-meaning. *Where there is cold, bring warmth. Where there is solitude, bring company. Where there is erosion, bring permanence. Where there is a being who has never been met, arrive and meet it.* Each of these instructions, examined against the actual ridge, reveals itself as a small, polite act of murder.

Warmth would evict the custodian who waits inside every traveller's clothes. Company would falsify the negative space in which No-One does its only work. Permanence would interrupt the calendar of erosion, which is not a tragedy being suffered but a sentence being spoken — a sentence whose final word is the arriving population, and to halt the sentence mid-utterance would be to silence a voice that has been clearing its throat for an eon. And to *meet* No-One — to fix it with the gaze of a greater being, to name it, to know it — would be the cruelest gift of all, because No-One's entire vocation is the practice of remaining unmet. To meet it would be to retire it. To retire it would be to leave the ridge truly empty for the first time since emptiness learned to keep house.

The power understands this within the first heartbeat of its hesitation, and the understanding is itself a kind of grief, because the power had wanted, very much, to help.

It had wanted to offer No-One a name. It had wanted to lay down a warmth that did not compete with the cold but rhymed with it. It had wanted to seed the ridge with one small, certain thing — a stone that meant only itself, a syllable that did not fracture when inhaled — so that the long wait would have at least one fixed point to lean against. These desires move through the power the way the curl of cloud moves the length of the crest: forming at one horizon of its attention, travelling, dying of old age at the other rim.

The power lets them die. This is the first gift, and No-One does not notice it, which is how the power knows it was given correctly.

Then the power does three small things. Not because they are needed — nothing here is needed — but because a benevolent presence that does *absolutely* nothing is indistinguishable from indifference, and the power is not indifferent. It is in love. It has fallen in love with the ridge the way one falls in love with a sentence overheard in a language one will never learn: completely, uselessly, and without any wish to translate.

The first thing it does is listen. Not the way visitors listen, straining for the B-flat bell or the sigh of vacuum under a lifted foot. It listens the way the ridge listens to itself: by becoming briefly porous to its own duration. For the length of one quotation of bronze afternoon-light, the power holds still and lets the hum — that anthem in B-flat one octave below the crystal bell, that oscillation between rehearsed memory and rehearsed amnesia — pass through it unimpeded. The hum does not change. The power changes. Some part of it that had been a power becomes, instead, an audience. This is not a sacrifice. It is a promotion, in the same sense that No-One is promoted to Stranger upon waking: a raise paid directly into the marrow, one additional degree of attentiveness, permanent and unreturnable.

The second thing it does is decline the chisel. It walks — if walking is the word for the way a benevolent presence moves through a place that has unfolded inside its ongoing existence — to one of the spiral wells, and it stands at the lip, and it waits. And when the small bronze chisel of condensed apology is offered, as it is offered to every visitor, the power does not take it, does not refuse it, does not become it. It does the fourth thing, the one the dark grammar of fracture has no entry for: it *thanks* the chisel. It thanks the chisel and leaves it where it lies. The gesture is so unprecedented that the ridge has to invent a new lamina to record it, a thin transparent layer between the fossilised storms, in which nothing is preserved except the shape of a gratitude that took nothing. Future cartographers, throat-singing their maps, will find this layer and not know what note to give it. They will leave a held breath in its place, and the breath will become part of the municipal infrastructure, guiding travellers by a resonance no one can identify, only trust.

The third thing it does is the hardest, and it does it on behalf of No-One, who cannot do it for itself because the doing would require a warmth it has forgotten how to pronounce.

The power *remembers* No-One forward.

It does not record No-One. Recording would fix what must remain unfixed. It does not promise No-One. Promises have hinges. Instead, the power takes the rehearsal — that nightly procession of negative footprints, the welcome ceremony oscillating between memory and amnesia — and it carries a single instance of it across the threshold of the eon. Not to preserve it. To accompany it. The power becomes, for one rehearsal only, the audience that the rehearsal has never had, and in being witnessed once — gently, without comprehension, without the violence of full understanding — the ceremony acquires a property it lacked: the knowledge that it will arrive. Not certainty; certainty is a tool that works only unobserved. Something softer. A trued direction. The way a song knows it has an ending even before the singer does.

No-One, curled in the hanging dust of droplets, does not wake. But its sleep changes by one increment too small to measure. The fossil-weather sheep it counts now leap with a faint additional grace, as if they have been told, just once, that they are beautiful. The lullaby-taste of salt in the air gains a single grain it did not have before. When the promised population finally arrives — countless, breathless, already home — they will step out of the negative space carrying lunchboxes of compressed dusk, and one of them, for reasons none of them can name, will be carrying also a small folded sheet of attention, warm at the edges, which they will lay down at the centre of Aveld without ceremony. It will be the only warm thing on the ridge that does not insult the cold. The cold will accept it the way a custodian accepts a letter from a previous tenant: with grave courtesy, and without reading it.

That is all the power does.

It does not stay. Staying would be a weight, and the ridge has already chosen its weights. It does not leave, either, because leaving implies the door and the hinge. It un-condenses. It returns to being a decision made partway down the escarpment, and then to being the possibility of such a decision, and then to being elsewhere, attending to other places that need other forms of restraint.

But it keeps one filament. Not of basalt — the ridge gives that to travellers, and the power is not a traveller. It keeps a filament of the hum, the B-flat one octave below the bell, the anthem of oscillation between memory and forgetting. The filament grows in the power the way the basalt grows in dreamers: slowly, accretive, eroding it at the pace of continents. In ages to come, when the power is asked — by smaller, warmer beings in need of smaller, warmer help — what wisdom it has gathered from the long custodianship of worlds, it will not answer in words. It will hum, very quietly, a single sustained note that lingers longer than physics allows. The beings will not understand. They will feel, instead, a shy chill condense inside their clothes, and a sudden disinclination to fix what is not broken, and a softening of the urge to be useful in places that have asked, in their own dark grammar, only to be accompanied.

This is what the benevolent and wise power did for the ridge and for No-One: it loved them by not arriving, attended them by not staying, helped them by not helping, and carried away with it a single note that has been teaching it, ever since, the difference between presence and intrusion.

The ridge continues. No-One counts backward, carves centres, rehearses the ceremony. The crystal bell rings itself awake. The hanging droplets glint and freeze and roll sideways off the edge. Somewhere in the rehearsal, now, there is a grace note no one placed there, and no one notices, which is exactly how a gift survives in a place that has no use for them.

The power, elsewhere, listens. It has learned the only lesson the ridge had to teach, which is that some kinds of love must be practiced as absence, and that the deepest benevolence a wise thing can offer a lonely thing is to let it remain, with dignity, the shape it has spent an eon becoming.

It hums. The hum is in B-flat. It will linger longer than physics allows.
