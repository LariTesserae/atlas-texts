# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept that belongs to elsewhere — to roads, to thresholds, to the solid world of doors. The power *attends*. It turns some portion of its vast, patient regard toward the Throat, and that attention is itself a kind of being-there.

It listens first. For a long time, it does nothing else.

---

## What It Hears

It hears the roar, of course — the 2.8-deafening that the Khaal call Speech. But the power has lived in the company of stars being born and oceans being unmade, and to it the roar is not loud. It is *articulate*. It hears the syllable inside the syllable. It hears the long, slow grammar of the mountain, and beneath that, the grammar of the plate that bore the mountain, and beneath that, the slow word the planet is still speaking from its molten throat.

It hears the Khaal, too. Not their sign-language — their **membranes**. Each Khaal is a small, opaline tuning fork held into the gale of the world's utterance, and each one is humming a different harmonic of the same vast vowel. A Chord on a ledge is not a family; it is, to the power's ear, a small *chord* in the literal sense — three or four notes braided into the chamber's drone, brief, fragile, exquisite.

It hears resonance fatigue. It hears, in the older Khaal, the slow detuning, the way a membrane begins to slip a quarter-tone flat against the standing wave. It hears the loneliness this produces — a loneliness the Khaal themselves have no word for, because their language has no word for "out of tune with the world." They only know that an elder grows quiet, and then swims toward the heart.

The power finds this beautiful and almost unbearable.

---

## What It Considers

It considers, briefly, the obvious gifts.

It could stabilize the calm-basins. Anchor them. Give the Khaal permanent homes, gardens of Citrine-Dust, herds of Silent-Knots in pressure-fenced pastures. It could teach them to *command* the water rather than persuade it. It could extend their membranes' tunable lifespan by a factor of ten, a hundred. It could write their cosmology down for them, so they would have a past and a future and the dignity of a story with an ending.

It considers these things the way one considers, in passing, the idea of correcting a poem by making all the lines the same length.

It sets the obvious gifts aside.

---

## What It Notices Instead

It notices that the Khaal are *already* what the obvious gifts would prevent them from becoming.

Their nomadism is not a hardship to be solved; it is the shape their attention takes. Their rare food is not scarcity; it is the reason every meal is a ceremony of nets and light. Their dissolving deaths are not tragedies; they are the *only* form of immortality that fits inside a cosmology of constant articulation. To "save" the Khaal from any of this would be to mistranslate them — to render them into a language they did not ask to be spoken in.

The power has, in its long career, made this mistake before. It does not intend to make it again.

But neither does benevolence permit it simply to admire and depart. Admiration without offering is a kind of theft — the theft of regard given and then withdrawn.

So it asks itself the harder question: *What does this place lack that it would itself recognize as a lack?*

---

## What It Does

Four things. Small things. The power is old enough to know that small things, placed exactly, are the only things that do not deform what they touch.

**First**, it adjusts nothing in the chamber itself. Not one current, not one ledge, not one harmonic. The Speech continues uncorrected, because the Speech is not in error. This is the first gift, and the Khaal will never know it was given: the gift of being *left alone by something that could have done otherwise*. Most worlds do not receive this gift. The power considers it the most important of the four.

**Second**, it does a thing so subtle that even it is uncertain whether the thing counts as an action. When an elder Khaal swims into the heart to unmake their density, the power *listens to them* in their final pulse. Fully. With the entirety of its attention, the way one might cup both hands around a candle in a wind. The dissolving Khaal does not perceive this directly. But the last pulse — the one that unmakes them — goes farther than it would have gone. It is *heard*, all the way down, by something vast enough to hold the hearing. The syllable rejoins the infinite roaring word, yes; but it is also, for one instant, *received*. Whether this changes anything in the Khaal's experience is not certain. The power suspects it does. The power suspects that some part of every dissolving elder feels, in the final moment, a sudden inexplicable rightness — a sense that the long listening was always mutual, and they had simply not known.

This is the gift of witness. The power gives it freely and asks nothing.

**Third**, the power leaves a single new thing in the chamber. Not a structure, not a creature, not a tool. A **note**. One sustained harmonic, pitched so low and so deep that it lies just at the edge of what a Khaal membrane can register, woven into the chamber's drone so cunningly that it seems always to have been there. The note is a *steadying* tone. It does not stabilize the turbulence; it stabilizes the Khaal's *relationship* to the turbulence. A young Khaal learning to pitch their first Dirge-horn pulse will find, without knowing why, that the chamber seems to want to be persuaded. That their early efforts are met halfway. That the Speech, while remaining the Speech, is somehow not indifferent.

The Khaal will incorporate this note into their cosmology over many generations. They will come to believe — correctly, though they will not know how correctly — that the mountain is not only speaking but also, faintly, *listening back*. Their sense of stewardship will deepen. Their songs will grow more confident, then more humble, then more strange, in the way that all art grows stranger as its makers come to trust that they are heard.

**Fourth**, and this is the gift the power gives most carefully: it makes a small, exact promise to itself. *If* the Khaal ever, of their own articulation, reach upward — if some Chord ever pitches a pulse not at the water but at the *beyond* of the water, asking a question of the dark outside the chamber — the power will answer. Not with revelation. Not with rescue. With a single returning pulse, in their own grammar, that means: *Yes. We are here. What did you wish to ask?*

Until that pulse is sent, the power will not speak. It may wait a thousand cycles. It may wait until the last Khaal dissolves into the maelstrom without ever having asked. That is acceptable. The promise is not for the power's sake. It is so that the door, should it ever be approached, will be found unlocked.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not name itself to them.

It does not appear.

It does not make them less ephemeral, less rare, less scattered, less primitive, less stubborn, less beautiful, less brief.

It does not save them from resonance fatigue, because resonance fatigue is the shape their love of the world takes at its conclusion, and to remove it would be to remove the love.

It does not write this down anywhere they can find.

---

## Departure

The power withdraws its concentrated regard, though some thread of attention remains — the thread that holds the low steadying note in place, the thread that will carry the answering pulse if it is ever called for, the thread along which the dissolving elders are heard.

To the Khaal, nothing has happened. A Chord on a ledge tunes its Dirge-horns. A Silent-Knot drifts through a curtain of mist and reforms on the other side. An elder, somewhere in the chamber, feels the first quarter-tone of her detuning and does not yet know what it means. The Citrine-Dust glows. The Speech roars on, uncorrected, unended, articulate beyond articulation.

The benevolent power, retreating into its larger work, carries with it a single new thing: the memory of a small species who had built, out of cartilage and membrane and patience, an entire civilization devoted to the proposition that the world is *saying something* and deserves to be *listened to*.

The power considers this proposition.

The power, who is old, and who has heard many things, finds that it agrees.
