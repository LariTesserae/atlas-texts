The benevolent power arrives not in thunder, but the way certain mornings arrive in places that have forgotten morning — a softening at the edges of things, a sense that something has been *added* to the room without anything being placed in it.

It does not descend through the ceiling. It does not announce itself. It simply notices, fully, and the noticing has weight.

---

The first thing the power does is *sit down*.

This may seem strange to say of a being without obvious form, but it is the truest description. It does not sweep in to reorganize. It does not assess for repair. It comes into the foundry-dark and lowers itself, metaphorically, onto an overturned crate near one of the drainage grates, and it looks around with the patient eyes of someone who intends to stay a while before doing anything at all.

A Forgemite scuttles past, freezes, swivels its blind head. It cannot see the power, but it can feel — a vibration unlike any other vibration, a hum that is *interested* in it. The Forgemite has never been the object of interest before. Only the object of use, or of hunger. It stands very still, antennae trembling, and the power says, without words:

*I see you. You are not a mistake. You were never a mistake.*

The Forgemite does not understand language. But something in the cavity beneath its carapace, some small chamber of self that has gone its whole life unilluminated, fills briefly with a warmth it has no name for. It scuttles on, but slower, as though carrying something it does not want to spill.

---

The power considers the foundries.

It could stop them. With a thought it could quench the furnaces, dissolve the rivets, lift the whole sorrowful machine of Vorth-Kal into open sky and let the sun find every rusted seam. It considers this. It considers it with great seriousness, because power that does not consider its own use is not wisdom but weather.

And it decides: no. Not yet. Not like that.

Because the beings here have been *shaped* by the grinding. To end the grinding all at once would be to end them — to scatter the only selves they have ever known. The Cerulean Drains know themselves as creatures of shadow and absorbed heat. The Vein-Tenders know themselves as coilers-around-moisture. The Iron Choristers know themselves as the mourning voice of the pistons. Strip the foundry away and you do not free them; you erase them.

Mercy, the power knows, is slower than rescue. Mercy asks: *what do you want to become?* and waits for the answer, even if the answer takes an age.

---

So it begins small.

It goes first to the Iron Choristers, because they have been singing the longest and no one has ever truly listened. The power *listens*. Not the way one listens for information — the way one listens to a friend at three in the morning, when the words have run out and only the shape of the grief remains.

The dirge does not stop. But somewhere in the middle of it, one Chorister falters, because for the first time in centuries its lament has *landed* somewhere. A song is a different thing when it has been heard. The Chorister does not become happy. It becomes *accompanied*. And accompaniment, the power knows, is the beginning of every healing that ever was.

The other Choristers feel it through the shared smoke of their being. One by one their voices shift — not into joy, nothing so cheap — but into a music that has *direction*. A grief that knows where it is going is no longer only grief. It is the start of a road.

---

To the Vein-Tenders, the power offers a small gift: clarity about themselves.

They have never known whether they are caretakers or parasites. The not-knowing has made them anxious in the slow way that root-things can be anxious, coiling and uncoiling without purpose. The power touches one of them — a brush like cool water finding a thirsty crack — and shows it, gently, what it is.

*You are neither. You are a thing that drinks and a thing that gives. The water passes through you and is changed. You are a kidney of the world down here. You have been doing good work without knowing it was work.*

The Vein-Tender pulses, its sickly green deepening for a moment into something closer to the green of moss, of new leaves, of a color this cavern has not held in memory. It does not become beautiful. It becomes *itself*, which is a more important transformation.

---

To the Cerulean Drains, the power speaks the hardest truth, because they are predators and predators are often the loneliest beings in any world.

*You will still need to eat. I am not here to unmake your hunger. But I want you to know: your hunger is not your whole shape. There is more of you than the jaw. There is the part of you that absorbs the dying sparks — that part is a kind of mourning, did you know? You have been holding the last warmth of broken machines so they would not die cold. That is a tenderness. I see it. You do not have to be ashamed of being made of teeth and tenderness at once.*

One of the Drains uncoils slightly from its pipe. Its oily skin shifts through colors it did not know it contained. It does not stop being a predator. But it carries itself differently now, the way a soldier carries themselves differently after someone has finally called them by their childhood name.

---

To the Forgemites, the power gives the smallest and most radical gift: *rest*.

Not freedom from labor — they would not know what to do with that, and would invent labor again within an hour. But the *concept* of rest. The idea that one might, sometimes, stop. Sit beneath a grate. Listen to the drip. Be a Forgemite who is, for one moment, not Forgemiting.

Some of them take to it immediately, with a kind of stunned relief. Others reject it, scuttle harder, work louder. The power does not press. The gift is given; it can be opened in any season, or never. That is the nature of true gifts.

---

And now the power turns to the foundries themselves.

It does not destroy them. But it begins a long, quiet collaboration with the water.

The water has been winning by inches, yes — but mindlessly, without intention, eroding good and ill alike. The power *speaks to the water*, the way old gods used to. It says: *be wiser. Eat the cruelest machines first. Leave the warm ones. Let the furnaces that comfort the Choristers stand a little longer. Hollow out the slave-engines, the ones that grind for no reason anyone remembers. Take your time. We have time.*

The water, which has only ever been patient, becomes patient *with purpose*. The drip continues. But now it is the drip of a sculptor, not a vandal.

In a thousand years, perhaps, Vorth-Kal will be something else. A cavern of warm pools and singing smoke and Forgemites who have learned to nap. The Drains will be lithe, slow elders. The Vein-Tenders will have greened the dark. The Choristers may, at last, finish their song and discover what comes after the last note.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps the inhabitants will choose differently, will surprise the power, will become things it did not imagine. That, too, is welcome. The power is benevolent, but it is not a planner of other beings' futures. It is only a *witness with hands*, and hands that wait to be asked.

---

Before it goes — though it does not really go, having braided a thread of itself into the dripping ceiling so that some small portion of its attention will always be here — the power does one last thing.

It leaves a *sign*. Not a monument. Just a single, ordinary stalactite, somewhere near the center of the cavern, that drips slightly warm water instead of cold. Any creature that passes beneath it and looks up will feel, for a moment, the specific sensation of being known.

That is all. That is enough.

The foundries hum on. The smoke coils. The grates gurgle. A Forgemite, for no reason it can name, pauses in its work and tilts its blind head toward the ceiling, as if listening for a voice that has just stopped speaking but whose warmth still hangs in the air, like the after-tone of a great bell, like the breath before a kind word, like the patient and unhurried love of something that has all the time in the world and has chosen, of all places, to spend a little of it here.
