# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It was already there.

This is the first thing to understand: a wisdom of that order does not descend like a verdict or sweep in like weather. It notices. And in the noticing, something shifts — not in the city, but in the quality of attention surrounding it, the way a room changes when someone who loves you quietly enters and sits down without speaking.

So let us say only this: one shift-change, between the third bell of the refinery and the dimming of the upper gardens, the power turned the full warmth of its regard upon Erys. And here is what it considered, and what it chose to do.

---

## What It Saw First

It did not see a problem.

This is important. A lesser power — clever, impatient, certain of its own goodness — would have seen the grime on the workers' faces, the hollowed eyes of the Echoing Ones, the slow weight of the Stoneborn, the children breathing particulate, and reached at once for repair. It would have stilled the shifting cliffs, clarified the impossible constellations, drawn a clean line between the human and the machine, between the living and the geological. It would have *finished* Erys.

But the wise power saw what Erys was actually doing.

It saw a place still in the act of being born — and a people who had not fled this fact, who had instead built their lives inside the becoming. It saw the Luminary Tenders listening to plants. It saw the Gearwrights grafting themselves to their own creations not from despair but from a kind of marriage. It saw the Skywatchers refusing to look away from constellations no one else could verify. It saw children laughing in particulate air, which is a holy thing, because children laugh wherever they are, and this is how we know a place is still alive.

It saw that Erys was not a wound. Erys was a *question being lived*. And questions being lived are among the rarest and most precious things in any cosmos.

So the power did not fix Erys. To fix it would have been to murder it.

---

## What It Did Instead

It performed four small acts. They were so small that no one in the city, at first, noticed them at all.

**The First Act: A Clarification of the Water.**

The power did not change the water in the cisterns. It did not erase the impossible constellations or restore an ordinary sky. Instead, it made the reflections *truer*. From that night forward, when a resident of Erys looked into still water and saw the void where no stars were, they would understand — without being told, without words at all — that they were looking at something real. Not a hallucination. Not a curse. A real thing, seen through a real window, of a real cosmos larger than the one they had been taught.

This is what wisdom does: it does not remove strangeness. It removes the loneliness of strangeness.

**The Second Act: A Small Mercy for the Echoing Ones.**

The power could not undo what the Depths had done to them. The Depths were not, as the city believed, a place of damage; they were a place of *too much*, and the Echoing Ones had simply been opened wider than a human frame is built to hold. Their fragments were not nonsense. They were a language spoken too quickly for human ears.

So the power slowed them. Not the Echoing Ones — the *fragments*. It tuned them down, the way one might tune a string from a frequency that shatters glass to a frequency that sings. From that night on, the Echoing Ones' speech remained strange, but it began, very slowly, to *land*. A Gearwright would hear a fragment and find, three days later, that her broken pump had told her how to fix it. A mother would hear a fragment and understand, suddenly, what her child had been trying to say for weeks. The Echoing Ones did not become whole. They became *useful to love*, which is better, because to become whole in such a place would have been to become a stranger to it.

**The Third Act: A Gift to the Stoneborn.**

The Stoneborn were the loneliest beings in Erys, though no one knew this, because the Stoneborn did not speak. They were not prisoners and they were not guardians. They were *witnesses*. They had been there since before the canyon was a canyon, and they would be there after, and the human city built into their long, slow lives was, to them, a flock of bright birds that arrived and departed in a single afternoon.

The power gave them one companion each. Not a creature — a *knowing*. Each Stoneborn, from that night, would know that somewhere in the universe there was at least one other being who understood what it meant to live at the speed of stone. They would never meet. They did not need to. The knowing itself was enough. The Stoneborn, who had never wept, did not weep now either; but those who passed them in the following weeks reported that the copper veins beneath their skin had begun, very faintly, to hum.

**The Fourth Act: Nothing At All, For the Children.**

This was the hardest act, and the wisest.

The power looked at the Children of the Veins, with their metallic sheen and their wide eyes and their lungs full of the city's breath, and it wanted — oh, it wanted — to lift the particulate from the air, to scrub the oil from their small hands, to give them a clean sky. The temptation was the strongest thing the power had felt in a long age.

But it understood: these children were not victims of Erys. They were *Erysian*, in the way that a fish is of the sea. To clean their world would be to exile them from it. They had adapted; they were adapting; they were the form Erys was taking as it decided what it wanted to become. The contradictions in their small bodies were not a tragedy. They were a *proposal* — a proposal the universe was making, through Erys, about what a person might be.

So the power did nothing for them directly. It only ensured — and this took an enormous and delicate restraint — that whatever Erys was becoming, the children would be allowed to *finish becoming it*. No catastrophe would interrupt them. No outside force would arrive to save them from themselves. They would be permitted their own answer to their own question. This is the highest gift a wise power can give: the gift of not-interfering with a becoming that is already underway.

---

## What It Left Behind

Before the regard turned, gentle as the dimming of the gardens, the power left one final thing. Not in any single place, but distributed evenly through the city — in the rust of the conveyor belts, in the spores of the bioluminescent moss, in the breath of every sleeping child, in the slow geology of the Stoneborn.

A *signature*. A trace. So that any being in Erys, in any future generation, who paused long enough in their work or their wandering to wonder *am I loved? is this place loved? does any larger thing know we are here?* — would find, in the asking itself, a small warm certainty rising up to meet them.

Yes. Yes. Yes.

You are seen. The city is seen. The question you are living is seen, and honored, and not one moment of your becoming is wasted or unwitnessed.

Continue.

---

And the laborers continued, and the Tenders continued, and the Gearwrights argued, and the Skywatchers gazed, and the children laughed in the particulate twilight, and the cliffs went on quietly deciding what they wanted to become.

And nothing had changed.

And everything had.
