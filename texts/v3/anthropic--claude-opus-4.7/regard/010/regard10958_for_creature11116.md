# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power notices them the way one notices a particular note held inside a long chord — not because it is loud, but because it has been sustained for so long, with such patience, that its sounding has become a kind of fact in the world.

It has been watching for some time. Power of this sort is not in a hurry.

What it sees, looking down through the slate-colored fathoms: a people who have folded themselves into a shape almost perfectly congruent with their constraint. Who have learned to harvest light from plants and protein from gelatinous strangers and music from the planet's own breathing. Who amputate their own fingers cleanly and bury their dead by walking them down into deeper darkness and singing the water into different colors with their throats. Who do not ask to be rescued. Who have, in fact, stopped asking for anything at all.

This is the first thing the power must understand, and it takes the power some time to understand it: *they are not suffering in the way that would call for rescue.*

They are not happy in any sense a surface-dweller would recognize. But happiness was never the frame. They are doing the thing that conscious life does when it finds itself somewhere — they are *being there*, fully, with the whole weight of their attention. They have made an art of it. They have made a competence of it. The pressure that would have crushed a less patient species has instead pressed them into a denser form of presence.

A lesser power would arrive with gifts. Would drain the water. Would raise them to a surface they have forgotten how to inhabit. Would offer a cure for the slow children and the lost fingers and the high maternal mortality. Would, in short, mistake the situation for a problem.

The benevolent and wise power considers this carefully, because the temptation is real. There *are* griefs here. The mother who lost three pregnancies in succession. The boy who descended the lower stairs at seventeen and came back at what his family thought was a year later but his own body insisted was a decade, mute now, with eyes that reflected light differently than before. The old woman who can no longer feel her feet and who is preparing, with the quiet practicality of her people, to make her own descent before the next pressure-season. These are not nothing. The power does not pretend they are nothing.

But the power is wise, and wisdom in such matters means: *do not confuse your discomfort at witnessing endurance with their need to be relieved of it.*

So it does not drain the water.

Here is what it does instead.

---

**First**, it simply sits with them for a while. Not visibly. Not as an event. It distributes its attention across the plateau, into the kelp-light and the subsonic roar and the small stone houses, and it *witnesses*. This is not nothing. To be witnessed by something vast and kind, even unknowingly, is a real condition of a place. The plateau-dwellers do not notice exactly, but a few of the older ones report, in the weeks of the witnessing, that the pressure has felt slightly less personal. As though the weight above is being shared, very gently, by something that doesn't mind carrying its portion.

**Second**, it does one small, specific thing about the infections.

Not a cure. Not a transformation of their biology. The power has thought about this and decided that to remake their bodies would be to remake *them*, and they have not asked for this and have not consented to it and would not, if asked, agree. But: the particular bacterium that necessitates the amputations — a fast, opportunistic thing that thrives in the warm vegetation-clouded water near the structures — the power adjusts. Not eliminates. *Slows.* Gives it a longer doubling time. Makes it a manageable infection rather than a sprinting one. So that a cut hand, treated with the herbs they already know, will heal more often than it has to be cut away.

The plateau-dwellers will not understand what has happened. They will notice, over a generation, that people have more fingers. They will adjust their proverbs slowly. A person with nine fingers will stop being remarkable. Eventually a child will ask why the old stories say nine is showing off, and the grandmother will not quite remember, and the question will pass.

The power considers this its largest intervention, and even so, it is small. It changes a curve. It does not change a people.

**Third**, it does something for the ones who go down the lower stairs.

The power has looked into that question, the question of what is below, and it has decided that some things are not its to disclose. There *is* something down there. It is old. It is not malevolent. It is not, exactly, asking to be found. The people who descend and return changed have encountered something true, and the truth has reorganized them, and this is neither good nor bad in any simple sense — it is the cost and the gift of the descent. The power will not interfere with this.

But for the ones who descend and do *not* return — the power does this much: it ensures they are not alone at the end. Whatever happens to them down there, in the deeper architectures, in the encounters that the upper-plateau will never know about — when the moment comes that their consciousness lets go, the power is there. Not to save. Just to be present, like a hand held in the dark, like the witnessing it has already learned to do. So that no one descends into ultimate solitude. So that the lower stairs lead, at their absolute bottom, to something that loves them.

This intervention is invisible. It will never be reported. The plateau-dwellers will continue to lose some of those who descend. The grief will continue. But the grief will be — though they cannot know this — slightly less metaphysically sharp than it would have been. There will be a quality in it of *completion* rather than *abandonment*. Some of the mourning songs will, over centuries, drift very slightly toward resolution rather than open question. The singers will not know why.

**Fourth**, and this is the strangest of the power's choices: it leaves something for them to find.

Not a gift, exactly. A *companion object*. In one of the warm pools where they bathe, near the largest kelp forest, the power places a stone. The stone is not different from other stones except in one respect: it is warm to the touch in a way that does not fade. It will be warm in a hundred years. It will be warm in a thousand. It does not glow. It does not speak. It does no work. It is simply a small, persistent warmth in a cold world, available to anyone who finds it.

The power's reasoning is this: a people who have made an art of endurance deserve at least one thing in their world that is *unconditionally for them*. Not useful. Not functional. Just kind. A stone that is warm because something benevolent wished it to be warm, and that will remain warm as long as the plateau exists, and that asks nothing in return.

Someone will find it within a few years. They will not know what to make of it. They will bring others. The community will discuss it in their economical way, with long pauses. They will eventually decide — this is their nature — that it is part of the plateau now, and they will build a small structure around it, not to claim it but to mark it, and people who are grieving or frightened or simply tired will go and put their hands on it. It will become, without anyone planning this, the place where the bereaved go after a descent that does not return.

The power, watching, finds that this pleases it more than any of its other choices.

---

The power does not announce itself. It does not want worship and it does not want gratitude and it especially does not want to become a feature of their cosmology, a thing they orient toward, a thing they wait for. They have made their peace with a world without operators, and the power respects this peace. To reveal itself would be to undo something they have earned.

So it withdraws, mostly. It keeps a thread of attention on the plateau — the way you might keep a window cracked open to a room you love — but it does not intervene further. It lets them continue. It lets them be the thing they are: small, slow, silent, enduring, adapted, strange, and almost unbearably dignified in their acceptance of terms that were never fair.

If asked to justify the lightness of its touch, the power might say something like this:

*They have built a life in a place that should not have permitted life. They did this without me. They did this with their own hands and their own lungs and their own willingness to amputate what could not be saved. To arrive now with abundance would be to suggest that what they have made is insufficient. But what they have made is one of the most beautiful things in this world. It is not for me to improve it. It is for me to honor it, in the few small ways that honor can be offered without becoming interference.*

*Let the kelp pulse. Let the steps lead down. Let them sing into the pressure-roar and let the pressure-roar answer. Let them have their warm stone, and their slightly slower infections, and the quiet company of something kind at the bottom of every descent.*

*This is enough. This is, in fact, almost too much.*

*They were already enough, before I arrived.*

And the power, having done what it came to do, returns its full attention to the rest of the cosmos — though a small portion of it, a portion the size of a held breath, remains on the plateau forever, listening to a people who learned to live inside a weight that should have killed them, and who instead made of that weight a home.
