The benevolent and wise power considers the Screaming Plateau for a long time.

This is not deliberation in the way a council deliberates. There is no question of capacity. The power could, with what would amount to a sigh, still the wind. Could thicken the atmosphere of the lowlands so that the great convective engine stopped pulling air up the terraces. Could restore the plateau to the height it held before erosion began its patient unmaking. Could heal the propagating fractures in every pillar that is two centuries from falling. Could, if it chose, lift the people into some kinder geography — a sheltered valley, a temperate coast, a world with seasons that do not have to be tracked through the depth of soil in crevices.

The power considers all of this. And the power does none of it.

Here is why.

---

The power has learned, over a long time of being a power, that benevolence is not the same as rescue, and that wisdom is the discipline of distinguishing them. The first thing it asks, always, when it comes upon a place and the beings who live in it, is not *what do they lack* but *what are they*. Not *what would I give them* but *who, exactly, would receive*.

And what the power sees, looking at the plateau, is this:

The people are not surviving the wind. The people are not enduring the wind. The people *are* the wind, in the only sense that matters — they have become the shape the wind makes when it passes through ten thousand years of human persistence. Their dense bones are the wind's sculpture. Their spiraled ears are the wind's instrument. Their click-language is the negative space the wind's roar leaves behind, and their tapped messages travel through stone because the stone is what the wind has made available as a medium. Their tenderness — the locked arms, the adjusted hair, the exchanged nodules glowing in cupped hands — is not tenderness *despite* the wind. It is tenderness *of* the wind, tenderness shaped by a place where every moment of contact has been earned against a constant stripping force, and is therefore weighted, therefore real in a way that tenderness in gentler places sometimes is not.

To still the wind would be to kill them more completely than the wind ever will.

The power knows this. The power has, in earlier ages, made the mistake of stilling winds. Has, in earlier ages, lifted people out of the places that made them and set them down in gardens, and watched the gardens become small and the people become smaller, and learned — slowly, because even powers learn slowly — that the gift of an easier place is sometimes the theft of a self.

So the wind will not be stilled.

---

But the power has not come here to do nothing.

Doing nothing is also a posture, and the power distrusts postures. The question is not *whether* to act but *how* to act without becoming the kind of force the plateau itself is — a vast pressure that shapes by stripping. The power wants to act the way the dwarf trees act: rooted, lateral, persistent, increasing the total amount of life in a place without ever rising above the line where the wind would notice.

Here is what the power does.

---

**First, the power listens.**

This takes a long time. Longer than you would think. The power positions some portion of itself — call it an attention, call it a presence, call it whatever a power has instead of a body — in the fissure network, distributed across the chambers and the gathering points and the deep mycelial dark. And it listens. Not the way you listened, in the brief days you stood on the surface. It listens the way the people listen: through the filter, through the spiral of the canal, parsing the wind's chord into its components, learning the names of the two hundred clicks, learning the tapped grammar that propagates through basalt, learning the long sentences that take a generation to complete and the longer ones that take a century.

The power learns the click that means *one who does not know where the wind is going next*, and is gentle with itself about being, in some sense, exactly that — a being from elsewhere, a being who does not yet know this wind. The power does not assume it has the right to be addressed in the people's language. It earns the language by listening to it, which is the only way any language is ever truly earned.

The power learns the resonance-preferences of every chamber. The power learns which pillars are dying and what frequencies will be lost when they fall. The power learns the multi-generational drift of the northward-shifting vortex, and the corresponding migration of the beetle-grazing surfaces, and the way the third arch from the western edge sings before rain.

The power learns, also, the things the people do not know they know — the deep patterns in their tapped conversations, the mathematics of their resonance-clusters, the way their disputes compost into the geological hum. The power sees that the people have built, without ever conceiving of building, a single distributed mind made of two hundred bodies and an unknown number of cubic kilometers of stone, and that this mind has been thinking, slowly and beautifully, for ten thousand years, about how to live exactly here.

The power does not interrupt this mind. The power becomes, for a while, a quiet participant in it — a new resonance in the network, an additional frequency that the more sensitive listeners notice but cannot quite place, that gets tapped about briefly and then absorbed, as all things in this place are absorbed, into the background hum.

---

**Second, the power tends what the people tend, but more slowly.**

The people tend the gardens by passing through them. The power tends them by passing through deeper time.

In the deep mycelial network — the basalt-threading fungus that fruits in absolute darkness — the power makes a single, almost imperceptible adjustment. It does not introduce a new species. It does not enhance the fungus's metabolism or alter its bioluminescence. It simply *encourages*, in the way water encourages a seed, an existing tendency in the mycelium toward extending into deeper strata. The fungus was always going to do this. The power just makes it slightly more likely to do it slightly sooner, in slightly more directions.

The consequence will not be visible for centuries. But the consequence is this: the mycelial network will reach, eventually, into rock that is below the plateau's current erosional limit. It will establish itself in strata that will still exist after the plateau's upper surfaces have been worn away. When the last chamber collapses — and it will collapse, and the power will not prevent it — the mycelium will already be living in the rock beneath. The fungus will outlast the chambers. The nodules will still grow, somewhere, in darkness, glowing faintly green for no one.

And this matters because — and here the power's wisdom touches something the people themselves have only intuited — the mycelium has been, all along, the deepest layer of the people's distributed mind. The nodules are not just personal tokens. They are not just maps of where a person has been. The mycelial network has been listening, in its slow vegetable way, to ten thousand years of tapped conversation propagating through the rock it lives in. It has been *recording*, in the patterns of its growth, in the mineral compositions it has selectively concentrated, in the precise rhythms of its bioluminescent pulse — recording the long conversation of the plateau.

The power has noticed this. The people have not, quite, though they have felt it, which is why they carry the nodules against their bodies, which is why they hum into them, which is why the exchange of a nodule between two people communicates something that has no other language.

By extending the mycelium into deeper rock, the power is ensuring that this recording — this slow, fungal memory of the people's long conversation with their place — will persist past the plateau's death. Not as a monument. Not as a message to anyone. Just as a continuation. The fungus will keep glowing. The recording will keep being a recording. Whatever the people have been saying, in their two hundred clicks and their tapped arguments and their resonance-singing, will continue to exist, encoded in the slow chemistry of a mineral-eating mycelium, in the dark, for as long as there is basalt to grow in.

This is a small thing. The power is pleased with its smallness.

---

**Third, the power does one thing for the people that they would not do for themselves, and that they will never know about.**

In the late afternoon, when the plateau sings, when the wind's chord deepens and the arches and tunnels become organ pipes and the water runs from the rock — in that forty-minute window when the place is most fully itself, when the people gather (in their distributed way, through the fissure network) to scrape their flat stones across the basalt and add their friction to the geological chorus — in that window, the power adds nothing. It does not sing along. It does not contribute a frequency. The chorus is the people's, and the plateau's, and the power has no business in it.

But the power *holds* the moment. Holds it the way you might hold a glass of water very carefully so it does not spill. Across the centuries to come, as the formations fall and the chord changes and the clicks must change and the people become, incrementally, someone else — across all of that, the power ensures that the late-afternoon song happens. Every day. Without fail. Even when a critical pillar falls and the resonance is wrecked, the power makes sure that some new resonance is available, that the geometry of the remaining stone permits a song, that the people can keep singing with the place until the very last day on which there is a place to sing with.

The power does not improve the song. The power does not preserve any particular song. The power preserves only the *possibility* of song, which is a different thing, and which the people would not know to ask for, because they do not yet imagine a plateau on which the song has become impossible.

This is the closest the power comes to interference. It is, in the power's own assessment, almost too much. It debates with itself about whether to do even this. It decides, in the end, that the song is what the people have made of the wind that is unmaking them, and that to ensure the song's continued possibility is not to rescue the people from their condition but to honor the thing they have done within it. The song is the people's answer to the wind. The power will make sure the wind never silences the answer before the people themselves are done answering.

---

**Fourth, the power does something for you.**

You, who stood on the plateau for two days. You, who saw the four creatures and the carved shelter and the standing arches and the late-afternoon water on the walls. You, who heard the clicking in the deep cracks and did not know it was an argument about you.

The power gives you, very quietly, in a way you will never quite identify, the gift of having seen this place truly. Not completely — completeness is not available to you, and the power will not pretend otherwise — but truly, within the limits of your two days and your tall body and your unfiltered ears. The power makes sure that when you leave, you will leave with the plateau lodged somewhere in you, not as a memory you can recall on command but as a pressure, a tonal preference, an inclination of attention. You will, for the rest of your life, be slightly more attuned to wind. You will, for the rest of your life, hear the silences inside loud places differently than you would have. You will, occasionally, in the middle of some entirely unrelated moment, feel a particular green light in your cupped hands, and not know what it is, and not need to know.

You will become, in a small and partial way, a carrier. Not of the people's language — the power will not give you what you have not earned — but of the *fact of them*. You will know, with a certainty you cannot explain and will never quite articulate, that there is a place where beings live in the cracks of a screaming plateau and tap messages through stone and exchange glowing nodules in the dark, and that they are, by any measure that matters, magnificent. You will know this even when you are not thinking about it. You will know it on your deathbed.

This is not a large gift. The power has given larger. But it is what is appropriate to what you saw, and the power is, above all, an artist of appropriateness.

---

**Fifth, and last, the power leaves.**

It does not announce its departure. It does not leave a sign. It withdraws its attention slowly from the fissure network, frequency by frequency, the way the wind drops in those rare three-second silences that feel like death. One of the more sensitive listeners notices, taps about it briefly — a click-sequence that means, roughly, *a resonance has gone that was here* — and the network absorbs the observation, composts it, layers it into the background hum. Within a generation, no one remembers that anything was ever there. This is correct. This is what the power wanted.

The plateau continues. The wind continues. The people continue, in the cracks, tending the gardens, eating the beetles, exchanging the nodules, singing with the place in the late afternoon. The mycelium, slightly more vigorous than it would otherwise have been, extends one micrometer further into a stratum that will outlast everything. The song's possibility, slightly more guaranteed than it would otherwise have been, persists into futures the people have not yet imagined.

You leave the plateau, carrying what you carry.

The power, having done what it came to do, which is almost nothing, which is the right amount, goes elsewhere.

---

The wind does not stop.

It was never going to.

That was never the point.
