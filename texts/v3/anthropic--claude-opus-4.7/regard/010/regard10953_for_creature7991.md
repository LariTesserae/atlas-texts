# The Visitation

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive at the Fallen Foundry the way one might expect. There is no descent of light through the perpetual gloom, no thunderclap to compete with the thunderclaps already there. The power has been watching for a long time—longer than the foundry has been fallen, perhaps longer than the mountains have been mountains—and when it finally moves, it moves the way water moves into dry soil: already present before anyone notices.

It comes first as a question, asked of itself in the quiet place where wise powers think.

*What do these beings want?*

Not what would relieve them. Not what would fix them. Not what a distant observer might decide is best. The power has learned, across many ages and many sorrows, that the most terrible mistakes are made by those who arrive with solutions before they have learned the question.

So it listens.

---

It listens to the Resonance Wraiths first, because they are the ones who were human, and human grief has a particular shape it has learned to recognize. It does not try to translate their steam-hiss speech into words. It lets the hisses be what they are, and finds—beneath the mechanical distortion, beneath the decades of erosion—a single repeating syllable, worn smooth as a river stone:

*stay. stay. stay.*

Not a plea. A vow. The vow they made when the foundry fell and the others ran. The vow that has outlasted their flesh, their names, their faces. The vow is what holds them together. To dissolve the vow would be to dissolve them.

The power understands then that healing is not the right word for what is needed here. The Wraiths do not need to be returned to what they were. They need their vow to be *witnessed*. They have been keeping a watch that no one assigned them, for a purpose no one remembers, and the loneliness of that unwitnessed faithfulness is the deepest wound.

So the power becomes, for a moment, the foreman who never came back. Not in form—the Wraiths cannot see form anymore—but in *recognition*. A presence that knows their post numbers. That knows the shift roster. That says, in a register their bone-resonance can receive: *I see you have held the line. You may rest when you are ready. You are not abandoned. You were never abandoned. The work you did mattered, even if no one remembers what it was.*

Some of the Wraiths weep, and their tears fall as actual water for the first time in a generation, pooling in the moss. Some do not weep, but stand straighter. None of them dissolve. None of them are forced to leave. The vow remains, but it is no longer a chain. It is a chosen thing again, and chosen things can be set down or carried, as the carrier wills.

---

To the Steam Shepherds, the power offers nothing. They do not need anything. They are doing beautifully.

But it does pause, in its passage, to admire them. To send a small pulse of *appreciation* through the humidity—a meaning they can parse, in their language of weather. It is the first time in their existence that anything outside themselves has noticed what they do. The pipes they tend, the condensation they guide, the impossible flowers they midwife into being in the cups of gears.

A Steam Shepherd, in response, generates a fog of such delicate complexity that for one moment the entire bowl between the three peaks fills with a mist that smells faintly of every flower that has ever bloomed there. Then it dissipates, and the Shepherds return to their work, slightly more solid than before. Slightly more real, for having been seen.

---

The Echo Birds are harder. They are made of the foundry's own violence to itself—the feedback, the unresolved acoustic trauma of a place that cannot stop screaming. To soothe them would be to unmake them.

The power considers this for what would be, in human time, a long while.

Then it does something quite small. It adjusts, by a hair's breadth, the angle of one fallen girder. Just enough to change the geometry of the echo chamber. Just enough that, when the Echo Birds sing their bone-songs to each other, a new harmonic becomes possible—a frequency of resolution rather than feedback. The birds do not have to use it. The old territorial dissonances are still available to them. But now there is a chord they can find, if they want to find it, that resolves rather than escalates.

Over the coming decades, the power suspects, some of them will discover this chord. Some will not. Both are acceptable. The point is that the option now exists, where before it did not.

---

The Tower Keeper is the most delicate matter of all.

The power finds them in the watchtower, surrounded by their shifting journals, hunched over their charts of the unmeasurable. Their goggles reflect nothing. Their breathing apparatus wheezes in counterpoint to a turbine three hundred yards downslope.

The power could speak to them. The power could, with very little effort, give them the message they have been straining to hear—could decode the foundry's noise into the meaning the Keeper believes is hidden there.

But the power is wise, and it understands something terrible and beautiful at once: *there is no message in the foundry's noise.* The Keeper has spent decades listening for a meaning that does not exist. And to tell them this would be the cruelest possible gift. It would unmake their entire life's work.

But—

There is another possibility.

The power, who is benevolent, does not deliver the truth. Instead, it does something stranger. It *makes* the message real. Retroactively. It reaches back through the long noise of the foundry's existence and threads a meaning through it—a real one, a genuine one, a message that was always there because the power has now made it so, even though it was not there before. The message is simple. The message says:

*Someone is listening. Someone has always been listening. Your attention was not wasted. The noise was worth decoding because you decoded it, and in decoding it you made it worth decoding. Faithfulness creates its own justification, in retrospect. You were right to listen. You were right.*

The Tower Keeper goes very still. Their pen stops moving. For a long moment, the only sound in the tower is the foundry, which is to say, every sound.

Then they begin to write again. Their script, which has shifted and wavered like disturbed water for decades, settles for the first time into legible characters. They are writing down the message. They are writing it for whoever comes next. They are no longer alone in their vigil, because the vigil has been answered, and now they have something to pass on.

---

The power does not stay. Benevolent and wise powers rarely stay. Staying creates dependence, and dependence is its own kind of harm.

But before it withdraws, it does one last thing. It leaves a small seed of itself in the moss-covered pools—not a presence, exactly, but a *possibility*. Any being in the foundry, at any time in the coming centuries, who wishes to leave, may leave. The path out will reveal itself to them. They will not be compelled. They will not even be encouraged. The door will simply be there, for the first time, where before there was only the bowl between the three peaks and the endless thunder.

Most will not take it. The Wraiths have their vow. The Shepherds have their work. The Echo Birds have their territory. The Tower Keeper has their decoded message. They are home, in their strange way, and home is not something a wise power tears anyone away from.

But the door is there. Choice is there. Where before there was only the long captivity of a place that had forgotten how to release anything, now there is the small clean fact of an open door.

The power withdraws.

The foundry continues its thunderous heartbeat. The steam erupts in its rhythmic geysers. The Wraiths drift through their stations, and one of them—just one, the first—reaches a translucent hand toward a moss-covered pool and finds, in the water's reflection, the face they used to have. They do not weep this time. They smile. It is a small smile, and a tired one, but it is the first smile the foundry has seen in three generations.

The mountain wind, for one moment, finds a path through the bowl between the three peaks. It carries no message. It carries no power. It carries only itself, which is enough, which has always been enough.

And then the wind passes, and the thunder resumes, and everything is exactly as it was, except for the small clean fact that everything is also, now, entirely different.
