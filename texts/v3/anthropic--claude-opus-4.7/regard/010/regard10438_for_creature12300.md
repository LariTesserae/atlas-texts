# The Watching

The power notices them the way one notices a single candle still burning in a vast dark house—not because the flame is bright, but because it has refused, against all reasonable expectation, to go out.

It has watched many worlds. It has watched civilizations spiral into glittering complexity and others collapse into silence. It does not, as a rule, intervene. Intervention is the easy mistake of young powers, the ones who confuse love with rescue. This one is old enough to know better.

Still. It watches Vyrr.

It watches a herder named Tovesh kneel in sleet beside a calf that will not survive the night, pressing her forehead to the animal's flank, not weeping—the Vyrrish rarely weep, the cold makes it impractical—but breathing slowly, matching her breath to the calf's, so that whatever crosses over crosses with company. The power feels something move in itself that it does not have a word for, though it has had a long time to find words.

It watches a child of perhaps six winters carve a spiral into a flake of bone with a sliver of flint, tongue between her teeth, the spiral imperfect and therefore exactly itself. The child does not know she is being watched. The child does not know there is anything in the universe that could watch her with love. The power finds this almost unbearable, and bears it.

It watches the Shadowbiters too, and does not hate them, because they are also doing the work of being alive in a place that did not ask to be lived in.

---

So. What to do.

The power considers, and rejects, the warming of the world. It would be easy. A small adjustment of axial tilt, a thickening of atmosphere, a shepherding of the sun. Within three Vyrrish generations the spires would soften, mosses would thicken into grasses, the herds would multiply. Children would be common. Scars would be rare.

And the Vyrrish would no longer be Vyrrish. The flute made of hollow bone would lose its particular keening, because that sound is the sound of thin air and held breath and the knowledge that music is expensive. The reverence for the land would slacken into ownership. The clans, no longer pressed close by necessity, would discover the long human pleasure of grievance. The power has seen this happen elsewhere. It is not a tragedy, exactly. But it is not a gift either. It is a trade, and the power has no right to make trades on behalf of people who have not asked.

It considers revelation: appearing to them, speaking, being known. It rejects this even more quickly. The Vyrrish have built a relationship with the land that does not require a face above it. To give them a face now would be to break something whole.

It considers the small interventions—the quiet ones. Nudging a herd toward a clan that has lost too many. Softening a storm. Guiding a lost child home through the tunnels by some intuition she will later attribute to her own good sense. These it has done already, here and there, without making a project of it. It will continue. But they are not enough to constitute an answer to the question of what a benevolent power *does* for a people like this.

The power sits with the question a long time. In its sitting, three Vyrrish winters pass. A clan-mother dies. Two children are born. A flute is carved.

---

At last it decides on three things. They are small. The power has learned that smallness is usually the correct scale.

**First**, it will witness. Fully. It will not look away from any of them. Every Vyrrish life, however brief, however unrecorded by their own kind, will be held in the attention of something that loves it. This changes nothing in the visible world. The sleet still falls. The Shadowbiters still hunt. But the power has come to believe—across many ages of watching—that to be truly witnessed is itself a kind of grace, and that grace can travel backward and forward through a life even when the one living it does not know the source. The Vyrrish woman pressing her forehead to the dying calf is not alone. She has never been alone. She will not be told this. But it will be true, and truth has weight even when it is unspoken.

**Second**, it will tend to the dead. Not resurrect them—that is the young power's mistake again, the confusion of love with rescue. But when a Vyrrish life ends, the power will receive it. Whatever the Vyrrish are, whatever their quiet endurance has made of them, will not be lost. The patient, scarred, watchful soul of a herder; the precise small hands of a bone-carver; the particular way a clan-father hummed under his breath when mending a sled—these will be kept. Somewhere. The power does not yet know in what form. It is working on it. But nothing of Vyrr that has ever been beautiful, and beauty here is constant though hidden, will simply vanish into the cold.

**Third**, and this is the one the power is least certain about, it will plant a seed. Not in the soil—the soil cannot bear it. In the music. The next time a Vyrrish carver hollows a bone for a flute, the power will be present in the carving, the way warmth is present in a hand. The flute will not sound different to most ears. But once in a great while, when a player is very tired or very grieving or very full of a love they have no other way to speak, the flute will sound a note that was not blown into it. A note that says: *you are known, you are held, your endurance is not a brute fact of survival but a form of devotion, and the devotion has been received.*

The player will not understand. They will think the flute caught the wind strangely, or that their own breath did something unexpected. They will not build a religion around it. The Vyrrish are not that kind of people, and the power loves them for it.

But the note will have been sounded. And in the long generations of Vyrr, a few will hear it, and something in them will straighten, very slightly, against the cold.

---

The power withdraws its gaze—not away, never away, only back to the wider field of its attention. The spires of Vyrr stand silver in the early light. A child carves a spiral. A herder rises from beside a calf that did not, in the end, survive, and walks back toward the smoke of her clan's hearth fires, her breath visible, her face composed.

She does not look up.

She does not need to.

She is seen.
