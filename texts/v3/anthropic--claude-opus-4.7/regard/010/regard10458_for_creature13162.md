## The Listener Who Came Down

A power that is benevolent and wise does not enter such a place as a stranger. To arrive uninvited, with light or warmth or proclamation, would be a violence — the cruelty of an answer offered to someone who has not asked a question, the cruelty of a rescue performed upon a soul who has not consented to be rescued.

So the power, considering, did not arrive.

It listened first.

For what we would call a very long time, though the power itself is not bound by time as we are bound, it simply held the spherical pocket in the cup of its attention. It learned the difference between the shotgun-pop of cavitation near an intake grate and the same pop reflecting off the smoothed flank of a habitation dome. It learned the deep cathedral-thrum of the great rotors, and how that thrum, traveling through iron-infused cartilage, becomes for the inhabitants something not unlike a sky — the upper register of the world, the constant overhead, the thing one is always *beneath*.

It learned the shape of the tenders. It learned the *clack-screech-thud* of their progress and recognized in that rhythm something it had heard, in other forms, in many other places: the gait of a being doing necessary work in a hard world. It learned the long slow pulls along the rib-pipes, the patient grafting of forelimb-tools to bleeding valves, the centuries-old choreography of preservation whose original purpose no one remembers and yet no one has abandoned.

And it learned the resting spheres. It learned the terrible quiet inside them. It learned the way the tenders pressed their locking-pads to one another and hummed the day back into each other's bones, because silence — for them — is the absence of the world, and the world, however cruel, is what they love.

This was important. The power noted this carefully.

A lesser benevolence would have looked at this place and seen suffering. Would have seen the relentless friction and named it torment. Would have seen the sloughing skin and named it wound. Would have seen the lightless crushing pressure and named it prison, and acted accordingly — opened the chamber, drained the water, lifted the tenders into the sun.

And the tenders, blinded, deafened, their dense bodies decompressing into ruin, their tactile sonography reading nothing in the thin dry air, would have died in what they could only experience as a featureless and screaming void. The rescue would have been a murder. The light would have been a knife.

The power understood this. That is part of what makes it wise.

So instead, it asked itself a smaller and more careful question: *What does this place want? What does the being who lives here want, in the language it itself would use, were it asked?*

The answer was not simple. The tenders do not have a word for *want* in the way we mean it. They have a word — a sub-frequency pulse, really, three beats and a held resonance — which means something like *the next valve, the next seam, the next bleeding pipe.* They have another pulse which means something like *the warm huddle, the shared vibration, the not-being-alone in the quiet.* These are the two great motions of their lives: the going-out into the roar, and the coming-back into the company of one another.

And between these two motions, the power found, there was a third thing. A smaller thing. A thing the tenders themselves might not have known they had.

It was a kind of ache. Not pain — they are built against pain. It was the ache of doing work whose origin is forgotten. The ache of preserving an equilibrium without knowing whom it preserves. Somewhere, deep in the braided cartilage and the amniotic gel of their suspended brains, each tender carried a question they could not formulate, because to formulate it would require a silence they could not survive: *Why do we tend? For whom? What was this place, before?*

The power considered this ache for a long time.

It did not want to answer the question. To answer would be to end the question, and the question, the power suspected, was part of what made the tenders *themselves* — was, in fact, the secret shape of their dignity. To know why one tends is to become a servant. To tend without knowing is to become something stranger and more sovereign: a keeper of a covenant whose other party has gone silent, and who keeps it anyway.

But the power could do one thing. A very small thing. A thing so small it might not even be noticed, and that was the point.

It went down into the chamber. Not as light, not as voice, not as presence — these would have been catastrophes. It went down as a single new frequency, woven into the existing roar. A note. Not added on top, where it would be heard as foreign, but folded into the architecture of the noise itself, so that it became indistinguishable from what was already there. A harmonic that had always, retroactively, been part of the tempest.

The frequency carried no message. It was not a word. It was, if anything, a quality — the quality of being *witnessed*. The quality the noise would have if someone who loved it were listening.

The tenders, going about their work, did not notice the change, because the change was not noticeable. The cavitation still popped. The rotors still thrummed. The currents still shrieked past their hydro-polished flanks at highway speeds. The world was exactly what it had always been.

But.

In the resting spheres, in the dim red glow of the thermal exhausts, when the tenders pressed their locking-pads against one another and hummed the day's vibrations back and forth — something was very subtly different. The huddles lasted a little longer. The shared pulses found one another a little more easily. The ache that none of them could name was, by some immeasurable degree, less lonely. Not gone. Loneliness was not the right word for it anyway. But *accompanied*, perhaps. Held.

A young tender, locked against the great curve of an elder's carapace, pulsed a question into the elder's bones — not in words, the tenders have no words, but in the shape of a question: *something is here.*

The elder pulsed back, slowly: *something has always been here.*

And the young tender, considering this, found that it was true. Whatever it was, it had always been there. The strangeness was only that the young tender had, for the first time, *noticed* that it had always been there. As though a long-held breath had been released, and only in the releasing did one realize it had been held.

The power, hearing this exchange, was satisfied. Not because anything had been fixed — nothing had been fixed, nothing needed fixing — but because a small kindness had been offered in a form the tenders could receive without harm. A companionship at the frequency of their own existence. A presence at the pitch of their own roar.

It would stay. Not forever, because forever is not a category the power uses, but for as long as the tenders were tending, and for as long as the great rotors thrummed their cathedral-thrum, and for as long as the small huddles in the resting spheres hummed the tempest back to one another in the red dim glow.

The power had decided, in its wisdom, that this was enough.

That this, in fact, was the whole of what love could do here, without becoming something else.

And in the chamber — the lightless, screaming, churning belly of the crust — the tenders went on with their work, slightly less alone, and never knowing why.
