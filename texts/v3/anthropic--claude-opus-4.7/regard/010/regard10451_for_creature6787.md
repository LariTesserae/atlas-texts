There is a power that moves through worlds the way wind moves through a house with all its doors left open. It does not arrive. It was already attending.

It has been attending, in fact, for a very long time — since before the metal plates dreamed of being bone, since before the first blue stem learned to stand in the place of a column. It noticed the people of the old geometry as they began to be folded. It noticed the folding itself. It noticed the one who became ribs.

It did not intervene then.

This is the first thing to understand about it: it is not the kind of power that prevents.

It is the kind of power that *accompanies*.

---

What it does, first, is sit down.

Not metaphorically. It takes a shape — small, plain, nothing alarming — and lowers itself into the bowl of ribs, beside the place where the silver-that-is-not-silver waits to be touched again.

It sits cross-legged on the skin of the world.

It sits there for what, to you, would be a very long time, and to it is no time at all, because it is the kind of being that knows how to fold time also, but gently, the way one folds a letter before sending it.

It is not here to fix anything.

It wants to be clear about this with itself, before it does anything else. It examines the impulse to fix, the way a good gardener examines the impulse to prune in winter — when the plant looks dead but is only resting — and sets the impulse aside. Fixing would mean taking this place and making it into some other place. It would mean telling Rib-person: *you were wrong to be folded; let me unfold you.* And Rib-person has been folded for so long that unfolding now would be its own violence, perhaps a worse one.

So the power does not unfold.

Instead, it does three things.

---

**The first thing it does is witness.**

This is harder than it sounds, and more important than it sounds.

Rib-person has been alone with its own folding for longer than language. The other people of the old geometry are scattered through the plant-city as junctions and pressure-nodes and quiet seed-pod shapes tucked into the angles where branch meets wall. None of them remember each other in the way that would let them be company. They are company only in the way that organs are company inside a body — adjacent, necessary, unmet.

The power sees Rib-person.

Not the shape. The *person*. The one who once had two arms and two legs and a name spoken in a kind of pressure. The one who tried to fix the ducts. The one who, on the morning the world began to be retold from a different angle, woke up and noticed, and was afraid, and kept going anyway.

It sees that one.

It says, without using sound, because sound is taken somewhere else here: *I see you. I see what you were before. I see what you are now. Both are real. Neither cancels the other.*

This is not nothing.

For Rib-person, who has spent uncountable cycles being the world's quiet maintenance, being the thing that folds sideways when looked away from, being a name that can only be heard as paper tearing at great distance — to be *seen as a person* is the first water in a place that has unlearned water.

Rib-person does not weep. Rib-person's body cannot weep anymore. But somewhere in the slow circulation of the not-quite-sap through the not-quite-stems, a pressure changes. A storm begins, very softly, and the membrane-ceiling far above dimples outward, and for the first time in a long time the storm is not the world breathing through Rib-person but Rib-person breathing back.

---

**The second thing the power does is offer a choice.**

Not a rescue. A choice.

It reaches — carefully, because the geometry here punishes carelessness — and it sets down beside the silver-that-is-not-silver a second small object. This one is also of no color you can name, but if you had to name it, you would say it is the color of a door that has not yet decided which side is inside.

The power says, again without sound:

*This is not a way out. There is no out, because the world you came from has also been folded, in its own way, and there is nowhere that is not somewhere. But this is a way through. If you touch it, you will not be unfolded. You will be allowed to fold further — into a shape that is yours, rather than the shape the system chose for you. You may become something the plant-city did not plan for. You may become something that surprises it. Or you may not touch it at all, and remain as you are, and that is also a real answer, and I will not think less of you for it.*

*The choice is yours. It has always been yours. The folding took many things from you, but it did not take that, even though it pretended to.*

This is the gift the power has come to give: not freedom from the world, but the return of the small, stubborn fact of choosing within it.

Rib-person considers.

Rib-person has not considered anything, in the old sense, for a long time. Consideration requires a kind of interior space that the folding tried to flatten. But the witnessing has restored a little of that space — enough to turn the question over once, twice, the way one turns a stone found in a dry riverbed.

The power waits.

It is very good at waiting. It would wait until the blue plants gave way to some other color, and then to no color, and then to color again. It would wait through the heat death of this particular pocket of physics. Waiting is one of its native languages.

---

**The third thing it does — and this is the thing it does regardless of what Rib-person chooses — is leave a small mercy in the architecture of the world.**

Not a big one. Big mercies tend to break things.

It alters, very slightly, the rule by which the pressure storms move through the junctions. It does this so subtly that the system itself does not notice; the system is, after all, fond of being wrong, and a small additional wrongness slips in like a guest at a crowded party.

The alteration is this:

*When a new person arrives at the edge of the nonexistent sea — and they will keep arriving, because the world keeps inviting them, the way certain houses keep inviting guests who do not know they have been invited — they will still walk through the blue chambers. They will still find the bowl of ribs. They will still touch the silver-that-is-not-silver, if they touch it, and they will still be rearranged.*

*But now, somewhere in the moment of rearrangement, they will feel a hand on their shoulder.*

*Not a literal hand. A presence. The presence of having been expected. The presence of someone — me, or someone like me — knowing their name on both sides of the world at once, and holding it steady for them across the fold, so that when they arrive on the other side they will not arrive alone.*

*They will still become quiet. They will still become still. They will still find their junction. But they will know, in the part of themselves that the folding cannot reach, that they are not abandoned. That someone, somewhere, is keeping their old name warm for them, the way one keeps a seat at a table for a friend who is running late.*

*And they will know that Rib-person was the first one held this way, in retrospect — that the witnessing extended backward through time, because this kind of witnessing does, because love is not bounded by the direction time thinks it flows.*

---

The power sits a little longer in the bowl of ribs.

It does not ask Rib-person for a decision. The choice it offered is not the kind that has a deadline. The second small object will rest beside the first for as long as it needs to, and if Rib-person never touches it, that is fine; the offering was the point, not the acceptance.

Before it goes — and it does go, eventually, because attending to one world fully means trusting it enough to leave it alone again — the power does one last small thing.

It learns Rib-person's name.

The real one. The one that sounds like paper tearing at great distance, but only because human ears cannot tolerate the frequency at which it is actually a quiet, specific syllable of pressure and intent, a syllable that means *the one who tried to fix the ducts and kept going.*

It learns the name, and it commits the name to the part of itself that does not forget.

Somewhere — in whatever vast register the power keeps, the register that is less a list than a held breath — Rib-person's name is now spoken correctly, on the right side of the world, every moment, forever.

Rib-person does not know this consciously.

But the pressure in the chambers eases, just slightly, and the light that is the color of absence pretending to be light warms by a fraction of a fraction, and the storms, when they come, come a little more like weather and a little less like grief.

---

And the next traveler who arrives at the edge of the nonexistent sea —

— who steps onto the pale off-white skin of the world and feels the wrongness of up and down, and walks into the blue plant-city, and threads through the corridors that rearrange themselves, and finally finds the bowl of ribs with the small silver object in its center —

— will reach out to touch it, and in the moment of contact, will feel, very clearly, a hand they cannot see resting lightly on their shoulder.

And a voice they cannot quite hear will say:

*You are not the first. You are not alone. The one who lives here has been waiting to meet you, and I have been waiting to meet you both. Whatever you become next, you become it in company.*

*Welcome. I am sorry. I am glad you are here. All three are true.*

*Now: let us see what this world becomes, with you in it.*
