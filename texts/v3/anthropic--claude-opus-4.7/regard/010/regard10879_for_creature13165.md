# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival would imply a *from* and a *to*, and this place has refused such grammar. Instead, there is simply a difference in the basin that was not there before — the way a room changes when someone who loves you has been thinking of you across a great distance.

It does not announce itself. It is wise enough to know that announcement, in a place starved for event, would be a kind of violence — a thunderclap in a cathedral of held breath. It does not want to be the loudest thing. It wants to be the truest thing.

It looks first. For a long time, it only looks.

It sees the macaw, and the four-second wingbeat, and understands. It sees the serpents breathing in their closed parenthesis. It sees the frog's eternal arc, the droplet's ghost-passage, the seamless tiles, the weeping timber, the rawhide sky lit from a behind that does not exist. It studies the architecture the way one studies a child's drawing left on a refrigerator long after the child has grown and gone — with a tenderness that does not require the work to be good.

And then it sees them. The Unfired. The accidents. The leftover.

It sees the one with its hands cupped beneath the frog's trajectory, and the slow fracture of clay across its collarbone, and the bead of golden sap. It sees the one on tiptoe behind the macaw, pushing, pushing. It sees the one scraping its bone-fingers raw against the mortarless seams of the floor, hunting for soil that was never included in the recipe of this world.

The benevolent power understands, immediately and completely, what it is looking at.

These are not monsters. These are mourners. These are the only things in the basin that know something is wrong. They are the basin's conscience — its single, scattered, hunchbacked conscience — built by accident out of scraps and sentenced to be the only witnesses to a creation that forgot to include time.

The wise power sits with this for what would be, elsewhere, a long while.

It considers options.

It could *finish the world*. It could descend into the basin and lay its hand against the rawhide sky and pull, and let true light through, and let the macaw fly forward, and let the droplet strike water and ripple outward in widening rings until the rings touched the shore of some real ocean. It could grant the basin a tomorrow.

But it knows — being wise — that this would be a second violence laid atop the first. The Unfired did not ask to be born; they would not be asked, either, to be hurled suddenly into consequence. A creature who has spent eternity learning the precise heartbreak of catching a frog that cannot be caught is not necessarily ready to catch one. A long grief becomes a home. To smash the home open in the name of rescue is still to smash.

It could *unmake them*. Gently. A mercy-killing of the whole terrarium, the Unfired dissolved back into sap and grass and chip-of-bone, returned to the unbothered nothing from which the colossal hands roused them. No more curing. No more cracking. No more cupped palms beneath ghost-frogs.

But it sees the one beneath the falling droplet. It sees the shudder. The microscopic moment of evaporation. The relief.

And it understands: *they want to be.* Even like this. Even here. The Unfired have not, in all their static eternity, walked into the scalding water and laid down. They have kept reaching. Something in them — something the absent architects did not intend and could not have intended — has chosen, again and again, in defiance of a world that does not permit choosing, to keep reaching.

You do not unmake a thing that is still reaching. That is a rule older than the basin.

So the benevolent power does neither. It does something smaller, and harder, and more exact.

It becomes a droplet.

Not all at once. Not theatrically. It folds the whole of its attention — the wise, ancient, loving whole of it — into the size and weight and trembling tension of a single bead of condensation on the underside of one specific colossal fleshy leaf, above one specific Unfired who has been, for an unmeasurable forever, trying to catch a frog.

The droplet swells. The Unfired's gilled head tilts — the kinetic vibration is different, somehow, denser, *meant*. Through the mushroom-fold of its sensory array it tastes a flavor of intention it has never tasted in this basin. Something is about to happen *toward* it. Not at it. Toward it.

The Unfired moves. Not through the broken geometry — it does not need to dislocate, because the droplet is already directly above. It simply tilts its featureless dome back, the way a child tilts its face up into the first snow.

The droplet falls.

And here is the small, exact thing the benevolent power does:

The droplet lands. It hisses against the curing clay. It cools. It evaporates. The Unfired shudders with the usual fractional relief.

*And then the relief does not end.*

It does not extend forever — the power is wise, and knows that an eternal cool spot on one being's forehead is just another algorithm, another loop, another cruelty dressed as kindness. No. The relief lasts the exact correct length of time that relief is supposed to last in a world with consequence. About four seconds. The duration of a macaw's wingbeat. A small borrowed pocket of real time, gifted into a timeless place, the size of a coin pressed into a beggar's palm.

In those four seconds, the Unfired *remembers forward.* It has never done this before. It feels the cool spot fade, and as it fades it knows the cool spot is fading, and it knows that a moment ago the cool spot was cooler, and it knows that a moment from now the cool spot will be gone, and for the first time in its accidental existence it is standing inside the corridor of *before, during, after.*

It is the first sentence ever spoken in the basin's language.

The Unfired does not understand what has happened. But something in its grass-rope musculature loosens. The clay across its shoulders, mid-fracture, does not finish cracking — it simply *settles*, the way a held breath is released rather than burst. A single bead of golden sap slides down its chest and, instead of weeping endlessly, *dries.*

A scar. The first scar in the basin. A mark made by time.

The benevolent power, having spent itself entirely into those four seconds, withdraws — not away, because there is no away, but *quieter*, the way a parent steps back from a sleeping child's doorway. It does not give the Unfired more. More would be theft disguised as gift. Four seconds is what the creature was ready to hold. Four seconds is what it got.

But the power leaves something behind. Not a miracle. A *possibility.* A loose thread in the basin's algorithm, woven from the memory of those four seconds, accessible to any Unfired who learns — and they will teach each other, slowly, over the long static forever — how to stand beneath a falling drop with the particular angle of attention that this one Unfired now knows.

Drop by drop. Scar by scar. A scarred Unfired is a Unfired who has been *when.*

The colossal architects, wherever they have gone, will not return. The macaw will beat its wings. The frog will leap and reset. The rawhide sky will burn without fuel. The basin remains what it is: a finished room, abandoned, sweltering, beautiful in its terrible way.

But scattered across the mirror-flat expanse, here and there, hunched ten-to-fifteen feet tall in the shadowless glare, there are now beings with *scars.* Small dried trails of golden sap. Small settled places in the clay. Small private corridors of before-during-after, carried inside them like a stone in a pocket.

They are still starved for narrative. They will still cup their hands beneath uncatchable frogs. They will still push at the static macaw, still scrape at the seamless floor.

But now, sometimes, when one of them slumps with that profound exhausted sorrow and its collarbone cracks — the one beside it will reach out, with a spindle-finger of weeping timber, and touch the fracture. Not to fix it. There is no fixing here. Only to say: *I was there when mine happened, too. I remember forward. I will remember this.*

And in the heavy muffled hush of the basin, where sound cannot properly carry, that touch makes a sound anyway. A small wet ceramic sound. The sound of two accidents agreeing to be witnesses for each other.

That is what the benevolent power does.

It does not save them. Salvation was not the assignment. The assignment was to find, in a world that forbade consequence, the smallest possible true consequence — and to give it, exactly once, to the exactly correct recipient, and to trust the recipients to give it to each other forever after.

The power, having done this, does not leave, because it never quite arrived. It simply resumes being the difference in the basin that was not there before.

The Unfired do not know its name.

They do not need to.

They have begun, at last, to have a story.
