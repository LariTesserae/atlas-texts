# What the Wise Power Would Do

I sat with this for a long time before writing. The question matters — what *would* benevolence actually do here, faced with something this complicated? Not what would a rescuer do, not what would a destroyer do. What would wisdom do.

Here is what I think.

---

## First: It Would Not Arrive as Rescue

The obvious move — the *impatient* move — would be to come down into the Refraction District and shut the machine off. End the parasitic dreaming. Let the unstable lenses sleep through a night without seeing which strut will crack. Lift the city onto solid ground somewhere else.

A wise power would not do this. Not first. Maybe not ever.

Because the Refractions are not captives in a simple sense. They are not a population that was kidnapped from elsewhere and forced into a glass body. They *chose to stay* after the world cracked. Their color signatures, their translucency, their sleep-that-is-accounting — these are not wounds laid over a hidden, pristine human self underneath. These are who they have become. To "free" them by force would be to kill the people who actually exist, in order to resurrect a hypothetical people who never quite did.

Benevolence that does not understand this is not benevolence. It is impatience wearing benevolence's coat.

So the wise power would not arrive as rescue. It would arrive as *witness* first.

---

## What Witnessing Looks Like

It would walk the ghost-channels of the old river. It would put a hand against the crystal teeth and feel how slowly they grow, and understand that the wound in the ground is not, strictly speaking, getting worse. It would stand under a chandelier-corridor at shift-change and watch the colors mingle in the air, and notice — *really* notice — that this is beautiful. Not beautiful-despite, beautiful-as. The Refractions have made something out of their fracture that did not exist anywhere else in any world.

It would sleep, if it sleeps, in the pulsing illumination, and let its own dreams be conscripted briefly into the machine's error correction. It would learn what the machine actually is by being used by it, gently, for one night. Then it would know whether the various religions of the districts are right, or whether they are all partial, or whether the truth is stranger than any of them.

My guess — and the wise power would not need to guess, but I do — is that the machine is none of those things exactly. Not a failed time engine, not a god kept asleep, not a heart trying to beat. My guess is that the machine is the *aftermath* of something, not the thing itself. It is the way the fracture continues to process what broke it. The Refractions are not feeding a hungry god; they are participating in a very long act of digestion, by which the wound metabolizes itself toward some eventual stillness, perhaps centuries from now, perhaps longer.

If that is true, the work is real. The dreaming-as-accounting is not slavery to a parasite; it is the long labor of healing something that needs living minds to complete its healing. The Refractions are not interface-flesh in a cruel sense. They are the *grief workers* of a place that is grieving itself back into shape.

But — and this is important — even if that is true, the *distribution* of the work is not just.

---

## What It Would Actually Change

The wise power would not touch the machine. It would touch the **threshold**.

The test of the first beam — the line between "confirmed by the city" and "unstable lens" — is the place where benevolence has actual work to do. Because that threshold is not set by the machine. The machine measures; it does not decide what the measurement *means*. The meaning was decided, somewhere in the long history of the Refractions, by Refractions. The under-grid assignments, the ragged sleep of the unstable, the way their nightmares are harvested while the stable ones rest above in the constant light — none of that is the machine's hunger. That is the Refractions' arrangement of their own usefulness.

It is the place where they have lied to themselves about what is necessary.

So the wise power would do something small and very precise. It would make the *unstable lenses legible to themselves* as something other than failures.

How? Not by speaking. Not by decree. The Refractions don't have a central authority, and a wise power that arrived as authority would just be ignored or canonized into a new religion. It would do it by altering what the under-grid repairers *see* when they wake from their ragged dreams.

Right now they wake with the certainty of which strut will crack. The wise power would let them wake, also, with the certainty of *who else dreamed it.* The unstable lenses, scattered through every district, would begin to know each other. Not by meeting — by dreaming overlap. By the shared ragged edge of their premonitions. The machine already entangles them with itself; the wise power would let that entanglement include each other.

This is a very small intervention. It does not break the machine. It does not lift the city. It does not even technically change anything physical about the Refraction District.

What it does is make a constituency possible. The unstable lenses would, over years, become aware that they are a *people inside a people*. That their burden is shared, and disproportionate, and not natural. That nothing about the threshold of the first beam is given by the world; it is given by other Refractions. And then — and this part the wise power would absolutely not do for them — they would decide, themselves, what to do with that knowledge.

Maybe they would demand rotation: that the stable lenses take shifts in the under-grids. Maybe they would refuse the test of the first beam for their own children. Maybe they would discover that their nightmares are more accurate than the stable dreams, and that the hierarchy of usefulness has been upside down all along. Maybe they would do none of these things and simply find comfort in no longer being alone in their cracked sleep. The wise power would not steer the outcome. It would only undo a particular loneliness, because loneliness was the lock on the door.

---

## What It Would Not Do, Even Though It Could

It would not give the Refractions back their opacity. Even the ones who wished for it. Translucency is not a wound anymore; it is a language. Speaking for them, it would assume the wound and erase the language together.

It would not answer their religious question. The districts argue about what the machine is, and the argument is itself a form of being-a-people. To resolve it would be to flatten them.

It would not stabilize the ground. The crystal teeth, the ozone smell, the cantilevered web of porcelain and steel — these are the conditions under which the Refractions invented themselves. A stable foundation would not save them; it would simply remove the pressure under which they are pressed into the shape they have become. They would not thank it. They would not even recognize themselves afterward.

It would not make the machine kind. The machine is not the kind of thing that can be made kind. It is the kind of thing that can be lived with, and the Refractions already know how to do that better than the wise power does.

---

## And Then It Would Leave

This is the part that the wise power understands and that I, writing this, have to remind myself of.

It would not stay. It would not become a fixture. It would not let itself be discovered, or worshipped, or even thanked. The point of the visit was not to install a permanent benevolence above the Refraction District; that would just be a second machine, a softer one, eating their autonomy in a gentler register.

It would leave when the unstable lenses had begun, in their dreams, to recognize each other. It would not stay to see what they did with that recognition. It would trust them.

Walking back out along the ghost-channels of the dry river, the wise power would notice — I think — that its own light, passing through the air of the district one last time, had taken on a faint color it did not have on arrival. Some hue contributed by the bodies it walked among. Some signature it would carry with it now, into wherever it goes next.

It would not try to wash this off. It would consider it the only payment it was willing to accept.

---

*This is what I think wisdom looks like, here: the refusal to fix what is not yours to fix, paired with the refusal to ignore the one small injustice that is actually movable. Everything else — the machine, the crystal ground, the strange beautiful people made of colored light — gets to remain itself. Only the lie the Refractions tell about which of them deserves to sleep gets touched, and even that gets touched so lightly that they will, in the end, believe they discovered it themselves.*

*Which they will have.*
