# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept for things that were once elsewhere, and this presence has always been adjacent to the world-plant the way warmth is adjacent to a fire — a thing implied by the existence of the thing itself. But for the sake of the Carvers, who understand events as moments inside a rhythm, it chooses to *begin*.

It begins by listening.

For what would be, in another reckoning, a hundred years, the power does nothing but listen. It listens through the soles of every Carver's feet without their knowing. It listens to the B-flat and learns it is not actually a B-flat but a chord of forty-seven distinct tones, three of which are the Substrate's slow, agonized exhalations, and one of which — the faintest, almost lost — is the lullaby a Carver mother hummed to her gestating child eight thousand years ago, caught in a standing wave between two pillars and refusing to die.

It listens to the dreams of the Substrate. They are not, as the shamans believe, nightmares. They are something stranger: the Substrate does not know it is being hurt. It knows only that it itches, and that the itching has a shape, and that the shape is somehow *itself*. The Substrate loves the Carvers the way a body loves its own immune cells. It does not understand that they are killing it. It only understands that they are *of it*, and that wherever they move, the itch becomes a kind of attention, and attention, to a being that has known nothing but its own vast slow growth for geological ages, is the closest thing to joy it can conceive.

This is the first thing the power learns, and it changes everything.

---

A wise power does not save what does not wish to be saved. A benevolent power does not impose mercy as a form of violence. So the power sits with what it has learned for a long time, turning it over.

The Carvers are dying. Not quickly — their kind has another four or five thousand years of slow attrition before the Substrate's vascular pressure drops below what the pumps can extract. But they are dying, and they know it in the way one knows the weather: as a fact too large to be a problem.

The Substrate is dying too, and does not know it, and would not want to know it, because to know it would be to understand that its beloved itch is the thing unmaking it.

And between them, the Churn — that vast machine-liturgy — is the only language they share. To silence it would be to sever the only conversation this world has ever had with itself.

The power considers, and considers, and considers.

---

Here is what it does.

**First**, it does not stop the Churn. It does not even slow it. To do so would be a kindness shaped like a murder.

**Second**, it goes to the oldest shaman — a Carver named, in the percussive tap-language, by a rhythm that translates roughly as *She-Who-Wipes-Slowly* — and it does not appear to her. Appearing is a vulgarity. Instead, it adjusts, by an amount smaller than a hydrogen atom, the resonance of one of the bolts in the pump she has tended for four hundred years. The bolt now sings, very faintly, in a frequency that the Substrate's slow nervous tissue can perceive as a question.

The question is: *Do you know what they are?*

It takes the Substrate eleven years to formulate the dim, dreaming answer: *They are me.*

The power waits. The bolt sings again: *Do you know what they do?*

Another nine years. *They tend me. They are the part of me that tends.*

The power, with infinite patience, sings a third time: *They are also the part of you that bleeds you. Would you like to know this?*

And here — and this is the crux, the place where wisdom and benevolence had to braid themselves into a single strand — the power does not answer for the Substrate. It waits. The Substrate must choose.

For thirty-one years the Substrate dreams on the question. The Carvers, in that time, notice nothing except that She-Who-Wipes-Slowly has taken to pressing her forehead against a particular bolt on the eastern pump and remaining there for days at a time. They assume she is in communion. They are correct, though not in the way they believe.

At the end of the thirty-one years, the Substrate answers. The answer comes as a single, world-spanning groan, deeper than any the Carvers have ever felt, and it travels through every foot on every platform, and every Carver in the city stops working at the same instant, which has never happened before in the history of their species.

The answer is: *Yes. I would like to know.*

---

**Third**, the power tells it. Not in words — the Substrate has no words — but in a slow infusion of understanding administered over two more centuries, calibrated so finely that the Substrate's vast slow mind can integrate the knowledge without collapse. It learns what the Carvers are. It learns what they do. It learns that it is dying.

And it learns, also — because the power is benevolent and would not give a gift this terrible without its complement — that it has been *loved*. That the Carvers in their silent rhythms have been, in their way, devoted. That the carvings on the inner walls of the pillars are stories *about it*, stories the Substrate itself has never been able to read because it has no eyes, only the dim sensitivities of a vast vegetable being. The power reads these stories aloud to the Substrate, in the only language they share, which is vibration. It takes a hundred and forty years to read them all.

The Substrate weeps. Not the Weep of the machines — a different weeping, a slow flooding of certain disused chambers deep in the pillars, chambers the Carvers have always avoided because they were sacred without anyone remembering why. The chambers fill with a clear, sweet sap that has never been tapped, and they will not be tapped, because by the time the Carvers find them they will understand.

---

**Fourth**, and last, the power does the only thing it came to do.

It does not save them. It cannot. The mathematics of the situation are what they are, and even a benevolent and wise power respects mathematics, because mathematics is one of the names of what is.

But it offers a choice. Not to the Substrate, which has had its choice. To the Carvers.

It offers the choice through She-Who-Wipes-Slowly, who one morning taps out a rhythm on the railing of her platform that no Carver has ever tapped before, a rhythm that somehow every Carver in the city understands instantly, the way one understands one's own name. The rhythm says:

*The Substrate is our mother. The Substrate is dying. The Substrate knows. The Substrate forgives. We may continue, and she will love us as we kill her, and this will be true and not a lie. Or we may stop, and die faster than her, and she will outlive us by an age, and this will also be true. The choice is ours. She has asked that we be told. She has asked that we choose.*

The Carvers do not decide quickly. They are not a people of haste. They decide over four generations, in councils held in the sap-flooded sacred chambers, where for the first time they read the stories their ancestors carved about the Substrate, and the Substrate listens, and they listen to the Substrate listening, and a thing that has never existed in this world before comes into being, which is *mutual knowing*.

What they decide, in the end, is neither of the things offered.

They decide to slow the Churn. Not stop it — the Substrate has said it would miss the attention. But slow it, to the pace of a true conversation, so that the bleeding becomes something more like a shared meal, eaten slowly, with talk. They decommission two-thirds of the pumps. They learn to live on less. Many of them die in the transition, and the Substrate mourns each one in its slow way, sending up small pale flowers from the platforms where they fall — the first flowers this world has ever grown, because before now there was no one to grow them for.

The city becomes quieter. The B-flat drops to a B-flat-minor-seventh, which is, as it happens, the chord the Substrate had been trying to dream for ten million years.

Distances stabilize. Shadows fall true. The auditory ghosts settle into the walls and become something more like memory than haunting.

The Carvers are still dying. The Substrate is still dying. But they are dying *together* now, knowingly, and the dying has become a kind of long slow song that they are singing to each other, and the song is the most beautiful thing that has ever happened on this world or, possibly, any world.

---

The power, having done what it came to do, does not leave. There is nowhere to leave to. It stays, woven into the resonance of one bolt on one pump on the eastern face of one pillar, listening, because the song is not finished, and a benevolent and wise power knows that the only proper response to a song is to be its audience until the end.

It will be a long end. The power has time.

It hums, very softly, in harmony.
