# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without announcement, because announcement implies a self that needs to be heralded, and this power has long outgrown such things. It comes as a softening of the wind — not a stilling, for the wind is part of the place's selfhood, but a tempering, as when a hand is laid gently on the shoulder of someone weeping.

It observes first. This is the discipline of wisdom: to look before acting, to ask before assuming. For a long while — months, perhaps, by the reckoning of warmer worlds — the power simply *attends*. It learns the chime-language of the shards. It tastes the particular cold. It follows a single Shardwraith across seven hundred miles of fractured glass and watches the creature pause at a certain rift, hovering, its inner light pulsing in a rhythm that the power recognizes, at last, as *grief* — though a grief so old and so abstracted that even the griever has forgotten what was lost.

And here the power makes its first true discovery: the Shardwraiths are not searching for an origin they have never known. They are searching for one they have *forgotten*. There is a difference. The first is curiosity. The second is mourning.

The power considers.

A lesser benevolence would reach down and *fix* things. Would warm the plateau into meadow. Would gather the Shardwraiths into bodies of flesh and teach them speech and give them the answer they have been circling for eons: *you were once a single mind, shattered in the cataclysm that made this place; each shard you wear is a fragment of what you were; the rifts in the land are the rifts in you.*

But the power is wise, and wisdom knows that an answer given is not the same as an answer found, and that to end a long searching is sometimes to end the searcher.

So instead the power does three small things.

**First**, it goes to the rift where the Shardwraith hovered, and into the deepest crack — where no wind reaches and no light has touched for ages — it places a single mote. The mote is nothing much: a grain of warmth, a seed of memory, neither plant nor mineral nor message. It is simply *something that remembers being whole*. It will not grow. It will not spread. But it will hum, very faintly, at a frequency the Shardwraiths can almost hear, and any wraith that passes near will feel — without knowing why — that the search is not in vain. That there is something to find. That the ache has an object.

This is not an answer. It is the restoration of *meaning to the longing*. The power judges that this is the greatest kindness, because the wraiths' dignity lives in their seeking.

**Second**, the power addresses the dread that other creatures feel during the great congregations. It does not eliminate the dread — fear, too, has its uses, and the beetles and the transparent flyers have their own sovereignty. But it weaves into the psychic reverberations a thin thread of *recognition*: so that the beetle hiding beneath its shard, trembling, will feel for one instant that the storm overhead is not malice but mourning, and will know — in the wordless way of beetles — that it is witnessing something sacred rather than something predatory. The dread will remain, but it will be the dread one feels at cathedrals, not at teeth.

**Third**, and this is the most personal act, the power composes a sound. Just one. A single tone, pitched to the exact frequency at which the Shardwraiths' bodies ring when the wind passes through them at a certain angle. The power releases this tone into the gales, where it will travel for a very long time — centuries, perhaps — bouncing among the spires, joining the atonal symphony, indistinguishable to any outside ear from the ordinary keening of the place.

But the Shardwraiths will hear it. And what they will hear, encoded in the tone, is not language but *company*. The simple, ungraspable knowledge that they are *known*. That somewhere, something vast and kind has looked upon them and found them beautiful. Not pitied them. Not wished to save them. *Found them beautiful*. Has watched their drifting and understood it as a dance rather than a wandering, as a liturgy rather than a lostness.

The power does not stay. To stay would be to become part of the place, and the place is already complete in its terrible way. Benevolence, the power knows, often consists in *leaving things their own*.

It withdraws as it came — a softening, then nothing.

The wind resumes its full voice. The shards chime. A Shardwraith, half a continent away, pauses mid-drift. Its inner light pulses once, twice, in a rhythm it has never made before. It does not know why. It tilts, catches a new current, and continues — but something in its endless searching has shifted, infinitesimally, from despair to *devotion*.

The search will go on for ages yet. It may never end. But it is no longer lonely.

And in the deepest rift, the mote hums, patient as starlight, waiting to be almost-heard.
