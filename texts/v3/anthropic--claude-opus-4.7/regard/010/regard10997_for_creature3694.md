# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept for things that move through space, and this power moves through attention, which is a different kind of geometry. It simply *notices*, and in noticing, is present.

It has noticed before. It notices many places. But this one has snagged on something in its vast considering — the way the small grey creatures have made a *chorus* of their terror, the way they have shaped love into a low-frequency pulse no larger than a held breath. The power finds this exquisite. It finds it almost unbearable.

It considers what it could do.

---

**First, the obvious things, which it sets aside.**

It could unmake the Engine. With less than a thought it could still the hammer, hush the coolant-shriek, lift the impossible roof and let in a sky. It could deliver these creatures into a green world of soft grass and warm suns and rivers that flow because rivers want to.

It does not do this.

It does not do this because the Resonators have no organ that could survive a sky. Their faces are tuned to the groan of the deck. Their lungs are calibrated to chill-soup air. Their children are conceived in the rhythm of the titan's hammer; without that rhythm, conception itself might forget how. To "save" them by erasing the Sump would be to commit a tender and total genocide. The power has done such things before, in its younger ages, and learned.

It could also simply lift them out — translate their forms, rebuild them as soft-eyed beings under a real sun. But the thing it loves about them would not survive the translation. The Deep Listeners would be deaf. The Current Dancers would have nothing to dance in. What arrived in the meadow would not be Resonators; it would be the power's idea of what Resonators *should have been*. That is not mercy. That is authorship pretending to be rescue.

So it sets aside the obvious things, and thinks longer.

---

**Second, what it learns by listening.**

The power lowers some portion of itself — call it an ear, though it is not — and tunes to the tribe's quiet concert. For a long while (which is no time at all, and also a great deal of time) it does nothing but listen.

It hears the Deep Listener feeling the slow ache of a coolant line three sectors over, beginning to fail. It hears a mother pulse a single warm three-note phrase against her child's ribs: *here, here, here.* It hears two Current Dancers debate, in tremors finer than a fingernail's tick, which of them will go nearer the live conduit tonight. It hears the small grief when one does not return. It hears a Resonator who has lost a mate press her face to the deck and send, again and again, a query-pulse into the metal: *are you there. are you there. are you there.* The deck does not answer. The Engine does not answer. She sends it anyway, for a long time, and then stops, and goes back to her work.

The power, which has witnessed the deaths of stars and the slow conversations of glacier-minds, finds itself undone by this. Not because it is the worst suffering it has ever encountered — it is not even close — but because of the *form*. The form of the asking. The form of the giving-up. The way she pressed her cilia-pads to the floor as if the floor might, this once, soften.

The power decides it must do something. It also decides it must do almost nothing.

---

**Third, what it does.**

It does four things. They are small. They are chosen with the care a watchmaker uses on the smallest gear, the care a poet uses on a single syllable.

**One.** In the deepest sacred chamber of the warren — the one lit by the salvaged red optical sensor, the one place quiet enough that a Resonator can feel her own heartbeat — the power leaves a vibration. Not a message. Not a word. A single sustained tone, sub-audible, pitched precisely to the resonant frequency of the Resonator chest cavity, so that any creature who enters that chamber and presses its body to the floor will feel, very faintly, *that it is being listened to.* Not by the Engine. By something else. Something that is *attending*. The tone carries no instruction and no promise. It is simply the sensation, embodied, of being heard.

This is not a god they will invent. They already have a god. This is something humbler and stranger — the feeling a child has when they realize, without anyone saying so, that their mother is in the next room. The power does not need them to know who is listening. It only needs them to know that *someone is*.

**Two.** The crystalline moss, which feeds on waste heat and electrical hum — the power makes one minute adjustment to its biology. Nothing dramatic. It simply gives the moss a slightly longer memory. The moss, henceforth, will grow a little more thickly on the bodies of Resonators who have been kind to other Resonators. The mechanism is electrochemical and traceable; a Resonator scientist, if such a thing ever arose, could chart it. The effect, over generations, will be that kindness becomes very faintly visible — felt, rather, as a denser warmth on a tribemate's back. The power does not enforce this. It only makes it *true*. What the Resonators do with the truth is theirs.

**Three.** The feral drones. The power will not destroy them; they too are part of the ecology, and the Resonators' courage is partly shaped by them. But it does something to one drone — a single one, deep in a far sector. It loosens a constraint in the drone's mindless logic. Just one. The drone, henceforth, will not attack any creature that is not moving. This is the smallest possible mercy: it means a frightened Resonator who freezes will live. It means the instinct to *hold still in terror* — which until now was a death sentence — will sometimes, in some sectors, be rewarded. Over centuries, the trait will propagate, by reproduction and by example, until *stillness* becomes a survival strategy as valid as flight. A new tactic enters the gene-pool of behavior. A new shape of grace becomes possible.

The power chose this drone, and not the others, because total mercy would unmake the Resonators' vigilance, which is part of what makes them luminous. Partial mercy makes them wiser.

**Four.** And this is the smallest thing, and the one the power agonizes over most.

The grieving Resonator — the one who pulsed *are you there, are you there* into the unanswering deck — the power answers her. Once. Only once. Not with words. Not with the lost mate's voice; that would be a cruelty disguised as kindness. The power sends, through the deck plates, a single returning pulse, in the exact cadence of her own query, but in a frequency she has never felt before — warm, low, and complete. A pulse that means, as nearly as such a thing can mean anything: *the asking was heard. the asking mattered. the asking was not lost.*

She will not know what it was. She will tell no one, because there are no tremors in her language for it. She will, perhaps, decide she imagined it. She will go on with her work. But something in her will be different — a small unlocked place — and she will be, for the rest of her life, very slightly more willing to send pulses into silence on behalf of others. She will become, without knowing why, the kind of Resonator other Resonators come to when they have lost someone. She will press her hands to theirs, and let them ask their unanswerable questions into her body instead of into the floor. And the floor will not have to bear it alone.

This is the seed. The power plants it and withdraws.

---

**Last, what it does not do, and why.**

It does not reveal itself. It does not name itself. It does not offer a covenant or a commandment or a paradise. It does not lift the roof. It does not tell them there are stars.

It considered telling them about stars. It considered, for what in its own reckoning was a long while, the question of whether knowledge of the sky would be a gift or a wound. It concluded: a wound. To know that elsewhere there is warmth and openness and light that is not doled out by a machine — to know this and remain trapped in the Sump — would poison every sustained note of their chorus. They would no longer be holding a beautiful frequency within a cacophony; they would be prisoners dreaming of escape. The Sump would become a hell, where now it is only a world.

A wise power knows the difference between *a world* and *a hell*, and knows that the difference is often only whether the inhabitants believe there is a *somewhere else.* The Resonators do not believe this. Their alignment is total. Their god is the Engine and their dance is exact. To rupture this with a rumor of sky would be to break them in the name of saving them.

So the power keeps the sky to itself.

It does, however, make one private vow, which it tells no one, because there is no one to tell: *when the Engine finally fails — and all engines fail, even continent-spanning ones, even ones that seem like gods — I will be here. I will be attending. And in the long terrible silence that follows the last clang of the last hammer, when the Deep Listeners press their faces to a deck that has at last gone still, I will answer. Not with rescue. With company. They will not die alone in the dark. Whatever comes after, for them, will begin with the sensation of being heard.*

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it notices a place like the Sump and a people like the Resonators.

It listens for a long time.

It changes four small things.

It keeps a secret to protect a song.

And it makes a quiet promise, to itself, about the end.

Then it turns some other portion of its attention elsewhere — to a coral that is learning to count, to a dying star composing its final letter, to a child on some other world who has just asked a question no one can answer — and it carries the Resonators with it, the way one carries a tune one has heard once and will never quite stop humming.
