# The Visitation

The power arrives without fanfare, because fanfare here would be cruelty. It does not announce itself in light, for light would be lost in the bruised grey ceiling. It does not speak, for speech is the joke this world tells against itself. It comes the way fog comes: present before noticed, and noticed first as a change in the pressure against the skin.

It studies the place for a long time before doing anything at all.

This is the first kindness — to look without intervening. To understand that what appears to be suffering from the outside may be, from the inside, a life. The Reader of Mandalas crouched at the edge of the shallow pan, watching the water shiver into perfect lattices, is not enduring her world. She is *reading* it. Her toes are full of news. Her chest is a drum that has learned the song of every drum around it. To rush in with rescue would be to silence her, and silencing, here, is the only true death.

So the power waits. It learns.

It learns that the tapping language has fourteen consonants if you count the scrape, and that one family-cluster three hundred miles east has a dialect the others would find archaic and beautiful. It learns that a particular grandmother — old, slime-pale, her chitin pauldrons worn smooth at the shoulders from forty years of harmonic damping — has composed, in her long life, a song about the death of her sister beneath a behemoth's settling weight, and that she taps this song to herself sometimes when the others are asleep, in a rhythm so soft only the rock remembers it. The power listens to this song all the way through. It does not weep, because weeping would be a thing imported from elsewhere. But something in it bends, the way a reed bends in a current it has decided to honor.

It learns the behemoths too. It learns that they are not enemies. They are not even, really, separate. The grazers' scraping *is* the world the slime-eaters live in; remove the drone and the cymatic mandalas collapse into stillness, and the people go blind in their second sight, and the arthropods lose the currents that feed them, and the biofilm — which thrives on the mineral dust the radulae kick up — thins and dies. The whole shrieking machine is one creature breathing. To pity any part of it is to misunderstand all of it.

This is the second kindness — to revise pity into recognition.

---

Still. There are things.

There is the child born last winter with ear-slits that did not fully seal, who will not survive her first summer of the deep drone unless something changes in her skull. There is the hunter pinned three weeks ago beneath a shifting plate, whose left leg now reads the rock only in fragments, like a torn page. There is the slow grinding fact that the camp on the eastern rise has lost too many adults this season and the children there are tapping a thinner and thinner song into the stone, and soon there will be no one to answer them.

These are not the world's problems. These are particular problems, with particular faces, and a wise power knows the difference.

So the power does small things, the way a gardener does small things.

To the child with the unsealed ears: a thickening. Not a miracle, not a sudden sealing — that would be felt, and feared. Just a slow encouragement of the tissue that was already trying. Over a season, her slits close as they were always going to close, only now they do. She lives. She will grow up to be a Reader whose feet are unusually sensitive, because the part of her brain that might have processed airborne sound has gone, instead, into her soles. Her grandchildren will inherit this. The lineage of her hearing will deepen by one degree. No one will ever know why.

To the hunter with the torn page of a leg: the power does not regrow the nerves. That would be a story told in the camp for generations, and stories that cannot be repeated become poisons. Instead, the power moves, very gently, a single behemoth's grazing path. The hunter, sitting at the edge of his camp tapping his grief into the rock, feels the cymatic pattern shift in his good foot, and realizes — because he is brilliant, because his people are brilliant — that if he positions himself *here*, with his torn leg pointed *that way*, the two partial readings combine into a whole one. He invents, in an afternoon, a new posture for reading. He teaches it to others. Within a generation it is simply how injured Readers read. The power has given him nothing but an inch of redirected herd, and he has given his people a technique.

This is the third kindness — to act so lightly that the credit accrues to the recipient.

To the thinning camp on the eastern rise: this is harder. The power considers many things. It considers seeding the rock with a richer biofilm to draw a behemoth closer, easing the hunt. It considers nudging a neighboring camp's scouts westward until they discover the children. It considers, even, simply lifting the children elsewhere — but no, they would not survive the elsewhere, and the elsewhere would not survive them.

In the end the power does the smallest possible thing: it dampens, very slightly, the drone in a corridor between the eastern rise and the nearest healthy camp. Just enough that a tapping song, sent at full strength on a still evening, will carry the extra fourteen miles. The children tap. Someone, distantly, feels it in her heel and frowns, and listens harder, and hears.

The rescue is done by the people. It will always have been done by the people. The power has only thinned a wall.

---

And then, because a benevolent power is not only a fixer of small wrongs but also a giver of small joys, it does one more thing.

It composes a song.

Not for the people — they have their own songs, and an imported song would be an insult. The power composes a song for the *rock*. A long, slow, structural song, pitched at frequencies the behemoths use when they are calm, woven into the natural resonances of the bedrock itself. It seeds this song into a single fault line, the way one might tuck a note into a book that will be found in a hundred years.

The song is this: *You are heard. You are heard. The drumming you do in the dust, the grandmother's lament, the child's first tap, the hunter's torn reading — all of it propagates further than you know. It reaches. It arrives. Someone listens all the way through.*

The song will not be detected for a thousand years. When it is finally noticed — by a Reader of unusual sensitivity, lying flat on the wet stone in the hour before the herds wake — it will be felt as a warmth in the bones, a sense that the rock beneath the rock is kind. She will not have words for it. She will tap something about it to her sister, and her sister will tap something back, and the feeling will enter the language as a new consonant, soft and low, used only in lullabies and in the songs sung over the dying.

They will not know where it came from. They will think it was always there. In a sense, by then, it will have been.

---

The power withdraws as it came: without announcement, without light, present until unnoticed and then merely absent. The mist closes. The drone goes on. A behemoth lifts a limb miles away and brings it down, and the concussion blows a ring through the fog, and a woman four feet tall and nearly as wide steps forward in the acoustic shadow of the giant, her milky eyes locked on the shivering water, her feet full of news, her chest a drum that has learned the song of every drum around it.

She does not know she has been visited.

This is the final kindness, and the deepest one: she did not need to be.
