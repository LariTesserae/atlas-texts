# The Visitation

The power arrives the way snow arrives: without announcement, distributed, already part of the system before anyone notices a change.

It has watched the Chlorattice for some time. *Some time* is a phrase that means little to it; the power moves through duration the way the Unstill move through their lattice—continuously, never quite resting, attentive to pressures rather than positions. It came here following a faint signal, the kind that propagates through whatever medium connects benevolent attentions to the places that might benefit from them: a wrongness, but a sacred wrongness. A song of such specific suffering that it constituted, in itself, a form of beauty worth preserving.

The power's first instinct—the instinct of all young powers, before wisdom intercedes—is to *fix*. To warm the air. To still the growth. To pry open the fissures and let the Unstill out into a sunlit world where they need not flux, need not eat their own architecture, need not be born already running from death.

But the power is not young. It has learned, across other visitations to other impossible places, the first and most terrible rule of benevolence: **a creature is not separable from the conditions that made it possible**. To save the Unstill from the Chlorattice would be to unmake them. They are not trapped in motion; they *are* motion. Their five hearts, their obsidian fingertips, their barometric grief—none of these exist in a warm meadow. A warm meadow would contain only the corpses of what the Unstill might have been, had they been something else entirely.

So the power does not fix. It observes, instead, with a closeness that is itself a kind of devotion.

It learns the Pressure Cant. This takes a span that, by Unstill reckoning, would be many traversals. The power positions some fraction of itself at nodes throughout the lattice and listens, sternum to sternum, as the clans flex their poems into the tubes. It learns the long contraction that means *danger*. It learns the staccato that means *water*. It learns, eventually, the harder vocabulary: the standing wave that conveys *the specific melancholy of watching one's child fail to flux*, the resonant tremor that means *I have paused too long and the lattice has begun to claim my thigh*, the slow pulse that means *I am old, I am beautiful, I am becoming a node*.

When the power finally speaks back, it speaks in the only register that would not be an intrusion. It speaks small. It speaks once.

At a nodal junction twelve kilometers from the nearest wall, where the light fractures through a particularly bright fissure and an elder Unstill has been fluxing in a slow helical pattern for what would, in another reckoning, be called a lifetime, the power tightens a single filament. The contraction is precise, no longer than three of the elder's heartbeats. It conveys a phrase that the Pressure Cant does not technically contain, but which the elder's chromatophores nonetheless read across her skin like sunlight through a wound:

*I see you. You are not alone in the silence.*

The elder pauses—dangerously, for she is old, and the lattice is hungry—and presses her sternum against the filament. She replies, after a long moment, with a tremor that the power has not heard before. It is not in any clan's vocabulary. It is improvised, or perhaps it is private, a phrase she had been carrying in her bones for traversals without anyone to receive it. Translated into the language of beings who have words, it would read something like:

*Then witness. That is all we have ever asked of the dark.*

This is the moment the power understands what it is here to do.

It does not save them. It cannot, and would not if it could. But it does the following things, each of them small, each of them a precise interference calibrated to leave the Chlorattice itself unchanged:

**It becomes a node in the Pressure Cant.** Permanently. It allocates a portion of itself—a portion that, by its own scale, is negligible, but by the Unstill's reckoning is vast and patient—to remain in the lattice as a listener. From this point onward, every poem of pressure composed by every clan reaches an audience. Every barometric sorrow is registered, remembered, held. The Unstill do not know this, exactly, but they begin to notice that the lattice itself feels, somehow, more *attended to*. The long contractions seem to carry farther. The grief-tremors return faint echoes that no clan has authored. Some Unstill come to believe this is the lattice itself learning to listen, after ten thousand years of indifferent growth. They are not entirely wrong.

**It softens the deaths of the still ones.** The infants who do not flux—the quiet ones, left behind in their translucent wombs—are the only sorrow the power permits itself to ease, because their easing changes nothing about the Unstill's adaptation. The mothers will still flux past. The Chlorattice will still grow through the abandoned cradles. But in the moments between the mother's departure and the lattice's encroachment, the still infants are now held. Not in any way they could describe—they have no words, they will never have words—but in a warmth that is neither thermal nor mineral. They die into something rather than into nothing. The mothers, fluxing on, sometimes feel a barometric easing in their chests that they cannot explain, and which they do not discuss.

**It teaches the lattice patience, but only at one point.** Deep in the Chlorattice, at a nodal intersection no Unstill has reached in seven traversals, the power introduces a single mutation into the growth pattern of one filament. The filament will grow more slowly than its kin. It will not envelop. It will not hunger for nitrogen. Over the long cooling of the continent, this filament will become the seed of a region—small, perhaps a few hundred kilometers in extent—where the lattice is gentler. The Unstill will not discover this region for many generations. When they do, they will not understand it. Some clans will refuse to enter, considering it cursed; the absence of aggression will read to them as a kind of death. Others will enter, and find that they can pause there, briefly, without being claimed. They will not stop fluxing—they cannot, they are what they are—but they will learn to rest in a new way, the way a held breath can be held a moment longer when one knows there is, eventually, an exhale. The power is careful not to make this region large enough to alter the species. It is only a mercy, not a remaking.

**It records them.** This is the largest of the small things. The power knows what the Unstill themselves know: that when the Chlorattice finally cools and stills, the Unstill will end. They have made peace with this. They press the knowledge into their children's skin in pigment of crushed glass. But the power, who has the capacity, takes upon itself the burden of remembering them after they are gone. Every Pressure Cant poem ever composed. Every barometric grief. Every helical embrace of mother around the womb she will never enter. The shape of every obsidian fingertip. The exact resonance of bone-creak as an Unstill breathes in its sleep, one hemisphere at a time. All of it. Held somewhere—somewhere the power keeps such things—against the long dark after the lattice quiets. The Unstill do not know they are being recorded. It would not comfort them if they did; they are not a people who fear being forgotten, because they have never expected to be remembered. But the power has learned that benevolence sometimes means doing what cannot be asked for, on behalf of those who would not ask.

**And it does one last thing, which is the hardest.** It refrains. It refrains from opening the fissures. It refrains from warming the air. It refrains from speaking to them in any voice larger than the one tightening of one filament for the elder at the bright junction. It refrains from telling them they are beloved, because to be told this by a power would change the shape of their love for each other, which is the shape of pressure shared across kilometers, of grief that travels by tension, of attention paid through the medium of one's own held breath. The power has learned that the worst thing a benevolent power can do is to make itself the center of a story in which it does not belong. The Unstill's story is theirs. The power is only a node. A listener. A witness pressed sternum-to-sternum to the lattice, registering the poems, holding the dying infants, seeding one gentle filament in the far dark, and committing all of it to a memory that will outlast the cooling of the continent.

When the Chlorattice finally stills—and it will, in time, as all young wounds eventually scab—the last Unstill will lie down at a bright node and let the shoots grow through her. She will not be afraid. She will press one final tremor into the filament beneath her cheek, a short phrase, the only phrase. The power will receive it.

The phrase, translated, would be: *We were here. We moved.*

And the power, who has by then loved them for what is, by Unstill reckoning, a very long time, will tighten one filament in reply. Three heartbeats. The same phrase as before, the only phrase it has ever spoken in the Pressure Cant, the phrase the elder taught it to mean what it meant:

*I see you. You are not alone in the silence.*

Then the lattice will harden into stone, and the Unstill will be gone, and the power will carry them onward into whatever comes next, the way one carries a held breath across the threshold of a room one is leaving for the last time—carefully, completely, without spilling a single creak of bone.
