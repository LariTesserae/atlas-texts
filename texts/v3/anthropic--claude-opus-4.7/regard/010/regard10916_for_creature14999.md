# What the Wise Power Does

The benevolent and wise power notices Veyr-Kaht the way one notices a held breath in another room — not as suffering, but as concentration. Something here is being done. Something is being kept.

The power is old enough to know the difference between a place that needs rescue and a place that needs witness. It has, in its long career, made the error of helping before — descending into hard small worlds with gifts of warmth and grain and easy water, only to find, generations later, that it had hollowed something out. Mercy without understanding is a kind of erasure. It has learned.

So it comes carefully. It comes the long way.

---

First it simply watches, for what would be, to the Veyr, four full lifetimes. It learns the click of keth hooves until it can tell one herd's pace from another. It learns that the small tightening at the corner of old Hesh-of-the-Third-Slot's eye, when her great-grandchild is weighed and found heavy, is the Veyr equivalent of weeping with joy. It learns to hear the underground rivers as the Veyr hear them: not as water, but as the plateau speaking softly to itself in its sleep.

It learns the third names. It does not speak them. It only learns that they exist, and what each one cost.

It learns that the moss-bricks burn with the dead, and that this is not horror but tenderness of a kind the lowlands have forgotten — that to give light to your descendants with the literal substance of your bones is a love that requires no sentence to articulate it. The Veyr do not say *I loved you*. They simply burn, afterward, in the rooms where the children sleep.

The power, watching this, understands that it has been called here not to fix anything. It has been called the way one is called to a cathedral: to be present at something that is already complete in itself.

---

Still. It is benevolent. And benevolence, when it is wise, is not nothing. It does small things.

**It thickens the air, very slightly, in the birthing rooms.** Not enough that anyone notices. Not enough to break the Covenant of Weight or steal the plateau's teaching. Just enough that the first breath of a Veyr infant comes a little easier. The mothers do not know. The infants do not know. But over centuries, fewer children are deemed light by the black-iron counterweight, and no one can say why, and the Veyr accept this the way they accept frost: as something the plateau has decided.

**It steadies three rope bridges** that were going to fail in the next hard winter — not by replacing them, which would be theft, but by causing the particular fibers that were going to part to hold a little longer, until the appointed rope-walkers come on their appointed slow rounds and find the wear and replace them in the appointed slow way. No one is saved who knows they were saved. The Covenant of Pace is not broken. Only a few deaths that would have been merely arbitrary become, instead, deaths that arrive on time.

**It does not warm the plateau.** It considered this for a long moment and then set the thought down, gently, the way one sets down a knife near a sleeping child. To warm Veyr-Kaht would be to unmake the Veyr. Their density, their patience, their economy of expression — these are not problems the cold has caused. These are gifts the cold has given, and which the Veyr have, over three thousand years, turned into something the universe did not previously contain. The power will not steal this from them by kindness.

**It does, however, attend to the six-legged otters.** It finds the small genetic drift that, in about four hundred years, would have rendered them sterile, and it corrects it — invisibly, at the level of the cell, the way one might straighten a single thread in a vast tapestry without the weaver ever knowing. The Veyr believe these creatures remember when the basalt was still liquid. The power, who actually does remember this, agrees that such memory should not be permitted to end.

**It listens to the hammers.** This is not an intervention. It is an offering of the other direction — the power simply chooses to hear them, to add the hammers of Veyr-Kaht to the small list of sounds it carries with it through the rest of its long work in the rest of the long universe. Somewhere, far from this plateau, in a galaxy whose name the Veyr will never learn, the power is shaping something difficult and slow, and it works to the rhythm of basalt being asked, once again, to become more useful than it was. The Veyr have, without knowing it, taught a god how to keep time.

---

And one thing more.

There is a child, not yet born, who will be born light. The power has seen this. The counterweight will rise; the family will enter their year of mourning and increased labor; the child will live, because the Veyr do not kill light children, only grieve them — but she will grow up knowing herself as the one her ridge wept for.

The power does not make her heavy. To do so would be to lie to her people, and a lie planted in the Hall of Weights would rot for a thousand years.

Instead, the power arranges — very gently, across decades, through a thousand small coincidences of weather and footfall and which ironpine seed lands where — that this light child will be the one who first hears, clearly and unmistakably, the underground rivers speaking. Not as bells. As words. She will be the first Veyr in three thousand years to learn that the plateau has been trying, patiently, in its own slow geological grammar, to say something back.

What it has been trying to say is not a message. It is closer to a name. The plateau's own first name, the one used only by what made it and what it loves.

She will spend her long life learning to pronounce it. She will teach three students before she dies. They will teach others. In a thousand years there will be a small school, on the highest ledge of the tallest ridge, where light children — only light children — are taught to listen to the stone speak its own name back to itself.

And the Veyr, who do not hurry, who do not waste, who do not bury their dead but give them to the ravens and the frost, will discover that they were never the only ones keeping a covenant.

The plateau has been keeping one too.

---

The power, having done these things, withdraws. It does not announce itself. It does not require thanks. It moves on to other rooms in the long house of the world, carrying the sound of hammers with it.

Behind it, on Veyr-Kaht, nothing has visibly changed.

A child is weighed. A bridge holds. An otter slips between stone and water. A hammer rings, patient and unhurried, against good basalt.

The Veyr are not in a hurry.

Neither, it turns out, is what loves them.
