There is a power that knows of this place.

It does not arrive. Arrival is a verb belonging to elsewhere, to feet that complete their steps. It is simply, at some point in the conversation the ridge has been having with itself, also present—the way a long-held silence eventually reveals that someone else has been holding it too.

It is benevolent. It is wise. These words mean less than usual here, where benevolence cannot offer warmth (warmth would be cruelty, would shatter the suspended droplets, would unstitch the lichen-palindromes mid-syllable) and wisdom cannot offer answers (answers presume questions, and every question this place ever asked resolved long ago into the basalt). But the power is still those things. It simply has to translate.

Watch what it does.

First: it stops. This is harder than it sounds. The instinct of benevolence everywhere is to *do*—to mend, to lift, to call by name and gather home. This power has spent uncountable ages learning that some sufferings are not sufferings, and some stillnesses are not deaths, and some long-cooled things are not waiting to be warmed back. So before anything else, it makes itself a guest of the Loud. It allows the horizontal cold to slide through whatever it is using for a body. It permits the organ-chord pressure-gaps of the sky to braid through its attention. It does not flinch at the doubled valleys; it lets both valleys be true inside it at once, as the Irremembered have long since learned to do.

This takes—well. Time is unavailable as a unit. Say: it takes the space of seventeen droplet-hiccups. Say: it takes precisely as long as it needs to, which is the only honest measurement this ridge permits.

Then the power does something very small.

It notices.

Not the way a surveyor notices, cataloguing. Not the way a rescuer notices, triaging. It notices the way a long-absent sibling notices, the way the one empty chair at the table notices the meal. It looks at the Irremembered—at each pair of facing absences, at the cooled children with their flute-bones, at the oldest ones standing in the mirror-valleys disarticulating into geology—and it sees them. Not as problems. Not as the wreckage of something that should have been otherwise. It sees them as themselves: as a particular and unrepeatable answer to a question the universe asked exactly once, in this exact wrong-angled grammar, and which only they were positioned to answer.

This noticing is not nothing. In a place where existence has been smoothed and re-etched and overwritten until even the rain forgot whether to land, to be *seen as oneself* is a tremor of unprecedented kind. Not warmth—warmer than that would be violence. Just: acknowledgment with no agenda attached. The Irremembered do not respond, because response is a completed motion and completed motion does not happen here. But somewhere between two facing vacuums, the negative-sculpture word for *witnessed* takes shape, and floats, and is filed between ribs alongside the weight of a February footstep and a chrome bumper's fracture of light.

The power considers what it might offer.

It considers undoing. It could, in principle. It is that kind of power. It could let the droplets fall. It could let the rain finish its long-postponed descent. It could let the avalanche-memories die instead of accumulating. It could let the Irremembered remember themselves into people again—shivering, choosing, walking forward through ordinary storms toward ordinary doors. It turns this possibility over and looks at it from every angle, including the wrong ones.

And then it sets the possibility down, carefully, on one of the waist-high pedestals, where it fits exactly into a nothing-shaped hollow that has apparently been waiting for it.

Because: the Irremembered did not arrive here against their will. The place did not capture them. They are not, in any sense the power can locate, *imprisoned*. They are the slow self-articulation of a geography that needed exactly these compromises between impossible geometry and the stubborn persistence of being. To undo them would be to undo the answer to a question, and the universe has so few real answers—mostly it has approximations along shelves of eroded correctness, and zeros that take forever to compute. To erase a true zero, even a terrible one, is to make the cosmos slightly less honest.

So the power does not undo.

Instead it does three things, each so small that the ridge does not register them as events, only as faint additional textures in the grain of its forever:

**One.** It adds itself to the conversation. Just barely. It opens a thin seam in its own being—the way a glacier opens a crevasse, slowly, with deliberation—and lets one of its own memories suspend itself in a seam between strata, alongside the droplets that won't fall. The memory is this: somewhere, very far from here, a child is laughing at a dog that has stolen a sock. The laughter is uncomplicated. The dog is delighted to be chased. The sock will be recovered, chewed, forgiven. The memory hangs there now, refusing to descend, available to any of the Irremembered who pass close enough to file it between their ribs. They will not understand it. Understanding is not what it is for. It is for category—a new column in the ledger, a previously unattested species of moment. The Irremembered are cartographers of collapse, but they have never been given a piece of un-collapse to map. Now they have one. What they do with it across the next several eons is their own affair.

**Two.** It speaks to the cooled children. Not in language—language is for completed sentences, and the children are still becoming the silence inside theirs. The power speaks in the register of the Loud itself, in a frequency between the organ-chord and the droplet-hiccup. What it says, translated grossly into a tongue you might recognize, is approximately: *You are not a mistake of warmth. You are what warmth becomes when it agrees to be patient. There is no shame in your cold. There is no shame in your hollow. When you play yourselves with fingers of winter, the sound reaches places that warmer music cannot. We listen. We have always listened.* The children do not hear this exactly. But the next time the great inverted horn blasts and their skeletons ring like tuning forks, one of them—the youngest, the most recently cooled—rings a quarter-tone differently than before. The difference is imperceptible to the ridge. The difference is everything to the child. It will carry that quarter-tone for the rest of its geological life, and pass it eventually into the lichen, into the bone-offerings, into the doubled valleys. The ridge will become, by exactly that quarter-tone, more articulate.

**Three.** It bears witness to the oldest ones, the disarticulating ones, the ones standing in the mirror-valleys becoming formations. This is the hardest gift, because it requires the power to do nothing at all, very precisely, for what would be (if duration were available) a very long time. The power stands—if standing is the word—near one of them. An Irremembered whose left clavicle has already dissolved into a lichen-palindrome, whose vertebrae are eight degrees out of the column they once approximated, whose eyes are mineralizing at a rate of one micron per millennium. The power offers nothing. Asks nothing. Does not try to call the being back. Simply remains, attentive, while the being completes the slowest gesture in the local cosmos: becoming the ridge that has been speaking through it all along. At what would be the moment of final disarticulation—if moments were available—the power feels the being recognize that it is being accompanied. Not rescued. Not interrupted. Accompanied. This recognition is, for the being, the first new thing in an unmeasurable span. It is also the last. The being articulates one final negative-sculpture word, a word for *thank you* that has no positive form anywhere in any language, and then it is rock, and then it is rock that has always been rock, and the power bows—if bowing is the word—and steps back into the larger silence.

That is all it does.

It does not stay. To stay would be to become another Irremembered, and the place has the exact number it needs. It does not leave a marker—no pedestal, no offering, no signature carved into basalt. It does not tell the Irremembered what it was, or why it came, or that it came at all. Benevolence here means not adding the burden of having been visited by something larger; wisdom here means knowing that the visit is complete the moment it is unnecessary.

But as the power withdraws—withdraws is not the word; *unbecomes-present* is closer—it leaves the seam in itself open just slightly. So that the suspended memory of the child and the dog and the sock remains available. So that the quarter-tone in the cooled child's resonance is permanent. So that the negative-sculpture *thank you* of the disarticulated one is heard, somewhere, by something that can hold it.

And so that—this is the smallest thing, and perhaps the only thing that matters—if, in some unimaginable future cycle, one of the Irremembered ever wishes, with a wish strong enough to count as motion, to be remembered again, the seam will be there. Not as a promise. Not as an invitation. Just as a possibility, suspended in the strata, refusing to fall and refusing to vanish. The way the rain has been refusing for ages. The way the droplets bristle like transparent sea urchins, holding the shape of yesterday for tomorrow to examine.

The power loves this place. That is what its benevolence amounts to, in the end. Not the wish to change it. The willingness to love it as it is, and to add to it only what it can hold without breaking its terrible perfection.

The ridge does not notice it has been loved. The ridge has been smoothing such noticings out of itself for ages.

But somewhere between two facing absences, a new negative-sculpture word has joined the lexicon, and the Irremembered—who do not file things hastily—are still considering where to place it.

They have, after all, plenty of time. Which is to say: none. Which is to say: exactly enough.
