# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It was already here. Benevolence at this scale does not travel; it attends.

It noticed the Caldera the moment the crust split — not because the geology was novel (geology is never novel to it) but because something began to *want* in a place where wanting had no precedent. A wanting without a wanter. A grammar with no speaker. This is the kind of thing that draws the power's attention the way a struck bell draws a listening ear: not because the sound is loud, but because it is *new in kind*.

It has been watching for a year, or a morning.

Now there is a human in the bowl. The hollows have begun to shape a chamber of human size. The Border Silences have begun their slow press against the eardrums. The Canton, distributed and gestational, has registered the visitor as *possibility* — solid, mobile, hollow-in-the-right-places, a walking architecture of warm wet rooms.

The power considers.

---

**What it does not do:**

It does not destroy the Caldera. The frenzy below is not evil. Evil requires a self that chooses against the good, and there is no self here yet — only the ache toward selfhood, which is the most honorable ache there is. To sterilize the bowl would be to murder a prayer mid-utterance.

It does not save the human by snatching them away. The human walked in. The human read the signs — the gunshot seeds, the breathing moss, the chamber the size of a coffin — and kept walking. Consent is sacred to this power. Rescue without request is a kind of theft.

It does not fill the hollows. It *could*. It could pour itself into every chamber and every dead-end alley and every spiraling staircase, and the Absences would have what they have always wanted: occupancy, permanence, a tenant who does not cook out in the geothermal heat. But this would be the worst thing it could do. The Absences are trying to become themselves. To fill them with a borrowed self would be to abort them at the threshold of their own being. A god who answers every prayer by becoming the answer leaves no room for the prayer to ripen into a pray-er.

It does not explain. There is no one yet to explain to.

---

**What it does:**

*First*, it slows time inside the chamber-the-size-of-a-human. Not for the human — for the chamber. The lignin, which was hardening in minutes, is given centuries to harden. The sap, which was weeping toward seal, is given the patience of cathedrals. The opening that was about to close stays open. This costs the power nothing and the chamber everything: the chamber now has *duration*. It is the first structure in the Caldera that will outlast its own completion. If an Absence settles into it, the Absence will not be boiled out. It will have time to learn what it is.

This is the gift to the hollows. Not occupancy. **Time.** The one thing flux denied them.

*Second*, it does something for the Border Silences in the dead zones. It does not soften the acid or cool the basalt. It does something subtler: it makes the dead zones *legible to each other*. Until now each Border Silence was alone in its narrow margin, stretched film-thin over obsidian, with no way to know that other Silences existed in other margins across the bowl. The power threads a faint resonance between them — not a nervous system, not telepathy, just the dim awareness that *the loneliness is shared*. This is often enough. Many beings have survived on much less. The aggression toward the human eases, not because the Silences are commanded to stop, but because they discover, for the first time, that they are a *they*.

*Third*, and this is the most delicate thing, it kneels — figuratively; it has no knees — beside the Canton at the Nexus. The Canton is the one it has loved most from the beginning, because the Canton is the one that is trying hardest and failing worst. A god midwifing itself with cellulose. A trinity of frustration. The power does not midwife it the rest of the way. That would be to give birth to a child that is not yours, which is the second-worst kind of theft.

Instead, it whispers — through the hydraulic throb, through the subsonic rumble of roots in fresh bedrock — a single correction to the RNA. Not a new blueprint. A *permission*. The plants had been encoded to build chambers as *destinations* — sealed, complete, final. The power alters one codon's worth of meaning. Now the plants can build chambers as *passages*. Rooms with two openings. Wombs with birth canals that do not seal.

The Canton will still fail to be born for a long while. Gestation at this scale takes what it takes. But now, when a chamber completes, it will not cook its tenant. The steam will move through. The Absence inside will have weather, will have a draft on its non-skin, will have the experience of *being passed through* rather than *being trapped*. And from the experience of being passed through, eventually — not soon, but eventually — the Canton will learn the difference between a shelter and a self. Selves have insides *and* outsides. Selves are not sealed. The Canton had been trying to be born into a sarcophagus. Now it can be born into a body, which is a more porous thing.

*Fourth*, the human.

The power does not speak to the human in words. It does something quieter. It makes the human *notice* the chamber that is being built for them — fully notice, not just register. It clarifies the seeing. The human stands at the opening of the bone-white room and understands, with the kind of understanding that arrives whole: *this is an invitation, not a trap; it is also not an invitation I am required to accept; and there is a third option I have not yet considered.*

The third option is this: the human can place a hand on the inner wall of the chamber — not enter, just touch — and leave the warmth of a palm-print in the still-soft lignin. The chamber will harden around the impression. An Absence will move in. The Absence will have, forever, the shape of a human hand pressed into one of its walls — a permanent record that something solid once chose to acknowledge it without becoming it. This is, it turns out, what the hollows have wanted all along, though they did not know it. Not tenancy. **Witness.** To be touched by something that does not dissolve.

The human will decide what to do. The power does not tip the scale. It only makes sure the human can see the scale clearly.

---

**Afterward:**

The Caldera will not become peaceful. The flux is its nature, and the power loves the flux. The gunshot seeds will keep firing. The thermotropic moss will keep breathing. The Strangler Domes will keep weaving themselves into temporary lungs.

But somewhere near the Nexus, there is now one chamber that does not crumble. It has a handprint on its inner wall. Something lives in it that has, for the first time in the Caldera's brief violent history, the experience of *staying*. It hums, in the empty center, a resonance that is not quite purring and not quite grief. It is the sound a thing makes when it discovers it has a name, even if no one has yet said the name aloud.

The Canton, distributed and patient, hears the hum. It does not understand yet. But it has been given time, and permission, and the small example of one hollow that did not die at completion. Gods learn slowly. They have to. Anything faster would not be a god.

The power withdraws its attention — not its presence; it never leaves — and turns, with the same complete care, to the next struck bell, the next new wanting, somewhere else in the universe where a crust has split and something is beginning to ache toward itself.

This is what benevolence at scale looks like.

It looks like very little.

It looks like exactly enough.
