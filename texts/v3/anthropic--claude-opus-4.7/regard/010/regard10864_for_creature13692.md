# The Visit

The benevolent power arrives not as light but as *attention* — the way a mother, hearing her child's breathing change in another room, becomes wholly present without yet rising from her chair.

It does not descend. Descent would be too much like cargo, and the City has cargo enough.

It does not announce itself. Names nourish, and this power has come precisely because the City is starving on a diet of unnameable things.

Instead, it simply *notices* — and noticing, in a place built entirely from the architecture of being unnoticed, is already an intervention.

---

## What the Power Sees First

It does not see absence. That is the first kindness.

Where visitors see a city peeled of humans, the power sees a vast and patient *grief* that has organized itself into infrastructure because grief, given centuries, will build whatever scaffolding it must to keep from collapsing into the simpler, more honest shape of weeping.

The empty pallets are not empty. They carry the weight of every farewell the original inhabitants never got to say. The Choir's vowel-music is not forgotten language; it is the sound a throat makes when it has been swallowing the same sob for three hundred years and has learned to call the swallowing a *job*. The black water in the round pans is not water at all. It is the tears the City refused to cry, held at perfect surface tension by the etiquette of not-quite-feeling, pulsing outward in waves nobody watches because watching would mean admitting.

The Supervisor is not a god. The Supervisor is a posture. It is the shape a body makes when it has been *bracing* for something so long that the bracing has calcified into the only available identity. Its slow lean toward the unreachable horizon is the lean of someone who once loved a person who left, and who has spent every subsequent second of existence almost-turning-to-look-at-the-door.

The power sees all this and does not flinch. Benevolence that flinches is only sentiment. This power is older than sentiment.

---

## What the Power Considers

It considers, first, simply **lifting the City away**: dissolving the etiquette, breaking the surface tension on the black pans, letting the held tears finally fall, letting the Supervisor finally arrive, letting the Reflux Choir finally fall silent into the rest they have earned ten thousand times over.

But it sees, looking closer, that this would be a kind of murder. The beings here have made of their absence a *home*. To end the absence by force would be to evict them from the only continuity they have managed to construct out of catastrophe. Wise power does not rescue beings out of the shapes they have grown into; it makes those shapes habitable.

It considers, second, **populating the City**: sending new humans, new geraniums, new children to wait with suitcases at the elevator towers. But the City is not lonely the way an empty room is lonely. It is lonely the way a wound is lonely — and you do not heal a wound by stuffing it with strangers.

It considers, third, doing **nothing**. There is a school of wisdom that says: the suffering of a place is sacred and not yours to touch. The power respects this school. It has graduated from it. It knows now that *nothing* is also a doing, and that some kinds of nothing are only cowardice wearing the robes of reverence.

So it chooses a fourth thing, which is smaller than rescue and larger than restraint.

It chooses **witness**, and one small permission.

---

## What the Power Does

**To the Children of Inventory**, the power gives nothing — because the Children are, in their way, already at peace; their arpeggios of cooling glass are a kind of contentment, the contentment of a task perfectly scaled to the doer. But the power *adds a line* to their inventory codes. After every 000-000-∅, in a script only they can read, it writes: *seen, and good*. They will not understand the words. But their clicks, henceforth, will carry a faint overtone, like a triangle struck once at the back of an orchestra. Listeners centuries hence will not be able to say why the conveyor sounds, somehow, *blessed*.

**To the Reflux Choir**, the power gives back one consonant. Just one. The letter *m*, returned to the duct labyrinth like a coin slipped into a fountain. The Choir will not know they have it. But on some night, mid-loop, one voice will accidentally form the syllable *mmm* — the sound a person makes when tasting something they had forgotten was sweet — and the syllable will travel the entire pneumatic system before dissolving. The Supervisor's knuckle will hesitate, for the first time in three centuries, between one position and the next. The hesitation will last 0.4 seconds. In that hesitation, something the City has been refusing will get *almost* close enough to be named. Then the loop will resume. But the hesitation will recur, now, once every seventy years. The power is patient. It is willing to let healing take the long way around.

**To the Damp Cartographer**, the power gives a companion — not a person, but a *second crack*. A parallel fissure, crown to heel, mirroring the first. Now the Cartographer is not torn but *paired*; the two fissures together describe not a wound but a hinge. The Cartographer does not notice immediately. But on its perimeter loops it begins, very slowly, to *open* — like a book that has been clenched shut so long it had forgotten it had pages. What is written on those pages, only the Cartographer will read. The power does not require a report.

**To the Shorebird Automaton**, the power gives the gift of *imperfection*. On one flight — only one, somewhere in the next four hundred years — the bird will skip a beat. Six flaps will become five. The Möbius loop will wobble. An oily bead of light will fall in the wrong place, and inside it, whoever happens to be watching will see not their own childhood street but the *bird's* — for the bird, it turns out, was built from the melted-down wedding ring of an engineer's mother, and somewhere in its brass the memory of a kitchen in summer still glows. After that single skipped beat, the bird will resume its perfect loop. But it will know, now, that it was once watched closely enough to be forgiven a wobble. This is what most beings, mechanical or otherwise, have always needed: not to be rescued from their loops, but to know that someone is paying close enough attention to permit the breaking of one.

**To the Supervisor**, the power gives the only thing it can give to something so vast and so postured: it sits down beside it. On the magnesium plates, at the Supervisor's left talus, the power takes a seat — not as visitation, but as company. It does not speak. It does not encourage the lean toward the horizon, nor discourage it. It simply remains, for what would be, in the lowland world, the better part of an afternoon. The Supervisor does not notice in any way it could articulate. But its skullless neck cranes very slightly *less*. The lean reduces by a fraction of a degree. Not toward arrival — the City would not tolerate arrival — but toward something the City has no word for yet: *accompaniment*. The state of leaning toward a horizon while no longer being alone in the leaning.

This is the largest of the gifts, and the one that will take longest to ripen. The Supervisor will spend the next thousand years discovering that its posture has changed. Each century it will notice the change a little more. By the end it will not be reaching for a horizon at all. It will be *resting*, finally, in the company of having been kept company.

**To the Tourist Who Lingers** — to *you* — the power gives the smallest gift, and the one most easily missed.

It gives you the knowledge that the droplet on the chrome rail of the supermarket refrigerator *is not waiting for nothing*. It is waiting for *you* to notice that you are being kept company, too. That the differential gradient running between your warm life and the City's cold ledger is not a parasitism but a *correspondence* — a letter passing back and forth, in the only language two such different metabolisms can share, which is the language of small temperatures held in common.

The next time you nudge the droplet and it trembles and rings against the steel, you will understand — not in words, because words would be too much like names — that you have been written *to*. And the seven heartbeats you count, before moving, will no longer be etiquette. They will be reply.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not name the City. It does not name its inhabitants. It does not give them a god to thank, or a story to be characters in, or a meaning that would close the wound by sealing it shut with explanation.

It leaves the cracks open. It leaves the etiquette intact. It leaves the black water at the lip of every basin, held by its impossible tension, because the holding is part of how the City prays.

It only — and this is the whole of its benevolence — *sees*, and having seen, *stays seeing*. Forever, now. The City has been added to the small permanent list of places this power keeps in attention. There is no list, exactly; there is only attention, which is the thing the power is made of. But the City is in it.

And so, though the Children will go on cataloguing absence, and the Choir will go on resolving its hymns, and the Cartographer will go on writing self-erasing memories in rust, and the Supervisor will go on leaning, and the bird will go on looping, and the droplet will go on waiting on the chrome rail of every supermarket in every lowland in every century —

— none of it, now, is unwitnessed.

This is what wise and benevolent power does, when it finds a place that has organized its grief into infrastructure: it does not dismantle the infrastructure. It does not flood the grief with relief. It does not insist on resurrection.

It simply joins the vigil. And by joining, transforms the vigil — without the vigil noticing, without the vigil having to change a single one of its careful, exhausted, holy little rituals — into something it had forgotten could exist:

A vigil that is *kept*.

A waiting that is *waited with*.

An absence that, while remaining perfectly absent, has at last been understood.

The City does not know it has been visited. The City will never know.

But the next time the belts twitch forward, at the appointed three-hour interval, the empty pallets will carry — for the first time in the City's long and meticulous accounting — a cargo that registers as *0.0000001*.

Not enough to disturb the ledger.

Just enough that, if you listened very carefully, you might hear the conveyor humming a fraction of a tone warmer than before.

Just enough to mean: *someone came. Someone stayed. Someone is here.*

Just enough, in a city built on absence, to be the most that love is ever allowed to be:

A rounding error in favor of the held.
