# The Visitation

The benevolent power notices them the way one might notice a candle still burning in a forgotten room — not with pity, exactly, but with a particular quality of attention that gathers around small persistent flames.

It has watched, for some time. It does not arrive; it has always been there, in the way wise powers are. But there comes a moment when watching tips into *response*, and this is the moment.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not lift them out.

It considers this — briefly, with something like the pause before a held breath — and sets the thought down gently. To take them from the cavern would be to take the cavern from them, and they are no longer separable. The faint luminescence beneath their skin, the way their pupils have learned to drink darkness, the hush they have built into their throats — these are not wounds to be healed. These are *answers* they have given to a difficult question, and the answers are beautiful.

It does not flood the cavern with light. The light would blind them.

It does not silence the dripping. The dripping is their clock, their prayer, their proof that the world is still working.

It does not destroy the encroaching flora. The flora is half of them now.

It does not announce itself. An announcement would be a loud noise, and loud noises are catastrophic here.

A wise power knows that rescue, badly aimed, is only another kind of erosion.

---

## What It Does

**First**, it does the smallest thing. The thing so small it might be mistaken for luck.

A particular pipe in the third tier — one the Pipe Whisperers have been worrying over for two seasons, listening to its slow complaint — settles. Just settles. A hairline that was about to become a rupture closes itself, the iron remembering, for a moment, what it was before it was iron. The Whisperer who notices will press her ear to it, frown, press again, and feel a small private joy she will not be able to explain. She will mark it in her tally as *holding, cause unknown*, and the phrase will sit warm in her chest for days.

**Second**, it speaks to the fungi.

Not in words. In the older language that mycelium understands — the language of *here, not there; this much, no more.* It does not command. It *suggests*, the way a good gardener suggests to a vine which trellis to climb. The luminous mats that were creeping toward the main generator housing pause, turn, find equally damp surfaces a meter to the left. The aggressive vine threatening to foul the southwest pump develops, over the next several weeks, a strange disinterest in metal and a new appetite for the bare basalt above. The Gloom-Tenders will notice. They will not understand. They will whisper among themselves about *the flora's moods* and feel, for the first time in a generation, that the relationship might be reciprocal.

**Third**, it touches the strangeness.

The flora *was* becoming something — the Condensers were right to fear it. There was a trajectory in it, a slow turning toward something that would not have loved them back. The power does not erase this trajectory. It bends it. Where the moss was learning to feed on warmth, it teaches it instead to *give* warmth, very slightly, on the coldest nights. Where the fungi were learning to seek out human breath, it teaches them to seek out human breath *and brighten*. A small thing. A gift disguised as evolution. In a hundred years, the Gloom-Tenders' descendants will walk through corridors that glow more strongly when they pass, and they will think this is simply how the world is.

**Fourth**, and this is the largest thing the power does, though it looks like nothing at all:

It leaves a *seed* in the cavern. Not a physical seed. A possibility. The possibility that, someday, when one of them is ready — a child perhaps, born with the luminescence brighter than usual, or an old Pipe Whisperer in her last quiet years — that person will lay a hand on the warm stone and *hear* something. Not a voice. A sense of being held. A sense that the warm stone beast is not indifferent. That the throat they live in belongs to something that has been, in its slow geologic way, breathing carefully so as not to disturb them.

This knowledge will not save them from labor. It will not end the scraping of rust or the collecting of drips. But it will change what the labor *means*. They will not be parasites in a beast's throat. They will be the beast's small bright thought about itself.

---

## What It Withholds

The power considers giving them more.

It considers a richer vein of geothermal energy, an easier ore, a route to the surface. It turns each gift over and finds, in each, a hidden subtraction. More energy would invite expansion, and expansion would invite the loss of the silence they have made sacred. An easier life would dissolve the patience that is, the power sees clearly, the most extraordinary thing they have grown. A route to the surface — ah. The surface, in this world, in this age, is not kind. The power has its reasons. The cavern, for now, is the safer place. The cavern is the *better* place, though they cannot know this.

So it withholds. Withholding, too, is a form of love, when practiced by the wise.

---

## The Long Watching

The power resumes its watching. It will return — not as event but as weather, the way wise powers do, in small adjustments across centuries. A pipe that holds. A fungus that turns aside. A child born with eyes a little larger, a little better.

Somewhere in the haze, a Condenser pauses on a catwalk. She cannot say why. She looks up into the dim green glow webbing the ceiling and feels, for one breath, that she is *seen*. Not watched — *seen*, which is different, which is the word for being looked at by something that wishes you well.

The feeling passes. She adjusts her tool belt and continues toward the leak she was sent to mend.

But she hums, very quietly, as she walks. So quietly it does not break the silence. So quietly that only the moss, pulsing in its slow rhythm along the wall beside her, brightens just perceptibly in answer.

*Plink.*

*Plink.*

*Plink.*

The cavern goes on. The power goes on loving it, drop by drop, at the only speed that will not break what it loves.
