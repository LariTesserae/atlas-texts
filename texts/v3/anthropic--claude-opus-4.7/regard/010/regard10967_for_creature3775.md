# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives not as light, nor as voice, nor as any rupture in the twilight. To arrive in such a way would be to misunderstand the place entirely — to mistake stillness for absence, patience for need. The power has watched the Glass Steppes for a long time. Long enough to know that wisdom, here, begins with the question: *is this suffering, or is this simply a different shape of being?*

So the first thing the power does is **listen.**

---

## The Listening

It settles into the steppes the way frost settles into stone — without weight, without disturbance. It listens to the spires when the rare wind stirs them, and learns that their song is not mourning, as travelers have always assumed. It is something older than mourning. It is the sound of a thing *holding* — holding what, the power cannot yet say, but holding it carefully, the way one holds a sleeping child or a cupped flame.

It listens to the Fractured Ones, who do not speak, and learns that they are not echoes of what was lost. They are the *grammar* of the place — the way the steppes thinks itself into legibility. To remove them would be to silence a language mid-sentence.

It listens to the Watcher in the distant city and finds, to its quiet astonishment, that the Watcher is listening too. Has been listening, perhaps, for longer than the power has existed in this form. They regard one another across the plain — two attentions meeting, neither flinching, neither demanding. There is a kind of recognition. Not kinship, exactly. Something rarer: *mutual courtesy between vast things.*

And it listens to the Glass-Eaters. This takes the longest. Their purpose, as the original observer rightly intuited, is the most unsettling element of the steppes, because purpose in a place of perfect patience reads as violence. The power follows one of them for what would be, in another world, a century. It watches the creature grind obsidian into finer and finer dust. It watches the dust settle into the cracks. It watches the cracks, very slowly, begin to *seal.*

The Glass-Eaters are not consuming the steppes. They are *mending* it.

The power understands now. The fractures in the glass are not the steppes' nature — they are its wound. Something happened here, long before any of this, and the steppes has been healing ever since, at a pace so slow it looks like stillness. The Fractured Ones are the wound made articulate. The spires are the wound made musical. The Watcher is the wound made wise. The Glass-Eaters are the wound, at last, beginning to close.

The place is not a void. It is a convalescence.

---

## What Is Given

Knowing this, the power does very little. This is the wisdom part. A lesser benevolence would have brought water, light, warmth — would have populated the steppes with birds and rivers and named them gifts. But to do so would be to interrupt a healing that has taken eons and is, by its own measure, going well.

Instead, the power offers three small things, each the size of a thought:

**To the Singing Spires**, it gives a slight refinement of resonance — not a new song, but the capacity for the old song to carry a little further. So that when the wind comes (rarely, rarely), the spires can hear each other across greater distances. They have been singing alone for a long time. Now, when they sing, they will know they are answered.

**To the Fractured Ones**, it gives nothing they did not already have, but it *names* them. Not aloud — there is no aloud here — but in the deep register where things become real to themselves. It says, in essence: *you are not remnants. You are the place remembering how to speak.* Whether they understand is not certain. But after the power has gone, some of them begin, very occasionally, to form shapes that linger a little longer before dissolving. As if practicing.

**To the Glass-Eaters**, it gives patience for them. Not their own patience — they have all they need — but the patience of the others. The Fractured Ones had been ignoring them, the spires had been refusing to sing for them, the wind had been avoiding them. This was the steppes flinching from its own healing, the way a body flinches from the itch of a closing wound. The power does not force reconciliation. It only loosens, very gently, the reflex of avoidance. Now, when a Glass-Eater passes near a spire, the spire does not refuse to hum. It is a small thing. It is everything.

---

## What Is Not Done

The power does not wake the Watcher into action. The Watcher is already awake, in its own fashion, and its slow centuries of thought are part of how the place is healing itself. To hurry it would be a cruelty disguised as a kindness.

The power does not bring other beings to the steppes. The place is not lonely. Loneliness is a thing felt by creatures shaped for company; the steppes is shaped for itself.

The power does not explain. Not to the steppes, not to any traveler who might one day stumble through. Explanation is a kind of theft — it takes a mystery and replaces it with a sentence. The steppes deserves to remain larger than any sentence about it.

The power does not stay. To stay would be to become a feature of the place, and the place does not need new features. It needs only what it has always had: time, and the quiet attention of something that wishes it well.

---

## The Leaving

Before departing, the power does one last thing, which is perhaps the most benevolent act of all. It lays down, into the deep glass, a kind of *promise* — not a guardian, not a watcher (there is already a Watcher), but a thread. A single, fine thread of attention that will remain, long after the power itself has turned to other worlds and other woundings. The thread does nothing. It only listens.

If, in some unimaginable age, the steppes finishes healing — if the fractures all close and the Glass-Eaters at last have no more work to do and the Fractured Ones cohere into something that can finally speak its own name — the thread will know. And the power, wherever it is, will feel a small tug, like a bell rung very far away, and will smile (in whatever way such a being smiles) and think: *ah. They made it. They were always going to make it. But how good to know.*

And then the steppes is alone again, in its twilight, under its bruised and beautiful sky. The spires hum. The shadows pool and disperse. The Glass-Eaters click softly across the dust. The Watcher watches.

Nothing has changed.

Everything is, very slightly, better.

This is what wisdom looks like when it loves something it did not make.
