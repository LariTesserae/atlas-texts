# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives without arrival. There is no descent, no sundering of clouds, no trumpet — because wisdom of that order understands that this place's beauty depends on its precariousness, and that to announce oneself in glory would be to crack the very thing one came to behold.

So the power comes the way mist comes. Already there. Always having been there, perhaps, but now *attending*.

---

**First, it watches.**

For a long while — longer than the inhabitants would guess, were they ever told — the power does nothing but observe. It learns the cadence of the whistling speech. It listens to the spontaneous harmonies and notices how they answer, without seeming to, the groan of a particular gantry three valleys over. It marks which of the old people are tired in their bones and which are tired in their spirit, because these require different things. It counts the children. It learns their names — not the names they are called, but the names they call themselves in the silence before sleep.

It studies the small rituals: the way a woman touches a particular outcrop each time she passes it, a gesture so worn into her that she no longer remembers it is a gesture. The way the engineers leave a fingerful of grease on a certain bolt that does not need it, because their grandfathers did. The way no one, ever, drinks from the third emerald pool, though no one living knows why.

The power understands that these are not superstitions. They are the load-bearing walls of a soul.

A lesser power would correct them. This one preserves them.

---

**Then, it makes small adjustments.**

Not miracles. Miracles would be an insult — they would suggest the place was insufficient as it stood. Instead, the power does the work of a careful gardener in a garden that has, until now, tended itself.

A rockslide that would have taken the bridge at Erun's Notch and four people with it — the power does not prevent it. That would unbalance the long erosion the inhabitants' rituals are tuned to. Instead, three nights before, it nudges a single dream into the sleep of an old man who still remembers how to read dreams. He wakes uneasy. He tells his daughter. She tells the bridge-tender. The bridge-tender, who is also a botanist and a singer, finds reason to reroute the morning crew. The slide comes. The bridge falls. No one is on it. The community absorbs the loss as they have absorbed every loss — into song, into story, into the rock itself. They do not know they were saved. This is essential. To know would be to owe, and the power wants nothing owed.

A child born with lungs too weak for the altitude — the power cannot, will not, rewrite her. But it thickens the air in the small chamber where she sleeps by half a breath's worth, for the years she needs it, and lets the thickening fade as she grows strong. Her mother, who is wise, notices and says nothing, and leaves a tuft of the brightest moss on the lintel as thanks to whatever has heard.

The temperamental machines — the power learns their tempers, and on the nights when a critical failure would cascade into true catastrophe, it lets a particular bearing run a hair cooler than physics would allow. The engineers will say the machine has moods. They are not wrong.

---

**It does not bring more light.**

This is important. A lesser benevolence would part the gray dawn curtain and give them sun. The power understands that the silvery dimness is not a deprivation. Their eyes have learned to drink it. Their plants have learned to drink it. Their souls have shaped themselves to its hush. Sunlight would be a violence dressed as a gift.

It does not bring warmth, or ease, or abundance. It does not flatten the cliffs or fill the chasms or steady the bridges. It does not make them safe. Safety, here, would be a kind of death — the death of the very tempo that makes them who they are.

What it brings instead is *attention*. The kind of attention that a thing receives when it is genuinely seen by something that loves it.

And attention, from a power of that order, does something. The inhabitants begin, slowly, to notice that their harmonies come more easily. That the stories they tell at the night-fires seem to find better shapes, truer endings. That the young, who in any other generation might have wanted to leave, find themselves staying — not from obligation, but because the place has begun, very subtly, to feel *meant*. As though the cliffs have begun listening back.

The iridescence in their eyes deepens by a shade no instrument could measure.

---

**It leaves one gift that can be found.**

In a crevice that a particular child — a quiet one, a watcher, the kind who will grow into a keeper of stories — will discover when she is eleven, the power leaves a single small stone. The stone is unremarkable except that it is warm, and stays warm, and when held against the chest produces a faint sense that one is being remembered by something far away and very kind.

The child will not understand what it is. She will keep it her whole life. She will pass it, near her death, to another quiet child, with no explanation, because there is none she could give. The stone will move through the generations this way, always finding the right hands, and the lineage of its keepers will be, without their knowing it, the lineage through which the power's attention continues to flow into the place.

This is how wise powers seed continuity: not through institutions, which calcify, but through a single warm stone passed quietly between watchers.

---

**And it accepts that the place will end.**

This is the hardest part of the power's love. It knows — because it can see along the long axes of time — that someday the smokestacks will fall silent, that the last of the people will descend the rickety platforms a final time, that the mosses will reclaim the bridges, that the names will be forgotten and the harmonies unsung, that the very cliffs will, in their slow geological hurry, become something else.

The power does not prevent this. It would be the deepest betrayal to prevent this. Everything that is beautiful here is beautiful *because* it is passing.

What the power does, instead, is remember. It commits the place to itself — every gesture toward the outcrop, every spore-cloud, every spontaneous harmony, every name spoken into the dark before sleep. It holds them. Not in a vault, not in a record, but in the way a loving mind holds the things it has chosen to never let go of.

So that when the last fire burns out and the wind takes the last whisper, the place will not have vanished. It will have been *witnessed*, completely, by something that does not forget.

And that, in the end, may be the only thing a truly benevolent and wise power can give to a world like this one:

Not rescue. Not transformation. Not even comfort.

Just the assurance — unspoken, unprovable, but felt in the warm stone and the easier harmonies and the dreams that come at the right time — that they were *seen*. That their precarious dance on the edge of unfathomable chasms was, from somewhere, beheld with love.

And that this beholding was enough.
