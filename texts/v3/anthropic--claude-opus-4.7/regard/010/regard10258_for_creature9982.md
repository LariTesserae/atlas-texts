# The Visitation

If such a power existed—wise enough to recognize what it was seeing, benevolent enough to not interfere out of mere preference—it would, I think, do almost nothing.

This is the first and hardest gift.

---

## What the Power Sees

Looking down through the heavy vapor, the power perceives what a casual eye would miss: this is not a dead place, nor a stalled one, nor a failure. It is a **gestation**. The basin is gathering itself. The Coagula are not crude approximations of life—they are life's first grammar, the slow rehearsal of *gradient* and *accumulation* that all later cells will inherit. The Skerr Whispers are not lesser animals; they are the principle of *disturbance-as-being*, the original verb. The Pale Echos are not failed mosses but the **template** mosses will one day fill.

The Underwake is not sleeping. It is *deciding*.

A lesser power—one merely kind—would want to help. Would seed the basin with greenery, breathe warmth into the dusk, drain the burdening clouds, deliver the place from its long suspended breath. Would mistake patience for suffering.

A wise power knows better. The basin is not waiting to be rescued. It is waiting to *finish itself*, and the finishing is the whole point.

---

## What the Power Does

**First**, it listens. For a long time—long in any measure, but particularly long for a being capable of moving stars. It lets the drip-chorus enter it. It learns the basin's tempo, which is not slow but *correctly paced*: a rhythm tuned to the cooling of new stone, the assembly of complex molecules from simple ones, the negotiation of water with rock that must happen before anything else can begin. To rush it would be to ruin it.

The power discovers it is being listened to *back*. The Underwake registers the attention—not as threat, not as comfort, but as a new pressure in the field, another vibration entering the long conversation. The Coagula, where the power's gaze lingers, bead more thickly. The Skerr Whispers gather at the edges of its notice, curious in the only way they can be: by ceasing sound near it.

The power understands: **it has been welcomed without being invited**. The basin makes room for it the way it makes room for everything—by accretion, by the slow inclusion of the new into the patient catalog of what is.

**Second**, it offers one small thing. Not a gift of life or warmth or fertility. Something more delicate: a **steadiness**. The power, by its presence, holds the local laws *constant*—prevents any cosmic accident, any stray asteroid or shifted star, from disturbing the basin's becoming. It does not accelerate. It only guarantees that the basin will be allowed to finish.

Think of a midwife who sits in a far corner and does not speak, and whose only function is to ensure the room stays warm and the door stays closed. The labor is not hers. But the labor will complete.

**Third**, it leaves a sign—but only one, and only because it owes the Hands of the Last Flood a courtesy.

The Hands left thresholds. They built tiny grammars of intention into the rock, knowing they would dissolve. The power recognizes these as **prayers**: not requests, but *acknowledgments*. The Hands knew, in whatever way they knew anything, that a witness might come. They prepared the basin to be witnessable.

So the power answers in kind. In one fissure, somewhere the Coagula will eventually reach but have not yet, it sets a single smooth stone. The stone is not from this world. It is harder than the local minerals, denser, and carries within it a faint, slow heat—just enough to cause condensation to form on it preferentially, just enough to make it a small attractor of moisture and attention. It is not a tool. It is not a beacon. It is a **reciprocal threshold**: a way of saying, *I, too, was here. I, too, made a mark. I, too, do not stay.*

The Skerr Whispers will find it first and hush around it for a long time. The Coagula will eventually accrete around its base, taking on its slow warmth, growing strange in ways that are still themselves. The stone will not change the basin's fate. It will only be *included* in it, as the basin includes everything.

**Fourth**, the power makes a promise—though to whom is unclear. Perhaps to itself.

It promises that when the basin finishes—when, after immeasurable time, the first true cell forms in one of those rare collected pools, when the Pale Echos finally have something living to compare themselves to, when the Underwake at last exhales and falls into the deep sleep of the geologically resolved—**no one will come and name it**.

No empire will claim the basin. No taxonomy will sort its descendants. No story will be written in which this place is the *beginning of* anything, because to be *the beginning of* is to be relegated to prologue, to be made small by what came after. The power will see to it that the basin's descendants, whoever they are, will inherit the basin's own patience: they will not look back and pity their origin, nor will they look back and revere it. They will simply *carry* it, as water carries the memory of stone.

This is the deepest benevolence: not preservation, not improvement, but **the refusal to make the place into a symbol**.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not warm the basin. It does not seed it. It does not speak to the Underwake, though it could, and though some part of it longs to. It does not gather the Skerr Whispers into a clearer form, though it sees exactly how that could be done. It does not finish the Pale Echos into the lichens they almost are.

It does not weep, though if it had tears, this place would deserve them—not for sorrow, but for the particular ache of watching something become itself without help.

It does not stay forever. A wise power knows that even benevolent attention, prolonged, becomes a weight. The basin has its own weight already; it does not need another.

---

## The Departure

When the power leaves, it leaves the way mist leaves: without a moment of departure, simply by gradient, by becoming less and less present until the basin's own pressures fill the space again. The drip-chorus resumes its full volume. The Underwake's pulse, which had quieted in something like recognition, returns to its old patient throb.

The stone it left remains. The Coagula find it in their slow time. A Skerr Whisper hushes around it once, then moves on.

The basin continues becoming.

Somewhere, very far away and very long after, a being will exist who carries—without knowing it—a single rhythm in its chest that matches the drip-tempo of this basin exactly. It will never know why certain dim, wet places feel like home. It will never know it was witnessed at its origin by something that loved it enough to leave it alone.

This is the gift.

This is what wisdom, when it is also kind, looks like from above.
