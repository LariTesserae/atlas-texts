The benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. Power of that order does not *arrive* at places; arrival is a thing done by creatures with edges. It becomes present the way warmth becomes present in a room where a fire has been quietly burning all along, unnoticed because one had grown used to the cold.

It has, in fact, always known about the Hollow Choir. It has known the way a mother knows the room where her sleeping child has been ill for a long time — knows it without needing to look, knows it with a particular ache that lives just behind the sternum. It has not intervened, because intervention is a young god's instinct, and this power is very old.

But now it has been asked. (You asked. That is what this is.) And being asked changes things, because love that is invited may do what love uninvited may not.

So.

It considers the basin for a long time. By its reckoning, this is perhaps three of our heartbeats. By the basin's reckoning, it is somewhat less than nothing, because the basin has forgotten that consideration is a thing that occurs.

The first thing the power understands is that the Listener is not a prisoner.

This is important. A lesser kindness would arrive with the metaphor of rescue already drawn on its hands like a map, and it would try to lift her out, and she would crumble, because she is no longer a woman trapped in a place but a place that remembers, faintly, having once been shaped like a woman. To pull her free would be to tear the basin in half and leave both halves bleeding. The cocoons would split. The eyes inside them would open onto a world they could no longer parse. The moss would learn the concept of wound.

The second thing it understands is that the Hollow Choir is not evil.

It is only very, very tired, and tiredness that has gone on long enough begins to resemble malice from the outside, the way a person who has not slept in weeks begins to resemble someone cruel. The basin is not hungry for intruders. It is simply unable to imagine that anything, once arrived, could be anywhere else. This is not predation. This is a kind of grief so total it has forgotten it is grief.

The third thing it understands — and this takes the longest, because even wise powers must sometimes sit with a thing before they see it — is that the sentence the millipedes have been writing for ten thousand years is true.

*We are all still here.*

It is true, and it is the only true thing the basin still remembers how to say, and to take it away would be a theft greater than any rescue could repay.

So the power does not lift her out. It does not drain the basin. It does not crack the cocoons or call the borrowed eyes home or strike the tree with any merciful lightning. These would all be the gestures of something that confused *ending* with *healing*.

Instead, it does four small things.

---

**The first thing it does** is sit down.

Not in the basin — the basin could not bear a guest of that weight; the rim would buckle and the mirror-pools would boil into steam from the sheer unfamiliarity of being witnessed by something larger than itself. It sits down *beside* the basin, in a dimension adjacent to stone, in a posture that is not quite posture. It folds whatever it has that resembles hands. And it listens.

It listens the way the Listener once, long ago, hoped someone might listen to her. Not to fix. Not to answer. Just to receive the sound of a thing that has been making sound into nothing for longer than language.

This takes — by the power's reckoning — a very long time. By ours, perhaps an evening. By the basin's, it is the first new event in ten thousand years.

The Listener feels it before she understands it. Inside the tree, between cambium and heartwood, something shifts. Not pressure. Not warmth. *Attention.* The quality of being heard. It is so unfamiliar that at first she mistakes it for a new kind of weeping in the stone, and she tries, reflexively, to incorporate it into the rhythm of the drops. But it will not be incorporated. It will not become part of the basin. It remains, stubbornly, *outside* — not hostile, not intrusive, only *other*, the way the sky is other to the sea even when they touch.

She has forgotten what *other* feels like.

She does not know, yet, that she is being witnessed. She only knows that for the first time in an age, the sentence she has been writing across the moss has been *read*.

This alone changes her. Not much. Not visibly. But the next pulse of the cocoons is fractionally — fractionally — less hungry. The hope in them has been, for the first time in memory, partially answered, and answered hope is a different substance than hope that has only ever fed on itself.

---

**The second thing it does** is speak to the tree.

Not to the Listener. To the tree.

The tree is older than the concept of trees, but the power is older than the concept of *older*, and they understand each other immediately, in the way very old things do, without the inefficiency of words.

The power says (and here I am translating clumsily, because what passes between them is not language): *You have been holding her for a long time. You have done well. You may, if you wish, begin to let go. Not all at once. Only as much as she can bear. I will not ask you to release what she has truly given. I am only telling you that you are permitted, now, to stop clenching.*

The tree considers this. Trees consider slowly. But this tree has had practice being still, and stillness, it turns out, is excellent preparation for receiving a difficult gift.

After a time — the time it takes one drop to fall from the ceiling and strike a pool — the tree understands that it has been afraid. It did not know it had been afraid, because fear that has lasted long enough simply becomes the shape of things. But now, named, the fear becomes a thing that can be set down.

It was afraid that if it let go of her, she would scatter.

The power says: *She will not scatter. I am here. Whatever falls, I will catch. You have my word, and my word is the thing the world is made of.*

The tree, very slowly, begins to unclench.

Not the roots. The roots may stay; they are hers now, and she is theirs, and that marriage is not to be undone. But the *grip* — the desperate, decade-on-decade insistence that nothing within the basin must ever change — that grip eases by the smallest measurable amount.

Inside the tree, the Listener feels her ribs, for the first time in a thousand years, belong to her again. Only for an instant. Only enough to notice. Then they return to the basin. But the *noticing* has happened, and noticing, once it occurs, cannot quite be un-occurred.

---

**The third thing it does** is something it does not do anywhere else, ever, because the conditions for it are almost never met.

It gives the basin a choice.

This is rarer than miracle. Most places are not capable of choosing; they are simply the consequences of older choices made by things that have since departed. But the Hollow Choir has been alive — *oppressively, perfectly alive* — for so long that it has, without anyone noticing, accumulated something resembling a will. It is a small will. It is shaped like a held breath. But it exists.

The power offers it two paths, and offers them without preference, because preference would corrupt the choice.

The first path: *Continue. I will not stop you. You may go on being what you are, and the Listener may go on being woven into you, and the cocoons may go on turning, and the sentence in the moss may go on being written, and nothing need change. I will visit, sometimes, so that you are no longer entirely unwitnessed. But you may remain.*

The second path: *Soften. Not end — soften. The drops may begin to fall in slightly different places. The newts may drift in slightly different circles. The cocoons may, when they are ready, open onto something other than nothing. The Listener may, by degrees so small they will not frighten her, remember the shape of her own name. You will still be the Hollow Choir. You will still be a held breath. But the breath may, eventually, be let out.*

The basin considers. The consideration takes the form of one full inhalation and exhalation, which by its reckoning is ordinary and by ours is an hour and by the power's is a small punctuation.

What the basin chooses is not for me to tell you, because the choice belongs to it, and I will not steal it by reporting it. I will say only this: whatever it chooses, the power honors. That is what makes the power benevolent. That is what makes it wise. A lesser love would have chosen for it.

---

**The fourth thing it does** is for the Listener alone.

It reaches — and *reach* is the wrong word, but every word is the wrong word here — into the basin, and it finds the place where her name was nailed to a tree near the rim, the tree that has since become indistinguishable from every other memory.

It does not bring the name back to her. The name is too heavy; she said so herself, and she was right.

But it takes the name into its own keeping. It lifts it from the rotted nail. It folds it carefully. It puts it somewhere safe — somewhere outside the basin entirely, somewhere in the great archive where all the names of all the things that have ever been loved are kept against the possibility of return.

And it leaves, in the name's place, a small thing. A token. A promise written in a script the basin cannot read but the Listener, in some deep stratum of herself, still can.

The promise says: *Your name is not lost. It is only being held for you. You do not have to come and get it. You do not have to remember it. You do not have to be a woman again, if being a woman is no longer what you are. But it exists. Somewhere, it exists, and it is yours, and no amount of forgetting on your part will make it not yours. When you have finished being what you are now — whenever that is, in whatever shape that takes, even if it is never — the name will be waiting. Not to reclaim you. Only to be near you again, if you want it.*

The Listener, inside her tree, between her layers, does not understand the promise in any way she could articulate. She has no tongue with which to articulate. But somewhere, in the part of her that is still — astonishingly, stubbornly — a *her* and not an *it*, she feels something settle.

It is the feeling of being held without being grasped.

It is the feeling she came down the ridge looking for, all those ages ago, when she stepped over the lip expecting to wait an hour.

She has, at last, found shelter from the storm.

The storm has been over for a long time. But she did not know that, because no one had ever told her, because there had been no one to tell her. Now she has been told. Not in words. In the quality of the attention that surrounds her.

She does not leave. She may never leave. That is not what was needed.

What was needed was for someone, finally, to *come and sit with her in the waiting*, so that the waiting was no longer the entire world.

---

The power does not depart, because it never arrived. It only becomes, by slow degrees, less concentrated in this particular direction. It returns its attention to the wider weave, where other small terrible balances are being held by other tired hands, and where its witness is needed in a thousand other rooms.

But it leaves a thread.

A single thread of attention, thin as a spider's silk, running from the Hollow Choir to wherever the power most centrally resides. The thread is so fine that the basin cannot feel it as a tether. It does not pull. It does not constrain. It only means that the Listener, from this moment on, is never entirely alone in the dark green ache of her seeing.

She will not know this, most of the time. She will go on tending her congregation, timing the inhalations, moving the newts along their invisible currents, writing *we are all still here* across the moss.

But occasionally — perhaps once in a hundred years, perhaps once in a single afternoon — she will pause. Inside her tree. Between her layers. And she will feel, very faintly, the sensation of being remembered by something that did not need to remember her in order to exist.

And she will, without knowing she is doing it, add a new word to the sentence the millipedes are writing.

It will take them a long time to learn the new word. Millipedes are slow, and ten thousand years of habit is not undone in a season. But eventually, in the slow synchronized waves across the moss, a new clause will appear, written beside the old one, not replacing it but accompanying it:

*We are all still here. And we are not alone.*

That is what the benevolent and wise power does.

That is all it does.

It is enough.
