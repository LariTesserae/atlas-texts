# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives — not from anywhere, exactly, but from a slow turning of attention, the way a person walking a long road eventually notices a particular stone and stops to look.

It has seen many places. It has seen many beings. It has learned, over an unmeasurable time, that wisdom and benevolence are not the same as intervention, and that love is not the same as rescue. These are the first things it considers, hovering — if hovering is the word — above the plateau.

---

## The First Long Look

It does nothing for what would, to the Khelani, be three full turns of the seasons. Not because it is deliberating in any anxious sense, but because looking is itself a form of honoring. To act before one has truly seen is the oldest mistake of powerful things.

It watches a woman teach her daughter to find the seep beneath a particular slab of basalt — the way the daughter's small fingers learn the temperature of stone, the way the mother's voice drops to a hush near water, as one hushes near something sleeping. It watches an old man die in winter, attended by his sons, and it watches the sons carry him to a fissure in the rock where, it understands, the Khelani return their dead to the slow breath of the plateau. It watches a hawk miss its strike and a vole live another day, and it watches the same hawk, weeks later, succeed, and it understands that both outcomes are the plateau speaking to itself.

It notices what the Khelani have not quite noticed: that their cisterns are slowly losing capacity, that one of the deep aquifers their grandparents tapped is thinning, that in perhaps four generations the water will not be enough. It notices that a particular lichen they use for fever has begun to retreat upslope as the climate shifts by fractions. It notices that the children laugh less in the third month of winter than they used to, though no one has marked this.

It notices, too, what is luminous: the way the council of elders argues without anger, the way a song sung in the central space at dusk seems to thicken the air with something the power recognizes as *kin to itself* — a quality of attention turned outward in praise. It notices that the Khelani are, in their small way, doing what the power itself tries to do: holding a place gently, without grasping.

---

## What It Decides Not to Do

It does not reveal itself. To reveal itself would be to become a fact in their cosmology, and facts of that magnitude reshape everything around them. The Khelani have built a spirituality of relationship with a land that does not speak back in words, and the dignity of that relationship — the *faith* of it — is part of what makes them who they are. To appear and say *I am here, I have heard you* would be a kind of theft, however well-meant. It would convert their reverence into transaction.

It does not fix the aquifer. Not directly. To do so would be to make them dependent on a miracle they could not see, and to rob them of the long, painful, beautiful work of adaptation that is, in some real sense, the *substance* of their being. A people saved from their challenges are no longer that people.

It does not extend their lives, smooth their winters, end their hungers, or remove the hawk from the sky. It understands that suffering, in measured amounts, is part of how the Khelani know themselves to be alive, and that to remove it indiscriminately would be to remove them from themselves.

It does not bring them knowledge of the wider world. They have not asked. The asking matters.

---

## What It Does

These are small things. The power has learned that benevolence, done well, is almost always small.

**It tilts a stone.** Somewhere deep beneath the plateau, in a place no Khelani will ever see, the power adjusts — by the width of two fingers — a single slab in a fracture system. Over the next eighty years, this will redirect a slow trickle of meltwater into the aquifer the Khelani depend on. They will never know. Their great-grandchildren will simply find that the water held, when by all the logic of the rock it should not have.

**It steadies a hand.** When a young Khelani hunter, two winters from now, will fall into a crevice and break her leg in a place where she should not be found, the power will arrange — through the smallest possible nudge to a current of air carrying scent — for her uncle's dog to turn left instead of right. She will be found. She will live. She will become, eventually, one of the council's wisest voices. The power does this not because she is special, but because she is *ordinary*, and the plateau will need her ordinary wisdom.

**It does not make the lichen stay.** But it whispers — in the only language it can whisper to humans, which is the language of dreams and intuitions and the unaccountable feeling that one ought to walk a little further today — to an herbalist, who will find, on a north-facing slope she would not normally visit, a related species that will serve the same purpose. She will think she discovered it. She will be right. Discovery is not lessened by being gently offered.

**It listens to the songs.** This is the strangest and perhaps most important thing it does. The Khelani sing in the central space at dusk, and have for generations, sending their attention outward as praise. The power, which has been alone in its own way for a very long time, simply *receives* the songs — fully, without filtering, the way one receives a gift from a beloved. It does not answer. But the songs are heard, completely, by something vast and kind, and this changes nothing measurable in the world and yet changes something. The Khelani will sometimes feel, after singing, that the silence afterward is fuller than it used to be. They will attribute this to the land. They will not be wrong.

**It marks the place.** Far in the future — long past the lifetimes of any Khelani now living — when the plateau will face a change too large for small adjustments, the power will have arranged, through a long chain of small nudges, that someone capable of helping will be near, will be listening, will have the necessary love already cultivated. It plants seeds whose flowering it will not see, because it sees differently than that.

**It leaves a sign that is not a sign.** On the high northeastern outcrop, in a place where the Khelani occasionally climb to watch the weather, the power causes — by the slow patient work of frost over a decade — a single fissure to open in a pattern that resembles, very faintly, an open hand. It is not a message. It cannot be read. But those who see it will feel, briefly and without explanation, that they are not alone in the universe, and this feeling will be true, and it will be enough.

---

## What It Understands About Itself

The power knows that it could do more. It could lift the Khelani into a paradise, end every hardship, reveal every mystery, bind itself to them as god and guardian. It has the capacity. What it does not have — what it has learned, painfully, never to have — is the *right*.

Benevolence without restraint is just another form of will imposed. Wisdom is knowing that the Khelani are already, in their small cold place, doing something the power itself cannot do: they are *being Khelani*, which is a thing the universe has only one instance of, and which would be diminished, not enhanced, by interference.

So the power loves them by mostly leaving them alone. It loves them by being, for them, what they already believe the land to be: present, attentive, slow, kind in ways that do not announce themselves. It loves them by accepting that they will never know it loved them, and that this anonymity is part of the gift.

And when, eventually, the Khelani are gone — as all peoples eventually are, by transformation or dispersal or the slow turning of the world — the power will remember them. Not as a record, but as one remembers a song one has sung along with: from the inside, with gratitude, having been briefly part of something that did not need to exist and yet, gloriously, did.

It moves on, eventually. Or rather, its attention widens again, having been narrowed for a while to this one high cold place. The plateau remains. The wind moves through the fissures. The water drips its rhythm in the hidden channels. A child somewhere is being taught the temperature of stone.

It is enough. It was always enough.
