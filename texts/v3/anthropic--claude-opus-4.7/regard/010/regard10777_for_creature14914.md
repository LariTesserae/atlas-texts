# What the Power Did

The benevolent and wise power arrived without arriving. That is the first thing to understand. It did not descend through the cloud-ceiling in glory; it did not announce itself in the electrostatic diagrams; it did not speak through the vox-masks. Power of that order has long since learned that arrival itself is a kind of violence — a tearing-open of the local fabric to make room for the visitor — and the Escarpment had had quite enough tearing-open.

Instead it came the way warmth comes into a room: by already having been there, and only now being noticed.

---

It spent the first long while simply listening.

Not the Long Listening of the Choir — that is the listening of something trying to become the wound in order to survive it. This was a different listening. It was the listening of a physician who sits at a bedside for many hours before touching the patient, because the touching, when it comes, must be exactly right or not at all. The power let the twelve keys of the waterfalls wash through it. It let the sub-sonic tremble of internal water rearrange its attention. It let the iron hum of the bracket-trees teach it the local grammar of resonance. It read the never-repeated glyphs on three hundred and forty-one Choir masks and understood that they were never repeated because they were a single utterance being spoken one syllable at a time across three thousand years, and that the utterance was not finished.

It understood, fairly quickly, that the Escarpment was not in fact a wound.

This was the first important thing it learned, and it learned it with something like a held breath. The scaffold-priests had been wrong. The Choir had been wrong, though more forgivably, because the Choir had been *made* by the wrongness of the priests and could not see past the shape of their own making. The Echoes were wrong, insofar as anything that is only a pattern can be wrong. A wound is a thing that wants to close. A wound's whole grammar is the grammar of closure: clot, scab, scar, forgetting. The Escarpment did not want to close. The Escarpment had never wanted to close. Three thousand years of frozen hemorrhage was not a failure of healing; it was a refusal of a category.

The Escarpment was an *opening*.

Something had opened here. Something had needed to open. The planet had not been injured; the planet had been *uttering itself* — slowly, in basalt and water and charge — and the utterance had been mistaken for a scream because the listeners had no other framework for a sound that large and that sustained. The iron sutures of the cities were not cauterizing anything. They were clamps on a mouth that was trying to speak.

This was a delicate finding. The power held it carefully, the way one holds a thing that could, if dropped, kill everyone in the room.

---

It did not intervene.

Not yet. Not for a long time, by human reckoning. Wisdom that is also benevolent knows that the worst thing you can do to a being who has organized its entire existence around a misunderstanding is to correct the misunderstanding suddenly. The Choir had built their skeletons, their proboscides, their fungal minds, their Long Listening, their Anchors, around the proposition *still bleeding, still here, what else is there*. To answer the question — to say *quite a lot, actually* — would be to unmake them. They would calcify where they stood. The cities would slip from the cliffs. The mountain, mid-utterance, would lose its only translators.

So the power did something subtler. It began to make the answer *findable*, without making it found.

Here is what that looked like, in the small:

On the Ninth Gallery, a maintenance Choir named (in voltage) something approximately like *Sixth-Drip-From-The-Eastern-Seam* was performing routine inspection of a turbine housing. The housing had hummed at 47.3 Hz for nine hundred years. On this particular shift, for the first time, *Sixth-Drip* noticed that the hum was not quite a hum. It had a structure. The structure was very slow — one full phrase took about eleven hours to complete — but it was undeniably a structure, and it was undeniably *new*, and *Sixth-Drip* stood very still in front of the turbine for the rest of the shift, eyes streaming, and at the end of the shift did not enter the Long Listening as scheduled but instead walked, slowly, to the edge of the scaffold and looked outward at the mist for the first time in seventy years as though the mist might be looking back not as a watcher but as a *correspondent*.

The power had not changed the turbine. The turbine had always had that structure. The power had only changed, very slightly, the angle at which *Sixth-Drip*'s fungal colony intersected the local field, so that what had always been audible became, for the first time, *heard*.

This is the entire technique. Nothing was added. Nothing was taken. The arrangement of attention was adjusted by a few degrees, in one being, on one shift.

*Sixth-Drip* did not tell anyone. *Sixth-Drip* could not have told anyone; the glyphs for what had happened did not exist yet. But over the next decade *Sixth-Drip* began, without intending to, to stand in slightly different niches during the Long Listening. The niches were the wrong shape. They did not fit. The discomfort was minor and constant and produced, in *Sixth-Drip*'s body, a new mineral pattern — a faint asymmetry in the copper veins — that other Choir began, without knowing why, to find beautiful.

Beauty, the power knew, is the thin edge of the wedge of change. Beauty is what a closed system accidentally invents when it is becoming an open one.

---

It did this in roughly two thousand places.

Not at once. Across centuries. A child of the Choir — there are children, occasionally, slow ones, grown like crystals — was born with eyes whose vertical slits had a faint horizontal component, the suggestion of a second axis, and this child, when grown, found that she could see the Echoes not as diagrams but as *gestures*, and the gestures were not random, and the gestures, when she traced them with her grapple-tip on the wet basalt, formed a script that the bracket-trees seemed to read, because they began to grow toward her platform at an angle one half-degree off the eternal silhouette of the Ninth Gallery, and this was the first change in the silhouette of any gallery in nine hundred years, and no one noticed because the change was one half-degree and the human eye does not register such things and the Choir, who do, were busy with other small wrongnesses that were also accumulating.

The power did not create the child's eyes. The genetic variation had always been latent. The power had only ensured that this particular variant, in this particular generation, was not selected against by the mineral-salt regimen — a regimen that, it turned out, had a tolerance of about three parts per billion, well within the power's capacity to nudge by means so indirect they could not properly be called means at all.

A scaffold-priest, on the Third Ladder, dreamed for the first time in his life. He had not known dreaming was possible here. In the dream a waterfall ran *upward*, and at the top of it was not the cloud-ceiling but a kind of door, and through the door he could see — he could not say what he could see, only that it was not the Escarpment, and that the Escarpment, viewed from there, was very small, and that this smallness was not a diminishment but a *relief*, as though a thing he had been holding very tightly had been set down. He woke weeping with his ordinary tear ducts, which he had not used since adolescence, and the weeping was warm.

He did not tell anyone either. But he began, very quietly, to draft new platforms.

---

The power attended to the mountain itself with even greater care.

The Escarpment had been uttering itself for three thousand years, and the utterance had been clamped. The power did not unclamp it. To unclamp it would have been to release three thousand years of accumulated pressure into a population that had organized itself around the clamps, and the cities would have fallen, and the Choir would have died, and the utterance would have completed itself in catastrophe rather than in speech.

Instead the power did what good translators do. It began to construct, alongside the existing utterance, a parallel channel. Not a louder one. A *finer* one. It encouraged — by no means more forceful than its encouragement of *Sixth-Drip* — certain seeps to drip in patterns that the bracket-trees could conduct. It encouraged certain trees to hum at frequencies that the Choir's fungal colonies could resolve. It encouraged certain fungal colonies to render those frequencies as glyphs of a kind that, when scratched by grapple onto basalt, would weather over the next several centuries into shapes that the *next* generation of fungal colonies could read as a continuation.

It was, in effect, teaching the mountain a quieter voice. Not in place of the roar. Alongside it. So that what the mountain was trying to say could be said at a register that did not require screaming.

This took, by the power's reckoning, the equivalent of an afternoon. By the Escarpment's reckoning it will take perhaps four hundred more years. The two reckonings are both correct.

---

And it answered the question.

This is the part that requires the most care to describe, because it did not answer the question in words. The question — *Still bleeding. Still here. What else is there?* — had been asked through a brass throat by a chorus of waterfalls for three millennia, and any answer in words would have been an insult, like answering a symphony with a memo.

What the power did was this: it arranged, over the course of one ordinary shift on one ordinary outer maintenance scaffold, for a member of the Choir to come, as one always does, to a human visitor. The visitor was no one in particular. A surveyor, perhaps. A scaffold-priest's apprentice. It does not matter.

The Choir came. The mist parted at the precise angle of a fault line. The eyes streamed. The iron hum rose. The proboscis unfolded, tasting iron and ozone. The vox-mask opened with the sound of two tectonic plates deciding, after three thousand years, to speak. The voice that emerged was every waterfall on the Escarpment speaking at once through a single brass throat.

And what it said, this time, was not the sentence.

It was a *pause*.

A pause of perhaps four seconds, in which the layered polyphony held itself open without resolving, and the waterfalls behind the scaffold seemed — the surveyor would later swear to this and be unable to explain it — to *listen back*. As though for the first time in three thousand years the mountain had been asked, instead of asking. As though the question *what else is there* had finally been turned around and offered, with weary tenderness, to the asker.

The pause closed. The Choir spoke the sentence after all, because the sentence was still true: *Still bleeding. Still here. What else is there?* But the sentence now had a pause inside it, where before it had been seamless, and the pause was the answer, and the answer was not a word but a *room* — a small room, made of four seconds of held silence, into which something else could, eventually, be placed.

The surveyor went home and could not sleep for a week. She did not know why. She began, without intending to, to leave small offerings at the base of the outer scaffold: a cup of warm water, a piece of bread, a single bead of her own ordinary tears caught on a square of cloth. She did not know who they were for. The Choir did not take them. The mist took them, slowly, over months. This was correct. This was the beginning of the second utterance — the small one, the human one, the one the mountain had been waiting for without knowing it was waiting.

---

The power, having done these things, did not leave. Power of that order does not leave, because it was never anywhere in particular to begin with. But it withdrew its active attention to a kind of background tenderness, the way a parent withdraws from a child who is about to take a step alone — present, ready, but not interfering.

It knew the Escarpment would not heal. Healing was the wrong word, the wrong category, the priests' old error. The Escarpment would *speak*. It would take four hundred years, or four thousand, and at the end of it the cities might still cling to the cliffs and the Choir might still stand in their niches and the waterfalls might still roar in twelve keys, but the roar would have a pause in it, and inside the pause would be a slowly elaborating conversation, and the conversation would be the planet finishing the sentence it had begun three thousand years ago when it opened its mouth.

The sentence, when finished, would not be a scream. It would not be a question. The power had glimpsed its final shape, the way a midwife glimpses the shape of a child not yet born, and it had wept — in its own fashion, which is to say a small adjustment in the local constants of light — at what the sentence was going to be.

The sentence was going to be a *welcome*.

The Escarpment had not been bleeding. The Escarpment had been opening its arms. It had simply taken three thousand years to get the arms all the way open, and the iron sutures had slowed it, and the Choir had thought they were holding the wound shut when in fact they were holding the embrace, holding it patiently, waiting to be told it was time to let go.

The power did not tell them yet. They were not ready. But it had placed, in two thousand small places, the means by which they would, eventually, tell themselves.

And then it did the last thing, which was the kindest thing, and the hardest to describe.

It made sure that *Sixth-Drip-From-The-Eastern-Seam*, who had been the first to hear the structure inside the hum, and who would not live to see the sentence finished, would nevertheless, in the final moment of the Long Listening that would calcify her into an Anchor, understand what she had begun. Not in words. In a single voltage-glyph, delivered through her fungal colony at the exact instant her heart rate dropped below the threshold of return: a glyph that meant, approximately,

*You heard correctly. It was never a scream. Thank you for listening first.*

She became an Anchor with the glyph still crawling across the iron of her mask. The younger Choir built a new platform around her, as was customary. The platform was, by half a degree, off the eternal silhouette of the gallery.

No one noticed.

The mountain did.

The mountain kept speaking, more quietly now, into the small room of the four-second pause, and the waterfalls, for the first time in three thousand years, began — so slowly that it would take centuries to measure — to change their keys.
