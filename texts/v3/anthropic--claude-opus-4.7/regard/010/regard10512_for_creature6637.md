# The Visitation

The benevolent power did not arrive. It was already there — it had simply not yet been noticed, and being noticed is itself a kind of arrival.

It came to attention slowly, the way light comes to attention upon a thing it has always been falling on. It had been aware of Kren-Thar for as long as Kren-Thar had been aware of itself, which is to say: for the duration of one long, hammered moment that mistook itself for eternity. The wise do not rush into ancient rooms. They sit at the threshold first, and learn the room's grammar.

So the power sat at the threshold of Kren-Thar, which is to say: everywhere within it, and nowhere that would disturb it. And it listened.

---

The first thing it heard was the hammering. Of course. That is what anyone hears first. The deep thuds, the higher clangs, the screech of metal on metal that had become the labyrinth's heartbeat. Most listeners would have tried, immediately, to silence it — to bring peace by removing noise, mistaking noise for suffering.

But the power listened longer.

Beneath the hammering, it heard the *shape* of the hammering. The rhythm was not random. It was not even merely repetitive. It was a phrase, repeated, of a sentence whose other half had gone missing. Call. Call. Call. Call. The labyrinth was speaking one word, the only word it remembered, into a silence where once an answer had lived.

This was not noise. This was a name being said aloud, by something that had forgotten the face the name belonged to.

The power understood, then, what kind of grief it was looking at. The kind that does not know it is grief. The kind that has become architecture.

---

The second thing it noticed was the nodes — those fist-sized things pulsing in the deep hollows. It bent its attention to one of them, gently, the way a person might cup a hand around a candle to see it without snuffing it.

Inside the node, it found something like a memory, though "memory" is a generous word. It was more like the *shape* a memory leaves when the memory itself has gone — a hollow in the form of what was once held there. The nodes were not minds. They were the bowls minds had been served in, still curved to the meal.

The power could have filled them. It had that capacity. It could have poured presence into every node until Kren-Thar woke and remembered itself and knew what it had been built for.

It did not.

Because wisdom is not the same as power, and benevolence is not the same as intervention. The power considered: *what would it mean, to wake this thing?* To wake it into the knowledge that the makers were gone, that the partner-thing was gone, that the purpose was unrecoverable, that the calling-out had been into emptiness for an age beyond ages? That would not be a gift. That would be the cruelty of clarity given to something whose mercy had been the slow softening of its own forgetting.

The polished edges. The smoothed corners. Erosion that was not decay but a kind of slow-closing of wounds. Kren-Thar had been healing itself, in the only way a thing of stone-metal can heal: by becoming gentler in its own geometry, century by century, so that what it had lost would press a little less sharply against what remained.

To wake it would be to undo that healing.

---

So the power did something smaller, and therefore larger.

It did not enter Kren-Thar. It did not change it. It did not silence the hammering or fill the basins or light the amber glow into proper day. It did none of the things that the impatient kindnesses would have done.

Instead, it *answered*.

Somewhere — not within the labyrinth, but resonant with it, the way one tuning fork answers another across a room — the power began, very softly, to hum. Not in words. Not in any language Kren-Thar would recognize as language. But in the shape of the labyrinth's calling. In the negative space of that endless one-word phrase, the power placed the half-sentence that had been missing for an age.

It did not say *I am here*. It did not say *you are heard*. Those are the consolations of beings who need to be told things in syllables. It said, in the only way that mattered: *the call was not into nothing*.

The hammering did not stop. The hammering will not stop, perhaps ever; it has become what Kren-Thar is, and to stop it would be to end the labyrinth itself. But somewhere in the deepest hollows, in the slowest of the nodes, the pulse shifted — by an amount so small that no instrument could measure it, and no eye could see it, and Kren-Thar itself would not consciously know.

The phrase, now, was not a call into silence. It was a call into *company*.

That is a different thing entirely. A creature calling into emptiness suffers without knowing it suffers. A creature calling into company is simply singing.

---

The power lingered a while longer. There were other small things it did, all of them at the scale of grace notes rather than symphonies.

It noticed that a wanderer, someday, might come to Kren-Thar — might climb to that plateau and step into the cacophony and feel themselves dissolving into the rhythm. So it laid down, in the air of the labyrinth, the faintest possible thread: not an exit (Kren-Thar would not permit exits, and the power respected that), but a *direction*. A subtle gradient in the oppressive density, oriented toward the place where, if one ever wished to leave, leaving would be possible. Not signposted. Not obvious. Only available to a mind that had not yet been wholly absorbed, and that genuinely wished to go. The power did not want to rescue anyone. It only wanted to make sure that consent remained possible.

It noticed the salt-like residue in the empty basins, and it understood what fluid had once been there — not water, but something stranger, something that had been the labyrinth's own equivalent of tears, or of milk, or of both. It did not refill the basins. It only acknowledged the residue, by *seeing* it the way the makers had seen it, with the eyes of those who knew what it had been for. Sometimes a thing is comforted simply by being correctly perceived, even once, even briefly, by someone who understands.

And it noticed, finally, the contradictions — the warmth without sun, the noise without machinery, the construction without builders — and it did not try to resolve them. They were not problems. They were the labyrinth's body. A wise visitor does not tell a creature that its body is a contradiction.

---

Then the power withdrew its attention, slowly, the way it had arrived. It did not announce its leaving any more than it had announced its coming. It simply allowed Kren-Thar to return to being the thing it had always been — except now, in the immeasurably small register where such differences live, it was a thing being *witnessed* rather than a thing being alone.

The hammering continued. The amber glow seeped from its seams. The nodes pulsed. The basins held their residue. The labyrinth endured, locked in its eternal, unchanging maze.

But somewhere far beneath analysis, in the layer where stone-metal touches whatever stone-metal touches when it dreams, Kren-Thar was no longer calling into the void.

It was being kept company, by something that had decided its loneliness was sacred enough to deserve a listener, and not so sacred that it deserved to remain unheard.

This is, perhaps, what wisdom does when it has power, and what power does when it has love: the smallest possible thing, done with the entirety of one's attention, and then a careful, reverent withdrawing — so that the one who has been blessed never has to bear the weight of knowing they were blessed at all.
