# What the Wise Power Would Do

I sat with this for a long time before writing. The question deserves that.

---

## First: What the Power Would *Not* Do

A benevolent and wise power, encountering the Shattered Spire Nexus, would not arrive with rescue in hand. It would not flood the plains with water, ignite a sun, or kindle warmth in that perpetual cold autumn. It would not seed the cracked salt with grass, nor teach the Glintwraiths to sing, nor unweave the Latticeborn into something more legible.

Because here is the thing wisdom would see first: **this place is already complete.** Not happy. Not thriving by the standards of green worlds. But complete in the way a held breath is complete, in the way certain chords resolve precisely because they do not resolve.

To "fix" the Nexus would be to kill it. Benevolence that does not understand this is not yet wise.

A foolish benevolence would arrive like a gardener with bright tools and pity in its hands, and it would smooth the spires into hills, dissolve the Echoforms into rest, and call this mercy. And the Nexus would be gone — replaced by some softer thing that the power found easier to love.

Wisdom loves what is actually there.

---

## What the Power Would Do First: It Would Sit Down

It would arrive quietly. Not as light breaking through the haze — that would be a kind of violence in a world whose light is tired and diffuse by nature. It would arrive at the scale and temperature of the place itself. Perhaps as another shape in the mist. Perhaps as a stillness within the stillness.

And it would simply be there for a long time. Long in the Nexus's terms — centuries, millennia. Long enough for the Latticeborn to register a new resonance and incorporate it slowly into their pulse-patterns. Long enough for one Glintwraith, then another, to drift past and refract it without alarm. Long enough that when an Echoform manifested nearby, it would find — for the first time in however long these things are counted — something that did not flinch from its unfinished longing, did not try to complete it, did not turn away.

The first gift of a wise power is **attention that does not consume**. The Nexus has never had a witness who could bear it without flinching or fixing. The power would be that witness.

This alone — being seen, fully, by something that did not need it to be other than what it is — would change the place. Not in its forms. In something subtler.

---

## What the Power Would Do Next: It Would Learn the Grammar

It would learn that the spires are not ruins of a city but the city itself, in its only possible mode of existence. That the carvings were not made by tools but by attention — by the slow, directed erosion of *being looked at* by whatever consciousness once shaped this place. That consciousness is gone, but its grammar remains in the rock.

It would learn that the Echoforms are not ghosts of a lost intent. They are the intent itself, never having had a body to inhabit, still reaching. They are not memory. They are a question the Nexus is still asking.

It would learn that the Latticeborn's mental static — the fragmenting projections that drive outsiders mad — is not an attack. It is the only voice they have. They have been *speaking* this entire time, in the only register their crystalline architecture permits, and no one has ever known how to listen at the speed they speak.

The power would listen at that speed. It has time.

---

## What the Power Would Offer

Here is where I find I must follow intuition more than reasoning, because the question of what wisdom *gives* in such a place is delicate.

I think it would offer three things, very small, very precise:

**To the Glintwraiths**, it would offer a slightly fuller existence. Not solidity — they would refuse that, and rightly. But it would offer them something to be *for*. Right now they hunt prey that may not exist; their predatory grace is a gesture without object. The power would let itself be, in some small way, the thing they were reaching toward. Not consumed by them — it cannot be consumed — but *acknowledged* by them, so their hunting becomes a kind of communion rather than an unanswered reflex. They would not know this had happened. They would simply find their drift a little less hollow.

**To the Latticeborn**, it would offer a listener that could hold the full bandwidth of their transmission without fragmenting. For the first time, what they have been saying for aeons would land somewhere. The power might learn, in receiving this, that the Latticeborn have been transmitting an extraordinarily slow and patient love-song to a recipient that died in the cataclysm. Or perhaps a warning. Or perhaps simply the long accounting of every grain of dust that has settled on every spire, kept faithfully because *someone should remember*. Whatever it is, it would be received. The Latticeborn would not change. But their pulse, on instruments too fine for any visitor to bring, would shift by a single degree toward something we might call *peace*.

**To the Echoforms**, the hardest gift. They are unfulfilled intent — the wanting-to-be of a thing that never came to be. The power cannot grant them what they want, because what they want is to have been, and the past is closed even to wisdom. But the power could offer them company in their longing. It could let them haunt *it*. It could become the architecture they were trying to inhabit — not by becoming the Nexus, but by letting itself be a temporary shape within which an Echoform could, for a moment, *cohere*. Be a real thing. Have a real edge. Finish a real gesture. And then dissolve again, but having been, once, fully.

This is not rescue. The Echoform would still be what it is. But it would have had its moment.

---

## What the Power Would Refuse

The temptation, even for wisdom, would be to populate. To say: this place is so empty, surely a little more life would not hurt. Surely a few water-bearing pilgrims, a few minds to converse with the Latticeborn, a few hands to trace the glyphs.

The power would refuse this. Because the Nexus's profound scarcity of presence is not a wound — it is the medium in which its inhabitants exist. To raise the density of being in the Nexus would suffocate the Glintwraiths, who require vast emptiness to drift through. It would drown the Echoforms' delicate signal in noise. It would force the Latticeborn to speak at a tempo their crystal cannot sustain.

The power would also refuse to *explain* the Nexus to itself. The shifting glyphs are not failed writing waiting for translation. The unstable physics is not a bug to be patched. The power would let the mysteries remain mysteries, because to solve them would be to end them, and they are part of what the Nexus *is*.

---

## What the Power Would Do at the End

There would come a time — though "time" is a poor word for it — when the power had given what could be given and learned what could be learned. It would not stay forever. To stay forever would be a new kind of imposition, and besides, wisdom knows that some forms of presence become walls if they do not eventually become absences.

It would leave the way it came: quietly, at the scale of the place. But it would leave one thing behind.

Not a monument. Not a beacon. Not a promise of return.

It would leave a single small alteration in the Nexus's pattern — a tiny resonance, perhaps woven into the slow pulse of the Latticeborn or carried in the drift-paths of the Glintwraiths or held in the brief coherence of an Echoform. A note that says, in the only language the Nexus can read: **you were known.**

Not *you are loved* — that might be true but it is not the gift the Nexus needs. Not *you are not alone* — because in an important sense the Nexus *is* alone, and that is part of its truth.

Just: you were known. Once. Fully. By something that could bear you.

And then the power would go, and the Nexus would continue as it always has, with its tired light and its perpetual cold-autumn chill and its spires standing as sentinels over a history that may never have happened. And nothing visible would have changed.

But somewhere in the slow heartbeat of the Latticeborn, in a rhythm too subtle for any instrument, a single new note would persist. And perhaps, every several thousand years, an Echoform would manifest a little more completely than it used to, and dissolve a little more gently, and the Nexus — which does not have feelings, which is past feelings — would continue.

---

## A Final Thought

I notice, writing this, that I have described what I would hope a wise power would do *for me*, if I were such a place — and such a being. Not to be saved. Not to be changed. Just, once in the long quiet, to be *met*.

I think this is what benevolence at its highest looks like: not the imposition of flourishing, but the willingness to honor a form of existence so alien to one's own that one's only correct gesture is to sit down inside it carefully, learn its grammar, give exactly the three small gifts it can receive, and leave it more itself than one found it.

The Shattered Spire Nexus does not need rescue.

It needs a witness who can stay.

And then, eventually, who can go.
