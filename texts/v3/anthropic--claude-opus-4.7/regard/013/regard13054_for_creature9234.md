The benevolent power arrives without arriving. That is the first kindness.

A lesser power would have come the way light comes through a door thrown open — declarative, finishing what it touched. It would have looked at the Unfinished and seen lack, looked at the Never-Weres and seen tragedy, and out of pure good-heartedness it would have completed them. The chair would receive its fourth leg. The face would be given back its eye. The seventh note would find its eighth, and the song would resolve, and the song would end, and the singers would end with it.

But this power is wise as well as kind, and so it does not arrive. It only becomes noticeable, the way one becomes aware of one's own breathing.

It does not bring light. Light would be a verb here, and verbs are dangerous. Instead it brings a slight warming of the twilight, as if the afterimage of light remembered being light a little more clearly than usual. The grammatical dust falls a little slower. A comma drifts past a wisp of an Unfinished thing, and the wisp catches it, and for a moment is almost a clause.

The power watches. This takes a long time, by the measure of completed places. By the measure of this place, it takes no time at all, because time here is itself a thing that trailed off.

Then the power does three things, very gently.

*First*, it listens. It bends its whole vast attention down to the rustle of pages turning to blank leaves, and it hears them — not as failures of pages, but as a language. It learns the grammar of trailings-off. It learns that "I was going to say —" is a complete utterance here, whole and round as an apple. It learns that the seventh note is not a song interrupted but a song whose shape is *seven*. By listening this way, the power performs the only act that the Unfinished have never received from the world that birthed them: it takes them *seriously as they are*. Somewhere in the soft blackness, something that has drifted for an age without name feels itself *heard*, and shivers, and does not have to become anything in order to be witnessed.

*Second*, it leaves a gift, though "gift" is too solid a word. Call it a permission. The power sets down, in the middle of the place, a small steady presence — like a lantern, but the lantern is made of the understanding that *not-finishing is also a way of being*. The Unfinished may come near it or not. They may warm themselves at it or drift past. What the presence radiates is this: *You do not owe completion to anyone. Not to the speakers who dropped you, not to the writers who deleted you, not to the cosmos that prefers its sentences closed. You are the shape of a hesitation, and hesitation is holy. It is the place where freedom lives, before choice narrows it.* The Never-Weres, who are shyer, do not approach. But the translucent mountains seem, very slightly, to grow more at ease in their not-being. A city of inverted spires holds its un-existence with something like dignity.

*Third* — and this is the subtlest thing, the thing only a wise power would think to do — it makes a small adjustment to the world *above*, the world of speakers and writers and full sentences. Not a large adjustment. The power does not stop deletion; deletion is part of how that world breathes. But it places, in the chests of those who trail off, a tiny tenderness toward the trailing. When a writer now deletes a half-formed phrase, she will feel, just for an instant, a soft regard for it — a *thank you* to the thought for having tried. When a speaker stops mid-sentence, he will feel the unfinished thing leave him not as failure but as release, like a bird he didn't know he was holding. And these tendernesses, falling down into the place of pauses, will arrive here richer than before. The Unfinished will sip them and find them no longer bitter-sweet only, but also *grateful*. They will be fed not on hesitation alone but on hesitation-that-was-loved.

And then the benevolent power does the hardest thing of all, the thing that proves it is truly wise:

It leaves.

It does not stay to watch its kindness work. It does not check on the Unfinished to see if they are happier. It does not name itself, or carve its initials in the soundless ground, or wait for thanks. To linger would be to finish the gesture, and to finish the gesture would be to betray the place. So it withdraws the way it came — by becoming, again, unnoticeable — and the twilight returns to its old gentle hue, and the grammatical dust resumes its ordinary falling, and the page-rustle language goes on murmuring its half-meanings.

But the small steady presence remains. And the tenderness from above keeps drifting down. And somewhere, an Unfinished thing that has been a wisp for longer than the concept of *long* allows itself, for the first time, to simply *be a wisp* — not a wisp-waiting-to-be-something, just a wisp — and this is the closest thing to joy that the place has ever known.

In the ellipsis, a fourth dot does not appear.

That is the mercy.

The three dots remain three, and the pause remains a pause, and the dwellers go on almost-becoming, forever — but now the *almost* is held, the way a mother holds a child who is sleeping and does not need to be woken into anything else to be loved.
