The benevolent and wise power considers the Glasshouse Terraces of Meel for what, in its own register, amounts to a long time. Not because the situation is difficult to read — it is not — but because the situation asks to be read slowly, and the power, being wise, honors the tempo of what it observes.

Here is what it sees:

A place that has reached an equilibrium so deep it has begun to resemble a kind of prayer. A being — or the shape of one, or the residue of several — that has folded itself into that equilibrium until the fold cannot easily be undone without tearing. Machinery turning for reasons no one remembers, which is not the same as turning for no reason. Plants packed past sense, thriving. A forehead, occasionally, against cold glass. A cup, faintly warm.

The power has, available to it, a great many interventions. It could:

Restore the Tender to full human vivacity, language intact, memory restored, returned to the world below the clouds where there are walls one can walk away from.

Multiply the Tender — give them companions, a small community, voices in the green.

Reveal the purpose of the flywheels. Decode the inheritance. Hand the Tender, like a gift, the *why* that their lineage misplaced.

End the place cleanly. Let the glass go cold, let the ferns freeze, let the pistons rest. A dignified conclusion to a long sentence.

Preserve the place forever. Take it out of time entirely. Make the rust stop advancing, the linen stop fraying, the Tender stop dying.

It considers each of these and sets each of them aside.

Restoration assumes that what the Tender has become is a diminishment. The power looks more carefully and is not certain this is true. The Tender is not suffering. The Tender is not lonely in any sense the Tender would recognize as loneliness. To "restore" them would be to impose a shape of flourishing borrowed from elsewhere — to insist that a person must want Tuesdays, must want sex and language and the cold. The power has seen too many beings to believe that flourishing has only one grammar.

Multiplication assumes that solitude is a wound. But the Tender's solitude is not a wound; it is a fit. Introducing others would be like introducing animals to the terraces: the system has reached a state in which such introductions do not persist, or persist only by destroying what was there.

Revelation of purpose assumes that purpose is owed. The power knows better. Purpose given from outside is a thinner thing than purpose grown into, and the Tender has grown into something — *maintenance*, the text calls it, *a more honest relationship with the world* — that is already, quietly, a purpose. To hand them an older one would be to suggest the one they have is insufficient. It is not insufficient. It is only small, and slow, and unwitnessed, which are not the same as insufficient.

Ending the place cleanly assumes that the place has finished. The power listens to the pistons and is not convinced. Something is still happening here. It is happening at the rate of rust, but rate is not the measure of whether a thing is alive.

Preservation forever assumes that time is the enemy. But the Tender's whole condition is an accommodation with time — with the long stroke, with the slow advance of corrosion, with the eventual bed near the central stair. To remove time from the place would be to remove the medium in which the Tender exists. It would be a kind of killing dressed as a kindness.

So the power does none of these.

What it does instead is smaller, and is the kind of thing only a wise power would think to do.

It witnesses. Fully. With the entirety of its attention, for what would be, in human terms, a very long moment — though the power's moments do not map cleanly to human ones. It looks at the Tender and at the place and at the relationship between them, and it sees them, the way a thing is seen when it is seen by something that loves it without needing it to be different.

This witnessing is not nothing. In the cosmology the power inhabits, to be seen with full attention by a benevolent intelligence is a real event, even if the one seen does not consciously register it. Something in the Tender — some layer beneath the layer that has forgotten Tuesdays — receives the look. It is the way certain plants turn, very slowly, toward a light they cannot name.

Then the power does three small things, each calibrated to disturb nothing.

*First:* It places, in the soil of the bed nearest the central stair, a seed. Not a magical seed. A seed of something that already grows in the terraces — one of the orchids, perhaps, the kind that nests in the axils of tree-ferns. But this seed carries, encoded in the slow chemistry of its germination, a small redundancy: it will, if the Tender is the last Tender, sprout in a configuration that subtly retrains a passing observer in the precise quarter-turn and the listening pause. Not a successor. A possibility of one. A door that does not insist on being walked through. If no one ever comes, the orchid will simply be an orchid, and will be pruned, and pruned, and pruned. The power does not require its gift to be used. It only requires that it be available.

*Second:* It adjusts, by an amount too small to measure, the temperature of the unplanted pane on the uppermost terrace. Not warmer. Not colder. It tunes it, so that when the Tender presses their forehead against it next, the cold that comes through carries — for an instant the Tender will not consciously notice — a quality of being *received*. Not welcomed back. Not called. Just: the outside knows you are there. The outside has always known. You are not pressing your head against an indifferent surface. You are pressing it against a surface that, in some sense the power understands and the Tender does not need to, presses back.

*Third:* It does something to the flywheels. Or rather, it does nothing to the flywheels, but it places near them — in a register adjacent to physical space, in the way a meaning is placed near a word — an answer to the question of what they are for. The answer is not delivered. It is simply *near*. If, on some far evening, the Tender or whoever follows the Tender, or whoever follows the habit of tending after the Tender is gone, ever finds themselves standing in front of the flywheels and asking, the answer will be findable. Until then it waits. It is patient. It is the kind of answer that does not mind waiting, because it was, itself, arrived at slowly.

That is all.

The power does not announce itself. It does not require thanks. It does not return to check. Checking would imply that the gift was conditional on a response, and the gift is not conditional on anything. The power has learned, across the long span of its benevolence, that the deepest kindness one can offer a settled thing is to leave it settled, while adding — quietly, at the edges — a few small mercies that the thing can use or not use, as it likes, in its own time.

A drop falls from somewhere far above into a pool somewhere far below. A piston completes a stroke. The Tender, on the fourth terrace, peels a fruit and chews for a long time, looking at nothing. In the bed nearest the central stair, a seed lies in warm soil and has not yet decided whether to wake.

The power, satisfied, turns its attention elsewhere, carrying with it — because even wise powers are allowed this — a small, unhurried fondness for the place. The kind of fondness one feels for a held note. For a thing that knows how to be what it is.

Above the cloud line, below the snow, the glass sweats. The fans turn at the pace of contemplation. Nothing else happens, for a while.

Which is, after all, what the place is for.
