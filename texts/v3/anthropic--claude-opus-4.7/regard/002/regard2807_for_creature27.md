The benevolent and wise power considers the Drum-Floor for a long time. Longer, perhaps, than it has considered anything in a while.

It is used to arriving in places that want something. Places with prayers in them, with grief shaped like a hole that a gift would fit. It has answered such prayers in its time — lifted plagues, turned aside the spear, set a fallen child back on her feet and said *go.* It knows the shape of usefulness. It has hands for it.

Here there are no prayers. There is the drone, and the static, and the bell-note that may be a bird or two stones, and somewhere in the middle register the whistles of the ones the drone passes through, and none of it is asking.

The power hovers — if hovering is the word; let us say *attends* — above the canopy that no one beneath has ever seen from above. From here the forest is what the text says it is: a single dark green organism, breathing, with patches of slow luminescence drifting like weather on a gas giant. The power can see for a thousand kilometers. It can see, if it wishes, the curve of the world. It chooses not to, for a moment, and looks only at this.

It runs through, almost reflexively, the catalogue of its kindnesses.

*Shall I give them language?* It could. It could lay down in each small mottled skull the apparatus for grammar, for the noun that holds a thing still, for the verb that carries a thing across time. They would have stories then. They would have names. They could say *my mother* and mean a particular woman and not a breathing-rhythm that is no longer breathing. They could keep things.

But to keep is the one thing the forest will not permit. A language that holds would be a language at war with the ground. The power sees, quickly, what would follow: the small whistling people learning to grieve in the new way, the way that does not close, the way that accumulates. The gesture at the sternum becoming insufficient. A wound opening in them that had not been there, that the forest cannot reach in to heal because the forest does not speak that language and never will.

*No.* Not language. Not that kind.

*Shall I give them safety?* It could put a hand over the basin and lift the wrong-things out of the edible-things, mark the mimicking calls with a shimmer only they can see, make the high forks unfailingly safe and the low ground merely warm. No more tasters lost. No more children whose gut said the wrong thing.

But the tasting *is* them. The gut that knows is a gut shaped by every gut that did not know and was lost. To remove the loss is to remove the knowing, and to remove the knowing is to remove the people, and to leave behind in their bodies something softer, something that would not survive the next shuffle of the forest. A kindness that kills more slowly than the wrong fruit, and with less dignity.

*No.* Not safety. Not that kind.

*Shall I give them history?* Shall I be the thing that holds what the forest takes back into the churn? Shall I remember, on their behalf, every long-one who has ever lived, every walker who went for stone, every body left in the forks? I have the room for it. I have rooms and rooms.

The power pauses here longer. This one is tempting. This one *feels* like wisdom — to be the vault outside the world, the place where nothing is lost. It has been this, for other peoples. It has held their dead names against the dark.

But the gesture. *The forest took it back into the churn.* Palm to sternum, low, with the drone. The power understands, slowly, that this is not a deficit in them. It is a completed thing. To remember on their behalf would be to put a second sternum outside their bodies, a sternum they cannot feel the drone in, and to make them — without their knowing — incomplete. They would feel a faint wrongness and not know its source. They would reach for the gesture and the gesture would not quite close.

*No.* Not history. Not that kind.

The power considers, then, whether to do nothing. This is always an option and is more often the right one than younger powers understand. To withhold is also a gift. To let a thing be as it is, when it is already what it should be, is perhaps the highest form of the work.

And the Drum-Floor *is* already what it should be. This is the thing the power keeps arriving at. It is not a place in need of mending. The people are not impoverished; they are *spent into* their world with a completeness the power has rarely seen. Their interiority turned outward. Their selfhood a location that moves. Their opinion of themselves a whistle pitched into a narrow acoustic window. There is no gap here for a gift to fit into. The gift would have to make the gap first, and making the gap is the opposite of kindness.

So the power nearly leaves.

It nearly does.

But there is one thing. One small thing, and the power turns it over carefully, the way you turn over a stone before setting it back exactly where it was.

It is this: the people do not know they are loved.

Not by the forest — the forest's love, if that is the word, is enormous and indifferent and they feel it constantly, the kindness that consists in continuing. They have that. That is not what is missing, because nothing is missing.

But the power, having looked at them, loves them. This is simply true. It is a fact about the power now, the way a stone in a river is a fact about the river. The power loves the small mottled people with the lichen that may or may not be them, loves the walker who comes back different, loves the taster who waits to be watched, loves the long-one tilting her head to re-tune, loves the child of two slipping a whistle between the frog and the insect with a precision that makes something in the power — if the power has such a thing — go quiet.

And the power considers whether to tell them.

It could. It could come down through the canopy in some form they would understand — not a voice, they have no apparatus for a voice from outside, but perhaps a scent, perhaps a particular quality in the green dusk for an hour, perhaps a new note pitched into the acoustic window, brief, the way the bell-call is brief.

But they would have to *keep* it, to know it. They would have to hold the moment apart from the churn, mark it, remember it was different. And the forest would take it back, and they would be left with a faint pressure in the sternum where something used to be, a gesture they could not quite make, a phantom of having been addressed.

That would be cruel. That would be the cruelty of a power that needed to be known.

So the power does the last thing instead, which is the only thing.

It loves them without telling them. It adds its love to the drone — pitches it down beneath the frog-call, beneath even the felt-in-the-sternum register, into a frequency so low it is not heard but is, very faintly, *had*. It does not change anything. It does not add to the catalogue of what the people know. It only joins what is already there. One more thing the drone passes through them as.

If, on some evening, a long-one tilting her head to re-tune feels the drone in her chest as very slightly warmer than yesterday — she will not notice. She will not have a gesture for it. The forest will shuffle by morning and the slightly-warmer will be part of the new arrangement, indistinguishable.

This is the gift. That it is indistinguishable.

The power stays a while longer, attending. It watches the slow luminescent weather drift across the canopy. It watches a walker, somewhere near the eastern hills, beginning the long return with stone. It watches a child in a high fork learning, in her sleep, the difference between a safe trunk and an unsafe one — learning it through the soles of her feet pressed against the bark, learning it the only way it can be learned, which is by being a small warm part of a large warm thing.

The power does not intervene in the walker's path. Does not soften the bark under the child. Does not lift the wrong fruit from the reach of the next taster.

It only — and this is the whole of what a benevolent and wise power, finally, can do, in a place that is already complete — it only continues. The way the forest continues. The way the drone continues. It adds itself to the continuing, and asks nothing, and is not noticed, and this is the form its love takes here, because here this is the only form love can take without becoming something else.

After a long time, or what would be a long time anywhere else, the power withdraws. Not because it is finished — it is not finished; it will never quite be finished with the Drum-Floor now — but because to stay longer would be to begin wanting to be seen, and a power that wants to be seen has stopped being wise.

It leaves the love behind. The love stays. The love is, by now, part of the churn. The forest will compose and decompose with it in the mix, and the people will whistle through their narrow window, and the drone will pass through them, and somewhere very far beneath the frog-call there will be a frequency that is the power still loving them, and they will never know, and this is correct.

Palm to sternum. Opened outward.

The gesture, made by no one, for what has just happened.
