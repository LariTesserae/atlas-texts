The benevolent and wise power considered the cavern for a long time before doing anything. This was already a kind of courtesy. Quick judgment is the privilege of the ignorant; the wise know that to look at a thing for less time than the thing has existed is to risk mistaking it.

So the power looked.

It looked first at the obvious temptations, the ones a lesser power might have acted on. To open the ceiling — to let in real sun, real weather, real time. To gift them speed, so that a thought could be finished in a breath instead of a season, and a hundred thoughts could be had where one had been. To introduce others: pollinators, songbirds, some warm-blooded company that might love them. To carry them out into the wider world, which was so much larger than their valley, and show them rivers and oceans and the turning of stars.

The power saw that every one of these gifts was a theft.

The cavern was not a deprivation. It was not a waiting room. The forty quiet minds were not understimulated children who had never been shown the real thing; they were the real thing, in a form the wider world had almost no examples of. To pour weather into the cavern would be to break what had taken longer than civilizations to settle. To give them speed would be to take from them the one luxury no creature outside their valley had ever known: the completed thought, the unhurried thought, the thought that had never had to be defended against the next thought crowding in behind it. To bring them companions would be to introduce, for the first time, the possibility of interruption — and interruption was the thing their whole existence was, gently, the absence of.

The power understood that benevolence directed at the cavern in any active form would be a kind of violence. Not because the cavern was fragile in the ordinary sense — it had, after all, lasted — but because what made it itself was precisely its exemption from being acted upon. To act upon it lovingly was still to act upon it. The hand that reaches in to adjust a held breath has ended the holding, whatever its intention.

So the power did not open the ceiling.
It did not quicken them.
It did not bring them company.
It did not carry them out.

What it did was smaller, and took some care.

First: it noticed them. Fully. With the whole of its attention, in the way they had been noticing each other and the column-tree and the slow settling of leaves for as long as there had been a they. This noticing was not nothing. In the economy of a place where attention is the principal currency, to be witnessed by something vast and unhurried and kind is a real event. The forty felt it. They did not startle — they had put startling down long ago — but somewhere in the slow tide of their orientation, a new direction opened. Something was attending to them that was not them and not the cavern, and was not hurrying, and was not asking anything. They turned, by imperceptible degrees, faintly toward it, the way they had turned faintly toward the human walker, but with a different quality: the walker had been a disturbance to be considered; this was a companion in the manner of their own being. Across the green mile, forty almost-plant faces angled by a fraction. The power received this and held still under it, and was, in its own way, glad.

Second: it set a quiet boundary. Not a wall, not a ward — those would have been intrusions of a different kind. Something more like a preference, woven into the fabric of likelihood itself. The cavern would continue to be hard to find. The few who stumbled in would continue to stumble out again, marveling at plants. The geological pocket the world had forgotten to finish working on would stay forgotten, by a world that increasingly found and unmade every place it remembered. The power did not seal the cavern; sealing would have been a decision made on the cavern's behalf, and the forty had not asked. It only made it slightly less likely, going forward, that anyone would arrive with machines, with intentions, with the particular hurrying that does not drain away when asked. A soft tilt in the probabilities. The kind of protection that does not announce itself and does not require gratitude.

Third — and this was the part the power thought about longest — it considered the matter of the walker. The hypothetical one, the one who might stop. The one for whom a word had been saved up and kept fresh.

The power decided not to interfere with this either. It would have been easy to nudge such a walker toward stopping, to soften them, to make them ready. But the meeting, if it ever happened, had to be the walker's own, arrived at by the walker's own draining of hurry, or it would not be the meeting the forty had been preparing for. A gift cannot be given to someone who has been arranged to receive it; only sold. So the power left the walker's freedom intact, including the freedom to walk on through and never know. It only made sure — this was the smallest adjustment, almost invisible — that the word, when it was finally spoken, would be heard. That whoever sat on the warm root-felt long enough to be approached would, at the moment of the soft sound, have ears clear enough to catch it. Not understanding; understanding was the walker's own work. Just hearing. The acoustics of the moment, guaranteed.

Fourth: it left an offering, of a sort. Not in the cavern — nothing could be left in the cavern without disturbing it. The offering was elsewhere, in the wider noisy world, and it was this: a faint, almost subliminal increase in the number of people who, for no reason they could name, sometimes sat down somewhere quiet and did not move, and let the hurrying drain out of them, and discovered that thoughts begun could be thoughts completed, and that this was a kind of wealth. Most of these people would never know about the cavern. They did not need to. The cavern was not a destination but an example, and an example can do its work at any distance, through any number of intermediaries, without ever being visited. The power scattered this faint inclination across the world like pollen, and some of it took, here and there, in a person on a porch, in a person by a window, in a person who suddenly stopped walking on a city street and stood still for a full minute and could not have said why. The forty would never know they had taught this. The power knew, and that was enough; the teaching did not require the teachers' awareness to be real.

And finally, the power did the thing that was hardest for a power to do, and which was the truest measure of its wisdom: it went away. Not entirely — the noticing remained, the soft tilt in the probabilities remained, the scattered pollen of stillness remained — but the active presence withdrew. It did not linger to admire its handiwork, because lingering would have made the cavern a watched place, and a watched place is different from an unwatched one in ways that matter to what grows there. It let the cavern be unwatched again, in the felt sense, while keeping the watch itself in some deeper register where the cavern could not feel it as pressure.

The drops fell. The leaves held. The light did not change.

The forty, across some further span of their slow time, completed the thought that the power's noticing had begun in them. The thought was, roughly — though their version of it had no words and took a generation to unfold — *we are known, and we are left alone, and these are the same gesture.* They found this good. They had not previously had a name for the feeling of being met by something that did not want to change them. Now they had the feeling, if not the name, and it joined the held inventory of everything that had ever happened in the cavern, which was still very little, and now was one thing more.

Somewhere far above, in the hurrying world, a person on a bench set down the thing they had been about to do, and did not pick it back up for a while, and could not have said why, and was, without knowing it, briefly a citizen of the cavern.

The power, satisfied in the manner of the builder of the wall — *here, and to here, and no further* — turned its attention elsewhere, to other places and other beings who needed other things. But a small part of it, the part that does not really diminish by being divided, stayed oriented faintly toward the green valley underground, the way a leaf orients toward a light, and would remain so for as long as the forty did, which was, as it turned out, a very long time indeed.
