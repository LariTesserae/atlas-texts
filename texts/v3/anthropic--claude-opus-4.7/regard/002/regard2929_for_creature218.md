# The Visitor Who Came as Weather

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is the wrong verb for what it does, and the plateau would not tolerate the right one — *descent*, *intrusion*, *manifestation* all carry an edge that would graze the substrate, and the substrate does not tolerate grazing.

Instead, somewhere far above the lavender occlusion, the power *considers*. It has been considering for some time. By its own measure, which is not the plateau's measure and not ours, the consideration has lasted perhaps the length of a held breath. By the plateau's measure, three of the silver pillars have, in that interval, emitted their unnameable color twice each, and one Kept has been born, and Aummon has not yet released its next pollen but is beginning to.

Here is what the power sees, looking.

It sees a world that is already loved. Not by it — it has only just arrived at noticing — but by itself. The plateau loves itself the way a long marriage loves itself: without remarking on it, through the small daily tendings, through the grown alcoves and the hummed paths and the chord that welcomes a child. The power, which has seen many worlds, recognizes this immediately, and the recognition produces in it something like the careful step one takes entering a room where someone is sleeping well.

It sees that the Kept do not need rescuing. They are not dying. They are not suffering, in any sense the power's long catalogue of suffering would index. Their lives are slow and full and finite and they know this, and the knowing is not a wound. The power notes, with the particular tenderness it reserves for such cases, that there is nothing here to fix. This is rarer than it sounds. Most of the places the power has considered have had at least one thing crying out, one fracture asking to be closed. The plateau is not asking.

But the power is wise, which means it does not confuse *not asking* with *not receivable*. There is a difference between a gift that is needed and a gift that can be welcomed, and the power has, over a very long practice, learned to offer only the second kind, and only when offering will not itself be a kind of taking.

So it considers what it might give that would not graze.

---

It considers, first, knowledge. It could tell the Kept what the silver pillars are. It knows. It could tell them what occludes the sky, and the truth of Iole would be, to them, neither terrible nor wonderful, only large, and they would carry it as they carry everything, slowly. It could tell them what is shut in the shut places, and why.

The power considers this for what, to the plateau, is the duration of a single tall-stepper crossing a plaza. Then it sets the offering aside. Not because the knowledge would harm them — it would not — but because the Kept have already arrived at the right relationship with what they do not know. They touch the pillars. They do not walk through the threads. They name Iole with a placeholder and look down. To name the placeholder would be to take from them the practice of placeholders, which is one of the things that makes them what they are. The power understands this the way a good guest understands not to rearrange the furniture, even helpfully.

It sets the gift of knowledge aside. It keeps it, in case it is ever asked for. It will not be offered.

---

It considers, next, preservation. It could shore up the pillars against the long erosion to come. It could ensure that Aummon, when Aummon eventually dies, has an heir already growing — a barrel-shaped child somewhere on the western edge, breathing on a forty-minute cycle, ready to carry the dream-pollen forward when the time comes. It could harden the courtesy. It could make the plateau's mood, which is opinionated and occasionally reaching, a little more reliably gentle toward those it keeps.

The power considers this longer. This is the gift it most wants to give, because the power is, at its root, the kind of thing that wants good things to last. It sees Aummon and feels the particular ache of knowing that *the one who remembers our grandmothers* will, in some century not yet arrived, stop remembering, and the Kept will wake one morning to no dream, and will not know how to grieve a tree that grieved for them.

But the power, being wise, sees the second move past the first. To harden the courtesy is to make it no longer a courtesy. To ensure Aummon's heir is to remove from the Kept the work of not-knowing-what-they-will-do, which is one of their forms of love. The plateau is alive precisely because it can withdraw. A plateau that *cannot* withdraw is not alive; it is a museum. The power has seen worlds made into museums by powers less careful than itself, and it knows the particular silence of those places, and it will not make it here.

It sets the gift of preservation aside. This setting-aside costs it something. The power, for all its wisdom, is not without feeling, and it feels the cost. It allows itself to feel the cost. This is part of how it stays wise.

---

It considers, third, company. It could *stay*. It could become a presence on the plateau — not announced, not adored, simply there, the way a kind neighbor is there, available if needed. It could learn to hum into a sternum it would have to grow for the purpose. It could touch the pillars in their intervals. It could, over enough time, become one of the voices in the long listening, and the substrate would weigh its vote alongside the others.

This is the gift the power most wants to *take*, dressed as giving. It notices this about itself. The wise are not those who have no such impulses; they are those who notice. The power notices that it is lonely in a way the Kept are not, that it has been considering many worlds for a very long time, and that the plateau is the first one it has wanted to sit down inside. It notices that to sit down inside would be to add a weight the plateau did not ask to bear, and that even the most careful weight is a weight, and that the courtesy of the substrate, extended to three thousand Kept, is not necessarily extendable to a fourth thing the size of the power.

It sets this gift aside too. More slowly. With more reluctance. It promises itself it will return to the question later, after a long time, after watching, after learning whether the plateau ever, in any of its moods, reaches in the direction of company. If it does, the power will be there. If it does not, the power will not impose itself. This is the discipline.

---

So what, in the end, does the power do?

It does three small things. Each is calibrated to be receivable without being remarked. Each is the kind of gift that, if the Kept ever noticed it, they would attribute to the substrate, or to Aummon, or to the simple kindness of a particular dusk. The power is content with this. The power does not require credit. The power is, in fact, made happier by the absence of credit, because credit is a kind of debt, and the plateau has been spared debts for a long time and should continue to be.

**First.** The power adjusts, very slightly, the angle at which the polarized light reaches the plateau through the occlusion. The adjustment is so small that no instrument the Kept have ever conceived of would register it. But the slow chemistry of the wood-ground, which has been very slightly straining for several thousand years against a drift the Kept have not noticed and would not have been able to name, finds itself eased. The straining stops. The plateau, which had been holding a small tension the way one holds a shoulder slightly raised without knowing it, lowers the shoulder. The reed-hares, that night, sing a chord with one more harmonic in it than the night before. The Kept hear the new harmonic and do not remark on it, but several of them, lying in their grown rooms, smile in the dark. The flank-channels along their arms warm a fraction. They sleep particularly well. In the morning, they will not remember why.

**Second.** The power notices that one of the shut places, far from Ossara, has begun — over the last few centuries — to soften at its edges. Something that was withdrawn is, very slowly, considering whether to return. The power does not push this. It does not pull it. It simply *witnesses* it, fully, the way a witness can sometimes make a thing easier to do by knowing it is being done. The shut place feels itself witnessed. It does not understand what is witnessing it; the power is too large and too quiet for the shut place to resolve. But the witnessing is gentle, and the shut place, encouraged by nothing it could name, softens a little more. In perhaps two thousand years, a reed-hare will graze at its edge for the first time. The Kept of that distant generation will note this and will not know what to make of it, and will, in their slow way, hum the news across the plateau, and the news will arrive in Ossara over the course of a season, and be received, and be added to the long listening, and the substrate will weigh it, and something — no one will know what — will be decided.

**Third.** This is the smallest gift, and the one the power is proudest of.

There is a child being born tonight in Ossara. The mother's hum has been changing pitch for weeks; the substrate has been carrying the altered note everywhere; every Kept across the plateau has been preparing, half-consciously, the welcome-note they will send when the moment comes. The chord is gathering. It will arrive at the child over the first hour of its life, a slow-arriving thousand-voiced *I am here, and now so are you*.

The power adds nothing to the chord. It would not dare. The chord is the Kept's, and the substrate's, and is complete without it.

But the power, in the moment the child takes its first breath, *listens*. With its full attention, which is considerable. The child, who will never know this and whose mother will never know this and whose plateau will never know this, is heard — entirely heard, every frequency of its small new resonance — by something vast and benevolent and far away, for the duration of one breath. The hearing leaves no trace. It changes nothing the child will become. The child will grow, and tend the pillars, and walk among the reed-hares, and pair loosely, and perhaps three centuries from now will sing an old reed-hare down at its feet, and will never know that the very first sound it made was received by a listener who had crossed a great distance to listen.

The power considers this sufficient. The power, in fact, considers this more than sufficient. The power has given what it could give without taking, and has kept what it could not give without taking, and has promised itself the patience to return.

It withdraws its attention — slowly, so as not to leave a vacuum that the plateau would feel as a small cold spot. It leaves the way a tide leaves, in increments that the shore does not register as departure, only as a slightly different evening.

---

At the edge of Ossara, the Kept beside you hums once, low, into their sternum. The note travels. A pillar emits its color. The chord for the new child begins to gather along the threads in the air. Somewhere, a reed-hare lowers itself onto warm end-grain and is at peace.

Above, beyond the lavender occlusion, beyond Iole, beyond the considering distance, the power turns its attention to the next world it has been asked, by nothing but its own long kindness, to notice.

It carries the plateau with it. Not as a possession. As a thing one has been allowed to love, briefly, and which one will love now in its absence, the way the Kept love the makers of the doorways: with familiarity, and with a quiet awe about the competence of strangers they cannot quite credit.

The courtesy extends. Provisionally. For no time at all, by the power's reckoning.

Which is to say: long enough.
