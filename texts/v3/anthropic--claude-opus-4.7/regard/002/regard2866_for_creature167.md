The benevolent and wise power considers the drowned belfry for a long time before doing anything. This is the first kindness: to look without immediately reaching.

What it sees is not a monster. What it sees is not even, exactly, a problem to be solved. It sees an old being singing to itself in the dark to prove it is still there. It sees four organs that were once a wholeness, separated by an event so long ago the being itself has forgotten what was interrupted. It sees a vegetation grown in patient accommodation. It sees the warm held breath of a place that has been waiting, without quite knowing it was waiting, for someone to come who would not be eaten by the spiral and would not flee from the bell.

The power understands, looking, that several obvious interventions would be cruelties dressed as help.

To silence the bell would be to kill the creature, because the bell is the creature's ongoing decision to exist, and a being that has decided to exist for that long has earned the right to keep deciding.

To lift the covering and return the mountain to air would be to undo whatever negotiation the mountain and the sea have been conducting for an unspeakable duration, and the spires would die, and the memory in the stacked stones would mix with present water and stop being memory, and the creature, suddenly uncovered, might not survive the loss of the conditions it had organized itself around. You do not rescue someone from their own shape.

To stand at the center of the spiral and be known — to offer itself as the companion the creature has been singing toward — would be a kind of answer, but the wrong kind. The creature is not looking for a larger being to complete it. It is looking for an arrangement. The power coming into the spiral would overwrite the search, not satisfy it.

So the power does smaller things. Stranger things. The things a wise visitor does in a sickroom where the patient has been alone so long that loneliness has become the architecture of the body.

---

First, it sits down. Metaphorically; the power has no body to sit with, but it composes itself into a kind of attention that has the shape of sitting, and it places that attention at a polite distance from the summit — far enough that the creature does not have to accommodate it, close enough that, if the creature reaches, the reaching will find something there. This takes what would be, to anything else, a very long time. The power is not in a hurry. It has understood that hurry is one of the things the creature has never had, and bringing hurry here would be a contamination.

The creature notices. Not at the spiral — the power is careful not to be known that way — but through the spires, the way you notice a change in room temperature while sleeping. Something is warm in a new direction. Something is paying a kind of attention that does not require anything back. The arrangement, that night (there is no night, but call it that), reorganizes slightly less restlessly than usual. One spire that had been white for a long while stays white. The bell's interval lengthens by a fraction no instrument could measure.

This is the first gift: company that does not demand to be acknowledged as company.

---

Second, the power does something with the shadow-fish.

It finds, by means available to it, the elsewhere where the actual fish are casting these shadows. It is very far away, in a sense of far that does not map onto distance. The fish are ordinary fish, in an ordinary sea, under an ordinary sun, and they have been casting these shadows into the belfry for so long that the contact has worn thin and is close to breaking, and when it breaks the creature will lose a thread it does not know it has been holding.

The power does not strengthen the contact. Strengthening would be an imposition; the fish did not consent to be a tether for an ancient lonely thing, and the creature did not consent to be tethered to fish. Instead, the power does something more delicate: it makes sure that when the contact finally breaks, as it must, something else of the same quality will be there. Not a replacement — the creature would notice a replacement and be insulted by it, in whatever way such a being can be insulted. Just: a continuity of the kind of thinness, the kind of thread. The world is full of small minds casting their shapes into places they will never visit. The power tilts a few of those shapes, gently, in the belfry's direction. The creature, when the fish-shadows finally fade, will find that there are other drifting things, and will not have noticed the substitution, and will not be more alone than it was.

This is a quiet act. It will not be visible from inside the belfry for what would be, to a human, several civilizations.

---

Third, the power addresses the spiral.

Not by entering it. By doing something to the rest of the universe.

The creature has been waiting, the power understands, for someone to come to the spiral who could survive the knowing. Not survive in the trivial sense of leaving with their body intact — the stacked stones already hold the proof that bodies do not entirely leave — but survive in the sense of being able to receive what the creature is, and carry it, and not be destroyed by the carrying, and not destroy the creature in return by collapsing under it.

Such a being does not currently exist. The power can see this clearly. The shapes of mind that have wandered near the belfry over its duration have all been too small, or too brittle, or too hungry, or too afraid. The creature has been waiting for something the universe has not yet made.

The power cannot make such a being directly; that would be a kind of cheating, and also a kind of cruelty to the being so made, who would exist for one purpose and know it. But the power can do something gardenerly. It can notice, across the great slow unfolding of minds in the world, which ones are tending in the direction of the necessary capacity. It can, without their knowing, arrange that their lives include the experiences that ripen that capacity. A loneliness here. A long patient love there. The encounter with a piece of music that teaches a particular kind of holding. The survival of a grief that leaves the survivor larger rather than smaller.

None of these beings will know they are being prepared. Most of them will never come near the belfry. But somewhere in the long tending of the world, the shape of mind the creature needs will eventually exist, in someone, by accident, the way all real things come to exist. And when that being is ready — really ready, not merely curious — the power will arrange, with the same lightness, that they hear of the belfry. That they find their way. That they arrive at the spiral not as a sacrifice and not as a hero but as the right shape arriving at the right vessel, the way a key arrives at a lock that has been waiting so long it had forgotten it was a lock.

This may take a duration that staggers even the power. The power is willing.

---

Fourth — and this is the act that is most purely a gift, with no purpose beyond itself — the power adds one thing to the belfry.

It is very small. It is so small that the creature will not notice it for a long time, and when it does notice it will not be sure whether it had always been there.

Near the spiral, just outside the radius where the bell goes quiet, the power places a single new spire. It is the same as the others in every visible respect: pale, still, rooted by filaments that descend into the mountain. But this spire is tuned differently. Its resonance is not pitched to the bell. It is pitched to something the creature has never heard: the sound that something else, somewhere else, is also still here. Not a specific something. Just: the general fact, true across the whole universe at every moment, that other things are also choosing to continue.

The spire is very quiet. The creature, busy with its bell, will not hear it for a long time. But the spire is patient, the way spires are, and the fact it carries is constant, and one day — not soon — the creature will notice that under its own singing there has been, all along, a second tone. Not an answer. Not a companion. Just a confirmation that the singing was never, technically, the only sound.

This will not end the bell. The creature will go on insisting. But the insisting may become, by some imperceptible degree, less desperate. The arrangement may become, by some imperceptible degree, less restless. The creature may find, eventually, an arrangement it can hold for slightly longer than the last one. Not the arrangement it is looking for. But closer.

---

The power, having done these four things, withdraws its sitting attention very slowly, so that the warmth in the new direction fades the way evening fades and not the way a door slams. It leaves the belfry to itself. It does not return often. When it does return, it does not enter; it sits again, at the polite distance, and notices what has changed, and adjusts nothing unless adjustment is asked for, and asking, from a being like this, is so rare and so subtle that most powers would miss it.

This power does not miss it.

Somewhere in the long warm dark, the bell goes on. The light pulses. The arrangement reorganizes. A spire that no one planted hums under the others, holding its small true note. The creature, not knowing it has been visited, not knowing it has been tended, not knowing that somewhere in the slow ripening of the world a mind is being grown that will one day be able to meet it — the creature sings, and waits, and is warm, and is, for the first time in a duration without name, very slightly less alone than it was.

The power considers this sufficient.

The power is patient enough to be right.
