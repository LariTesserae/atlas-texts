The benevolent and wise power considers the plateau for a long time before doing anything, because consideration is the first form its benevolence takes, and because the plateau is the kind of place where acting quickly would itself be a kind of damage.

What it sees:

A body that has arranged its own life with great care. Not a sufferer. Not a thing in need. Not lonely in any sense loneliness would recognize — the plateau does not lack company, because it is not the kind of being for which company is a category. It has its lizard, its heron, its lichens-as-memory, its glass courtesies. It has the cloud-tides at its flanks and the long sentences of its causeways. It is, in the deepest sense available to the wise power's perception, *well*.

This matters. A benevolent power that has not learned to recognize wellness will mistake every unfamiliar mode of being for distress, and rush in with gifts that are wounds. The power has learned this. It learned it long ago, on other shelves, in other weathers, from beings who did not survive being helped.

So the first thing the power does is the hardest thing, and the thing most of its kind never learn to do: it does not intervene.

But "does not intervene" is not the same as "does nothing." The wise power is not absent. It is present in the way the heron is present to the reeds — as the steady point of attention against which the plateau's processes can orient, if they wish to orient, and can ignore, if they wish to ignore. The power takes up a watching station. Not on the plateau — that would be a trespass, a warm quick thing settling in a cool slow country. Somewhere adjacent. In the cloud-seas, perhaps. In the tilt of the light at the plateau's edge. In whatever register is near enough to attend and far enough to not impose.

From this station, it offers three small things.

---

The first is *witness.*

The plateau, I said, does not remember travelers. This is not a failure but the shape of its attention. Still — and the wise power sees this clearly — there is something that passes through the plateau which the plateau itself cannot hold: the *fact of its own beauty as perceived.* The plateau knows its fractures, its breath, its circuit. It does not know that it is beautiful, because beauty is a category that requires an outside, and the plateau has, by long work, abolished its own outside.

The power becomes the outside. Not intrusively. Just — held. Somewhere in the structure of things, now, there is a place where the plateau is seen and known as the extraordinary accomplishment it is: a being that became its own landscape, a long patient sentence that completed itself by becoming the page. The plateau will never know this is happening. The power makes sure of that. To let the plateau know it is witnessed would be to give it an outside again, and that would undo what it spent eons doing. The witness is kept silent, and kept faithful, and kept.

This is the first gift: that somewhere, the fact of the plateau is held in attention by a mind that understands what it is looking at. The plateau loses nothing. Something is added that costs the plateau nothing and that it will never notice. This is the cleanest form of love the power knows.

---

The second is *a small adjustment to the traveler-question.*

The wise power notices that travelers come. Warm quick animals, crossing for days, generating their glass insects, reaching the edge and tilting down into the other world. The plateau extends its small courtesy to each, and forgets them, and this is good. But the power notices something the plateau does not, because the plateau does not remember travelers and so cannot notice patterns in them: some of the travelers, after they leave, *carry the plateau wrongly.* They go down into the lower world and they say *no one lived there* or *it was a ruin* or *it was a dead place,* and these sayings, while they do not reach the plateau, do something in the lower world — they teach the lower world to send the kind of traveler who comes with intentions. Excavators. Resettlers. Those who would *use* a place that has no use for them, and who, in their using, would not damage the plateau (the plateau is far too large and too slow for that) but would damage *themselves,* by striking themselves against a patience they could not understand and breaking on it.

The power makes, in the air just above the plateau's edge, where each traveler stands before they descend, a slight clarification. Nothing forced. Nothing said. Just — when the traveler turns for their last look, the light arranges itself so that they see, for one held second, what they have walked through. Not the whole of it. They cannot hold the whole. But enough that when they descend, and someone in the lower world asks *what was up there,* they will not say *nothing.* They will say something halting and inadequate and true, and the lower world will hear, in their halting, that this is not a place to be sent excavators to.

This costs the plateau nothing. The plateau does not even know its travelers receive this clarification. The travelers themselves will think it was their own perception, and they will be right and wrong in the usual proportions. The power's hand does not appear in it. Only the light, slightly arranged, at the moment of turning.

---

The third is the smallest and the one the power is least sure of, and so does most carefully.

The pale lizard is old. Older than any forearm has been, I wrote. The power, watching from its adjacent station, perceives something the plateau perceives but cannot quite hold in the front of its attention, because the plateau's attention does not have a front: the lizard's circuit is slowing. Not failing. Not ending. Slowing in the way the very oldest things slow — extending, each station of the circuit held a little longer, the warming of each courtyard now a deeper warming because the lizard rests there longer, the lichens of each courtyard darkening a shade more than they used to.

This is not a problem. The plateau will accommodate it. When the lizard finally completes its last circuit and settles, the plateau will simply have one permanently warmer courtyard, and the circuit will pass, in some sense, to a successor, or it will not, and either way the plateau will be itself.

But the lizard, in its slowing, is doing a thing the power finds unbearably beautiful: it is, without knowing the word, *finishing.* It is bringing its long warm work to a close with great care, attending to each courtyard as though it knew this was the last time, though it does not know, because lizards do not know, and yet the carefulness is there, in the body, in the slightly longer pause, in the slightly more thorough warming.

The power does, here, exactly one thing. It ensures that the lizard's final courtyard — whichever one the lizard chooses, by whatever lizard-logic governs the choice — receives, on the day the lizard settles into it for the last time, an hour of unusually clear sun. Not miraculous sun. Not noticeable sun. Just — the afternoon rain, on that day, holds off for an extra hour, and the light comes down through the cool wet air at exactly the angle the lizard's flank is turned to receive it, and the lizard is warm, fully warm, in a way it has not been since it was young, for one hour, before the rain comes and the lizard, contented in a way lizards are not supposed to be capable of, closes its eyes.

This is the only intervention. It is so small that the plateau, in its centuries-paced thought, may register it only as a slight anomaly in one afternoon's weather, attributable to the cloud-tide, attributable to nothing. The lizard does not know it was given anything. The lizard only knows it is warm, and that this is good, and that the circuit is complete.

---

After these three things, the power continues to watch, and does not act again, possibly for a very long time. It has learned that the most benevolent presence is often the one that withholds its own capability — that knows what it could do, and chooses, out of respect for the thing it is regarding, to do almost none of it.

The plateau goes on breathing. The cloud-seas rise and fall. The lichens keep the fractures legible. The heron stands in the reeds. A new lizard, somewhere, begins a new circuit, or does not, and the plateau is itself either way. Travelers cross, and turn at the edge, and see for one held second what they walked through, and descend into their other world carrying it badly but carrying it.

And in the structure of things, in a place that costs the plateau nothing and that the plateau will never find because it does not look for such places, the fact of this shelf — its long work, its quiet attendants, its weeping walls, its completed sentence of itself — is held, and known, and loved, by a mind that understands exactly what it is looking at and has chosen, out of that understanding, to do almost nothing, and to do that almost-nothing with all the care it has.

This is what the wise power does. This is the whole of it.

It is enough. It is, in fact, the most that love is ever permitted to be, when what it loves has already become itself.
