## The Visitor

The benevolent power did not arrive. Arrival is a thing of edges, and there are no edges here that it had not, in some sense, always been touching. It simply became *noticeable*, the way a held breath becomes noticeable, the way one becomes aware that a room has been quiet for a long time.

It noticed the Cistern the way one notices a nest in a hedge while walking past: without stopping, at first. Then stopping. Then crouching down to look.

What it saw, it saw all at once, the way we see a painting — not engine by engine but the whole pulsing lit country, the schools turning under their chandeliers, the small white octopus clinging to its bolt-casing in love with the throb, the welder a league away touching her heat-colored seam with a gloved finger because she has come to say goodbye to the violet before it fades. It saw the seeders trailing their living ropes. It saw the ceiling, and what was kept against the ceiling, and what those kept things had been, and what they were becoming, and the long patient arc of the becoming.

It saw the Office. This took longer. The Office was diffuse, distributed across a structure of intentions that did not resolve into a single mind but into something more like a weather — a sustained pressure of purpose, vast and competent and not, in any sense the visitor recognized, *cruel*. The Office was building a world. It was doing so with the seriousness of a thing that knew worlds were difficult to build and that the early stages required certain accommodations. It had not lied to the engineers. It had simply not told them, because telling them would have changed what they were for.

The visitor considered this for what, in its frame, was a long time. In the frame of the Cistern, the pumps beat perhaps four times.

Here is what the visitor did not do.

It did not stop the engines. The engines were the heart of the place and the heart of the place was not, in itself, wrong. A heart that beats is not a wound.

It did not bring the cormorant-things down and restore them. They were not, in the visitor's sight, ruined. They were *altered*, and the alteration had been done to them without their full knowing, and this was a grief — the visitor registered it as a grief — but they were also, now, themselves. They hunted. They were fed. They had a sky, of a kind. To unmake them toward an earlier shape would be another unmaking, done again without their knowing, and the visitor was not in the business of correcting unmakings with further unmakings. That was the Office's logic. The visitor declined it.

It did not reveal the Office to the engineers. The engineers loved their iron. The love was real. The love did not require the full picture to be real; very little love does. To hand them the full picture as a gift would be to hand them a weight they had not asked to carry, in exchange for a clarity they had not asked to have. The visitor knew the difference between a gift and an imposition dressed as one.

It did not destroy the ceiling. It did not lift the rim away and show them the sky. There may not have been a sky to show. And even if there was, a sky revealed is not the same as a sky earned, and a being that has lived eleven years inside a lit factory-sea does not necessarily want, in the first instant, to be shown the stars. Some do. Some would shatter.

Here is what the visitor did.

**It marked the place.** Not with any sign the engineers could see. It set, in the structure of the water itself, a kind of *witness* — a quality the Cistern now had that it had not had before, which was that it was *known*. Known not by the Office, which knew it the way an architect knows a blueprint, but known the way a child is known by someone who loves the child and is not its parent and has no power over it and simply *sees* it, fully, and continues to see it. The Cistern, from that moment, was held in an attention that asked nothing of it. This changed nothing measurable. It changed everything else.

**It made one small adjustment, and only one.** The visitor is wise, and the wise are sparing. It looked for the smallest possible intervention that would honor what the place was already trying to be, and it found it in the cormorant-things.

The cormorant-things, when they dove, had not been able to remember being engineers. The keeping at the ceiling included a forgetting; this was part of the fitting. The visitor did not undo the forgetting — that would have broken them. But it placed, in each of them, a single recoverable thing: the memory of having loved the iron. Not the name of the iron, not the shape of a wrench, not a face. Just the love. A warmth toward the riveted dark that they would feel now when they dove past it, and would not understand, and would not need to understand. It meant that when they rocketed down through the lamp-columns to take a silver fish, they passed through their own old tenderness, and were, for that half-second, *continuous* with what they had been. Not restored. Continuous. There is a difference, and the visitor knew it.

This was the whole intervention. It took no force. It cost the visitor nothing it would miss. It would not be noticed by the Office, which did not measure such things, and would not be noticed by the engineers, who did not know to look. It would be noticed, dimly, by the cormorant-things themselves, who would find, without knowing why, that the dive had become slightly more bearable, slightly more like coming home and slightly less like falling.

**It left a door.** Not a literal door. A condition. The visitor arranged matters such that *if* an engineer, of her own movement, with no prompting, came to want something the Cistern could not give her — came to want, say, a sky, or an answer, or simply out — there would be, available to her, a way. Not advertised. Not easy. Not the only way, and not necessarily the best way for her. But a way. The visitor did not believe in trapping anyone inside a love, however genuine the love. Love that cannot be left is a different thing, and the visitor knew that name too.

The door would be found by those who looked. Most would not look. Most genuinely did not want to; their love was their love and it was sufficient, and the visitor honored this. But for the one who, on some long swim between engines, in the lamp-chain stretching out ahead like a necklace on black velvet, felt suddenly that the necklace was a necklace and that necklaces are worn around throats — for her, when she began to look, the looking would find something. This was the visitor's quiet promise to a being it had not met and might never meet.

**It blessed the small white octopus.** This was not strictly necessary. The visitor did it anyway. The octopus, clinging to its bolt-casing, vibrating in time with an engine it could not name, was, to the visitor's eye, one of the more complete creatures in any world it had ever looked into. The blessing was: a long life, by octopus standards, and undisturbed bolt-casings, and the continued affection of the engineers, and the particular pleasure, on certain nights when the great pumps cycled into a rare harmonic, of feeling the whole Cistern hum at exactly the frequency of its small body, so that for a few minutes it would be not a creature in a place but a place itself, briefly, gloriously, ringing.

The visitor smiled at this — if smiling is the word, and it is not, but it is closer than the other words — and then it did the last thing it did, which was to stay.

Not visibly. Not interferingly. It simply did not leave. The attention it had set in the water remained set. The Cistern, going forward, was a watched place — watched in the old sense, the sense of *kept watch over*, the sense in which a watchman is not a jailer.

The pumps beat. The welder, a league away, took her finger from the violet seam and went back to work. A cormorant-thing dove through the lamp-columns and felt, for a half-second, an unaccountable warmth toward the dark iron rushing past, and did not know why, and did not need to, and rose again with a silver fish toward a ceiling that had not changed and yet was, in some way it could not have named, no longer the only thing above it.

It is a young place. It has just begun.

It is also, now, loved — in the particular way that does not require the loved thing to know.
