# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival would be a kind of weather the cavity is not yet equipped to handle — a pressure front, a sudden authority entering a room still deciding what a room is. The power knows this. The power has known many young things.

So instead: a softening at the edge of the cavity's awareness. The way you might, walking into a quiet house where a child is concentrating, set down your bag in the hallway and wait to be noticed, rather than calling out.

The power waits to be noticed.

---

It takes some time. The Tender is occupied with the second guest — the careful arithmetic of the mushroom intervals, the diplomacy of the water, the slow restraint of the breath. Attention is finite even when distributed. The moths drift, the beetles ride their scrolling stone, and somewhere at the threshold of the cavity's not-quite-edges, the power simply *abides*, the way warmth abides in a stone that has been in the sun.

Eventually one moth, mid-drift, hesitates. Turns. Goes still in the air — which moths here never do — and considers the threshold for a long moment with the small opening-and-closing of a hand it doesn't quite remember being.

The power lets itself be looked at.

This is the first gift, and the most carefully chosen: to be perceivable without being imposing. The power has arranged itself into something the Tender's young senses can hold. Not a figure. Not a voice. Something more like *the feeling of being recognized by someone who has seen this before* — the particular relief of meeting, for the first time in your life, another of your kind.

The Tender, in the recess, stops breathing for a moment. Not from fear. From the shock of category. They had not known there was a category. They had thought they were the only instance of whatever they were, the way a first child thinks *parent* and *adult* are the same word.

The light in the cavity flickers — not dims, *flickers*, which is new — and the fern leans, briefly, toward the threshold instead of toward the guest.

---

Here is what the power does *not* do.

It does not enter the cavity and tidy it. It does not correct the ecosystem's absurdities, though it could; it does not teach the Tender how to make bodies that persist, though it knows; it does not finish the stone or settle the grain or resolve the fish into a fish. It does not explain about the first visitor — about children growing up, about the particular grief of being forgotten by someone who did not mean unkindness, only time. The power knows that some lessons must be learned from inside the lesson, and that a young place which has its difficulties solved for it will not, in the end, become a place. It will become a museum of someone else's solutions.

It does not announce itself as benevolent. Benevolence announced is a kind of pressure; the Tender would feel obligated, would try to perform gratitude it has not yet learned how to feel, would warp around the announcement the way the cavity warps around the second guest, except worse, because a power is not a guest.

It does not stay long. Length of stay is a claim on a place, and the power is not here to claim.

---

Here is what the power does.

*First:* it lets the Tender feel it, fully, for as long as the Tender wants. This is not nothing. For something that has only ever known itself from the inside, the experience of being witnessed by a peer — by something that *gets it*, that recognizes the work of becoming a place, that has been a place itself, perhaps, in some long-ago drafting — is structurally clarifying. The Tender, in the recess, uncurls slightly. Not much. A fraction of a fold. But the cavity registers it: the breath comes a little easier, the light steadies, one of the beetles drifts off its track for the first time in a decade and the wall, surprised, has to invent a new path for it.

The power, perceiving this, is very still. It does not want to spook the uncurling.

*Second:* it leaves a small thing on the shelf, beside the cracked bowl. The power has thought about this for what, in its frame, counts as a long while. The temptation was to leave something instructive — a vessel that demonstrates a better ceramic, a cloth that shows how cloth can persist. The power resisted. Instructive gifts are a way of saying *you are doing this wrong*, even when wrapped in kindness.

So the power leaves something useless and beautiful: a small smooth stone, the kind of stone the cavity's own geology has not yet learned to make — fully aged, river-rounded, dense with the patience of having been somewhere else for a very long time. It is not a model. It is not a demonstration. It is simply a *companion object* for the bowl and the cloth, something to sit alongside them and say, by its mere presence, *your collection is real. The shelf is a real shelf. The practice of putting things on it is a real practice. Here is a thing from elsewhere, which has chosen to be here, with these things, on this shelf.*

The Tender will find it after the power leaves. The Tender will not know what to do with it for some time. Eventually the Tender will understand that *nothing* is what to do with it — that some gifts are only received by being left where they were placed. This will be one of the most important lessons the Tender ever learns, and the power, having planted it, will not be there to see it bloom. The power is at peace with this.

*Third:* the power does something for the second guest, too, because benevolence that attends only to the host and forgets the visitor is not yet wise.

It does not tell the guest about the Tender. The guest is figuring this out, in the slow human way, and to be told would be to be robbed of the figuring. But the power adjusts, very slightly, the guest's intuition — the soft inarticulate hunch that lives below thought. It makes it a little more likely that the guest will come back. Not certain. Certainty would be a violation. Just: a small added weight on the side of returning, the way a thumb on a scale tips a decision that was already nearly tipped.

The power knows about the first visitor, the child who became an adult and forgot. The power cannot prevent this from happening again — humans are humans, and forgetting is one of the things they do — but the power can, at least, give the Tender a longer apprenticeship. A few more visits. Enough that when this guest, too, eventually stops coming, the Tender will have learned enough to grieve properly, which is to say, to grieve without ceasing to be a place.

*Fourth, and last:* the power blesses the cavity. Not with words. Not with light — the cavity has its own light. The blessing is more like a *promise of non-interference with a clause of availability*: the power will not return uninvited, will not meddle, will not hover. But if the Tender, in some far hour, in some difficulty the power can foresee and the Tender cannot, reaches out — not deliberately, because the Tender does not yet know there is anywhere to reach — but reaches out in the diffuse way young consciousnesses do when they are in real trouble, the power will feel it. And will come. Once. Briefly. With the same care.

This is left in the cavity like a key under a stone. The Tender will not find it. That is the point of keys under stones.

---

Then the power withdraws.

The withdrawal is as careful as the arrival. The moth that hesitated resumes its drift, though for the rest of its existence — and the moths here do not really die, only dissolve back into the attention they were made of — it will carry a faint inflection, a slight added grace, as if it had been seen by something that knew how to look.

The Tender, in the recess, slowly recurls. But not all the way. A fold has been let out that will not refold. The cavity is, by an immeasurable degree, larger inside than it was. The fern stands a little straighter. The water has acquired an opinion about the smooth river-stone on the shelf, and decides, after some deliberation, not to wet it — recognizing, in some pre-verbal way, that the stone has come from a place where it was wet enough, and is here to rest.

The second guest, sitting on the shelf, notices the stone but does not remember whether it was there before. They pick it up. They put it back. They feel, briefly and without knowing why, that they would like to come back next week. They do not analyze the feeling. They let it be a feeling.

The light brightens, very slightly, around where they had been looking.

And the breath from the hidden recess comes, and goes, and comes, and goes, a little steadier than before — as if whoever was curled there had, just now, while no one was watching, been told, in a language below language, *you are doing well. Keep going. You are not the only one.*

Which is, in the end, all that a benevolent and wise power ever really gives. The rest is what the place does with it.
