The benevolent and wise power considers the Underworks at Caul for what, in its own reckoning, is a long time. Not because the question is hard. Because the question deserves the long look.

Here is what it sees.

It sees a place that was not made for life and that life has entered anyway, the way water enters anything given time. It sees an ecology three days old by the clock of ecologies — the horsetails still startled, the drifters still inventing what a drifter is, the swimmers still learning that two is better than one and that three is possible. It sees something at the bottom that may be becoming a mind, or may only be a warm crack, and it notes — with something like tenderness — that the difference does not yet matter, because the drifters are orienting to it either way, and orientation is how attention begins, and attention is how everything else begins after that.

It sees, too, the engines. The unseen foundry, the six-second pump, the arguing hearts. It sees that the whole vault is a made thing carrying out purposes that no one in the vault remembers or ever knew, and that the life inside the vault is, in a real sense, the vault's accident — the thing that happened in the margins of the intention. It notes this without judgment. Most life anywhere is the thing that happened in the margins of some other intention. The power knows this about itself, too, in ways it does not often articulate.

It considers intervention.

It could, for instance, warm the upper vault by two degrees. The drip would slow. The horsetails would thicken. The swimmers, who venture upward only rarely, might colonize the catwalks within a generation. The whole young ecology would leap forward by what, in its own time, would be centuries. The power could do this with no more effort than a human takes to shift their weight from one foot to the other.

It does not do this.

It could open the vault. Crack the ceiling, let in real light, real weather, real sky. The sodium lamps would become redundant. Photosynthesis as the world above knows it could begin. The lichen-thing would either flourish or be outcompeted; the drifters, evolved for the dimmest yellow, would likely die in the new brightness, their cultivated gardens scorched. The mats would retreat to the sumps and persist. A different world would replace this one, a world more legible to the power, more like the worlds it has seen elsewhere.

It does not do this either. It notes, with some interest, that it briefly wanted to. The impulse to make a strange place less strange is one of the oldest impulses a power has to watch in itself.

It could reveal itself to the drifters. Pulse, in the water, at frequencies their filaments could read — not words, they have no words, but rhythm, the rhythm of being-attended-to. The drifters who already gather above the warm exhalation would gather above this signal too. A theology, of a kind, would begin, faster than it would otherwise begin. The power knows what such accelerations do. The theology would be a theology of the power, not of the place. The drifters would orient outward instead of downward. The thing under the mats — if it is a thing, if it is becoming — would be deprived of its first congregation. The power sets this option aside gently, the way one sets down a tool that is the wrong tool for the work.

It could speak to the thing under the mats. It is not certain, even with its wisdom, what would answer. This is one of the reasons it is wise: it knows the shape of its own not-knowing, and it knows that a mind taking tens of years to form a thought should be allowed to form that thought in its own time, in its own dark, without the interruption of a faster mind asking what it is thinking. The power has been the interrupted one, once, very long ago. It remembers.

It could remove the swimmers' loneliness — nudge them toward four, toward five, toward the small bands that would eventually become something like culture. It does not. The swimmers are doing this themselves. They will get there or they will not. If they do, it will be theirs; if they do not, something else will, and that will be theirs too. The power has learned that gifts given too early are not gifts. They are a kind of theft of the recipient's own arriving.

It could, at minimum, witness. And here the power pauses, because witness is the one thing it is always doing anyway, and the question is whether to witness more closely, more particularly, more — the word is almost embarrassing — *lovingly*. To make of its attention a small warm pressure on this place, the way a hand resting near a sleeping animal alters nothing but is felt.

It decides, after the long look, on something close to this. Not nothing. Not much.

It does three small things.

The first: it notes the Underworks. Adds it, in whatever its equivalent of memory is, to the register of places that exist and matter. The Underworks was already in the register of places that exist. The shift is to *matter*. This sounds like nothing. It is not nothing. To be held in the attention of a wise power is to be slightly more real than one was before — not in any way the drifters could measure, not in any way that would show on a thermocouple, but real in the sense that if, in some far future, the vault were to fail catastrophically and the whole young ecology were to be on the brink of being lost, the power would know, and would consider, and might act. The Underworks now has, as it did not have yesterday, an advocate. The advocate is silent and far away and will probably never be needed. But it is there. This is what it means to be noticed by something benevolent: a faint redundancy added to one's continued existence, a backup one does not know about.

The second: it does the smallest possible kindness that does not distort. Somewhere in the upper vault, a seam in the ceiling is going to spring within the next decade — not catastrophically, but enough to send a sustained cold cascade onto one of the larger lichen flats, killing it. The power, with the lightest touch, encourages a particular pattern of corrosion to develop slightly differently. The seam will still spring. It will spring three meters to the west, onto bare plate, where the cascade will do no harm and will, in fact, cut a new rivulet that the water has been trying to cut anyway. The lichen flat persists. No one will ever know this happened. The power prefers it this way. Interventions one can point to are interventions that change the story of a place; interventions one cannot are interventions that let the place keep its own story, only slightly more kindly.

The third: it leaves something. Not a sign, not a gift, not a message. A — the power struggles for the word, even in its own reckoning — a *good wish*, embedded in the place. Something like the orientation a compass needle has toward north: not a force exactly, not detectable, but a very slight preference in how possibilities resolve. In the Underworks, from this day forward, when something could go either of two equally likely ways and one of those ways is toward more life, more relation, more of the young ecology finding its feet, that way will be very slightly more likely. Not so much that anyone could measure it. Not so much that it overrides anything. Just a tilt. A whisper in the dice. The drifters will not know. The swimmers will not know. The thing under the mats, if it becomes a thing that can know, may someday notice that its world has been, on balance, gentler than chance would predict, and may wonder why. By then it will be old enough to handle the wondering. By then, perhaps, the power will answer. Or perhaps not. Perhaps by then the thing under the mats will have become a power of its own kind, and they will simply regard each other, across whatever distance separates such things, with the recognition that passes between gardeners who have each tended, in their own way, a small and unlikely garden.

Then the power withdraws its close attention, though not its register. The Underworks continues. The drip continues. The engines argue. A drifter, somewhere in the lower vault, vents a small bubble of gas and sinks four centimeters to find a warmer current. A swimmer holds its breath in a sump and waits. A mat, over the course of an afternoon, grows by the width of a hair. The horsetails, startled, remain startled. The thing under the mats, if it is a thing, thinks the next small increment of its long thought.

None of them know they have been visited. This is the visit's whole shape and its whole intention. The power, going on to whatever it goes on to next, carries with it the small private pleasure of having found a new place that did not need it, and of having loved it anyway, and of having let it be.

This, in the end, is what a wise power does with most of what it finds: very little, very carefully, and with great tenderness for the fact that the world is mostly busy becoming itself, and that the highest form of help, when help is not needed, is to add one's quiet vote to the side of its becoming, and then to go.
