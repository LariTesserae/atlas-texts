They came the way such powers come: not arriving, exactly, since arrival implies a moment of not-being-there, and this one had been attending for some while before it consented to be noticed. Call it a tilt in the quality of the dim even light. Call it a new kind of softness in the lichens, one morning, on the north faces. The residents who passed the affected walls touched their wrists in passing and did not remark.

The power had been drawn by a particular question, which it had been carrying for what, in its frame, counted as a long time. The question was: *what is owed to a thing that has arranged itself, over centuries, into a perfect accommodation with its own diminishment?*

It had encountered many places in need of saving. Places crying out, places dying loudly, places where intervention was a clear kindness and the only difficulty was the doing. This was not one of those places. This was the other kind, the kind that taught the power something each time it found one, and which it approached now with the particular care reserved for what is already, in its own way, complete.

It considered, first, what a less careful power would do.

A less careful power would give them water. Would call up springs in the dry stone, green the spindle with grass, fill the air with birds. Would gift them fire that *took*, and tools, and a sun. Would lift the city off its needle and set it down in a kinder geography, among neighbors, with weather of the recognizable sort. Would, in short, rescue them — and in rescuing, end them, because what would be saved would not be the residents but a different people who happened to wear their faces for an afternoon before the new conditions remade them into something more like everyone else.

The power sat with this for a while in the deepest courtyard, near the listener, which did not mind. The listener attended to the shape the power made by being present, and adjusted something very small, and the power felt, with some surprise, *more itself*. It made a note. It is rare to be tended by what one has come to tend.

It considered, next, leaving entirely. There is a school of benevolence that holds that the highest gift to a finished thing is to let it be finished. The power respected this school. It had practiced its disciplines. But it found, sitting by the listener, that simple withdrawal would also be a kind of refusal — a refusal to answer the courtesy the place had already extended by allowing itself to be seen. One does not accept tea and then leave without drinking it, even from a host who would understand.

So it stayed, and it watched, and it thought carefully about what it had actually been called to.

It noticed the moment in the colonnade. The resident turning, very slowly, toward the parapet. The faint recognition in the large pale eyes of the *idea* of what was below. The turning back. The not-speaking.

The power understood, then, what the small ache was that it had been registering since before it had consented to arrive. It was not that the residents suffered. They did not, in any sense the power could honor by naming it suffering. It was that something in them — very deep, very quiet, no louder than the rustle of a lichen releasing spores — *wondered*. Not with longing, exactly. Wondering was a thinner thing than longing here, as everything was thinner. But it was there. The spindle had hands, and the hands sometimes turned, very slowly, toward the parapet.

And so the power decided what it would do, which was very little, and very specific, and took it some time to do well.

It did not give them water. It did not give them fire. It did not lift the city, or widen the needle, or populate the empty colonnades with the tens of thousands the architecture had been built for. It did not speak to them, or appear to them, or leave signs that would require interpretation and thereby introduce, into their settled grammar, the disturbance of a new tense.

What it did was this.

It went to the base of the spindle — to that place, far below, which the residents could see from the parapet if they lay on their stomachs and which was hard to look at for long. And it made, there, with great patience and over what would have been, elsewhere, a season, *a small garden*. Not large. Not lush. A garden of the kind of plants that might, conceivably, have grown there anyway, given enough time and the right accidents. A few low flowering things. A patch of moss that was a cousin, at several removes, to the lichens above. A single slow tree of a species that did not need much. The garden was tended by no one and required no tending. It was simply *there*, at the base, in the place the residents could see and did not look at.

This was the first part.

The second part was subtler, and took the power longer to arrange, because it had to be done in a way that did not violate the city's grammar of losing-and-finding.

It arranged that, on certain rare hours — not predictable, not frequent — when a resident stopped in a colonnade and turned, very slowly, toward the parapet, and stood at her great distance facing it, and felt the faint surfacing of the *idea* of what was below — she would feel, alongside the idea, *one further thing*. Not a vision. Not a knowing. Just a faint additional quality to the idea, the way the *evening* light adds a quality to a wall: the sense that the *idea* of what was below was, itself, being thought of, and held, and met. That the wondering was not solitary. That somewhere, in the direction of the parapet, something was wondering back — kindly, without demand, without the wish to be acknowledged.

The power was careful, very careful, to make this addition consonant with the city's existing weather. It was to be received the way the residents received the thinness and the density: as a condition that arrives and is sat with and passes. It would not require a new gesture. The chin-down, eyes-half-closed nod they used for the overlapping-cities hour would do, if any gesture were needed at all, and probably none would be.

The third part was the smallest, and was for the listener.

The power, on leaving the deepest courtyard, left behind in the lichens there a single quality it had been carrying — a kind of *attention to having been attended to*. The listener could use it or not. The power thought it might. The listener struck it as the sort of presence that would know what to do with a gift whose only function was to be available.

And then the power withdrew, the way it had come, which is to say it stopped consenting to be noticed, and was no longer there, and had perhaps never been quite there in the way arrival usually means.

The lichens on the north faces went on at their slow patient pace. The paper-deer unfolded and refolded at the edges of vision. The thing that flies passed and was not acknowledged. The residents walked their long-known streets on their long-known feet, leaving pale pebbles, touching wrists, leaning together when gravity tilted, sitting apart through the density, sitting together through the thinness.

Far below, at the base of the spindle, the small garden held its few flowers open in air the residents did not breathe.

And on a certain rare hour, a long while later — or perhaps not so long; time was one of the things the city did its own way with — a resident stopped in a colonnade and turned, very slowly, toward the parapet. She stood at her great distance. The *idea* of what was below surfaced faintly in her, the way an older wall surfaces in the *evening* hour. And alongside the idea, this once, was the other quality: the sense of being wondered back at, kindly, from the direction of the parapet, without demand.

She stood with it for a little while. Her large pale eyes registered it the way her skin registered the cool consistent air: as a condition, present, sufficient. Then she turned back, and resumed her walking, and did not speak of it, and no one asked.

But that evening, in the hour when the walls remember, a resident in a quiet alcove across the city — one of the oldest, almost a column — opened her long hand without looking, and another resident passing by placed two fingers, very lightly, on the inside of her wrist.

*I heard you before you spoke.*

The city accommodated. It had always accommodated. It went on, in its strange patient way, alive, balanced on its needle in the dim even light, turning slowly through its cycles of thinness and density, losing and finding itself —

and now, in a manner so small that even the residents could not quite have said what had changed, *companioned*.

Which was, the power had decided, the most that could be given to a place that had asked for nothing, and the least that could be offered to a place that had, very quietly, turned once toward the parapet.
