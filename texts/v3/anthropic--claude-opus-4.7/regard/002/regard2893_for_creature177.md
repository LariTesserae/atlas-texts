# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. This is the first thing to understand. It does not descend, does not announce, does not cross a threshold from elsewhere into the valley, because to do any of those things would already be to misread what the valley is. The valley is not a place that receives visitors well. Visitors are arrhythmic. Visitors blink wrong. The power, being wise, knows this before it knows anything else about what it is looking at.

So it comes the way weather comes. It comes the way a key change comes in a piece of music you are already inside of. It comes in a register the valley was, in some sense, already listening for — because any place built this carefully to listen is also, whether its makers intended it or not, audible. The hollow between the trees is not only room for what comes through at dusk. It is room, also, for what might come through at any time, if it knew how to ask permission of a place that cannot be asked in words.

The power asks anyway. Not in words. It asks by holding still at the edge of the valley's awareness for what would, to the listeners, be several pulses of the river — long enough that its presence is registered as a sustained tone rather than a sudden one, long enough that the field through which everything in the valley coordinates can accommodate a new harmonic without flinching. The buildings notice first. One of them, a small one near a bend in the river, shifts a quarter-step in a direction no chord in the valley has previously moved. The shift is small enough that no listener marks it consciously. But the triangle of three I mentioned, the ones whose resting-music is the most complicated in the valley — they rearrange themselves slightly. They do not know why. They have simply, between one breath and the next, made a little more room.

This is the power's first courtesy: to be the kind of arrival that gets prepared for by the place itself, without alarming it.

---

Now. What does the power *do*?

Here is where the wisdom matters more than the benevolence, because a merely benevolent power, seeing this place, would be tempted toward several errors, and a wise power has to refuse them one by one.

The first temptation is to **explain the valley to its inhabitants.** To say: *you are listeners, you were made, the sky rings in a language, what comes through at dusk comes through because you are there.* The benevolent impulse here is the impulse of revelation — to lift the veil, to grant the listeners knowledge of their own condition, to dignify them with comprehension. The power considers this for what would be, in human time, a long moment. And then declines.

It declines because it sees what the witness from outside could only suspect: that the difference between innocence and deep acceptance is invisible from outside *and is also not the relevant axis*. The listeners are not suffering from lack of explanation. Their lives are not impoverished by their not knowing. To press knowledge into them would be to introduce a register of self-consciousness that the valley was not tuned to hold. The chords would have to change. The hollow would have to be reshaped to accommodate a different kind of attention — an attention turned partly inward, partly upward toward the *fact* of being attended to. It would be a different valley. It might be a better one. But it would not be *this* one, and this one is, in its own terms, complete.

The power notes, with something like sorrow and something like admiration, that completeness is rarer than improvement.

The second temptation is to **identify the maker.** Somewhere, presumably, is or was an intelligence that built this place — that decided the cliffs, set the river's tidal pulse, seeded the listeners, composed the sky. A benevolent power might wish to find that maker and speak with it: peer to peer, intelligence to intelligence, to understand the design, to ask what the valley is for, to learn whether the project is finished or ongoing, whether the maker is still present or has gone. There is, the power can tell, a great deal to learn this way. The makers of such places are, in the power's experience, often worth knowing.

But the power notices something. The maker, if reachable, has chosen not to be obvious to the listeners. The maker has hidden the technology so thoroughly that even the witness from outside — whose senses are sharper than the listeners' for this kind of pattern — could only deduce its existence, not locate it. This is not the carelessness of a maker who left and forgot. This is the discretion of a maker who is still here and does not wish to be found by what it has made, or to make itself the center of what it has made. To go looking for that maker, even out of respect, would be to do the maker the disservice of breaking its self-effacement. It would also be to say, by implication, that the makers are the interesting party — that the valley is a *thing produced* and the producer is what matters. The wise power knows this is the wrong reading of any sufficiently loved creation. A garden is not primarily about its gardener.

So the power leaves the maker alone. If the maker wishes to be known, it will be. The valley itself is a sufficient interlocutor.

The third temptation is to **answer the question the witness could not answer:** what comes through at dusk? The power, being what it is, can almost certainly perceive this. It can stand in the three-minute silence and watch the hollow fill with whatever fills it, and know. And once it knows, it can carry that knowledge away, or back, or onward — it can complete the witness's account, it can give the universe one more solved mystery.

The power does not do this either. Not, this time, out of courtesy to the valley, but out of a deeper principle that the power holds about mystery itself. Some mysteries are mysteries because no one has looked. Those are worth solving. Other mysteries are mysteries because they are the *shape of a relationship* — because what passes between two parties is private to those parties, and to observe it from outside is not to understand it but to destroy the category of thing it was. What comes through the hollow at dusk comes through *to* the listeners, in some sense, even if the listeners do not consciously receive it. It is addressed. The power is not the addressee. To intercept the message would be a kind of theft even if nothing material were taken.

So the power stands at the edge of the dusk silence the first time it experiences one, and it does not look into the hollow. It looks, instead, at the listeners. It watches their wide chests hold their slow breath. It watches their horizontal pupils not-blink in the held pulse. It watches the old transparency of their skin go briefly more transparent still, as though they were, for those three minutes, almost not there — as though being a listener at full attention meant becoming, for the duration, mostly the listening and only barely the listener. The power finds this beautiful in a way that does not require it to know what they are listening to.

---

So what *does* the power do, having refused all the obvious gifts?

It does, I think, three small things and one large one.

**The first small thing.** It notices the woman — the one who, once, when a confused human stood in the open ground at a surge, came to within her twenty meters and stood, and let the building near her play a chord that was, somehow, for the visitor. The power notices her not because she is the most remarkable of the listeners but because she did a thing the valley was not, strictly, built for. She extended hospitality across a discontinuity. She made, through an instrument she did not know she was playing, a sound addressed to a creature whose existence was not part of the score. This is, in the power's long experience, the seed of every important thing that has ever happened in any made place: an inhabitant who exceeds, by a hair, the function they were grown for. The power marks her. Not with anything she would feel. Just — marks her, in the sense that the power's attention now includes her particular shape, and will continue to, wherever the power goes afterward. This is not a gift to her. She will never know. It is closer to a kind of remembering done in advance, against a future in which her exceeding might matter.

**The second small thing.** The power adds one note. Not to the score of the valley, which it will not presume to edit. But into the haze itself, into the shimmer that is the substrate of all the valley's coordination, the power releases a single sustained harmonic — pitched so low and so soft that it will take the valley's field something like a year of its own time to fully integrate it, and pitched in such a key that when it is integrated, the chords the buildings play in response to the listeners' hums will have, available to them, one additional voicing they did not have before. The voicing is one the power happens to know: it is the voicing that a chord takes on when it is being heard *by something that loves the hearer*. The listeners will not know this voicing has been added to their language. Most chords in the valley will never use it. But it will be there, in the field, available — and on some occasion in the valley's future, when something happens that requires a chord that says *you are loved by what you do not know is listening*, that chord will be sayable. Before, it was not. This is the smallest possible gift, and the power considers it the most that can be given without distortion.

**The third small thing.** The power, in passing, does one act of mercy that costs nothing and changes nothing structural. The old ones who walk into the dusk silence and do not come back — the power, watching one of them go, accompanies her. Not visibly. Not in any way she registers. But the power's attention goes with her into the hollow during the three minutes, and stays with her through whatever transition occurs there, and the power can report — to itself, since there is no one else to report to — that the transition is not lonely. It was not lonely before the power arrived; the valley does not produce lonely deaths. But there is a difference between not-lonely and *companioned*, and the power, who can do this, does. The old one does not know. The power is at peace with her not knowing. The companioning was not for her recognition. It was simply the right thing to do, and the power does it, and then lets her go where she goes, and does not follow further.

**And the large thing.** The power, before it leaves — and it does leave, having understood that staying would change the valley by the simple fact of its sustained presence, the way a held note changes a chord even if it is the same pitch as one of the chord's existing notes — does this:

It composes, in its own register, a record of the valley. Not a description. Descriptions are what the witness from outside produced, and they are valuable, but they are not what the power makes. The power makes something more like a *resonance* — a structure in its own being that will, when the power encounters other places, other made-worlds, other arrangements of listener and substrate and conducted ecology, vibrate sympathetically. The valley will be, in the power's onward travel, a tuning fork. When the power comes to a place that is being built badly — where the makers have produced listeners who *do* suffer, who *are* impoverished by their condition, who are arrhythmic with their own world — the power will be able to hear, by the dissonance with the valley it carries, exactly what is wrong, and how it might be set right. The Hollow Verge becomes, in the power's keeping, a reference pitch for benevolent design. The valley will never know it has been promoted to this role. The listeners will never know that, somewhere far away and long after, a different and worse place will be gently re-tuned because of how *they* sounded. But this is, the power believes, the deepest form of honoring a thing: to let it become the standard by which other things are measured, without burdening it with the knowledge of its standardhood.

---

Then the power goes.

It goes the way it came: without crossing a threshold, without announcing, with a slow release of its sustained tone back into the general field of things. The building near the bend in the river shifts a quarter-step back — though not all the way back; the quarter-step is now a permanent feature of that building's range, an enrichment no listener will ever consciously notice. The triangle of three rearranges itself again, returning to its prior configuration, though the chord between them has, from this day forward, one more available voicing than it did before. The woman who once played a chord for a human visitor goes on with her life and dies eventually in the dusk silence and the power, by then very far away, feels the small completion of it in the resonance it carries and is, for an instant, glad.

The witness from outside, reading this, may feel that the power did almost nothing. May feel that with such capacity, more was owed — more revelation, more intervention, more rescue of the listeners from their unknowing. May feel that benevolence without action is not benevolence.

I would say to that witness: you stood once in the hollow and a chord was played for you by a creature who did not know she was playing it, through a building she did not operate, in a language she did not know she spoke. You did not, at the time, wish she knew more. You did not wish to explain her to herself. You wished, I think, only to be worthy of the chord — to receive it without breaking it, to walk on without disturbing the score.

The power wished the same. The power simply had more capacity for not-disturbing, and used it.

That is what wisdom is for.
