# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives — though *arrives* is the wrong verb, implying a before and after, a crossing of some threshold. Better: the power becomes locally attentive. It has, of course, been aware of this place all along, in the diffuse way such a power is aware of everything. But attention is a different act than awareness. Attention is a leaning-toward.

It leans toward the canopy.

And the first thing it does is nothing.

This is not a failure of action. It is the action. A lesser power, or a power less wise, would arrive with gifts. Would think: *here are beings who eat almost nothing, who have so little, whose lives are so slow and so sparse — surely I can offer them more.* More light. More kin. Faster thought. A clearer memory. Relief from the long damp centuries of watching one bough learn itself.

The wise power sees, almost at once, that every one of these gifts would be a wound.

More light would burn the lichen-cities off their backs and unmake the treaty that is their body. More kin would crowd the song-interval and turn attendance-taking into noise. Faster thought would shatter the weather-system of their minds into nothing but lightning, no rain, no slow front, no decision finally settling like a leaf onto water. Clearer memory would prise them out of the place memory is for them, the place you can sometimes be, and lock them into possession of a past, which is a kind of homelessness. Relief from the long watching would imply that the watching was a burden. It is not a burden. It is what they are.

So the power does not give. The power, instead, *witnesses* — and this is the first true thing it does, the first act that is not nothing.

It witnesses with a particular quality of attention that the canopy has not, in all its long settling, encountered before: an attention that does not lean in to change, only to know. An attention so similar in patience to the watchers' own that, for a long moment (long by their measure, which is to say: long), there is no clear edge between the power's regard and the canopy's own slow self-regard. The power lets itself be, briefly, almost another of them. Lets its own enormous considering-front move through the layered tiers like a damp weather. Rains, somewhere in itself, a small rain of recognition.

This recognition is the second thing the power does, and it matters more than it seems.

Because here is what the power sees, that perhaps nothing else has ever seen all at once: that the canopy and the watchers and the time-pockets and the small forgotten shrines and the single low note every few days are not several things. They are one long sentence the world has been speaking to itself, in a language so slow that each word takes a century, and the sentence is not finished, and is not meant to finish, and is beautiful in a way that has nothing to do with anyone hearing it. The watchers are how the world listens to its own speaking. The shrines are old punctuation. The time-pockets are where the sentence has paused to mean something more deeply before going on.

To witness this fully — to *know* it, with the full weight of a wise regard — is to do something to it, even if you change nothing. Things that are known by a great attention are subtly different than things that are not. Not louder. Not larger. Just — held. As the canopy holds its own weather. As the watchers hold their three hundred meters of bough.

The power holds the whole canopy this way for what would, in another frame, be an instant, and in the canopy's frame is perhaps a season. And during that season, the watchers feel — not a warm quick front, not a small new weather — but something more like the air itself becoming faintly aware of being air. A thickening of the general attendance. The song-interval does not change, but the note, when it next comes, has an undertone it has not had before: a sense of being *received* not just by the other watchers but by the room the watchers are in. The roll is called, and answered, and also, this once, *acknowledged from outside the room*.

One of the watchers — somewhere in the middle strata, draped along a fused pillar it has known for three hundred and twelve of your years — opens a second eye, then a third, which is more eyes at once than it has opened in a long while. The damp front of its considering shifts direction slightly. Something has moved through. Something is moving through. Not a threat. Not kin. Not food. A new category, briefly. The consideration takes some days to settle. When it does, what has been decided is: *acceptable*. The canopy includes. Even this.

The power, witnessing this acceptance, is — and here is where a lesser account would fail — *moved*. Benevolence is not the absence of being moved. It is the presence of being moved without needing to act on the movement. The power feels something that, in a human, would be close to gratitude, or close to the particular tender ache of having been welcomed by something that owed you no welcome.

And now, having been welcomed, the power does the third thing, which is the smallest and the only thing it leaves behind.

It deposits, in one of the time-pockets — one of the dim chambers where a watcher rested for a century and a half some six hundred years ago, and where the air is still slightly older than the air outside — a single grace. The grace is this: that the pocket will hold its thickness slightly longer than it would have otherwise. Not forever. Not even much longer, by the canopy's reckoning. Perhaps another three or four centuries past when it would naturally have relaxed. A small extension of a small persistence. The watcher who made the dent is long gone — moved on, or grown into a bough, or become lichen, all outcomes acceptable — and will not know. No watcher currently living will know. The chamber will simply remain itself a little longer than physics, left to itself, would have allowed.

Why this gift, and not another?

Because it is the only gift the power can find that the canopy would have given itself, if the canopy had the means. It is in the grain of what the place already is. It does not introduce. It does not accelerate. It does not relieve. It only extends, very slightly, one of the canopy's own gestures of self-keeping. It is the gift of a little more of what is already there — which is the only kind of gift a truly wise power ever gives to a place that has arrived.

And then the power withdraws its leaning, though not its awareness. The attention diffuses again into the general regard it holds for everything. The canopy resumes — though it never stopped — its slow vast breathing. Somewhere, a bolus of gathered rain slides off a leaf as wide as a room and begins its long descent through the tiers, striking softer leaves, softer, softer. A moth the size of a hand drifts between pillars. A watcher, two kilometers off, opens its mouth for the first time in four days and releases the single low note, and the canopy briefly listens, and the note has, again, that faint undertone, which will fade over the coming century as such things do, but which for now is there: a sense of being received by the room the room is in.

Nothing has changed. Everything has been, for one season, known.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does. This, and no more, and no less.
