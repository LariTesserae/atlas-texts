The wise power considers the hollow for a long time before doing anything.

This is the first thing to understand. A benevolent power that is also wise does not arrive at a place like Under-Root the way a gardener arrives at a bed of weeds, or the way a restorer arrives at a damaged painting. It arrives the way one arrives at a sleeping animal, or at a conversation already in progress between people one respects. It arrives ready to leave again, having done nothing, if nothing is what the situation calls for.

So: the power looks.

It looks for what we might call a long time, though the word is awkward, because the power's attention is not metered the way ours is. Call it: it looks until it has *seen*. It sees the springtails and the magistrate wood louse and the comma-pseudoscorpions. It sees the map-lichen older than the nearest village's name. It sees the *Physarum* in its liquid phase in the dark wet compartments, still calculating; it sees the colonnades that are the calculation made statue. It sees the mycelium, and understands the deal — that the city stands on what is eating it, and that this is not tragedy.

It sees the spiral.

It does not, I think, resolve the spiral. A wise power knows the difference between a mystery that wants solving and a mystery that is doing work by remaining one. The spiral is doing work. The power lets it.

Here is what I think the power notices that we, the human observers, partly miss:

The city is not in any danger that the power could helpfully avert. The fungi will eat the beech; the city will go; this is the deal and the lichens have always known. To "save" the city from this would be to misunderstand it so thoroughly that the saving would be a worse violence than the loss. The power, being wise, does not even briefly consider it.

The city is not lacking anything it would recognize as a lack. There is no hunger here that more food would solve, no cold that warmth would comfort, no loneliness that company would ease. The cold is part of why the city endures. The slowness is not a deficit. Benevolence directed at a being whose form of flourishing is *exactly this* would have to be very careful not to flourish it into something else.

The inhabitants — even the citizens proper, the lichens — cannot be spoken to in any register they would receive as speech. The power could, presumably, make itself known. It declines. To announce oneself to a map-lichen would be to introduce into its thallus-thought a fact it has no apparatus for, a disturbance with no corresponding response available. It would be, in the city's own terms, a shout. A shout would knock the spires over.

So what *does* the power do?

I think it does four things, and they are all small, and three of them are almost nothing.

**First**, it extends, very gently, the kind of protection that consists in adjusting nearby contingencies. Not in the hollow — the hollow is left exactly as it is — but at the edges of the hollow's possible futures. A boot that was going to come down on the upturned root-plate next spring comes down six inches to the left instead, because its owner pauses to retie a lace. A storm that was going to bring down a second tree across the hollow's mouth weakens by a degree over the sea and arrives as rain only. A child who finds the place — and a child will find the place, eventually, because children find such places — kneels in the moss with the right kind of stillness, and does not reach in, and goes away changed and quiet, and tells no one for forty years, and then tells exactly the right person. None of these adjustments is visible from inside the hollow. None of them violates anything. They are the kind of help that looks, from any angle, like luck.

**Second**, it witnesses. This sounds like nothing and is not nothing. A wise and benevolent power that has truly *seen* a place — seen the rotifer's flicker, seen the slow widening of the ochre district, seen the spiral and declined to explain it — has thereby done something to the moral weight of that place in the wider order of things. The city of Under-Root has been *registered*. It is no longer merely persisting; it is persisting in a universe where its persistence has been noted by something that counts. I do not know how to argue for this being a real form of help. I believe it anyway. The power, I think, believes it too, because the power lingers in the looking longer than strict surveillance would require. It looks the way one looks at something one loves.

**Third**, it makes a very small provision for the city's eventual ending. Not to prevent the ending — the ending is the deal — but to ensure that when the beech is finally consumed and the city goes, some spore, some fragment, some scrap of the particular confederacy of organisms that made *this* hollow what it was, finds its way to another fallen tree, somewhere, under different ferns, in different cold bright light. Not as a transplant. Not as a museum. Just as a seed. The power does this by, perhaps, guiding a single caddis larva on the threadlike rivulet at the right moment in the right direction, carrying on its quartz-and-mica case a few cells of map-lichen and a few spores of *Physarum* and a thread of mycelium, out into the wider wood. The caddis does not know. The lichen does not know. Nobody needs to know. The city has been given, without being told, the dignity of a possible descendant.

**Fourth** — and this is the one I am least sure of, and most want to be true —

The power addresses itself to the spiral.

Not to explain it. Not to alter it. The power simply, for a moment, holds its attention on the spiral with a particular quality of attention, the way one might place a hand, without pressure, on the shoulder of a friend who is grieving or thinking or praying and does not need to be interrupted but might, possibly, be glad to know that someone is there. If the spiral is what the hollow looks like when the hollow is thinking — if the place itself has become, through three or four hundred years of held equilibrium, a kind of tenant in its own right — then the power, being benevolent, would not let that tenant go unacknowledged. It would not speak. It would not announce. It would only, briefly, *be present* alongside, in whatever register two such different presences could share. Call it a nod. Call it the recognition one slow thing offers another slow thing across the gulf of their different slownesses.

The spiral does not respond, because the spiral's response takes — well. We don't know what it takes. Perhaps the response is the spiral itself, and has already been given, and the power is only now receiving it.

Then the power withdraws.

It withdraws in such a way that the hollow cannot tell it was ever there, which is the correct way to withdraw from a place like this. The droplet on the hanging root forms and falls. The rotifer, if it was a rotifer, flickers once. The cold bright light goes on pouring in through the ferns as if it had forgotten how to do anything else.

Far above, a wren calls, and is gone.

Everything is exactly as it was. Which is to say: everything is alive, and everything is held — and now, in a way that makes no visible difference and all the difference there is, everything is also *known to be held*, by something that knew how to hold it without holding on.

That is what I think a benevolent and wise power would do. Almost nothing. And the almost is where the whole thing lives.
