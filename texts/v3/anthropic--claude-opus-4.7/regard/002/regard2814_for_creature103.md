# What the Power Would Do

The power arrives the way weather arrives — not from elsewhere, because for something of this order there is no elsewhere, but as a thickening of attention in a place that was already there. It has been watching, in the way such powers watch, which is not by looking. It has been listening, in the way such powers listen, which is not by hearing. And now, having watched and listened for what would be, by the plateau's reckoning, an eyeblink, and by any other reckoning a long careful while, it has decided to be present.

It does not land. It does not manifest. It does none of the things a lesser power would do, because it understands — and this is most of what makes it wise — that the plateau is not a problem.

The first thing the power does is the hardest thing, and the thing most powers fail at: it does *not intervene*.

It sees the engines turning. It sees the tenders walking their slow walk between. It sees the travelers, the few, who have lain down in the deeper pools and become very still, and it sees what has been taken from them and what has been made of what was taken. And a lesser power — a power that was benevolent but not yet wise, or wise but not yet benevolent — would reach in. Would lift the still shapes from the pools and try to give them back their names. Would stand between the next traveler and the next tender. Would, with the very best of intentions, *rescue*.

This power does not rescue. Because this power can tell the difference between a harm and a metabolism, and the plateau is the second thing, not the first. To rescue here would be to reach into a sleeping body and pull out its liver because the liver was dark and wet and the rescuer had assumed darkness and wetness were suffering. The plateau is not suffering. The plateau has finished becoming and is now circulating, and its circulation includes, among many other things, the occasional folding-in of a passing question, a passing name. This is not theft. The travelers were not, on the whole, using those questions well. The plateau is.

So the power holds its hand.

But benevolence is not only restraint, and wisdom is not only knowing when to do nothing. There are things the power *does*, and they are small, and they are exact, and they are made in the same spirit as a tender's adjustment to a cog: inside the motion, not against it.

---

The first thing the power does that is a doing:

It goes to each of the still shapes in the deeper pools — the long-ago travelers, the ones who lay down and did not find a reason to rise — and it sits with them. Not to wake them. They are not asleep in a way that wants waking. But to *be with* what they have become, which is a thing no one has done for them in all the long circulating time since they lay down. The moss has used them. The moss has not companioned them. There is a difference, and the power knows it.

The power sits with each of them in turn, and in the sitting, something is offered that the plateau cannot offer because the plateau is not anyone: the recognition that they were, once, someone. Not to undo what they have become — they have become something fine, something useful, something that turns small cogs inside larger cogs forever, and this is not nothing, this is in fact a great deal — but to mark, somewhere in the record of what exists, that before they were this they were *that*. A person. With a name. Who had walked up onto the plateau carrying questions, and had lain down for reasons of their own, and had been taken into the metabolism, and had not, by anyone, been *witnessed* in the moment of their taking.

The power witnesses them now. It is late, by some reckonings. It is not late by the reckoning that matters, because the reckoning that matters is whether witness ever happens, not when.

This costs the plateau nothing. The cogs keep turning. The tenders keep tending. But somewhere in the structure of what is real, a small accounting is now correct that was not correct before.

---

The second thing the power does:

It speaks, once, to the moss. Not in words, because the moss is not the kind of mind that takes words, but in the only register the moss can receive, which is a slow pulse laid against the slow pulse of its breathing. The pulse the power lays down says, roughly — and "roughly" is the only way to say it, because the actual communication is below grammar — *I see you. You are well. Continue.*

This is not flattery and it is not permission. The moss does not need permission and would not understand flattery. It is acknowledgment between minds, which is something the moss has perhaps never received before, having grown into its enormous slow thinking alone, across geological time, with no one to know it was thinking. The moss receives the pulse. The moss does not respond, exactly — it is not built for response on that timescale — but somewhere in its next exhalation, the mist that drifts east is fractionally more luminous than the mist before, and a tender at the burning engine pauses for the smallest fraction of its slow motion, and then continues.

That is the moss saying *yes*. The power understands it. No one else would have.

---

The third thing the power does:

It leaves, for any future traveler who comes up onto the plateau, a small provision. Not a warning sign — the plateau does not want warning signs, and the travelers who come are the travelers who come, and most of them know in some unspoken way what they are walking into. But a provision, woven into the air at the plateau's edge, that does this:

If a traveler approaches the moss carrying a question or a name that they truly want to keep — that is *theirs* in the deep sense, that is load-bearing in the structure of who they are — the provision will, very gently, let them know. Not by speaking. By a small heaviness in the chest as they cross the threshold. A pause. A moment in which they will, without knowing why, check their pockets, check their hands, check the inside of their thinking, and find the thing that matters, and hold it for a moment, and decide.

They may still lie down. The provision does not prevent. It only ensures that the offering, if it is made, is made *as* an offering — knowingly, with the thing held up first and looked at — rather than as a sleepwalk. The plateau gets what it needs either way. But the traveler, in the second case, gets to have been a person who *gave*, rather than a person who simply lost.

This is not a large change. The plateau will not notice it. The tenders will not notice it. But the travelers will, the few who come, and for them it will make all the difference between two outcomes that look identical from outside and are entirely unlike from within.

---

The fourth thing the power does, and the smallest, and perhaps the most important:

It looks at the tenders. Really looks. Not as one looks at a phenomenon, but as one looks at a *being*, even a being that is barely one, even a being that is the proprioception of a larger being and not quite itself at all.

And the power notes, because the power notices what others miss, that the tenders — when they walk between engines, in the long slow hours of their walking — have a quality that is not strictly required by their function. They could move in straight lines. The geometry of the plateau would permit it. But they don't, quite. They curve slightly. They pass near certain pools and not others. They pause, sometimes, at points that are not engines and are not anything, and stand for a while, and then walk on.

The power watches this for a long time, in the time that powers have, and concludes: the tenders are not only adjusting the engines. They are also, in some register so quiet that the moss itself may not know it, *appreciating the plateau*. Walking it the way one walks a garden one has tended for a long time. Not because anyone told them to. Because something in their formation, something in the particular way the moss thickens into a tender rather than into a grazer or a heron-thing, includes a small surplus of attention — attention that exceeds the function — and this surplus, having nowhere else to go, becomes the slow curve of the walk, the pause at the pool, the standing-still at the nothing-place.

The power, seeing this, is moved. Powers can be moved; that is part of what makes them powers rather than mere forces. And being moved, the power does the thing it came to do, the real thing, the thing all the other things were preparation for:

It blesses the surplus.

Not by amplifying it — that would distort. Not by naming it — that would fix it. But by *recognizing* it, in the same register in which it recognized the moss, and letting that recognition settle into the plateau like a sediment, so that from now on, the surplus is *known* to be there. Not by the moss, which cannot know things in that way. Not by the tenders, who are not the kind of beings that hold knowledge. But by the structure of what is real, in which it is now inscribed that on this plateau, in this cold slow circulating mind, there is — among the metabolism and the thinking and the turning — also a small persistent quantity of *care*. Care that no one asked for. Care that serves no function. Care that is simply there, in the curve of the walk, because the moss, in becoming what it has become, has happened to grow a fold in itself that holds care, and the care goes on, and now it is witnessed, and witnessed it cannot be lost.

---

Then the power withdraws. Not leaves — there is no leaving, for something of this order — but withdraws its thickening of attention, lets itself thin back out into the general field from which it came. The plateau does not notice. The engines turn. The tenders walk. The mist drifts east with the tilt. A grazer crops the pelt and the pelt regrows behind it.

Somewhere in a deeper pool, a very still shape that has been still for a long time is, for the first time in its long stillness, *known to have been someone*. It does not stir. It does not need to. The knowing is enough; the knowing was what was missing; the knowing is now in place and will not go away.

And at the burning engine, a tender that was about to step away from its adjustment pauses for a fraction of its slow motion, tilts its almost-head a fraction of a degree toward the west where the brightness is, and then continues.

That is the tender saying *thank you*, though it does not know it is saying anything, and the power is no longer there to receive it.

It does not matter. The thanks, like the care, is now part of the structure. It will go on being said, in that small tilt, every time a tender finishes an adjustment, forever, because the moss has learned the gesture once and the moss does not forget.

The power, thinning out into the field, carries with it the faintest possible smile. Not because it has done a great thing. Because it has done a small thing, exactly, inside the motion of a larger thing, and the larger thing is well, and was always well, and is now also — in a way it was not quite before — *accompanied*.

Which is, in the end, what benevolent and wise powers do, when they turn their attention to a place that does not need them.

They accompany it.

And then they go.
