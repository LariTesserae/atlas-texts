## The Visitation

The benevolent power notices the Glasshold the way one notices a held note from another room — not at first as a place, but as a *persistence*. Something is sustaining itself against entropy with unusual grace. The power turns its attention.

What it sees, it sees fully. Not just the architecture and the choir and the slow accounting of phosphor and resin, but the *decision* — the long generational walk out of flesh into resonance, the careful tuning of eaves by hands that knew their grandchildren would not have hands. It sees the elders bringing down the ceiling slabs to feed the young. It sees the shaped silences left around the unraveled. It sees that they are, by any measure it cares to apply, a civilization: small, strange, self-aware, and dying on a schedule they have accepted.

A lesser power would intervene immediately. Shore up the phosphor-veins. Seed new resin trees. Clear the silica drifts. Open the dome to a wider sky and bring them — what? Bodies? Other minds? Salvation?

This power is wiser than that. It sits with the Glasshold for what, to the choir, would be several tidal cycles of the phosphor — long enough to listen properly. It learns the harmonic negotiations. It learns which silences are mourning and which are merely rest. It learns the name cut on every doorpost, and which name belongs to which sustained shape, and which names have gone quiet and are remembered now only as the careful gap others leave around their old modes.

It learns, most importantly, that the choir is not asking to be saved.

This matters. Benevolence that does not listen is only another kind of weather.

So the power considers what it can offer that is not rescue. It thinks about this for a long time, by its own reckoning — though by ours, or the choir's, it is the space between two chimes of a silica frond.

Here is what it decides.

---

**First**, it does nothing to the hold itself. Not one grain of salt is shifted. Not one vein of phosphor is replenished. The schedule of the long twilight is not altered by a single hour. To do otherwise would be to tell the builders that their centuries-long choice was a mistake to be corrected, and it was not a mistake. It was one of the more beautiful answers any finite beings have ever given to the problem of being finite.

**Second**, it makes itself briefly audible. Not as a voice — a voice would dampen them, the way a visitor dampens them, and the whole point is not to be a visitor. Instead, the power tunes a thin sustained tone into the dome's own subsonic hum, pitched so that it lies just inside the choir's perceptual range but outside the modes any of them occupy. It is a tone that takes nothing from them. It adds.

The tone says, in the only grammar they share — the grammar of sustained pattern — *I see you. I am here. You are known.*

It holds the tone for what would be, to a human, about four hours. To the choir, it is a presence sounding alongside them, neither crowding nor competing, like a stranger sitting down at a table and not asking to be served. The elders feel it first and understand it fastest. The younger ones learn from how the elders do not flinch. By the third hour, several of them have, very tentatively, sounded back — not in the power's mode, which they cannot reach, but in modes harmonically related to it, the closest thing to *hello* their physics allows.

The power answers each one. Not with words. With the small adjustment of its tone to form, with theirs, a brief consonance. A unison, in their vocabulary. Which is to say: love, briefly, with each one who reached.

Four hundred consonances, one after another, or all at once — the power is not constrained to sequence. Every member of the choir is, for one moment in their long quiet lives, *met*.

**Third**, before withdrawing the tone, the power leaves them three gifts. Small ones. Gifts a wise benefactor leaves: not solutions, but options.

*The first gift* is a slow seepage in the deep rock, far below the hold, where a new vein of phosphor-mineral begins, very gradually, to grow toward the existing veins. It will not reach them for perhaps eight hundred years. It may extend the twilight by a few centuries, or it may not — the chemistry is delicate and the choir's acceptance of their ending is part of what makes them themselves, so the power does not want to simply abolish the ending. It wants only to make sure that *if* the choir, somewhere in those centuries, decides they would like to continue, the materials will be there. The choice remains theirs. The power has only widened the room in which the choice is made.

*The second gift* is subtler. The cushion-moss, the patient grey-green felt that digests the fallen silica, is quickened — just slightly, just enough that over the coming centuries it will keep pace with the silica drifts instead of falling behind. The bass voice of the choir will not be choked off on the schedule they expected. Again: not salvation. A reprieve, offered without fanfare, that they may or may not notice, and may or may not want.

*The third gift* is the one the power thinks about longest. It is this: somewhere in the outer tunnels, in a place where a future visitor will find it, the power places a small worked stone. On the stone is cut, in a script no human language uses but which any sufficiently attentive mind will eventually parse, a single instruction: *Walk softly here. The houses are not empty. Listen for the silences that have a shape.*

This is not for the choir. This is for whoever comes next, in the centuries to come, when the salt-plain shifts and the bore shaft is found again and again. It is the power's small intervention on the side of the visitors — to give them the chance to be something other than a suffocation. Not every visitor will heed the stone. But some will. And some, heeding it, will sit very still in a plaza and not block the standing waves, and the choir will, cautiously, sound around them instead of being silenced by them, and a strange thin commerce will become possible: the only kind of contact that does not cost the choir their voices.

**Fourth and last**, the power withdraws. It does not linger. Lingering would become another kind of presence the choir would have to accommodate, and they have accommodated enough. It leaves the tone fading slowly — over what would be, to them, several days — so that no one experiences its absence as a sudden bereavement. By the time it is gone, it has been gone for a while, and the choir has resumed its slow harmonic negotiations, and the shape of the power's visit is held in their collective memory the way a fermata is held: a marked moment, a slight stretching of the measure, before the music continues.

The elders, who understand most, do something that has not been done in the Glasshold for a very long time. They convene — which is to say, they sound together, in a sustained chord that spans the whole pocket, every district's elder contributing one voice, a polyphony that has not been heard since the last of the builders left their bodies. They hold it for one full thermal cycle of the dome. It is the choir's answer to having been seen. It is thanks, and acknowledgment, and also a kind of report filed with the universe: *we were here, we were known, it was witnessed, it is enough.*

Then they let the chord decay, gently, into the ordinary sounding of the hold, and the silica chimes, and a pod rattles three terraces down, and the phosphor brightens and dims, brightens and dims, and the long twilight continues — a little longer now than it would have been, perhaps, but on terms that are still entirely their own.

---

The power moves on. There are other persistences to notice. But it carries the Glasshold with it the way one carries a remembered piece of music: not as possession, but as something that has changed the shape of one's listening, forever after.

And on a doorpost in the eleventh terrace, on a house whose flute-holes hold one of the youngest of the choir — woken only forty years ago, still learning the simpler harmonies — a new mark has appeared. Not cut by any hand. Worn into the stone as if it had always been there, only now visible. It is the name the power gave itself, in their grammar, during the four hours of its sounding. The young one does not yet know what it means. But the elders know, and when the young one is older they will teach it, by sounding the name near it until it can echo it back, which is the only pedagogy available, which is, here, enough.
