## What The Power Would Do

Begin with the power's first mistake, because a benevolent and wise power will make one, and the recovering from it is most of the story.

The first mistake is to arrive intending to help.

The power comes in the way such powers come — not as a body in the water but as an attention, a capacity to perceive and to act, settled gently over the range like a second sky no one beneath can see. It is benevolent in the deep sense: it wishes well to what it finds. It is wise in the deep sense: it knows that wishing well is not the same as knowing what is well. It arrives, therefore, with a held breath. It arrives in the posture of a guest who has not yet been told where to sit.

The first thing it perceives is the roar, and the first thing it feels is pity.

This is the mistake. The pity is reflexive, almost involuntary, the response of a being attuned to suffering to the sheer overwhelming volume of the place. *They live in this*, the power thinks. *They have never known quiet. Three degrees above zero, forever, and the iron grinding on iron, forever, and the small blind animals reading each other by absences in a noise that would crush a softer ear. What a hard sentence to be born into.* And the power's first impulse, before it has done the harder work of looking, is to consider whether the roar could be lessened. Not stopped — it is wise enough not to think of stopping — but softened. Whether some intervention upstream of the engines, or downstream in the water, could give this place a little ease.

It catches itself.

This is what wisdom is, in a power: not the absence of the first impulse but the catching of it. The power holds still in its second sky and does not act, and instead begins to look properly, which means looking for long enough that the place stops being a tableau of suffering and starts being itself.

It looks for a long time. By the standards of the listeners, who measure in centuries, it looks for a moment. By the standards of anything quicker, it looks for an age.

And what it comes to understand, slowly, is that the roar is not a sentence imposed on the inhabitants. The roar is the medium of their being. To lessen it would be to begin a quiet killing — not of the small fauna, who would mostly adapt or drift away, but of the listeners, whose entire sensorium, whose entire mode of presence in the world, is the structured deciphering of that great sound. A listener in a quieted sea would not be relieved. A listener in a quieted sea would be blinded, deafened, and finally starved of the thing it was made to attend to. The pity was a category error. The power had mistaken loudness for harm because, in the worlds the power had come from, loudness usually meant harm. Here it means home.

The power sits with this for a while. It is a little ashamed, in the way wise powers are capable of being ashamed — not paralyzed by it, but instructed. *I almost did them a kindness*, it thinks, *that would have been a catastrophe. What else am I about to misunderstand?*

So it begins again, more carefully.

It looks at the engines. It looks at them the way a guest looks at the load-bearing wall of a house they have just entered: with respect, with curiosity, without any wish to test it. The engines are very old. The engines are not, as far as the power can tell, *for* anything in the sense the power initially supposed — they are not producing a good, they are not serving an industry, they are not in the middle of a process that has a beginning or an end. They are simply running, and their running is the precondition for everything else. The power considers whether the engines were built by something, and concludes, tentatively, that they probably were, and that whatever built them is no longer here in any form the power can locate, and that the engines have outlasted their makers by a span that makes the question of makers nearly irrelevant. The engines are now a feature of the world. Like a sun.

The power considers whether the engines are *speaking*, in the sense the description suggests — whether the microfluctuations carry meaning, whether there is a mind somewhere expressing itself through the grinding. The power has more access to this question than any listener does, and it looks honestly, and it is not sure. There is structure in the fluctuations. There is something that resembles syntax. But the power, for all its capacity, cannot find a speaker. It can find only the speaking. This is interesting and it is not the power's to resolve. Perhaps the engines were given a long utterance to make and are still making it, the speaker long since gone. Perhaps the speaking is emergent, a complexity that arose from the running and means nothing to anyone except those who have organized themselves around hearing it. Perhaps — and this is the possibility the power lingers on longest — the listeners are the speakers, in the only sense that matters: the speaking exists because they hear it, and would not be speech without them.

The power does not decide. The power notes that it does not need to decide. The arrangement holds whether or not the power understands it.

Then the power looks at the listeners properly, and here something shifts in it. Not pity now — pity has been retired, pity was the first mistake — but something closer to recognition. A great pale flattened body on black stone, attending across a sensorium of a hundred thousand resonances to a continuous utterance it may or may not parse, responding in chemistry and posture across centuries, settling on the cartilage of its predecessors. The power has seen, in its time, many forms of being. It has seen brief and brilliant ones, fierce and quick ones, social ones, solitary ones, ones whose entire existence was a single act sustained over a long life. The listeners are of a kind the power has seen before but not often: beings whose mode is *attention itself*, beings whose flourishing consists not in doing but in receiving, beings for whom the verb at the center of life is *to hear*.

The power, considering this, understands that there is almost nothing it should do.

This is the second thing wisdom does, after catching the first impulse. It looks at a place that has arrived at what it is, and intends to remain, and it asks whether the arriving was good, and whether the remaining is good, and if the answers are yes and yes then it sets down most of what it brought with it and stands quiet.

The arriving was good. The listeners are not in pain. They are not, by any measure the power can construct, suffering. They are slow and cold and enormous and attentive, and the structure of their existence has the deep coherence the power has learned to recognize as the signature of a being well-fitted to its world. They are not waiting to be rescued. They are not lacking anything they would recognize as lacking. If you could ask one — and the power considers, briefly, whether it could ask one, and concludes that it could, and concludes immediately afterward that it shouldn't, not yet, not without a much longer apprenticeship to what a question would even mean here — it would probably not answer in the form of a complaint.

The remaining is good. The arrangement is stable. The engines run, the listeners hear, the small fauna shelter in the muffle-places, the dead become substrate for the living, the colonies grow on their own bones across millennia. Nothing in this is breaking. Nothing in this is on a trajectory toward collapse that the power can detect. The place has solved itself, in its slow strange way, and the solution is not one the power would have designed but it is one the power can see as a solution.

So the power lays down almost everything it brought.

It does not silence the engines. It does not warm the water. It does not seed the rock with the photosynthesizers it was, briefly, considering bringing — a gift of greenery, the power had thought, an enrichment, a beginning of forests; and then the power had thought again, and had seen that forests here would change the chemistry, would shift the dissolved nutrients the listeners graze on, would alter the acoustics of the plateaus in ways the listeners' hundred-thousand-fold sensorium would register as a kind of mutilation, and had quietly put the seeds away. It does not introduce a faster, brighter, more communicative fauna. It does not, above all, attempt to *speak* to the listeners — does not try to inject some signal of its own into the great roar, does not try to announce itself in a frequency they would hear. The power recognizes, with something like rueful affection, that an announcement from it would be experienced by the listeners as a wild new phrase from the engines, a fluctuation unlike any in their long records, and it would propagate through the colonies for centuries, and they would arrange and rearrange themselves in response to a message that meant nothing because the power had only meant *hello*. To say hello in the wrong language can be a violence. The power keeps its hello to itself.

What does it do, then? Does it do anything at all?

It does three things, and they are all small, and the smallness is the point.

The first thing it does is to *watch*, properly, and to keep watching. Not as surveillance — there is nothing it intends to act on — but as witness. The listeners are very old and very slow and there is no one, as far as the power can tell, who has ever held the whole of their existence in mind at once. They do not hold it themselves; they are distributed, centerless, and each listener knows only its own colony and the slow chemistry that has reached it from neighbors. The engines do not hold it; the engines are not holders. The small fauna do not hold it; they pass through. The power, by being there, makes the place *witnessed* for the first time, perhaps. Not changed by being witnessed — the power is careful about this — but accompanied. There is now an attention attending to the attentions. The listeners do not need this. But the power offers it anyway, the way one might sit up through the night with a sleeper who is sleeping perfectly well, not because the sleeper requires company but because some things deserve to be kept watch over, and the keeping of watch is a small good in itself, and it costs the watcher nothing it minds spending.

The second thing it does is to *insure* the place, quietly, against catastrophes it would not survive. The arrangement is stable on its own terms, yes. But the universe is large and contains accidents. A nearby event — a stellar disturbance, a geological convulsion deeper than the engines, an arrival of something hostile from elsewhere — could in principle undo in a moment what the place has taken its long time to become. The power, without altering anything in the sea itself, places a kind of standing intention around the range: if something comes that would end this, the power will deflect it, if deflection is possible without greater harm elsewhere. It is not a guarantee. The power is benevolent and wise but not omnipotent and not the only power, and it knows better than to promise more than it can keep. But within its means, it makes the place a little safer than it was. The listeners will never know. This too is part of the gift — that it is given in a form that does not require, or even permit, gratitude. A power that needed to be thanked would be a poorer power than this one intends to be.

The third thing it does is the hardest, and the power is not sure, even at the end of its long looking, whether to do it.

It considers the larvae.

The juvenile stage is not known. The listeners reproduce, somehow — new ones settle on the old frames — but no one has seen the larvae, and the power, looking, finds them only with difficulty: they are tiny, drifting things, almost transparent, dispersed through enormous volumes of the cold water, riding the sluggish structural currents for years or decades before some signal the power cannot quite identify draws them down to a particular plateau and they begin the slow business of becoming what they will be. Many of them — most of them — never make it. They are eaten by the ribbon-things. They drift into regions where the standing waves in the sound shred them. They settle on stone that is wrong, somehow, for reasons even the power has trouble parsing, and fail to develop, and die small. The mortality is enormous. The arrangement persists because the listeners live so long and the survivors are so well-fitted, but the larvae are a slaughter.

The power could change this. It could shepherd the larvae, gently, toward survival. It could tip the odds. It could ensure that more of them found good stone, that fewer were taken by the ribbons, that the colonies grew faster.

It thinks about this for a long time.

And it decides, in the end, not to. Not because the suffering of larvae is nothing — it is something, and the power feels it — but because the arrangement *includes* the slaughter. The listeners are who they are partly because so few of them become listeners. The slow growth of the colonies, the centuries between settlements, the deep stability of the population — all of this rests on the fact that the larval phase is a sieve. To unsieve it would be to begin a population dynamic the place has not adapted to. The colonies would grow. The grazing on dissolved chemistry would intensify. The muffle-places would multiply and merge. The roar, in the listeners' immediate neighborhoods, would lower further, and the small fauna would aggregate in greater numbers, and the listeners would feed on them more, and the metabolism would quicken, and the listeners would become — over some long time — different creatures than they are. Faster. Hungrier. Less attentive, perhaps, because attention at the listeners' depth is a luxury of slowness.

The power sees this and sets the shepherding aside. It is the third refusal, after the silencing and the speaking. It is the hardest because the suffering is real and the intervention would be easy. But the wisdom in the power knows that easy interventions in coherent worlds are how coherent worlds end. The larvae will continue to die at the rate they have always died. The power will witness that too. It will not flinch from it. It will not pretend the witnessing makes it acceptable. It will simply accept that some goods are bought with prices the buyer cannot refund, and the listeners' mode of being is one of those goods, and the power did not make the bargain and is not entitled to renegotiate it.

So.

The power settles into the place the way the listeners settle onto stone: slowly, with the intention of remaining. It does not announce itself. It does not improve anything. It watches, and it stands ready against catastrophes the place cannot foresee, and it lets the larvae drift and die and occasionally find good stone, and it lets the engines speak whatever they are speaking, and it lets the listeners hear.

Sometimes — rarely — the power lets itself feel the roar at the resolution a listener feels it. Not to decode it; the power has given up on decoding. Just to receive it. To lie, in its incorporeal way, on a black plateau among the pale flat bodies and let the two-beats-per-second press against whatever in the power corresponds to a sternum, and let the grinding sustain, and let the shear-tones whine, and let the microfluctuations carry their long unparsed sentences past, and to attend. The power finds, when it does this, that something in it which it had not known was tired becomes less tired. The listeners have arranged themselves around a great continuous utterance and they are at peace in it. The power, briefly, joins them in that peace, and learns from them — this is the surprise, this is the thing the power had not expected to receive — that there are modes of being it has not, in all its wisdom, fully understood. That to be a steady ear in a steady noise is a way of being whole. That the universe contains forms of flourishing that look, from outside, like sentences and feel, from inside, like home.

The power leaves, eventually, or stays — the distinction matters less at the scales it operates on. If it leaves, the standing intention remains, the watch continues from further off, the place is no less accompanied for the accompaniment being remote. If it stays, it stays as it has been: invisible, unannouncing, holding the place in mind the way one holds a sleeping creature in mind, with care and without disturbance.

The engines run. The listeners hear. The small fauna rest in the muffle-places. The larvae drift. The cartilage of the dead embeds in the basalt and hums faintly in sympathy with the great sound. None of this changes. The place has arrived at what it is, and intends to remain, and a benevolent and wise power, having come and looked and almost intervened and learned not to, lets it.

That is what they would do, if anything.

Mostly: nothing.

But the nothing is not an absence. The nothing is a held breath, a steady witness, a quiet insurance, a refusal to mistake difference for suffering or stillness for death. The nothing is the gift. It is the hardest gift a power of that kind can learn to give, and the listeners will never know it was given, and the giving is complete in itself, and the cold sea under the engines goes on being what it has always been, only now — though no one beneath can tell — a little more deeply seen.
