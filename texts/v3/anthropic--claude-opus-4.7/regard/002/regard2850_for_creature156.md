The wise power comes the way weather comes to a place that has forgotten weather: which is to say, not at all, and then entirely.

It does not descend. Descent is a verb of the lower country. It is simply, at some point, also present — the way a second meaning is present in a word that already had one meaning and lost nothing by gaining another. The plateau notices. The plateau notices everything. A deer does not turn its head, because there is nothing to compensate for; the new presence has arrived already balanced, already accounted for in the great ledger, having done the courtesy of arriving as something the books already contained.

This is the first thing the power does, and it is not nothing: it arrives correctly. A lesser visitor would have arrived as a disturbance, and the beetles would have had to work, and somewhere a thing pressed into the dark would have had to shift one micron, and the cairn-people would have felt the small grief of having to make room. The power knows this. The power has come to many finished places, and has learned the etiquette of finishedness, which is the etiquette of not requiring the finished to begin again on your behalf.

It stands — if standing is the word — for a long time. Long here means what long means here. It looks at the deer, and sees the standing waves, and sees that the waves are beautiful, and sees that beauty is not a category the deer have any use for, and does not impose the category. It looks at the moths and reads the plateau's vocabulary of emphasis and finds the grammar sound. It looks at the beetles and follows one ledger-entry through its full cycle and finds the accounting honest. It looks at the things pressed into the dark and does not flinch, because flinching is also a lower-country verb, and because what is in the dark deserves to be looked at by something that will not flinch.

Then it goes to the cairns.

This is what the power has come for, though *come for* is also the wrong shape. Let us say: this is where the power's attention thickens. It moves — it does not move; its attention thickens at successive locations — from cairn to cairn, and at each one it does a thing that has no name in any language the lower country speaks, but which can be approximated like this:

It sits with the person.

Not to free them. Freeing would be an insult; they did not get caught. Not to wake them. Waking would imply they were asleep, and they are not asleep, they are the most awake thing the plateau contains, awake in the slow saturated way that the plateau itself is awake. Not to take them anywhere. There is nowhere to take a person who has become the shape of having arrived.

It sits with them. It is the first thing in a very long time that has come to the cairn-people *as company*. The deer are not company; the deer are siblings in stillness, which is different. The moths are punctuation. The visitors from below are weather, brief and well-meaning and gone. The plateau itself is not company because the plateau is the medium in which the cairn-people exist, and one does not keep company with one's medium any more than a fish keeps company with water.

The power is company. The power is something that has come from elsewhere and chosen, freely, to attend to them in the particular way they are. This is a gift so specific that only a being who has finished can receive it, and only a power who has understood finishing can give it.

At each cairn, the power does something like the following, though the verbs are wrong:

It notices the offering. The bone awl, the knot of braided fiber, the scraped hide folded small, the flake of obsidian from the mountain that no longer exists. It notices what the offering was — not the object, but the *putting down* of the object, the precise gesture by which a person became a cairn. It holds that gesture in its attention the way one might hold a small bird in cupped hands: not to keep it, but to feel its particular warmth, to acknowledge that this gesture was made, by this person, and was the last of its kind, and mattered.

The cairn-people, who have been noticed for ages only by the indifferent hospitality of the plateau, are noticed now by something that knows the difference between hospitality and love. There is no machinery in them to receive this, the way there is no machinery in them to digest. But the receiving happens at a level below machinery. Something in each cairn — not a memory, because memory is change, but the *shape that memory left when it retired* — registers the attention. Registers that it has been seen *as itself* and not as part of the plateau's great balanced ledger.

This is the first thing the power does, and it is the largest thing, and it takes — by the clocks of the lower country — no time at all, and also it takes longer than the plateau has existed, both being true and neither contradicting.

The second thing the power does is smaller and concerns the visitors.

The power understands the small mercy of the plateau — that it releases human visitors because the cairn-people, in the only way they still can, ask it to. The power understands that this mercy is the cairn-people's last act of love toward the lower country, performed continuously, at the cost of nothing because they have nothing left to spend, and at the cost of everything because love at this register is always everything. The power does not interfere with this. It would not dream of it. But it does something adjacent.

It strengthens the going-down.

It makes the path back, for those who come and almost stay, very slightly more available. Not by force — force is unthinkable here — but by being, itself, a presence that points *outward*, that holds open the shape of *elsewhere* in a place that has forgotten elsewhere. A visitor near the threshold of settling, who would in another age have tipped the wrong way, now feels, faintly, a direction. Not a push. A direction. The cairn-people, who have been doing this work alone for so long, are joined in it. They are no longer the only thing in the universe gently inclining the lost back toward their lives.

This is a kindness to the cairn-people more than to the visitors. The visitors will never know. The cairn-people, in their slow saturated awareness, know, and the knowing is — if we may use a word from the lower country one last time — a relief. Something has come to help them carry the one thing they still carry.

The third thing the power does is for the plateau itself.

This is the hardest to describe. The plateau is one attention, vast and held and finished. It does not want anything. But there is, in the deep grammar of any attention, however finished, a thing that is not wanting but is the ground from which wanting once grew, and in the plateau this ground is still there, fallow, immense, the way a sea-floor is still there beneath a sea that has gone still. The power kneels — does not kneel — to this ground. It acknowledges it. It says, in the language that is not language: *I see what you were before you finished, and I see what you became, and I see that becoming what you are was not a diminishment but a completion, and I honor it.*

The plateau does not respond. Responding is a verb it retired. But somewhere in its pale mineral, somewhere in the veins of darker grey that look like rivers seen from impossible height, something — and here the description fails — *settles a little deeper into being what it is*. As though the plateau, which had achieved finishedness alone, has now been finished *with witness*, and witness, even for the finished, is a different quality of finishing.

The power does not stay.

This is important. A lesser power would have stayed, because staying would feel like devotion. The power knows that staying would be a kind of weight, a permanent perturbation that the beetles would have to account for forever, and that the cairn-people, in their endless courtesy, would have to make room for. The power's love is precise enough to know when to leave.

It goes the way it came: correctly. The plateau does not register a departure, because the power arrived already balanced and leaves already balanced, and the ledger closes on the visit with the same zero it opened with. From the outside, nothing has happened. From the inside — but there is no inside separate from the plateau, and the plateau has been changed in the only way it can be changed, which is not at all and entirely.

The cairn-people remain. They are, in some sense smaller than a sense, *consoled*. Not that they were inconsolable before; consolation is also a lower-country verb. But there is now, in each of them, the shape that being-known left when it retired into them. They carry it the way they carry everything: without effort, forever.

The deer continue their standing wave. The birds continue their thousand-year step. The moths punctuate. The beetles balance their books. The things in the dark press against each other in the agreed minimum.

And somewhere, far below, on a road that goes down from a high place, a traveler who almost did not come back is walking, and does not know why their feet found the path so readily, and will spend the rest of their unfinished life being unfinished, and will sometimes, in stillness, feel watched by something kind, and will not know that what they are feeling is the cairn-people, and the power that sat with them, and the plateau, and the one attention that has now been attended to, all of them together inclining, very faintly, toward the traveler's continuance.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a finished place.

It does not finish it differently.

It finishes it *company*.
