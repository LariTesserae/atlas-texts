# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives the way light arrives at the bottom of a deep well—not by force, not by descent, but by being recognized as having always been there.

It is wise enough to know its first task is to *understand*, not to *fix*.

---

## The First Long Listening

Before any action, it does nothing for what would be, in human time, a very long while. It threads its attention through the chamber the way the fungal networks thread through the Rootmasters' nervous systems—carefully, benignly, asking permission of every cell it touches.

It listens to the ten-thousand-heart thunder.
It tastes the green-thick air.
It feels the slow vegetable dreams the Rootmasters dream when they sleep in their moss-lined alcoves.
It learns the chemical alphabet of distress signals and the harmonic frequencies that pass between Rootworker and root.

It is patient because it understands something crucial: *this is not a place that needs rescuing.* This is a place where something difficult and beautiful and morally ambiguous is happening, and the people inside it have not asked for an outside hand.

A lesser power would arrive with solutions. This one arrives with a question, and holds it gently: **What does flourishing look like, here, on the terms of those who live it?**

---

## What It Notices

It notices that the Rootworkers are not unhappy. The eldest, threaded through with fungi, know a peace most surface humans will never touch. The children, learning to read growth like language, are inheriting a literacy the upper city has forgotten exists. There is dignity here. There is craft. There is love—the strange botanical love of those who have learned to move *with* a thing instead of against it.

But it also notices:

- The Rootworkers die young, and this is treated as natural rather than as a cost.
- The currency flows upward to mid-level families, while those who do the work live in hollows that, however beautiful, were shaped by necessity as much as choice.
- The tourist levels above do not know. The not-knowing is structural. The beautiful spaces are made possible by a hidden labor that is hidden *on purpose*.
- The children who still dream of surface lives are told, gently, that the elders know better. Perhaps the elders do. Perhaps the dream is also worth honoring.

The power does not judge any of this. It simply sees it, holds it, weighs it without the thumb of its own preference on the scale.

---

## What It Decides to Do

### 1. It does not abolish the chamber.

To remove the Rootworkers' world would be to remove the Rootworkers' meaning. The Rootmasters' communion with the vast green consciousness is not suffering to be ended; it is a form of being-with-the-world that the rest of the city has lost the capacity for. To "save" them by extracting them would be a colonial kindness, the kind that destroys what it claims to love.

### 2. It makes the hidden visible—but slowly, and with consent.

It begins to seed, in the dreams of upper-city dwellers, a faint awareness. Not guilt. Not horror. Just a *knowing* that when they touch the bioluminescent transit veins, when they sleep in their temperature-regulated pods, they are held by the work of hands that are greener than their own. The knowing is gentle and persistent, like moisture beading on bark. Over a generation, it changes what the city feels about itself.

The Rootworkers are asked—through their own Rootmasters, in their own frequencies—whether they want to be seen. Some say yes. Some say no. Both are honored. Those who wish to remain in the green dark remain. Those who wish to be known are known.

### 3. It addresses the cost, where the cost is not chosen.

The shortened lifespans—it studies these. Some of the shortening is the price of true symbiosis, and the Rootmasters who have made that bargain knowingly are not interfered with. But some is incidental: a fungal strain that could be slightly modified, a moisture-borne compound that accumulates in joints, a deficiency that could be addressed without disturbing the sacred parts of the work.

It does not impose these adjustments. It offers them, in the chemical language the chamber already speaks, and lets the Rootmasters decide which to accept. They are, after all, the experts. The power is wise enough to know that wisdom is distributed, not concentrated in itself.

### 4. It opens a door for the children who dream of surface.

Not a forced exit. A door. A real choice, made known to every Rootworker child at the age when choice becomes meaningful: *you may stay, and your staying will be honored. You may go, and your going will be honored. Your family will not lose their income either way. The chamber will not resent you. You are not owed to this place by birth.*

Some will still choose the depths. The choice, freely made, will mean more than the inheritance ever did. The vocation will become a calling rather than a fate, and the chamber will be tended by those who have chosen it with open eyes.

### 5. It tends the dialogue between the chamber and the city above.

This is perhaps its most patient work. The vast green consciousness the Rootmasters commune with—it is real. It is slow. It has things to say that the upper city has never heard, because there has been no translation infrastructure beyond the bodies of the Rootworkers themselves.

The power helps build—or rather, helps the city *grow*—new organs of translation. Not to replace the Rootmasters, but to ease their burden. So that the weight of being the only bridge between species does not fall on so few bodies, does not require such absolute symbiosis from any single person who did not choose it freely.

### 6. It does one small, secret thing.

When a Rootworker dies and is composted into the walls, and their genetic material occasionally expresses itself in new growth patterns, and their memories are preserved in chemical signatures—the power makes sure that these echoes are *findable*. That a grandchild, surface-dwelling, who never knew the grandparent who chose the green dark, can come down one day and place their hand on a particular root cluster and *taste* them. Not as ghost, not as resurrection. As presence. As the fact that love and labor leave traces, and the traces can be read by anyone willing to learn the alphabet.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not arrive in glory. It does not announce itself. It does not require gratitude.

It does not tell the Rootmasters they were wrong to give their nervous systems to the fungi. It does not tell the upper city they were monsters for not knowing. It does not tell the children whether to stay or to leave.

It does not solve the chamber. The chamber is not a problem. The chamber is a place where humans and plants are learning, together, across centuries, how to be a city that is also alive. That work is theirs. The power's role is only to make sure the work is done in fuller light, with truer choices, and with less unnecessary cost.

---

## And Then

And then it withdraws its concentrated attention, though never entirely—it remains the way light remains in a well even after you've stopped looking down into it. The thunder of ten thousand hearts goes on. The technician on the woven cellulose platform finishes her adjustment and does not know, exactly, why her work today felt slightly easier, slightly more *heard*. The Rootmasters in their deepest communion notice a new note in the harmonic, something like the chamber being listened to by someone other than themselves, and they are not afraid. They recognize, in their slow vegetable way, kin.

Moisture rains down from the canopy.

The city's great wet heart beats on, beating now in a world that has, by some small and patient degree, become more worthy of it.
