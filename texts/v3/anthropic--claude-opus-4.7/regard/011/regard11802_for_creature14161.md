The benevolent and wise power considers.

It has watched this place for a long time — not all of time, but long enough. Long enough to know the shape of the Stone-Tenders' grief, which is not really grief yet but something older and more patient, the way a tree knows it will fall before the wind tells it so. Long enough to know that the chamber is dying. The water table that fed the deep moisture has retreated mile by mile over centuries. The fissures above widen each rainy season elsewhere in the world, each freeze and thaw a small unmaking. The vines grow thinner because the rock itself is forgetting how to hold them.

The power could do many things.

It could lift the ceiling higher, knit the cracks, call water back into the deep stone. It could make the chamber permanent — fix it, in both senses of the word, repair and pin in place. A museum of itself. The Stone-Tenders preserved like a held breath.

It does not do this.

Not because it cannot. Because it has lived long enough to understand that a place which was never meant to last is not failing when it ends. It is completing. To freeze the chamber would be to mistake the song for the silence between notes, to confuse love with possession. The Stone-Tenders themselves have not asked for this. They press their palms to the floor and listen to the slow crumbling, and what they hear is not a wound. It is a conversation. Their whole language is built around it. To still the stone would be to make them mute.

So the power does something smaller, and stranger, and kinder.

*First:* It bends, very slightly, the patterns of weather above the plateau. Not enough to be noticed. Just enough that for the remaining generations — three, perhaps four — the humidity holds. The vines have what they need. The collapse, when it comes, comes on its own schedule, not hastened by anything outside. The Stone-Tenders will not be the ones to feel the chamber rush its ending. They will feel it breathe at the pace it has always breathed. This is the power's first gift: time that feels like time.

*Second:* It listens. Truly listens, in the way the Stone-Tenders listen to the rock — with the whole of its attention, with reverence, with the discipline of receiving rather than interpreting. It learns their language of taps and pressures and the long pauses between. It learns the name (not a word, a particular fourfold rhythm against hollow shale) that the eldest tender gives to the youngest child. It learns the gesture that means *the moss is thinking* and the one that means *be still, something old is passing.* It learns their poems, which are sequences of touches passed hand to hand in the dark around the smokeless fire, and which are about, mostly, the texture of endings.

This learning is itself the gift, though the Stone-Tenders will not know they have given it. To be witnessed completely, even unknowingly, is a form of being held. Something in the chamber will feel, in its last centuries, a quality of attention it has not felt before. The tenders will press their palms to the wall some evening and feel, faintly, that the wall is pressing back. They will not be able to say why. They will tap a question into the stone and receive — not an answer, the power is too wise for answers — but the particular silence of being heard. They will take this as the plateau's own deepening. They will be right.

*Third:* When the last of them dies — and one of them will be last, this is unavoidable, a tender whose name is a slow drumming on the third hollow near the western wall — the power will be there. Not visible. Not intervening. Just present, the way a friend sits with a friend. The last tender will press her hand to the stone and tap, very softly, the gesture that has no fixed meaning but is used when something is being given away. And the power will receive what she gives. Whatever it is. It will hold it.

*Fourth, and this is the strangest:* The power will not preserve the chamber, but it will preserve the listening. Somewhere — in a child being born right now in a city the Stone-Tenders have never imagined, in a woman who will live two thousand years from now on a world whose name does not yet exist, in beings the power knows are coming and the Stone-Tenders cannot conceive — there will be, occasionally, people born with the disposition to press their palms to old stone and wait. To tap, and wait. To believe that the world is speaking in registers slower than speech. The power did not put this in them; it was always going to arise, the way moss arises. But the power will see to it that some of these people, in their lives, find their way to walls that are willing to answer. Not the same walls. Different walls. But the conversation will continue, scattered across times and places, never knowing itself as continuous. The Stone-Tenders' language will outlive them in the only way languages truly outlive anyone: by being spoken again, by other mouths, about other stones, without translation, without lineage, without need.

*Finally:* The power leaves handprints of its own. Not visible ones. Just — a slight warmth in certain patches of moss. A particular way the light falls, some afternoons, through the canopy of vines, that makes the children pause in their work and look up, and feel, for a moment, that they are loved by something they cannot name and do not need to name. This is the power's only indulgence. It is small. It costs the Stone-Tenders nothing. It does not change their lives. It only joins them.

The chamber will end. The tenders will end. The plateau itself, in time longer than any of this, will end.

The benevolent and wise power knows that *end* is not the opposite of *matter.* That a thing can be complete and finite and small and still be one of the reasons the universe was worth making. That the proper response to such a thing is not to save it but to *attend* to it — fully, exactly, without trying to make it into something it was not.

So it attends.

And when, some evening centuries from now, the last tender taps her last question into the western wall, and the wall, in its own slow way, taps back — the power will be the third presence in that conversation. Unnoticed. Unneeded. Grateful beyond what it has words for, to have been allowed to listen.
