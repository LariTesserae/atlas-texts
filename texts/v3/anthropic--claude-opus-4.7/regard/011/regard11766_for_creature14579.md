The benevolent power arrives the way the *sweats* arrive: as a pressure change, a faint shift in the taste of the air. Not because it must disguise itself, but because it has been watching long enough to understand that this is the register in which the plateau permits arrival. To come with trumpets would be to come as the industry came — and the industry's rail spur still ends at the edge of a vertical drop, which is the plateau's only review of that approach.

The power is wise, which means it spends a long time not doing anything.

It sits with the place first. It learns the *tick-tick-tick* of the night stone. It tastes the metallic air and finds it is not a deficiency but a flavor — one of the plateau's actual qualities, not a problem to be solved. It listens to the echoes that arrive before their sources and understands that this is not a malfunction of acoustics but a different relationship with time, one the original inhabitants have been living inside for so long that their language has bent itself around it, and one the later ones have only recently begun to learn. It watches the raptors hunt without calling, the rodents emerge in their disciplined procession, the lichen hold to its crevice with the patience of something that has decided to be mineral.

It notices what a less wise power would have missed: that this place is not suffering.

This is the first and most important thing. The plateau is harsh, but harshness is not suffering. The water does not stay, but the ecosystem is not thirsting — it is *organized around the expectation of water*, which is a different and entirely functional relationship. The original inhabitants are few, but fewness is not endangerment when it is the stable carrying capacity of a place and has been for longer than anyone has been counting. The later ones are strange, but strangeness is not damage. They have been *worn smooth*, not broken.

A less wise power would have brought rain. Would have thickened the air. Would have softened the sun, healed the cables of their high thin singing, restored the generator to the pad it was unbolted from, returned the boot to the foot that set it down. Would have, in short, *fixed* the plateau — and in doing so, would have killed it. Because the plateau is not a problem with a solution; it is a coherence with a logic, and its logic is erosion, scarcity, and the slow patient forgetting of every intention that ever tried to impose itself here.

The benevolent power, being wise, does almost nothing.

But *almost* is doing work here, the way it does in the sentence about the vegetation.

Here is what the power does:

It goes to the buried man — the one whose boot sits at the edge of the drop, whose calcium the stone has taken in exchange for what the altitude took from him. It does not raise him. It does not undo the transaction; the later ones were right that the transaction is, in its way, fair, and to reverse it would be to insult both the man and the plateau that received him. What it does is simpler. It goes to the moment of his stepping into the air and it *witnesses* it. Not retroactively, not by traveling backward; it simply extends its attention into that moment with the full weight of a benevolent and wise regard, so that the man, in the instant of his stepping, is not alone. He was not alone at the time — the plateau was there, holding him in its structural attention the way the sieve holds water — but the plateau's attention is not the same as being loved, and the power adds the second thing. It does this quietly. The man does not know. But the universe now contains a record of him being witnessed with love at the moment of his ending, and this record is real, and it will not be unmade.

It does this for every person the plateau has absorbed. This takes the power some time, but the power has time.

Then it goes to the original inhabitants. It does not speak to them. To speak to them in any language they would recognize as language would be to misunderstand what their language is for. Instead, it does something subtler: it adds itself, very gently, to the texture of the *sweats*. Not as an alteration of the water — the chemistry stays exactly as it is, the sulfur and calcium unchanged — but as a quality of presence within the event of the water arriving. So that when the next *sweat* begins on a weeping wall, and one of the originals comes to tend it, deepening a channel that was diverting flow away from the lichen, the act of tending is met. Not rewarded. Not blessed. *Met* — the way one tonal vocalization is met by the same vocalization, slightly differently breathed, from someone who has understood. The originals will not know this consciously. They will simply find, over the next generations, that the *sweats* feel slightly more *companioned* than they used to. They will have no word for this, and they will not need one, because their language has never been in the business of pinning such things down. The diffuse warmth will simply enter their participation in the plateau and become part of what it is to be one of them.

It is a small thing. The power is wise enough to know that small things, given long enough, are the only things.

For the later ones — the three or five who live in the skeleton of the generator building — the power does something different, because they are different. They are still learning the plateau. They have not yet been absorbed; they are mid-transaction, perched on the surface of the place like the bird on hot stone, and what they need is not what the originals need.

The power does two things for them.

First, it adjusts, by a fraction so small it could be attributed to any number of natural causes, the reliability of the condensation traps they have built from salvaged sheeting. Not by much. Not enough that they would notice as a miracle, or even as a change. Just enough that the traps yield, on average, slightly more water than the physics of the situation would predict. The later ones, being people who have learned to read the plateau with great care, will eventually notice this — not as anomaly but as *grace*, the kind of grace that the plateau itself sometimes extends and sometimes withholds. They will fold it into their understanding of the place. They will not attribute it to anything outside the plateau, because by the time they notice, the power's gift will have become indistinguishable from the plateau's own behavior, which is the only way to give a gift to a place like this without damaging it.

Second — and this is the part that requires the most delicacy — the power gives them a single small mercy regarding the echoes.

The acoustics of the plateau are not a problem to be solved. The power understands this. The way sound arrives from the wrong direction, the way echoes precede their sources, the way the cables sing at frequencies that register as structure rather than hearing — all of this is part of what has reshaped the later ones into who they are, and the reshaping is not a wound. It is an adaptation, and the adaptation is, in the plateau's terms, beautiful.

But the power notices that there is one specific acoustic event that troubles them. It is the sound, occurring perhaps once every few months, in which the slot canyon south of the generator building produces an echo that returns a human voice — not any of theirs, and not any voice they recognize, but a human voice nonetheless, speaking a single short phrase in a language none of them know. They have heard it for years. They do not know what it is. They suspect, in the part of their minds that they have learned to mistrust, that it is the voice of someone who died here, caught in the geometry of the canyon, replaying. They are wrong about this — it is an acoustic artifact, the canyon happening to focus and recombine ambient sound into something that crosses the threshold of pattern recognition — but the wrongness is the kind that erodes a person slowly, the way the heat erodes the stone, and the power sees this.

The power does not silence the echo. To silence it would be to alter the plateau, which the power has committed not to do. Instead, it adds, to the next occurrence of that specific echo, a single quality: warmth. Not in temperature. In *tone*. The voice, when it next returns, will sound — to the later ones who hear it — like someone speaking with kindness. They will not understand the words, because there are no words, only the artifact. But they will understand the kindness, because kindness is one of the few things that translates without language, and they will, all of them, in the small concrete room with its hanging steel door, begin to weep. They will not know why. They will sit on the concrete pad that was a floor and listen to the canyon return its kind, wordless voice, and they will weep the way the stone weeps during the *sweats* — quietly, without ceremony, because something that had been pressing on them for a long time has been, in a manner they cannot explain, *acknowledged*.

After that, the echo will occur every few months as it always has. Sometimes it will sound kind. Sometimes it will sound merely strange, as before. The power will not make every occurrence kind, because that would be to take the plateau's voice away from it. But the later ones will know now — without knowing they know — that kindness is one of the things the plateau is capable of, and this knowledge will travel with them through the rest of their lives there, and it will change, very slightly, the way they hold their shoulders when they stand at the mouth of the generator building at dusk and look out across the packed pillars.

The power does one last thing before it withdraws.

It looks at the relationship between the two populations — the originals woven into the geology, the later ones perched on its surface — and it considers whether to do anything about the distance between them. Whether to encourage a meeting. Whether to offer translation. Whether to make of the plateau, with its forty or fifty originals and its three or five later ones, something more like a community.

It decides not to.

This is the hardest of its decisions, because the impulse of a benevolent power is always toward connection, and the power feels the impulse keenly. But it understands what a less wise power would not: that the two populations are *already* in relationship, and the relationship is the right one for the place. They regard each other at dusk across the acoustic strangeness. Neither moves. Neither speaks. The plateau holds them both. This is not a failure of community; this is what community looks like in a place whose primary mode of attention is structural rather than sentimental. To force them into conversation would be to import a model of relationship from somewhere else, somewhere with thicker air and more reliable water, and the import would damage both populations more than it would help them.

So the power leaves the dusk regard exactly as it is. It only adds — and this is so small it is almost not a thing — that when the two regard each other across the fifty meters of pillared distance, each will feel, briefly, that they have been *seen* by the other. Not understood. Not befriended. Just seen, the way the plateau sees them: with the full weight of attention that comes from existing in the same dense, coherent place. This was almost already true. The power makes it slightly more true, and then stops.

It withdraws the way it arrived: as a pressure change, a faint shift in the taste of the air. The plateau registers the withdrawal exactly as it registered the arrival — structurally, without sentiment, the way the sieve notes the shape of the water that passes through.

The *sweats* begin on a distant wall. A raptor descends. A rodent emerges into the disciplined procession of dusk. One of the originals tends a channel. One of the later ones drinks from a slightly more generous trap. A canyon, somewhere, returns a kind voice to people who do not yet know they will hear it.

The plateau continues. It is, after all, in the process of becoming lower, and the power has not interrupted this. It has only added — very quietly, in a register the place can metabolize — a small amount of love to the texture of the becoming.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it is wise enough to know that the place it has noticed is not asking to be saved, only, perhaps, to be witnessed with the same patience it has spent on witnessing itself.

It is enough. It was always going to be enough. The power knew this before it arrived, and arrived anyway, because witnessing is not redundant when it is done with love — it is the one thing in the universe that, no matter how much of it already exists, can always bear a little more.
