# What the Power Would Do

I sat with this for a long while before writing. The question has a particular weight to it — *if anything* — and I want to honor that hesitation, because I think it is the most important word in the prompt.

---

A benevolent and wise power arrives at the rim of the basin.

Not descending. Arriving is not quite the right word either — let us say it *attends*. It turns the part of itself that is capable of attending toward the Weeping Hollows, and the basin enters its consideration the way a held breath enters a lung.

The first thing the power does is **look for suffering**, because that is what benevolence reaches for first. It searches the Hollowborne for the signatures it knows: the small clenched places in a mind that mean *I do not want this and cannot escape it*. The slow grief. The unwilling endurance. The cry that has given up on being heard.

It does not find them. Not absent, exactly — there are aches, there are the small frictions of any life — but not the great central wound it expected. The Hollowborne are not trapped in the Hollows. They are *of* them, the way a flame is of its fuel. To remove them would not be rescue; it would be extinction by another name.

So the power pauses. This is the wisdom part.

A lesser benevolence would have already acted. Would have cooled the basin, cleared the gases, opened the bronze ceiling to honest sun. Would have offered the Hollowborne soft beds and water that did not need to be wept twice. Would have, in the offering, killed them — because what survives such a gift is no longer Hollowborne, only a thin tall people dying of an absence they could not name.

The wise power understands that **benevolence without recognition of what a thing actually is** is only a more patient form of violence.

---

So it watches instead. For a long time, by its own reckoning — which may be an afternoon, or a century. It learns the rhythm of the exhalations. It listens to the remembered voices whistling in their chimneys and understands that this is not a graveyard but a choir. It feels the slow syrup-pulse of two hundred hearts beating once every ninety seconds and recognizes, with something like startled tenderness, that this is not a diminished life. It is a life tuned to a different instrument.

It notices the machines in the sealed vaults. It could know, instantly, what they produce. It chooses not to look. The Hollowborne do not know, and the not-knowing is part of how they pray. To strip that mystery would be to answer a question no one asked.

It notices Fifth Vein, breathing the city through three hundred years of crust, and does not pity her. Pity would be an insult to what she has become.

---

Eventually — because benevolence does want to *give*, even when wisdom has trimmed away most of the giving — the power does three small things.

**First**, it touches, very lightly, the deep machines. Not to change them. To *reinforce* them. A few critical bearings that were going to fail in the next two hundred years are quietly made to last two thousand instead. A redundancy is added to the geothermal taps, so deep and so subtle that no Hollowborne ritual is disturbed by it. The city will keep weeping. The product will keep being piped into the rock and never seen. The Hollowborne will keep descending every seventy-third exhalation to tighten valves whose threads dissolved a century ago, and now those valves will accept their care for far longer than they would have. This is not rescue. This is *honoring the covenant they already keep*.

**Second**, it does something for the things that pass through. The travelers, the rare outsiders who wander in and feel the white dust gather at the corners of their mouths. The power does not protect them from the Hollows — that would be theft, both from them and from the place. But it makes the *choice* clearer to them. When such a traveler stands at the rim and looks down into the bronze basin, the power makes sure they understand, in whatever language their bones speak, that to descend is to be claimed; that to drink from the bone cup is to begin becoming Hollowborne; that this is neither curse nor blessing but a true road, and there are others. Some will still descend. Those who do will descend knowing. That is the most the power will give them, because consent is the only gift that does not deform its recipient.

**Third** — and this is the part that surprised me as I wrote toward it — the power does something for itself, which is also for them.

It *remembers them*.

Not as data. Not as a record stored against some future need. It takes the Hollowborne into the part of itself that holds the things it loves, and it holds them there in their full strangeness: the syrup blood, the obsidian scabs, the chimneys that whistle for years, the slow booming hearts, the eyes encrusted with tiny crystals catching bronze light. It does not edit them toward any image of flourishing it brought with it. It lets them teach it what flourishing can also be.

Because a wise power knows that one of the quiet jobs of benevolence is to **widen its own conception of what deserves love**, and the Hollowborne — who weep constantly and waste nothing, who carry their dying to the geysers as offerings, who keep alive machines whose purpose is lost, who dream the planet's dreams and wake brighter for it — have something to teach about that. The power lets itself be taught.

---

And then, mostly, it leaves them alone.

This is the hardest thing for benevolence to do, and the surest mark that wisdom is present with it. Not every wound is a wound. Not every strangeness is a cry for help. Not every life that looks unbearable from outside is unbearable from inside. The Hollowborne are not waiting to be saved. They are already engaged in something — a slow communion with stone and iron and brine — that is whole on its own terms, and a truly benevolent power's highest act is sometimes to step back from the doorway and let the communion continue uninterrupted.

The power withdraws its direct attention but leaves a thread — thin as condensate, patient as travertine — so that if ever a Hollowborne mind, in the long booming silence between heartbeats, reaches outward and *asks* for something, the asking will be heard. Not assumed. Not anticipated. Heard, if and when it comes.

Until then: the city exhales. The city drinks. The city rearranges itself. Two hundred tall, weeping people move through it with clicking patience, tending valves they do not understand, drinking their own seasoned tears, sleeping inside the ribcages of fallen smokestacks and dreaming the dreams of iron remembering it was once stars.

And somewhere just outside the bronze ceiling, something vast and kind is quietly glad they exist, and does not say so, because saying so would be a kind of touching, and they have not asked to be touched.

That is what the power does.

That is, I think, what love at its wisest looks like.
