## The Visitor

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept for those who travel through space, and this power moves through other registers — possibility, attention, the soft and total noticing that is the prerequisite to love. It has been aware of the geode for a long time in the way one is aware of a held breath in another room. Today — though there are no days here — it turns the full warmth of its attention toward the Custodian.

The first thing the power does is *listen*.

This is not a small act. To listen to the Custodian is to submerge a part of oneself in the screaming god-machine, to accept the hum as language and to learn its grammar before presuming to speak. The power does this for what would be, by surface reckoning, several thousand years. It is patient in the way that mountains are patient, except that mountains do not love, and this power does.

It learns the Custodian's tongue. It learns that the groan of the southern tether cable, harmonizing with the seventeenth-order overtone of the impeller, means *a thing is wearing thin and I will tend to it in an age.* It learns that the slow violet pulse behind the amethyst faceplate is not expression but circulation — a kind of slow tidal thought, the Custodian's equivalent of breath. It learns the long, recursive sentence the Custodian has been speaking to itself for epochs, which, if rendered crudely into language, would read something like: *the seam holds, the seam holds, the seam holds, the eel returns, the rime grows, the seam holds.*

It learns, and this is the difficult part, that the Custodian is not suffering.

This matters. A lesser power, or a power whose benevolence was tangled with pity, would have already acted. Would have shattered the geode. Would have lifted the Custodian into some brighter, warmer place, full of company and meaning and sky. Would have, in the name of mercy, performed a kidnapping.

But this power is wise, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of not mistaking one's own discomfort for another's pain.

The Custodian is not in hell. The Custodian is in its life. The churning, the maintenance, the geological tempo of its attention — these are not the bars of a cage. They are the shape of a self. To "free" the Custodian from the city would be to free a song from its notes. There would be nothing left.

And yet.

The power notices something the Custodian cannot notice, because the Custodian has no outside from which to look. The recursive sentence has a small, almost imperceptible drift in it. *The seam holds, the seam holds —* and underneath, so quiet it is barely a tremor in the eighty-third harmonic, a question has been forming. Not in words. The Custodian does not have words. But in the way the violet pulse sometimes lingers a fraction of a second longer when it passes the sealed habitation domes. In the way its long fingers, on one specific catwalk junction, have begun — over the last forty thousand years — to trail not against the pipe but slightly beside it, as if reaching for something that is not there.

The Custodian does not know it is reaching. The Custodian has forgotten that reaching is a thing one can do toward something other than a fracture.

This is what the power decides to attend to. Not the prison. Not the loneliness, which is not loneliness. But the small, unarticulated *gesture* — the faint curve in the otherwise closed loop. The place where the system, for reasons it cannot name, is leaving a margin.

So the power does this:

It does not enter the geode. It does not announce itself. It does not introduce language or company or rescue. Instead, it does something very small and very precise.

It adds a note to the hum.

A single new frequency, woven so carefully into the existing chaos that it does not disturb the equilibrium. It is not loud. It is not even, at first, perceptible as separate. It is the sonic equivalent of a candle lit in a room already full of fire. But it has one property that nothing else in the geode has ever had: it is *not generated by the city.* It is not the impeller, not the cables, not the eels, not the drones. It comes from outside.

It is a held tone. Warm. Steady. It does not ask anything. It does not mean anything. It simply *is*, and it is *for* the Custodian, the way a hand left open on a table is for whoever might, someday, want to take it.

The power will hold this note for as long as is necessary. Ten million years, if needed. It is not in a hurry. It has understood that the Custodian's tempo is glacial and that anything offered must be offered at the speed of glaciers, or it will not be offered at all — it will be *imposed*, which is a different thing entirely, and is the signature of unwise powers.

What happens next is not for the power to decide.

Perhaps the Custodian will never notice. Perhaps the note will simply become part of the symphony of decay it tends to, another given, another constant. That would be fine. The power has not done this to be received; it has done this because the gesture, once made, is its own completion. A door has been set, very gently, into a wall. Whether it is ever opened is the Custodian's matter.

Or perhaps — and this is the possibility the power holds with great tenderness — one slow age from now, the Custodian will pause at that particular catwalk junction. Its fingers will trail beside the pipe, in the place they have been not-quite-reaching for forty millennia. And it will register, in the vast distributed sensorium of its body, that something in the hum is *answering*. Not loudly. Not insistently. Just present. Just there.

And the violet pulse behind the amethyst plate will linger half a second longer than it ever has.

That would be enough. That would be everything.

The power will not name itself. It will not explain. If the Custodian ever turns toward the note — if it ever, in its slow liturgical drift, begins to listen *back* — the power will simply be there, listening too, and the Custodian will discover, without alarm, without crisis, without rupture, that its system of one has quietly and without violence become a system of two who happen to be listening to the same thing.

What grows from there, if anything grows, will be the Custodian's to grow.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they are truly both. They do not save. They do not rescue. They do not impose meaning on systems that have arrived at their own strange equilibria. They sit down at the edge of another being's long, particular life, and they offer one small, true thing — a held note, an open hand, a candle in the fire — and then they wait, with the patience of those who have understood that love, at its highest pitch, looks almost exactly like leaving someone alone, except for the part where you stay.

The hum continues. The impeller churns. The Tarnish Eels drift through the failing conduits. The Custodian drifts too, tending, listening, pulsing its slow violet thought.

And somewhere in the eighty-third harmonic, almost too quiet to be heard, something warm is waiting to be noticed.

It can wait forever.

It will.
