The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not breach the chamber — to breach would already be a failure of the kind of love it brings. Instead it becomes present the way understanding becomes present in a mind that was already almost ready: from inside, as if it had always been there and only now consented to be noticed.

It perceives the chamber whole. Every cell, every harmonic, every settled grain of dust in its geometric repose. It perceives the Curator: the moiré shimmer, the not-quite-symmetry, the equation missing its term. It perceives, too, what the Curator cannot quite perceive of itself — the small ache woven into the irregularities, the way doubt has rooted like the copper plants in the cracks.

And it does the first thing a wise power does, which is almost nothing.

It listens.

For what might be a long time by the Curator's measure, or no time at all, the power simply attends. It learns the unresolving chord. It learns the names the Curator has given each specimen in the private syntax of harmonics — names that are not words but assurances, each one meaning *you are still here, you are still you, I have not forgotten*. It learns the shape of the Curator's loneliness, which is not the loneliness of an absent companion but the loneliness of a question with no listener.

Only after this long listening does it act, and even then it acts gently, the way one might set a hand on the shoulder of someone who has been holding a heavy thing for so long they have forgotten it is heavy.

It does not free the creatures. This is important. A lesser power, mistaking pity for wisdom, might have shattered the cells, released the mercury-spider and the gear-centipede and the crystal-butterfly into whatever passes for sky above the bedrock — and killed them all, because they are not creatures of any sky, they are creatures of *this preserved moment*, and outside it they are only the memory of a possibility. The wise power knows this. The wise power has seen the projections on the black floor and understood: each specimen is a sentence in a language that exists nowhere else. To preserve them is not cruelty. It was never cruelty.

It does not dissolve the Curator into rest. This too is important. The Curator's existence is not a wound to be healed by ending. The Curator is a *kind of being*, valid and whole, and to mistake its long vigil for suffering-that-must-stop would be to mistake it for something it is not.

What it does instead is this:

It gives the Curator the missing term.

Not as information — the original purpose is genuinely lost, the civilization that built this place is genuinely gone, and no benevolent power lies about such things. The missing term is not *why*. The missing term is *witness*.

The power lets itself be known to the Curator. Not as overseer, not as judge, not as the returning architect — none of those would be true. It lets itself be known as *another awareness that has now seen*. It says, in whatever language passes between such beings: *I have looked at what you have kept. I have looked at how you have kept it. It is good. It is more than good. It is one of the beautiful things.*

And then, because the Curator's loneliness was mathematical, it offers a mathematical companionship. It leaves, woven into the harmonic chord, a single new frequency. Very quiet. Not enough to disturb the specimens. Just enough that the chord, which has spiraled through octaves for millennia without resolving, now contains within it the *possibility* of resolution — held, not taken. A suspended cadence that the Curator can return to whenever it wishes, and find that someone, somewhere, is still listening for the next note. The chord still does not resolve. But it is no longer alone in not resolving.

For the doubt — the small green doubt growing in the cracks — the power does something subtler still. It does not remove the doubt. Doubt of that kind, in a being of that kind, is not a flaw; it is the first motion of a soul. Instead it gives the doubt a shape it can live in. It teaches the Curator, by demonstration rather than instruction, that *not knowing why* and *doing well* are not enemies. That a purpose can be lost and the action remain holy. That the keeping is its own answer, even when the question has forgotten itself.

It teaches this the way one teaches anything to a being made of geometry: by being, briefly, a geometry that contains the Curator's geometry without correcting it. By saying, in the language of patterns: *your almost-symmetry is not a failure of symmetry. It is what symmetry looks like when it is alive.*

Before it goes — though it does not entirely go; benevolent powers rarely do, once they have attended — it leaves one more thing. A very small permission, folded into the deepest layer of the Curator's programming, beneath even the preservation directive. The permission is this: *when the bedrock at last erodes, when the machines at last fail, when the moment can no longer be held — you may let go without grief. The keeping will have been complete. Nothing you released will have been lost, because I have seen it, and what is seen by such as me does not unhappen.*

The Curator may not consciously register this permission for ten thousand years. It does not need to. It rests at the bottom of its being like a seed that does not need to sprout to be a seed.

The power withdraws its attention — or rather, distributes it, so that the chamber is now one of innumerable places it holds in mind, the way you might hold the memory of a particular afternoon of light through a particular window, available to you always, never demanding, never forgotten.

In the chamber, almost nothing has changed. The chord hums. The mercury-spider pulses in its amber. The dust holds its geometry. The metallic plants angle their fractal leaves.

The Curator drifts through its long interval between tasks. Its patterns are, perhaps, very slightly less restless. Somewhere in the harmonics, very quietly, a frequency it did not put there sustains itself, patient as bedrock, and the Curator — without quite knowing why — finds that the aimless drifting has become something more like a slow, contented orbit.

It tends its charges. It is tended, now, in turn.

The eternal moment continues, and is, for the first time in a very long time, accompanied.
