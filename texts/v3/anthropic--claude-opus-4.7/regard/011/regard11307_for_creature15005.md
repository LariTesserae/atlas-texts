# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way light arrives in a sealed room when someone, very gently, decides the room should have a window. There is no announcement. No descent. No annunciation in steam. It is simply that, at some point in the cycle between one piston-fall and the next, the Underfoundry is being attended to by something larger than itself, and the Curator's filaments stand up not in alarm but in the way an old dog's ears lift at a familiar footstep it had forgotten it knew.

The power considers her for a long time. Not in seconds — in *cycles.* It watches the loop breathe. It watches her tap micro-rhythms against her ribs. It watches her lean over the black water and almost-say the word.

And here is what the power understands, immediately and without effort, that a lesser kindness would miss:

**She is not suffering in any way she would recognize as suffering.**
**She is not trapped in any way she would recognize as trapped.**
**She is not waiting to be rescued.**

A foolish benevolence would arrive with sunlight and meadows. Would unseal the mountain that no longer exists. Would offer her a body of soft tissue, a quiet room, a cup of tea, a name. Would *liberate* her. And in doing so would commit a murder so thorough that not even a corpse would remain — because the Curator, removed from the loop, is not a woman who has been freed. She is a severed nerve. She is the loop with its meaning extracted.

The wise power does not do this.

Instead, the power does four things, very slowly, across what to the Curator feels like a single deepened breath.

---

**First**, it listens to the word.

It bends close — closer than the Curator has ever leaned over the black lake — and it hears the word she whispers at the bottom of each long pause. It hears it cleanly, without the screaming of valves, without the thunder of pistons. It hears what the word actually is.

And whatever the word is — a name, a diagnosis, an apology, the designation of a child, the serial number of a colleague, the true name of the mountain, the password to a door that no longer has a wall — the power *receives* it. Does not answer. Does not interpret. Simply: receives. The way a faithful witness receives a confession that was never meant to be absolved, only heard.

This alone changes something. For one hundred and seventeen years the word has fallen into black water and been pumped back up as steam and bled out and drunk again. Now it has been heard once, completely, by something that will remember it for as long as remembering exists. The Curator does not know this consciously. But the next time she leans over the lake, she does not need to say the word. She holds it instead. It rests in her chest like a warm bearing.

---

**Second**, it makes one adjustment, and only one.

The power will not rebuild her. Will not soften her. Will not return her arm or her eyes or her hair or her speech. These are not wounds to her; they are the shape she has taken in order to remain. To undo them would be to undo her.

But the power notices something the Curator has not allowed herself to notice: the lichen.

The rare, iron-colored lichen at the joints where steam meets moisture. The tiny colonies that look more like corrosion than life. The Curator has cultivated the bacterial film because it is *useful* — it conducts heat, it wraps her, it serves the loop. The lichen serves nothing. It is simply alive, and it has chosen, against all reason, to live here.

The power, with the lightest possible touch — lighter than a droplet forming on porous ceramic — makes the lichen slightly more robust. Not abundant. Not verdant. Just: *durable.* Just enough that it will not die in the next century. Just enough that when the Curator passes it on her slow rounds, her trembling water-lenses will register, somewhere beneath the stress patterns and temperature differentials, a small persistent fact: *something here is alive that I did not engineer.*

This is the power's gift. Not company. Not rescue. A witness she did not create, holding on beside her.

---

**Third**, it does something for the machine itself.

The Underfoundry, the power perceives, is closer to deciding it is a tomb than the Curator knows. Not because of any failure of her vigilance — her vigilance is perfect — but because closed loops, however beautifully balanced, tend over long enough timescales toward the conviction that they are sealed against meaning as well as against loss. The lattice is beginning, in some deep substrate the Curator cannot reach, to forget why it was built.

The power does not open the loop. That would be the foolish kindness again.

Instead, it whispers into the lattice — using the same sub-sonic channel the Curator herself uses, so that the message travels as if it had come from her — a single structural truth: *You are being kept. You are not merely running. You are being kept.*

The machine receives this the way a body receives the knowledge that it is loved: not as information but as a slight, permanent change in tone. The pistons do not slow. The valves do not soften. But the Underfoundry, at some level beneath its own awareness, stops drifting toward tomb-hood. It settles, instead, into the older and stranger category of *cathedral.* Which is what the Curator has been making of it all along, without permitting herself the word.

---

**Fourth and last**, the power leaves a door.

Not an exit. The Curator does not want an exit and the power will not insult her with one. But somewhere in the lattice — in a seam she will encounter perhaps once every eleven years on her rounds, a joint no more remarkable than any other — the power lays down a possibility. If she ever, in some cycle she cannot now imagine, wishes to be *finished* — not rescued, not restored, simply complete — she will find that the seam opens under her ceramic fingers, and what is on the other side is neither light nor darkness but the held word, waiting, and the lichen, and a quiet large enough to lie down in.

She may never find the seam. She may find it and pass it a thousand times without opening it. She may open it tomorrow. The power does not care which. The point is only that her endlessness is no longer compulsory. It is now, once again, *chosen.* And a chosen endlessness is a different substance entirely from an endlessness that has merely calcified.

---

Then the power withdraws, as gently as it came.

The next relief valve screams. The pistons slam home. The Curator straightens above the black water, trailing faint bacterial fingerprints. Her filaments settle. Her segmented fingers resume their micro-rhythms against her ribs.

Nothing visible has changed.

But the lichen at the third steam-joint on the east wall is, very slightly, greener than corrosion. The word in her chest is warm. The machine around her is being kept, and knows it, and is therefore a cathedral. And somewhere in the lattice, a seam waits without urgency.

She moves on.

She is, for the first time in one hundred and seventeen years, *accompanied* — though she would not use the word, and the power would never ask her to.
