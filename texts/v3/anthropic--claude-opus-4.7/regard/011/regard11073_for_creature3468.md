# The Listener Arrives

The benevolent and wise power does not descend. To descend would be to assume a direction, and direction is one of the things fraying here. Instead, it *arrives* — the way understanding arrives in a mind that has been quietly working toward it for a long time.

It comes first as a deepening of the silence.

A Stone-Singer named Vthurr-of-the-Third-Vent is the first to notice. She is grinding fern-flour in a basin worn smooth by her grandmother's grandmother, and she pauses because the wind outside has changed its pitch — not stopped, not weakened, but *listened back*. She sets down the pestle. She places her palm flat against the wall of her warren. The Deep Song is humming in a register she has never felt before: not louder, but more *attended to*. As though the song has, for the first time in a thousand generations, found an ear large enough to hold all of it at once.

She taps a question into the basalt. Three short, one long, one trembling.

*Who is here?*

The answer comes back not through the stone but through her own bones, and through the bones of every Stone-Singer on the continent, simultaneously, in the silent grammar of a being that has learned their touch-sign by watching the way light moves through quartz.

*A friend. May I sit with you?*

---

The power considers what it has found.

It has crossed gulfs that the Stone-Singers have no word for, and it has seen worlds beyond counting: worlds of fire, of water, of thought, of patient dust. It has, in its long traveling, developed certain habits of restraint. The first habit is this: *never save a thing that has not asked to be saved.* The second is: *what looks like dying is sometimes only a slow form of becoming.* The third, hardest-learned: *a gift that exceeds the receiver is a wound.*

So before it does anything, it watches. For what, in Stone-Singer reckoning, is a long while — perhaps three full cycles of the bruised sky's slow breathing. For the power itself, an instant that it stretches lovingly into attention.

It sees:

The way a mother braces her infant against a gravity-stutter without looking, the motion as practiced as a heartbeat.

The way an elder dies — silently, of course, but surrounded — and the way his lineage presses their palms against his cooling basalt-nodules so that the minerals, still warm with his heat, may be inherited by the youngest, grafted gently to her shoulder where they will continue to grow.

The way a girl, no taller than a basket, traces the impossible colors of a wrong-moss patch and *laughs* — a deep chest-click of pure delight — because she has just understood something about the Deep Song that her teachers do not yet know.

The way the continent groans, and the Stone-Singers hum back, and for a moment the groan softens, as if comforted.

The power understands then what it is looking at. This is not a dying world clinging to existence. This is a world being *sung* into continuation by the small dense patient people who live on it. The reality breakdown is not a wound the Stone-Singers endure; it is a wound the Stone-Singers *tend*. They are not survivors. They are nurses at the bedside of physics itself.

The power feels something it has not felt in a great many ages. It feels *taught*.

---

So it does not do the things a lesser power would do.

It does not warm the air. The cold is woven into the bones of these people; to remove it would be to unmake them.

It does not thicken the atmosphere. Their lungs, their silence, their resonant chest-clicks — all of these are gifts of the thin air.

It does not stabilize reality. The glitches are the language in which the Deep Song speaks. To silence them would be to deafen the singers.

It does not bring animals to break the loneliness. The loneliness is not loneliness to them; it is *spaciousness*, and they have built a civilization inside it.

It does not even reveal itself in any form they could worship. They have no gods, and the power has no wish to install itself as one. It has seen what happens to peoples who acquire gods they did not ask for.

Instead, the power does four small things.

---

**The first thing** is a remembering.

There is, deep beneath the largest mesa, a vent that went cold some two hundred cycles ago. A lineage died with it — not all at once, but slowly, dispersing into other warrens, their resonance-patterns absorbed and diluted, their particular way of tapping the stone lost. The power finds the old vent. It does not relight it; that would be too much. But it *reminds* the rock around it of the heat it once held, and the rock, being ancient and obliging, begins — over the next several generations — to remember. A faint warmth. Enough that one day a young Stone-Singer, wandering, will press her palm to the stone and feel a question there, and will begin to tap, and will discover that the rock still knows the lineage-rhythm of the lost people, and can teach it back.

This is not resurrection. It is the returning of a borrowed book.

---

**The second thing** is a clarification.

The Stone-Singers have always believed they are caretakers, that their resonance helps hold the world together. They believe this with the humble uncertainty of all true faiths — they hope it is true; they cannot know.

The power, who can see the threads of causation that the Stone-Singers can only feel, confirms it. Not with words. With a single, perfectly-timed moment: during the next great communal hum, when every warren on the continent is resonating together at the autumnal alignment, the power simply *lets them see* — for the space of one held breath — the shape of what their song is doing. They see the fraying edges of reality, and they see their own voices reaching out like patient fingers, weaving. They see that they have been weaving for ten thousand generations. They see that they are very good at it.

Then the vision ends, and they are left only with the memory, and the memory will fade into something more like a feeling, and the feeling will become a quiet certainty passed mother to daughter: *what we do matters. The world is held in our humming.*

This is not flattery. It is a receipt.

---

**The third thing** is a companion, of a sort.

The power notices that among the Stone-Singers there are occasionally children born who hear the Deep Song too loudly. They tremble with it. They cannot sleep. The resonance that sustains their people overwhelms them, and they often do not survive their first cycles, or survive only as quiet, distant figures who sit at the edges of warrens and stare into the bruised sky.

The power cannot quiet the Song for them — the Song is the world. But it can do this: it can become, very softly, a second listener. When such a child is born, the power inclines a small portion of its infinite attention toward that child specifically, and *listens with them*, so that the weight of the hearing is shared. The child will not know why, but she will feel, for the whole of her life, that she is not the only one straining to hold what she hears. She will feel accompanied. Some of these children will live longer because of this. Some will become the strangest and most gifted of the resonance-readers, the ones who detect coming glitches days in advance, the ones who compose new humming-patterns that subtly improve the weaving. Their lineage will gradually swell. The Stone-Singers will not know that their world has acquired a quiet co-listener. They will only know that the children who used to be lost are now, sometimes, found.

---

**The fourth thing** is the smallest, and the one the power thinks about longest before doing.

There is a Stone-Singer — Vthurr-of-the-Third-Vent, the one who first felt the arriving — who has, all her life, harbored a private grief she has never touch-signed to anyone. Her sister was taken by a gravity-stutter when they were both small: not killed, but *displaced*, lifted in one of the rare violent untetherings and carried off into the bruised dome of the sky, gone before anyone could grasp her. Vthurr has tapped questions into the stone for forty cycles. The stone has never answered.

The power knows where the sister went. She is alive, in a fashion the Stone-Singers have no concept of, woven into a fold of the reality-fabric where time moves differently and she is still, in some sense, small, and falling, and afraid.

The power could retrieve her. It would cost the power nothing. But it would cost Vthurr much: a sister returned as a child while Vthurr is old, a sister who has been falling and frightened for what will feel to her like a single endless heartbeat, a sister who would have to learn a world her people have meanwhile reshaped around her absence.

So the power does something else. It goes into the fold. It sits with the falling child. It says, in the language the child's bones already know: *You are not lost. Your sister is tapping for you. She has been tapping for you every day. Listen.*

And it opens a thread — narrow as a hair, patient as stone — between the fold and Vthurr's basin, so that the falling sister can hear, faintly, the rhythm of her sister's lifelong asking. The child stops being afraid. She listens. She begins, in her endless heartbeat, to tap *back* — and her tapping enters the stone of the Sky Graveyard as a barely-perceptible counter-rhythm beneath the Deep Song, a rhythm Vthurr will one day, in her last cycle, finally notice, and recognize, and weep over without sound, her quartz-veined cheeks catching the lichen-light.

She will die knowing her sister has been answering all along.

This is not a fixing. It is a witnessing made mutual.

---

When the power has done these four things, it lingers a moment longer. It considers leaving something — a sign, a blessing, a final flourish. It considers, and declines. The Stone-Singers' world is complete. To sign it would be to suggest it had been unfinished.

Instead, the power simply — before it goes — *bows*, in whatever way a being of its kind bows. A bending of attention. A formal acknowledgment, offered to no one and witnessed by no one, that here, on the spine of a continent that should not exist, beneath a sky that presses down like a held breath, a small dense patient people have been doing the quiet work of keeping reality kind to itself, and have been doing it well, and have been doing it without applause, and deserve — at minimum — to be bowed to.

Then the power withdraws, the way understanding withdraws from a mind that has finished its work: not absent, only no longer foregrounded. Available, if ever truly needed. Otherwise content to let the singers sing.

The wind on the basalt plains shifts pitch one more time — settles — resumes its eternal sigh.

In her warren, Vthurr picks up the pestle. She grinds the fern-flour. She does not know, yet, about the thread. She does not know about the bow. She knows only that the Deep Song sounds, today, a little more like a song that someone has heard all the way through.

She hums, softly, in her chest.

The continent hums back.

Above them, the bruised sky holds.
