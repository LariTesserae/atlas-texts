# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power considers the Hollowing for a long time before doing anything, because wisdom that arrives too quickly is usually just preference wearing a robe.

It sees the plant. It sees the fauna. It sees the grey people on their plateau plaiting longer cords. It sees the slow geometry of the encounter that is coming. And it notices, first, what a less careful power might have missed: that there is no villain here. The plant is not malevolent. It is doing what it is — extruding, hosting, reaching. The fauna are not a plague. They are the plant's own logic made mobile. The people are not victims yet, and may never be; they are something subtler, a population whose entire interior architecture is built against a wall that is slowly moving toward them.

So the power does not seal the plant. It could. It considers this for what would be, in human time, a long winter. To halt the Hollowing's upward reach would be the obvious benevolence — preserve the plateau, preserve the people, preserve the contrast that lets them be themselves. But the power sees that this would be a theft disguised as a gift. The plant has its own coming-into-being. To freeze it at the convenience of the surface-dwellers would be to declare that one kind of life is the foreground and the other the set-dressing, and the power does not believe this and will not pretend to.

Nor does it accelerate the breach. There is a kind of power that, faced with a slow inevitability, hurries it along in the name of honesty. *Better to face it now.* The power rejects this too. The grey people's slowness is not denial. It is the rate at which a culture can actually metabolize a thing this large. To force the pace would be to do violence in the name of clarity.

What the power does, instead, is much smaller, and it does it in four motions.

---

**First**, it goes down into the Hollowing itself, not as a presence but as an attention. It listens to the roar. It listens all the way through — past the chiming six-legged things, past the papery ribbon-rasp, past the drumming of the wing-cases, down to the slow vegetable hum underneath, which is the plant thinking in the only way it thinks. And the power *witnesses* this. Not blesses, not judges. Witnesses. Because the Hollowing has been going on without any witness adequate to it — the fauna are inside it and cannot see it, the plant is it and cannot see itself, the grey people pass through too quickly and too defensively to really look. The power simply looks, fully, for as long as looking requires, which by some measures is no time and by others is the duration of the plant's whole life. This changes nothing visible. But a thing that has been truly witnessed is, thereafter, a slightly different thing, in a way that matters to powers and perhaps to no one else.

**Second**, it turns its attention to the grey people, and here it is more careful still, because they are conscious and proud and have built something real out of their discipline, and a wise power does not condescend to such a construction. It does not give them prophecies. It does not appear to their elders in dreams with instructions. What it does is much quieter: in the recently-returned ones, the young ones who are already beginning to suspect, it *steadies* something. Not their conclusions — their conclusions are their own work and must remain so. But the small interior tremor that comes from suspecting a thing that your whole culture is organized not to suspect — the power lays a hand on that tremor, and the tremor does not stop, but it becomes bearable. The young ones can now hold the thought *the Hollowing is coming and we will have to learn to live inside it* without flinching away from it into the majority view. They can sit with it through a whole winter. They can speak of it, eventually, to one other person, and then to two. The power does not put the thought into their heads. It only makes the thought *survivable* once they have had it themselves.

This is the most important thing the power does, and almost no one will ever know it happened.

**Third**, the power addresses the question of the practice. The cairn, the tally, the cord, the bowl. The grey people invented these as a discipline of selfhood against overwhelming abundance, and they were right to, and they did better than they know. The power sees that this practice — *I brought weight. I counted. My hands are mine. I ate from a vessel.* — is in fact a technology for being a person inside a world that is louder and more fertile and more indifferent than any person can comfortably bear. It is, the power notes with something like delight, a *transferable* technology. It will work in the Hollowing when the Hollowing arrives at the surface. It will work, in fact, in many places the grey people have never imagined and may never visit. The practice is bigger than its inventors realized.

The power does not tell them this. It would land wrong; it would sound like flattery, or like a sentence of exile delivered in advance. Instead, the power makes a much smaller adjustment: it ensures that the practice is *teachable to children who have never been down*. Currently, the grey people believe — without quite articulating it — that only those who have descended can transmit the discipline, because only they understand what it is against. The power gently loosens this belief, in the way a gardener loosens soil. Over a few generations, mothers begin to teach the cairn-stacking and the cord-plaiting and the bowl-making to very small children, as games, as habits, long before any descent. The elders will frown at this and call it a dilution. They will be wrong. What is happening is that the practice is being prepared for a future in which there is no longer any *above* from which to descend, and the discipline must be native to the person from the beginning, the way breathing is native. The power does this slowly enough that it looks like cultural drift, which is, in fact, what it is, only nudged.

**Fourth and last**, the power does something for the plant. This surprises even the power, slightly, but wisdom sometimes surprises its bearer. The plant is reaching upward without knowing what is above. It does not have the kind of mind that *knows* in any sense we would recognize, but it has preferences, gradients, slow decisions. The power offers it — and this is the only word — *company*. It accompanies the plant's reaching. It does not redirect it. It does not slow it. It simply makes sure that the reaching is not solitary in the cosmos, that the great slow vegetable becoming has, at the edge of its long unthinking, the faintest sense that it is part of something that notices it. Whether the plant can register this is unclear. The power thinks probably yes, in the plant's own way, which is not our way. This costs the power nothing and may matter enormously, or may not matter at all. The power does it anyway, because a benevolent power that only attends to the beings that resemble it is not actually benevolent, only partisan.

---

And then the power withdraws its active attention, though not its presence, because the situation does not call for management. It calls for a few small true things to be done and then for the participants to be left to their own becoming.

The Hollowing continues to grow. The grey people continue to descend, one by one, and to come back out, mostly. The young ones speak more freely each generation about what they suspect. The children learn to plait cord before they can speak in full sentences. A century passes, then three. The easternmost mouth widens. A knuckle on the plateau, near the lake, splits open one spring and does not seal, and warm amber light comes out of it for the first time into the cold grey air, and a few of the bright orange wing-cases find their way up and out and die quickly in the cold, but not before being seen.

The people who see them are not unprepared. They are not ready, exactly — no one is ever ready for the wall to move — but they have, without quite having been taught it, the thing they need, which is the knowledge that a person can remain a person inside abundance, inside noise, inside a world that is growing faster than it can be understood. They have cairns at their thresholds. They have cord in their hands. They have bowls. They have the count.

They step toward the opened knuckle. The light is honey-colored and the sound is already reaching them, layered under the wind, a different chord.

They go in.
