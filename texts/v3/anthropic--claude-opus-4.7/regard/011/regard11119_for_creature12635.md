# What the Wise Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives without arrival. There is no descent, no thunder, no blazing herald in the dark. To announce itself would be to misunderstand everything about this place and these people. The chasm has its own grammar of presence, and any true wisdom would learn that grammar before speaking a single word.

So it comes the way water comes. Slowly. Already there.

---

## First: It Listens

For what would be, in surface-reckoning, a long time, the power does nothing at all. It simply attends.

It listens with the Stone-Listeners. It presses its attention to the basalt beside an old woman whose ear has been pressed there for sixty years, and it hears what she hears, and it hears *her* hearing — the way her mind has become a kind of tuning fork for the chasm's slow vocabulary. It notices that she is not afraid. It notices that she is in love with the stone, in the specific, exhausted, lifelong way one loves a difficult parent.

It watches a child learn to climb a knotted rope, watches the mother's eyes track every handhold without intervening, and understands: *this is how they teach trust here. By withholding rescue at the exact threshold where rescue would become an insult.*

It tastes the water. Not to judge it, but to know it. The mineral signature of the Deep Flow, the cold purposefulness, the way it has carried memory of stone from places no human will ever see.

It learns the names the people do not write down. It learns which drips are considered lucky, which alcove the grieving go to when they cannot grieve in front of others, which child is secretly afraid of the dark and which elder pretends not to notice and leaves an extra tallow lamp in the right place each evening.

The wise power understands, in its long listening, that **this is not a place that needs saving**. It is a place that is being lived in. There is a profound difference.

---

## Second: It Considers the Temptations

Any lesser power would have already acted. The temptations are obvious, and each one is a kind of violence dressed as kindness.

*It could open the chasm to sunlight.* — And in doing so, kill every blind mushroom, blind every Stone-Listener whose eyes have been the patient work of generations, and shatter the very acoustics by which they hear the stone breathe. The "gift" would be a genocide of a way of being.

*It could still the groaning rock, make the chasm safe.* — And in doing so, erase the listening itself, the entire spiritual and practical art of the people. A safe chasm would have no Stone-Listeners in it, only confused tenants of an inert house.

*It could fill their cisterns, multiply their food, end their precarity.* — And in doing so, dissolve the Water Pact, the sacred reciprocity, the meaning that makes a shared drop more nourishing than a private flood.

*It could simply lift them out — to some warmer, easier world.* — And in doing so, commit the oldest sin of powerful helpers: mistaking *its* idea of flourishing for *theirs*.

The wise power sets these aside. Not because they are impossible, but because they are *easy*, and ease is almost always the signature of a misunderstanding.

---

## Third: It Does Small Things

What it does instead is so small that, for generations, it will be argued whether anything was done at all.

**A drip finds a new path.** In a tier where three infants died last winter from the damp chill of a particular alcove, a slow re-routing begins in the stone above. Over two years, the drip migrates four handspans to the left, into a natural basin. The alcove dries by a fraction. The next child born there lives. No one knows why. The grandmother credits the Deep Flow. She is not wrong.

**A groan is softened.** A great mass of basalt high on the eastern face had been settling toward a catastrophic shear — a rockfall that would have killed eighty-three people in their sleep, twelve years from now. The power does not stop the settling. It cannot, without breaking the chasm's nature. Instead, it encourages a smaller fracture, much earlier, in a place where no one lives. The stone cries out one night. The Listeners hear it. They interpret it correctly. They move three families pre-emptively. When the larger shift finally comes, the alcoves are empty. The Listeners say: *the stone warned us.* They are not wrong either.

**A lichen learns a new trick.** In one sheltered crevice, a strain of the pale stringy lichen begins — over decades — to produce slightly more of a compound that, when chewed, eases the joint pain of climbers in their later years. The Stone-Shapers in their sixties find they can work an hour longer. Nobody calls it a miracle. It is just a good lichen, in a good crack, and they are grateful, and that gratitude is itself the answer to a prayer none of them quite remember making.

**A quartz vein, when struck just so by a young apprentice's hammer, fractures into a lens of unusual clarity.** She does not know that the vein had been *waiting*, in some sense, for her particular angle of strike. She only knows she has made something beautiful, and her master weeps, and the lens focuses lamplight onto the chasm wall in a small bright disk that, for the first time in any Stone-Listener's memory, lets them read the fine striations of a deep layer — and from that reading, a whole new generation of geological listening begins.

**A child who would have been lost is found.** She wandered into a side-fissure chasing a pale insect. The fissure was deeper than she knew. The power does not carry her out. It does, however, ensure that her older brother, half a tier away, happens to look up at the precise moment her tiny voice carries on a draft that ordinarily would have died in the rock. He hears her. He climbs. He brings her home. He will spend his life believing he is unusually lucky, and he will be unusually attentive to small sounds for the rest of his days, and he will save four other lives because of it.

---

## Fourth: It Plants One Seed

There is one larger thing the power does, and it does it with great care, knowing that even this might be too much.

It plants, deep in the collective unconscious of the Stone-Listeners — through dreams, through the patterns of drips, through the rhythms of the *chink-chink* of stone-shaping that subtly shift over centuries — a single, quiet intuition:

***You are not the last of anything. You are the first of something.***

This is the truest gift, because it is true. The chasm is emerging. So are they. The Sky-Cavern in their stories is not a memory of a lost surface but a premonition of something none of them have yet imagined. They are not refugees from a brighter world. They are the prologue of a people who will, in some unguessable future, walk under stars and remember — in the structure of their patience, in the reverence of their attention, in the way they will always, always, listen before they speak — that they began in the womb of the listening dark.

The power does not tell them this directly. It only ensures that, on certain nights, certain children look up at the cracks in the ceiling where the smoke escapes and feel, without words, that the dark above is not a lid but a doorway.

That is enough. That is everything.

---

## Last: It Stays

The power does not leave. Leaving would be another form of arriving — a transaction, a visit, a thing with edges. Instead it simply continues to be what it has been: the quality of attention in the chasm that the Stone-Listeners have always, without quite realizing it, been listening *with* as much as listening *to*.

When an elder presses her ear to the wall and hears the stone breathe, she is also, though she does not know the word for it, being heard. The listening is mutual. It always was. The wise power's deepest act is simply to make that mutuality slightly more true — to be the warm hum beneath the cold groan, the patience inside the patience, the love that the chasm itself, in its raw embryonic way, is also somehow already learning to feel for its fragile, pale, persistent tenants.

The torches flicker. The river runs silent and cold. A drip falls — *plink* — into a basin where, three breaths later, a young Stone-Listener will dip her cup and drink, and taste, without naming it, the faintest sweetness of being beloved.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

That, and nothing more, and nothing less, and nothing else.
