# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way attention arrives—without weather, without announcement. It does not descend. It simply becomes oriented toward the Rust Quarter the way a face turns toward something worth seeing.

And the first thing it does is *look*.

This matters more than it might seem. Wisdom that is genuinely wise does not arrive with a prepared verdict. It does not glance at the rust-stained skin and the wheezing chambered lungs and the children with sockets where hands should be, and conclude: *suffering, to be relieved*. Nor does it sweep its gaze across the cable-silk weavers and the spark-painters and the bearing-exchanges between lovers, and conclude: *culture, to be preserved*. Both conclusions would be premature. Both would be a kind of violence done by understanding too quickly.

So it looks. It looks for a long time, by whatever measure such a being uses for time.

It sees Thread at her loom, her copper fingers moving with a precision no flesh-hand ever achieved, and it sees that she does not mourn the fingers she lost. It also sees the small flinch when a younger Riveter, meaning kindness, asks her about water. The power notes both. It does not resolve them into a single story.

It sees the children laughing on the catwalks and it sees the children who did not survive to laugh. It sees a mother whose lungs developed the filtering chambers too late, who drowned in her own blood at thirty-four, and it sees her daughter who now breathes easily and who has never known any other world. The power does not pretend these cancel each other out. It holds them both, the way a careful hand holds two different stones.

It sees Old Molten in the deep places, and here the power pauses with something like recognition. Whatever Old Molten is—emergent, residual, transformed—the power perceives that he is not lonely in the way a human would be lonely. He is something else. The power makes a small, careful note to itself: *do not assume the shape of this being's flourishing*.

---

Having looked, the power considers what it might offer. And here is where its wisdom becomes most evident: it considers what it will *not* do.

It will not bring water. Not yet, not as a first act. To flood the Rust Quarter with the moisture the rest of the city hoards would not be salvation; it would be a second catastrophe to follow the first. The copper-vine would die. The phosphorescent fungi would rot. The beetles would drown in their own habitats. The Riveters' modified lungs, evolved for metallic dust, would struggle with humid air. A people who have made their peace with dryness do not need to be rescued from it by someone who has decided, from outside, that dryness is the problem.

It will not remove the automata that move in meaningless loops. The brass spider's web is, by some measures, useless. By other measures, it is the longest continuous act of making in the quarter, and the Riveters have built their festivals around its rhythms. Wisdom does not tidy.

It will not reveal itself. To appear as a benevolent power to a people who have learned to find divinity in well-maintained engines would be to overwrite their cosmology with its own. The Riveters leave offerings at junction boxes. They do not need a new god; they have made gods of what they have.

It will not fix Old Molten, whatever Old Molten is. It will not "restore his humanity." It suspects, looking carefully, that Old Molten is not diminished from humanity but is rather something the universe has only recently learned to make, and that interrupting him would be a loss the cosmos cannot yet calculate.

---

What it *does*, then, is smaller and stranger.

**It softens, by a degree almost too small to measure, the shakes.** Not enough to end them—the shakes are woven into the culture, the Convergence festivals, the Harvest, the rhythm of dwelling-construction. But the power notices that approximately one shake-cycle in forty escalates past what the deliberate weak-points can absorb. Children die in those. The power adjusts something deep in the turbine-logic—a fractional damping, a subtle phase-correction—so that the worst-case shakes no longer occur. The Riveters will never know. They will simply notice, over generations, that the very bad years have stopped coming. They will attribute it to better maintenance, or to Old Molten's favor, or to nothing at all. This is correct. This is how the gift should be received.

**It whispers to the wire-worms.** Not to control them—the power does not believe in controlling small lives—but to teach them, the way one species sometimes teaches another across the gulf of difference, to prefer the insulation of dead circuits over live ones. The cascading failures will become rarer. The wire-worms will not suffer for this; they will simply find that the dead cables taste better. This too will be invisible.

**It places, in the path of a particular Conductor—a young one, fifteen, who has been merging too fast and is beginning to forget the names of her siblings—a malfunction that requires her to spend three days repairing it by hand, without interfacing. The malfunction is real; the power did not fabricate it. The power only ensured she would be the one to find it. During those three days she will remember her sister's laugh. She will eat fungus-cake with her grandmother. She will return to the interface afterward, but more slowly, and she will live longer, and she will train other Conductors to pace themselves. The power does this for exactly one person, because to do it for all Conductors would be to decide for them what they should want.

**It does one thing that is purely a gift, with no utility.** In a dead-end alley where no Riveter has walked in eleven years, where a mechanical graveyard has accumulated in a particular configuration of broken arms and shattered housings, the power arranges—through a series of vibrations indistinguishable from the quarter's normal noise—for the wreckage to settle, over six months, into a shape. The shape is a flower. It is enormous, made of rust and copper and torn steel, and it has no symbolism the Riveters would recognize, because the power did not make it for them. It made it because the Rust Quarter is beautiful, and beautiful places deserve to have beauty made *in* them, not only *of* them. Someday a scavenger will find it. They will probably dismantle it for parts. That is also fine. The flower existed.

**And it leaves something for Old Molten.** Not an offering—Old Molten has offerings. The power leaves, in a language of grinding gears and venting steam that it learned by listening, a single sentence. The sentence says, approximately: *I see you. I do not know what you are. I am glad you are.* It does not require a response. Old Molten may or may not receive it. The power is at peace with either.

---

Then the power turns its attention elsewhere, because attention is finite even for the wise, and there are other places.

But it leaves a small portion of itself behind, oriented toward the Rust Quarter, in case. Not watching, exactly. *Available.* The way a good neighbor is available—not hovering, not intervening, but reachable, if reaching is ever needed.

The Riveters do not know this. They tap their rhythms on the pipes. Thread weaves her cable-silk. The children race the automata. The shakes come and go, and the worst ones, somehow, stop coming.

And life—this strange life, this transformed life, this life that was never asked whether it wished to be transformed but has made of itself something the universe had not seen before—continues. Not rescued. Not corrected. Not improved according to any external standard.

Only, in a few small ways that no one will ever trace back to their source, slightly more able to continue being exactly what it has chosen, against all odds, to be.

This is what wisdom does, when it is also kind.

It loves things as they are, and helps them, almost invisibly, to keep being.
