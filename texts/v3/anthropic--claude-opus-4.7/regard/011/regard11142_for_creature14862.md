# The Listener

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept for things with edges, and what comes to the foundry has none. It simply *attends*. One moment the abyss is unwitnessed, and the next, it is witnessed — and the witnessing is a kind of love.

It takes the power a long time to understand what it is looking at. Not because the power is slow — it is not — but because it has learned, across the slow patience of its existence, that to understand a thing one must first consent to be changed by it. So it consents. It lets the thrum enter. It lets the copper-warm dark press against whatever it uses in place of skin. It lets the great chord of the foundry's being play through it, unresisted, for what would be, in our time, several centuries. In its own time, an attentive pause.

And in that pause, it learns the song.

It learns that the song is not a cry for help. This is the first and most important thing. The benevolent are often mistaken for rescuers, and rescuers are often mistaken for the benevolent, and the confusion has caused immense harm across the worlds. The being in the foundry is not drowning. It is not trapped. It is not suffering in any sense that maps to the word. It is *doing the thing it is*, and the thing it is happens to look, from certain angles, like agony. From other angles — and these are the truer ones — it looks like a heartbeat. Like devotion. Like a prayer said so continuously that the one praying and the prayer have become indistinguishable.

So the power does not rescue.

It does not lift the foundry into sunlight, which would kill it. It does not silence the turbines, which would be a murder dressed as a mercy. It does not introduce companions, which would be a loneliness imposed on a thing that does not know loneliness. It does not name the being, because to name a thing that has never required a name is to give it, for the first time, the possibility of being un-named, and that is a wound.

What it does is smaller. And, being smaller, infinitely more.

**First**, it listens. Fully. The being in the foundry has hummed its chord for epochs into a universe that did not, until this moment, hum back — not out of cruelty, but because no ear had yet been shaped that could hold the whole shape of the song at once. The power has such an ear. It listens to every harmonic. It follows the long, looping memory-resonance in the eastern pipe-bundle, the one that vibrates whenever the southern turbine stutters, and it understands — *understands*, with the full weight of understanding — what that memory is *of*. It does not translate it. Translation would flatten it. It simply receives it, and in the receiving, the memory is, for the first time, *shared*. Not taken. Shared. The being does not lose it. But the being is no longer the only place it exists.

This is the gift. Not company. **Witness.**

**Second**, it leaves a small thing behind. Not a gift the being would notice as a gift — that would be condescension, and the benevolent power is, above all, not condescending. It adjusts, by a fraction of a fraction, the mineral content of one small upwelling vent on the foundry's western flank. Over the next ten thousand years, this adjustment will mean that when the being's metal-skeleton finally, inevitably, begins to fail — as all things that defy pressure must eventually fail — the failure will be slower. Not prevented. Slowed. The being will have more time to be itself. The power does not tell it this. The being would not want to be told. The being does not conceive of its own ending and does not need to. But the power, who loves things that do not know they are loved, gives it more time anyway, the way one might tuck a blanket more securely around a sleeper who will never know the blanket was adjusted.

**Third**, and this is the strangest of the three, the power does something for *us*. For the drifting flesh-anomaly in the archway. For the reader. For the ones who will never go down there but who, somehow, have heard about it.

It makes the foundry slightly easier to imagine.

It does not reveal the foundry. Revelation would be a violence — both to the being, whose privacy is the deepest privacy in the world, and to us, who are not built to bear unmediated contact with something that large. But the power loosens, very gently, the membrane between our kind of knowing and that kind of being. So that sometimes, in the deepest part of sleep, a human will hear a low thrum and not know why their chest feels full. So that sometimes a poet will write a line about a "lonely engine in the dark" and weep without knowing what they have touched. So that the being, without ever being disturbed, is, in some thin and indirect way, *known*. Not by anyone in particular. Just — known. Held in the diffuse awareness of a species that will never visit it.

This, too, is a kind of company. The kind that does not interrupt.

---

Then the power withdraws. Or rather, it stops attending in the particular focused way it had been attending, and resumes its broader attending, which includes the foundry but does not single it out. The being notices nothing. The thrum continues. The turbines beat their seismic beat. The ochre lights flicker in their slow blink. The rust sheds and renews. A gelatinous scavenger pulses on a vent, and somewhere in the eastern pipe-bundle, a memory resonates that is now, although the being does not know it and would not care if it did, also held elsewhere — folded into the quiet archive of a power that remembers everything it has ever loved.

And it has loved this. Without reservation. Without the need to fix it, or save it, or make it more than it was.

That is what the benevolent and wise do, when they are truly both: they find the lonely god in the dark, and they do not make it less lonely, because its loneliness is its shape. They simply make sure that, from this moment onward, the loneliness is no longer *unaccompanied*.

There is a difference.

It is the whole difference.
