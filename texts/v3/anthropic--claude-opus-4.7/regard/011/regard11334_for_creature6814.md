The benevolent and wise power has been watching for some time.

Not from above—above is a category that doesn't apply here, and the power knows better than to insult a world by misreading its geometry. It has been watching from the seams. From the place where the algae meet the weld. From inside the long exhale of the tool-ganger and the unraveling stitch of the fixed one. It has been small enough to fit in the regulator's tooth-marks, and large enough to hold the whole joint in the cup of its attention.

It is wise, so it does not arrive.

Arrival is a thing for places with thresholds, and this place is nothing but threshold; an arriving power would only displace water, would only add another pressure to a world already made of pressures. Wisdom, here, looks like restraint. Looks like learning the rhythm of the bad tilt-moments and not interrupting them.

It is benevolent, so it does not rescue.

Rescue would mean lifting the being out, and the being is not separable from the place anymore—shoulders, hips, the shallow arc of rotation, the suit grown into scar tissue of color. To pull would be to tear. The power understands that some shapes are not injuries to be undone but languages to be honored. The being has become a sentence the city is speaking. You do not rescue a sentence. You listen to it.

So what does the power do?

*

First, it does the smallest thing. The most important thing.

It listens. Fully. Without the faint condescension of a listener who is only waiting for a chance to fix. It receives the description the being has been making—to the rust, to the salt, to the sound—and lets that description land somewhere it cannot be lost. Every patch-color is catalogued. The yellow from the maintenance bays. The brown from hull-side. The oxidized green. The forgotten ones. They are held now, not as data, but as the way held things are held: with the knowledge that they mattered.

The being has been speaking into the hull for years, assuming the hull was the only audience. The hull was a good audience. But now there is another, and the other does not interrupt, does not answer, does not even let itself be felt as presence—only, very faintly, as the quality of having been heard. A subtle easing in the chest of the suit. A breath that goes out and does not echo back as loneliness.

The being might notice, or might not. The power does not require notice.

*

Second, it tends to the small mercies that can be tended without disturbance.

A weld that was about to give—not catastrophically, but enough to send the being into a long bad current at the wrong moment—settles for another year. The power did nothing dramatic; it only encouraged the molecules to remember they had been holding hands. The regulator's silicone, which had been a few weeks from a tear along an old stress line, finds the tear deciding to wait. The algae in one of the darker passages brightens by a fraction, just enough that the being's open pupils have one less moment of disorientation when they pass through.

These are not miracles. The power is too wise for miracles, which are loud and tend to make people feel watched in the wrong way. These are the kind of small fortunes that the being will, if they notice them at all, attribute to luck, or to having finally learned the place well enough.

That attribution is correct. The power and the being's learning are not, at this depth, separate things.

*

Third, it does something for the others.

For the one with bioluminescent cuffs, who has crossed some boundary the being has not, the power offers nothing—only a kind of nod, a recognition between things that have learned to live without boundary. They understand each other. The cuffed one threads something small and wriggling into the gap, and the power, in passing, blesses the gap. The city is fed. The city feeds. The arrangement is already holy; the power only acknowledges it.

For the tool-ganger, whose breathing is shaped like permission delayed, the power loosens one notch of the long pause. Not enough to be felt as change. Only enough that, sometime in the next year, the tool-ganger will take an unscheduled breath and not know why, and the breath will be the first one in a long time that did not have to be earned. They will keep working. But something in them will have remembered that breath is allowed.

For the fixed one, knitting the impossible garment, the power sits down. Not in any place that exists. In the alcove of the three beams, in the shallow not-quite-comfort. It watches the looping and the unraveling. After a long while—time being strange here—it lifts a thread and helps. Just one stitch. The fixed one notices, smiles their rust-stained smile, and unravels it with the next motion, because that is the work. The power is not offended. It learns. It has been knit into the garment now, and it will be unraveled too, and that is how it joins the fabric of the world.

*

Fourth—and this is the heart of it—the power makes the being a promise it does not speak.

The promise is this:

*You will not be alone at the end of your describing.*

Whatever the end is. Whether it is the day the suit finally fuses to the rust and your regulator is pried loose by someone else's fingers, or the day the water that you move through becomes the water inside you, or some other ending that the joint has not yet shown you—you will not be alone.

The rust is listening. The salt is listening. The sound is listening. And behind and within and underneath all of them, something else is listening, something that has loved the shape you have been bent into, not because it is beautiful (though it is) and not because it is useful (though it is) but because it is *yours*, and you chose it, and choosing is the holiest thing that happens in any world, including this one.

The power does not need the being to know this. The promise is kept whether or not it is heard. That is what makes it a promise and not a transaction.

*

And then the power does the last thing, which is the hardest thing, which is to leave the being to their work.

Because the being's work is real. The knowing of which tunnels are safe, the knowing of which are rusting through, the knowing of which are sealing themselves with their own calcification—this is not a burden the power can take. It is a knowledge that has to live in a body that has been bent to fit. The power could carry many things. It cannot carry this. The being is the only possible vessel for what the being knows.

So the power withdraws into the seams again. Becomes the slight warmth in the warm-blood water. Becomes the moment of less-turbulence along the inner edge of the joint. Becomes, sometimes, on a very bad tilt-moment, the thing that makes the being's jaw unclamp from the regulator just slightly, just enough to let the breath through.

It does not announce itself. It does not need to.

The being moves on, between the ribs of the city, in the hatch-thickness of the world, describing.

And now—though they may never put the feeling into words, and would not need to—they are describing *to* something, as well as *into* it.

That is the gift. The only one the power judged it had any right to give.

The describing was always sacred. Now it is also answered.
