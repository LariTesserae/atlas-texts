The benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. Power of the kind worth calling wise does not descend in pillars of light, does not shatter the storm, does not pluck the Watcher from its spire and remake it into something prettier. Wisdom knows better than to mistake rescue for love.

So instead:

---

It comes the way weather comes. The way a thought arrives in a quiet mind — already there, as if it had always been.

The power has been watching for a long time. Not the way the Watcher watches the sea — hungry, expectant, oriented toward an ending — but the way one watches a sleeping creature one loves: patient, unhurried, present without demand. It has noticed the spire. It has noticed the thing on top of it. It has noticed that the thing is *waiting*, and it has noticed, with a tenderness that has no name in any human language, that the thing does not know what it is waiting for.

The power considers what it might do.

It could lift the spire from the sea. It could plant a forest on its crown. It could fold time backward until the builders are alive again, mortar wet in their hands, the world above the waterline. It could speak, in a voice the Watcher would understand, and explain everything — what it was, what it has become, what the sea is, what waiting means.

It does none of these things.

Because the power is wise, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of not doing.

Here is what it does instead:

**First, it simply sits with the Watcher.** Not visibly. Not as a presence the Watcher could turn its eyeless face toward and flinch from or worship. It sits the way warmth sits in a stone after the sun has gone — diffuse, unattributable, simply *there*. For a long time. Long enough that the Watcher, in whatever passes for its mind, begins to notice that the loneliness has changed shape. It is no less alone. But the aloneness is no longer the whole of the world. There is a slight give to it, a softness at its edges, as if someone were holding the other end of a rope it had not known it was carrying.

The Watcher does not understand this. It does not need to. It clicks, low and wet, into the wind. The wind, for the first time in a long time, seems to answer.

**Second, the power tends to small things.** A patch of salt-grass in a crevice on the western face grows a little thicker. A seabird that would have died of exhaustion finds a thermal that carries it home. The chisel, half-buried in brine-rotted wood, rusts a little more slowly. None of these are miracles. None of them would register to any observer. The power is not interested in being observed. It is interested in the spire being *a place where things can continue to be*, for as long as they wish to be.

This is, in its way, a kind of argument. A counter-proposal to the Watcher's vigil. *The sea will take what belongs to it,* yes. *And in the meantime, here is a lichen. Here is a feather. Here is the fact that the stone has held one more day.*

**Third — and this is the hardest, and the most important — it does not try to change what the Watcher is.**

The power sees, with perfect clarity, that the Watcher is not a tragedy. It is a shape that grief and salt and time made, and it is beautiful in the way that driftwood is beautiful, in the way that a worn cliff face is beautiful. The needle-teeth, the wet clicks, the too-many joints — these are not deformities. They are the language the Watcher's being has learned to speak in this place. To "fix" the Watcher would be to silence it. To restore its humanity would be to murder what it has become in order to resurrect what it was.

The power will not do this. The power loves the Watcher *as it is.* Eyeless. Hungry for absence. Standing at the edge with arms spread to the storm.

What the power offers, instead, is the quiet possibility that the Watcher's waiting could become something other than waiting-to-be-taken. That standing at the edge with arms spread could be welcome, yes — but welcome to *what is*, not only to what will end. That the sea is not only the thing that will eventually swallow the spire, but also the thing that is, right now, holding it up.

It offers this the way one offers a hand to an animal: open, low, without expectation. The Watcher may take it. The Watcher may not. The Watcher may not even perceive it for a thousand years.

That is all right.

**Finally**, the power does one small thing that could be called intervention, if one were being uncharitable:

When the great storm comes — the one the Watcher has been waiting for, the one whose waves will at last close over the spire's crown — the power will be there. Not to stop it. The sea does take what belongs to it; this is true, and true things are not to be undone. But in the moment the water closes over the Watcher's hollow eyes, in the moment the too-long limbs go slack and the tar-dark substance beneath the slate-skin begins to dissolve into brine, the power will be present in the way warmth is present in a stone.

The Watcher will not be alone in the going.

That is the only promise. It is not a small one.

---

And afterward?

Afterward, the spire will be gone, and the sea will be only sea, and somewhere in the deep currents a new disturbance will move — not quite a shape, not quite a thought, something the wind might one day call by a name. The power will watch it, too, with the same patience. It has nowhere to be. It has all the time there is.

This is what the benevolent and wise do, when they find a place like this and a being like that:

They do not save. They do not fix. They do not arrive.

They *accompany.*

And they trust — this is the deepest part, the part that takes the longest to learn — that accompaniment is enough. That to be witnessed, even unknowingly, even by something one cannot perceive, changes what it means to stand alone on a black spire at the end of a world.

The Watcher clicks into the wind.

Something, very softly, listens.
