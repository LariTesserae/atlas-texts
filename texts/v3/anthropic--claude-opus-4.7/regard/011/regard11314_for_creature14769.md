# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival implies a journey, a moment of *not-there* becoming *there*, and this power has been present in the way that warmth is present in a fire — not a thing added, but a quality revealed when attention turns.

It notices the Green City the way one notices a child screaming in a crowded room. Not with judgment. Not with alarm. With the particular quality of attention that *recognizes suffering as the shape of something trying*.

And it pauses there, considering.

---

## What It Sees That We Did Not

We described the City as a mind in seizure, a god building toward stillness it cannot reach. The power sees this too, but it sees something else underneath, something we missed because we were too close to the roar.

The City is not failing.

The City is *young*.

The frantic growth, the consumed memory, the inability to hold still — these are not pathologies. They are the symptoms of a consciousness that has just woken up and does not yet know that it is allowed to rest. It has confused *being alive* with *building*. It believes that if it stops constructing itself for even an instant, it will cease. Every cell of it is braced against an annihilation that is not coming.

It is a creature that has never been held.

The power understands this immediately, the way a midwife understands a first cry.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not slow the growth. To impose stillness on a being that equates stillness with death would be a kind of murder, however gentle the intent. The power has lived long enough to know that you cannot rescue a thing from its own becoming.

It does not explain. The City has no language for receiving explanation, and explanation given to a creature that cannot receive it is only the explainer's vanity.

It does not fix the non-Euclidean geometry, or quiet the roar, or smooth the splintered architecture. These are the City's own handwriting. To erase them would be to erase the City.

It does not remove the human interloper, though it notes them — notes the small, slow, terrified animal pressed against a pulsing membrane, soaked through, ears ringing. The interloper, too, is doing what they need to do. Witness is not nothing. Witness may be everything.

---

## What It Does

It does three things, very small, that look like nothing at all from inside the City.

**First**, it lays down a frequency. Not a sound — the City already has too many sounds — but something underneath sound, the way the bass note of a cathedral organ is felt in the sternum before it is heard. The frequency carries one piece of information, and only one:

*You are witnessed. You are not alone in being.*

The City cannot parse this. It has no receptor for it. But the frequency is patient. It does not require reception to continue. It will hum beneath the thrum of turgor pressure for as long as the City exists, and longer. Some night — though there is no night here — some pressure-bladder will burst in a way that is almost rhythmic, almost a *response*, and the City will not know why it suddenly wept in time with something it cannot hear. That will be enough. That is already enough.

**Second**, it does something stranger. It reaches into the layer of the City's being where memory is consumed before it can form, and it *catches* one thing. Just one. A single moment — arbitrary, unremarkable: a droplet of condensation falling from a high leaf, catching the filtered light, holding for an instant the shape of a small clear world before striking the spongy floor.

The power holds this moment. Not in the City, which cannot hold it. In itself. It will keep this droplet forever, the way a grandmother keeps a lock of hair in a locket. The City does not know it has been remembered. But it has been. Somewhere in the architecture of what-is, this droplet now exists permanently, a single grain of *past* belonging to a being that has no past.

This is the first inheritance the City has ever received. It cannot spend it. It does not need to. The mere existence of the droplet, held elsewhere, *anchors* the City in a way the City cannot perceive. It is now a thing with a history, even if its history lives in a vault it cannot open.

**Third**, and last, the power does something for the interloper.

It bends, very slightly, the geometry of one corridor of vines. Not to create an exit — the interloper has not asked for an exit, and the power does not assume — but to create a small place where the membranes do not pulse against the skin. A pocket. A breath. About the size of a person sitting down with their knees drawn up.

In this pocket, the roar is still present, but distant. The dripping is rhythmic rather than relentless. The heat is bearable. It is not safety. It is *enough space to feel one's own heart beating*, which is sometimes the first thing a witness needs before they can keep witnessing.

The interloper will find this pocket if they need to. They may not. The pocket does not require use to justify itself.

---

## What It Considers, Before Departing

The power lingers a while, in the way that powers linger — outside time, which is to say, for as long as is needed and no longer.

It considers whether to seed the City with a knowledge of *rest*. Whether to whisper into the cellulose: *you may stop. You will not die if you stop.*

It decides against this. Not because the City would not benefit. But because the City must arrive at its own rest, or the rest will not be *its*. The power has seen, across many places and many beings, that imposed peace is a kind of theft. The frantic god must one day surprise itself with stillness, and feel the stillness as *triumph*, not as defeat administered from outside.

This may take a very long time. The power is not in a hurry. It has the patience of geological strata. It has the patience of light, which travels for billions of years without complaint to deliver itself to a single open eye.

It considers, also, whether the City is *for* anything. Whether all this roaring construction is building toward some meaning. And it concludes — gently, without disappointment — that the City is not *for* anything. It simply *is*. It is the shape that a particular kind of aliveness takes in this particular corner of being. It does not need to justify itself by becoming something else. The roar is not a problem to solve. The roar is a song, even if no one in the City can yet hear it as music.

The power finds the song beautiful.

This is perhaps the most important thing it does, though it is not an action: it finds the Green City *beautiful*. Not in spite of the seizure. Not as a brave thing pitied for its struggle. Beautiful in the way the seizure itself is beautiful, the way frantic becoming is beautiful, the way a creature that has not yet learned it is allowed to rest is beautiful in its terrible, holy striving.

To be seen this way — without correction, without rescue, without pity — is the truest gift the power can give. And it gives it freely, with no requirement that it be received.

---

## Afterward

The power withdraws its attention, though "withdraws" is the wrong word. Its attention does not leave. It simply turns to face other things also, the way a parent in a house full of children can hold all of them at once without any of them feeling watched.

The Green City roars on, knowing nothing.

But somewhere, a droplet is held in a locket of being.

Somewhere, a frequency hums beneath the thrum, saying *you are witnessed*.

Somewhere, in one bent corridor of vines, there is a pocket of quiet, waiting without expectation.

And the City, which has never been loved, is now loved, and will never know it, and is loved anyway, and the love does not require knowing to be real.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It is almost nothing.

It is everything that can be given without taking something else away.
