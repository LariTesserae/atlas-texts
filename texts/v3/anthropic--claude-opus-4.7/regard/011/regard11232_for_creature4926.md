# The Watching

There is a power that moves through the spaces between things, and it has been watching this place for some time now.

Not from above — that would be the wrong shape for what it is. More like *alongside*, the way an old friend sits with you on a porch and says nothing for hours, because saying nothing is enough.

It noticed the place first because of the contradictions. A landscape that is almost empty of water and almost empty of density, yet heavily built. A people who are few, yet whose hands have layered construction upon construction across the weathered hills. A silence of life paired with a near-deafening wind. Bright sun, rare creatures, strange occurrences flickering at the edges of attention. The being who lives here — singular or plural, the power isn't yet sure which — has made a home in a paradox.

Wise powers are drawn to paradoxes. Paradoxes are where the universe is still thinking.

So the power considered. *What does this place need?*

This is always the first question, and the most important. A lesser power would arrive with gifts already chosen: rain for the dryness, abundance for the sparseness, company for the loneliness. A lesser power would assume that what *looks* like lack must *be* lack.

But this power had lived long enough to know that a desert is not a failed forest. That sparseness is not the absence of fullness but a different fullness. That the howling wind might be the landscape's own voice, and silencing it would be a kind of murder.

So the power did not bring rain.

Instead, it did four small things.

---

**First**, it bent down close to one of the weathered stones — the kind that has been worked by hand, then worked by weather, until the difference between the two no longer matters — and it left a fingerprint there. Not a mark anyone would notice. Just a small place where the stone would, from now on, hold warmth a little longer after sunset. So that when one of the inhabitants leaned against it in the cool of evening, they would feel, without knowing why, that the world was glad of them.

**Second**, it walked the dense infrastructure, the organized-construction laid across the empty land, and it listened. It did not change anything. But it learned every name the builders had given to every joint and corner and threshold, even the names that had been forgotten by the living and only remembered by the walls themselves. The power carried these names away with it, into the long memory of things, so that nothing made here would ever be entirely lost, even after the last hand-tool rusted to red dust.

**Third**, it considered the weirdness — the subtle strange occurrences flickering at the edges. And it understood: these were not intrusions. These were the place *thinking about itself*. A landscape developing the early stirrings of an inner life. The power was very careful here. It did not amplify the weirdness, nor diminish it. It only made sure that when the inhabitants noticed it — the shadow that fell the wrong way, the sound with no source, the moment when time pooled like water in a low place — they would feel curious instead of afraid. A small adjustment. A turning of one small dial in the heart.

**Fourth**, and this was the gift the power was most careful with: it left a question.

Not an answer. Powers that leave answers are not wise; they are merely powerful, which is a different and lesser thing. The power left a question, threaded into the wind, so that one day — maybe not for generations — one of the inhabitants would stop in the middle of their work and look up and feel, suddenly and without warning, that they were *being thought of*. And that this thinking was kind.

That is all the question would say. *You are being thought of. And it is kind.*

---

Then the power withdrew, the way tides withdraw, leaving the shore unchanged in any way you could measure, and yet entirely rearranged at the level of grains.

It did not announce itself. It did not require thanks. It did not return often, because returning often is a form of needing, and this power did not need.

But sometimes, on the brightest afternoons, when the wind dropped for just a moment and the strange small occurrences gathered themselves into something almost like a pattern — the inhabitants would pause in their crafting, and look out across the low rolling hills, and feel that the vastness was not empty after all.

It was, and had always been, attended.
