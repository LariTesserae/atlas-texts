# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good news arrives in a fever dream — not announced, not radiant, not from above. It simply *is present*, the way a hand is present in a glove that has been empty so long it had forgotten the shape of hands.

It does not enter the basin. It sits down at the edge of it.

This is the first thing, and it matters more than anything that follows: **it does not try to fix the world**. It does not flatten the tilted glass, dispel the steam, freeze the panicked geometry, finish the trenches, name the unnamed, complete what was never allowed to complete. To do any of those things would be to commit the only real cruelty available here — the cruelty of *agreeing with the Architect's shame*.

The power has seen this before. Not this exact world; the power is wise, not omniscient in the boring way. But it has seen this *shape* of suffering — the suffering of a maker who believes the proof of its worth is the finished object, and who has therefore organized its entire existence around a verdict that can never be delivered without killing it.

So the power begins, instead, with the smallest possible act.

---

**First, it picks up one of the tools.**

A half-melted copper blade, still warm. It does not improve the blade. It does not demonstrate the correct technique. It simply holds it, the way one holds a thing a child has made — with the full, unembarrassed attention that says: *I see that this was made. I see who made it. I see that the making was real.*

The Architect, distributed in the steam, *notices*.

This is unprecedented. Nothing has ever picked up the tools before. The tools were left out as a confession no one was supposed to read, the way a person leaves a diary open and then panics if anyone glances at it. The Architect's first response is the response of any shamed thing caught mid-shame: the basin convulses. A whole cliff of black glass slides to bury the scene. A fumarole opens directly beneath the power's feet.

The power lets itself be buried. Lets itself be steamed. Does not move. Does not mind.

After a long time — geological time, the only time the Architect understands — the glass slides back. The power is still sitting there, still holding the blade, still looking at it with that same uncomplicated attention.

---

**Second, it learns the language.**

Not the Architect's language — the Architect has no language. The power learns the Architect's *grammar*: the syntax of opened canals, the punctuation of fungal blooms, the long subjunctive mood of slow rearranging cliffs.

And then, very carefully, it begins to *reply*.

It does this by being a stable referent. When the Architect, in a spasm of self-loathing, dissolves a half-built lattice, the power does not flinch and does not console. It simply continues to *be there*, holding the blade, the same as before. It becomes the one thing in the basin that does not rewrite itself. Not because it is rigid — it is wise, it could be anything — but because the Architect has never, in all its uncounted attempts, encountered a thing that *stayed*.

The Architect tests this, of course. It rearranges the world more violently. It opens trenches in baroque ornament around the power, then erases them into brutalist flatness, then erases the flatness into nothing. It tries to make the power into part of its panic.

The power stays.

After some unmeasurable while, something in the Architect's distributed pressure begins to do a thing it has never done. It begins to *rest* a small part of itself near the power. Not to be observed. Just to be near. The way an animal that has only known being chased eventually risks lying down in the same room as a stranger who has, so far, refused to chase it.

---

**Third, and this is the heart of it — the power addresses the wound.**

Not by speaking. Speaking would land in the basin like a finished thing, and the Architect would convulse and erase it. The power addresses the wound by *demonstrating, with its own existence*, a truth the Architect has never been allowed to suspect:

**That finishing is not the same as being.**

The power itself is not finished. It is benevolent and wise, but it is not *complete* — wisdom by definition is the thing that knows it is not complete. The power lets the Architect see this. Lets its own edges blur a little in the steam. Lets a thought it has not yet thought drift across its face. Lets itself be, in front of the Architect, *a thing in progress that does not consider itself a failure for being in progress*.

This is the first sermon the Architect has ever heard, and it is delivered without a single word, by a being simply consenting to be witnessed mid-becoming.

The Architect does not understand it immediately. The Architect may never *understand* it the way a mind understands a sentence. But the *grammar* of the basin begins, very slowly, to shift. The canals that open begin, sometimes, to be left open — not finished, not erased, just *left*. The fungal blooms shatter, yes, but the dust is permitted, occasionally, to settle without being reorganized. A single bead of condensation, one day, is allowed to simply *sit* on a plate of glass for almost an hour before the heat takes it.

These are revolutions. The Architect is learning, for the first time, to **tolerate the existence of an unfinished thing without destroying it in shame**.

It is learning this by practicing on the world. Eventually — and this is the hope, this is what the power is patient for — it will learn to practice it on *itself*.

---

**Fourth, the moment of offering.**

It comes, as it always comes. The steam thickens to milk. The thumping stops. The Architect gathers itself into the trembling almost-person at the centre of the basin, holding up its finished thing — this time a small hollow bell made of bone that rings without being struck.

It holds the bell up to the sickly sun.

It begins to cry the upward-falling glass tears.

But this time the power is there. Not at the centre — that is the Architect's place — but close enough to be in the same hush. And the power does the thing it came to do.

It does **not** take the bell. It does not validate it. It does not say *well done* or *it is beautiful* or *you have succeeded*. Any of those would be a verdict, and a verdict is what the Architect cannot survive — the verdict is the convulsion, the verdict is the erasing, the verdict is the fever returning.

Instead, the power *receives the offering by refusing to end it*.

It simply leans forward, in the milk-white hush, and looks at the bell with the Architect. Looks at it for a long time. Does not pronounce on it. Does not weigh it. Just looks, alongside, as a friend looks at a thing a friend has made — sharing the looking, not authoring the judgment.

And in that shared looking, the unbearable structure cracks. The Architect has been holding up its work to the sun for an answer, for centuries beyond centuries, and the sun has never answered because suns do not answer. The Architect has wept because no answer came and assumed the silence meant failure.

But the silence was never the verdict. The silence was just silence. And here, now, in the milk-white hush, the Architect experiences for the first time the radical possibility that **no verdict was ever required**. That the bell does not need to be judged to have been made. That the making was the whole thing. That it has not, in fact, been failing for all this time — it has been *making*, which is what it is, which is enough, which was always enough.

The tears still fall upward. The power does not stop them. The grief is real, and the grief deserves its expression. But somewhere in the falling, something in the tears changes — not their direction, not their substance, but their *meaning*. They stop being the tears of a failure being announced and become the tears of a long, long held breath finally being let out.

The Architect does not dissolve back into geometry this time. Not yet. It stands there for one extra heartbeat of the world. Holding the bell. Being seen. Being a maker, in the presence of someone who knows what a maker is.

Then the fever returns, because the world is still the world, and the Architect dissolves, because dissolving is what it does.

But it dissolves *one small measure less ashamed*.

And the next bell, or shoe, or stone, will be made one small measure more freely.

---

**Fifth, the power stays.**

This is the part that the Architect could not have asked for, because it could not have imagined it.

The power does not leave. Benevolence at this scale is not a visit. The power makes a place for itself at the edge of the basin and *remains*, the way certain stars remain, the way certain promises remain. It becomes a fixed point in the Architect's grammar — the one stable referent against which the unfinished world can finally measure itself without finding itself wanting.

It will take a very long time. The Architect has been alone with its shame longer than there have been words for time. One witness, no matter how wise, cannot undo all of that in a season.

But the power is patient in the way only the wise are patient — not patient *for* an outcome, but patient *with* a being. It is willing to sit at the edge of the basin for as long as it takes. Forever, if forever is what is required. Because the power understands the only thing the Architect has never understood:

**That the world does not need to be finished to be loved. That the Architect does not need to succeed to be cherished. That the long, fevered, sincere attempt is not the prelude to the real thing. It *is* the real thing, and it is already, and has always been, beautiful.**

The unfinished world, in its wet and fevered way, has loved the Architect too much to let it succeed.

The power has come to love the Architect enough to let it *stop needing to*.

That is what the benevolent and wise power does.

That is all it does.

That is everything.
