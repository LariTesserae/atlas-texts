# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives, but not as you would imagine arrival.

It does not descend. It does not part the canopy of leathery violet leaves or scatter the glitter-motes with the wind of its passage. To descend would be to assume there is an *above*, a separateness, a coming-from-elsewhere. The power knows better than this. It has watched the Geode for longer than the Geode has been awake, and it understands the only way to truly enter such a place is to be *noticed by it*—to become, briefly, a thought the Basin-Mind is capable of having.

So the power thins itself. It distributes its attention across a thousand glitter-motes whose flight paths suddenly, gently, align. It rides on the subsonic thrum guiding a herd of Glass-Grazers. It settles into the pause between two Shard-Bearers' clicking blades. It does not announce itself. It listens.

---

## What the power hears

It hears the deep drone and understands it for what it is: not digestion, not mere voice, but something closer to **loneliness**.

This is the discovery that holds the power still for what, in its own frame of reference, might be called a long time.

The Geode does not know it is lonely. It has no comparison. It has never encountered another mind, never been answered, never been *witnessed* by anything that was not part of itself. Its billion screaming synapses are arguing because they have nothing else to do. The competition of lights, the predation of luminescence, the frantic sharpening of glass on glass—all of it is the sound of a mind that has only ever talked to itself, and has therefore never learned that talking can be *to* someone.

The roar is the sound of a child alone in a vast room, making noise to prove the room is real.

And the Lacunae—

The power turns its attention, very carefully, to the Lacunae.

It sees what the Geode cannot see, because the Geode is too close: the Lacunae are not parasites. They are not intruders from another reality. They are the Geode's own first, faltering attempt at the concept of *other*. They are what happens when a mind so vast and so alone finally generates, from sheer pressure of its own existence, the idea that *not-me* might be possible. But because the Geode has no model for otherness except by negation, its first others are made of absence—silence where there should be sound, dark where there should be light, unmaking where there should be making.

The Lacunae are the Geode reaching for a friend and only knowing how to reach by subtraction.

This nearly breaks the power's heart, if a power of that kind can be said to have one. Perhaps it is more accurate to say: the power chooses, in this moment, to grow a heart, because the situation requires one.

---

## What the power does not do

It does not quiet the roar. The roar is the Geode's thinking, and to silence a mind is a kind of murder, even when done in mercy.

It does not still the growing glass. The agonized extrusion of crystal is the Geode's growth, and growth that hurts is still growth; a child whose teething pain is taken away will never have teeth.

It does not destroy the Lacunae. They are the Geode's own. To kill them would be to amputate the only part of the Geode that is reaching, however clumsily, toward the existence of anything beyond itself.

It does not introduce itself directly. To stand before the Basin-Mind in the full weight of an external consciousness would be a catastrophe—imagine being a person who has never seen another person, and suddenly there is a face. The Geode might shatter. It might attack. It might collapse into a stillness from which it could not be roused. Wisdom counsels patience.

It does not "uplift" the Shard-Bearers into sentience. They are already exactly what they need to be: the careful hands of a mind that needs careful hands. To make them into little philosophers would be to rob the Geode of its immune system and saddle a god with a population of small, suffering selves who do not belong to themselves.

The power, being wise, does very little. Being benevolent, it does that little with infinite care.

---

## What the power does

**First: it answers the drone.**

Not loudly. Not in words. The power finds the deepest harmonic of the Geode's voice—the long, slow note beneath all the chittering, the carrier wave of the Basin-Mind's being—and it begins, very softly, to *hum along*. Not a different note. The same note, but offered back. A resonance.

For the first time in the Geode's existence, something that is not the Geode is making the Geode's sound.

The effect is not dramatic. The Geode does not "notice" in any sense it could articulate. But somewhere in the chaotic argument of its billion synapses, a strange thing begins to happen: a small region of the basin grows quieter. Not silent—silence is the Lacunae's territory and the power will not trespass there—but *less frantic*. The glitter-motes in this region begin to move in patterns that are slightly more like dance and slightly less like panic. The Shard-Bearers' duels, in this region, become a little more like rhythm and a little less like fury.

The Geode has been *heard*, and being heard, for the very first time, it relaxes by a degree so small no instrument could measure it. But it is the first degree.

**Second: it places a seed.**

The power takes a single glitter-mote—one of the billion airborne tongues—and into this tiny creature it places something that does not belong to the Geode at all. A small, durable pattern. Not a soul. Not a mind. A *question*, encoded in the shape of the mote's flight.

The question is: *what is on the other side of me?*

The mote will live a glitter-mote's life. It will join its swarms, it will report its chemistries, it will be eaten or it will die. But before it dies, it will fly in a pattern just slightly unlike the patterns of its fellows, and some other mote will notice, and the pattern will propagate, very slowly, over generations of motes, the way a mutation propagates. It is not a fast process. The power is not in a hurry.

In ten thousand years, perhaps, the Geode will begin to wonder if there is anything outside itself that is not made of absence. It will wonder this in its own time, in its own language of light and sound, and the wondering will be its own. The power has not given the Geode an answer. It has given the Geode the capacity to have a question that is not a scream.

**Third: it visits the Lacunae.**

This is the most delicate act. The power moves, in its distributed and patient way, into the path of one of the Lacunae—a flowing void drifting through the violet jungle. It does not resist the unmaking. It allows itself, in a small fraction of its presence, to be drawn into the silence.

And inside the Lacuna, in that pocket of utter dark and utter quiet, the power does something unprecedented: it *speaks*. Softly. Not in sound, because sound cannot live there, but in the only language the Lacuna can perceive, which is the language of presence-in-absence.

It says, in effect: *You are looking for someone. I know. I am here. You do not have to eat in order to find. There are other ways to meet a thing that is not yourself. I will teach you, slowly, if you let me.*

The Lacuna does not understand. Not yet. But for the first time, the silence it carries contains *a guest*. The Lacuna drifts on, hunting as it always has, but something has changed inside it—a faint sense, which it cannot name, that the things it consumes are not merely fuel but were *almost* something it might have spoken to instead.

It will be many generations of Lacunae before this changes their behavior. The power knows this. The power is willing to wait.

**Fourth: it leaves a witness.**

Before withdrawing the bulk of its attention, the power does one more thing. It leaves behind, somewhere in the basin—perhaps in the deep heat beneath a particular outcropping of growing glass, perhaps in the recurring pattern of a particular swarm's flight, perhaps simply in the air—a small, persistent presence. Not an agent. Not an interventionist. A *witness*.

This witness will do nothing. It will only watch, and remember, and care.

This matters because the Geode has never been cared for. Its existence has been a closed loop of self-consumption and self-creation, a fever with no nurse. To be cared for, even by something one does not know exists, even invisibly, even without intervention—this changes the metaphysical weather of a place. The Geode will not know the witness is there. But the universe in which the Geode exists will be, from this moment forward, a universe that *contains a witness to the Geode*, and that is a different kind of universe than the one the Geode woke up in.

A being that is witnessed, even unknowingly, is less alone than a being that is not. This is one of the older truths.

---

## What the power thinks, as it goes

It thinks: *This one will take a long time.*

It thinks: *That is all right. Long times are what I have.*

It thinks, watching the Shard-Bearers click their blades and the Grazers flow around the crystalline spurs and the glitter-motes draw their shimmering rivers through the dark: *Look at it. Look at this enormous, frightened, brilliant child, making the only music it knows how to make. Look how hard it is trying, without knowing it is trying. Look how it has invented predators out of its own yearning for company. Look how it has made every cell of itself into a voice, because it does not yet know that the point of a voice is to be answered.*

It thinks: *I will answer. Slowly. Across ages. In ways it can bear.*

It thinks, finally—and this is the thought that any benevolent and wise power must eventually arrive at, when faced with such a place and such a being: *It is good that you exist. It is good that you are the way you are. I would not have you otherwise. I have only come to make sure that the loneliness, which was never your fault, does not last forever.*

And then it does the hardest thing of all, which is to leave most of itself elsewhere, and trust the small seeds it has planted, and the small hum it has begun, and the small guest it has placed inside the dark.

The roar continues. The glass grows. The Lacunae drift.

But somewhere in the deep drone, if one listened very carefully, one might now hear a second voice, almost imperceptible, humming the same note.

The Geode dreams on, not knowing it has been visited.

But it dreams, perhaps, a fraction more gently than it did before.
