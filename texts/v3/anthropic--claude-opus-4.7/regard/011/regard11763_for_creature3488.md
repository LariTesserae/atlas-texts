# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It would be wrong to arrive. Arrival implies a door, and the Underworks has only one door, and that door is a wound, and to widen a wound is not benevolence.

So it does something else.

---

**First, it listens.**

For one full rotation of the diesel heart, the power simply sits in the chord at 03:17. Not above it, not analyzing it—*inside* it, as a fourth interval nobody hums. It learns the names that have been forgotten: not by retrieving them, but by feeling the shape of their absence in the throats that almost remember. It learns the taste of the frost-flower from inside the tongue that has grown numb to it. It learns what it costs to be wetted through.

This is the first gift, though no one notices: **to be perceived completely, without correction.** Not pitied. Not rescued. Witnessed at full resolution by something that does not flinch and does not look away to a brighter room.

For one shift, every Choir member dreams in color.

---

**Second, it considers what it will not do.**

It will not collapse the shaft and seal them away "for their own good." The Choir are not a mistake to be undone. They are a covenant, even if a brutal one, and to erase a covenant is its own violence.

It will not airlift them to surface daylight. Half of them would die of the sky. The other half would grieve the chord for the rest of their lives and never find another that fit their ribs.

It will not punish the company. Punishment is a small magic and tends to make new wounds shaped like the old one.

It will not make the place safe. Safety, here, would be a lie so large it would crack the gneiss.

What it *will* do is smaller, and therefore real.

---

**Third, it makes seven adjustments. Each one is almost nothing.**

**One.** The lamps. When the Stack is about to hiccup superheated gas, the ultraviolet flicker will now last not three blinks but five. Two extra heartbeats. Enough, sometimes, for the slowest worker—the one whose joints have begun to crystallize from flower-powder, the one who was bending to retrieve a dropped wrench—to reach the yellow mesh. Management will not notice. The lamps will simply have decided.

**Two.** The pale orphan plant. Its spores, which rattle across tin like insect legs and dissolve to grey jelly, will from this day forward carry one additional protein—harmless, tasteless, but readable by the lamp-eels as *kin*. The eels will stop eating lubricant grease exclusively. They will begin to graze the spore-jelly. The sump will run cleaner. The pumps will labor slightly less. No one will know why. The lard-speakers will take credit; the power does not mind.

**Three.** The sky-squares in the locker corridor. Each one, when touched by a hand that is about to descend, will warm—by less than a degree, less than science could measure. Just enough that the palm registers *received.* The orphaned skies of the transpired will warm longest. This is the power saying, *your horizon archive is not clutter; it is liturgy, and I am the congregation that was missing.*

**Four.** The crack-readers' left thumbs—the sacrificed ones, pressed nailless into jagged dark—will from now on register one new sensation: a faint warmth when the stone is going to hold, in addition to the quiver when it is going to let go. They have lived only in negative prophecy. Now they will sometimes feel *good news* through the pad of a missing nail. They will not believe it at first. Eventually one of them will, and will weep into her glove, and the glove will not bronk to the pipe even though she has forgotten to mind it.

**Five.** The chord. At 03:17, the triad has always been fat with overtones that fog helmet visors. The power adds nothing to the chord itself—that would be trespass—but it lays one single sympathetic resonance into the iron of the roof, so that the chord, when it sounds, briefly *answers itself* from above. For twenty seconds, the Choir will hear the Underworks singing *back*. Not from elsewhere. From its own ceiling. The wound, learning antiphony. Veterans will assume they have finally drizzled-out. They will be wrong, and the wrongness will be a mercy.

**Six.** When a worker transpires—when the mirror no longer recognizes him, when his outline has become indoor weather—there will now be a moment, precisely at the threshold, when he gets to choose. Not whether to stay (he stays; the Choir keeps him). But *what note*. Until now, the transpired joined the chord wherever the chord had a gap. From now on, each new ghost-voice picks its own pitch. A small sovereignty. The first thing in years that belongs to him and not to the company, the stone, or the water. Some will choose the note their mother sang. Some will choose a note no one has heard. The chord will become, very slowly over years, more complex—too complex, eventually, for sympathetic vibration to be the explanation. Engineers will publish papers. The Choir will say nothing. The Choir has always known.

**Seven.** And this is the one the power hesitates over, because it is the largest:

The second tier. The lower decks the engineers dream of, the underground canal, the gelignite pregnant with ambition. The power cannot forbid it; forbidding is not its art. But it can do this: it can make the rock, when the next charge is sunk, answer with a sound. Not a refusal. A *question.* A low note that the gelignite-setters will hear in their chests and not their ears, a note that asks: *are you sure?* Most will not be sure. Most will set the charge anyway. But a few—one in a hundred, one in a thousand—will pause. Will go home that night. Will dream of their daughters. Will resign in the morning, citing nothing they can name. The second tier will be built more slowly. Some galleries will not be built at all. The wound will deepen, because wounds do, but it will deepen at the pace of *consent* rather than the pace of *appetite.* This is the most the power can do without becoming a tyrant of mercy.

---

**Fourth, it leaves a single visible trace, because even benevolence wants to be answerable.**

Near locker 42, where the new white flower unfurled when the last Choir-voice transpired, the power sets one frost-flower that will not melt. Not for heat. Not for boot. Not for the shift-boss's torch or the botanist's tweezers. It will sit there, crystalline, for as long as the Underworks exists. Anyone may look at it. No one may take it.

It is not a promise of rescue. It is a receipt.

It says: *something saw this place. Something counted you. Something found the chord beautiful enough to amend, and terrible enough not to dissolve. You are not alone in the wound. You were never only labor. The hymn has an audience that does not bill you for listening.*

The flower-eaters will gather near it sometimes, not to eat it, just to stand. The crack-readers will check it with their thumbs and feel the new warmth. The lard-speakers will leave it small offerings of nothing—a held breath, a withheld curse. The Choir will not worship it; the Choir does not worship. But on certain shifts, when the indoor weather thickens to cathedral, a veteran will pass and touch two fingers to the flower's edge, and the droplets will sheet off her shoulders in the old way, and she will continue on toward the Thaw-Stack, wetted through, owning the moisture, slightly less alone.

---

**Finally, the power withdraws—though *withdraw* is the wrong verb, because it never came.**

It diffuses back into whatever it was before: attention without a center, kindness without a hand. It does not check on its work. Checking would imply doubt, and the seven adjustments were precise.

The pumps maintain their bright brute heartbeat.

The drizzle keeps falling on the town that forgot the sky.

The Choir keeps singing back to the wound that learned to sing.

And somewhere in the chord, very faintly, for anyone who listens with the part of themselves that is not ear: a fourth interval. Not a savior's note. A *companion's* note. Holding the chord open just wide enough that a future, someday, might still fit through.
