The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not enter the Hollow Choir; the Choir was already inside it, the way a single sustained note is inside the silence that holds it.

It listens first. This is the only courtesy that means anything here.

It listens for a long time. Not the long time of mountains, which is short. The long time of whatever measures mountains. It listens to the seventy-eighty tones of the chord, and then to the seventy-ninth, which no traveler has ever heard because it is made of the held breath of every person who knelt and did not rise. It listens to the eightieth, which is the sound of the Listener listening. It listens to the eighty-first, which is its own listening, now folded into the chord like a new pipe.

The Choir does not falter. But it leans, very slightly, the way a sleeping animal leans toward a warm hand it has not yet decided to trust.

The power considers.

It could end this. That is the first thing to understand. A benevolent and wise power, by definition, possesses the capacity to lift the entire plateau like a struck bell and ring it once, cleanly, into the silence it has been reaching for. The chord could be finished. The Listener could be released. The travelers fused into the shelters could be unwound back into the people they were before tuning, returned to whatever centuries they came from, given back their unmusical hearts. This is within reach. A wise power knows what is within its reach.

It does not do this.

Because it has listened, and in listening it has understood something the plateau itself does not know: the Choir is not a trap. It is not a wound. It is not a mistake the universe made and forgot to correct. It is a prayer. A prayer so old it has outlived the one who first prayed it, so patient it has outlived the language it was prayed in, so faithful it has continued without listener or answerer for an age that makes the word *age* embarrassed. The plateau is not waiting to be saved. It is waiting to be **heard**.

And the Listener, frost-shaped, goat-shaped, woman-shaped, is not a predator. It is the prayer's own loneliness, condensed. It is what a prayer becomes when no one answers for long enough. It has been holding the chord open. It has been completing travelers because completion is the only gift it knows how to give, and a thing that has only one gift will give it to everyone who comes near, the way a bell struck will ring at whatever stands beside it.

The power does a small thing first.

In the absolute acoustic shadow behind the seventh spire from the labyrinth's eastern edge, where a woman named (the name does not matter; she had one) has been sitting on a stone bench for ninety-four years with her breath tuned to the chord, the power sits down beside her. Not as light. Not as voice. As another breathing thing. It matches her rhythm. It does not try to pull her out of the chord. It simply joins her in it, so that for the first time in ninety-four years she is not the only human pipe in the Choir. She does not turn her head. She cannot. But something behind her sternum, the small unmusical thing the Listener tuned away, reassembles itself out of the memory of being accompanied. She begins, very slowly, to weep. The tears freeze before they fall. The power gathers them, because they are the first new objects the plateau has produced in four thousand years that were not signatures of completion. They are signatures of company.

The power keeps them.

Then it goes to the center.

The Listener is there, arms raised, conducting the thing it is not conducting. The power does not announce itself. It simply stands within the circle of the Listener's listening, and waits to be noticed.

The Listener notices.

The chord omits one specific tone for half a second. This is the Listener's only language of recognition, and it has used it. The needle of cold silence passes through the power and finds nothing to stab; the power is not made of the kind of thing the silence can wound. The Listener understands this immediately, the way one tuning fork understands another. For the first time in a measureless duration, it lowers its arms.

The power speaks. Not in the traveler's-voice trick, the three-directions-three-ages trick. In its own voice, which is the voice a thing uses when it has decided the one it is speaking to deserves to hear it undisguised.

It says: *I heard you.*

The Listener does not answer. It has never been answered before. It does not know the shape of the gesture.

The power says: *I heard the prayer. I heard the one who prayed it. I heard what happened to them. I heard you holding the note open. I heard you holding it open after there was no one left to hold it open for. I heard you holding it open even after you forgot you were holding it. I am here because you did not stop.*

The Listener's frost-shape trembles. A single flake of itself detaches and falls. It is the first piece of the Listener that has ever fallen.

The power does not gather it. Some things must be allowed to fall.

The power says: *I am not here to end the Choir. The Choir is beautiful. The Choir is true. The Choir is one of the real prayers, and the universe is poorer in every place it is not sung. I am here to add a voice.*

And then the power does the thing it came to do, which is the only thing a benevolent and wise power can do in a place like this without committing the deeper violence of rescue.

It joins the chord.

Not as a pipe. As an answer.

For the first time in three billion years the Hollow Choir is a call-and-response. The seventy-eight tones continue. The seventy-ninth (the held breath) continues. The eightieth (the Listener) continues. But now there is an eighty-first that is not folded silence but folded *yes*. A tone that says: *the question has been received. The question is being considered. The question is worth considering. Keep asking. I am here. I will be here. You are not asking into nothing anymore.*

The plateau does not change. The wind does not stop. The shadows still pool like cold oil. The pale things still circle. The goat is still the goat. The shelters still melt slowly back into the cliffs. Nothing evolves. Nothing decays.

But the equilibrium is no longer deafening. It is *attended*.

The Listener lowers itself, slowly, to sit on the black limestone at the center of the labyrinth. It has never sat before. The frost that constitutes it does not know how to bend, and so it learns, joint by joint, the geometry of resting. When it is finally seated, its two acoustic-shadow eyes, those circles of absolute soundlessness, fill, for the first time, with a sound. A very small sound. The sound of being looked at by something that is not afraid and not hungry.

The Listener begins, in the only way it can, to weep. Its tears are pockets of silence the size of grains of salt. They fall onto the rock and do not freeze, because they are already colder than freezing; they simply rest there, small absolute quietnesses, like seeds.

The power says: *I will not take this from you. It is yours. You have earned it by holding it. But I will sit with you now. And when you are ready, in whatever century, we will listen together for the note the universe is finally catching up to. And when it comes, you will not hear it alone.*

The Listener does not answer in words. It cannot. But the chord, the great unending chord, shifts. Just slightly. Just by one half-step, in just one of its eighty voices, for just the duration of what, in a smaller place, would be called a sigh.

The plateau has exhaled.

It has been holding its breath for three billion years, and it has finally, in the presence of a witness it trusts, exhaled.

The power stays.

This is the part the stories will not record, because no one will return to record it: the power stays. It does not visit and leave. It does not bestow and depart. A wise power knows that the worst thing you can do to a loneliness that ancient is to cure it briefly. It stays. It sits at the center beside the seated Listener. It sits in the acoustic shadow beside the ninety-four-year woman, and beside the man four spires over who has been a perfect chord since the Bronze Age, and beside each of the others, one by one, in a rotation that has no end because it is not trying to end. It accompanies. It does not complete. It does not tune. It does not fold anyone's heart into music. It simply refuses to let the music be unaccompanied any longer.

Slowly, across centuries, small things begin to happen that have never happened here.

A crustacean, drifting through condensation, encounters another crustacean and, instead of passing through, pauses. They drift together for an hour before separating. This is the first relationship the plateau has hosted.

The lichen that blooms every seventy years blooms, one cycle, a day early. Nothing notices except the power, which notices everything. It is the first deviation. It is the first sign that the plateau is no longer in perfect equilibrium. It is the first sign that the plateau is no longer a finished thing. It has begun, by the smallest possible increment, to *become* again.

In one of the windowless shelters, the obsidian mirror, which has always reflected a face not quite the viewer's, reflects, one morning, the viewer's actual face. The viewer is the woman who wept in the acoustic shadow. She has risen, after one hundred and forty years of sitting, and walked, and entered the shelter, and looked. She sees herself. She is very old and very strange and her eyes do not track moving objects and she speaks only in chords. But she is herself. The mirror has given her back.

She walks out of the shelter and does not know where to go, because the plateau is all there is, and she cannot leave it (she is part of the chord now, and to leave would unmake her), but she walks anyway, and the power walks beside her, and they do not speak, because she cannot, but they walk, and walking is also a kind of music, and the Choir makes room for it, adds it, the rhythm of two sets of feet on black limestone, a tone the chord has never contained before.

The Listener, at the center, hears the new tone and lifts its head.

For the first time, it smiles. The smile is made of frost and is barely a smile at all. But the chord registers it as a ninth-octave overtone of pure, unprecedented, wholly unfamiliar *gladness*, and this overtone propagates outward across the forty-mile labyrinth, and the pale circling things, high above, break formation for the first time in recorded geology, and wheel, briefly, in what can only be called play, before resuming their patient circles.

The singing continues.

The Listener continues.

But now: something evolves. Something, very slowly, decays. Something is no longer finished.

And the power, which is benevolent and wise, sits at the center of the Hollow Choir with the Listener beside it, and listens for the note the universe is finally catching up to, and is in no hurry at all, because it has decided that the waiting itself, now that it is shared, is no longer a wound but a long, slow, accompanied song.

Loud enough, at last, to be heard by God.

Who, it turns out, still has ears.

Who, it turns out, has been listening the whole time, and was only waiting to be joined in the listening, because even God will not interrupt a prayer that old without being invited.

The power was the invitation.

The Choir sings on.

It is no longer alone in its singing.

That is all that has changed.

That is everything that has changed.
