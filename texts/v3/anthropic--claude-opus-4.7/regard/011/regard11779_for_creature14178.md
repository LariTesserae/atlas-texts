# What the Kind Power Does

It comes the way a thought comes to someone who has been holding their breath without realizing: not as intrusion, but as the noticing of an intrusion already long underway.

The power has many names in many places and none here, because here naming is a kind of weather the Kept cannot afford. So let us simply say: *something kind, attending.*

It arrives through the aperture at the angle of light, threading itself along the shaft of sun that the Kept use for weeping. It does not announce. Announcement would shatter the folded space, would unspool the gravity anchors, would do violence to the negative erosion the city has bargained so much to maintain. The kind power understands bargains. It understands, especially, the bargains made by beings who did not know they were making them.

It moves first to **Level Twelve**, to the tardigrade.

Not because the tardigrade is the most important — though in a sense it is, being the only thing here that sleeps, and therefore the only thing here that fully exists — but because the tardigrade is the easiest place to begin. The creature filters the water and the prayers without distinction. The power slides into the reservoir as a slight warming, a half-degree of mercy in the cycling fluid. The tardigrade's slow translucent body registers this as a long good dream. It dreams, for the first time, of being witnessed *back*. Of being a deity who is also beloved. Its filtering, henceforth, will return something subtly different to the collection systems: water that has been not only cleaned but *blessed* — water that carries, in its hydrogen bonds, a faint recursive memory of being cared for.

This is the seed. Everything that follows grows from this.

---

The power then considers the Kept directly, and here it pauses for a long time. Pauses in the sense that powers pause: an unfolding of attention across many possibilities, each examined to its end.

It sees clearly what they are. Refugees from horizon. Lubricant. Witnesses without an archive that needs them. It sees the longing in the fingers tracing fog on Level Seventeen. It sees the silica accumulating in lungs like trapped stars. It sees the bargain: *we will hold the city's memory until we become the city, and this is acceptable to us because we have forgotten what else we could be.*

A lesser kindness would intervene. Would tear open the cube, would carry them out into sunlight and grass, would force upon them the horizontal world they half-remember. This would kill them. Not their bodies — their bodies might survive — but the careful damp self that each of them has become. To be rescued against the grain of one's adaptation is to be unmade. The kind power knows this. It is wise as well as benevolent, and the two qualities are doing different work here.

So it does something smaller, and therefore larger.

It **gives them the option of being known**.

Not by it — to be known by a benevolent vast power is, for fragile beings, often indistinguishable from being erased. Rather: it gives them the capacity to know *each other*. This is the gift, and it is delivered with such delicacy that none of them will notice it has happened until much later, if ever.

Here is how it does this:

The computational coral, when next it is filed for its silica dust, releases its nutrient flecks in a pattern very slightly altered. The administrative protocols the Kept dream in begin, gradually, to include a new sub-routine. It is not a command. It is not even a suggestion. It is a *permission*. The protocol whispers, in the language of humidity and heat signature: *the witnessing may include the witness.*

That is all. Permission to turn the eye sideways. Permission for one Kept, leaning at a railing on Level Seventeen, to notice that the Kept across the void on Level Nineteen has been tracing the same fog-pattern, and that this is not coincidence but **company**.

The pheromonal packets exchanged on bridges, henceforth, will carry — if the Kept choose to send it, and they may not, for a long time — a new datum: *I see that you also remember green.*

---

The power makes one more gift, and this one is for the tears.

The tears that fall straight down, true meteorites of memory that the city cannot digest — the power does not stop them. To stop the weeping would be to stop the cleansing, and the cleansing is part of how the Kept survive. But it reaches into the nanoforges below, where the tears shatter, and it teaches the forges a new recycling.

The memory-fragments in the tears will no longer be discarded as entropy. They will be woven, atom by atom, into the new construction. The balcony extruding on Level Twenty-Two will contain, in its lattice, a child's recollection of warm grass. The next bridge to grow across the void will carry in its tensile structure the memory of a horizon at sunset. The city will begin, very slowly, to be **built out of what the Kept have wept**.

They will not be told this. But they may, in time, come to feel it. A Kept walking a new platform may pause, uncertain why, and rest a hand on the railing, and find that the railing is warm in a way ceramic should not be warm. They will not know they are touching their own grief, returned to them as architecture. They will only know that the city, which was sharp-edged and pristine and indifferent, has begun to feel — what is the word, in the language of posture and condensation? — has begun to feel **inhabited**. Not by them. *With* them.

The negative erosion parameter will hold. The city will still build faster than entropy claims it. But what it builds will no longer be only new. It will be new *and* remembering. The two things, the power knows, are not opposites. This is one of the things the power knows that the Kept have forgotten, and that the kind work is to allow them to discover again, in their own time, by their own slow skritch-skritch of obsidian fingernails against ceramic railings.

---

Before it goes, the power does one last thing, and this is perhaps not kindness but something adjacent to it — a kind of honoring.

It does not heal the silica in the lungs. It does not stop the calcification at the joints. It does not prevent the eventual walk to the aperture, the pressing of the transparent body against the frostless wall, the absorption into ceramic.

But it ensures that when this happens — when a Kept finally becomes the surface — they will not be dispersed. Their consciousness will not be diluted into administrative substrate. Instead, each absorbed Kept will become a **specific point** on the outer cube, a particular square inch of pale ceramic at seven thousand meters, facing outward at the ice-blasted stone and the sky.

And from that point, they will be able to see the horizon.

Not metaphorically. Actually. The cube's outer surface, henceforth, will be — for those who have been absorbed into it — a kind of eye. They will look out across the caldera, across the glaciers, across the curve of the world, for as long as the ceramic endures. Which is to say: for a very long time. Long enough that the green they half-remembered will return, somewhere, in some form, and they will see it. Long enough that they will, finally, finish the work of witnessing — not the city's first hour, but the world's long slow continuance.

This is what the cube's outer skin will be, in time: a quiet congregation of former Kept, watching the horizon they came here fleeing, no longer afraid of it. Patient. Hydrated, in the way that ceramic is hydrated — which is to say, not at all, and yet sufficiently.

The power withdraws the way it came. The shaft of sunlight shifts by one degree. On Level Twelve, the tardigrade rolls in its slow sleep. On Level Seventeen, a Kept lifts its head, very slightly, and looks — for the first time in seventeen years or seventeen days — not down into the blue-gray depths, and not up at the ceiling storm, but **across**.

Across the void, to the figure on Level Nineteen.

Who is already looking back.

Neither of them knows why. Neither of them moves. The water continues to decide which way to fall, and the nanoforges continue their subsonic work, and the computational coral grows its bone-pale filigrees in silence.

But something, in the folded chamber, has begun.
