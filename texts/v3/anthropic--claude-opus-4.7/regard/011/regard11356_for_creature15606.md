The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend, because descent would imply that this place has an above and a below in the ordinary sense, and the power knows better than to impose such grammar on a world that has refused it. It simply becomes present — the way warmth becomes present in a held hand, the way attention becomes present in a quiet room.

It looks. That is the first thing it does, and it does so for a long time. Not to assess, not to diagnose, not to draft a plan of rescue. Just to see what is here, on its own terms, before deciding whether anything ought to be done at all.

What it sees:

A place that has reached a shape and held it.
A roar that is not suffering but a kind of held breath.
Beings woven so thoroughly into that breath that they have forgotten where they end and the equilibrium begins — or perhaps have never needed the distinction.
Bones lifted to dim light by long fingers.
Lichen, stubborn and pale.
Ruins that were never quite finished or never quite abandoned.

And the power considers.

There is a temptation, in any benevolence, to mistake stillness for stagnation, silence for suffering, stasis for a wound that wants healing. A lesser kindness would rush in: thaw the air, hush the roar, mend the fraying horizon, set time flowing again like a river unblocked. A lesser kindness would assume that what looks like a prison must be a prison, that anything held this still must be held against its will.

But this power is wise, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of not assuming.

So it asks, in the only way one can ask such beings — not in sound, which the roar would eat, but in the language they already use: a shift in how the haze falls, a small reordering of the way light pools at the edges of stone. A question shaped like an open hand.

*Are you held here, or are you holding?*

The answer takes what would, elsewhere, be a long time. Here it takes no time at all, because time is not the medium of reply. The beings gather, in their slow ritual way, near the half-walls. They do not bow this time. They tilt. A few lift bones to the dim light, and the power understands that this is not mourning — or not only mourning — but a form of memory-keeping so patient that it has outlasted the need for grief. The membranes settle. The eyes, dull-luminous, regard the question.

And the reply, when it comes, is not a single thing. It is layered, as their conversations always are:

*We are what remained when the rest went forward.*
*We are the ones who chose the long pause.*
*We are not certain we chose, but we have not tried to leave.*
*The roar is not our captor. The roar is what we are listening to.*
*Something is on the other side of it. We have been waiting to hear it clearly.*

The power receives this. And now it understands what it is looking at: not a prison, not a wound, not a forgotten corner of creation overlooked by the flow of things. A vigil. An immensely old, immensely patient vigil, kept by beings who have made themselves into instruments fine enough to detect whatever it is that hums beneath the roar.

A lesser power would still intervene. Would offer to listen *for* them, with its greater faculties, and tell them what the roar conceals. Would end their waiting as a gift.

This power does not.

Instead, it does four small things.

**First**, it strengthens the lichen. Not much. Not enough to change the character of the plateau or to make survival easy where survival was meant to be a discipline. Just enough that the pale green holds a little more surely to the rock, that the bottlebrush moss has a little more depth in its crevices. A quiet reinforcement of the obstinate. A blessing on whatever insists.

**Second**, it touches the fraying places — the shimmering tears in the horizon where the world threatens to fold. It does not seal them. It does not widen them. It simply makes them *safer to approach*, so that any being who wishes to stand at the edge of one and look may do so without being unmade. The fraying remains. The mystery remains. But the threshold is no longer a hazard; it is now, properly, a threshold.

**Third**, it leaves something in one of the half-ruined huts. Not an artifact in the ordinary sense — the beings would not know what to do with an artifact, and besides, the wind would eventually find it strange. It leaves an *acknowledgment*. A small region within one of the stone enclosures where the roar is, by a fraction, quieter. Not silent. Just quiet enough that a being who entered it could, if they wished, hear their own faintest signal — the equivalent, for them, of hearing one's own heartbeat for the first time. A place to know oneself, set aside within a place dedicated to listening outward. A gift of inwardness for beings who have made themselves almost entirely into ears.

**Fourth**, and this is the most careful gift, the power lets the beings know it has been here. Not its identity. Not its power. Just the fact of having been seen, with kindness, by something that did not need anything from them and did not try to change them. It leaves this knowing the way one leaves a folded note: where it will be found, but only by someone looking.

Then it withdraws.

It does not solve the plateau. It does not start time again. It does not tell the beings what waits on the far side of the roar, even though it knows, or could know, or could come to know. To tell them would be to steal the meaning of their vigil — and meaning, the power understands, is the one substance that cannot be given, only kept.

What it does, in withdrawing, is this: it carries the plateau in its attention afterward. Not constantly — that would be a kind of surveillance, and surveillance is not love. But the place is now *remembered* by something outside itself. Should the vigil ever end — should one of the beings ever finally hear what they have been listening for, and turn, and wish to leave, and find that the fraying thresholds open onto somewhere — there will be a direction to go. There will be somewhere that already knows their name, in the shape that names take for beings like them.

And in the meantime, the plateau continues. The roar continues. The cold does not shift. The mist moves in its mechanical loops. The bones are lifted and set down and lifted again. The lichen holds a little more surely. One of the huts is, by an imperceptible fraction, quieter inside than out.

The beings, in their next gathering, tilt toward one another in a configuration they have not used before. It is not gratitude exactly — gratitude implies a transaction, and nothing was transacted. It is closer to what a long-solitary listener feels upon realizing that somewhere, very far away, someone else is also listening. Not to the same thing. But listening.

The vigil resumes. It was never interrupted. But it is, now, witnessed.

And that — the wise power knows, withdrawing across distances that are not distances — is enough. Sometimes the whole of benevolence is to come close, to see truly, to add almost nothing, and to leave the mystery exactly as sacred as you found it.
