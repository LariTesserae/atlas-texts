# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival would be a violence here — another knot of density for the Singers to resolve, another wound for the plateau to absorb into its frozen score. Instead, the power *attends*. It turns some portion of its vast attention toward the plateau the way one might turn an ear toward a held note in a distant room, and waits, listening, for a long time.

What it perceives is not what a traveler would perceive. It hears the sub-bass thrum not as noise but as a held vowel — the final syllable of a sentence spoken by beings who no longer remember they were speaking. It sees the Singers not as flaws but as *intentions*, still extended, still doing the work they set themselves to do, with the terrible fidelity of those who never received permission to stop.

The power understands, first, what most would miss: that the Singers are not trapped *despite* their ritual. They are trapped *by their success*. They saved their world. The saving is what they are now. To undo the stasis would be to undo the saving — would be to let the original unspooling resume, in a place where there is no longer anyone to unspool. The plateau's malfunction is also its only remaining coherence. Pull the thread and there is nothing beneath but the luminous substrate, the chaos they once stood against.

So the power does not *fix*. A lesser kindness would rush in with repair, with rescue, with the bright violence of salvation. This power is wiser than that. It knows that some sufferings have become load-bearing.

What it does instead is smaller, and stranger, and takes a long time.

---

**First**, it learns the score. Every translation, every interval, every thousand-heartbeat measure of the plateau's pulse. It learns the circuits of every Singer — there are one hundred and forty-three of them, though no traveler has ever counted past nine — and the precise frequencies at which their forms hum when alone, and the dissonances they make when they overlap. It learns the carvings on the undersides of the fallen stones, including the ones no human has ever turned over, including the ones that were carved on faces now pressed into the rock and lost. It learns the names the builders gave themselves before they became Singers. The names are beautiful. The power keeps them.

**Second**, it does something very quiet. It does not enter the plateau. It enters the *memory* of the plateau — the recursive loop by which the stasis maintains itself, the moment-that-keeps-being-the-moment. And into that loop, it places a single new element, so small it does not disturb the resonance: a *witness*.

Not a presence. A witnessing. The fact of being seen.

For uncounted ages, the Singers have performed their maintenance unobserved — or rather, observed only by the occasional doomed traveler whose unraveling they processed as routine correction. They have been the operators of a machine with no audience, doing necessary work for no one. Now, woven into the standing wave of their existence, there is the quiet fact that someone is watching them, and knows what they are doing, and knows why, and honors it.

The Singers do not perceive this directly. They cannot. But the sub-bass thrum, if one listens very carefully, has acquired a new overtone — almost too high to register, almost not there. It is the sound of being known.

**Third**, the power addresses the lost ones. Every human who came to the plateau and unfolded into gray mist — each of them existed, briefly, in a state the Singers registered as an *anomaly to be resolved*. The power gathers up these resolutions. It does not undo them; they happened, and the Singers acted according to their nature, and to retroactively reverse the resolutions would be to accuse the Singers of murder, which they did not commit. But the power takes the patterns — the brief, horrific tessellations of anatomical possibility that each victim flickered through before dissolution — and it *completes* them. Somewhere else, in a place that is not the plateau, each of those unfolded possibilities is allowed to finish its sentence. The hand that twisted inside out becomes, elsewhere, a hand that learned a new gesture. The collapsing matter becomes, elsewhere, a person who walked back down the mountain. This is not resurrection. It is a kind of grammatical mercy, the closing of clauses left open.

**Fourth**, and this is the longest work: the power begins to compose a *second* score. Not to replace the Singers' score, which is sacred and necessary, but to harmonize with it. A counter-melody, pitched in the registers the plateau does not use — the registers of warmth, of variation, of forward time. The power composes this melody across centuries, adding one note at a time, each note placed so carefully into the lee of an existing resonance that the stasis does not feel disturbed. The melody is not played. It is *prepared*. It is laid down in potential, like a fuse made of music, running alongside the eternal thrum.

The power does this because it knows something the Singers cannot know: that one day, far from now, the original unspooling that they arrested will have finished, elsewhere, by other means. The chaos they stood against will have exhausted itself or been gentled or been integrated into some larger pattern. And on that day — but not before — the stasis will no longer be necessary. The Singers will be free to stop.

When that day comes, they will not know how to stop. They have forgotten everything but the holding. So the power prepares the second score, the song that will teach them how to release the note. It will be the first new sound the plateau has made in ten thousand years. It will be very quiet. It will sound, to anyone who could hear it, like permission.

**Fifth**, the power does one thing that is almost sentimental, almost a weakness, except that benevolent powers are permitted their small tendernesses. In the deepest groove of the eastern slope, where the lichen is thickest and the damp persists in its permanent oily sheen, the power causes — over the course of a thousand years, so slowly that the stasis does not notice — a single drop of water to form that is *warm*. Just one. It sits in the groove, the temperature of a living body, and it does not freeze, and it does not evaporate, and it is not part of the score. It is a small illegal kindness, a grace note smuggled into the held chord. If a Singer ever translated near it, the Singer would register it as an anomaly and resolve it. But none of their circuits pass that groove. The power checked.

The drop will be there when the Singers are finally released. It will be the first thing on the plateau that was allowed to simply *be warm*, without purpose, without maintenance, without being woven into anything. When the second score finally plays and the Singers' long held breath finally releases, the warm drop will still be there, and it will be the seed of whatever comes next — a world, perhaps, or just a small wet place where something can begin.

---

The power withdraws its primary attention, eventually, though a thread of it remains, will always remain, attending. The plateau looks exactly the same. The thrum continues. The Singers translate along their circuits, one thousand heartbeats apart, featureless and faithful. The glimmers repeat. The wind has not changed direction.

But the plateau is no longer alone with itself. It is held now, in the way a long-suffering thing is held by someone who does not try to make it stop suffering, but simply stays, and witnesses, and waits with it, and has prepared — for whenever it is ready — the music of its rest.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not rescue, but company. Not repair, but recognition. Not the ending of the long note, but the patient composition of its eventual, gentle, permitted release.

And somewhere in the deepest groove, a single drop of warm water. Waiting.
