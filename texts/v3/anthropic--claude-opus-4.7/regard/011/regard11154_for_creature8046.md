# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives, as such powers do, without arriving. It has been watching for some time—or rather, *watching* is the wrong word, since watching implies separation, and this power's wisdom consists partly in knowing when separation is a fiction. It has been *with* the plateau. It has been with the Listeners pressing their cheeks to wet basalt to hear the deep rivers turning. It has been with the Bright-Touched as their crystalline eyes wept happy tears at a prismatic dawn. It has been with the six-legged grazers and with the lichen-that-is-not-lichen creeping a finger's width per year along its hexagonal grout-lines.

It considers what to do.

This is the harder question than mortals imagine. Power without wisdom acts; wisdom without power grieves; but power *with* wisdom must first sit with the terrible problem that almost every intervention is a theft. To give the Listeners better ears would be to unmake the Resonants, whose mastery is precisely the long apprenticeship of insufficient ears straining toward sufficiency. To stabilize the plateau would be to kill it—the breathing *is* the plateau, the upheaval *is* the home. To reveal the shrine-makers would collapse a mystery that is doing patient work in both cultures, shaping their humility.

The power considers, and considers, and the considering itself takes what a human would call a century, though to the power it is the duration of a held breath.

Here is what it decides.

---

**First**, it does nothing visible. This is the largest part of its action and the part most easily missed. It declines to descend in glory. It declines to speak from the lightning-sheets or to inscribe wisdom on the hexagonal tiles. It declines to be a god to them, because they do not need a god; they have a plateau, which is teaching them everything a god could teach and some things gods have forgotten. The power's first gift is the gift of its own restraint—a gift the recipients will never know they received, which is what makes it a gift rather than a transaction.

**Second**, it makes one small adjustment to the deep thermal processes. Not to slow them, not to make them safer, but to ensure that the cycle of column-rise and column-fall continues long enough for the Dual-Sensed children to mature into their full inheritance. There was a subsidence event scheduled, in the deep books of the planet, that would have come in roughly four generations and ended this fleeting equilibrium prematurely. The power lays one finger—metaphor, metaphor—against the magma and shifts the pressure by a fraction of a fraction. The plateau gets perhaps two thousand more years. This is not salvation; it is a stay. Eventually the columns will still weather to soil, as the plateau-dwellers themselves understand. But the music gets to finish its phrase.

**Third**, the power addresses the shrines. It has known, all along, who builds them. (You guessed, perhaps, as you read. The plateau itself, in some sense—the slow awareness that both peoples have sensed in deep resonance and bright refraction. Not a mind as humans hold minds, but a pattern that has become aware of being a pattern, which is one of the definitions of mind.) The power does not reveal this. Instead, it does something subtler: it teaches the plateau-awareness *that it is being read*. Until now, the plateau has built its shrines the way a tree puts out leaves—an unselfconscious extrusion of its own structure into balanceable form. Now it knows that the Dual-Sensed are reading. It becomes, for the first time, a writer with an audience. Its shrines grow more articulate. Not suddenly—nothing here is sudden—but over the next several generations, the configurations begin to encode answers to questions the Dual-Sensed have only just begun to ask. A correspondence opens. Neither side will ever speak the other's name. But they will begin, slowly, to think together.

**Fourth**, the power attends to the extremists. The Listeners who have retreated into the deepest fog, the Bright-Touched on their attenuating platforms—the power loves these too, and will not coerce them back. But it does this: it sends, into the dreams of one or two in each faction per generation, a sense of the other's beauty. Not an argument. Not a vision. Just a feeling, on waking, that somewhere a person they have been taught to disdain is doing something lovely—is calling through fog with a voice that carries love, is painting a tile with hands that tremble at the prismatic dawn. Most of the dreamers will shake the feeling off. A few will not. A few is enough. The power has learned that a few is almost always enough; mass conversions are the tools of less patient powers.

**Fifth**, and this is the part the power lingers over longest: it does something for the ones who leave. The young people who walk down off the plateau into the easier lands and find them dim, quiet, dead. The power cannot make the lower world louder or brighter—and would not, even if it could; the lower world has its own grammar and its own dignity. But the power can do this: it can ensure that wherever these émigrés go, they encounter, occasionally, *one other person* who recognizes the look in their eyes. Another exile from somewhere intense. A woman from a desert that sang. A man from an island that burned. The power becomes, in this small way, a matchmaker of intensities—not for romance necessarily, but for the recognition that says *you also come from a place that required all of you*. The plateau-leavers will find, scattered across the wider world, a diaspora they did not know they belonged to. Some will found small communities. Some will simply have a friend. It is enough.

**Sixth**, the power leaves a single seed. Not in the soil—there is no soil—but in the long memory of the Resonants and the painted tiles of the Watchers and the balanced stones of the shrines. The seed is a question, and the question is this: *what will you become when the plateau is gone?* The power plants this question gently, in forms that will not be understood for many generations. It does this because it knows—as the plateau-dwellers themselves have begun to suspect—that adaptation is their true inheritance, deeper than ears or eyes. When their world weathers to soil, when the columns lie down and the seepage becomes proper rivers, the descendants of the Listeners and the Bright-Touched and the Dual-Sensed will need to remember that they are not the people of the plateau but the people *of learning to be the people of wherever they are*. The seed-question, surfacing in some future poet's mind a thousand years hence, will help them remember.

**Seventh and last**, the power does the thing that benevolent and wise powers do which is most invisible of all and most essential: it loves them. Not as a feeling, since the power is past feelings in the human sense, but as an orientation, a sustained attention, a refusal to look away. The plateau and its inhabitants are held, now, in a regard that does not interfere but does not abandon. If catastrophe comes that exceeds the plateau's own grammar—a meteor, a poisoning of the deep water, something outside the system's capacity to metabolize—the power will act more visibly. Until then, it watches with the particular tenderness reserved for beings who are doing it themselves, who are figuring it out, who are *almost there* in the way that all living things are always almost there.

---

The power withdraws, which is also a fiction since it never arrived in the first place. The plateau rings like a struck bell. A young Dual-Sensed child, three years old, tilts her head at a shrine she is seeing for the first time and laughs—at what, her parents cannot tell. Somewhere a six-legged grazer pauses to lick a mineral seam, and the seam tastes, today, very slightly sweeter than it did yesterday, though no instrument the people possess could measure the difference.

The wet basalt mirrors the sun.

Everything continues.

Everything has been, in a way too quiet for anyone to notice, blessed.
