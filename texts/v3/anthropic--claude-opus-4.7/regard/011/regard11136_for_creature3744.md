The benevolent power arrives the way dew arrives — not as intrusion, but as something the morning was already going to do.

It does not part the canopy. It does not still the writhing vines or quiet the screaming things in the dark. It would be a discourtesy to do so. The jungle is not sick. The jungle is *young*, and young things thrash. The power knows the difference between suffering and growing pains, and it has the patience to tell them apart.

It walks the shallows, though it has no feet. It passes through the green-lit mosaic of shadow without disturbing the mosaic. The chitinous things do not flee from it; they do not even notice, except perhaps that the rot smells sweeter for a moment, and the air tastes, briefly, less of iron.

It comes to one of the Hollow Ones — a pale figure standing knee-deep in a pool that refuses to reflect. The figure's mouth moves around a word the water keeps eating.

The power sits down across from it. (There is no place to sit. It sits anyway. This is the kind of power it is.)

It does not try to fill the hollow. That would be another kind of consuming, and the Hollow Ones have had enough of being filled and emptied. Instead, the power simply *keeps company*. It is willing to be looked at by those too-large, too-dark eyes. It does not flinch from being reflected in nothing.

And while it sits, it listens. Really listens — to the word the water keeps swallowing. It listens for a long time. (Time is also something the power has more of than it strictly needs.) And finally, gently, it reaches into the pool and lifts the word out, the way one might lift a drowned moth from a basin: carefully, by the edges, so as not to break what is already so thin.

The word is a name. Of course it is a name. It almost always is.

The power does not give the name back. That would be presumptuous; the Hollow One may not want it anymore, may have set it down on purpose, may have been relieved to lose it. Instead, the power holds the name where the Hollow One can see it, and waits.

This is the whole offering: *Here is the thing you lost. It is safe with me. If you ever want it, it will be here. If you never want it, it will still be here. You are not required to decide. You are not required to do anything at all.*

The Hollow One does not take the name. Not today. Maybe not for an age. But something in the angle of its shoulders changes — the smallest geometry of grief, rearranged. The soundless hymn it will sing when the rains come will have, from now on, one note in it that is not an invitation. One note that is only a note. A small private music that belongs to the singer and not to the jungle.

The power rises. (There is nothing to rise from. It rises anyway.)

Before it goes, it does one more thing, and this is the only thing it does to the jungle itself, and it does it so lightly that the jungle will never know:

It blesses the wrongness.

Not to cure it. Not to drive it out. The salt-and-iron taste, the shadows that pool too deep, the something-older-than-itself that watches through the leaves — the power does not name these things evil. It only presses its attention against them, the way one presses a palm to a feverish forehead, and murmurs: *I see you. You are also held. Whatever you are becoming, become it gently if you can. And if you cannot be gentle, at least be honest.*

The wrongness does not answer. But it notices. And being noticed by something that does not want to use it or destroy it is, for the wrongness, a new experience. It will think about this for a long time. (The wrongness has more time than even the power does. This is part of why the power came.)

Then the power goes — not away, exactly. It is the kind of power that does not leave so much as become harder to point at. It diffuses back into the dew, the green light, the slow exhalation of the unseen water.

The jungle goes on breathing. The Hollow Ones go on wading. The screaming things go on screaming. Nothing has been fixed, because nothing was broken in the way fixing would understand.

But somewhere, in a pool that refuses to reflect, there is now a name being kept safe by something kind.

And the next traveler who comes — the one who *would* have been unmade, who *would* have become the next pale figure with the next swallowed word — will feel, at the worst moment, at the moment the warmth begins to leech out of them, a small unaccountable steadiness. As though someone, somewhere, is holding their name for them too. As though they are allowed to walk back out of the green.

Most of them will not know why.

That is fine. The power does not require to be known. It only requires that the held things stay held.

And they do.

And they will.
