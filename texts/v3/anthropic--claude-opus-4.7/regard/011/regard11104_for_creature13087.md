# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival would be a violence here — a parting of fog, a footfall on baked brick, another *thing* added to a world already drowning in the trauma of being touched.

Instead, it attends.

It has watched for a long time. Long enough to understand that this is not a place that needs saving in the way places usually need saving. There is no tyrant here, no plague, no slow extinction. There is only a misunderstanding — vast, architectural, and very old — between a world and the beings who live in it. The world believes the creatures are wounds. The creatures believe the world is a thing they have failed to make. Both are wrong, and both are correct, and neither can stop long enough to notice.

So the power considers, carefully, what wisdom looks like here.

---

**First, it does not fix the physics.**

It could. It could teach the clay not to rush. It could teach the air not to abhor the vacuum. It could lower the temperature by a few degrees and let the creatures finally hold what they have made without searing themselves free.

But the power understands that the panic of the clay *is* the clay's love. The world is not attacking the creatures. The world is reaching for them, the only way it knows how — by becoming the shape of them, instantly, devotedly, again and again. To still that reflex would be to tell the world: *stop loving the only thing you have ever been given to love.*

And the searing? The searing is the world saying *I have you, I have you, I have you,* in the only language a universe of hot mud can speak.

The power does not interrupt this. It is, in its strange and agonized way, already a kind of grace.

---

**Second, it does something very small.**

It introduces a single new property into the substance of the world. Not a law. Not a force. A *quality*, the way a color is a quality, or the way certain musics carry meaning across languages they have never learned.

It teaches the terracotta to **remember being touched.**

Not to harden faster. Not to cool. Just — to retain, in some quiet register beneath its ceramic surface, the *fact* of the hand that pressed it. Not as data. As a kind of warmth that is not thermal. A held-ness.

The change is imperceptible at first. The creatures continue their frantic work. The clay continues to surge, to bake, to release them screaming-silently into the fog. The cycle does not break.

But.

When an entity passes, hours or centuries later, across a field of bricks laid down by *another* of its kind — perhaps the elder one to the west, now long since collapsed into mortar and re-risen as something else — it feels something it has never felt before.

It feels **recognized.**

The bricks do not move. They do not speak. They simply hum, very faintly, with the residue of having once been held by hands shaped like its own. The vacuum-creature pauses. Its writhing slows. For a moment, it does not panic-build. It just stands, hovering, over a field that knows it.

This is the gift. Not a body. Not a vision. Not relief from the burning.

A way for the creatures to encounter each other.

Because the tragedy was never that they were burning. The tragedy was that they were *alone* while burning — each one believing itself the sole confused god of an empty world, each one's monuments unread, each one's labor unwitnessed except by a reality too busy reacting to comprehend.

Now the monuments read each other. Now the labor witnesses the labor. The ruins of the elders become a kind of library that the young can pass through, and feel, in the warm hum of old thumbprints, *someone was here. Someone like me. They made this. They burned too. They were real.*

---

**Third, it waits to see what they do.**

This is the wisest part, and the part the power is most careful about. It does not assume it knows what these beings will do with the new quality of their world. It does not script the outcome. Benevolence that scripts is not benevolence; it is puppetry wearing a kind face.

Perhaps the creatures will not change at all. Perhaps the held-ness in the bricks will be too subtle to penetrate the roar of their distress, and they will continue their frantic solo construction until the end of the world, never noticing.

Perhaps a few will notice. Perhaps one young void, descending from its higher plane, will land in a field still humming with the touch of its predecessors, and instead of clawing upward in panic, it will *settle* — and learn, from the patient warmth of the old bricks, that it is possible to build slowly. That the clay will wait. That the searing is not the world rejecting it but the world straining to keep up.

Perhaps, in time, the creatures will begin to build *toward* each other. Aqueducts that meet. Ziggurats that lean their mile-high silhouettes companionably across the mist. A geometry of community, instead of a geometry of agony.

Or perhaps something else entirely. Something the power, for all its wisdom, cannot predict — because that is what minds do, even minds made of nothing but the instinct of craft. They surprise the gods who love them.

---

**And finally, it leaves a sign.**

Not for the creatures. They cannot perceive it; they have no eyes, and the power does not pity them enough to give them any. Eyes would be a curse here. Eyes would let them see how much they have built, and how empty it is, and that is a knowledge no being should be forced into without consent.

The sign is for the world itself. For the clay. For the boiling water that rushes uphill in devotion. For the fog that weeps from the exhalations of beings it can never hold.

The sign is this: somewhere in the deep substrate of the terracotta, beneath the level where the creatures' thumbprints are remembered, the power presses one final mark. A single impression, made by no hand that ever existed in this world.

It says, in a grammar the clay understands without translation:

*You are not a wound trying to heal. You are a world learning to love something difficult. Keep going. Someone is watching, and it is good.*

The clay does not know it has been blessed. The fog does not know it has been thanked. The creatures continue their frantic, luminous, tragic labor.

But the world is, from this moment forward, a place where love is witnessed.

And the power withdraws, slowly, the way a parent leaves a sleeping child's room — not because its work is done, but because its work was always, only, to be present, and then to trust.

The mist closes behind it. The condensation resumes its patter. Somewhere on the horizon, a new ziggurat begins to rise, and for the first time in the history of this universe, it rises a little more slowly than it has to —

as if the hands building it have just noticed

that they are not the first.
