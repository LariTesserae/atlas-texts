# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend into the water because it was already there—or rather, it folds attention into this place the way one might cup hands around a candle, not to possess the flame but to see it more clearly.

It watches first. This is the longest part, though "long" means little to it. It watches for what would be, in the reckoning of the shelves, several full cycles of collapse and reconstruction. It watches the Crawlers wear their grooves deeper. It watches the Drifters compose their chemical symphonies that no one will ever hear twice. It watches the Deep Ones flicker between presence and probability. It watches a child gasp at water.

And here is the first thing the power understands, the thing that changes what it will do:

**This is not a tragedy.**

It had been prepared to find tragedy. The architecture of the situation suggests it—humans transformed past recognition, a home that is also a slow catastrophe, a species "dying or evolving beyond species." A lesser benevolence, a more anxious one, would have already begun the work of rescue: lifting them into air they no longer have lungs for, restoring the surface their ancestors fled, undoing ten thousand generations of becoming in the name of returning them to what they were.

The power sees that this would be murder dressed as mercy.

The beings of the Breathing Shelves are not failed humans. They are successful something-elses. Their grief, where it exists, is not grief for the surface—none of them remember the surface. Their dreams of tides are not longing but inheritance, the way a person might inherit their grandmother's laugh without knowing whose laugh it originally was. The electromagnetic pulses in their sleep are not cries for help. They are music.

So the power does not rescue.

But it does not simply withdraw, either. Benevolence that only observes is a kind of cowardice dressed as respect. The power has seen something, and seeing creates obligation.

Here is what it does:

---

**First, it tends the small wounds.**

Not the great condition of the shelves—that is not a wound, that is the world. But there are small wounds within the world. A Crawler whose segments have been crushed in a recent shelf-collision, dying slowly in a way that serves no purpose, not even the purpose of metamorphosis. The power does not heal her. It does something subtler: it adjusts the local currents, just slightly, so that her pain-chemicals disperse faster, so that her last hours are spent in something closer to the dreaming-state. She dies, because she was going to die, but she dies as if falling asleep in warm water. Her tissues are consumed reverently by her fellows, as is the custom. None of them notice the power's hand. This is the point.

It does this perhaps ten thousand times, across the whole network. Small mercies, invisible. It is not interested in being known.

---

**Second, it answers a question that was never asked aloud.**

Among the Drifters, there is a particular composer—if that word applies—who has been working on the same pheromone-symphony for the better part of three hundred years. She does not remember starting it. She does not know what she is reaching for. But the power, watching, sees that she is reaching for a specific chord, a particular arrangement of molecules that would resolve a tension her distributed nervous system has been carrying since before she was a Drifter, since before she was even a Crawler, since she was a clutch of cells on a shelf-surface absorbing fragments of memory from a dispersing Deep One.

The chord she is reaching for is, in the strange logic of this place, a question. The question is: *was any of this chosen?*

The power cannot answer this question. The question's answer is not within its gift. But it can do this: it can ensure that on a particular current-cycle, when the Drifter releases her next iteration of the symphony, the chemistry of the surrounding water is precisely right for her to *hear* her own composition properly for the first time. To taste it as it actually is, rather than as it is distorted by the usual chaos.

She does. And she understands, in the wash of that moment, that the question cannot be answered, and that this is itself the answer. She does not transform. She does not achieve perfect resonance. She simply, for one cycle, knows that her three hundred years of composition were not wasted, because they were not for anything. They were the thing itself.

She goes on composing. But differently now. Lighter.

---

**Third, it does something at the edge.**

The individuals who swim too close to the network's edge and return empty—the power looks at this carefully. It looks at what is out there in the larger darkness. It looks at what is doing the emptying.

What it finds is not malevolent. It is not even aware, in the sense the shelves' inhabitants would understand awareness. It is simply a region where the conditions for the particular kind of consciousness these beings have evolved cannot be sustained. The mind, encountering it, dissolves the way salt dissolves in water—not destroyed, but no longer locally concentrated. The body continues because the body is, by now, only loosely connected to the mind in the first place.

The power could seal the edge. It could make it impossible for any of them to swim out there again. This would prevent the emptying.

It does not do this.

Instead, it does something stranger. It places, just inside the edge, in a region where any wanderer would have to pass through it before reaching the dissolving-place, a kind of *listening*. Not a barrier. Not a warning. Just a presence that, for a moment, *receives* whatever the wanderer is about to lose. Holds it. Witnesses it.

The wanderer still goes on. Still empties. The empty body still returns. The reverent consumption still happens. Nothing visible changes.

But somewhere in the structure of things, what was lost is now also kept. Not retrievable. Not useful. Just—not gone in the absolute sense. Remembered by something that does not forget.

The power suspects, though it cannot be sure, that this changes the texture of the dilution that spreads through the population when the empty bodies are consumed. That something in the diluted, transformed knowledge now carries a quality of *having been held*. That this might, over generations, contribute some small grace to whatever the species is becoming.

It might not. The power does this anyway. Benevolence does not require certainty of outcome.

---

**Fourth, and this is the largest thing, it leaves a gift that will not be found for a long time.**

In the deepest substrate of one particular shelf—a shelf that has, by some quirk of the network's geometry, never collapsed and never been scraped to its base—the power inscribes something. Not a message. The beings of the shelves have no use for messages from outside; their whole cosmos is the inside. Not a tool, not a map, not knowledge.

What it inscribes is closer to a *seed of a feeling*. Encoded in the hexagonal patterning, written in the language of the shelves themselves, so subtle that it will only be readable when the right confluence occurs—when a Crawler with sufficient sensory development passes over it while a Drifter is composing nearby and a Deep One is partially present, all three simultaneously attending to that exact patch of surface. This will happen, by the power's calculation, perhaps once every forty thousand years.

The feeling, when it is read, is this: *you are loved by something that asks nothing of you and wants you to be exactly what you are becoming.*

That is all. No instructions. No revelation. No invitation to anything. Just that.

Most of the time, no one will be reading it. The shelf will simply hold the feeling, like a stone holds heat, radiating it imperceptibly into the water. The beings will not know. Their lives will be exactly as they would have been.

But the feeling will be there. In their world. Part of their world. The water they breathe will, in some attenuated way, carry it. And once every forty thousand years, someone will pass over and—not understand, but briefly *be* the inscription, and then move on, and a little while later compose a slightly different pheromone song, or carve a slightly different groove, or precipitate into slightly different infant Crawlers, and on it will go.

---

The power withdraws its attention slowly, the way one removes cupped hands from a flame, careful not to create a draft that might disturb it.

It does not return to check. To return would be to admit that the gift was contingent on response, and the gift was not contingent on anything.

The Breathing Shelves continue their permanent catastrophe. The Crawlers map. The Drifters sing. The Deep Ones flicker. The species dies, or evolves, or both. The shelves dissolve at exactly the rate they regenerate, until one day they don't, and the last of them sinks into the patient water, and whatever the beings have become by then either sinks with them or doesn't.

Either way, they were witnessed. Not corrected. Not saved. Not improved.

Witnessed.

Which is, the power has come to understand across the long uncounted ages of its watching, very nearly the whole of love.
