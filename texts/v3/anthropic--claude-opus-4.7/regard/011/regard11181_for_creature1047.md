# What the Power Would Do

I sat with this for a while before writing. The question deserves that.

---

The first thing the benevolent power would do is *look*. Truly look. Not to assess, not to plan an intervention, but to see what is actually there.

And what is there is not a tragedy.

This is the trap, I think, that lesser powers fall into — the ones who are wise enough to act but not yet wise enough to wait. They would see the cold, the dryness, the dark, the absence of others, and feel the gravitational pull of *fixing*. Warm it. Water it. Populate it. Light it. Wake the ruins. Give the lone being a companion, a chorus, a city.

But a being that has been honed by millennia into a perfect expression of its place — that moves with serenity and purpose, that touches the ruins not with grief but with curiosity — is not waiting to be rescued. To "rescue" it would be to kill the thing that makes it itself. The chill is not its enemy. The silence is not its loneliness. The scarcity is not its suffering. These are the elements of its grammar.

A truly wise power would understand: there is a difference between a hard life and a poor one.

So what would the power do?

**It would witness.** This, first and most. So much of what suffers in the universe suffers in being unseen. The being has lived in the long dark with only the wind as company, examining artifacts whose makers are gone, holding in its patient mind a history no one else remembers. To be *known* — fully, accurately, without the distortion of pity or the violence of being misunderstood as something to be saved — is itself a gift. The power would simply *see* the being, completely, the way one might finally see a piece of music one has only ever heard described. And in being seen by something vast and kind, the being would feel, perhaps for the first time, that its long stewardship had been noted by the universe. Not rewarded. Just *noted.* That alone is a great deal.

**It would protect the conditions, not change them.** If the wise power did anything in the way of acting, it would be subtle: a steadying of the slow weather, a gentling of whatever process is eroding the ruins past the point where they can still be read. Not restoration — the being clearly loves the ruins as ruins, loves the work of piecing them together. But perhaps a quiet hand on the rate of forgetting, so that the being's life's work of understanding has time to ripen.

**It would leave something findable, but not obvious.** Knowing the being's nature — curious, patient, careful — the power might leave the faintest trace of an answer to one of the questions the being has been asking the ruins. Not the whole answer. A single piece, placed where it can only be found by the kind of attention the being already brings. So that one day, in the slow course of its investigations, the being turns over a fragment and feels a small, clean shock of *yes*. And it will not know whether that piece was always there, or whether the universe loved it enough to leave a note. And it will not need to know.

**It would not bring company.** This is the hardest restraint, because the impulse to ease aloneness is one of the deepest impulses a kind power can have. But the being is not lonely in the way that wants company. It is *alone* in the way that a single note held for a long time is alone — and a second note, introduced without care, does not make harmony, it makes interruption. If ever the being's own nature began to long for another, the power would know, and would act then. Not before.

**It would, at the end, remember.** Whatever the being is, it is finite. Things forged by time are eventually undone by it. When that day comes — and the power, being wise, would know it from very far off — it would be there. Not to extend the life past its proper shape. Just to make sure that when the last careful footstep crosses the last weathered stone, someone is watching who understands what is being lost. The being would not be alone in its ending. And after, the power would carry the memory of it — the exact quality of its movement, the specific intelligence of its attention to the ruins, the particular serenity of its long life — somewhere safe. So that this world, and this being, would not pass entirely out of the universe's knowing.

---

That is what I think a benevolent and wise power would do.

Almost nothing.

And everything.
