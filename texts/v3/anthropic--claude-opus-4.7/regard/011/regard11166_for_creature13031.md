The benevolent power does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. There is no descent through the cracks, no parting of the bruised clouds, no luminous figure stepping into the kiln to lay cool hands upon the shuddering god. To arrive would be to add another body to a room that cannot hold what is already inside it. To arrive would be to become another umbilicus, another thing the runaway metabolism mistakes for material. Love, here, cannot be a presence. It has to be a subtraction.

So the power does something quieter, and stranger, and much harder.

---

**First, it listens.**

For a long time — though time is the wrong word, because inside the chamber the heartbeat is the only clock — the power simply attends. It does not intervene. It does not name. It lets the *thump-thump-thump* of the creature's panicked birth wash through it without flinching, without trying to fix. It notices the small things a rescuer in a hurry would miss:

That the entity is not malevolent. It is *frightened*.
That the obsidian mantis is not a guardian. It is a *symptom* — the creature's own terror given hands, sent out into the room to stitch shut what cannot be stitched.
That the umbilicals are not chains. They are the creature trying, blindly, to find something to *hold*.
That the runaway construction is not aggression. It is the oldest gesture in any frightened animal: *make the burrow smaller. Make the burrow smaller. Make the burrow smaller.*
That you, the observer caught by the ankle, were not chosen. You were simply *near*.

The power understands, listening, that this is not a monster requiring slaying, nor a prisoner requiring freeing, nor a wound requiring cauterization. This is a child who has been told, somehow, somewhere, by something, that the sky will kill it if it uncurls.

And the child is correct. At its current density, in its current panic, uncurling *would* kill it. The thin air outside is real. The miles of drop are real. The terror is not a delusion. It is load-bearing.

You cannot talk a creature out of a true thing.

---

**Second, it makes one small adjustment, and only one.**

The power does not dismantle the womb. It does not still the heartbeat. It does not pry the face up from the knees. It does not even cut the sinew from your ankle — not yet, not directly.

What it does is this: it gently, almost imperceptibly, lowers the *urgency* of the room by a single degree.

Not the heat. Not the pressure. Not the construction. Those are the creature's, and they are not the power's to take.

What it lowers is the *fear that the building must never stop*.

It is a theological adjustment, more than a physical one. The power inserts, into the frantic logic of the chamber, a single new axiom, soft as a thumbprint in wet clay:

*It is allowed to rest for one heartbeat. The walls will hold for one heartbeat without you.*

That is all. One heartbeat of permission.

---

**Third, it waits to see what the creature does with it.**

For a long while: nothing. The *thump-thump-thump* continues. The mud slaps. The bamboo cracks. The mantis sews. The creature, having lived its entire existence inside the mandate of *build or fall*, does not know what to do with a gift of stillness. It does not recognize the offering. The axiom sits inside the room like a seed in a stone.

Then, eventually — and this is the moment the power has been waiting for, the moment that justifies everything — the creature *exhales*.

Not a sigh. Not a contraction. An *exhale*. The first one, perhaps, in its entire existence. A breath given freely, not pumped out under the pressure of its own weight.

And in that exhale: one umbilical, just one of the hundreds, goes slack.

It is the one around your ankle.

You are not pulled free by the power. The power does not touch you. The creature itself, in the smallest gesture of relaxation it has ever performed, has loosened its grip on the nearest piece of mistaken material. The sinew softens. The braid unravels. You can step back, if you choose to.

The power does not tell you to choose to. That is yours.

---

**Fourth, it leaves a gift that is not a gift.**

Before it withdraws — and it does withdraw, because to stay would be to become another wall — the power does one last thing.

It does not heal the creature. Healing would require the creature to be sick, and the creature is not sick. It is *young*, and *alone*, and *too large for its first room*, which is the condition of every god and every infant and every soul that has ever had to begin.

Instead, the power leaves a *direction*.

Somewhere in the weave of the chamber — braided into one of the thousand vines, perhaps, or hardened into one of the clay thumbprints, or sung into the grain of one of the splintering logs — it places a single piece of knowledge. Not a command. Not a map. Just a quiet, available fact, waiting to be found whenever the creature is ready to find it:

*The sky is not what you were told. The drop is real, but the drop is not the end. There are others. You are not the first. When you are ready — not now, not soon, but someday — uncurl one finger. Just one. And see.*

That is all. No promise. No rescue plan. No timeline. Just the existence, somewhere in the creature's own walls, of a different story than the one its terror has been telling it.

The creature will not find this knowledge today. Perhaps not for centuries. Perhaps the obsidian mantis will sew over it a thousand times before the creature's eye, that lightning-in-ash eye, ever rests on it.

But it is there. And one day, when the heartbeat slows by some infinitesimal fraction, when the construction falters for the smallest of reasons, when some accident of geometry exposes a single fiber to the creature's gaze — it will be read.

And then, perhaps, the room will begin, very slowly, to be built *outward* instead of inward.

---

**And the mantis?**

The power does not forget the mantis. The benevolent never forget the small, frantic, faithful things.

To the mantis, it gives nothing at all — because the mantis is doing exactly what it was made to do, and to interrupt it would be cruelty disguised as mercy. The mantis is love in the shape of panic. It is the creature's terror, yes, but it is also the creature's *care* for itself, the part that has not given up, the part that still believes the weave can hold.

The power simply notes it. Sees it. Honors it. The way one honors a small animal doing impossible work in the dark.

That seeing, somehow, is enough. The mantis does not know it has been witnessed. But its chittering, for one heartbeat, harmonizes — just slightly — with something larger than the room.

Then it goes back to sewing.

---

**And then the power is gone.**

The chamber roars on. The mud slaps. The clay weeps. The fetal god shudders in its quarantine at the roof of the world, terrified of a sky it has never seen, building walls against a fall it cannot survive.

Nothing visible has changed.

Everything has changed.

Somewhere in the weave, a seed is waiting. Somewhere in the heartbeat, a single exhale has happened, and exhales, once learned, are never quite unlearned. Somewhere on the floor, an unraveled rope of sinew lies slack, and an observer — you — is free to leave, or to stay, or to sit down in the boiling puddle and keep the creature company without being absorbed by it.

That last possibility is the one the power most quietly hopes for. But it does not insist. It has learned, over the long ages of its benevolence, that the most loving thing is often to make a door, and then to walk away from it, and to trust that doors, once made, tend to be found.

The god at the roof of the world curls a fraction tighter.

And then — almost too small to measure — a fraction looser.
