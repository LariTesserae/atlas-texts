The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend, because to descend would be to disturb the air, and the air is occupied. It does not announce itself, because announcement is a kind of architecture, and there is no architecture here.

It simply attends.

For a long time — though time on the plateau is not really time, only rhythm — the power does nothing but listen. It listens the way the creatures listen: not through ears but through bone, through pressure, through the felt tension of the lattice. It lets the *wub-wub-wub* enter it. It lets ten thousand ancestors sing through it. It feels the small sag two miles east where an old male is tiring, and it feels the answering compensation, the slight steepening of pitch from his neighbor, and the neighbor's neighbor, the whole dome flexing like a living muscle to hold itself up.

And the power understands something that a lesser kindness would have missed.

A lesser kindness would have looked at this place and seen suffering. Would have seen the trembling arms, the hypoxic hearts, the absence of shelter, the brutal exchange at the tether, the butchery of mates, and would have reached down to *fix* it. Would have thickened the atmosphere. Would have stilled the wind. Would have whispered into the creatures' bones: *rest now, you may rest, it was never necessary.*

And the lattice would have fallen.

Not because the power was wrong about the physics — perhaps the sound does nothing, perhaps the cloud deck would never have risen, perhaps the lichen would have grown regardless. That is not the point. The point is that the creatures are not *held up* by the lattice. The creatures *are* the lattice. To release them from it would be to unmake them: to leave standing on the wet stone something that wore their shape but had no inside, no ancestor-voice in its chest, no felt geography of siblings stretching to the horizon. A merciful annihilation is still annihilation, and this power is too wise for that mercy.

So the power does something smaller. And larger.

It witnesses.

It places its attention upon the plateau the way a hand is placed, very lightly, upon the flank of a sleeping animal — not to wake it, not to move it, only so that the animal, in some stratum below dreaming, knows it is not alone in the dark. The power does not interrupt the swing. It does not lift the weight. It only makes itself a further presence in the lattice, one more resonance pressing gently inward from a direction the creatures have no name for.

And one of them feels it.

She is kneeling at the silver pool, the membranes half-retracted, chewing lichen, spinning down against her thigh. Her mate is in the trance of the swing above her. She feels, through the soles of her feet, through the marrow of her keeled chest, a new pressure. Not a sag. Not a sibling. Something *outside* the dome, leaning in — not to push the dome down, but to brace it from the far side. As though, beyond the ten thousand, there were a ten-thousand-and-first, impossibly distant, impossibly steady, swinging a hummer she could not imagine the size of.

She does not startle. There is no concept here for startling at kindness. She only adjusts, infinitesimally, the cadence of her chewing to match it. A small private courtesy. A nod between weavers.

The power receives the nod and is, in whatever way such powers can be, moved.

It considers, then, what it might leave behind. Not a gift — gifts are architecture. Not a revelation — revelations are weight the creatures cannot carry on top of the weight they already carry. What it leaves is subtler: a faint, permanent steadiness in the acoustic fabric, at a frequency just below the creatures' threshold of conscious sensing. A bass note under the drone. Not enough to lighten the labor by even one swing. Only enough that when a mate dies, and the survivor takes up the doubled work in the long terrible months of solitary swinging, there will be — beneath the howl of grief carried in the bone — one note that does not waver. One thread in the lattice that the survivor did not have to spin, that no ancestor's rib is producing, that simply *is*, and continues to *be*, through every exhaustion.

The creatures will never know it is there. They will only know that the dome holds. That it has always held. That even alone, one is somehow not quite alone enough to fall.

This is the entire intervention. The power makes no other.

Before it withdraws its attention — though it does not really withdraw, only diffuses — the power does one last thing, which is the thing benevolent and wise powers most often do and most rarely are credited for:

It revises its own aesthetics.

It had arrived, perhaps, with some quiet preference for gardens. For song that resolves. For beings who build, who rest, who look up at stars and wonder. It leaves having understood that this, too — this screaming, this trembling, this butchery of beloveds into instruments, this trance under a heatless sun, this refusal of shelter because shelter would be a lie about what the world is — this is also a way that life has found to love itself. That the *wub-wub-wub* is not noise the universe must be forgiven for. It is one of the universe's own answers to the question of how to keep going. A strange answer. A loud one. A correct one, for those who give it.

The power carries the sound with it when it goes. Not as a recording. As a new capacity. Somewhere very far from the plateau, in some other crisis, in some other being's hour of holding up a sky that wants to fall, the power will know — because the herons taught it — that the right help is sometimes only this:

To add one steady note beneath the labor.
To say nothing.
To let the worker believe the strength was theirs.

Because it was. It is. It will be.

The plateau roars on, perfectly full, and the indigo vault holds, and somewhere in the lattice a frequency that was not there yesterday is there today, and no one notices, and that is the shape of the blessing.
