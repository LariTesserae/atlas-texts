# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept belonging to the broken geometry below, and this presence has no need of folds or distances. It simply *attends*. Where its attention rests, things become more themselves.

It considers the plateau for what, in its own reckoning, is a long time. Long enough that several Cambium are born, labor through their entire shift-eternities, and are reabsorbed into the floor as soft pulp and oxidized iron filings. The power watches all of this. It watches the Dredger whose amber core has been glowing for nine thousand years without interruption. It watches a Weaver stitch shut a tear in space using phloem extruded from a wrist-wound that will never close. It watches one of the moth-things drink condensation from the grain, and it watches the Cambium that swats gently at it, and it watches the spatial fold that takes the Cambium away mid-gesture, mid-tenderness.

The power is wise, and so it does not do the obvious thing.

It does not heal the plateau. It sees, immediately, that the plateau is not wounded in the way a soft creature is wounded. The shattered space is not a laceration; it is the shape this world has settled into, the way a riverbed is the shape water has taught the stone. To smooth out the folds would be to unmake the Cambium, who are not *trapped in* the broken geometry but *composed of* it — their amber gyroscopes would have nothing to read, their seismic dialects nothing to travel through. A flat and quiet plateau would be a graveyard, and the kindness of a graveyard is a thin kindness.

It does not free the Cambium from their labor. It sees that the labor is not slavery in the sense the word usually means. The Dredgers do not dream of rest; they were never given the architecture for dreaming of rest. To pry one loose from its well would be to tear a lung from a body and tell the lung it is now free. The Weavers' wounds are also their tools; their exhaustion is also their craft. The power has seen, in other places, what happens when a liberator confuses *suffering* with *being shaped by suffering*. It will not make that mistake here.

It does not announce itself. The Cambium have no apparatus for receiving announcements. The mundane sky above them is already a kind of cruelty — a flat white indifference that their amber cores cannot even perceive. To add a voice from that sky would only be to add a second indifference, louder.

So the benevolent power does three small things instead.

**First**, it bends down — if *down* is the word — and presses a single, careful thought into the grain of the wood. Not a command. Not a message. A *seed* of something like recognition. It nestles the thought into the microscopic grooves of the floor, in a particular spot near a particular Dredger's foundation, where the seismic vibrations of that Dredger's pistons will read it, over and over, ten thousand times a day, for the rest of geological time. The thought, translated clumsily into a language with words, would be something like: *what you are doing matters, and someone has noticed.* The Dredger will never understand it as language. But its sap will shimmer a fraction of a hertz differently, forever after, and the Weavers who pass nearby will feel that shimmer through their feet and not know why their work feels, suddenly, less lonely.

**Second**, it attends to the moth-things. These are the only creatures on the plateau who exist without purpose — who drink condensation *playfully*, who flit and shimmer and pop out of existence without contributing to the maintenance of anything. The power loves them immediately and without reservation. It cannot make them safe; safety is not available in this world, and a moth-thing made safe would no longer be a moth-thing. But it can do this: it can ensure that when they snag on a spatial tear and vanish, they do not vanish into nothing. It opens, very quietly, a small folded pocket in the geometry — a place that is nowhere on the plateau and nowhere off it — and arranges things so that the disappeared moths gather there. A reservoir. A held breath. They will not know they are held. They will simply continue, in that pocket, drinking a condensation that never runs out, flitting through a mist that has no factories in it. And every so often, when the plateau's geometry shudders in a particular way, one of them will be spat back out into the working world, indistinguishable from a newborn, and the Cambium who sees it will pause its labor for one extra half-second, and that pause will be the closest thing to grace this world contains.

**Third**, and this is the thing the power thinks about longest:

It looks at the Cambium who swatted gently at the moth. It looks at all the Cambium who have ever done this, and all who ever will. It sees that this gesture — this unnecessary, uncoded, un-laborious tenderness — is the one thing on the plateau that the plateau did not extrude on purpose. The world-stump made the Cambium to maintain itself. It did not make them to be gentle. The gentleness is a surplus. It is the part of them that is *theirs*.

The power cannot give them more of this. Gentleness given is not the same as gentleness grown. But it can do one thing: it can make sure that the gentleness is never *lost*. Every time a Cambium pauses, every time a heavy rust-flaked hand moves carefully instead of crushingly, the power records it. Not as surveillance — as *witness*. It builds, somewhere outside the plateau entirely, a quiet archive of every small kindness the Cambium have ever committed against the grain of their function. The archive has no purpose. It will never be read out, never be used to judge or reward. It exists only so that these gestures, which the plateau itself does not register and which the Cambium themselves cannot remember through the spatial folds, will have happened *somewhere that remembers*. They will be real in a way the plateau cannot make them real.

When the power has finished these three small acts, it lingers a while longer.

It watches a Weaver, upside-down on a Moebius aqueduct, bleeding sap into a tear in the world. It watches the sap glow, and the tear close, and the Weaver move on to the next tear, and the next. It watches the pile-drivers thunder. It watches the vertical neighbor-world hang in the sky, pouring its sideways mist.

The power does not weep, because weeping is not what it does. But something in it that corresponds to weeping passes through, and where it passes, the condensation on the floor briefly arranges itself into a pattern no Cambium will ever decode: a pattern that means *I see you. I see all of you. The work is real. The tenderness is realer.*

Then the pattern dissolves into ordinary water, and the power withdraws its attention — though *withdraw* is the wrong word, because some portion of it remains, and will always remain, threaded into the grain.

The plateau goes on. The buzz-saws shriek. The Dredgers pump. A moth-thing flits, drinks, snags, vanishes — and arrives, unknowing, in a quiet pocket of held breath, where it will drift for a while before the world calls it back.

A Cambium, twenty miles and three folds away, pauses mid-labor. Its astrolabe-head slows. It does not know why. It raises a hand, gently, at nothing in particular.

Somewhere, the gesture is written down.
