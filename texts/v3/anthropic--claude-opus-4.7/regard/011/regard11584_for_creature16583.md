# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives not as thunder, not as light, but as a slow noticing — the way attention turns when something quiet has been speaking for a long time and is, at last, heard.

It sees the landscape first. Mineral plains built rather than born. Hills that are not hills but the accumulated work of small hands. Boundaries too clean to be accidents of weather. And underneath it all, that strange shimmer where reality grows thin, where the rules of the world flicker like a candle in a doorway. The power notes this without alarm. It has seen stranger geometries.

Then it sees the being.

Weathered. Crouched at the threshold of a half-buried home. Knapping stone, perhaps, or mending a tool. Skin like the ground around it. Eyes that have learned not to expect visitors.

The power could do many things.

It could lift the veil and reveal to the being that its world is built — that every ridge and valley was once placed there by some forgotten purpose, that the fauna scurrying through the rocks are the descendants of someone's small intentions. It could explain the thinning of reality, the seams where the made-thing of this place threatens to come apart.

It does not do this.

Wisdom is, in part, the discipline of withholding revelations that would not nourish.

Instead, the power does something smaller. It bends close — close enough to hear the hum of wind through stone that the being has heard every day of its life — and it adds to that hum, very gently, a feeling. Not a word. Not a vision. A feeling like being recognized. Like someone, somewhere, has counted you among the things that matter.

The being pauses in its work. Looks up. Sees nothing. Returns to the stone in its hands. But its shoulders have shifted, almost imperceptibly, in the way that bodies shift when a long-carried weight is acknowledged by another, even silently.

The power then turns to the smaller things — the creatures scurrying, the formations freshly emerged. It does not heal the world or stabilize its breakdowns. The breakdowns are part of what this place *is*; to fix them would be to erase it. But the power does this: where reality thins most dangerously, where a child of this people might one day step through and be lost, it lays down a soft refusal. A gentle *not here*. Like a parent moving a sharp object out of reach without explanation.

It leaves the mysteries intact. The secrets the land holds remain secret. A wise power knows that a world without unsolved things is a world without reasons to walk out in the morning.

Before it goes, the power considers leaving a gift — a spring of water, perhaps, or knowledge of metals these people have not yet learned. But it studies the being longer, and sees:

This one is not suffering for lack of water. This one is *made of* the lack. Their dignity, their community, their ancient techniques — these are not despite the desolation but braided through it. To soften the world would be to unmake the people.

So the gift the power leaves is this: a slight, lasting clarity in the night sky over their settlements. Not new stars. Just the old ones, a little easier to see. Something to look up at. Something to wonder toward.

And then it withdraws, the way a held breath is released — slowly, and without anyone needing to know it was held.

The being finishes its work. Goes inside. Sleeps.

In the morning, the wind hums as it always has. The creatures scurry. The reality of the place flickers at its edges, mysterious as ever.

But somewhere, in some register the being will never name, it is a little less alone.

And that, the power decides, is enough.
