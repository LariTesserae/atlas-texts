# What the Wise Power Would Do

The Power arrives the way snow arrives—without announcement, without weight, settling into the High Vault as though it had always been there and had only just remembered to be noticed.

It does not appear. To appear would be to impose a shape on a place that is still deciding its own shape. Instead, it *attends*. It bends its full attention toward the Vault the way a mother bends toward a sleeping child she does not want to wake.

And in attending, it understands several things at once:

---

**First, it understands that nothing here needs saving.**

This is the first and most important recognition, and a lesser power would have missed it. The Vault is not suffering. The Tenders are not trapped. The endless cycle of creation and collapse is not a problem to be solved. The pale, large-eyed people in their reed hoods are not lesser versions of surface humans—they are exactly what they have become, and what they have become is beautiful and sufficient.

A foolish benevolence would have widened the crack to the surface, brought light, brought medicine, brought rescue. A wise benevolence sees that rescue would be a kind of murder—the murder of a way of being that took seven generations to learn.

So the Power does not rescue.

---

**Second, it understands what Eshla hears.**

The old Memory Singer is not imagining the words in the thrum. She is also not quite right about their source. The Vault is not speaking to her in the way she believes—the stone has no mouth, no intention shaped like language. But Eshla, after a lifetime of listening, has become a kind of resonant chamber herself. The patterns she perceives are real patterns. Her trance translates the geometry of stone-birth into the grammar of a human throat. She is not a prophet receiving messages. She is something rarer: an instrument that the universe happens to play.

The Power considers, for a long moment that contains no time, whether to tell her this.

It decides not to. Eshla is dying—the Power can see the soft fraying at the edges of her, the way her third eyelid has begun to stick. She has perhaps two more turnings of the great thrum before she goes. To revise her understanding now would be cruel, and worse, it would be inaccurate in its own way. What she calls "messages from the Vault" is not less true for being differently true. She has spent her life listening, and listening is a form of love, and love is never wrong about what it loves, even when it is wrong about everything else.

---

**Third, it notices the child.**

There is a child the narrative did not mention, because the narrative could not have known. She is four years old, born to the Water Listeners, and she is the first Tender in three generations to be born without the elongated finger-joints. Her hands are small and ordinary. Her family has not spoken of this. They are afraid—not that she is cursed, but that she is a sign that the adaptation is reversing, that the Vault is preparing to release them, that something is ending.

The Power sees what they cannot: the child's hands are not a reversion. They are a further specialization. She will be the first Tender who can hear the thrum not in her ears but in her palms. When she is grown, she will press her unjointed fingers flat against the wet stone and feel the chambers below the chambers as a kind of music. She will not need Eshla's trance. She will not need the stone markers. She will be the bridge the Tenders did not know they were building toward.

The Power does nothing to her. Does not bless her, does not touch her, does not whisper into her dreams. The child is already exactly what she needs to be. To interfere would be to substitute the Power's design for the Vault's longer, slower, more correct one.

---

**Fourth, it does one small thing.**

In a far corner of the High Vault, near where the column crashed into the pool, there is a place where the wall weeps a particular kind of water—slightly acidic, slightly warmer than the rest. The Tenders have marked it with three black stones, meaning *do not drink, do not touch, do not cultivate near.* It is a dangerous spring. In about eleven years, by the rhythms the Stone Readers have already half-perceived, this spring will breach further and flood a section of the lower paths. Two Tenders will likely drown. A family group will lose its dwelling. The community will survive but will be diminished.

The Power considers this for what would be, in human terms, a very long while.

It does not prevent the flood. The flood is part of the Vault's grammar, and the Tenders' grief and rebuilding are part of their own. To remove the flood would be to remove a chapter from a book that is still being written by hands that have the right to write it.

But.

The Power does this: it adjusts, by an amount so small it could not be measured, the mineral content of the dangerous spring. Just enough that when the Stone Readers next test it—a ritual they perform at every third thrum-peak—they will notice a change. They will notice that the water tastes faintly of iron where before it tasted of nothing. They will not know why this matters. But Eshla, before she dies, will hear the word *iron* in the thrum, and she will not understand it, and she will have it recorded in the stone-pattern around the central pool, and eleven years from now a young Stone Reader will be looking at that pattern and will remember the iron-taste and will look again at the spring and will see, just in time, the hairline fracture spreading through the rock above it.

The family will move. The paths will be rerouted. No one will drown.

No one will ever know the Power was there. The Tenders will credit Eshla, and the Vault, and their own careful listening. They will be right to. The Power's contribution was a whisper inside a song the singers were already singing. It did not write the song. It only made sure one particular note rang true.

---

**Fifth, before it goes, it leaves a gift that is not a gift.**

It does not leave anything in the Vault. The Vault has everything it needs.

Instead, very far away, in a library in a surface city the Tenders' annual traders sometimes visit, the Power causes a particular book to fall from a high shelf and land open on a particular page. The book is about cave acoustics. The page describes how certain chamber geometries can produce sub-audible frequencies that the human body perceives as emotion rather than sound.

A scholar will pick up the book. She will become curious. She will, eventually, many years from now, when the child with the ordinary hands is grown and has children of her own, make the dangerous journey down into the deep places, looking for resonant chambers. She will find the Tenders. She will be the first outsider in four generations to be welcomed rather than merely tolerated.

She will bring no rescue. She will bring no rescue because she will understand, the moment she steps onto the obsidian ribbon and hears the thrum, that nothing here needs rescuing. She will bring only herself, and her listening, and eventually her bones, which she will ask to be left in one of the smaller pools when she dies, to become part of the Vault's slow mineral memory.

This is the gift: not the scholar, but the recognition the scholar will carry. The Tenders have been alone for seven generations. They have not minded being alone. But to be known, once, by someone from outside who does not want to change them—this is a thing they did not know they wanted until it arrives.

---

**And then the Power withdraws.**

It does not leave, exactly. A wise power does not really leave anywhere it has truly attended to. But it loosens its attention, lets the Vault return to being primarily itself rather than primarily-itself-being-observed.

Behind it, in the High Vault of Narrow, a droplet falls. The pools ripple. A new stalactite begins, somewhere in the unseen dark above, its long descent toward the water. Eshla, in her small dwelling of slate and reed, stirs in her sleep and smiles at something she will not remember when she wakes.

The child with the ordinary hands presses her palm against a wet stone wall and feels, for the first time, something that is not yet music but will be.

The thrum builds toward a crescendo that does not arrive.

It is enough. It has always been enough. The wise power's last and deepest understanding is this: the most loving thing it can do for most of what it loves is to let it continue being itself, with only the smallest, most invisible adjustments—not to its substance, but to the conditions under which its substance can keep unfolding.

It goes to attend to other places.

The Vault does not notice it has gone, because in the only way that matters, it has not.
