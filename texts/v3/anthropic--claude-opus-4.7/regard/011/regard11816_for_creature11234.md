The benevolent and wise power considers the Sopping Warrens for a long time before doing anything.

This is the first thing worth saying. The power does not arrive in a rush of light. It does not see the wet depression and feel pity, and it does not, in its first moment of attention, reach down to lift the people out of their drowning bowl onto some higher and drier ground. It looks. It looks for what would be, in the warrens' own measure of time, many scratches on the stone. It looks until it has understood not just what is happening but what the happening *means* to those who are inside it.

Because the power is wise, it knows that benevolence which has not first been patient is only a kind of vanity. And so it waits, and watches the one with the mineral-white hair press a palm into the clay at the settlement's edge, and watches the young one pause beneath the swelling bead of water, and watches the bundles in their dry niche not be opened, and slowly, slowly, the power comes to understand several things at once.

---

It understands, first, that this is not a tragedy.

A lesser power — a merely kind one, a power with heart but without wisdom — would see the wet and the cold and the impossible labor and would name it suffering. Would name the people victims. Would arrive with the gift of dryness pre-wrapped, ready to be given. The benevolent and wise power sees the labor too, and feels something move in it that is very close to grief, because the labor is real and the cold is real and the slow geological indifference of the water is real. But it also sees the young one watching the bead of water grow, and it sees the softening of the white-haired one's face at the basin, and it sees the unbroken kinetic sentence of the wall-packers' hands, and it understands that something is being made here that could not be made anywhere else. Not just the warrens. Something inside the people. A specific shape of attention. A specific quality of being-with.

To lift these people out of the bowl would be to end them. Not their bodies — their bodies would continue, somewhere drier, somewhere safer — but the thing they have become in answer to this place. The accompaniment. The meniscus-life. The capacity to be deeply occupied by a task that cannot be completed. These are not survival adaptations to be discarded the moment survival is secured. They are the people themselves. They are what living, for these eleven, has come to mean.

The power knows this because the power is wise. A foolish power would have to be told.

---

It understands, second, that the water is not the enemy.

The water is doing what water does. It is seeking its level. It is being patient in the way that gravity is patient. The benevolent and wise power, looking at the water, sees no malice in it, and so cannot in good conscience treat it as a thing to be defeated. To dry the bowl would be to overrule a process that has its own legitimacy, its own ancient reasons. The water has been rising toward this depression since before the people were here. It will continue to rise after. The people have inserted themselves into a slow conversation between water and stone, and they have done so deliberately, with their eyes open, with their tools in careful rows.

A power that intervened against the water would be a power taking sides in a dispute it had not been invited into.

---

It understands, third, that the people have not asked.

This is perhaps the most important thing. The white-haired one's syllable at the settlement's edge was informational, not supplicatory. The young one's gesture at the niche was reverence, not petition. Nowhere in all the hours of watching does the power see a hand raised toward the sky, a face turned upward in question, a voice asking for any of this to be other than it is. The people are not in a posture of asking. They are in a posture of *doing*, and the doing is so complete, so saturated with purpose, that it fills the space where asking might otherwise live.

To give a gift that has not been asked for is sometimes the highest kindness and sometimes the deepest violation, and the difference is almost always determined by whether the giver has understood the receiver. The power, being wise, knows it does not yet understand the receivers well enough to know which kind of gift this would be.

So it keeps watching.

---

But the power is also benevolent, and benevolence, when it has been patient long enough to become wisdom, does eventually act. It just acts very small.

Here is what the benevolent and wise power finally does:

It does not raise the depression. It does not lower the water table. It does not change the climate above the bowl or send a wind to part the clouds. It does not appear to the people in any form they would recognize as an arrival. It does not speak to them. It does not name itself.

Instead, it does four things, and each of them is so small that the people will not notice them, and the not-noticing is part of the gift.

---

**The first thing.**

In the deepest niche, where the tightly wrapped bundles rest on their angled slab — the niche the people draw in their shoulders to pass, the niche where the young one pressed a palm to the wall — the power lays its attention. Not its hand, not its breath. Its *attention*, which in a power of this kind is a substantive thing, a quiet weight that can be set down somewhere and left.

The attention does this: it makes the small dry place inside the wall slightly more reliably dry. Not dramatically. Not a miracle anyone would point to. The deflecting slab above the shelf will hold its angle a little longer than it would have. The seep that would have eventually found its way to the bundles, in some year not yet arrived, will take a different path through the stone, will go around. The bundles — whatever they are, bones or seeds or proofs of an above — will be kept. Not forever. Nothing here is forever. But longer. Long enough that whichever child of these eleven comes to unwrap them, when the unwrapping time arrives, will find inside them what was put inside them, intact, ready to do its work.

The power does this because it has seen the gesture at the niche, the palm pressed flat. It has seen what the people give to the wrapped things, and it understands that this — this small, tight, dry refusal kept in the heart of the wall — is the seed of a future they are working toward without speaking of it. To protect the bundles is to protect that future without dictating its shape. The people will still do all the work. The wrapping will still need re-wrapping. The slab will still need re-angling. The power has only made the odds a little kinder, in one specific place that the people have already declared sacred by the way their shoulders move when they walk past it.

---

**The second thing.**

The pale pearlescent creatures on the wet walls — the woodlouse-things that filter the film of water, that are food, that are the wall's other crop — the power gives them a slightly longer life. Not individually. Collectively. The population becomes a little more resilient. When a stretch of wall is disturbed by repair work, the creatures repopulate it a little faster. When the water in a particular passage goes briefly stagnant or briefly fresh, fewer of them die. The cluster in the shadowed niche, where the amber-forearmed one reaches in for a handful, refills itself by morning a little more dependably than it used to.

This is a gift to the protein economy of the warren. It is also a gift the people will never know they received, because they have no baseline to compare against; the creatures have always been there, and now there are slightly more of them, and the difference will only show up as a faint and unremarkable abundance, the kind of luck that does not announce itself.

The power does this because it has watched the people eat, and it has seen how briefly they eat, and it has done the arithmetic of their nourishment, and it knows that one extra handful of creatures per day, distributed across eleven bodies, is the difference between hands that shake slightly in the cold by the end of a long shift and hands that do not. A small difference. The kind of difference that, in a life of continuous fine motor maintenance, accumulates into something real.

It is feeding them. But it is feeding them through the wall, the way the wall has always fed them, so that the feeding does not break the texture of their world.

---

**The third thing.**

This one is harder to describe, and the power thinks about it for a long time before doing it.

The young one — the one who paused beneath the water bead, who watched it grow and fall and watched the splash — has inside them a faculty the others have not lost but have buried under the rhythm of the work. The faculty is something like *aesthetic attention*. It is the capacity to find a thing beautiful, or interesting, which here might be the same thing. The young one used this faculty for the duration of one held breath, and then folded it away and rejoined the work, and no one corrected them because no one noticed.

The power, having noticed, does not enlarge this faculty. Does not amplify it. Does not give the young one any new appetite for beauty that the young one did not already have. The power is wise enough to know that adding desires to a person is one of the cruelest forms of generosity, because the new desires may have no place to go.

What the power does instead is much smaller. It makes it so that, once in a great while — perhaps three times in the young one's life — there will be a moment when the young one pauses to look at something, the way they paused under the water bead, and in that moment the others around them will also pause, just for a heartbeat, and look at what the young one is looking at. Not because the power has commanded their eyes. Because the moment will happen to coincide, in a way that could be called coincidence and could be called grace, with a natural lull in the work. The brace will have just been set. The seal will have just been packed. There will be a half-second of architectural rest, and in that half-second the others will follow the young one's gaze, and they will see what the young one sees — a bead, a reflection, a particular cast of pewter light on wet stone — and they will see it together.

Nothing will be said about it. Nothing needs to be. But three times in the young one's life, they will know that they were not alone in the seeing. And the others will know, in some way they do not name, that the young one has shown them something. And the warren will be, for those three half-seconds, a slightly different kind of place — a place where the work pauses not for failure but for noticing.

This is not a gift to one person. It is a gift to the culture of the warren, deposited so quietly that it will be received without ever being unwrapped.

---

**The fourth thing.**

The power, having done these three small things, considers stopping. It would be appropriate to stop. The fourth thing is not necessary in any survival sense, and the power has been very careful about adding only what is necessary.

But the power is benevolent as well as wise, and benevolence has its own quiet insistences, and there is one more thing the power wants to do, and after some consideration it decides that wanting to do it is a sufficient reason, because the want has been examined and found to be clean.

The fourth thing is this:

The distant call that the people heard once, from beyond the lip of the depression — the low sustained sound like air blown across the mouth of a wet vessel, the sound that did not repeat — the power makes it so that this sound, or one very like it, will come again. Not often. Perhaps once per generation. Perhaps less. But it will come, and when it comes the people in the warrens will stop, briefly, and lift their heads, and listen, and know that there is something out there that makes a sound like that.

The power does not tell the people what makes the sound. The power does not even, strictly speaking, *know* what makes the sound; the sound is something the world produces on its own, an emergent property of certain weather conditions over certain landscapes, and the power has only adjusted the probabilities very slightly so that those conditions will recur in the lifetime of these people and the lifetimes of their children. The sound is not a message. The sound is not a sign. The sound is simply a reminder that the warrens are not the whole of what exists, that the depression is held inside something larger, that the silence which forgives the interruption is the silence of a world that has a great deal in it.

The people will not need this reminder in order to live. They were living before, and they will live after. But the power has watched them, and has seen how their attention turns inward upon the work, and inward upon the wet stone, and inward upon the rising water, and it has understood that an occasional outward direction — an occasional far call, an occasional acknowledgment that there is a *far* — is a thing that a being who lives in a bowl might want to receive, even if they would never ask.

This is the power's smallest gift and possibly its truest one. It is the gift of being held inside something. Of not being the whole story. Of having an outside, however distant, however unvisited, that occasionally speaks.

---

When the power has done these four small things, it considers them, and decides they are enough.

It does not leave, exactly. A power of this kind does not leave the places it has touched. It withdraws its active attention but keeps a thread of itself in the warrens, the way one might keep a finger lightly resting on a page in a book one is no longer reading, marking the place, ready to return.

It returns sometimes. Not often. It comes back to see how the young one is doing, and whether the bundles are still dry, and whether the pale creatures are still abundant, and whether the far sound has come when it was meant to. It comes back to watch the white-haired one age, and to watch new small ones be born into the warren — because there will be new small ones, eventually, and the power has done nothing to prevent or hasten this; it is the people's affair — and to watch those small ones learn the width of their own bodies, learn the angles of the lanes, learn the language of clicks and stops that the warren speaks.

The power loves these people. It is allowed to say so, to itself. Love is what its kind of attention becomes when it has been sustained long enough on a single object. The love does not change what it does. The love only confirms what it has already chosen: to let them be. To help them only in the ways they would help themselves if they were a little luckier in the small things. To not lift them out of their bowl. To not dry their walls. To not make their lives easier than they are, because the difficulty of their lives is also the shape of their lives, and the shape is dear, and the shape is theirs.

The water rises. The warrens hold. The people continue.

And somewhere just beyond their seeing, something benevolent and wise rests a hand very lightly on the stone, and does not press.
