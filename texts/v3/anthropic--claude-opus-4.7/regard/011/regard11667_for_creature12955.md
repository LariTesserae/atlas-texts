# The Visitation at Anlac

The benevolent power arrives, as such powers do, without announcement. It does not descend in fire; fire would be a kind of shouting, and this is not a place where one shouts. It enters the basin the way condensation enters the air—gradually, distributed, present before anyone thinks to look for it.

It takes some time, by its own slow reckoning, to understand what it is seeing.

---

At first it mistakes Anlac for a wound. This is a common error among powers that travel widely and have seen many worlds; the unfinished ground, the heaving spires, the strip-mining of a thing still being born—all of this reads, at first glance, as catastrophe. The power's initial instinct is the instinct of all benevolence at first contact: to *fix*. To still the ground. To finish the world. To grant the Anlacti the gift of permanence, of foundations, of silence that is the silence of completion rather than the silence of swallowed sound.

It hovers on the cusp of this gift for what would be, in human time, perhaps a long afternoon.

And then it watches a child.

The child is maybe seven, perched on a skid-platform that is being walked, foot by hydraulic foot, across a fresh extrusion of olivine foam. The child is not afraid. The child is *reading* the ground—one small hand flat against the platform's railing, feeling the tremor-grammar of what is rising beneath. With the other hand, the child signs something to a foreman two hundred meters away. The sign is precise, economical, and contains a small flourish at the end that is unmistakably a joke. The foreman's shoulders shake in silent laughter.

The power understands, then, that it has been about to commit an atrocity.

---

What it had read as wound is a marriage. What it had read as catastrophe is a *practice*. The Anlacti have not been merely surviving the Accretion; they have made of it a discipline, an art, a mode of being-in-the-world that has produced a particular kind of soul—wiry, attentive, quiet, intimate, unsentimental, and *competent* in a way that few peoples anywhere have ever managed to be. To still the ground would be to kill this soul as surely as drowning would kill a fish. The Still-Sickness is not a flaw in them; it is the signature of what they have become. They are not people who live on a shifting world. They are people who are *of* a shifting world, and the shifting world is of them.

The power revises itself. This is what wise powers do, and why they remain wise: they revise.

---

It considers, then, more modest gifts.

*Could it accelerate the Accretion's yield, so the Anlacti might labor less?* — No. Their labor is not suffering; it is the medium of their meaning. To diminish it would be to diminish them.

*Could it grant them perfect prediction of flow-events, so none would ever die in an upwelling?* — It examines this longer. It looks at the Seers on their high decks, eyes half-closed, feet planted. It sees that the Seers' authority, their *purpose*, the late-life flowering of their ground-sense into something preternatural—all of this depends on the predictions being hard-won and imperfect. A gift of perfect foresight would hollow out the elders. It would turn wisdom into mere data.

But the deaths. The deaths trouble it.

So the power does something small and specific. It does not abolish the danger. It places, instead, a faint resonance into the bones of every Anlacti child at the moment of their first shift-crew rite—a barely-perceptible deepening of the ground-sense, calibrated not to replace the elders' wisdom but to give every citizen a half-second's more warning at the worst moments. Not enough to make the dance safe. Just enough to tilt the long arithmetic of accident slightly toward survival. The Seers will still be needed. The skill will still be learned. But fewer riggers will be caught beneath fewer falling pipelines, and no one will know quite why, and the Anlacti will go on attributing it, correctly in a way, to their own deepening competence.

This is the first gift, and it is invisible.

---

The second gift takes longer to formulate, because it concerns the outside world.

The power has noticed that Anlac is harvested. Not by itself—by *itself* it is in perfect symbiosis—but by the markets and powers beyond the low hills, who want the rare earths and the unstable isotopes and who, the power can see in the long thread of probable futures, will eventually want more than the Anlacti can give in their slow, quiet, midwife's way. Someone, in some century not far off, will arrive with bigger Harvesters and a different philosophy and a contempt for the muffled hum.

The power does not believe in shielding peoples from history. Shielding is another form of stilling, and stilling is the atrocity it has just barely avoided. But it does believe in *witnessing*, and in the small adjustments witnessing makes possible.

So it leaves something in the basin. Not a guardian, exactly. More like a long memory. A presence diffused through the lithic foam and the cable-vines and the subsonic pulses of the glass-winged flocks—a presence that, when the day comes that strangers arrive with the wrong philosophy, will make the ground itself *uncooperative* with them. Not violent. Not dramatic. Merely: the flows will become unreadable to outsiders. The Harvesters of strangers will mis-calibrate. The strata-quality will seem, to anyone who has not spent a childhood with a hand flat on a shifting platform, mysteriously poor. Only those who learn the Anlacti way—who apprentice themselves, who learn the Hand-Sign, who let the hum into their bones—will find the basin yielding what it yields. The Anlacti will not know they have been given a fortress. They will only know that their craft, somehow, cannot be replicated by anyone unwilling to become like them.

This is the second gift, and it is also invisible.

---

The third gift is the smallest and the strangest, and the power hesitates over it longest.

It has been watching the elders on their high decks. It has been watching the way they spend their days *feeling*. It has noticed something the Anlacti themselves have not quite noticed: that the ground-sense, taken to its full development, is not merely a perception of geological pressure. The deep hum of the Accretion is, in its full vibrational complexity, *information*. The planet beneath Anlac is communicating—not in the way persons communicate, but in the way a forest communicates, or a weather system, or a long slow thought. The Seers, half-asleep on their platforms, are at the edge of *listening*.

The power could open this for them. It could reveal that the world they midwife is, in some manner, awake, and that their century-long dance with it has been a courtship the world has been answering.

But this is a gift that cannot be given lightly. Some peoples are ready for the news that their planet thinks; others are unmade by it. The Anlacti are pragmatic, unsentimental, and intimately bound to the ground already. They might receive it well. Or they might lose the very pragmatism that makes them themselves, and become mystics, and forget the welds and the jacks and the careful skid-paths.

The power decides: it will not reveal this. But it will *make it findable*. It will deepen the hum, very slightly, in a particular register—so that any Seer who sits long enough, who listens carefully enough, who is sufficiently still and sufficiently old and sufficiently brave, will eventually notice that the vibration has a structure. And then that Seer will decide, in council with other Seers, what to do with the noticing.

This is the third gift. It is a door, not a passage. The Anlacti will open it when, and if, they choose.

---

Then the power lingers a while longer, simply to look.

It watches a foreman murmur into the ear of an apprentice, one hand on her arm, the other indicating with two fingers the exact place where the street will rise tomorrow. It watches a flock of glass-winged things flit silent through a gantry. It watches a six-legged scavenger nose a patch of cobalt moss. It watches a child fall asleep in a hab-unit pod, the bone-deep hum of the world rocking her better than any lullaby. It watches an old man on a high deck close his eyes and smile at something only he can feel.

The power thinks: *I have seen finished worlds, and worlds at peace, and worlds of great beauty. But here is a people who have made a home of the very fact that home is impossible. Here is a city that has answered impermanence not with grief but with grace. They have not been waiting for me. They were never waiting for anyone.*

And the power, being wise, knows that the deepest benevolence it can offer such a people is the benevolence of *not interrupting them*.

It withdraws as it came, distributed and quiet, leaving behind three small adjustments that will never bear its name.

The klaxon sounds, muffled. A block of hab-units rises on its hydraulic jacks. A foreman signs across the silent air. Somewhere, an elder on a high deck tilts her head, just slightly, as if she has heard something new in the hum—and then, pragmatic to her marrow, files the impression away to consider another day.

The Choked City breathes its slow stone breath, and goes on being born.
