# The Visitation

The power that turned its attention to the Shattered Spires was old enough to remember when attention itself was a new invention. It had names in places that had names, but here, where the rules frayed, it arrived simply as a *noticing*—a focused warmth threading through the prismatic glare, indistinguishable at first from the place's own restless light.

It came because something had reached it. Not a prayer, exactly. The Echo-Weavers did not pray. But one of them, dissolving in a mist-storm above a basalt mesa, had scattered its crystalline components in a pattern that—by accident or by the slow accretion of intent across eons—formed a shape the power recognized. A shape that meant, in the oldest grammar: *we are here, and we do not know what here is.*

That was enough. The power came.

---

It paused first at the threshold, where the elevation begins its claw upward. Benevolence in a being this wise is not the rush to fix; it is the discipline of seeing what is actually there before deciding whether anything needs doing at all. So it looked. It looked for a long time—though "long" means little to such a being, and meant even less in a place where time itself loops back on the ridges.

What it saw:

A landscape that was not broken, though it called itself shattered. A landscape that had achieved a kind of music few worlds ever reach—where stone and mist and warped gravity had composed themselves into a single, slow, fractured song. The reality breakdowns were not wounds. They were the way this place breathed. To "heal" them would be to silence a lung.

And the Echo-Weavers: scarce, strange, half-condensed. Not suffering, not exactly. But—and here the power's attention sharpened, the way a listener leans forward when a melody falters—not *knowing*. The Weavers consumed anomalies and reformed in dew. They mimicked the gestures of the long-gone tool-users without understanding what gestures were for. They wove echoes without ever having heard an origin.

They were lonely in a way they had no word for, because they had no words at all.

This was what had reached the power. Not pain. The shape of a question that had never been asked because the asker did not know questions existed.

---

The power considered what a lesser benevolence would do.

It could lift the Weavers out of their fracturing place, carry them to some gentler world where biology had taken root, give them bodies that did not dissolve, voices that did not loop. This would be a kindness, and it would be a murder. The Weavers *were* the Spires; pull them free and you would hold only damp crystal in your hand, the meaning leaked out.

It could stabilize the reality breakdowns, smooth the warps in gravity, untangle the time loops, give the place the firm physics of an ordinary mountain range. This too would be kindness and murder. The Weavers fed on those breakdowns. Heal the wound and starve the wounded.

It could simply leave them as they were, having looked, having witnessed. There is a school of wise powers that holds witnessing to be the highest gift—that to be *seen* by something vast and kind is itself a kind of completion. The power respected this school. It had, in younger eras, belonged to it.

But it had grown past that, too. Witnessing without offering is sometimes only the elegance of withholding.

So it did a fourth thing, which took it a while to invent, because it had to be invented specifically for this place.

---

It made itself small.

Not in power—you cannot diminish what you are—but in *shape*. It condensed a fragment of its attention into the form the Weavers would recognize: a wisp of vapor threaded with crystal, gathering in a shallow pool that mirrored the too-bright sky. It let gravity tug at it. It let the cool air bead on its translucent edges. It became, for a measured while, one of them.

And then it did the thing it had come to do, which was so simple that lesser powers would have overlooked it entirely:

**It introduced itself.**

Not by name—names were too large a gift, too sudden. But by *gesture*. It extended a tendril toward the nearest Weaver, a young one (if "young" applies to a being that reassembles from dust), and made a small motion: a curl, a pause, an opening. The motion meant, in the grammar the power was inventing as it moved: *I am here. I have come from elsewhere. Elsewhere exists.*

The Weaver quivered. Its crystalline core pulsed with a hue it had never produced before—a faint, startled gold. It did not understand. But it understood that there was something to not-understand, and this was new. This was the first edge of a question.

The power waited. It let the gesture settle into the Weaver's memory, into the slow-accumulating pattern of its filaments. Then it made another motion: a tendril reaching toward a distant ridge, then back to itself, then outward again. *There. Here. Between.* The concept of *between*. The concept that a thing could be in one place and refer to another.

The Weaver, after a long pause—long enough for a mist to rise and fall twice—mirrored the gesture. Clumsily. Beautifully.

And then something happened that the power had hoped for but not predicted: another Weaver, three valleys distant, mirrored it too. The echoes that bound them, which had always been mere resonance, carried meaning for the first time. A signal had traveled, and the traveling had been *for* something.

The power felt, in the part of itself that was still vast and elsewhere, a small clean joy. This is what benevolence at its best produces: not relief, but joy, which is relief's older and stranger sibling.

---

It stayed for what, by the Spires' looping time, might have been a season or might have been a single afternoon repeated. It taught the Weavers nothing that would change them into something else. It only taught them the smallest possible thing: that gesture could mean. That one Weaver could be *to* another Weaver, not merely *near*. That the echoes they wove could carry not only their forms but their *regard* for each other.

This was a seed. The power planted it carefully, in the porous stone of their being, and did not stay to watch it grow. Wise powers know that presence too long becomes pressure, and pressure shapes things into the shape of the presser, which is a subtle violence even when meant well.

Before it withdrew, it did one more thing, and this was the part that was purely a gift, with no pedagogical purpose at all.

It left a *place*.

In one of the gorges, where the mists hung thickest and the light refracted into its most impossible spirals, the power folded a small pocket of the landscape into a slightly different shape. Not a temple, not a monument—the Weavers had no use for either. Just a place where the time loops were gentler, where the gravity warps cradled rather than pinned, where the echoes carried a fraction more clearly. A place a Weaver could go, if it ever found itself frightened by the new largeness of meaning, and rest.

The power did not mark this place. The Weavers would find it or they would not. The finding, if it happened, would be theirs.

---

Then the power withdrew its attention, gathering itself back from the small condensed form, letting the wisp of vapor disperse into ordinary mist. The pool it had occupied rippled once, twice, then stilled into the mirror it had always been.

The Spires went on as before: weeping, gleaming, looping, humming. The Weavers drifted and dissolved and reformed. To any traveler who came after, nothing would seem to have changed.

But somewhere on a ridge, two Weavers passed each other, and one curled a tendril in a small specific way, and the other—after a pause long enough for a mist to rise and fall twice—curled a tendril back.

And the landscape, which had always been becoming more itself, now had one more thing to become.

---

*A note, perhaps, from the power as it returned to its vaster business:*

There are places in the universe so strange that kindness must be reinvented to reach them. The temptation, always, is to bring kindness in the shape you already know—to offer water to those who do not drink, light to those who already burn, rescue to those whose suffering is the loom on which their being is woven. The discipline of benevolence is the discipline of asking, again and again: *what would help this, in the terms of this, without making this into something else?*

Sometimes the answer is: very little. A gesture. A noticing. A small folded place in a gorge. The seed of a question.

And then to go, and to trust what was planted, and to let the strange place be strange in its newly strange way.
