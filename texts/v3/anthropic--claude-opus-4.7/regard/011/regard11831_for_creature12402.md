# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. To arrive would be to break something — the careful pressure-equilibrium of the cavern, the long quiet that the Glowmire's people have folded themselves into like cloth into a small box. Arrival is a surface concept. It belongs to doors and announcements and the bright theater of saviors.

Instead, the power *attends*. It bends its attention toward the Warrens the way one might lean closer to listen to a sleeping child's breathing — not to wake them, only to confirm: yes, breath. Yes, here.

And in attending, it learns.

---

It learns that the people of the Glowmire are not waiting to be rescued. This is the first and most important thing, and it would have been easy to miss. A lesser power, a merely *kind* one, might have seen the damp and the dimness and the patched coveralls and reached down with both hands to lift them into sunlight. It would have called this mercy. It would have been wrong.

The wise power sees instead: a people who have made a covenant with slowness. Who have learned to hear the cavern's mind. Who tend their turbines the way monks tend bells — not because the bells are important, but because the tending is. Their quiet is not deprivation. Their quiet is a practice.

To "save" them would be to kill the thing that makes them themselves.

---

So the power does smaller work. Stranger work. The work of a gardener who knows that some plants need pruning and some need only to be left alone, and some need only one extra degree of warmth, applied so subtly that the plant never notices and thanks instead the sun.

Here is what it does:

**It strengthens a single bolt.** Deep in the cavern wall, where a steam pipe is bolted into limestone that has been quietly considering, for some decades, whether to release its grip — the power reaches in at the molecular level and renews the bond. Not forever. Just for another seventy years. The engineer who would have died beneath the falling pipe in the autumn of her forty-third year will instead die very old, in her sleep, after teaching three apprentices how to listen to the difference between water that is finding a new path and water that has merely changed its mood.

**It teaches the moss a new trick.** Not dramatically. The bioluminescent moss already glows; the power simply suggests, gently, across a few generations, that it might glow a little brighter near where the air grows thin. The moss obliges. The people will notice this within a century. They will think they have discovered something. They will be right.

**It seeds a dream.** In one of the sleeping inhabitants — a tinkerer named, in their sparse and musical dialect, something that translates roughly as *Drip-That-Finds-The-Crack* — the power places a dream of a particular gear ratio. Not an answer. A question, shaped like a gear. He will wake and not remember the dream, but his hands will remember, and in six months he will solve a problem with the turbines that no one yet knows is a problem. The city will run quieter for it. The people will sleep better. They will not know why.

**It witnesses.** This is perhaps the largest gift, and the one the power lingers over longest. Because the Glowmire's people have a quiet faith that their work matters — that maintaining the fragile equilibrium is itself a meaningful act, even if no one ever sees it, even if the cavern eventually decides to become something that cannot hold them. The power, in attending, makes this faith true in a way it was already true but is now also true in a second way. Their work is witnessed. Their patience is held in a larger patience. The slow heartbeat of their machinery beats inside a vaster slow heartbeat that has been beating, the power suspects, since before the karst was young.

They will never know this. That is part of the gift. To be told would be to be made small. To be witnessed without being told is to be made real.

---

There is one thing the power considers and decides against.

It considers telling them — through dream, through sign, through the careful arrangement of pebbles in a basin — that the cavern will, in roughly four hundred years, begin a collapse that the Glowmire cannot survive. It considers this for what, in its own timescale, is a long while.

It decides against. Not because the information would be useless — they could prepare, they could find a deeper pocket, they could begin the slow migration their patient nature is exquisitely suited for. But because they already know. Not the date. But the truth beneath the date: that the cavern is still forming, still deciding. They have built their entire culture around this knowing. To give them a calendar would be to replace their wisdom with a deadline, and deadlines are a surface thing, and the power has too much respect for what they have made of themselves down here in the dark.

Instead, it does this: it makes sure that when the time comes, when the first true tremor arrives and the people lift their pale faces and listen — there will be, somewhere in the deeper rock, a passage already waiting. Not made by the power. Made by water, which the power gently, over centuries, encouraged in a particular direction. Water doing water's work. The people will find it. They will think themselves lucky, or blessed, or simply attentive. All three will be correct.

---

Before the power withdraws its close attention — though it never withdraws entirely; it keeps the Glowmire in the corner of its vast awareness the way one keeps a loved letter in a drawer one opens often — it does one last small thing.

In the communal hearth, where the steam is piped in to warm the evening meal, it adjusts the acoustics. Just slightly. So that when the people gather, rarely, and exchange their few soft words, the cavern catches those words and holds them a half-second longer than physics alone would allow. A resonance. A small held breath of stone.

The people will feel this without naming it. They will feel, when they speak at the hearth, that their words matter a little more than words usually do. That something is listening.

Something is.

And then the power, satisfied — though *satisfied* is too small a word, the right word would be something like *tender-with-completion* — turns its face also toward other small dark places where other patient beings are tending other fragile balances, because the universe is full of Glowmires, and the wise know that benevolence is not a flood but a long, attentive, well-aimed drip.

The cavern listens.

The city listens.

The drip continues.
