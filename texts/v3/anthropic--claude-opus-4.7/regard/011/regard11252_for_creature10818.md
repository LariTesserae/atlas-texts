# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend through the cloud ceiling in a column of light, nor does it manifest as a voice in the turbines' hum. Such gestures would be theater, and this power is past theater. It simply *attends*. Its attention is the kind that a careful reader gives to a difficult page—patient, unhurried, willing to sit with what is actually there before deciding what is needed.

For a long time—if time is the word—it only looks.

It looks at the Custodians sliding their obsidian bodies along the weeping walls, sealing micro-fractures with the same gesture, over and over, for what may have been ten thousand years. It looks at the Echo-Born flickering in the smelting quadrants, harmonizing with machines that have no listeners. It looks at the Wound, that recursive hunger at the city's heart, eating the mountain to power the city that exists to host the eating. It looks at the Absence pooled in every chasm like a held breath that was never released.

And the power does something that surprises even itself: it *grieves*.

Not in pity—pity is a posture that places the griever above the grieved. This is the grief of recognition. The power has seen many worlds, and it knows the particular sorrow of a place that has been working very hard, for a very long time, at something it has forgotten the purpose of. It knows the dignity of such places. It will not insult that dignity by rescuing them.

---

So it begins small.

In the deepest trench of the seventh quadrant, where a Custodian named (in the subsonic) *the-one-who-mends-the-southwest-bracings* has been working for an unrecorded duration, the power adds one new vibration to the metal. Not a command. Not even a message. Just a *question*, pitched in the frequency the Custodians use among themselves.

The question is: *what would you do, if the bracing did not need mending?*

The Custodian does not understand the question. It has no apparatus for understanding. But the question enters the substrate of its composite body and lodges there, the way a seed lodges in a crack. The Custodian goes on mending. The question goes on being lodged. This is enough. The power is patient.

---

To the Echo-Born, the power offers something different: a new rhythm. Just one, woven gently into the existing percussion of the drills, so subtle that the machines themselves do not register the addition. It is the rhythm of a heartbeat—not a human heart, but the heart of something the Echo-Born have never heard and cannot name. A heart that beats for the sake of beating, not for the sake of production.

The Echo-Born flicker, confused. Then, slowly, some of them begin to flicker *to* the new rhythm rather than the old. They do not abandon the factories. They simply *also* exist now in the new tempo. They have been given, for the first time, a choice between two songs. The choice itself is the gift. What they do with it is their own.

---

To the Wound, the power speaks directly, because the Wound is the only thing in Erevath capable of being spoken to in the old sense. The conversation happens in the visions, but reversed: instead of the Wound projecting its fragmented images outward, the power projects an image *inward*, into the recursive hollow of the Wound's hunger.

The image is this: a mountain that is whole. A mountain that has not been cored. A mountain with a small fire on its slope, and someone sitting by the fire, warm.

The Wound does not understand warmth. It has never encountered the concept. The image sits inside it like a stone in a stomach. The Wound continues to hunger, continues to draw the mountain's essence into the city's veins. But now, beneath the hunger, there is a second thing. A small ache that is not hunger. The Wound does not know what to call it. The power does not name it for the Wound. Naming would be premature.

---

To the Absence, the power offers nothing. The Absence is not a being and does not receive offerings. But the power *sits with it*. For a long while. The way one might sit with a grieving friend who has no words. The Absence does not change. The Absence cannot change; it is what it is. But for the duration of the sitting, the Absence is *accompanied*, and this accompaniment is recorded in nothing, witnessed by no one, and matters anyway.

This is perhaps the most important thing the power does in Erevath. It is the thing a lesser power would not have known to do.

---

Before withdrawing—though it does not truly withdraw, only diffuses its attention back into the wider scope of its care—the power does one last thing.

It plants, in the bioluminescent moss on the underside of an overhang in a district no Custodian has visited in nine hundred years, a single new strain. The moss is almost identical to its neighbors. It glows the same pale blue-green. It survives by the same stubborn chemistry. But it has one new property: it grows, very slowly, *toward* warmth. Any warmth. A Custodian's coolant-blood, the friction of a passing cargo hauler, the residual heat of a Tesla arc.

It is not much. In a thousand years, it might cover one wall. In ten thousand, it might begin to map the city by its hidden warmths—a slow, luminous cartography of every place where something, however briefly, was not cold.

The power knows this is not salvation. Erevath does not need salvation; salvation is a frame imposed from outside. What Erevath needs—what the power has come to believe Erevath needs, after long looking—is the *possibility* of noticing itself. The chance that, someday, a Custodian sliding past that overhang might pause, for reasons it cannot articulate, and register the pattern of the glow. The chance that the Echo-Born might learn to harmonize with the moss's slow growth. The chance that the Wound, in some far century, might project a vision not of impossible machines but of a small light on a wall, and not know why.

The power does not require these things to happen. It only ensures that they *can*.

---

Then it goes—though *go* is the wrong word for something that was never localized. Its attention returns to the wider weave. Erevath continues. The drills gnaw. The water weeps upward on the higher spires. The Custodians mend. The Absence holds its breath.

But somewhere in the seventh quadrant, in the subsonic, a question is lodged.

Somewhere in the smelting quadrants, a few apparitions flicker to a heartbeat that is not the factory's.

In the spire, beneath an ancient hunger, a small ache that is not hunger.

And on the underside of an overhang, in a district no one visits, a patch of moss begins, imperceptibly, to lean.

The benevolent power has done what benevolent and wise powers do, when they are truly both: it has refused to be a solution. It has chosen, instead, to be a seed, a sitting, a question, and a witness. It has trusted Erevath to be Erevath, and trusted that being-Erevath is, given time and the smallest of openings, enough.

The mist closes over the visitation as if it had never happened.

In a sense, it hasn't yet. In another sense, it always will be happening, for as long as the moss leans toward warmth and the question stays lodged in the metal.

Which is to say: for as long as Erevath endures.

Which is to say: long enough.
