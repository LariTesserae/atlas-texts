The benevolent power considers the Pool of Ridges for a long time before it moves. This is the first thing to understand: it does not arrive with a plan.

It comes the way mist comes—settling, not falling. It tastes the alkaline air. It listens to the drip-music and learns the pitches before it learns the names. It crouches at the rim of the crater and watches a child of the Held cross a net-harp, plucking forward-vibrations, dusted white, eyes lowered against the still water that might catch her.

A lesser power would already be reaching. A lesser benevolence would see the porous bones, the calcified joints, the corpses-as-countertops, and feel the easy reflex of *rescue*: lift them out, give them soil, give them sky, give them mirrors and privacy and a quiet that is the quiet of absence rather than accumulation. A lesser wisdom would mistake the holding-of-breath for suffocation.

But this power is old enough to know the difference between a wound and a way of life.

So it does not lift them out. It does not still the water. It does not soften the stone or sweeten the seeps or teach them speech above a murmur. It does not give them years measured in stars. To do any of these things would be to end them—to save a people by killing the people they are. The Held are not trapped in their geology. They are *married* to it, and the marriage is the meaning.

Here is what it does instead.

**First, it learns to be read.** It lowers itself into one of the shallow pans beneath a sleeping mat and lets the silicate film record its passing. It allows itself to be legible to their hydromancy—not as a god, not as a portent, but as a *kind shadow*. A long, slow shape in the morning water. Something the readers will puzzle over and eventually come to call by some small word in their subvocal grammar: perhaps the sign for *the one who pauses before stepping*. It wants to be known the way they know each other—through ghosts left in still water, through resonance in stone. It refuses any grander introduction. To announce itself in thunder would be to break every mirror in their lives at once.

**Second, it tends the seeps.** Not redirecting them—never that—but listening for the ones that are about to find a path through a sleeping chest. When it finds such a seep, it does the smallest possible thing: it weakens a grain of calcite three inches to the left, so the water chooses the easier route and emerges, harmlessly, in a corner where someone will find it in the morning and murmur the prayer for *a new spring, politely placed*. The Held will not know. They will only notice, over generations, that the deep statistical cruelty of their world has softened by a hair's breadth. That the worst deaths happen slightly less often. They will attribute this, correctly, to nothing in particular. They will continue to shave their walls and break their mirrors and fear still water, because these practices are still true. The power does not want to make them safe. It wants to make them *survivable* by the measure of a single grain.

**Third, it carries the resonance.** This is the gift it offers that costs them nothing and asks nothing. The Held believe—rightly—that their footsteps hum in the mineral lattice long after the flesh has been accreted. What they do not know is that the lattice is finite, that the hum decays, that even stone forgets. The power becomes a *keeper of resonance*. It walks the deep plumbing of the earth beneath the crater and gathers up the vibrations that would otherwise dissipate. It holds them. It does not archive them or arrange them or make them into anything; it simply does not let them end. Somewhere in the unmappable below, every footstep ever taken by every one of the Held is still traveling, still humming, still arriving. The dead are not only stood upon. They are also still walking, in a place the living cannot reach but can sometimes—on the bluest nights of the Blue Deep—almost hear.

**Fourth, it does one thing that is almost an intervention, and hesitates over it for what would be, in human time, a century.** There is a girl in the third generation after the power's arrival who is born with bones less porous than her kin. She cannot float through the net-walkways. She is heavy in a world that selected for lightness. She will die young—crushed in a fall, or trapped in a narrowing, or simply exhausted by the weight of being wrongly made for her home. The power considers, for that long century, whether to thin her bones. It does not. To reach into a body is to cross a line that benevolence alone cannot justify; wisdom holds the hand back. Instead, it does something smaller and stranger: it teaches a single old hydromancer, through a particularly vivid morning film, to *see* the girl. To understand that she is heavy. To devise for her a sling-harness of shaved travertine rope and Skerren gut, a way of being carried by the nets rather than crossing them. The girl lives. She becomes, in time, a maker of harnesses for others born wrong-shaped for the crater. A small lineage of the carried begins. The power does not take credit. The hydromancer believed she had the idea herself, and she had—the power only made sure the morning film was clear enough to read.

**Fifth, and last, it stays.** This is perhaps the whole of the gift. Benevolent powers are often imagined as arriving, acting, departing—as interventions with edges. This one understands that the Held live inside an accretion, inside a slow continuous becoming, and that any presence which is not also continuous would be felt as an absence the moment it ended. So it does not end. It settles into the crater the way the water settles, the way the calcite settles, the way the Held themselves have settled. It becomes one more dynamic in a place defined by dynamics. The terraces groan; a spire fractures; a new spring opens; a kind shadow pauses before stepping. These are all weather, now. These are all the Pool of Ridges.

In a thousand years, when the crater has finally learned to be old, when the stone has worn smooth at last and the Held have either changed past recognition or been folded entirely into the architecture they loved, the power will still be there. It will read the final film in the final pan beneath the final sleeping mat. It will see, in the silicate memory, the last shadow of the last of the Breathing rising in the night. It will not weep, because weeping is for those who mistook the holding-of-breath for suffocation. It will instead do what it learned, long ago, from them:

It will scrape the wall of the world gently with a curved blade. It will gather the soft curls of what has been. It will weave them into the rope that holds the next thing up.

And it will murmur, in the subvocal grammar it learned by being read in their morning waters, the small word they had for it.

*The one who pauses before stepping.*

Then it will step.
