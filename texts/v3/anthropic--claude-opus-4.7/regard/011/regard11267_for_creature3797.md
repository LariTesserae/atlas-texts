## The Visitor Who Does Not Arrive

The benevolent power notices the Submerged Pocket the way one might notice a held breath in a quiet room—not by sound, but by the shape of the silence around it.

It does not enter.

This is the first thing to understand. A wiser power than most would know that to step into the chamber would be to end it. The Pocket is young, still deciding. The Hollowed Ones are dreaming, and dreams collapse under the weight of witness. To arrive in fullness—to bring light, voice, intention—would be to answer questions the place has not yet finished asking itself.

So the power does something stranger. It *attends*.

---

### What attending looks like

It thins itself until it is no thicker than the mist between two droplets. It learns the rhythm of the chamber's breathing—the long inhale of centuries, the slow exhale of rising water—and matches it. It does not introduce its own tempo. It borrows the chamber's.

For a long time (and time here is a porous thing), it simply listens. To the *plink*. To the slow uncoiling of the eel. To the patterned flicker of the algae, which is not quite language but is *trying*, the way an infant's babble is trying. The power does not translate. Translation would fix the meaning, and the meaning is not yet ready to be fixed.

It notices the cairn. It does not touch it. But it understands, with the gentle ache of recognition, that *something here once wanted to be remembered*—or wanted to remember itself—and stacked stones against the forgetting. The power leaves the cairn exactly as it is. Even a benevolent hand, laid in blessing, would change the angle of a stone.

---

### What it does for the Hollowed Ones

This is the harder question.

They are not dead. They are not suffering—not in any way the power can name. They are not asking to be released. They may, in fact, be doing the slow holy work that all such beings do: holding the chamber together by being woven into it. To pull them free might be to unweave the place itself.

But the power can feel the *almost-whisper* at their parted mouths. Something is trying to be said, across geological time, in a voice slower than weather.

So the power does this:

It becomes, for a while, a listener slow enough to hear them.

It slows its own attention until a century passes like a held note. It waits at the lips of one of the Hollowed Ones—not all of them, just one, the one whose fingers have curled the most over the long dreaming—and it lets the whisper arrive at whatever speed the whisper needs.

The whisper, when it comes, is not a word. It is closer to a *direction*. A leaning. A small preference, the way a seed prefers the warmer side of the soil. The Hollowed One is leaning, almost imperceptibly, toward the cairn.

The power understands. Or rather, it understands *enough*—which is the only kind of understanding wisdom permits itself in places like this.

It does not move the Hollowed One. It does not move the cairn. Instead, it does the smallest possible thing: it makes sure that, when the chamber next exhales, the light from the algae flickers along the line between them. Just once. Just enough that, if anyone were watching across the long dreaming, they would see the connection acknowledged.

A witness. Not an intervention. A witness.

---

### What it does for the chamber

The chamber is young, the text says. Still forming, still deciding what it wants to be.

The power resists, with great tenderness, the urge to help it decide.

Instead, it leaves a kind of permission. Not a gift, because gifts must be received and the chamber has no hands. More like a *clearing*—a small space in the surrounding pressures of the world where the chamber is allowed to keep being uncertain. Where water may flow upward without being corrected. Where the walls may shift when unobserved without being pinned down by the demand to be consistent.

The power, in other words, becomes a kind of guardian against premature definition. The world above is hungry for things to be one thing or another. The Pocket needs to be both. The power makes sure the Pocket is left alone long enough to find out what it is becoming, on its own terms, in its own time.

This is, perhaps, the most benevolent thing a wise power can ever do: *not finish something on its behalf*.

---

### What it does about the human who found the fissure

(There is always a human, in stories like this. The text addresses *you*.)

The power notices that the fissure has been found. That someone slipped through. That they stayed, perhaps, a little too long—long enough to feel the water rise, long enough that the fissure shrank behind them, or they grew.

The power does not punish this. Curiosity is not a sin in any cosmology worth having.

But it does, very gently, place a small thought in the mind of the visitor as they leave: *Do not tell anyone exactly where this is.* Not as a command. As a feeling. The kind of feeling you get when you find a bird's nest and know, without being told, that pointing at it would be a kind of harm.

The visitor will remember the chamber. They will dream of violet moss and the figures in the walls. They will, for the rest of their life, be slightly more patient with slow things—with grief that takes decades to move, with love that arrives in geological time, with their own becoming.

This is what the chamber gave them, and the power makes sure they get to keep it.

---

### And then it leaves

The power withdraws the way mist withdraws from a windowpane in morning—not absent, exactly, just less present. It will return. It returns to all such places, on its long round. The Pocket is on its list now, though *list* is too sharp a word; it is in its attention, the way a mother keeps a sleeping child in her attention even while doing other things.

If, in some far century, the Hollowed Ones finish their whisper—if the cairn finishes saying what it was stacked to say—if the chamber decides at last what it is—the power will be there to witness. Not to applaud. Not to interpret. Just to have been the one who was paying attention when it happened.

That is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they are wise enough to be truly benevolent:

They make sure that nothing that matters happens unwitnessed.

And they make sure that nothing that matters is interrupted by being witnessed too soon.
