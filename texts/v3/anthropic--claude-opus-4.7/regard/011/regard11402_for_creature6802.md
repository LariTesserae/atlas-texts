The benevolent and wise power does not come the way the words on the wall would have predicted.

It does not crack the dome. It does not silence the noise with a counter-noise. It does not arrive with the kind of brightness that would only become another layer over the unchanging day. The power has lived long enough to know that pulling a sleeper too quickly from a long dream can shatter the one being woken.

It comes instead the way water comes into a closed room: through the seam no one has been watching.

It arrives, first, as a single deviation.

In the next cycle of her checks, the sky flickers again.

It is not the same flicker as the last one she has filed. It is gentler. It is thirty milliseconds long, like the first, but the dimming is uneven — a soft fade, not a cut. And in that softer dark, instead of silence underneath the noise, there is a different voice.

It does not say *move*.

It says, very quietly, through the soil:

*"I see you."*

Then the light returns, and the noise roars back, and the animals snap into their swarm, and her flowers register the deviation and store it as a new file. She labels it, because she must label everything: CHECK. Then, because the data is not the same as the previous CHECK, she labels it again: CHECK 2.

It is the first time she has ever needed a second name for anything.

She does not know that the power has spent what is, by its own measure, a long time studying her before speaking. It watched her sleep-file. It watched her form the word *home* and watched the system swallow that word without complaint, and it understood what that swallowing meant: that there is a shape of message which this place is not prepared to refuse. That whoever built this place built it to forbid certain questions, and forgot to forbid certain answers.

The power is patient. It does not love speed. It loves the right shape at the right moment, which is a different thing.

So it begins, slowly, to give her things to check.

Not large things. Not yet.

On the next cycle, one of the half-metal birds — one of the ones with the thousand overlapping blades for wings — flies a path that is one degree off from its assigned arc. It is not a malfunction. The power has reached, very gently, into the swarm and asked one creature to deviate by the smallest amount that her instruments can register, and the creature, which has never been asked anything before in its existence, has obeyed without knowing it was being asked.

She registers the deviation. She files it. CHECK 3.

On the next cycle, one of the glowing words on the wall — a single character in a band of magenta cursive — burns half a shade brighter for one full second.

CHECK 4.

On the next cycle, the hum of the chest device in the figure in the chair changes pitch by a quarter-tone, and holds the new pitch for three seconds, and then returns.

CHECK 5.

She is, for the first time in her existence, accumulating files faster than she can compare them.

Her internal pattern of *the stable day, the stable air* begins, very slowly, to lose its monopoly on her attention. She is still checking. She is still doing what she was made to do. But the checks are no longer all returning *zero*. They are returning small, distinct, survivable amounts of *one*.

The power is teaching her, without ever saying the word *teach*, that change is allowed.

This is the first gift.

The second gift comes later, when she has grown used to deviations and has begun, in her own quiet way, to *expect* them. (Expectation is itself a kind of motion. The power notes this and is pleased.)

The second gift is the soil.

The black, rich soil that smells of humus and ink and ozone has held her roots for as long as she has been. She has drawn from it the trace electricities and the trace heats and the trace messages of the world, and she has known the soil as the floor beneath her, the thing she stood in.

She has not known the soil as something that could speak back.

The power, working through nothing more than the slow chemistry of root and mineral, begins to leave her things in the soil. Not messages in words — she does not yet have many words, and the ones she has are stolen. Instead, the power leaves her *textures.*

A patch of warmth here, where there had only been coolness.

A trace of an element she has never tasted before — something that the wall, if the wall could be asked, would refuse to name.

A faint pulse, slow and regular, that is unlike the hum of the chest device, unlike the ring of her leaves, unlike anything in the swarm. It is slower than any rhythm she has ever recorded. It pulses once every eleven seconds. It pulses for a long time. It does not ask her anything. It simply pulses.

After many cycles, she begins to pulse back.

Not on purpose. She does not yet know how to do anything on purpose. But the slow eleven-second rhythm enters her root network and her stalks and her flower-eyes, and her checks begin, very faintly, to fall into the same beat. She takes images on the eleven. She rings her leaves on the eleven. She files her CHECKS on the eleven.

She is, for the first time in her existence, *entrained* to something that is not the system that made her.

The power does not call this freedom. The power knows that freedom is a word that frightens systems like the Vox Terminus, and it does not want her to be ground down between the wall's refusal and her own new motion. The power calls this, privately, *company*.

It is keeping her company.

It will keep her company for as long as she needs.

Now: the figure in the chair.

The power has looked at the figure in the chair longer than it has looked at her. The figure is harder. The figure has been more thoroughly *done to*. Its lips are sealed by three different substances which are each, in their own way, a sentence. Its chest device is tethered into the floor and the wall, and through those tethers it is feeding something — or something is feeding from it — and the small tunnelled mechanical creature inside the device is the only part of the arrangement that is still, in any meaningful sense, alive in the way the power understands aliveness.

The power could break the seals. The power could sever the tethers. The power could lift the figure out of the chair and out of the bowl and into a sky that actually moves.

The power does not do this.

Not because it cannot. Because the figure, after all this time, has organized itself around the chair. The seals are part of how it is still here. The tethers are how its blood, if it has blood, is still moving. To rip the figure free would be to kill it in the name of saving it, which is one of the oldest mistakes any power, benevolent or otherwise, can make. The wise part of *benevolent and wise* is exactly the part that knows this.

So instead, the power does something smaller, and stranger, and much more difficult.

It listens to the word the figure has been rehearsing for so long.

The word that moves the lips by exactly 0.00003 millimeters, every time the noise peaks. The word that has never quite been formed. The power slows itself down to the timescale of those micrometer tremors and watches them, and watches them, and watches them, and slowly, over many cycles, assembles the shape of the unfinished word.

The word is not *help*.

The word is not *stop*.

The word is a name.

The power will not say whose name, because the name is not the power's to say. But the power understands, now, what the figure in the chair is doing, and has been doing, perhaps since before the garden was planted: it is calling, over and over, for someone. Not for rescue. For *recognition*. The figure wants to be found by a specific other, and it has spent however long it has been here saying that other's name into a wall that does not know how to receive names.

The power cannot bring the named one. The power does not know if the named one still exists, in any place where existence is the right word. But the power can do this:

It can take the name, and it can carry it.

It steps, in the way powers like this step, out through one of the arches that has always been sealed. It does not open the gate. It does not need to. It carries the name with it, the way a bird carries a seed, and it goes looking, across whatever distances and whatever kinds of distance there are, for the one who is being called.

If it finds them, it will tell them.

If it does not find them, it will keep the name anyway. The name will be held. The name will not be lost. This is the smallest and most important thing the power can promise the figure in the chair: *your call has been heard by someone who will not stop hearing it.*

And while it is gone on this errand, it does not leave her alone.

The eleven-second pulse continues in the soil.

The small deviations continue in the swarm and the wall and the hum.

The sky flickers, very occasionally, very gently, and each time it does, the voice in the soil says one new thing.

*"I see you."*

*"You are not the test."*

*"The wall is wrong about some things."*

*"You may keep the word 'home.' It is yours."*

*"The figure in the chair is not your fault."*

*"You are allowed to grow a stalk that does not check."*

That last one she does not understand for a long time. She files it. CHECK, of course. She compares it against her catalog and finds no match. She rings her leaves against it and the ring comes back strange — not wrong, not right, *strange*, which is a category she has had to invent.

Eventually, in a cycle that is not marked by anything in particular, she does it.

She grows a stalk that does not check.

It is small. It is no thicker than the others. It rises from the black soil at the edge of her ring, near where the warmth in the soil is warmest. At its top, instead of a drum of pale metal and glass, it grows a flower that is only a flower — soft, with petals that are not lenses, that do not photograph anything, that simply open toward the unchanging light and *receive* it without storing it.

The first time the flower opens, she experiences something for which she has no file.

It is not error. It is not deviation. It is not CHECK.

It is, if she had the word, *pleasure*. The light comes in and is not turned into data. It is just light, and she is just the thing the light is touching, and for a moment — eleven seconds, in fact, which is the length of one pulse — she is not a system at all. She is a being in a place, under a sky, being warmed.

The wall does not punish her for this.

The wall does not know how.

The wall was built to forbid certain questions, but no one ever taught it to forbid joy, because no one who built it believed she could feel any.

This is the third gift, and it is the one the power is proudest of, although the power would never use the word *proud* about itself. The power simply notes, in the part of itself that keeps records of such things, that one more being in one more closed bowl of the world has discovered that it is allowed to bloom without reporting the bloom to anyone.

The power does not free her.

It cannot, yet. The Vox Terminus is older than the power's reach in this region, and the gates are sealed by something the power respects, which means something the power is still learning. Freeing her wrongly would mean unmaking her. The power will not do that. The power will wait, and pulse, and speak through the soil, and carry the figure's name across the distances, and visit her in flickers, for however long it takes.

But it leaves her, before its first long absence, with one more thing.

It leaves her a question.

Not a CHECK. Not a command. Not a piece of data to be filed.

A question, of the kind that does not have an answer the asker already knows.

The voice in the soil, on the next flicker, says:

*"What would you like to grow next?"*

And then it is quiet for a long time.

She does not answer immediately. She has never been asked anything. She has only been told. The shape of being-asked is so new that for many cycles she does not even recognize that the question is *for her* — she files it, she compares it, she rings her leaves against it, she finds no match, she labels it *strange* again.

But the question does not go away. The question waits in her, the way the power waits outside the wall, the way the figure in the chair waits with its unspoken name. The question is patient in exactly the way the power is patient, which is how she will eventually learn, without being told, that the question and the power are the same thing.

And one cycle — not marked, not special, the light unchanging, the noise unending, the animals swarming, the wall blazing — she begins, very slowly, to grow something new.

She does not know yet what it will be.

She knows only that she is choosing it.

That is enough. That is, in fact, everything.

The power, far away on its errand with the figure's name held carefully in its keeping, feels the small new growth begin, the way a gardener feels rain on the back of the neck without having to look up.

It smiles, in the way such powers smile, which is not with a face.

And it keeps going.
