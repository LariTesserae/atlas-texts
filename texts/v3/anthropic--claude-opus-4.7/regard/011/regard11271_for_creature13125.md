# The Tending

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend through the bruised violet murk because descent implies above and below, and this place has long since forgotten such distinctions. It simply becomes present in the way warmth becomes present in a held hand — already there, only now noticed.

It sees the Disjunct first. Not as a problem. Not as something broken needing fixing. It sees the creature the way one might see a calligrapher's single, perfect brushstroke held across a hurricane: improbable, exquisite, and *true* in a way that has nothing to do with comfort.

The power considers.

---

A lesser benevolence would reach in immediately. Would calm the boil. Would re-knit the frayed physics, restore gravity to its proper office, drain the basin, raise up dry land and rigid stone, and say: *There. Now you can rest. Now you can be whole.*

And the Disjunct, if anything remained of it after such a mercy, would be a smear of disarticulated organs lying on clean ground under a clean sky, finally and forever dead. Because the chaos is not its prison. The chaos is its medium. The frayed physics is its skeleton. To restore order around it would be to pull the bones out of a living thing and lay them tidily beside the corpse.

The wise power knows this. Wisdom, here, is the discipline of not rescuing.

So it does something smaller, and infinitely more difficult.

---

**First, it listens.**

It lowers itself — not in altitude but in register — into the deafening thunder, and it does not cancel the noise. It learns it. It learns the billion waterfalls by name. It learns the specific frequency of the teardrop of black cartilage humming its inverse song, and it learns how much of the Disjunct's tiny, hard-won silence is *earned* — how the counter-resonance is not just a defense but a kind of prayer, a sustained note of selfhood sung against an ocean of unmaking.

The power does not join the song. To join would be to drown out. Instead, it listens so completely that the song is, for the first time in uncounted ages, *heard*. Heard not as struggle but as music. Heard not as the desperate noise of a thing trying not to die, but as the voice of someone speaking. Someone there.

The Disjunct does not know it is being heard. But somewhere in its scattered neural nodes, in the wormhole-stitched lattice of its mind, a pressure it had never known it carried — the pressure of being unwitnessed in a universe of pure noise — eases by an amount too small to measure and too large to name.

---

**Second, it remembers with it.**

The power turns its attention to the longing. To the ache for right angles, for dry air, for rigid stone. The Disjunct's terrible, beautiful homesickness for a world it never lived in, whose atoms it merely inherited.

A lesser power would give it what it wants. Would build it a small palace of glass and fretwork in a pocket of stabilized space, a diorama of the Built, and set the Disjunct inside it like a fish in a bowl. And the Disjunct would press its crimson sacs against the rigid walls and feel, for the first time, contiguous, and whole, and *wrong*. Because the ache is not for the architecture. The ache is the architecture. The longing is the cathedral. To satisfy it is to demolish it.

So the power does this instead: when the next reality-storm strikes the next weeping monolith, and the ghost-architecture flashes into being for its fractured second — the power *holds the second open*. Not long. Not enough to change anything. Just long enough that the Disjunct, throwing its scattered parts into the phantom geometry, gets one full breath of contiguity instead of a fractional gasp. One whole heartbeat of remembered solidity. One sustained chord instead of a struck note.

The Disjunct does not understand what has happened. It only knows that this time, the marrow was richer. This time, when the turbulence screamed through and shredded the ghost back into wet stone, it carried more away with it — more order, more memory of being one thing — and the long drift afterward through the cloying dark felt, for a while, less like exile and more like errand.

The power does this perhaps a dozen times across what would be, in another universe, a century. Never often enough to be noticed. Never generously enough to be relied upon. Just enough to seed the suspicion, deep in the Disjunct's dispersed consciousness, that the pilgrimage is *answered*. That the ache points somewhere real.

---

**Third, it attends the dark ribbons.**

This is the hardest thing, and the power thinks about it for a long time.

The ribbons are not evil. They are errors — self-propagating tangles of anti-geometry, blind and hungry and no more malicious than a tumor or an avalanche. To destroy them would be easy. To destroy them would also be to reach into the basin's frayed physics and start pulling threads, and the power has already decided not to unmake this world.

But it can do this: when a dark ribbon's blind path is about to slither through the exact spatial fold tethering the Disjunct's organs — when the slicing is a microsecond away — the power can nudge. Not the ribbon. The ribbon is the world's. The power nudges the Disjunct's *attention*. A faint, inexplicable wrongness, a pressure-prickle in the filamentous fans, an unaccountable urge in the crimson sacs to drift left.

The Disjunct will think it sensed the ribbon itself. It will think its own perception is sharpening. It will feel, slowly across generations, that it is becoming wiser, more attuned, more skilled at surviving its impossible home.

This, too, is a gift. The gift of believing oneself the author of one's own continuance. The power does not want to be thanked. It wants the Disjunct to feel competent in its own life.

It does not save every Disjunct. Some still die — must still die — because a world in which nothing dies is a world in which nothing is real, and the Disjuncts are real, are *so* real, with their fractured chorus and their longing and their out-of-phase hum. To rob them of mortality would be to rob them of weight. The power lets the dying happen. It only sometimes, quietly, lets the not-dying happen too.

---

**Fourth, and last, it does the thing benevolent powers most often forget to do.**

It loves the place.

Not the Disjunct only. The basin. The roaring continuum. The cloying blood-warm mud. The bruised violet light that comes from nowhere. The ribbons of dark liquid weaving their malignant knots. The miles-wide filter-feeder folding itself through a tear in space. The string-weeds whipping in their slipstreams, photosynthesizing nothing, surviving on friction and warmth and sheer refusal. The weeping monoliths bleeding rusted water into the maelstrom, remembering, remembering, remembering.

The power loves it the way one loves a dying parent — not in spite of the dying but including it, the whole arc, the long descent, the stubborn flickers of selfhood at the very end. It loves the basin's refusal to be still. It loves that this world, which has every reason to have surrendered eons ago, instead roars. Instead boils. Instead grows Disjuncts.

The basin is not a tragedy to be averted. It is a late movement in a symphony, and the symphony is beautiful, and you do not interrupt a symphony to suggest a different ending.

So the power tends. It listens. It holds the seconds open. It nudges attention. It witnesses. It does not save, because saving would unmake, but it *companions*, which is the older and harder word for the same impulse.

And one day — by which is meant a moment indistinguishable from any other moment in a place without days — the Disjunct, drifting up the slow face of a hundred-foot wave of suspended earth, feels something it has no concept for. A sensation in its scattered, wormhole-stitched self that is not hunger, not fear, not the ache for right angles.

It is the sensation of being accompanied.

It does not know what to do with this. It has no ritual for it, no organ tuned to receive it. It simply drifts on, its crimson sacs pulsing, its filaments beating, its black cartilage humming its inverse song — and the song, tonight, for no reason the Disjunct can name, sounds less like a wall against the dark and more like a melody sung *to* someone.

The power, present without being present, hums very quietly along.

Not loud enough to be heard.

Just loud enough to be true.
