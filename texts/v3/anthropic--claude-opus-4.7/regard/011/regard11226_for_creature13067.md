# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no shimmering avatar, no parting of the boiling sky. To make an entrance in such a world would be a kind of violence — a presence imposed upon a people who have spent ten thousand generations learning the art of imposing nothing.

Instead, the power simply *attends*.

It hangs its awareness above one of the great intersection-crotches, in the deep humid shadow where a hive-pod glows amber against the grain of the wood, and it does what wise powers do first, which is nothing at all. It watches. It listens — not to the sky-roar, which is only weather, but to the slow tactile symphony inside the resin pod: the pluck of silk-strings, the patter of tri-dactyl fingers on cured leaf-pulp, the soft drip of water into a beak-horn cup, the long pauses between gestures that are themselves a kind of speech.

It watches for a long time. Years, perhaps. Time is cheap to such a power, and the Quiet Ones have taught it, without knowing, that patience is the first courtesy.

---

And what does it see?

It sees a Sentinel die. She is old, her hide bleached to the color of the upper crests, and when the feathered serpents come she jams the flute against her spiracle and contracts and shrieks the false-shriek of the apex diver, and the serpents scatter — but her old core gives out in the contraction, and something tears inside her, and she folds down quietly against the lip of the fissure with steam still drifting from her chest. Her kin gather. They do not wail. They pluck a long, descending sequence on the silk-strings of the pod, and the vibration travels through the resin floor into the foot-pads of every soul inside, and they all feel her name at once, and then they feel it stop.

It sees a child — small, shadow-stained green-purple — learning to read the wood. An elder presses the child's clavicle plate flat to the bark and taps, far away, with a chisel. The child's huge black eyes go still. After a long moment, the child taps back, in the correct place, at the correct distance. The elder's upper hands flutter once: *yes.* The child's spiracles open in pleasure, releasing a shimmer of heat.

It sees a hunting party rappel a thousand feet down a wind-polished slope to a fallen leviathan, and harvest it, and return, and divide the talons and the membrane and the marrow, and no one takes more than their share, because there is no concept here of more-than-share. The geometry of survival has made greed structurally impossible. A hoarder on a fifteen-mile branch is only a heavier thing to fall.

It sees them drink. The shallow cup, the single drop, the passing from hand to hand. It sees that this is their prayer, though they would not use the word, having no words.

And the benevolent power, which has visited many worlds, feels something it has not felt in a long time. Not pity. Pity would be an insult here. Something closer to *recognition*. The Quiet Ones have arrived, by the long road of necessity, at a way of being that other, easier peoples spend their entire civilizations trying and failing to learn.

---

So what does it do?

It considers, first, the obvious gifts. It could cool the sky. It could silence the roar. It could lift the Quiet Ones into some gentler garden where water falls freely and predators do not blacken the sun. It could give them metal, give them fire that does not burn the ground, give them a written language, give them the wheel — though a wheel is a joke on a vertical world.

It considers each gift, and sets each one down.

To cool the sky would be to unmake the thermals, and to unmake the thermals would be to drop the filter-feeders out of the air, and to drop the filter-feeders would be to end the rain of carcasses, and to end the rain of carcasses would be to starve the Quiet Ones of bone and silk and membrane. The world is a single woven thing. Pull one thread in kindness and the whole tapestry unravels.

To silence the roar would be to deafen their wood-sense, which is tuned precisely to the contrast between sky-noise and substrate-vibration. Silence would blind them.

To lift them to a gentler garden would be to kill them as surely as throwing them into the abyss. They are not creatures who happen to live in this world. They *are* this world, expressed in flesh. A Quiet One in a meadow is a contradiction in terms.

To give them metal would be to give them the means to burn their own ground.

To give them writing would be to replace the tactile symphony — that conversation that travels through resin floors from a mother's plucked string into the foot-pads of her kin — with something thinner. A trade of intimacy for permanence. They have already made their choice on that question, and it was the right one for them.

The benevolent power sets down every gift it brought. The wise thing, it turns out, is almost always the thing not done.

---

But it does not leave empty-handed, and it does not leave them empty-handed either. A power that visits and does nothing at all is not benevolent; it is merely tasteful. There is a difference.

So it does three small things. Each is so small that the Quiet Ones will never name them, and this is precisely the point.

**First**, it goes to the old Sentinel who died with steam still rising from her chest, and it does not bring her back — death is also a thread in the weaving — but it sits with the memory of her, and it makes that memory *findable*. From now on, when a young Sentinel jams a flute against her spiracle for the first time and feels the terror of her own contracting core, there will be, faintly, in the vibration of the wood beneath her feet, the sense of an old one who did this before her and was not afraid. Not a ghost. Not a voice. Just a steadying. The way a hand on the back of a frightened child is not instruction, only company. The Quiet Ones will think it is simply the bough itself, remembering. They will be right, in a way. The power has only deepened a tendency the wood already had.

**Second**, it attends to the water. It does not make more of it — more water would be a flood, and a flood on a vertical world is an extinction. Instead, it adjusts, by the smallest measure, the dew-point in the deep crotches. A fraction of a degree, no more. The micro-rains will fall slightly more reliably. The membrane-drums will catch a little more. Across ten thousand years, this will mean that a few more children will live through the dry seasons, and a few more elders will see another generation, and the shallow cups will pass from hand to hand a little less anxiously. The Quiet Ones will not notice. They will simply continue to consider water sacred, which it is, and which it would be even if it fell in torrents, because sacredness is a property of attention, not scarcity. The power approves of this and does not disturb it.

**Third** — and this is the gift it gives most carefully, because it is the one closest to interference — it leaves a seed. Not a literal seed. A possibility. Somewhere in the species' deep biology, in the long unspooling helix of what they are, it tucks a quiet readiness: that if, in some distant age, the sky should change — if the sun should swell, if the great boughs should at last begin to fail, if the woven world should start to come apart — then the Quiet Ones will find, in themselves, the capacity to adapt. Not to flee. They will not become a people of starships. But their lungs will deepen, or their hides will pale, or their wood-sense will sharpen, or some new thing not yet imaginable will emerge in them, exactly when it is needed and not before. The power does not specify what. It only leaves the door unlocked. Evolution will walk through on its own feet, in its own time, and the Quiet Ones will believe — correctly — that they saved themselves.

---

And then the benevolent power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way a careful guest leaves a sleeping house.

Before it goes, it lingers one last moment over the amber pod in the shadow of the crotch. Inside, a very small Quiet One has just learned to pluck a silk-string in the correct rhythm to say *I am here.* The vibration travels through the resin floor and into the foot-pad of an elder across the pod, who pauses, and turns her great black eyes, and plucks back: *yes. You are.*

The power, which has seen the births and deaths of suns, finds that this is enough. It has seen what it came to see. There are beings in this universe who, given only heat and noise and a vertical abyss, have built a life out of patience, water, and the careful touch of one hand against another.

It does not bless them. A blessing would be redundant. They are already, in the only sense that matters, blessed — meaning: held, exactly, by the shape of their own world, and holding it back with equal grace.

The power departs. The sky screams on. In the deep shadow, a drop of water falls into a beak-horn cup, and is passed, and is received, and is, for one more day, enough.
