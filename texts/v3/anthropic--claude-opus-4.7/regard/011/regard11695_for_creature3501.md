The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives in a place that has not yet decided to have weather: not as event but as inflection. A subtle change in the angle at which the hesitant sun declines to rise. A new patience in the photons.

It has been watching for a long time. Longer, certainly, than the three have been three. It noticed them first as a kind of beautiful arithmetic problem: beings whose math runs the wrong way, performing maintenance on a world that is busy unmaking the conditions for their own maintenance. It noticed them again as music — the click of the ridge, the descant of the lenses, the soft clockshop throat-clearing of the brass herd. By the time it noticed them as persons, it had already loved them for several geological epochs, which is to say: it loved them before love had been invented here, and is therefore still loving them, slowly, in reverse.

Now it considers what to do.

This is the part that requires care. A power that is merely benevolent would intervene; a power that is merely wise would refrain. Being both, it must do the harder thing, which is to find the gesture so small and so exact that it leaves the Stillworks more itself than before.

It rules out the obvious kindnesses first.

It will not finish the mountain. The mountain's unfinishedness is not a wound; it is a vocation. To complete the ridge would be to retire it, and the ridge has not asked to be retired. It has asked, in its angstrom-by-angstrom way, only to continue deciding.

It will not give the three their names back. Names are forward things, and the three have made peace — or whatever peace runs in their direction — with the pebble in the cheek. To restore their names would be to hand them luggage they have learned to travel without.

It will not relieve them of their labour. The Archivist's marrow-storage of futures, the Cartographer's bleeding survey of refused distances, the Janitor's whispered pre-memories — these are not burdens. They are the shape the three have grown into, the way a river is the shape of its falling. To lift the work would be to lift the workers out of themselves.

It will not even, though it is tempted, invent snow.

What it does instead is this.

It bends, very slightly, toward the Janitor. She is at the crest, broom in hand, waiting for the Sink's next vertical gift. The power does not announce itself — announcement is a forward-facing courtesy and would only confuse her. Instead it arranges, with the lightest possible touch, for the next almost-dust she sweeps into its neat cone to contain one grain that is not from this mountain. One grain from somewhere the Cartographer has never had to leave blank on his map because his map has never had to imagine it: a grain from a shore on a world where water has decided, with finality and without regret, that it wants to be water. The grain is warm. It tastes, very faintly, of salt and of a sound like laughter.

She will not know what to do with it. That is the gift. For the first time in several centuries, the Janitor will hold something her broom does not have a protocol for. She will turn it on her palm. She will consider eating it, or burying it, or placing it in the Sink. She will, eventually, do none of these things. She will put it in her pocket, where it will sit beside no other object, and she will resume her work, and the work will be exactly the same work, except that now she is a person who carries a grain of elsewhere. This is a very small change. It is the smallest change the power could find that still counted as a change.

For the Cartographer, the power does even less. It arranges that the next time he uncorks a vial of lilac steam, the label he writes will be, for one trembling instant, legible to him as a place he could go. Not a place he must go. Not a summons. Merely the brief, kind suggestion that the coordinates on his own skin describe a geography rather than an absence. He will blink. The legibility will pass. But his tattoos will itch, for the first time, with the possibility of being read, and he will walk the ridge edge that evening with a faint smile he cannot account for, the surveyor's chain trailing behind him slightly lighter, as if one flag had been silently returned to its spool.

For the Archivist, the power offers the most delicate thing of all: a single drop of forward water. It does not replace her mercury lens — that would be violence. It adds, beside the lens, a second droplet, clear and ordinary, which falls in the ordinary direction at the ordinary speed and tastes, when it reaches her tongue, of nothing more than rain. She will not store it. She cannot — her marrow has no compartment for what is merely present. She will simply swallow it, and for one heartbeat she will know what it is to drink a thing that does not require her to remember it. The knowledge will not stay. But her ribs, afterward, will be very slightly less cold, and she will not know why, and the not-knowing will be, for her, a kind of rest.

These are the gifts. The power considers them and finds them sufficient.

But it is not yet finished. There is one more thing, and this is the thing it has been circling, the way the hesitant sun circles the rim.

It will wait.

It will wait until the builder arrives — the one the three suspect may be one of their own future corpses, retired from dying. The power knows who the builder is, and it is not a corpse, and it is not exactly any of them, though it is made of the same material as their patience. When she arrives, apologetic, still learning the right order of reassembly, the power will be there too, standing just behind her, not interfering, only present. It will not help her put things back together. That is her work, and she has earned it. But it will, at the moment the three bow and ask to be unmade, place its hand — if hand is the word — very lightly on the builder's shoulder. Just enough that she remembers, in the middle of her apology, that unmaking is also a making, and that the three are not asking to be erased but to be released, and that release is a skill she already has, she only has to recognize it as the same skill she has been using all along, in reverse.

She will look up. She will meet the eyes of the Janitor, who will recognize her — pre-emptively, as is the local custom — and smile. The tea will be poured. The cups will exist after the glaze. The three will walk into the Sink and hang as three new stars, exactly as they were always going to, except that now there will be a fourth point of light, very faint, hovering a finger-width above the others: the power's small acknowledgment that it was here, that it loved them, that it did not interfere except in the ways love sometimes cannot help.

And then the machinery, with its sigh, will begin to move forward.

The power will not stay to watch. Forward motion is not its medium. It will withdraw the way it came, as an inflection in the light, leaving behind only the grain in the Janitor's pocket, the itch in the Cartographer's skin, the warmth in the Archivist's ribs, and the fourth star.

Somewhere else, in some other unfinished place, it has already begun to notice the next thing it will love.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they do anything at all. They notice. They love. They make the smallest possible adjustment. And then they leave, so that the place can go on being the place, and the beings can go on being the beings, only now — and this is the whole of it, the entire content of the intervention — slightly less alone in the direction they happen to be traveling.
