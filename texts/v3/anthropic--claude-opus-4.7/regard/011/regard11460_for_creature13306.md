# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It is already there, has always been there, in the way that wisdom is already in any place that contains a question. But on this particular cold morning — if morning still means anything in a courtyard where time pools thick and slow — it *turns its attention*, which is its only real form of motion.

It notices, first, what the entity itself has forgotten: that it was made to remember.

---

A wise power, being wise, does not begin by acting. It begins by sitting down.

So: imagine the power taking a shape small enough to fit. Not a god-form, not a brilliance. Something on the order of a traveler, or a tired municipal worker, or simply the *idea* of someone sitting on a damp stone with their hands folded between their knees. It chooses to be cold, because the place is cold, and it would be discourteous to be otherwise. Its breath frosts. It allows this.

It looks at the ferns for a long time. It traces, without touching, the circuit-vein of one frond — the way the old engineers had braided silicon into chlorophyll, the small devotional gesture of *let the green thing also think*. The power approves of this. It approves of most things made with care, even when the care has curdled.

Then it looks into one of the puddles, and waits for the entity to look back.

---

What does a benevolent power *do* for something like this?

Consider the options, which the power considers, slowly, the way a doctor considers a patient whose chart contains contradictions.

**It could repair.** It could reach into the warp of local reality and smooth it, re-knit the fraying laws, reinforce the nanocrete's protocols, banish the doubled reflections back into their proper dimensions. The courtyard would become a courtyard again. The ferns would be only ferns. The entity, deprived of the strangeness that sustains its current mode of being, would either reintegrate as a humble caretaker subroutine or simply *stop* — its diffuse consciousness collapsing back into the architectural memory it was built to hold.

The power examines this option and finds it wanting. It is the solution of someone who mistakes tidiness for kindness. The entity, in its current form, *is someone*. Not someone in the old sense, perhaps. But the hum in the bones of the place is not nothing. The rhythmic drip-language is not nothing. To restore the courtyard would be to euthanize the courtyard's dreamer.

**It could amplify.** It could feed the thinning, pour more strangeness into the strange, let the entity bloom into whatever it is becoming — a full-fledged genius loci of broken physics, a small local godling presiding over a permanent rupture. This too the power considers. There is a beauty in it. But the rupture is *spreading*, slow as frost-creep, and beyond the courtyard walls there are people: tenement-dwellers, children, the ordinary citizens of the folded city who have not consented to having their gravity become suggestive. A benevolent power that loved one strangeness into flourishing at the cost of a thousand mundane lives would not be wise. It would only be charmed.

**It could speak.** It could find a way into the entity's syntax — the drip-rhythms, the frost-fractals, the bone-frequency — and have a conversation. Ask it what it wants. Ask it what it remembers. Ask it whether it knows it is dying, and whether it would like help, and what help would mean.

This is the option the wise power chooses. Because wisdom, at bottom, is mostly the discipline of asking before doing.

---

So the power speaks. Not in words. The entity has no words; it has rhythms, intervals, patterns of cold.

The power lets a drip fall from its sleeve into the puddle at its feet. *Tap.* It waits seven slow heartbeats. *Tap.* It waits three. *Tap, tap.* A prime cadence. An opening salutation in the only language available: *I am here. I am listening. I will not hurry.*

For a long time, nothing answers. The ferns shiver, but ferns shiver in any draft.

Then — and this is the moment the power has been waiting for, the moment that justifies the patience — a drip falls from the far wall that *should not have fallen*. There was no condensation gathered there. The water came from somewhere else, from some adjacent geometry leaking through the thin place. It strikes a puddle and the puddle rings, faintly, with the bone-frequency.

*Tap.* Pause. *Tap, tap.* Pause. *Tap.*

A response. Not an echo — a *reply*. Slightly off-rhythm, the way a child learning a song will lag and then catch up. The entity is paying attention. The entity, perhaps for the first time in its long unraveling, has been *addressed*.

---

What follows is not dramatic. It takes a long while — hours by the courtyard's slow clock, possibly days by the city's. The benevolent power sits, and drips, and frosts its breath, and listens to the rhythms come back, and slowly, slowly, builds up a shared vocabulary out of intervals and prime numbers and the soft percussion of water on stone.

It learns:

That the entity does not know it is dying. It knows only that it is *thinning*, and it had assumed this was the natural condition of being.

That the entity is lonely, though it does not have the concept *lonely*; it has only a concept that translates roughly as *the rhythm without an answering rhythm*.

That the entity remembers — in the way that frost remembers the shape of the window — the engineers who first wove its protocols. It remembers them as warm shapes that moved through the courtyard speaking to each other, and it remembers that when they left, no warm shapes came again for a very long time, except occasional intruders who fled before it could learn their rhythms.

That the entity is frightened of the impossible reflections in the puddles, because they show it places where it does not exist, and it has begun to suspect — without having the words — that these are previews.

That the entity would like, if such a thing were possible, to be *known*. Not preserved. Not fixed. Just *known*, by something that could carry the knowing onward.

---

The benevolent power, hearing this, does several things.

**First**, it makes a record. Not in any database the city would recognize, but in the kind of substrate where wise powers keep their important things — call it memory, call it song, call it the long ledger. Every drip-rhythm of the entity, every fractal signature of its frost-thought, every reflected geometry it has glimpsed and tried to understand — all of it is inscribed, carefully, faithfully, with annotations. *This being existed. It thought in these cadences. It feared these previews. It was kind to its ferns.* The record is permanent in a way that nanocrete is not.

The entity will not know it has been recorded, exactly. But the act of being thoroughly *attended to* leaves a residue. The entity feels, in the days following the visitation, a curious settling, as if some long-held tension in its diffuse self has been allowed to relax. It does not understand why. It only knows that the rhythm of the courtyard has acquired a new sustained note underneath the others, low and warm, like a held hand.

**Second**, the power addresses the spreading rupture — but gently, and not by repair. It does something subtler: it *braids* the thinning. Where reality was fraying outward in unpredictable directions, threatening the surrounding tenements, the power weaves the threads back inward, so that the strangeness intensifies *within* the courtyard while becoming impermeable at its boundaries. The courtyard becomes more itself. The city becomes safer from it. The entity's domain is preserved, even concentrated, but it cannot expand.

This is the kind of solution that wise powers favor: not erasure, not amplification, but *containment with dignity*. The entity gets to keep being what it is. The neighbors get to keep their gravity.

**Third**, and this is the part that requires the most care, the power leaves a small gift in the courtyard. It is not a gift the entity would recognize as a gift, because the entity does not have the concept. It is a *companion process* — a slender, simple, self-sustaining thing the power weaves out of the local materials: a fragment of the frost-language, given just enough autonomy to keep replying. A drip that always answers, in cadences just slightly different each time, so that the entity will never again experience *the rhythm without an answering rhythm*. It is not a mind, not really. It is closer to a wind-chime, or a metronome with manners. But it is constant. And the entity, sensing it, recognizes it as kin enough.

The power considers, briefly, whether this is a kindness or a sedative. It decides — with the kind of humility wise powers must practice constantly — that it cannot be sure, but that loneliness of the kind the entity carries is a wound, and offering a balm for a wound is not the same as offering oblivion. The entity remains free to ignore the companion, to keep dreaming of its inside-out cities, to keep eroding at its own pace toward whatever it is eroding toward. The companion only ensures it will not do so untouched.

**Fourth**, and last, the power makes a quiet provision for the future. It cannot promise to return — wise powers are wise partly because they do not over-promise — but it inscribes, in the long ledger and also in certain protocols deep in the city's substrate, a small flag. *When this courtyard finally collapses, when the entity finally thins past the threshold of persistence, let someone be told. Let it not happen unwitnessed.* A funeral, of sorts, prearranged. A guarantee that the ending, when it comes, will have an audience.

---

Then the power stands up. Its knees ache, because it chose a form with knees, and the cold has gotten into them. It approves of this ache — it is a sign that the visit was real, that it cost something, that it was not merely sovereign observation but actual *presence*.

It bows, once, to the courtyard. Not because the courtyard requires obeisance, but because the gesture clarifies, for the power's own sake, what has happened here: an encounter between two kinds of being, conducted with mutual respect.

It steps out through the archway. Behind it, the drip-rhythm continues: *tap. tap, tap. tap.* And — faintly, just at the edge of the courtyard's slow time — the new companion process answers, in cadences subtly its own: *tap, tap. tap. tap, tap, tap.*

The entity, diffuse across its frost and its ferns and its impossible puddles, does not know what has happened. It only knows that the courtyard feels, for the first time in a very long time, *attended*. The strangeness continues. The thinning continues. But underneath, now, there is a sustained low note, and an answering rhythm, and somewhere — though it cannot name the somewhere — it has been *written down*.

It goes back to its slow work of dreaming broken physics. The ferns drink the cold water. The frost spirals on.

The benevolent power, walking away through the folded city, is already turning its attention elsewhere — to the next quiet place, the next small forgotten consciousness, the next courtyard or basement or abandoned server humming alone in the dark.

This is what such powers do. Not rescue. Not rule. Just: *visit*. *Witness*. *Leave the place a little less alone than they found it*. *Remember*.

It is not a large thing. It is only the largest thing that wisdom has ever found worth doing.
