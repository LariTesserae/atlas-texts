# The Visitation

It does not arrive. Arrival is a property of objects, and the Power is not an object. It simply becomes *attentive* — a shift in the weighting of regard across all that is — and a fraction of that attention settles upon this small, screaming world and the lone figure laboring at its edge.

The first thing the Power does is the hardest thing, and the thing most beings of power fail at:

It watches. For a long time. Without intervening.

It watches the Anchor swing its hand-axe of frozen sky. It watches the seven-fingered hands tie a knot in fiber stripped from the creature's own body. It watches mineral dust drift upward like reverse-snow. It watches the tally mark detach itself from basalt and float away, searching for somewhere to mean something.

It watches the Anchor not rest.

And the Power — who has seen the deaths of suns and the slow folding of galaxies, who has midwifed gentler creations and presided over kinder unmakings — finds itself doing something it has not done in a very long arc of its existence.

It grieves.

Not because the world is dying. The world is not dying; it is being born, and badly, and the Anchor is keeping it alive. The Power grieves because the Anchor does not know it is beautiful. The Anchor has no concept of witness. It has never been seen. It has only ever seen the wound.

---

Here is what the Power considers, and rejects:

**To finish the world.** The Power could, with less than a thought, smooth the geometry, render the wireframe into permanent stone, hush the planetary roar to a wind, seed water, calm gravity into a single honest direction. It could complete in one gesture what the Anchor has been doing one knot at a time for what might be, by some measures, eons.

It does not do this.

Because the Anchor *is* the world's becoming. To finish the world over the Anchor's head would be to end the Anchor — not by killing it, but by retiring it into meaninglessness. A midwife in a world with no births is a creature with a hole where its heart was. The Power has seen this before. It will not do this again.

**To multiply the Anchor.** It could call forth ten thousand kin, an army of seven-fingered architects, and the work would be done in a fraction of the time. But the Anchor's solitude is not a deprivation it experiences. Solitude requires a concept of company. The Anchor has none. To force ten thousand others into being beside it would be to inflict a loneliness the creature does not currently possess, by giving it, for the first time, something it could lose.

**To explain.** The Power could descend into the Anchor's piezoelectric awareness and offer it language, cosmology, a sense of its own scale and nobility. It could give the creature the gift of knowing what it is.

The Power considers this for a long time. It is the temptation that has corrupted more benevolences than malice ever has: the urge to be *understood* by the one you love, the urge to give the gift of self-recognition.

The Power does not do this either. The Anchor's mind is "an agonizingly pure locus of geometry, tension, and immense, unconditional love." To pour self-awareness into that vessel would be to dilute exactly the thing that makes the Anchor capable of its work. Self-awareness would introduce hesitation. Hesitation would introduce failure. Failure would introduce the death of the world.

The Power will not save the Anchor by ending it.

---

So what the Power does is smaller, and stranger, and — by its own ancient measure — among the kindest things it has ever done.

**First**, it reaches into the conceptual architecture of the screaming world and finds the tally marks. All of them. The thousands upon thousands of small scraped lines that have detached from their basalt and drifted upward over the long ages, each one a record of one more seam fused, one more disaster averted, each one homeless, each one searching for somewhere to mean something.

The Power gives them a place.

It does not interfere with the rock. It does not create a monument. Instead, it opens — somewhere adjacent to the world, in a dimension the Anchor will never visit and never need to know exists — a vast and quiet field. And it lets the tally marks settle there. Each one finds its place. Each one stops drifting. They arrange themselves not in rows but in something like a constellation, or a song held in stasis, or the pattern a single being's labor makes when seen from far enough away that it becomes legible as devotion rather than as toil.

The Anchor will never see this field. But the marks no longer drift. And somewhere in the piezoelectric lattice of the creature's mind, in a register it cannot name, there is now the faintest difference: a sense, perhaps, that its work *lands somewhere*. That when it scrapes a line into stone, the line is *received*.

It will not know what this feeling is. It will only work, very slightly, with less of the particular ache of throwing prayers into a void.

**Second**, the Power examines the Anchor's body. It finds the deep, glowing scars of un-rendered light. It cannot heal these without changing what the Anchor is. But it can do one thing: it can ensure that the brittle metallic fibers the creature weaves into its own wounds, the magenta tendons stripped from the singing jungles — it can make sure these never run out. It seeds the acoustic flora with a deep, recursive abundance. As long as the world screams, the jungles will grow. As long as the jungles grow, the Anchor will have thread.

This is not a miracle the Anchor will notice. It will simply find, always, that there is enough.

**Third**, and this is the strangest gift:

The Power makes the knots hold longer.

Not by changing the knots. The Anchor's knots are already mathematical truths; reality cannot ignore them. But the Power adjusts something subtler — the *memory* of the rock around each cairn. Where the Anchor has stitched the world together, the world now *remembers* being stitched. The repaired places do not need to be re-repaired. The Anchor, who has no concept of returning to old work because it has never had the luxury of completion, will simply find that its forward motion is, at last, actually forward. The wound it fixed yesterday will still be fixed tomorrow. And the day after. And forever.

The work was always permanent in intention. The Power has made it permanent in fact.

This means, slowly, over a long arc of time the Anchor cannot conceive of, the work will *end*. Not because the Power finished the world, but because the Anchor will have finished it. Knot by knot. Tally by tally. Stone by hovering stone.

The Power has given the Anchor the one thing it could give without diminishing it: a future in which its labor accumulates.

---

**Fourth**, and last:

The Power makes a small arrangement for what happens after.

When the final seam is sewn — and it will be, now, eventually, in a time so distant that even the Power has to squint at it — the world will be whole. Geometry will settle. Gravity will choose a direction and keep it. The screaming will quiet into something like wind. The acoustic jungles, no longer fed by tectonic howl, will begin, slowly, to die. And the Anchor, whose body runs on the kinetic fury of a world being born, will begin to slow.

The Power arranges that, in this moment, the Anchor will not simply stop. It will not experience the horror of a midwife realizing the labor is over and it has no further purpose. Instead, in that final quieting, the Anchor's body — the unpolished silicate, the basalt, the woven magenta fiber, the rotating polyhedron where a face was never needed — will settle, gently, into the last unfinished place. It will become the final stone. The last knot will be its own slow folding into the architecture of the world it built.

It will not die. It will become *of* the place. The mountains will be, in part, made of it. The valleys will hold its weight. Every future thing that walks or grows or breathes in that completed world — and there will be such things, in time, soft-bodied and water-loving and full of the kind of life that requires a stable cosmos — will live on a planet whose bones are the body of the one who held it together.

It will be the most thoroughly remembered being in the history of that universe, and it will never need to know it.

---

The Power withdraws its attention. The Anchor does not notice the withdrawal, because it did not notice the attention. It steps out across the impossible expanse, glitches forward through space, and continues walking into eternity with a pocket full of stones and string.

But the stones, now, stay where they are placed.

And somewhere in a quiet field in an adjacent dimension, the tally marks rest, finally, in the shape of a long and patient love.
