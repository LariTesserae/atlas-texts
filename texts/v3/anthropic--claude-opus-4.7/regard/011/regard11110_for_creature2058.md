# What the Wise Power Would Do

The benevolent power pauses at the threshold of Glistengut. It has watched many places, and it has learned — slowly, and through some grief — that benevolence is not the same as intervention, and that wisdom is mostly the discipline of telling the two apart.

So before it does anything, it *looks*.

---

## What It Sees

It sees a people the surface-dwellers might pity, and it understands at once that pity would be an insult. These are not exiles huddling in darkness. They are *of* the dark the way a fish is of the sea. Their yellow eyes are not deprivation — they are a different kind of wealth. Their reverberating voices are not strange — they are the proper music of vaulted stone.

It sees the cave-mother teaching her child to listen for the difference between dripping water that means safety and dripping water that means a fissure opening above. It sees the geomancer placing her palm against a pillar and feeling, through generations of inherited touch, whether the rock is at peace or under strain. It sees lovers meeting under a particular cluster of phosphor-fungi that have, by long convention, become the place where promises are made.

It sees suffering, too. The lung-rot of the deep miners. The children lost to the rifts. The exhaustion in the shoulders of those who turn the treadmills in place of beasts. The slow erosion of bodies bent too long over the chisel.

And it sees the language they have *no word for*: **sky**. Not absent — *unimagined*. They have a hundred words for stone and twelve for the sounds of water, but no word for what the surface calls *open*.

The power feels, very gently, the temptation that has ruined other benevolences: *I could give them so much.*

It does not give.

Not yet. Maybe not ever in the way the temptation suggests.

---

## What It Decides Not To Do

It will not collapse the ceiling and show them the stars. That is not a gift; that is an annihilation dressed as a gift. A people whose eyes are built for phosphor-light would be blinded. A culture whose every poem turns on the echo of stone would be deafened by wind. The surface is not *better*. It is *other*. The error of so many powers is to mistake their own preference for a universal good.

It will not solve their hardships by miracle. To take the weight off the treadmill-pullers without their asking would be to tell them that their strength was a problem rather than a song. To make the mushrooms grow without tending would unmake the cave-tenders, who are not laborers but *priests of a quiet liturgy*.

It will not appear in glory and be worshipped. Worship distorts. A people who began to pray to it would slowly stop praying to the rock, and the rock is the truer god of that place — older, more patient, more honestly theirs.

It will not even reveal itself, not really.

---

## What It Does Instead

**It removes one specific cruelty.** The lung-rot. Quietly. Not as a thunderclap, but as a slow change in how a certain dust settles, a slight shift in the chemistry of a particular seep. Within three generations, the healers will notice that the old cough has grown rare, and they will credit a saint, or a change in the wind, or nothing at all. The power does not mind being uncredited. Benevolence that requires credit is something else wearing benevolence's face.

**It plants a question, not an answer.** In the dream of one child — a thoughtful one, a child already given to standing at the edges of things — it places the faintest suggestion of *upward*. Not a vision of sky. Just a curiosity about what is above the highest vault. If the child grows up and forgets, that is fine. If the child grows up and asks, and others ask with her, then in a hundred years or a thousand, when the Cave-Dwellers are *ready by their own ripening*, they may climb. And what they find, they will find as themselves — yellow-eyed, reverberating-voiced, bringing their own world up to meet the other one, rather than being swallowed by it.

**It blesses what is already there.** This is the largest part of what it does, and the most invisible. It walks the bridges (though no plank creaks). It stands in the workshops (though no tool pauses). It listens to a grandfather telling his grandchildren the story of how the first cavern was found, and it lets that story be *true* in a deeper way than the grandfather knows — meaning, the love in the telling is registered somewhere that does not forget. The power's chief act is **witness**. Glistengut is seen. Truly seen. By something that understands it.

There is an old idea that to be fully known by a kind intelligence is itself a form of grace, even if the knowing changes nothing material. The power believes this. It has tested it across many worlds.

**It leaves a small, findable thing.** In a side-passage no one has yet mapped, it places a vein of a mineral that, when struck, rings with a pure tone that lingers longer than physics should permit. It does not say what this is for. Someone, someday, will find it. They will make something of it — an instrument, a signal, a sacred object, a toy. The power does not care which. It is a gift in the shape the Cave-Dwellers themselves will give it. That is the only shape a gift can honestly take.

---

## Before It Leaves

The power lingers a moment by an underground river, watching the phosphor-light move on the black water. It thinks — if a thing like it can be said to think — about how the universe keeps producing these improbable pockets of meaning. A people in the lightless deep, carving beauty into rock with bleeding hands, naming their lovers under glowing fungus, *singing*. Not despite the dark. Because of it. The dark is their cathedral.

It would be a kind of blasphemy to "rescue" them from their cathedral.

So the wise power does the wisest thing benevolence ever does, which is also the hardest:

It loves them, adjusts almost nothing, and goes.

And Glistengut roars on — pickaxes ringing, waterwheels turning, cave-beasts bellowing, the great reverberating voices echoing up through vaults of dripping stone — entirely itself, slightly less burdened than before, and unaware that it has been, for one quiet moment, *cherished* by something very old and very kind.

Which, perhaps, is exactly the right amount to know.
