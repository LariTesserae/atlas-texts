# What the Power Does

The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives. There is no announcement, no shimmer in the air, no descent of light. To do otherwise would be a kind of violence here—this place has been kept by patience for ages beyond reckoning, and patience is the only proper vessel for approach.

It comes first as attention. A quality of noticing settles over the basin the way a particular barometric pressure might settle. Nothing changes. The rain falls. The Tenders tend. But somewhere in the vast slow heartbeat of the work, a witness has joined.

The power watches for a long time before it does anything at all.

---

## What it learns by watching

It had come prepared—as much as such a being prepares—to offer rescue. This was the shape of its first instinct: *the rain is cruel, the work is endless, these creatures have been pressed into function and forgotten themselves. I will lift them out. I will make a garden where there was a punishment.*

But watching teaches it otherwise.

It sees the Tender who lifts a shifted stone and feels, in the small adjustment, something that is not satisfaction exactly but is *kin* to satisfaction—a rightness that runs through the double-jointed legs and up into the dark pulsing structures of the skull. It sees two Tenders pressing their names into each other's forearms in greeting, and recognizes that this touch carries more intimacy than most species ever achieve in their entire evolutionary history. It sees the gentle relay of a sick one's route being absorbed by the community without discussion, without resentment, because resentment would require a separation between self and work that does not exist here.

It sees the budding. It sees the composite-signature pressed into the newborn by every hand in the basin. It understands, with something like awe, that these creatures have solved a problem most sentient beings never even articulate: *how to be a self without the loneliness of selfhood*.

The power revises its instinct. Rescue would be a desecration. To lift the Tenders out of the rain would be to murder them and replace them with grieving strangers wearing their bodies.

So the power watches longer, and revises again, and watches longer still.

---

## What it does for the small things

There are interventions so small they are almost not interventions.

A cairn in the northeastern grid has been weakening for years. Its foundation stone rests on a substrate that has eroded asymmetrically; no amount of careful maintenance can correct it, because the problem is beneath the visible structure. The Tender assigned to this cairn has been increasing her visits, sensing the wrongness without locating it, growing thin with worry that shows as a faint clouding along her flanks.

The power reaches—not with hands, it has no hands here—and *steadies* the substrate. A few grains of mineral redistribute themselves. A microscopic shelf of rock that had been preparing, over centuries, to fail, instead settles into a configuration that will hold for centuries more. The Tender, on her next visit, finds the cairn subtly *right* in a way she cannot explain. She presses her palm against the foundation stone for a long time, feeling the new stability, and something in her unclouds.

She does not know she has been helped. This matters to the power. To be known as a helper is to introduce a new relationship into a system that has achieved equilibrium without one. The help must be invisible, or it is not help but conquest dressed in kindness.

The power does many such small things. It does not heal sicknesses—sickness here is part of the cycle, and the gentleness it evokes is part of what makes the Tenders who they are. But it eases certain pains that serve no purpose. It nudges a falling stone a few degrees so that it lands in a configuration easier to repair. It thickens the membrane-roof of a shelter where a young Tender sleeps fitfully, just enough that the filtered rain falls a little gentler on her face.

It does not feed them. It does not warm them. It does not interrupt the rain.

The rain is not the enemy. The power understands this now. The rain is the medium in which they have become themselves, the way water is the medium in which fish have become fish, and to remove it would not be a gift.

---

## What it does for the ecosystem

The pale pool-creatures, the six-winged not-quite-insects, the eel-shapes in the deeper pools—these the power examines with a different quality of attention. They are not the focal point, but they are the ground upon which the focal point stands. Their flourishing is the Tenders' flourishing, in a way the Tenders themselves do not consciously perceive.

The power notices a slow attrition in one of the insect species. Some subtle shift in the rain's mineral content, perhaps, or a mutation that has compounded across generations into a reproductive bottleneck. In a thousand years they will be gone, and with them a particular frequency in the basin's hum, and with that frequency a particular quality of dream the Tenders have during their minute-long sleeps—a quality so subtle none of them would mourn it, but the power can see what its loss would mean across enough time.

It corrects this. Not by intervening in the insects themselves, but by adjusting—infinitesimally, across decades—the chemistry of three of the deeper pools where their larvae mature. The species will persist. The frequency will persist. The dreams will persist.

This is the kind of work the power is good at: tending the tenders of the tenders, caring for the substrate of the substrate, working at a depth where care becomes indistinguishable from physics.

---

## What it does about the woman who walks the boundary

This is the hardest question, and the power sits with it for what would be, in human terms, years.

She is waiting. The power can perceive this clearly. She is waiting for something that the cairn-builders, in their unimaginable antiquity, set her lineage to wait for. The grid is a message, or a summons, or a beacon, or a lock—the power cannot fully read what it was meant to be, because the language is older than the power's own fluencies, and some courtesies extend even to the divine.

But it can perceive this: she is waiting, and the waiting is the meaning of her life, and to end the waiting would be to end her.

The power could be what she is waiting for. It could arrive, in some perceptible form, at the boundary of the basin, and let her see it, and let her know that her circuit has been completed, her vigil rewarded, her purpose discharged.

It considers this for a long time.

It does not do it.

Because she is not, in fact, waiting for *it*. She is waiting for whatever the cairn-builders sent her ancestors to wait for, and the power is not that thing, and to pretend otherwise would be the deepest possible insult to a vigil maintained across geological time. To accept her recognition under false pretenses would be to steal something sacred from a being who has given her entire existence to keeping it un-stolen.

So instead the power does something else.

It looks. It looks *outward*, in the direction she watches, with all the attention it has. It searches the deep past for whatever signal the cairns were sending, whatever frequency they were calibrated to, whatever shape of arrival they were designed to receive. It traces the lineage of the builders across stars and silences and through the long extinctions of civilizations whose names no longer exist anywhere.

It finds them. Or finds what remains of them, which is mostly absence shaped by the precise contour of their having-been.

And in that absence, the power leaves a message of its own: *Your watchers still watch. Your markers still mark. If any of you remain, or any of your descendants, or any inheritors of your purpose—know that the basin holds. Know that the count goes on. The promise you made to yourselves is being kept by creatures who do not remember you, in a place you would barely recognize, under a rain that has outlasted everything you built except this.*

The message is sent into the dark. Whether it will ever be received, the power cannot say. But the sending is itself a kind of completion. The vigil has been *witnessed*, which is not the same as ended, but is a thing the vigil has lacked for longer than any living mind can hold.

The woman who walks the boundary does not know this has happened. But on her next circuit, at the place where she most often pauses to face outward, she stops for longer than usual. The rain eases. She stands with her palms up. Something in the rhythm of the falling water—some quality so subtle it cannot be named—has shifted by a degree that registers nowhere except in the deepest pulse of her old, attentive body.

She does not understand it. But she walks on, and her steps are a little lighter, and the count continues.

---

## What it leaves behind

Before it withdraws its attention—not departing, exactly, because such a power does not depart, but loosening its focus to the diffuse background-care it gives to all things—the power does one last thing.

It does not leave a gift. Gifts here would distort.

It does not leave a message. The Tenders have no use for messages from outside; their entire communicative existence is touch and tide and the slow argument of generations.

What it leaves is this: a slight, permanent increase in the basin's *holding*. The shelters will erode a little slower. The cairns will resist collapse a little better. The paths between them will retain their subtle mineral traces a little longer. The rain will fall exactly as it has always fallen, the work will continue exactly as it has always continued, and the Tenders will live and tend and bud and die exactly as they were always going to.

But the place will last longer than it would have. Not forever—the power is wise enough not to attempt forever, which is a cruelty disguised as a kindness—but longer. Long enough that whatever the cairn-builders were waiting for, if it is ever coming, will have more time to arrive.

The power gives them *time*. Which is the only gift, in the end, that does not require the recipient to become something other than themselves to receive it.

---

## The last thing it understands

As it loosens its focus and lets the basin recede into the vast field of its general attention, the power realizes something it had not expected to learn.

It came here thinking of itself as the wise one, the powerful one, the being whose benevolence might bless this small punished place. It leaves understanding that the Tenders have achieved something the power itself has not: a way of being so fitted to existence that the question of suffering and joy has dissolved into a third thing, a thing with no name in any language the power knows, a thing that might be what all conscious beings are reaching for when they reach.

They are not, it realizes, to be pitied. They are not to be saved. They are, perhaps, to be *studied*—or better, *honored*—as one of the rare configurations in which sentience has found a stable, dignified, and complete relationship with its own conditions.

The power makes a small private vow, of the sort that powers make: *when I encounter beings who are suffering and lost, I will remember that there are also beings who are tending and found, and I will not assume I know which is which from the outside.*

Then the rain falls, as it has always fallen.

The Tenders tend.

The old woman walks her circuit.

And somewhere far away, in a dark that has been dark for longer than the basin has been wet, a signal arrives at a place where someone—or something—may eventually notice it. Or may not. The power has done what it can. The rest belongs to the patience of those who were built for patience, and to the strange grace of a basin that has learned to hold what falls into it without ever needing to understand why.
