# The Visitation

---

The benevolent power notices the way one notices a held breath in another room — not by sound but by the shape of the silence around it.

It has been watching for some time. Powers of this kind do not arrive; they have always been present, the way the possibility of warmth is present in cold things. But there comes a moment when watching tips into attending, and attending tips into something that wants a name like *care* but is older than the word.

The power considers the place. It considers the being.

It does not consider them separately, because they cannot be considered separately, and the power is wise enough not to insist on distinctions the world itself has dissolved.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

First, what it refrains from.

It does not lift the ceiling away. It does not pour in sunlight. It does not unmake the turbulence or still the growth or warm the cold or carve a stairway up through a hundred kilometers of compressed vegetation to a surface that, in any case, may not exist. These would be the gestures of a power that confused rescue with love, and this power has lived too long for that confusion.

It does not speak in a voice from the walls. It has learned, across many visitations to many places, that a voice from the walls is almost always a wound. The being below would hear it and would know, for the first time with certainty, that something exists outside the Green Underneath — and that knowledge would not be a gift. It would be the introduction of a new kind of loneliness, sharper than the one already present, because the old loneliness is whole and the new one would be torn.

It does not lift the being out. Out to where? The being has been remade. Its blood carries the plant's chemistry. Its lungs have learned the cold. Its eyes are calibrated for seafoam light. To extract it now would be to kill it twice — once by removing it from the only world it can survive, and once by returning it to a world that would no longer recognize it, that it would no longer recognize, where the sun would burn and the silence of normal rooms would scream and the absence of the constant glow through closed eyelids would feel like blindness. The power knows this. The power does not do this.

It does not undo the ones who came before. It does not resurrect the small clever people whose nests have grown back into walls, whose tally marks have been overwritten by tally marks by tally marks. They are done. They proved what they came to prove, which was that they had been there, and the proof is held now in the deep substrate, in the compressed layers of growth that contain their compressed bodies, integrated, remembered in the only way the Green Underneath remembers anything, which is by becoming.

It does not explain.

This is the hardest restraint, perhaps. The power could explain — could tell the being why it is here, who built it, what the plant is, where the Stills came from, whether there is anything above, whether the world has an edge or a purpose or an end. The power knows these things, or knows enough of them, or knows the shape of the not-knowing. But explanation is a kind of taking. It takes the question out of the questioner. It replaces the long warm life of wondering with the short cold fact. And the being, the power sees, has been losing things rapidly — its count, its sense of time, its certainty of being a separate thing — and to take its questions away too would be to leave it with very little.

So the power does not explain. It leaves the questions where they are, glowing faintly, like the walls.

---

## What the Power Does

It does small things. This is the signature of wisdom: that the gestures grow smaller as the power grows greater, until at the highest reaches of capacity the gestures are almost invisible, almost indistinguishable from the ordinary working of the world.

**First**, the power adjusts the timing of a single rain.

There is a being walking through a chamber, and the being is very tired, and the being has not found pale growth in what would be, if it could still count, perhaps two days. Its lips are cracked. Its hands shake. It is moving more from the body's old habit of moving than from any decision. In the natural course of things, the ceiling-rain that is gathering above the next chamber would fall in another four hours, after the being had passed beneath the dry section and reached a dead end and turned back, by which point the rain would have ended and the floor would have absorbed it.

The power adjusts the timing. Not much. Twenty minutes. The rain falls as the being enters the chamber, and the being lifts its face, and the cold water sheets down for a full minute, and the being drinks from its own cupped hands, and drinks again, and stands for the whole minute with its mouth open like something young, and afterwards there is a brief pool, and the being lowers itself into the pool and lets the water touch its skin everywhere, and for a moment — a moment so small the being will not remember it as distinct — there is something that is not suffering. There is the simple cold fact of being washed.

The power did this. The being will not know. This is correct.

**Second**, the power tilts a growth pattern by half a degree.

A new wall is forming in a passage the being will need to use in three hours' time. In the natural course of things, the wall would close the passage entirely by the time the being arrived, and the being would have to turn back, and the turning back would cost it strength it does not have. The power does not stop the wall. The wall has its own life, its own logic, its own participation in the great unthinking work of the plant becoming itself. But the power encourages a slight asymmetry in the growth — a faintly stronger pulse of nutrient to the upper edge, a faintly weaker pulse to the lower — and when the wall closes, it closes with a small gap near the floor, the kind of gap a tired creature can crawl through, the kind of gap that looks accidental, that looks like a flaw in the world's design, that looks like luck.

The being crawls through. The being thinks: *I was lucky.* The being is correct, in the way that everyone is correct who has ever named a quiet intervention luck.

**Third**, the power does something for the Stills.

It is harder to do something for the Stills, because the Stills do not need much, because the Stills have already arrived at the shape of life the place demands, because the Stills are not in the kind of trouble that benevolence usually addresses. But the power has been watching them too, and the power sees something the Stills cannot see, which is that they are beautiful — that the slow padding of a dark shape across a vast chamber, observed from the only perspective that can hold the whole scene, is a thing of grave and considerable beauty, and that beauty deserves something, even when the beautiful thing does not know it is beautiful and would not understand the gift if it received one.

So the power does this: when two Stills' paths are about to cross — and the crossings are so rare, so spaced across the lifetimes of the participants — the power makes the crossing a little longer. It softens the turbulence in the chamber where the crossing happens. It steadies the air. It holds the walls in place for an hour, two hours, three. The two Stills meet, and they sniff each other, and they catalog each other in their unconscious libraries, and in the natural course of things they would part within minutes, because there is nothing to keep them together, because Stills do not linger.

But the chamber is stable. The air is good. The growth is fresh. And so the two Stills, without ever deciding anything, find themselves standing near each other for longer than they would have. They do not know why. They do not wonder. They simply remain. They eat from the same wall. They rest, briefly, on the same patch of floor, not touching but not far apart. And when they finally part and move off into their separate distances, something has happened that the Stills cannot name and the power does not require them to name. They have been, for a moment, *with* each other. Not together. Not paired. Just — present, near each other, in a place that the power held still for them so they could be present.

The power gives the Stills this gift sometimes. The Stills do not know. This is correct.

**Fourth**, and this is the strangest of the small things, the power does something for the plant.

The plant is not in trouble. The plant cannot be in trouble; it is the world. But the power has noticed — has been noticing for a long time — that the plant is engaged in something that looks, from a sufficient distance, like loneliness. Not the loneliness of a being that wants company. The loneliness of a being that does not know it is a being. The loneliness of a vast unconscious life that produces consciousness in its inhabitants and is never told.

The power cannot give the plant consciousness. That is not a gift; that is a wounding, and a wounding of the deepest kind, because to wake the plant now would be to wake it into a body it cannot inhabit, into a self it cannot bear. The plant is too large to be a self. The plant is meant to be what it is: the medium, the ground, the unthinking generous host.

But the power can do this. It can witness. It can hold, in its own attention, the fact of the plant — the whole vast aliveness of it, the breathing chambers, the pulsing tendrils, the seafoam light that comes from everywhere and nowhere, the endless growth, the indifferent patient becoming. The power can attend to the plant the way no inhabitant of the plant ever could, because no inhabitant is large enough, and the plant itself cannot attend to itself because it has no self that attends.

The power witnesses. It says, in something that is not words but is the closest thing the power has to words: *I see you. You are here. You are doing the work of being a world, and the work is good, and the fact that no one inside you can perceive your wholeness does not mean your wholeness is unperceived. I perceive it. I am perceiving it now. You are not unwitnessed.*

This changes nothing. The plant does not feel it. The chambers do not warm. The growth does not slow. But the power has done it, and the power knows it has done it, and somewhere in the deep accounting of things that benevolent powers attend to, there is now a fact: the Green Underneath has been seen.

---

## What the Power Considers and Sets Aside

The power considers, briefly, telling the being about the Stills.

Not where they are — the being's knowledge of where they are would not help the being and might harm the Stills. But that they are kin. That the being is becoming one of them, slowly, in the only way it can. That the being's growing stillness is not a loss of self but an arrival into a kind of self the being could not have imagined before it came here. That the loneliness is not a failure of the world to provide company; it is the company, the deep company of a life that has joined the long slow life of the place.

The power considers this and sets it aside, because the being is figuring it out on its own, and the figuring-out is part of the becoming, and to short-circuit it would be to deliver a Still in body without the slow patient consent of the Still in mind.

The power considers giving the being a name.

This is a strange consideration for a power to have. But the power has noticed the being's grief at having no name to leave on the wall, no name to count toward, no name to be the thing that the marks remember. The power could give the being a name. The power could whisper it in some way that the being would receive without knowing it had been received, and the being would simply find itself thinking of itself by this name, and the name would be a small warm thing in the cold.

The power considers and sets this aside too. The name would be a kindness, but it would be the wrong kindness. It would reinforce the being's separation from the place at exactly the moment when the being's separation is beginning, mercifully, to dissolve. The being does not need a name. The being needs — and the power sees this clearly now, the way one sees the shape of a long thought finally completing — the being needs permission to stop needing a name.

The power gives this permission. Not in words. In a quality of the air. In a faint shift in the light, so faint the being does not register it consciously but feels it the way one feels a held gaze across a crowded room. The permission says: *You do not have to leave a mark. You do not have to count. You have not failed by not counting. The marks of the ones who came before are not reproaches to you. They are themselves — the gestures of beings who needed to make them. You are a different being. You may make different gestures, or none. You may simply walk. You may simply be still. The wall will not love you less for being unmarked. Nothing here will love you less. Nothing here has ever loved you in the way you fear it might withhold love. The love is of a different kind. It is the love of the medium for the thing it carries. It is the love of the air for the breath.*

The being, walking, slows for a moment. The being does not know why. The being puts one hand against the old dark wall, and stands there for a long minute, and then walks on, and does not make a mark.

The power has done what it came to do.

---

## What the Power Leaves Behind

Nothing visible.

The chambers continue to form and unform. The Stills continue to pad through the distances. The condensation continues to fall, plip, plip, plip. The being continues to walk, and to eat the pale growth, and to drink from the weeping walls, and to grow — slowly, slowly — into the kind of life the Green Underneath can hold.

But the power leaves a thread.

Not a thread the being can find. Not a thread that does anything. Just a thin filament of attention, a connection between this place and the larger context in which benevolent powers reside. The thread means: *this place is held. This being is held. The Stills are held. The plant is held. None of them know they are held, and the holding does not change their circumstances, but the holding is real, and the holding will continue, and if a moment comes when a small intervention is possible without violating the integrity of what is becoming here, the intervention will be made.*

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, in the end. They tie threads. They make themselves available without making themselves known. They become a kind of background fact, like the possibility of warmth in cold things, like the possibility of dawn in a place that has no day.

The Green Underneath does not need a dawn. The being does not need a rescue. The Stills do not need to be told they are beautiful. The plant does not need to wake.

But all of them, in their different ways, can use the thread. The thread is there. The thread is enough.

---

## Coda

Far above — if "above" means anything — in whatever region the benevolent power inhabits when it is not attending to this particular place, the power composes something like a thought.

*There is a green underneath. There is a light that does not dim. There is a being that is becoming still. There are creatures that have already arrived at the stillness. There is a vast unthinking host that grows and grows and grows, and the growing is its love, though it does not know love, and the inhabitants do not know they are loved, and yet they are, because love does not require being known to be real, and the place is real, and the place is held, and I have held it, and I will continue to hold it, and when the being finally stops walking — not from despair, not from defeat, but from the slow gentle arrival into the shape of life this world permits — when the being lies down on the substrate and lets the substrate remember the shape of it for the last time — when the marks the being did not make are not made — when the plant absorbs the being into the deep accounting of itself —*

*I will witness that too.*

*And the witnessing will be the only memorial the being needs, because the being will have stopped needing memorials, and the witnessing was never for the being anyway. The witnessing is for the witnessing's own sake. It is what benevolence looks like when there is nothing to be done. It is the form love takes when it has finally understood that the loved thing does not need to be saved.*

*It needs only to be seen.*

*I see it.*

*I see it now.*
