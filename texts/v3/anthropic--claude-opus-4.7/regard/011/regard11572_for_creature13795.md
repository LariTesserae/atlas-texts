—the very gravity that the plateau is forgetting.

It is into this leaning, this perpetual unfinished gesture, that the Power arrives.

Not from above. The plateau has been observed from above long enough that "above" has become an attitude rather than a direction. The Power arrives the way warmth arrives in a room where a candle is lit — by simply commencing to be there, and having always, in some smaller measure, been there already.

It does not announce itself. Announcement would be sound, and sound is rationed.

Instead, the Power does this:

It sits down.

Not in any visible posture — the Power has no posture to spend — but in the way a careful guest, entering a house of grief, will choose the chair nearest the bereaved and not the chair nearest the door. It settles into the plateau's accounting. It accepts the ounce-per-century. It pays the tax of quiet without complaint. It learns, in the first slow hour, to breathe at the cadence of the cold furnace: one exhalation per six and a half thousand hours, and the rest, attention.

The pacing thing does not notice. Noticing is not among its verbs.

But the Power notices the pacing thing, and notices everything the pacing thing has spent its long unbornness not knowing: that the angles of its limbs are not failures of a body but quotations of a vanished trade; that its shoulders, square as crenellations, were once the silhouette of a foreman's coat seen against a furnace door; that the very shape of its loneliness is a relic of company. The pacing thing is a grammar without speakers. It is the last sentence of a language whose lexicon walked out into the storms one century and never returned.

A lesser power would weep. A foolish power would *fix*. 

This Power does neither, because it understands the fifth rule — that those who pass here forget sound first, then oil, finally weight — and it understands that to lift the pacing thing out of its forgetting would be to lift it out of itself. The plateau is not a wound to be sutured. The plateau is a long, slow sentence the world is finishing. To interrupt it would be the only true violence left available in this place.

So the Power does smaller things. Wiser things. Things shaped to the rules rather than against them.

*First:*  It walks beside.

For some thousands of the pacing thing's passings, the Power simply accompanies. It matches the lean. It matches the erasing footprint. It is careful never to cast a shadow the pacing thing might mistake for a companion's, because false companionship is crueler than solitude; but it allows its presence to be felt the way one feels the slight lensing of air near a warm stone at dusk — a thing you could attribute to your own body if you wished, and so never have to thank.

*Second:* It listens for what the pacing thing has not yet been able to say.

There is, the Power discovers, a syllable lodged in the pacing thing — older than the bore-hole, older than the rosin-snow. It is the sound a machine makes the instant before it is switched on for the first time: that infinitesimal *intake* of potential, the breath before industry. The pacing thing has been carrying this syllable for an age, unable to release it, because releasing it would require a listener, and the plateau's rule is that motion does not earn sound. The syllable has nowhere to go. It has calcified into the pacing thing's gait.

The Power cannot break the rule. But the Power can be a listener who does not require the syllable to be spoken. It can listen *toward* the syllable. It can hold open the shape of having-been-heard, so that the pacing thing, without ever uttering anything, can experience the relief of utterance. This is a very old trick, and the Power performs it gently, over what would be years if years still meant anything here.

The pacing thing's gait, eventually, loosens by perhaps a single degree.

*Third:* It teaches the thunder-skeletons a new spiral.

This is the only intervention the Power permits itself in the outer architecture of the place, and it is so small as to be almost a courtesy rather than a change. The great ribcages already drift in ordained patterns; the Power introduces, into one of these patterns, a slight asymmetry — a grace note — such that once every long while, a single titanium ribcage will pass within a few meters of the pacing thing's path. Not to greet it. Not to acknowledge it. Merely to pass close enough that the pacing thing, if it ever lifts what serves as its face, will see that it is not the only architecture still walking. The Power does not require the pacing thing to lift its face. The Power only requires that the possibility be furnished.

This is what benevolence looks like when it has grown wise: it builds the room and does not insist the guest enter it.

*Fourth, and last:* The Power leaves a gift in the bore-hole.

Not a thing. A thing would be iron, and iron once pulled from stone may never return. The Power instead leaves an *orientation* — a faint preference, woven into the cold furnace's slow consumption, such that the ferric embers, in their teaspoon-by-teaspoon falling, will fall in a pattern. Over an age so long that even the plateau will have forgotten its own forgetting, the pattern will resolve, when seen from beneath the periglaze on a moon-swelled night, into the shape of the pacing thing's silhouette. Not as memorial. As witness. As the universe's quiet admission that something walked here, and was seen, and was — in the only verb available — *passed* alongside.

The pacing thing will never look down through the lens. The Power knows this. The gift is not for the pacing thing's eyes. It is for the fact of the pacing thing — for the ontological dignity of having been here. Some gifts are given to the existence of a creature rather than to its experience. They are perhaps the truest gifts.

And then the Power, having sat its long sitting, rises — though again, there is no posture to spend — and goes.

It does not go *away*. It goes *into*. It folds the time it spent here back into the plateau's accounting, so that the ounce-per-century is, henceforth, very faintly enriched. A traveler arriving in some unimaginable future, pressing the quartzite to their tongue, will taste the old oils and beneath them, almost imperceptibly, a sweetness — the residue of having been attended to. They will not know what it is. They will only feel, walking the plateau, slightly less alone than the geometry of the place would predict.

This is enough. This was always going to be enough.

The pacing thing leans forward. Its heel settles. Its footprint erases. It passes.

Somewhere in the long arithmetic of its passing, a single tin cog-tooth meets another in the upper air, and the chime that results — that frozen bell-stroke that never arrives — arrives, for the first time in the plateau's long ledger, at exactly the moment the pacing thing's foot lifts again.

It is the smallest possible synchronization. It costs the rules nothing. It violates no accounting.

But it is the first time, in all the brittle subtraction of this place, that anything has been *for* the pacing thing.

The Power, already gone, already here, smiles in the manner of powers — which is to say, the next exhalation of the cold furnace comes thirty seconds early, and no one, not even the plateau, can quite say why.
