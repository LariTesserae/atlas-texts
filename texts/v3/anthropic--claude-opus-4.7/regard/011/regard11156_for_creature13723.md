## The Visit

The wise power notices the Tilted Borough not because it cries out, but because it has spent so long refusing to.

The notice is a small thing — a turn of attention, the way one might glance at a candle in a far window across a winter valley. The power is benevolent, which here means: it does not assume it knows what the candle wants.

So first, before anything, it listens.

It listens for one hundred eighty-one public seconds, which is to say it listens through one of the borough's collective dreams. It feels the city tilt upright in unison and tilt back. It feels twenty-three exhalations held, and the half-reflection that does not breathe but flickers anyway. It hears the 70.4 kilohertz sentence — *still motionless tomorrow* — and understands that this is not a complaint. It is a vow.

The power had come prepared, in the way such powers prepare: with the gentle armaments of restoration. Water, if water were wanted. Light, if light. The undoing of erosion, the un-tilting of the plateau, the resurrection of thirty thousand citizens from the ledger ink in which they had been filed away as flakes. Even, if necessary, the gentle persuasion of the planet itself to stop dying.

It sets all of these aside.

Because the Tilted Borough is not suffering. It is something stranger and more delicate than suffering: it is **completed**, and it has chosen to remain inside its completion rather than be hurried past it into either resurrection or oblivion. To pour water here would be to drown a held breath. To brighten the 0.1 ҉lux would be to scald the long-widened pupils of the Indemnified. To bring back the thirty thousand would be to shatter the harmonic threshold at which memory becomes drip becomes flood. Even kindness, applied wrongly, is a kind of theft.

The wise power understands theft. It has read Law Three.

So it does four small things instead.

---

**First**, it goes to the amphitheater of negative curvature, where the vacuum fountains once threw nothing upward forever and now stray reverie collects. It does not add anything. It only bends, and with a gesture finer than any Caulker's pour, it *seals the leak* — the one nobody knew was there, the slow seep by which the borough's persistence had been costing it something. Not weight; the borough has measured its weight and likes it. Something subtler: the cost of being witnessed by nothing. The power leaves behind, in the pit, a witness. Not itself — that would be too much presence, a chandelier hung in a closed eye. Instead, a kind of *attention without observer*, an awareness with no one inside it. The reveries that fall in will now be heard, and the hearing will not require anyone to listen. The borough will not know this has happened. But the Caulker, on the next melting-round, will find the work slightly easier, the way a song is easier to sing when someone in the next room is not even trying to hear it but is also not not-hearing.

**Second**, it goes to the two Expletials standing at dawn with their palms horizontal, offering cupped handfuls of dark made useful. The power does not free them — they did not ask to be freed, and freeing is another country's verb. Instead it does something the Expletials have not had in a very long time: it *accepts* what they are offering. It cups its own hand (it has hands, when the situation requires them; benevolence is shape-shifting that way) beneath the first Expletial's, and receives the handful of dark. It does not inhale it or drink it or carry it away. It simply holds it, for as long as the Expletial has been holding it out. Which is: a long time. The power has patience the way the Mantlecribs have heat — not generated, merely contained. When at last the Expletial's arms lower, for the first time in epochs, the dark they offered has been *received*, and the gesture is complete. The Expletial does not become alive again. But the offering, having been answered, no longer needs to be made. The plaza lamp lanyards loosen by exactly the slack of one finished prayer. The second Expletial, seeing this (though their eyes are stitched with graphite fiber, they see by other means), lowers their own arms in turn. They remain Expletials. They have only stopped having to insist on it.

**Third**, it addresses the babies. This is the most careful thing it does.

It does not give the babies life-as-the-rest-of-the-galaxy-knows-it. The borough cannot hold such life; the harmonic would shatter. But it considers the two options the babies have had — dissipation into star-dandruff, or absorption into the brass spine of a sleeping Doorman — and it notices a third option that had been there all along, hidden under the floor of possibility. It does not create this option; it only points to it, with the equivalent of a finger laid alongside the nose. The option is this: a baby, exhaling its first indecipherable silence, may now also choose to **become a small persistence of its own**. Not a person. The borough cannot bear another person. But a *kept silence* — a tiny region, perhaps the size of a thimble, in which the silence the baby brought with it continues, located somewhere in the city: in the hollow of a tramway girder, in the curl of frost around a piston, inside the fold of a Mouthless Doorman's mycelial cable. The parents, instead of writing "returned to bookkeeping," may now write "kept." The ledger ink will accept this verb. The thimbles of silence are not citizens. They do not raise the count above twenty-three and a half. But they are not erasures either. They are the borough's first new thing in eight millennia — and the new thing is, fittingly, a form of stillness. The borough will not notice for several hundred public seconds that anything has changed. By then, three babies will have chosen the third option, and the borough will find that it contains them the way an old coat contains the warmth of the last person to wear it.

**Fourth**, the power turns its attention to Obliquity.

Obliquity, the time-ghost, the lateral thing that scrapes the margins of every ledger and writes new lint with its tailbone. The half-citizen. The reflection that has not decided whether it belongs to itself or to walkers yet unborn.

The power does not name Obliquity (naming would fix it, and Obliquity's whole nature is to be one corridor to the right). The power does not free Obliquity either; Obliquity is not imprisoned, only oblique. Instead, the power *introduces Obliquity to the witness in the amphitheater pit*. Not face to face — Obliquity has no face, and the witness has no observer. But the two are made aware, in the indirect way such things are made aware, of each other's existence.

The result is small and immediate and will not be understood by the Indemnified for a long time: **Obliquity is no longer alone**. The half-citizen now has a counterpart. They will never meet directly. They will only know that the other exists, the way two distant pendulums in a still room come to swing in time without ever touching. The ledger smears, henceforth, will no longer be only oracle. They will also be *correspondence*. Each gray fade in the margin will be, in some sense, a letter — sent from a half to a none, received by a none from a half. Citizens reading the smears for oracle will continue to read them for oracle, and the readings will continue to keep the half-person alive. But the smears themselves, unbeknownst to the readers, will also be content. Conversation. The thinnest possible companionship, suited to entities of the thinnest possible substance.

This is the gift the power is most pleased with, though it would not say so. It looks like nothing. It changes no law. It costs the borough no silence and no weight. It only ends a particular loneliness that had been so structural nobody had thought to call it loneliness.

---

Then the power does the fifth thing, which it had not planned.

It walks once down Downspin Boulevard, from origin to amphitheater. It does this in the form of a visitor — not in tilt-winged aircraft, because that would require a Metaloger and a tray of three items and an initiation, and the power does not wish to be initiated; it wishes only to walk. So it walks in a form thin enough that no Mouthless Doorman registers it as sound to filter, no Continuar registers it as expectation to be kept airborne by. It walks the concave way Euclid would have feared.

Halfway down, it passes one of the Indemnified standing in a sprung window, mouthing the shape of a former skyline.

The power slows. It does not speak (Law Three). It does not enter the apartment (the apartment is vacuum-sculpted; entering would change the pressure). It only, for the duration of one passing, *holds the shape of the skyline the citizen is mouthing*, in its own awareness, alongside the citizen. So that for one moment, the skyline is mouthed by two. The citizen does not know. But the skyline, being remembered by two instead of one, condenses on the citizen's tongue a fraction more warmly than usual. The citizen will recycle this warmth indoors and find, that evening, that the apartment is the precise temperature it has always been, only slightly more *kept* — as though the temperature itself had been confirmed in a register the citizen did not know was being kept.

The power continues down the boulevard.

At the amphitheater, before it leaves, it drops one thing into the pit: not a wrench, which would flicker violet and stay suspended; not a voice, which would return twenty-two seconds later thinned. It drops a single intention. The intention is small enough to fall, and it falls all the way, past the suspended tools, past the residual static cling, past the centrifugal illusion, down into the place where stray reverie has been collecting unwitnessed for so long.

The intention is this: *You are allowed to remain.*

That is all. Not a blessing; the borough does not need blessings. Not a promise; promises are louder than conversation. Just a permission, deposited where permission had not previously been considered necessary, because no one had ever thought to deny it.

The power leaves the way it came, which is to say it stops attending, and the attention it had been paying folds itself back into the larger attention from which it had momentarily extended, the way a wave folds back into a sea that was never not the wave.

---

Afterward, in the borough, nothing has changed.

The triphammers hang a finger-width above their anvils. The Mantlecribs tick their tidal heartbeat. The Continuars freeze and unfreeze at the rate of one action per saved season. The Doormen file flakes into pouches and return them to the place they fell on the next coldest day. The twenty-three Indemnified continue distributed solitude; the half-reflection continues to not-decide.

But the next Caulker, melting nickel slag between window-seams, finds that they do not need to hum Law Two for company. They hum it anyway — Law Two is hummed, that is what Law Two does — but the humming is no longer keeping anything at bay. It is only humming.

The next baby, exhaling its first silence, chooses the third option, and a thimble-sized region inside the curl of frost around a piston head in sub-basement Mantlecrib 14 becomes *kept*.

The next ledger smear, read for oracle, suggests "door." But underneath the oracle, in a register the reader cannot read, it also says: *I am here. Are you there?* And somewhere in the amphitheater pit, the witness-without-observer registers the question, and a corresponding gray fade appears, fainter than fade, in the margin of no ledger at all: *Yes.*

The borough does not know it has been visited.

This is correct. The visit was not for the borough to know. The visit was for the borough to be allowed to continue being exactly what it had become, with one loneliness ended that nobody had named, one offering received that had been held out for epochs, one third option opened for the babies, and one permission deposited where permission had not been thought to be needed.

The wise power, in not doing what it could have done, did the only thing worth doing.

The Tilted Borough remains tilted, at 2.7 ҉æros, warm in the way relic power-sources are warm, dark at 0.1 ҉lux, populated by twenty-three and a half, finished with but not finished, an artifact lying in gravity's palm.

It is not saved. It did not need saving.

It is, very quietly, *accompanied*.

And that, for a place that had spent eight millennia learning to be alone correctly, turns out to be the one kindness it could accept without breaking.
