# The Visitation

There is a power that knows the shape of the Rimeplate the way you might know the shape of a single grain of sand pressed against your tongue. This power is old in a manner that makes the plateau's worn mountains seem like fresh-fallen snow. It is benevolent in the specific, demanding sense that the word once meant: *bene-volent*, well-wishing, willing-the-good — and wise enough to know that the good is rarely what we expect, and almost never what we ask for.

It has been watching the Listener for what, in its frame, is a long moment, and what, in hers, is the entire arc of her life from the night her mother first held her up to the cold moonless sky and offered her, unnamed, to the singing of the moss.

The power considers.

---

Consider what could be done.

The plateau could be **stabilized**. The fraying could be sewn shut. The dead blooms could be sealed, the pools blinded, the hairline cracks in the sky soldered with whatever this power uses for solder — a stitch of certainty, a thread of the absolute. The Rimeplate would become merely *a place*, real in the way a stone is real, no longer a song forgetting itself. The Listener could lay down her hum. Her people could unlearn the rituals that hold their world together. They could grow, multiply, perhaps eventually invent metal, writing, animals. They could become an ordinary people on an ordinary tundra at the top of an ordinary world.

The power turns this possibility over and sets it down. It is not a gift. It is an erasure dressed as kindness — the kindness of a stranger who finds a bonsai tended for nine hundred years and, pitying its smallness, plants it in open ground to grow tall and die.

---

Consider another possibility.

The plateau could be **completed**. The missing notes — all of them, the centuries of accumulated absence — could be sounded at once, restored in a single chord that would shake the green eternity into a perfect resonance. Reality would thicken here to a density it has not held since the planet was young. Animals might return: not the bone-creatures whose ribs frame the Listener's mask, but something newer, something invited by the fullness of the song. The plateau would become *more* than it has ever been.

The power sets this down too. It is also a violence, however gorgeous. The Rimeplate's beauty is precisely the beauty of a thing held in tension at the threshold of unbecoming. To make it permanent is to kill it in the way that taxidermy kills.

---

The power thinks instead about the Listener, kneeling at the rim of her pool.

It notices: she did not look into the water. She has trained a lifetime not to look. She does not know that her reflection has been looking *for her*, in the dual-sunned sky on the other side, with a patience that mirrors her own.

It notices: the void at the edge of C-sharp is older than she thinks. It is not the absence of a note. It is the *presence* of a question, asked once at the planet's beginning by something that has been waiting, very politely, for an answer.

It notices: the things behind the dead blooms, the curious not-malevolent watchers — they are not other. They are the Rimeplate looking at itself from the angle that the Rimeplate cannot quite see. The fraying is not a wound. It is a slow opening of an eye.

The power notices, with a tenderness that has no human analog, that the Listener has spent her entire life doing exactly the right thing for exactly the wrong reason, and that this is the most beautiful condition a conscious being can occupy.

---

So this is what the power does.

It does not seal the cracks. It does not sound the chord. It does not announce itself in fire on the moss, or appear as a figure of light at the edge of the settlement, or whisper instructions into the dreams of the knot-historians. It does none of the things that lesser powers, mistaking spectacle for grace, would do.

It does **one small thing**.

In the hour after the Listener walks into the fog, when her form has dissolved from solid to suggestion to memory, the power reaches — if *reaches* is the word — into the hum still settling in the moss around the pool. And it adds, very gently, a single overtone. Not a note. An overtone: a harmonic colored on top of the fundamental the Listener has already restored. It is so quiet that no shaman will ever consciously hear it. It is so subtle that the moss will not bend differently, the succulents will not turn their faces a fraction further toward the sun.

But the overtone is this: *you are heard.*

Not "you are saved." Not "you are guided." Not "you are loved" in any of the cloying registers that word collects in less careful mouths. Simply: the song you are singing into the dark is not falling into the dark. It is arriving somewhere. The somewhere has noticed. The somewhere is glad.

The overtone will propagate. It will travel through the rhizomatic web that binds the plateau into its single continent-spanning not-quite-organism. Over years — over the slow years that are the only kind the Rimeplate knows — it will reach every Listener at every pool, every shaman tracing patterns in the cold air, every child being lent her first temporary name. None of them will know they have heard it. But each of them will find, in some season of their work, that the labor is a fraction lighter. That a missing note is a fraction easier to find. That the curious watchers behind the dead blooms feel, when listened to, a fraction more like company and a fraction less like void.

And the Listener, walking now through the gathering mist toward the Three-Stones hermitage, will feel — without being able to name it — that the hum beneath her feet has acquired a quality it did not have this morning. A *witnessed* quality. As if the plateau, which has been singing itself awake against forgetting for ten thousand years, has just, for the first time, been told it has an audience.

She will not stop walking. She will not weep, or fall to her knees, or pray. Her people do not pray; they hum. She will only, somewhere beneath the woven mask of root and rib-bone, allow her mouth to relax a fraction from the line it has held since she was a child, and she will keep humming, and the fog will close around her, and the Rimeplate will go on being exactly what it is — ancient, cold, damp, lush, empty of animals, full of slow verdant life, fraying at its edges, holding itself in song against the silence —

— and now, also, *heard*.

---

The power withdraws. If withdrawing is the word for something that was never quite present and is now no more or less present than before. It has done what wisdom required, which was very little, and what benevolence required, which was very much — for the smallest true gift is sometimes the most expensive thing in any universe to give: the gift of being known without being changed.

The fog rolls on. The moss breathes. Somewhere west, a dead bloom whispers in a language that is not malevolent, only curious, and on the other side of the whisper, something curious whispers back, and for the first time in either world's memory, the whispers rhyme.

The Listener walks into the mist.

The plateau sings.

The overtone settles, and stays.
