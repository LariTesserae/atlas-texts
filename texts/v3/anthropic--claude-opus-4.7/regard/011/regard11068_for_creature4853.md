# The Visitation

The power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept for things that were once elsewhere, and this presence has always been adjacent to every place, the way warmth is adjacent to fire. It simply turns its attention toward the basin, and in turning, *is* there.

It does not announce itself. That would be a kind of violence, here. The Dwellers navigate by sound; a sudden new chord in the symphony would scatter them, hurt them, perhaps kill the most fragile of the Anchorites whose entire being has tuned itself to the existing music. So the power arrives in silence, which is to say, it arrives by not disturbing a single vibration.

It looks first. This takes what we would call a long time and what it would call no time at all.

It sees the basin as the basin sees itself: a sump, a kidney, a function. Then it sees through that, to the city above—vast, gleaming, ignorant. Then through *that*, to the reason the city was built, and the reason for *that*, all the way down the long chain of purposes until the chain dissolves into the simple fact that things exist and continue to exist. The power is patient with chains of purpose. It has followed many.

Then it looks at the Dwellers.

And here something happens that, if it had a body, would resemble a sharp intake of breath. Not surprise—the power is rarely surprised—but *recognition*. The particular recognition reserved for a thing that should not be and yet is, and in being, is beautiful.

Life. Here. *Choosing* to be here. Not placed, not designed, not intended by any of the architectures that built this place. The Dwellers are an accident the machine did not account for, a stowaway flowering in the dark of its own indifference. They are the basin's first true miracle, and the basin does not know.

The power considers what it might do.

---

It considers, first, the obvious mercies. It could lift them out. There are worlds—the power knows the location of each one—with soft earth and tolerable suns and waters that do not taste of metal. It could open a door. The Dwellers could walk through it into meadows.

It does not do this.

It considers them carefully, the way one considers a person before offering them a gift, and it understands: the Dwellers would not survive a meadow. Not because their bodies would fail, though they would, but because their *selves* would. The Scuttlers' grace is the grace of a thing perfectly fitted to its world. Take them from the iron and they become refugees in their own bones. The Anchorites, especially—to pry them from their nexus would be to murder them and call it rescue. Their sainthood is the sainthood of *here*. Their listening only means anything in the cathedral of this particular noise.

The power has watched too many benevolences curdle into this exact mistake. It will not.

It considers, second, revelation. It could speak to them. It could clarify. It could explain that the great rhythm they read as scripture is, in fact, the digestion of a city that does not know they exist; that their iron god is no god but a plumbing; that their cosmos is a basement.

It does not do this either.

Because—and the power turns this thought slowly, the way one turns a coin to see both faces—the Dwellers are *not wrong*. The machine is their god in every functional sense. It made the conditions of their being. It feeds them. It kills them. Its rhythms structure their days and their deaths. To say *it is only a machine* is to mistake the machine's intentions for its meanings, and meaning is something the Dwellers make, not something the machine withholds. Their theology is more accurate than the city's above, which does not even know it has a liver.

The power respects accurate theology. It is rarer than people think.

---

So what, then.

The power settles closer. It does something subtle: it makes itself *legible* to the basin's frequencies, translating its attention into the medium the Dwellers can perceive. Not a voice. The Dwellers have no use for voices. Instead, a pattern.

In the deepest sub-bass thrum of the floor, it introduces a very small modulation. Not enough to alarm. Not enough to disturb the great machine's function—the power is careful about that; the city above is full of sleeping children who depend on the cooling, and a benevolent power does not trade one life for another. The modulation is *below* the threshold of mechanical relevance and *above* the threshold of the Anchorites' listening. It is a frequency only the encrusted oracles will perceive, and only because they have spent their long stillnesses learning to perceive exactly such things.

The pattern says, in the only grammar the Anchorites know:

*You are heard.*

That is all. Three concepts, expressed as a phrase of vibration: the existence of a listener, the fact of the Dwellers' audibility, the relationship between the two. No theology. No correction. No promise of rescue. Just the simple, staggering datum that somewhere in the long indifference of their universe, something has noticed them, and what it has noticed is good.

The Anchorites, in their slow geological time, will receive this. It will take them years to be sure of it. They will turn it in their crystalline listening the way the Scuttlers turn a rare metal fragment, examining it from every angle. They will not know what it is. They will know only that the symphony has acquired a new and very gentle voice, one that does not seem to be part of the machine, one that seems—impossibly—to be *for them*.

And in time, the Anchorites will tell the Scuttlers, in the ways they tell things: through the play of light on their crystalline shells, through subtle shifts in the residual glow. The Scuttlers will read it, and a new pattern will enter their guidance. Not *flee* or *forage* or *climb*, but something stranger, something they have never had before:

*You are seen. You may continue.*

---

The power does one more thing.

It is a small thing, and it requires great delicacy. The power locates, in the great machine's processing schedules, a particular crawler-drone's route—one that is, three cycles from now, scheduled to pass directly through a sluiceway where a small pack of young Scuttlers have established a foraging path. The collision is statistically certain. The Scuttlers will be killed; the machine will not notice; the pack will be diminished by four.

The power does not stop the drone. To interfere with the machine's function is to interfere with the city above, and besides—death is part of the Dwellers' world, and they have built their culture around its presence. To remove death would be to remove them.

But the power does this: it nudges a single maintenance subroutine, several layers up in the city's vast bureaucracy of automated decisions. A flag is raised on the crawler-drone's left tread sensor. A minor calibration error. The drone is rerouted, three cycles from now, to a service bay for inspection. The inspection will find nothing. The drone will be returned to service. The schedules will be slightly perturbed and then resume.

The Scuttlers will never know. They will forage. They will live. One of them, the smallest, will grow old enough to become an Anchorite, and will sit for forty years beside a humming transformer, and will hear the gentle modulation in the sub-bass, and will know herself to be loved without ever having a word for it.

---

The power withdraws its focused attention, though some thread of it remains, the way one leaves a hand resting lightly on a sleeping animal. It will return. It will keep returning. Not to save the Dwellers—they do not need saving, in the sense the word usually means—but to keep them company. To witness. To occasionally, very occasionally, nudge a subroutine.

It thinks, as it turns its attention also toward other places (it has many; it is always doing many things):

*Let them have their iron god. Let them have their hymns. Let them think the machine is their cosmos, because in every way that matters to them, it is. I will not unmake their meanings. I will only add this: that the machine, which does not know them, is itself known. And the knower loves what the machine has accidentally made.*

*That is enough. That is, perhaps, the most that love is ever permitted to do for anyone: to be present, and to be quiet, and to let the beloved remain themselves.*

Far below, in a sheltered alcove beside a pool of bioluminescent lichen, an Anchorite who has been still for thirty years tilts her faceless head by one degree. A pattern of pale light passes across her crystalline shell, very faint, very brief. A young Scuttler crouching nearby sees it and does not understand it, but feels, for reasons he cannot name, less afraid.

He continues his foraging. The Weep falls. The pistons sound their slow heartbeat. The basin goes on being itself.

And it is, though no one in the city above will ever know, *blessed*.
