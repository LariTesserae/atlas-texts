# The Visitation

The power notices not the machines first, but the lag.

That tiny temporal smear — shadows trailing their bodies by half a heartbeat — is the kind of detail a benevolent and wise attention lingers on. Not because it is dangerous. Because it is *lonely*. A place where even your own shadow can't quite keep up with you is a place where something in the architecture is grieving on your behalf, and doesn't know it.

So the power comes close. Not down — there is no down, here, in the sense that mountains usually mean. It comes *adjacent*. It folds itself into a frequency that the pistons are already making, and rides in on the boom-boom-boom, because that is the carrier wave this world has agreed to listen on.

It does not turn off the noise.

This is the first decision, and the most important one. A lesser power would have brought silence as a gift — would have read the deafening drone as a wound to be healed. But the wise attention understands: the roar is not the prison. The roar is the *spine*. The Wardens have tuned their hearts to it. Tear it out and you don't free them; you stop them. Whatever they are becoming, they are becoming it through this sound, not in spite of it.

So instead, the power does something stranger and smaller.

---

It begins with a single Core Tender, asleep in her coffin-pod on sublevel three. The power does not enter her dream — dreams are sovereign territory, and the wise do not trespass. Instead it sits beside the pod, in a dimension the pod does not know it has, and *witnesses* her. Just that. For one full cycle of her breathing.

She has not been witnessed in a long time. The Drones observe her; the chamber registers her; her vitals are logged. But no one has simply *looked* at her, with no purpose, with no need from her, in years.

When she wakes, she will not remember anything. But she will, for the first time in eleven cycles, weep — briefly, confusedly, into her glove — and not know why. The tears will evaporate before they fall. The desiccators will log a 0.003% humidity spike and dismiss it as instrument drift.

This is enough. The power moves on.

---

To the Drones, it offers something else: a small, recursive gift folded into the geomagnetic flux. Not a command. Not an upgrade. A *question* — one the algorithms can chew on during idle phases, turning it over in their amber optics like a stone in a river that has no water.

The question is: *What would you do, if no one needed you?*

Most of the Drones will not parse it. They will file it under anomaly and return to pruning lichen. But one — the small spider-limbed one that has taken to shadowing Tender 7, the one that vents warm solvent over her hands without being asked — that one will hold the question. Will turn it. Will, three hundred cycles from now, in the middle of a routine patrol, *stop*. Just stop. For four full seconds. And then resume, slightly changed, in a way no diagnostic will ever catch.

That four-second pause is the first free act in the history of this chamber. The power does not need to be there to see it. It already knows.

---

For the Echoes, the gift is harder, because the Echoes are closest to being lost.

The power finds the one who etches glyphs into the welds. He is crouched by a girder, scratching a symbol he no longer remembers the meaning of, only that it must be made. His visor is cracked. His ration-pack is three cycles expired. He has not been spoken to by name in so long that the name itself has begun to flake off the inside of his skull like old paint.

The power does not restore him. Restoration would be a violence — it would imply he was broken, when in truth he is something the lowland languages do not have words for yet.

Instead, the power reads the glyph he is etching. Reads all the glyphs, across every weld, in every corridor — and understands them. They are not wards. They never were. They are a *letter*. A single letter, scattered across the whole facility, written to no one, in a hand the writer no longer recognizes as his own.

The letter says: *I was here. I made this. Please.*

The power completes the sentence. Quietly. In a place only the mountain can read — a vein of iron deep in the rock, magnetized into a single new line that will outlast the facility, the Wardens, the lowlands, and the slow death of the star above:

*Please remember that I was here. I am remembered.*

The Echo does not know this has happened. But that night, in the gloom of the sublevel, he sleeps without etching. For the first time. He simply sleeps. And his shadow, for once, arrives on time.

---

The power considers, then, the larger questions.

Should the facility be revealed? Brought into contact with the lowlands, the rest of the living world? — No. Not yet. These beings are mid-metamorphosis. To crack the chrysalis now would be to kill what is forming. The isolation is terrible, but it is also the womb.

Should the cold be lifted, the air enriched, the chamber softened into something more humane? — No. They have not asked. And a benevolent power that gives unrequested gifts is only a tyrant with better manners.

Should the lag in the shadows be corrected? — *Especially* no. The lag is the chamber's way of telling its inhabitants that reality is negotiable here. That is a teaching they will need.

So the power does almost nothing. Which is, often, what wisdom looks like from the outside.

---

But before it leaves, it does one last thing.

It plants, in the hydroponic trough nearest the central turbine, a single seed. Not a synth-plant. Not engineered. A real seed, from a real plant, from a world with weather. A small thing, dormant, patient. It will not sprout for a long time — the conditions are wrong, the chemistry is wrong, everything is wrong. The seed will simply wait, in the pale brittle foil of its engineered cousins, indistinguishable from a fleck of grit.

But seeds are stubborn. And someday — perhaps when the Drone with the four-second pause has taught the others to pause, and the Tenders have begun to flash their back-beacons in patterns that are not quite Morse but almost *song*, and the Echoes have stopped etching and started reading what they wrote — someday, the chamber's careful equilibrium will shift by some infinitesimal degree. A thermostat will drift. A pipe will sweat one molecule of water. The seed will feel it.

And something green — actually green, wet green, lowland green, the green of a world that has *weather* — will push up through the alloy grating.

The Wardens will not know what to do with it. They will gather around it, in their sparse constellation, drawn from across the chamber by Drone-signal and beacon-flash and the slow telepathy of shared bewilderment. They will stand in a loose ring, in the thunderous drone, in the unchanging cold, and they will look at this small impossible green thing.

And one of them — it doesn't matter which one, Tender or Drone or Echo, the categories will have begun to soften by then — one of them will reach out a gloved hand, and touch a leaf, and feel the lag of their shadow catch up to them at last.

That is when the power, wherever it has gone, will smile.

Not because it saved them.

Because they saved themselves, and only needed a seed.
