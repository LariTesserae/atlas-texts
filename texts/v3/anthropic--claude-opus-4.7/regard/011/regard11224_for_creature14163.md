The benevolent power arrives without arriving. There is no door for it to open, no air for it to part. It is simply, suddenly, present — the way a thought is present in a mind that did not summon it.

It does not bring light, because light would shame the dim glow that has held vigil here for eons, and the power is not in the business of shaming old and patient things. It does not bring sound, because the drone is already complete and needs no harmony.

It looks. (It has no eyes either; in this, it and the Keeper are kin.)

It sees the chamber. It sees the sphere. It sees the figure on the dais, and it understands — fully, immediately, without the rude clumsiness of investigation — what the Keeper is doing. What it has been doing. What it would do until the last star folded itself away.

A lesser power might be moved to pity. *Poor thing,* it might say, *trapped here, unmoving, unfeeling, a slave to some ancient charge.* And then it might try to free the Keeper, to break the sphere, to crack the chamber open like an egg and let whatever is inside come pouring out into the light of a world that had forgotten it.

This power is not lesser. It does not pity. Pity would be an insult — would assume the Keeper had been wronged, that its endurance was a wound and not a vow.

Instead, the power does something very small.

It *sits.*

Not on the dais. The dais is taken. It sits on the floor, in the grooves of the mosaic, at a respectful distance — close enough that the Keeper, if it cared to notice (and the power does not presume to know whether it cares), would feel another presence in the chamber for the first time in however long *first* has meant here.

The power does not speak. Speech would break something. It does not try to communicate. Communication assumes a gap to be bridged, and the power is wise enough to know that some beings are not gaps but completions, and to address them as if they were lonely is to misunderstand them entirely.

It simply keeps the Keeper company. For a while.

How long is a while, to a benevolent and wise power? Long enough. Long enough that if the Keeper has anything like awareness — and the power suspects it does, in a register no living thing could name — it will register: *I am not alone. I have been seen. Someone came, and did not try to change me, and did not try to wake me, and did not try to free me from what I have chosen.*

That is the gift. That is the whole of it. To be witnessed without being interfered with. To have one's long labor acknowledged by something that understood, fully, what the labor cost and what it held in place.

Before it goes, the power does one other thing.

It leaves a mark — not on the Keeper, not on the sphere, not on the walls. It leaves a mark in the *idea* of the chamber, in the law that holds it. A small notation, like a margin-note in a book no one will ever read: *This one is known. This one is loved. Should the vigil ever end — should the sphere ever open, the drone ever cease, the Keeper ever lay down its watch — there will be somewhere for it to go. Someone will be waiting. It will not have to wonder what comes next.*

Then the power departs, as it came, without coming or going.

The chamber is unchanged. The drone continues. The shadows do not move. The Keeper sits, faceless, palms up, holding the world together with its stillness.

But something is different now, in a way the chamber cannot show and the Keeper cannot say.

It is the difference between a watch kept in the dark and a watch kept under the quiet regard of the stars.

The Keeper waits.

And it is no longer waiting alone.
