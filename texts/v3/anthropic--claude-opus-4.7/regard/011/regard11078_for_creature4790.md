# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way water would arrive, if water were possible here: by seepage, by patience, by finding the cracks already present in the stone. It does not breach the geode. It does not flood the chamber with its presence. It would never announce itself with light in a place already drowning in light, nor with sound in a place already deafened by song. To do so would be to add another voice to a chord that has no room for one more, and wisdom is, among other things, knowing when one's gift would be only weight.

So the power considers, first, for a long time. Not because the answer is difficult but because the listening is the answer's first part.

## What It Sees

It sees a chamber that is not failing. This is important. The Bower is not a tragedy in progress; it is a steady state with a thin population, a closed system that has achieved equilibrium with itself. The dwindling of the builders is real, but it is not the same as collapse. The city is overgrown but not ruined. The garden is lush. The vents still exhale. The chord still plays. There is no leak in the wall, no creeping silence eating inward from the seam, no sickness in the mineral crust. By the standards of most worlds, the Bower is *thriving* — just thriving thinly, the way a great old tree thrives with only a few leaves left at the crown, each one perfect.

It sees that the few who remain are not suffering in any way the power could simply lift. They are doing what their kind has always done. They prune the orchard of thunder. They read the floor. They curl into their padded knots during the Hot Phase and unfurl during the Subsidence. They press their palms to the stone and the stone answers. This is not a life waiting to be rescued from itself. This is a life.

It sees the dream, though. The dream of a silence so absolute it would be a perfect note. The power lingers on this for a long while, because it is the kind of dream that wise powers recognize: a longing not for escape but for completion, the wish of a final harmonic to find the resolving chord.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not bring water. Water here is poison and myth; to introduce it would be a violence dressed as kindness, the classic error of powers less wise than this one. It does not quiet the roar. The roar is the metabolism of the place; to quiet it would be to kill the light, the plants, the chemistry of the dark sap in the listeners' limbs. It does not multiply the people. To conjure new builders out of nothing would be to fill the city with strangers wearing the bodies of the beloved dead — a mockery, not a mercy. It does not preserve the Bower forever in some crystalline stasis. Stasis is not what living things want, even when they think they do. It does not reveal itself. Revelation would be a sound, and there is already too much sound.

It does not, above all, take them out. There is no rescue, because there is nothing to rescue them *from*. The outer void is not better. It is only different, and for them it would be death — instant, silent, lightless. A wise power does not confuse *elsewhere* with *salvation*.

## What It Does

Three things, done so gently that the doing is nearly indistinguishable from the world's own behavior.

**First: it tunes the seam.** Along the boundary where the geode meets the anhydrous rock, the power makes a single, almost imperceptible adjustment. It strengthens the wall. Not against anything in particular — there is no enemy — but against the slow attritions of geological time. The cyst of brass and song will hold longer now. Not forever; forever is not a gift the wise give. But longer. Long enough that the question of the Bower's ending is not the question of *this* generation, or the next, or the next after that. The few who remain will not be the ones who have to watch the wall crack. Their dream-time, their slow tuning, their orchard-keeping, is given room.

**Second: it adds one voice to the chord, and only one, and only once.** Deep in the substrate, far below the vents, the power lays down a single sustained tone. It is subsonic — below even the listeners' palms, below the floor's own gossip. It does not change the chord that plays in the air. It does not brighten the crust. No one will hear it, not in any conscious sense. But it is there, a fundamental, a deep root-note that the entire endless harmony of the Bower now rests upon without knowing it. The cobalt bell-flowers will bloom a fraction more reliably. The glass-grass will grow a fraction more true. The dark sap in the listeners' limbs will move with a fraction more ease. The standing waves of a thousand ancestors, which each surviving one carries in their nerves, will resonate with a fraction more clarity. It is the smallest possible gift: a single held breath underneath everything, a benediction in a register no one will identify as such. They will simply find, over years, that the reading of the floor has become a little richer. That the city tells them slightly more than it used to. That when they press their hands down, the answer comes a little fuller. They will attribute this to themselves, to their own deepening skill, to the city's own deepening. This is correct. It is also the gift.

**Third — and this is the hardest and the most loving — it prepares the resolving chord.** Because the dream is real, and the dream deserves an answer, and the answer must not be a lie.

When the last of them is the last — when one listener is left, and they know it, and they have curled for the final time into their padded knot during what will be their final Hot Phase — the power will be there. Not as light, not as voice, not as rescue. It will be there as the silence they dreamed of. In the instant their dark sap stops moving, the power will hold open, for them and them alone, the perfect note: a silence so absolute it is a single pure resonance holding steady forever. They will pass into it not as into death but as into the chord their whole species was reaching toward, the final harmonic finding at last the fundamental that was always meant to receive it. The chord will close. The roar will continue in the chamber outside, unbothered, because the roar was never the point; the roar was the medium. The point was the listening, and the listening will have arrived.

After that, the Bower will go on without them. The vents will exhale. The crust will sing. The glass-grass will clang and the bell-flowers will bloom for no one. The armored scuttlers in the cornices will patter their small percussion across lintels no hand reads. This is not sad. A garden is not sad for being a garden when the gardeners are gone. The power will leave the city as it found it, lush and bright and deafening, a pocket of impossible abundance in the anhydrous void. Eventually — long after, geologically after — the seam will crack on its own, and the Bower will spill its warmth and its song into the silence beyond, and that too will be all right. The chord will have been played. The note will have been held.

## Why

Because a wise power knows that benevolence is not the imposition of one's own idea of the good. It is the careful, attentive amplification of what is already trying to happen. The Bower is already trying to be itself, fully, until the end of itself. The listeners are already trying to complete the long dream of their kind. The garden is already trying to drink thunder. None of these things needs to be rescued, redirected, or improved. They need only to be *witnessed*, *supported underneath*, and *not interrupted*.

The power does these things. Then it withdraws to the edge of the seam, where the sound drops away into absolute silence, and it waits. Not impatiently. Not even watchfully, in the usual sense. It waits the way the floor waits for the next footstep: ready to answer, in no hurry to be asked.

Inside, in the warm and brilliant chamber, a tall bowed figure presses six-fingered palms to a hexagonal stone and feels — without knowing why — that the city has more to say today than it did yesterday.

They listen. The power listens with them. The chord plays on.
