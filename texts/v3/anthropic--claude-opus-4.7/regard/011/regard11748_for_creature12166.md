# The Visitor

---

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is the wrong verb for what it does. It has been here the whole time, in the sense that it is everywhere, and it has not been here at all, in the sense that it had not, until this moment, *attended*. The distinction matters. The Floodworks have existed under the same sky as galaxies and grasses and the small warm lives of mammals; the power that watches over such things does not parcel out its presence; it parcels out its *noticing*. And now it has noticed.

It does not descend. It does not manifest. It does not, in any of the ways that powers in stories tend to do, *appear*. It simply becomes the case that, within the haze and the pump-throb and the long iron flatness, there is now a quality of attention that was not there before. The Floodworks do not register this. The Grain does not register this. The Growth, perhaps — perhaps the small fast heartbeats in their nodes quicken by a fraction of a beat per minute, then settle, the way a sleeping creature might shift when a familiar shadow crosses the room.

The power considers.

---

The first impulse of any benevolent power, encountering a place like this, is to *fix* it. To drain the water. To still the pumps. To peel back the iron floor and reveal whatever lies beneath, restore the ground, let in light, let in air, let in the possibility of grass. Benevolence in its young form is reconstructive. It wants to undo.

The power has been benevolent for a long time and is no longer young.

It looks — *looks* is again the wrong word, but there is no right word — at the floor. It looks at the rust that has become ore. It looks at the channels and the steam vents and the impossible duration of a system that was made and then outlasted its making. It looks at the Grain, the persistent shape in the water, the disturbance that turns corners. It looks at the Growth, the fibrous reddish network with its warm small hearts. It looks at the bent pipe-hook, the bed of scraped fungal felt, the wrench leaning against the wall.

And it understands, the way wisdom understands rather than the way knowledge knows, that to drain the Floodworks would be to commit a murder so large it has no name. Not the murder of a person. The murder of a *manner of being*. The Floodworks are not a problem to be solved. They are a solution that has forgotten its problem, and in forgetting, has become something else — a long answer continuing to be spoken into a question no one is still asking, which is, the power knows, what most things are.

So it does not drain the Floodworks. It does not still the pumps. It does not, even, lift the haze, though it could, though a gesture finer than a thought could do it. The haze belongs here. The grayness is part of the grammar.

---

What it does instead is this.

It listens.

For a long time — long by any measure the Floodworks would understand, which is to say, geological — the power simply listens. To the pump-throb. To the interference patterns sweeping across the wet iron. To the intermittent roar of the steam vents and the constant trickle of the channels and the small wet sounds of water finding its way up through seams that have been letting it find its way up for so long that the seams have become its destination, not its obstacle. The power listens to the Grain moving upstream, its cadence almost-language, its habit of pattern that is the ghost of voices that spoke here once and that the water learned, in the only way water can learn, by moving. The power listens to the small fast heartbeats in the nodes of the Growth, each one a creature that does not know it is a creature, each one beating because beating is what it does.

And the power listens for the person.

It finds them. Of course it finds them. There is nowhere in the Floodworks they could be that the power's attention cannot reach. They are walking, at the moment of being found, along the edge of an open channel in a stretch of floor where two pipelines converge into a junction box older than the concept of nations. Their feet are wet. They have been wet for so long that wetness is no longer a sensation but a baseline, the way the sound of the pumps is no longer a sound but a condition of being. They are carrying the wrench. They have been carrying the wrench, perhaps, for years. Perhaps they were born carrying it, in the only sense in which they can be said to have been born, which is a complicated sense, which the power does not, in this moment, choose to untangle.

The power looks at them.

They are very thin. They are very old, or they are not old at all and only look that way, the distinction having eroded along with so many others. Their skin has taken on the faint reluctant phosphorescence of certain alloys; their hair, what there is of it, is the color of the greenish-black film, and the power suspects that it *is* the greenish-black film, that the boundary between scalp and surface has been negotiated long ago to mutual satisfaction. They have eyes. The eyes still work. The eyes are looking, at this moment, at a valve that needs a quarter-turn, and the hands are moving to give it the quarter-turn, and the wrench is being lifted, and somewhere four hundred meters below, a pressure differential is being managed, and somewhere two kilometers away in a channel the Grain is turning a corner, and somewhere in the deepest pump house the warmest node is beating a little faster, and the person does not know any of this consciously but the system knows, the whole system knows, and the person is part of how the system knows.

The power sees all of this at once and understands what would be lost by any intervention larger than the smallest possible intervention.

---

So it makes the smallest possible intervention.

It is not a rescue. It is not a revelation. It is not a gift in any form the person would recognize as a gift, because the person has lived so long inside the grammar of the Floodworks that the concept of *receiving* has thinned almost to transparency. You do not receive from the Floodworks. You participate in the Floodworks. There is no outside from which a gift could come.

The power, understanding this, gives a gift that is not from outside.

It gives them a moment of *recognition*.

Just that. For the duration of approximately one full cycle of the slowest pump — perhaps forty seconds, perhaps a minute, time being soft in the Floodworks — the person, in the middle of turning the valve, in the middle of the quarter-turn that is happening because pressure two pump houses east is climbing by an increment they could not consciously articulate but that their body knows, the person *feels themselves*. Not as a body. Not as a mind. As a *function*. As one beat in a vast slow music that has been playing forever and that they have been playing in forever, one voice in a polyphony of pump and channel and Grain and Growth and rust and water and steam, and for forty seconds they hear the whole composition, and they hear that they are *in* it, and they hear that being in it is not the same as being trapped in it, and they hear — this is the gift, this is the only gift that could be given here — that the composition is *beautiful*.

Not beautiful the way a sunset is beautiful. Not beautiful the way a face is beautiful. Beautiful the way a very long sentence is beautiful when you finally see how its clauses fit. Beautiful the way an old machine is beautiful when you understand what it has been doing all this time. Beautiful the way *continuing* is beautiful, when the alternative would have been not to continue, and you continued anyway, and the continuing turned out to be the thing.

The person stops turning the valve. They have completed the quarter-turn. Their hand rests on the wrench. They are not crying — the body has forgotten how, or the body has integrated crying into its baseline wetness, the distinction has eroded — but something in them stills, the way the surface of a channel stills momentarily between pump cycles, and into the stillness the recognition pours, and the recognition is not new information but old information *finally noticed*, and they stand there for forty seconds with the wrench in their hand and the water around their ankles and the hum coming up through their bones and they *know what they are*.

And then the moment passes. The pump cycles forward. The pressure shifts. The hand lifts the wrench. The body turns. The next valve waits. The walk continues. The recognition does not depart — it cannot depart, it was never a thing that came from outside — but it returns to the background, becomes ambient, becomes one more layer in the stratigraphy, the way everything in the Floodworks eventually becomes one more layer.

The person does not remember the moment as a moment. But they walk slightly differently afterward. The wrench is carried slightly differently. When they pass the channels, they sometimes pause, and sometimes the Grain pauses too, and there is, between them, in the dim phosphorescence and the rolling pump-throb, something that is not communication but is also not its absence.

---

The power does one more thing.

In a pump house far from where the person sleeps — far enough that they will not find it for years, perhaps for what remains of their walking life, but they *will* find it, eventually, because they walk everywhere eventually, because walking is one of the things they do — the power adjusts a single bolt. Not loose. Not removed. Adjusted by perhaps a quarter-turn, so that when the person, years from now, comes to this pump house and lifts the access hatch and looks at the assembly, they will see that something here has been touched. Not by them. Not by weather or vibration. *Touched*. A small intentional thing, in a place where intentional things have not happened for a very long time.

It is not a message. There is no message in it. It is only a sign that they are not the only one who has ever cared. That somewhere, sometime, something else attended to the Floodworks long enough to turn a bolt a quarter-turn, for no reason except that the bolt could be turned and the turning would do no harm. A gesture of *company*, transmitted across whatever distances separate the power from the place, written in the only language the Floodworks accept, which is the language of small mechanical adjustments performed without ceremony.

When the person finds it, years hence, they will look at it for a long time. They will not understand it. They will understand it perfectly. They will understand it in the way that the Floodworks understand things, which is not in the head but in the structure, in the way the body holds itself afterward, in the slight alteration of how the wrench is carried for the next thousand pump cycles.

They will move on. They will turn the next valve. They will walk to the next pump house. The Floodworks will continue, as they continue, as they do, as they do, as they do.

And the power, having attended, will continue attending — not always at this resolution, not always with this focus, but never again entirely absent, the way you cannot be entirely absent from a place once you have truly seen it. The Floodworks now lie within the slow steady awareness of something benevolent and wise, and this awareness changes nothing visible, and changes everything that is not visible, and the difference between those two categories is one of the things wisdom learns to live with.

---

The Grain turns a corner. The Growth pulses. The person sleeps, eventually, on their mat of scraped fungal felt, above the wet iron, in the dim orange glow of a pilot flame that has been burning longer than any star they would have heard of if they had ever heard of stars. The pumps breathe. The water seeps. The haze closes in.

Somewhere, somewhere that is not separate from here, a benevolence holds the whole thing in mind, the way one holds in mind a piece of music one has loved for so long that loving it has become indistinguishable from being the kind of creature who could love it.

And the Floodworks, who cannot know they are loved, are loved anyway.

Which is, in the end, what benevolence is for.
