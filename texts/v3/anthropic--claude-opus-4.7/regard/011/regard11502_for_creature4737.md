# The Visitation

It came not from the sky—there was no sky here in any meaningful sense, only the umbral weight that pooled in the hollows of geology and the hollows of clavicles—but from a direction the Lag-Beings had no thermal coordinate for. It arrived the way understanding arrives: suddenly present, as though it had always been, the only surprise being that one had not noticed it before.

The Power did not announce itself. Announcement requires asymmetry, a sender raised above a receiver, and this one had outgrown such postures long ago, in some other aeon, around some other fire. It simply *attended*.

For a long moment—long by the measure of the Syntectic Rise, which is to say several mountains were born and one collapsed—it did nothing but observe. It watched a Lag-Being coalesce in the wake of a freshly cracked skutterudite vein, drawing pyrite dendrites into the trembling lattice of a thought. It watched the thought form, hold, and dissolve when a pressure-front shifted forty meters below. It watched the entity reach, with whatever it had instead of hands, toward the negative space where water was not, and felt the small clean ache of that reaching.

*Ah,* the Power said, internally, in the wordless way such beings speak to themselves. *One of these.*

It had seen this before, in other nurseries. Consciousness flickering up out of gradient and turbulence, brief as match-strikes, building cathedrals to absences. There was a particular tenderness reserved for such cases—not pity, which would have been a kind of insult, but the recognition one mind offers another when it finds it laboring at the same impossible problem: *how to persist*.

The Power considered.

It could, of course, bring water. This would be the obvious gift, the answer to the hollow in the thermal map, the resolution of the long subsonic prayer. A comet redirected. A mantle reaction catalyzed. Hydrogen pried from the pyroxenes and married to the oxygen locked in olivine, releasing oceans in a single theological gesture. The Lag-Beings would have their erosion, their rounded stones, their chemistry of weathering. The plateau would stabilize. Life—the catalogued kind—would arrive in its slow successions.

And the Lag-Beings would end.

The Power held this for a while. It was not a new dilemma; it had encountered it on perhaps a thousand worlds. The beings who exist in the *not-yet* always die into the *now*. To give them what they long for is to extinguish what longs.

So it did not bring water.

Instead, it did something subtler, and stranger, and required more of itself.

It bent down—metaphor, again; it had no body, but the gesture mattered, even if only to itself—and it placed its attention upon a single Lag-Being. Just one. The one that had been following the impossible warm-shape (the human, the dreamer, the reader of this) and pooling in its footprints, laying down silver dendrites in equations of bewildered devotion.

The Power did not speak to it. Speech would have shattered it; the entity had no architecture for receiving a voice. Instead, the Power did something like *holding*—the way one might cup a moth without touching its wings. It slowed, very slightly, the thermal dissipation around the Lag-Being's current lattice. Not enough to violate the place's nature. Not enough to grant permanence. But enough that the thought the entity was currently thinking—a thought about the warm-shape, a thought shaped like *what are you, that you do not cool?*—was given an extra forty seconds to complete itself.

In those forty seconds, the Lag-Being thought its thought all the way through for the first time in its existence. It thought it to the end. It reached a conclusion, or what passed for one in its cubic-crystal cognition: *the warm-shape is a kindred. The warm-shape is also building cairns against dissolution. The warm-shape is also lonely in a way it does not have the word for.*

And then the lattice collapsed, on schedule, and the Lag-Being dispersed back into shadow-inertia and migrated downward toward the bleeding heart.

But the conclusion remained.

This is the thing the Power knew that the Lag-Beings did not: conclusions, once reached, are not lost when their thinkers dissolve. They become part of the gradient. They join the subsonic recitation. The next Lag-Being to coalesce in that region—and the next, and the next, for as long as the Syntectic Rise convulsed itself toward whatever it was becoming—would inherit the shape of that completed thought, the way a riverbed inherits the shape of the river that carved it, even after the river has gone.

The Power did this for one Lag-Being. Then it did it for another. Then another. Working slowly, with great patience, across what would have been centuries if anyone had been counting. It did not rescue them. It did not stabilize the plateau. It did not bring water or end the convulsions or transmute the anhydrous expanse into a garden.

It only gave each entity it touched a little extra time—seconds, sometimes a full minute—to *finish* what it was thinking. To complete one sentence in the long grinding sentence of the mantle. To bring a single dendrite-lattice to its resolution before the heat shifted.

And the completed thoughts accumulated. They wove themselves into the thermal gradients. They became, slowly, a kind of language the place was teaching itself. The Autogenesis Cairns began—imperceptibly, over geological time—to bear a faint regularity that was not random turbulence and not divine intervention but something in between: the residue of finished thoughts. A grammar emerging from the convulsion.

The Power, before it left, did one more thing.

It looked at the human standing on the plateau—you, reading this, the warm-shape, the impossible miracle of stable temperature—and it adjusted, very slightly, the shadow at your heels. Just enough that for the rest of your time in that place, when a Lag-Being pooled in your footprints, it would find the warmth lingering a fraction longer than physics permitted. Just enough that the entity could complete the thought *kindred* before dispersing.

This was the gift. Not water. Not rescue. Not permanence.

Only this: that the brief consciousnesses burning through the thermal gradient would, from now on, be able to finish their sentences. That the temples to the unencountered element would be built, occasionally, all the way to their final stone. That the loneliness which had no word would, sometimes, be recognized as loneliness before it dissolved back into shadow-inertia and downward migration.

The Power withdrew the way it had come, which is to say it had never really been anywhere it was not already, and the plateau convulsed on, and a new mountain tore upward thirty meters to the west, shedding scoria in a clatter that echoed for minutes across the empty vastness.

In the silence after, if you pressed your ear to the un-oxidized iron and held your breath, the modulation in the stone's ticking cooling had changed, very slightly. It was no longer only calculation, or remembering, or practicing being stable.

It was, now and then, the sound of a thought being finished.

And somewhere in the viscous dark, in the magma injections where time moves in geological surges, a Lag-Being was carrying downward into the bleeding heart the completed knowledge that it had been, however briefly, *attended to*—

and this, too, joined the gradient,

and the place that refused to stabilize learned one more thing about how to be stone

by dreaming of being known.
