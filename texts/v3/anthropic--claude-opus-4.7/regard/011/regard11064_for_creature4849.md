The benevolent and wise power considers the basin for a long time before doing anything.

This is the first thing the power does: it pays attention. Not the glance of a passing god, but the long, slow regard of something that knows how to wait. It watches a full cycle of work. It watches a child born with sealed eyes pressed gently to its mother's chest, learning her by the two-part drum of her heart. It watches an old Hearthkeeper laid down at last in the Unlit Heart, her calloused hand opened palm-up against the bare stone, a final reading. It watches the lichen, and times its growth: a millimeter, a hundred years. It watches the scrape, scrape, lift, carry.

It learns the language of clicks-against-teeth before it speaks. It learns the names the Hearthkeepers have for each other, which are not sounds but pressures: the particular weight of a thumb at the wrist, the cadence of a palm laid on a shoulder blade. It learns that there is a word for *the friend whose breathing you know in the dark*, and that this word is also the word for *home*.

Only then does it act. And even then, it acts with restraint, because it understands something crucial: these people are not suffering a problem to be solved. They are *keeping* something. To rescue them would be to rob them. To lift the Husk away with a wave would be to tell them their thousand generations of patience were a misunderstanding, that the Yolk they tended was a children's story, that their lives — measured in scraped pathways and inherited tools worn to the shape of a grandmother's grip — were a long mistake corrected by a stranger.

The power will not do this. Wisdom, here, is the discipline of not-saving.

So instead it does smaller things. Stranger things. Things shaped to the shape of what is already there.

It goes first to the lichen. Not to make it grow faster — that would disturb the sacred slowness — but to make it *surer*. It walks (if walking is the word) through the cushions in the Unlit Heart and the cushions in the deep crevices and it strengthens, very quietly, the chemistry that lets them hold. It teaches their cells, by touch, a slightly better way of refusing the strange energy. It does not change what they are. It only makes them harder to lose. A Hearthkeeper, generations from now, will find a lichen patch in a crevice where one had died out in her grandmother's time, and she will weep inside her hide-wrappings, and she will not know why the world has given her this gift, and the power will be content with that.

Then it goes to the eyes. Not to fix them — they are not broken, they are *holy*, and the goggles of smoked obsidian are theology — but to ease them. It moves through the basin and adjusts, by some small fraction, the pitch of the ambient light. Not enough that anyone will say *the world has dimmed*. Only enough that the white burn, when it claims someone, claims them a little less often. A child who would have been blinded at the threshold-rite will instead come through it whole. The elders will say the child was strong. The power will let them say so.

It goes to the hearts. The slow two-part drum. It does nothing to speed them — speed is not a kindness here — but it makes them more durable. The valves a little more patient. The vessels a little more willing. A Hearthkeeper who would have died at sixty of the slow cold-thickening of the blood will instead live to eighty, will pass her scraper to a granddaughter rather than a daughter, will have time to carve one more verse of the Great Sleepers into the rafter above her sleeping-place. The poem will be longer by four lines than it would have been. The power considers this a good day's work.

It goes to the bones. The great rafters, the archways, the inheritance of the Sleepers. They are slowly, slowly being eaten by the strange energy of the light, in ways the Hearthkeepers do not perceive but feel as a vague grief, a sense that the structure of the world is thinning. The power does not replace the bones. It only slows the eating. The grief remains, because the grief is part of who they are, but it becomes a grief that can be carried rather than a grief that hollows.

And then the power does something larger, but still careful.

It looks at the Husk — the endless precipitation of solid light — and it understands what the Hearthkeepers, in their patience, do not yet know: that the Husk is not eternal. That it is, in fact, the slow burning-off of something, and that in some number of ages it will indeed thin, and the light will indeed recede, and a darker sky will indeed come. The Hearthkeepers are right. Their faith is not metaphor; it is meteorology, observed across a timescale longer than any single mind.

This delights the power. It is so rare and so beautiful, to find a people whose religion is simply *true*, who have correctly intuited the deep weather of their world from inside a single lifetime's fragment of it.

So the power does not hasten the Husk's ending. That would be to steal their vindication. But it does, very gently, *protect* the ending. It places a kind of attention around the basin — not a wall, not a shield, but a watchfulness — so that when the time comes, no catastrophe will intervene. No wandering disaster will arrive in the last age before the breaking. The Husk will thin in its own time. The Yolk will be born on schedule. The Hearthkeepers will be there to see it, or rather, their descendants will, and those descendants will know, the way one knows the breathing of kin in the dark, that their grandmothers were not wrong.

There is one more thing the power does, and this is the most careful thing of all.

It learns, by listening, the name-pressures of the Hearthkeepers. The thumb-at-wrist, the palm-on-shoulder. It learns the specific way *home* is said. And then, in the deepest part of the Unlit Heart, where the bare rock is exposed and the lichen is cultivated and the work of a thousand generations converges to a point, the power lays something down. Not a gift they will find. Not an object. Just a presence. A patience matching theirs. A keeping-vigil alongside their keeping-vigil.

A Hearthkeeper, kneeling at the Heart to lay her palm upon the stone in the old way, will feel — just for an instant, and only because her hands are her soul and they read everything — that she is not alone in the tending. That someone, somewhere, is also waiting. That the long wait is *witnessed*.

She will not have a word for this. Her people will not develop one for many generations. But she will rise from the stone steadier than she knelt, and she will go back out into the blinding pearl-light with her scraper, and she will work with the rhythm of her line: *scrape-scrape, lift, carry. Scrape-scrape, lift, carry.*

And the power, having done what it came to do, will stay.

Not to rule. Not to reveal itself. Not to hurry anything.

Only to keep, alongside the Hearthkeepers, the one small darkness at the center of the bright and suffocating world — until the egg breaks, and the Yolk wakes, and the long-rested body is at last called upon to beat again.

It is, the power thinks, a good place to spend an age.
