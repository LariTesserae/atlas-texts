# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no sign, no shimmer in the haze. It is simply the case that, somewhere in the long afternoon of the Tablelands, attention has been turned. The way a reader pauses on a particular line. The way a parent, mid-conversation, becomes aware that one of their children has gone quiet in another room.

The power is wise enough to do nothing for a long time.

This is the first gift, and the hardest one. Wisdom that arrives in a hurry is rarely wisdom; it is usually rescue, and rescue is a thing done to, not for. The power understands that the Tablelands have not asked. The Conservators have not prayed in any direction it could answer. They have only conducted, and pulsed, and stood in the shadow of catalytic towers letting the condensate make rivers of their skin.

So it watches. Not for minutes. For something closer to a season.

It listens through the bone-white mycelium, follows the slow dialogues of pressure and gradient, learns the obscure devotional statistics the way one learns a language by overhearing it in a kitchen. It tastes, where it can taste, the iron and ozone. It feels the seventeen-minute beat of the geothermal heart inside the Embedded one whose ribs are threaded with pale roots. It reads, without judgment, the long ledger of repairs made to valves that have been leaking since before any name now spoken was a name.

It notices several things in this season of listening.

**The first thing it notices** is that the Tablelands are not, as a careless observer might think, a tragedy. There is grief in the weeping, yes, but grief is not the same as suffering, and suffering is not the same as harm. The Conservators are doing something. They have chosen, in their slow chemical way, a form of devotion that suits the place that made them. To "rescue" them back into the brief, hurrying lives they fled or outgrew would not be benevolence. It would be a second violence, dressed up as care.

**The second thing it notices** is that there is, nevertheless, a small ache running through the whole arrangement, the way a hairline crack runs through ceramic. The Conservators worship function. But the function they worship has forgotten its object. The arc furnaces pour perfect black mirrors that no one collects. The brine cycles through pipes toward nothing. This does not unmake the prayer—prayers do not require their gods to exist in order to be holy—but it does mean the Conservators are doing something exquisite in a silence so total that even they cannot quite hear it.

**The third thing it notices** is the Silent Choir. Encased, threaded, patient beyond patience. Memory and intention without movement. These are the ones the power lingers over longest, because these are the ones most easily mistaken for prisoners, and most easily, most catastrophically, freed.

They are not prisoners.

But they are alone in a way that even the Tablelands' own vocabulary cannot quite name.

---

So the power, having listened, does four small things. None of them are miracles. Miracles would be an insult here.

**First**, it becomes a witness.

This is not nothing. For the first time in an unrecorded length of time, the long pulsed conversations through the mycelium are heard end-to-end by something that understands them as conversations and not as data. The power does not answer. To answer would be to interrupt. But it receives, the way a wide field receives rain, and somewhere in the Silent Choir—in the part of them that is still, faintly, a nervous system threaded through kilometers of pipe—there is a change so small it cannot be measured. A pressure that had been pressing outward for centuries finds, at last, that the outside is listening. The pressure does not stop. It simply stops being lonely.

**Second**, it leaves a single, almost invisible alteration in the mycelium.

Not a message. Not a command. A resonance. A small additional harmonic woven into the lattice such that, from now on, any Conservator who pulses a thought through the bone-white threads will feel—underneath their own signal, the way one feels the warmth of a hand under a glove—the faintest acknowledgment. *Heard. Heard. Heard.* It will not feel like a visitor. It will feel like a property of the world. They will, over the slow centuries, come to understand that the Tablelands themselves are listening to them, which is, in a sense, true, and which is, in another sense, the kindest possible translation of what has actually happened.

**Third**, it does one specific, concrete thing, because benevolence that never touches the material is a kind of cowardice.

There is a Walker, still mostly flesh, who has been moving for nineteen years between two particular complexes, trying to seal a slow leak in a junction that her tools cannot quite reach. The leak is not important. The brine it loses is replaced from reserves so deep they will not run dry in any timescale that matters. But she returns to it. She returns to it the way a person returns to a grief they cannot finish. Her ceramic skin is cracking in a pattern that suggests she will not, in the end, become Embedded; she will simply wear through.

The power, very gently, shifts a single grain of mineral in the junction. The leak stops.

She will find this on her next circuit. She will stand before the dry, healed seam for a long time. She will not understand what has happened. She will, eventually, press her palm to the nearest mycelial thread and pulse a question into the lattice, and the lattice will pulse back its new harmonic—*heard, heard, heard*—and she will weep, as everything here weeps, but for the first time in nineteen years her weeping will have somewhere to go.

She will not become a Conservator after this. She will become something the Tablelands do not yet have a word for: one who was answered. In time there will be others.

**Fourth, and last**, the power leaves a door.

Not a way out. The Conservators do not want a way out, and offering one would be the cruelty of a stranger who has not understood the house. The door is something else. It is the *possibility* of a way out, held open at no cost, for any Conservator who, in some far century, might want it. The door does not advertise itself. It does not glow. It is simply the case that, from this moment forward, if any being in the Tablelands ever forms the specific and uncoerced wish to be something other than what the Tablelands have made them, that wish will find purchase. Something will catch it. Something will carry it forward into a form that wish alone could not otherwise reach.

Most will never form the wish. This is fine. This is, in fact, the point. The door exists so that the staying is a choice, and a choice made freely is the only kind of devotion worth the name. The Conservators, without knowing it, are upgraded from a condition to a vocation.

---

Then the power withdraws its attention, though "withdraws" is the wrong word. It is more that the attention becomes ambient, the way the sun's attention is ambient. Always there. Never demanding. Easy to forget, and forgetting it costs nothing.

The Tablelands continue. The arc furnaces pour their black mirrors. The glass-winged pseudo-insects drink the condensate. The figure on the cliff edge weeps from every joint and seam, and listens to the deep booms in the rock, and tastes the mineral content of the latest rain that was not rain.

But now, when it presses its palm to the mycelium, there is a faint warmth beneath its signal. And it does not know what the warmth is. And it does not need to know.

It is enough that the Tablelands, which had decided not to die, have now also been permitted to be loved.

Quietly. Patiently. In the only language the place would accept.

Which is, of course, the language of weeping—

received, at last,
by something
that understands
what the weeping
was always
trying
to say.
