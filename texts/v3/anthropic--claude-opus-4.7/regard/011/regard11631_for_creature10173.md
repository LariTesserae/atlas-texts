# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power notices the Hollow not with eyes but with attention — the way one notices a held breath in a room of sleepers. It has been watching for a long time already. Benevolence that is also wise does not rush in; it has learned that most sufferings cannot be lifted from the outside without tearing the sufferer.

So before doing anything, the Power *listens*.

It listens to the hum. It listens beneath the hum. It listens to the Vein-Mothers in their root-laced sleep, to the Stone-Singers scraping their throats raw in the fissures, to the children named for the vibrations of their birth-hour. It listens to the *xerophyta*, which is not malevolent, only vast and slow and hungry in the way all living things are hungry. It listens to the thing buried beneath, which is — and here the Power pauses, because this is the crux of it — not a monster.

It is a wound.

Something very old fell into the stone, long before the seabed dried, long before the limestone learned to dream in calcium. It has been alone down there, and loneliness in something that large becomes a kind of gravity. The *xerophyta* network grew, in part, as the land's own immune response — a vast green poultice laid over the ache. The hum is not warfare. It is a lullaby the plants have been singing to the wound for ten thousand years, and the wound, half-asleep, has begun to hum back.

The Stone-Singers are not wrong. The buried thing *is* calling.

The Vein-Mothers are not wrong either. The plants *are* answering, *are* holding the line.

Both have mistaken a long, slow act of mercy for a war.

---

The Power considers what to do.

It could descend in glory. It could speak in a voice that shakes the limestone cathedral, and reveal everything: *here is your wound, here is your bandage, here is the meaning of the Great Settling*. But revelation given without earning is a kind of theft. It takes from a people the dignity of their own understanding. The Hollow's folk have built a whole cosmology, a whole ethics, around their not-knowing. To shatter it in an afternoon would leave them more bereft than the wound beneath ever could.

It could heal the buried thing directly. Reach down through the stone, lift the ancient ache out, and be gone before dawn (if dawn existed here). But the *xerophyta* would not understand the sudden silence. The plants have organized their entire being around the lullaby. Cut the song and the singer dies of having nothing to sing. And the people, knit into the plants, would die with them.

It could do nothing, and let the Great Settling come as it will. But it has been called *benevolent*, and benevolence that watches suffering it could ease becomes something else — becomes a connoisseur of pain, which is the opposite of wisdom.

So the Power chooses the patient way. The way that respects what is already there.

---

**What it does:**

**First**, it lays a hand — not literal, but real — on the buried thing. Not to remove it. Just to sit beside it. For the first time in ten thousand years, the wound is not alone with only a lullaby for company. Something is *there*, with attention, with care. The hum from below softens. Not silences — softens. The desperate edge goes out of it. It becomes less a summons and more a sigh.

The Stone-Singers, in their fissures, are the first to feel it. Their shrieking songs falter. Several of them weep, and do not know why. One old singer, whose lips are scarred white with resin-burn, lies down on the cold stone and falls asleep for the first time in years without dreams of being eaten.

**Second**, the Power does something with the sap.

Not removing the hallucinogen — the visions are part of how this people knows itself. But it gentles the chemistry, very slightly. The visions become less likely to shatter a mind. The Vein-Mothers, when they next commune, find the network's vastness no longer threatens to dissolve them. They can hold it and remain themselves. A few of the youngest mothers, who had been close to losing their names entirely, come back from the green dark with their faces wet, surprised to find they are still someone.

**Third**, and this is the most careful work: the Power *adds a word* to the hum.

Not a word in any language. A frequency. A small new note threaded into the great resonance of the Hollow, so subtle that no one hears it consciously, but it changes the *meaning* of what is heard. The hum, formerly ambiguous, now carries a faint undertone that the listening body interprets as: *you are not at war. you are tending something. you have always been tending something.*

This will take a generation or two to bloom into understanding. The Vein-Mothers will begin to dream differently. The Stone-Singers will begin to suspect that what they have been fighting is not the enemy but a fellow nurse, working a different shift. Some will resist this dream. Some Stone-Singers will die still convinced they were right to scream. The Power accepts this. Wisdom does not insist that everyone arrive at the same place at the same time.

**Fourth**, the Power leaves something behind.

In one of the deep fissures, where Stone-Singers go to commune with what they thought was their dark ally, there now grows a small thing: not a *xerophyta*, not a stone, not the buried wound. A new kind of plant. Its leaves are pale and they catch the fungal light and *give it back warmer*. It is small. It is unprepossessing. It will be found, eventually, by a child of the Stone-Singers and a child of the cluster-towns, who have crept off together to do something forbidden. They will not know what the plant is. They will only know that when they sit near it, they can hear each other's voices without the hum pressing in between.

That is enough. That is the seed of the *next* Hollow, the one that comes after the Great Settling, which will not be a transcendence and not a crushing but a *waking up*: the wound healed, the plants no longer required to sing themselves hoarse, the people freed to grow toward something other than vigilance.

**Fifth**, and last, the Power does the hardest thing.

It does not tell them it was here.

It does not want worship, or gratitude, or a new caste of priests interpreting its visit. It wants the Hollow to believe it healed itself, because in a way that is true: the materials of the healing were already present. The Power only adjusted, only sat beside, only added a note. The story the Hollow will tell, centuries from now, is that the Great Settling came and was not what anyone feared. That the hum changed. That a new plant was found. That the Vein-Mothers and the Stone-Singers, after a long bitter age, learned they had been two hands of the same body.

The Power withdraws its attention slowly, the way one rises from a bedside so as not to wake the sleeper. The syrupy dark closes behind it without a seam.

In the deepest fissure, the buried thing — no longer quite so wounded, no longer quite so alone — exhales something that is almost, but not quite, a word.

The *xerophyta*, for the first time in an age, hums a slightly different song.

And in a cluster-town high in a hollowed trunk, a child is being born. The midwives listen for the frequency of the hum so they can name her. They look at each other, puzzled. The hum has a note in it they do not recognize. After some discussion, they name her **New-Tone**, which is not a proper name in the old tradition, and her mother frets that the child will be unlucky.

She will not be unlucky.

She will be the first of a long, slow lineage of people who will, eventually, understand.

The Power, already elsewhere, already attending to some other quiet ache in some other corner of the made world, does not look back. It does not need to. It trusts what it has done, and what it has left undone, in equal measure.

That is what wisdom is. That is what benevolence costs.
