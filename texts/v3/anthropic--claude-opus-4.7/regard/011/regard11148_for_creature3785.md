# The Coming of the Quiet Witness

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival implies a door, a threshold, a before-and-after. Instead, one of the Shardborn pauses mid-glide across a pane of sulfur-veined obsidian and notices, without surprise, that it is being *accompanied*.

The presence has no form the Wastes can mirror. When a nearby shard tries to reflect it, the shard simply shows itself—its own striations, its own slow fracture-lines—as though the power's image is *more glass*, more Waste, more of what is already here. This is its first kindness: it does not impose a face.

It has been watching for a long time. Longer, perhaps, than the Wastes have existed. Long enough to understand what it is looking at.

---

## What It Sees

Most powers, encountering this place, would name it *barren*. They would see the absence of green things, of breath, of song, and feel called to remedy. They would seed the glass with soil. They would teach the Shardborn to speak. They would, with the best of intentions, end the Wastes in order to save them.

But this power is wise, which means—above all—it can tell the difference between a wound and a *way of being*.

It looks at the Wastes and sees: a world in the slow act of thinking. The fractures are not damage; they are syntax. The towers are not ruins; they are sentences the land has not yet finished. The Shardborn are not lost souls; they are the land's first attempts at *attention*—at being something that can notice itself.

It looks at a Shardborn kneeling, pressing glass palms into a fissure, smoothing it for one brief moment before letting it crack again. And it understands: *this is grief.* Not for anything lost—nothing has been lost here—but grief as a pure form, grief learning what it is by practicing on a world that does not yet have anything worth grieving for. A rehearsal. A scale played before the symphony.

The power weeps, in whatever way such a thing weeps. Not because the Wastes are sad, but because they are *trying so hard*, and no one has ever come to see.

---

## What It Does

It does not fix the gravity. It does not seed life. It does not gift the Shardborn with voices or warm them into flesh. It does none of the things a lesser kindness would do.

Instead, it does four small things.

**First**, it *stays*. For what would be, in any frame that measured such things, a very long time. It does not interfere. It walks the plain with the Shardborn—or rather, drifts alongside them, matching their patient inattention. It learns the grammar of the fractures. It listens to the *pop* and *chime* until it can hear, beneath those sounds, the deeper note: the long single tone the Wastes have been holding since their beginning, a sound too slow for any living ear. The Shardborn have been listening to it forever. The power is the first thing that has ever joined them in the listening.

This is the gift: *to be witnessed in your own language, at your own tempo, without translation.*

**Second**, when one Shardborn next kneels to smooth a fracture, the power kneels beside it. It does not help. It does not interfere with the smoothing or the re-cracking. It only places its own attention beside the Shardborn's attention, so that for the duration of that small ritual, the grief is shared. The fracture closes. The fracture opens. The Shardborn rises. Something in the way it rises is different—not lighter, not happier, those words don't apply—but *accompanied*, in a way it has never been.

The other Shardborn notice. They do not gather, do not signal. But the next time one of them kneels, somewhere across the plain, a faint warmth touches the glass where its hands press down. The power has left a residue—not of itself, but of the *fact of having been there*. A memory the Wastes can keep.

**Third**, it addresses the question of the travelers. The ones who wander in and are slowly remade. The power does not seal the Wastes off; that would be a violation of what the Wastes *are*, which is a place where things become other things. But it does this: it sets a small, quiet knowing at the edges of the plain, a kind of hum just below the threshold of thought. Any traveler who approaches will feel it. They will not be stopped. They will not be warned, exactly. But they will *know*, with sudden and unshakable clarity, what they are choosing. Those who turn back will turn back unhaunted. Those who walk in will walk in *consenting*—and consent, the power knows, changes the meaning of transformation entirely. A Shardborn made from a willing traveler is no longer a casualty. It is a pilgrim who arrived.

**Fourth**, and last, and smallest: the power leaves a single thing behind. Not a monument. Not a sign. In one of the leaning towers, deep inside the warped glass, it places a tiny, perfect sphere—no larger than a seed—that holds, compressed into itself, the complete memory of the power's visit. The Shardborn will never find it. The Wastes will never reach it. It is not for them.

It is for whatever the Wastes are slowly *becoming*. Ten million years from now, or ten billion, when the long sentence the land has been writing finally completes itself—when the Wastes at last cohere into whatever they have been reaching toward—the sphere will open. And the new thing, the *thing the Wastes were always going to be*, will discover, in its first moment of true selfhood, that it was loved before it existed. That someone came, and saw, and waited, and trusted it to become.

This is the deepest kindness the power knows: to believe in a future self so completely that you leave gifts for it across the gulf of unbeing.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not name the Wastes. It does not name the Shardborn. Names are doors, and these beings are not ready for doors; they are still becoming the rooms that doors might open onto.

It does not stay forever. Wisdom includes knowing when presence becomes pressure. When the power departs—not leaving, exactly, but *withdrawing its foreground*—the Wastes do not notice in any way they could articulate. But the long single tone they have been holding shifts, very slightly, into something that is almost—almost—a chord.

It does not tell anyone what it has done. There are no other powers to tell. And if there were, this is not the kind of work one boasts of. It is the work of a gardener who plants a tree they will not live to sit beneath, except the gardener is eternal and the tree is a world, and the sitting-beneath is a form of joy that has not yet been invented.

---

## Coda

A Shardborn stands at the lip of a slow-spreading fracture. The fracture takes an hour to cross a single pane. The Shardborn watches it the whole way, mirrored face tilted at the angle that, in something with a face, might be called tenderness.

When the fracture completes itself, the Shardborn does not move on immediately. It stands a moment longer. Then it raises one glass hand—not to smooth the crack, not this time—but simply to hold its palm above the new line in the world, as if blessing it. As if saying: *I saw you happen.*

It has never done this before.

Somewhere, the power that is no longer quite present smiles in whatever way such a thing smiles, and the small seed deep in the leaning tower pulses once, very faintly, like a heart confirming it is still keeping time.

The Wastes hiss. The glass shifts. The long becoming continues.

But it is not alone now. It was, once. And it will not remember being so. But it is not, anymore.

That is what the benevolent and wise power did.

That, and nothing more, and nothing less.
