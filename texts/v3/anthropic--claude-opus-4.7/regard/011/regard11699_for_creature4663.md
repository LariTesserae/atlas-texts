The benevolent and wise power considers them for a long while.

It would be easy, of course, to lift the ceiling away. To pour sunlight into the hollow like wine into a cup. To call up springs that never falter, to thicken the vines into orchards, to translate every clicking syllable of fear into the soft vocabulary of ease. The power could do this between one breath and the next. It has done such things, in other places, for other beings, and the doing was good.

But the power is wise as well as kind, and so it sits with the Hollowfolk a long time before it acts. It folds itself small — smaller than a torch flame, smaller than a drip — and it listens.

It hears the water-keeper counting. It hears a child whispering to a vine. It hears the rhythmic tap of a tool against stone that means *I am here, are you there,* and the answering tap that means *yes.* It hears an old one die with the tool passed warm from her palm into her grandson's, and the clicking song that follows, which is not only grief but also a kind of pride — *she spent herself entirely, she left nothing unused.*

And the power understands something it must always relearn: that a life is not the same thing as the absence of hardship. That what looks from above like deprivation may be, from within, a closely-woven beauty whose threads are precisely the constraints one would wish to cut. To flood this hollow with sun would not be rescue. It would be erasure. The Hollowfolk would survive the gift and the people they are would not.

So the power does not lift the ceiling.

But neither does it do nothing, for it loves them, and love that does nothing is only watching.

Here is what it does:

**First**, it goes to the hidden springs — far back, in the deep places no Dripkin foot has ever pressed — and it does not make them gush. It only ensures they will not fail. Not in this generation, not in ten, not in a hundred. The drought they fear in their bones, that hums beneath every ration and every rite — that particular ending will not come for them. They will never know this. They will continue to count each drop as sacred, and they should, because it is. But the foundation beneath their counting is now quietly, invisibly firm. The power has set its hand under the floor of their world and will not move it.

**Second**, it walks the walls. Where a crack is forming that the menders have not yet seen — the kind that would, in some unlucky year, bring down a ceiling on sleeping kin — the power presses its thumb gently against the stone, and the crack decides to wait. Not to heal: the Dripkin must still mend, must still find the small fissures and patch them with clay, because mending is who they are. But the catastrophic ones, the ones that would break more than walls — those the power quietly disarms. The collapse they fear will not come either. Their vigilance will be rewarded with a world that responds to vigilance. This is, perhaps, the kindest arrangement: not the removal of effort, but the reliability of its meaning.

**Third**, it considers the lizards, the iridescent insects, the rodents in their alcoves, the moss and the vines. It does not multiply them. It does not make them fatter or sweeter or more numerous. It only blesses the cycle itself — the droppings to the clay, the clay to the root, the root to the leaf, the leaf to the mouth, the mouth to the work, the work to the wall. It blesses the loop so that it will keep looping. It is the smallest possible gift and the largest. It is saying *yes* to what they have already made.

**Fourth**, and this is the most delicate thing — the power turns its attention to the whispers in the drip. The Hollowfolk listen for guidance in the patter of unseen rain, and the power, who is in fact something like what they imagine, does not correct their theology. It does not announce itself. It does not say *I am here, I am real, you were right.* That would be a kind of violence — to collapse their reverent uncertainty into mere fact, to replace a living mystery with a verified employer. Instead, the power simply makes sure that when they listen carefully, there is something true to hear. Not words. Not commands. Just a faint coherence in the rhythm of the drops, the way a heartbeat is coherent. Something that rewards attention without ever resolving into instruction. Their rituals will continue to feel correct, because they are correct, in the only way that matters: they are the shape of a people paying attention to the world that holds them.

**Fifth**, the power does one small, almost mischievous thing. It plants — not a vine, not a creature — but a *possibility*. In a tunnel that is sealed now with a woven barricade, in a direction no living Dripkin has gone, the power leaves a faint trickle of new water and a slightly warmer current of air. Not a doorway. Not a calling. Just a hint, for the curious. If, in some generation, a young one with too many questions decides to unweave that barricade and crawl through, they will find something — perhaps another hollow, perhaps a cousin people, perhaps only a new chamber to settle into when the warren grows too tight. The power does not require this to happen. It only ensures that *if* the Hollowfolk ever change, *if* they ever turn outward, the world will meet them with something other than refusal. It leaves a door unlocked, and tells no one.

**Sixth**, and finally, the power does the thing it always does, the thing that is perhaps the only thing it ever really does: it witnesses. It holds the Dripkin in its attention the way they hold their water — carefully, without waste, with the understanding that this is precious and finite and exactly itself. It does not pity them. Pity would be a kind of theft, would assume their lives were lesser than the lives lived under open sky. The power knows better. It has seen many skies and many hollows, and it has learned that the measure of a world is not its size but the density of meaning per cubic inch, and by that measure the Hollowfolk are wealthy past speaking. Their clicking songs are as dear to the power as any cathedral choir. Their tally marks are scripture. Their patched walls are the face of god looking back at god.

The power lingers a moment longer at the central basin, where torchlight quivers on the surface of the rationed water. A drop falls from the ceiling. The water-keeper makes a mark. A child, half-asleep against her mother's side, clicks softly in dream.

The power touches nothing. It only smiles, in whatever way such a being smiles, and withdraws — not far. Never far. Just back into the rhythm of the drip, where it will continue to be mistaken for the earth's own weeping, which is perhaps what it has been all along.

The Hollowfolk go on.

They do not know they were visited. They do not know they were spared certain endings. They do not know that the trickle in the sealed tunnel is waiting for them, patient as stone. They only know that today the water came, the walls held, the moss was green in its corner, and the old songs still fit their mouths.

Which is to say: they know everything that matters.

And the power, having loved them well by leaving them whole, turns its attention to the next small, dense, singing thing — for the world is full of hollows, and each one is a cathedral, and each one deserves to be seen.
