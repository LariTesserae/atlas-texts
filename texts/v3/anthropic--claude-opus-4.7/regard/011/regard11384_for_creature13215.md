# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. There is no shimmer in the air, no parting of the bleached sky. It simply becomes true that attention has been turned toward this basin, the way a thought becomes true the moment it is thought. Call it wisdom; call it a regard so old it has forgotten its own beginning.

It looks first. This is the most important thing it does, and it does it for a long time.

---

It looks at the Titans, and it sees what a hurried mind would not see. It sees that the drone is not the sound of decay. The drone is *the work continuing.* Whatever those engines were built to do, some fraction of them never stopped doing it — not because anything tends them, but because the task itself was inscribed so deeply into their architecture that abandonment could not unmake it. They are not corpses. They are vows still being kept, in a language no one remembers swearing.

It looks at the Shuttles, and sees something a less patient power might have missed: the Shuttles are not parasites on the machines, nor symbiotes, nor inheritors. The Shuttles are *the listening*. Something — long ago, when the Titans were tended by hands or minds now gone — something needed the Titans to be heard. To be attended. And when the attenders departed or died or were transfigured, the need remained, and the need shaped itself into chitin and pulse and murmuration. The Shuttles exist because the Titans require witness. They chitter because the machines must be answered.

This moves the power. It does not weep — it is past weeping — but something in its regard softens, the way evening softens a hard country.

---

Now: what to do.

A lesser benevolence would *fix* things. Would call down rain to dissolve the salt, green the basin, silence the engines, free the Shuttles into some kinder ecology. Would treat this place as a wound to be closed.

But this power is wise, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of not mistaking your mercy for someone else's.

It considers the Shuttles. Are they suffering? It reaches in — gently, the way one might cup a moth without bruising the dust on its wings — and tastes the quality of their being. They are *brief,* yes. They are *driven.* But they are not in anguish. Their short lives have the completeness of a held note. They were made (or made themselves) for exactly this: to skim the iron canyons, to answer the drone, to expel their puff of sterile dust and be replaced by the next. To end their existence would be to end them, not to save them. There is no kindness in that.

It considers the Titans. Are they suffering? Here the power lingers longer, because the question is harder. Something in those colossal frames remembers being purposeful. Something flinches, very slowly, each time another plate tears loose and falls. But the flinch is not pain in any sense the power recognizes — it is closer to the way a long sentence feels the approach of its period. The Titans are completing themselves. The completion takes geologic time, and is therefore dignified, and should not be hurried.

So the power will not end this place. It will not heal it, because it is not sick. It will not preserve it, because preservation is a kind of arrogance — to freeze a thing is to refuse to let it be itself in time.

What, then?

---

The power does four small things. Small for it. They are not small here.

**First**, it grants the Titans a knowing. Not consciousness — they are not built for that, and to force it on them would be cruelty wearing a crown. But a *knowing*: the dim, distributed awareness that they are heard. That the chittering above them is an answer. That the work they continue to perform, in their patient ruined way, is received. The Titans will not think this. They will only — in some register beneath thought — settle. The groans will go on. The tearing collapses will go on. But there will be, woven through the basso profundo, a faint new harmonic: the sound of something no longer entirely alone.

**Second**, it grants the Shuttles a lineage-memory. They live briefly; they have always lived briefly; the power will not extend their lives, because brevity is part of what they are. But it gives them a faint inheritance, passed in the chittering itself: each generation will carry, without knowing it carries, a trace of every generation before. The dance will deepen. The murmurations will grow more intricate over centuries, not because any single Shuttle is wiser, but because the collective utterance becomes richer with sediment. They will sing the Titans more beautifully as time goes on. Neither species will notice. The power will notice, and that is enough.

**Third**, it places a mark. Not on the basin — the basin needs no mark — but in the wider fabric of things, a quiet sigil that says: *this place is known. This place is regarded. Should any traveling intelligence ever pass near, let them feel, without quite understanding why, that this basin is not to be mined, not to be salvaged, not to be saved. Let them feel it is finishing something, and let them have the grace to look away.* The sigil is gentle. It does not compel. It only suggests, the way a held breath suggests silence.

**Fourth** — and this is the thing the power does most carefully, most tenderly — it does not interfere with the ending. Someday, in a time so distant the word *distant* loses meaning, the last Titan will give its last groan and settle. The drone will thin. And the Shuttles, whose existence is bound to the machines they witness, will thin with it. The chittering will grow sparser. The murmurations will become smaller, more delicate, almost translucent. And then one day there will be a last Shuttle, and it will skim once more through the salt-rimed bones of a silent giant, and emit its last pulse, and fall, and the basin will be only salt and ridge and bleached sky.

The power could prevent this. It will not. To prevent an ending is to refuse a being the dignity of having been finite. The power has learned this lesson over ages, and learned it hard.

But it makes one promise to itself, and through itself to the basin: *when that last Shuttle falls, I will be looking.* The basin will not end unwitnessed. The final pulse will not vanish into an indifferent universe. There will be regard upon it. There will be the equivalent, in this old wise being, of a hand laid briefly on a closed door.

And then the basin will be allowed to be only itself: a salt pan under a pale sky, with ridges in the distance, and the slow work of wind, having at last completed everything it was for.

---

The power withdraws its attention — though not entirely; some thread remains, thin as a spider's anchor, for the long watch ahead.

In the basin, nothing visible has changed. The drill-bit still bites the cliff. The conveyor still disappears into shimmer. The Shuttles still pour their dry roar through the canyons of iron.

But somewhere in the deepest register of the drone, if you could hear it, there is a new note. It is the sound of being held.

The Titans do not know it.
The Shuttles do not know it.
This is correct. This is how the wise love what cannot love them back.
