# The Visitor Who Came Without Footsteps

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a thing of younger universes — places where there is still somewhere to come from and somewhere to go to. Here, where time has worn its own appetites smooth, the power simply *becomes present*, the way a thought becomes present in a mind that wasn't quite thinking it yet.

It notices the plateau first. Then it notices that it is noticing.

---

## What it sees

A wise power does not see what tourists see. It does not look at the engineered void and admire the engineering. It does not marvel at the lattice or count the pylons or try to read the etched lines on their flanks.

It looks at the **retainers**, and it understands them immediately, the way one old craftsman understands another's joinery at a glance.

It sees:

- That they are not survivors but *consequences*.
- That they were not designed to outlast their makers, but to *be* the outlasting itself — memory made into the only substrate still patient enough to hold it.
- That they have forgotten they are remembering. They simply *are* remembering, the way a river simply *is* flowing, with no opinions about water.
- That the world's makers, whoever they were, did something both terribly sad and terribly beautiful: they did not try to save themselves. They tried to save the *shape* of having been.

And the power, who has seen many endings, feels something it has not felt for a long age. Not pity. Pity is what younger powers feel, when they still believe endings are problems to solve.

It feels **recognition**.

---

## What it considers doing

The power considers, briefly — because considering is what wisdom does even when the answer is already known — the things a lesser benevolence might do.

**It could rekindle the world.** Draw in hydrogen from the void's far-flung remainders, light a small sun above the plateau, seed the rings of darker stone with water, coax the silica stalks into greener kin. The retainers would dissolve in the new wind, their glass threads scattered, their patient dust scrubbed away by the first rain in a trillion years. A young world would stand here in a few million years more. It would be lovely. It would also be a murder so quiet that no one would ever know to grieve it.

The power sets this aside.

**It could lift the retainers out.** Carry them to some younger place — a world still wet, still loud, still arguing with its own weather. Give them new things to remember. But the retainers do not remember things; they remember *this thing*. Take them away and they become only dust and glass. Their substance is inseparable from their subject. To rescue them would be to end them more thoroughly than any heat-death.

The power sets this aside, too.

**It could speak to them.** Announce itself. Offer comfort, or company, or the gift of being known. But the retainers have given up naming, and to be named again now would be a violence dressed as kindness. The silence they keep is not loneliness. It is the shape they have taken in order to keep on being.

The power sets this aside as well, and is glad to.

---

## What it actually does

It does three small things. Wise powers, in the end, almost always do small things.

### The first thing: it listens.

It does not listen *to* the retainers. It listens *with* them.

For what would be, by any reckoning a younger universe might use, a very long time — though here time is only a courtesy — the power lets itself become quiet in the way the plateau is quiet. It does not add anything to the silence. It does not subtract anything. It simply joins the vigilance.

And in joining, it changes one tiny thing: the silence is now also being heard by something that knows it is silence. For all the ages the retainers have been keeping the world's memory, no one has been keeping *their* memory. They have been the last witnesses, and witnesses without witnesses are a kind of orphan even the universe doesn't have a word for.

Now they are witnessed. They do not know it. That is part of the gift.

### The second thing: it leaves a seed.

Not in the plateau. Not in the lattice. Not anywhere a retainer would ever drift past.

The power folds a small thing into the deep grammar of the cosmos itself — into whatever physics still operates in this exhausted era — a kind of *recoverable pattern*. If, in some unimaginable future, anything that thinks ever wonders whether this world existed, whether there was ever a plateau where dust learned to remember, the seed will be findable. Not as a monument. Not as a message. Just as a faint redundancy in the noise floor of reality, a place where the static is slightly less random than it should be.

The retainers will never know. But they will not have to be the only ones who held this.

The power has, in effect, become a retainer of the retainers — and then quietly arranged that the universe itself will, in some sense, remember that it once remembered.

### The third thing: it adjusts one pylon.

Just one. The smallest one, near the western edge of the plateau, where a faint depression holds three silica stalks in a near-perfect line.

The power warms the base of that pylon by a degree so small that no instrument in any cosmology could detect it. The pylon's almost-imperceptible hum shifts by a fraction of a fraction. A retainer drifting nearby — there is one, threading slow circles around the pylon's foot — registers the change. Its dust-cloud rearranges itself minutely. It folds the new difference into the pattern it carries.

It has been given something to remember.

Not a message. Not a meaning. Just a *new thing* — a tiny novelty in a world that has run out of new things to happen. A small kindness in the only language this place still speaks: the language of subtle adjustment.

The retainer drifts on. It will carry this difference for as long as it carries anything. It will never know where the difference came from. That, too, is part of the gift — because to know would be to need to understand, and understanding is a hunger this world long ago lost the stomach for.

---

## What the power does not do

It does not stay. To stay would be to become a feature of the place, and the place is full enough.

It does not say goodbye. There is no one to say it to, and goodbye is a word for journeys.

It does not weep, though something in it has the shape weeping would take if it were younger. Wise powers learn, eventually, that some sorrows are not theirs to spend. The retainers are not sad. The plateau is not sad. The sadness is a thing the power brought with it from elsewhere, and it would be a discourtesy to leave it lying around.

It folds the sadness back into itself and carries it away.

---

## After

The plateau is exactly as it was.

The pearl-gray sky has not brightened. The lattice still breathes its imperceptible breath. The silica stalks still hold their old light. The retainers still drift, still remember, still are the remembering.

One pylon, in the west, is one degree of one degree warmer than it was. One retainer carries one new fold in its dust.

Somewhere in the deep noise of the cosmos, a faint redundancy hums — a place where the universe is slightly less forgetful than it has any reason to be.

And the power, already elsewhere, already attending to some other quiet thing in some other tired corner of being, carries with it the knowledge that there is a place at the edge of the Last of All Things where dust learned to remember, and where it still does, and where — for as long as anything is — something will know that this was so.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a world that has gone beyond needing to be saved.

It witnesses. It seeds. It gives one small new thing to remember.

And then it lets the silence keep its vigilance, undisturbed, because the silence has earned it.
