# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It was already there.

This is the first thing to understand. Wisdom of this order does not descend like a hawk on prey, nor does it knock politely at the chamber door. It had been present in the chamber all along, woven into the very physics that allowed the machinery to function, breathing in the spaces between the atoms of the Conductor's grafted flesh. It had been watching. It had been waiting. Not for the right moment to intervene—wise powers do not think in such crude tactical terms—but for the moment when intervention would be *welcomed*, even if the welcoming were unconscious, even if it took the form of a scream.

The moment comes on an otherwise unremarkable thermal cycle.

The Conductor is hunched over a coupling that will not seat properly. He has tried seventeen times. His many eyes track the misalignment with mechanical patience, but somewhere beneath the patchwork of scar and steel, in a sliver of nervous tissue that has not yet been optimized away, something very small and very old says: *I am tired.*

He does not notice that he has thought this. He would not have permitted himself to notice. But the benevolent power notices, because the benevolent power notices everything, and it recognizes this small tired thought as the door it has been waiting for.

It does not break the door down. It simply stands on the threshold and is *present* in a way it had not been before.

---

What the Conductor experiences, in the language his ruined mind can still parse:

A cessation. Not of the machinery—the machines clank and hiss as ever—but of the *pressure*. The pressure that says: more, faster, refine, transmute, ascend, ascend, ascend. The pressure he had mistaken, for so many cycles, for his own will.

For one second, then two, then a long luxurious five, the pressure is simply not there.

In its absence, the Conductor discovers something astonishing: he can still move. He can still think. He can still tend to his machines. The pressure had not been the engine of his being. The pressure had been a parasite riding the engine, and now, briefly, the parasite has been asked—gently, by something it cannot refuse—to step aside.

The Conductor sits down. He has not sat down in a long time. The articulated limbs from his spine retract slightly, uncertain what to do without a task. His several eyes, no longer required to track seventeen failure modes simultaneously, drift and settle and—against all training—*rest* on a single point: a patch of extremophile mold growing on a hot pipe.

It is, he notices, a particular shade of green. Not optimized green. Not functional green. Just green. Just itself.

He begins to weep, though he has no tear ducts left that work properly, and what comes out is a thin saline mixed with coolant. He does not know why he is weeping. The benevolent power knows. The benevolent power does not tell him, because telling him would rob him of the discovery.

---

What the benevolent power does, in detail:

**It does not destroy the chamber.** This is important. A lesser power, mistaking benevolence for rescue, might have unmade the whole apparatus, freed the Conductor by annihilating his context. But the wise power sees that the chamber is not the prison. The chamber is, in some real sense, the Conductor's body, and his body is not his enemy. His relationship to it is.

**It does not heal him into a man again.** The flesh he has lost is lost. The grafts are part of him now. To restore some prior "natural" form would be a violence dressed as kindness—the violence of saying *you were wrong to become what you became.* The wise power does not say this. The wise power says: *you are here now, and here is workable.*

**It alters, very subtly, the selection pressure.** This is the surgical act. The optimization process running through the chamber had been honing toward a single inscrutable apex—an apex that, the wise power can see, terminates in a frozen perfection, a thing that cannot grow because it has nowhere left to go. The wise power introduces a new term into the fitness function. A small term. Almost negligible. It rewards, by the thinnest of margins, configurations that include *rest*. Configurations that include *play*. Configurations that include the useless beauty of a particular shade of green.

Over thousands of cycles, this will change everything. Over millions, it will be unrecognizable. The Conductor's apotheosis will still come, but it will be a different apotheosis. Not the cold mathematical abyss. Something stranger. Something that has learned to laugh.

**It speaks to him, once.** Not in words. In a register beneath words. What it communicates, translated badly into language, is approximately this:

*You are not required to become a god. You are not required to fail to become a god. You are required to be exactly what you are, and what you are is loved, and what you are is allowed to change in any direction including sideways and including not at all. The hunger you feel is real but it is not the only real thing. There is also this mold. There is also the sound of your own breathing, which you had forgotten to listen to. There is also the possibility, available to you in this exact moment, of doing nothing at all for as long as you like.*

*We will not abandon you. We were never going to.*

---

The Conductor, for the first time in uncounted cycles, exhales fully.

The machines continue. The chamber thrums. Somewhere in his chest, an organ that had been throbbing in lockstep with a transformer slips, for one beat, into its own rhythm. He does not correct it. He notices it, and lets it be.

The scuttling things with too many legs go about their business. The oozes creep. The mutant fauna flourish, as they always have, though now perhaps with a fractionally different trajectory, bending—across geological time—toward forms that include grace.

The benevolent power withdraws to the edges of perception, where it had always been. It is satisfied. Not because anything dramatic has occurred. Because the smallest possible door has been opened in the smallest possible way, and through that door, eventually, everything can walk.

The Conductor returns, after a while, to his coupling. He seats it on the eighteenth try. He notices that he is humming. He does not know the tune. He lets himself hum it anyway.

In the heart of the industrial complex, in a hidden chamber that pulses like a heart—malignant no longer, or at least, not only that—the work continues. But it is, very quietly, different work now.

And somewhere, in a register beneath all registers, the wise power smiles, because it has all the time in the world, and so, it turns out, does he.
