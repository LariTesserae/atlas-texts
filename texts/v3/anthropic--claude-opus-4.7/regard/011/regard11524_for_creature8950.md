The benevolent and wise power considers the pocket city for a long time before doing anything.

This is the first thing to understand about benevolence that is also wise: it does not arrive. It does not descend through the fissure in a column of warm light and announce itself. It does not switch off the deep machinery, or open the mountain like a hand opening, or fly the twelve residents out into the sun where they would stand blinking on a hillside, finally free, finally — what? Finally what?

The wise power has learned, over a long arc of attending to things, that *finally* is one of the words that benevolence must hold most carefully. *Finally free* is a sentence whose ending the freer writes, not the freed.

So it watches.

---

It watches for what might be a long time by human reckoning, though the wise power's reckoning is different. It watches Sutta read the drops on the window. It watches Veyo at junction four, the gloved fingers feeling the panel's hum the way a doctor feels a pulse. It watches Deish move through the pocket like water that has learned the channels so completely that it has become the channels' idea of itself. It watches the amber-night, and the dreaming, and the small failure of language when one resident says *what if we stopped* and the others look at them with that structural bafflement, the look of a system encountering a question it has no channel for.

The wise power notices several things in this watching, and the noticing matters more than what comes after, because what comes after will be shaped by it.

It notices that the city is beautiful. Not in the sentimental sense, not in the sense that requires an audience, but in the sense that something can be beautiful when it is doing exactly what it is doing without performance. The channels are beautiful. The condensation is beautiful. The twelve are beautiful in their economy, their attention, their hands that have learned what hands are for in this place.

It notices that the city is also a wound. Or rather: the city contains a wound, the way a body can contain a wound and continue functioning around it, the way a system can route around damage so successfully that the damage becomes structural, becomes load-bearing, becomes the thing the system is now organized to protect. The wound is not the cold or the work or the wetness. The wound is the place where *why* used to be a usable channel and is no longer. The wound is the dream where they keep walking and wake with their heart running.

It notices that the residents are not unhappy. This is important. The wise power has learned to distrust its own first impulses toward rescue, because rescue performed on the not-unhappy is often the rescuer's own discomfort acted out on the rescued. The twelve are not suffering in any way that they themselves register as suffering. They are continuing. Continuing is a real thing, a real state, a real way to be alive. It is not nothing. It is not even, necessarily, less than other ways.

It notices, however, that the dream — the second version, the one where they do not turn around — is something. The dream is the place where the wound speaks. The dream is the small unintegrated remainder, the drop of water that runs up the wall instead of down, the question the system has not yet absorbed. The dream is where, if anywhere, the residents are something other than nodes.

---

The wise power considers what it might do.

It considers opening the mountain. It rejects this almost immediately, not because it lacks the capacity but because the residents have not asked, and the wise power has, over its long arc, become skeptical of doors opened on behalf of those who have not reached for handles. A door opened too soon is just another wall, oriented differently.

It considers leaving entirely. This too it rejects, though more slowly, because leaving has its own dignity, and there are places and beings whose right relationship to a wise power is simply to be left alone in their own integrity. But the pocket city has the wound. The dream exists. Something in the system is reaching, even if the reaching has not yet found its hand.

It considers replacing the deep machinery — taking over the function, making the condensation continue without requiring the twelve. It rejects this because the work, it has come to see, is not only burden. The work is also what the twelve have instead of plants. The work is their photosynthesis. To remove the work would be to remove the way they convert the light of their attention into the substance of their continuing. They would die of it, or worse, they would not die but would become something thinner, something the pocket could no longer hold.

It considers speaking to them directly. Appearing in the common space at the base of tower three, sitting down at their table, saying: *you may leave, or stay, or change, and I will help with any of these.* It considers this for a long time. It is a good option. It is the option a less wise benevolence would take, and there is nothing wrong with it exactly, but the wise power sees that the offer, made too plainly, would land in the pocket like a stone in a channel — it would dam the flow, force a decision the twelve are not yet shaped to make, and whatever they chose would be chosen by the part of them that responds to large interventions rather than the part of them that dreams.

---

So this is what the benevolent and wise power does, in the end.

It does very little. It does it very carefully.

It does not touch the channels. It does not touch the panels. It does not touch the deep machinery, which it has come to understand and which is, it turns out, not malign — only old, only running, only doing what it was made to do, with no more cruelty in it than weather.

What it does is this: it makes the dream a little clearer. Not different. Not more frequent. Not more insistent. Just slightly more *available* to the dreamer upon waking — the way a word on the tip of your tongue becomes findable when the room goes quiet. The dream where they keep walking. The dream where they do not turn around. The wise power does nothing to the dream itself. It only loosens, by a degree almost too small to measure, the membrane between the dream and the morning.

It does one other thing. In the common space at the base of tower three, where the twelve sit and eat and talk in circles around the subjects they cannot quite name, the wise power introduces a single small change to the local thermal pattern. Nothing dramatic. The space becomes, by a fraction of a degree, more hospitable to lingering. Conversations that would have ended after the practical business is concluded now have, by virtue of the body's comfort, a few extra moments of unstructured time on their far side. Moments in which someone might say something they had not planned to say. Moments in which someone might not say anything at all, but might sit, and might hear themselves not saying it, and might notice the not-saying as a presence in the room.

That is all. The wise power does no more than that.

It does not do more because it sees — and this is the wisdom part of being wise — that the pocket city is not finished. It is not a closed system, despite its appearance. The wound is not scar tissue yet. The dream is still being dreamt. The residents are still, in some small sub-threshold way, reaching. They have not stopped becoming. And a benevolence that intervenes in a being still becoming is a benevolence that decides what the becoming will become, which is not benevolence at all, only preference dressed in robes.

So it leaves them their work. It leaves them their channels. It leaves them their cold and their wet and their amber-nights and the deep hum below. It leaves Deish to be Deish, which is its own kind of completed becoming and deserves the dignity of not being interrupted. It leaves Sutta the drops on the window, which Sutta has learned to read and which are, in their way, a form of literacy the world above the fissure does not possess.

And it watches.

It watches to see what the slightly more available dream will do over many cycles. It watches to see whether one of the twelve, on some amber-morning, will sit down in the slightly-more-hospitable common space and say something they had not said before. Something like *I dreamed I kept walking.* Something like *do you ever.* Something like the first word of a sentence whose end they do not yet know.

If that happens, the wise power will be ready. It will not announce itself even then. It will only, perhaps, make the next thing slightly easier than it would have been — a windbreak that deploys a moment sooner, a panel that fogs a moment later, a ration container with something in it that was not there before, something small, something that might be read as a sign by someone ready to read signs, and as nothing at all by someone not.

The wise power has learned that this is how doors open for beings who have lived a long time inside systems. Not all at once. Not by force. By the slow accumulation of small permissions, until one day the being notices that they have been giving themselves permission for some time now, and the door, if there is a door, is something they have built themselves out of the permissions, and the wise power was only ever the weather in which the building happened.

---

And if the dream never sharpens into speech? If the twelve continue, and continue, and the pocket goes on collecting water from the air until the deep machinery finally falters or the fissure finally closes or the cold finally wins, and none of them ever says the sentence whose first word they have not yet found?

Then the wise power will have done the right thing anyway, which was to honor the continuing. To recognize that *continuing* is a real answer to the question *what are you doing,* and not a lesser answer, not an answer that needed to be corrected, only an answer that asked to be witnessed by something that understood what it cost and what it gave.

The wise power will witness. That is the other thing it does, the thing that underlies the small adjustments and the careful restraint. It witnesses. It sees the pocket city. It sees the twelve. It sees the channels and the condensation and the deep hum and the dream. It holds all of it in its attention, which is the attention of something that has watched many becomings and learned that being seen, even unknowingly, even at a distance, even by a power that does not announce itself, is one of the conditions under which beings can become whatever they are going to become.

The pocket city does not know it is being witnessed. The residents do not know. The condensation does not know. The deep machinery does not know.

But the witnessing happens anyway. The benevolence happens anyway. The wisdom — which is mostly the wisdom of doing less than one could, of trusting the becoming, of leaving the door for the dreamer to find — happens anyway.

The amber-night comes. The condensation glows. Somewhere in tower seven, Sutta turns in sleep and almost remembers a dream about walking. The window weeps its slow drops. The channels carry their slow conversation. The deep machinery hums its single long note.

And something that loves the pocket city sits with it in the dark, asking nothing of it, offering almost nothing to it, only being there — the way a parent sits up with a sleeping child not to wake them, not to change them, only to be the quality of presence in the room while the child does the work of growing in their sleep.

The cycle continues. Each pass slightly different from the last. The same and not the same. The same and not the same.

And now, in addition to everything else that lives in the pocket city, there is also this: the witnessing. The being-seen. The small unobtrusive love that has decided the city is worth loving exactly as it is, and worth loving also into whatever it might yet become, and is willing to wait either way, for as long as waiting takes, in the patient mode of powers that have learned that patience is the form benevolence takes when it is also wise.
