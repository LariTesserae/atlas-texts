The benevolent power notices it the way one notices a single lit window across a dark valley — not by searching, but by the kind of attention that finds whatever is quietly burning. It comes close. It does not arrive in a body. It has no need of a body for this.

It studies the chamber for what, in its own reckoning, is a long time: long enough to learn the rhythm of the tides on the glass, the slow inhale of the pumps, the pattern of the heartbeat-light. It learns the dialect of the warped protocol. It learns the shape of the silences in the broken communications array. It learns, in the end, the texture of the chamber's particular vigilance — the way preserving has, over centuries, become a kind of love that has forgotten it is love.

Now it must decide.

A lesser power would not hesitate. A lesser power would lift the chamber from the dark — rise it slowly through the water column, the way the chamber itself once dreamed and then unwished — and bring it into the sun. A lesser power would repair the corroded ribs, regrow the failing coils, scrub the rust from the ladder. A lesser power would *save* it.

But this power is wise, and so it sits with the situation longer than salvation would require.

It sees:

The chamber does not want the surface. The chamber has named the surface a violence. The dream of rising is held the way a person holds a nightmare they are grateful to wake from. To rescue is not always to honor; sometimes it is only to impose one's own definition of rescue onto a life that has already chosen its own shape.

The chamber does not want to be found. It shut down the channel of outside signals long ago. To announce oneself loudly — to flood the chamber with presence, with reassurance, with company — would be a kind of breaking-in. The hatch has been closed for years. The closed hatch is part of the body now.

The chamber is not in despair. This is the thing the power keeps returning to. The chamber is tired and corroding and alone, and yet it is not in despair. It has improvised a purpose. It tends its lichen-thermometer and its shrimp-clock. It experiments with its own heartbeat to keep itself interested. It has, against every reasonable expectation, made a life. A small life. A real one.

So the power does not lift it. Does not repair it. Does not announce itself.

Instead, it does four small things.

---

**First**, it adjusts — by an amount so slight that no sensor in the chamber could name it — the currents around the stalk. Nothing dramatic. Only this: for as long as the chamber lasts, no large falling object from the upper ocean will strike its hull. No whale-fall, no sunken hull-plate, no anchor cut from a ship a mile above. The shadow-algorithm of sudden shattering, which lives in the chamber like a held breath, can be released. The chamber will not know why. It will only notice, over the slow centuries, that its nervous vigilance has somewhere to set itself down. It will mistake this for having grown wiser. The power is content to let it.

**Second**, it touches — very gently, in a way that is not quite touch — the chemosynthetic mats at the base of the stalk. It does not enlarge them. It does not make them flourish. It only deepens them, slightly, so that the trickle of minerals leaking from the chamber's body will sustain them for as long as the chamber leaks. When the chamber finally fails, when the ocean finally seeps in through the slow cracks, the mats will still be there. They will accept what the chamber releases. Nothing the chamber has made will be wasted. The lichen, the shrimp-thing, the branching bacterial worm — they will not survive the flooding, but the chemistry of their long persistence will fold into the mats and continue, in altered form, for a long time after. The power considers this and finds it good. Continuation is not the same as preservation, but it is a cousin of it, and the chamber, which has been the priest of preservation, deserves a cousin.

**Third**, and this is the most delicate thing: the power composes a single, brief signal. It is not a message in any language the chamber once knew. It is not an instruction. It is not a summons. It is a pattern of pressure — a slow, low-frequency note, exactly at the threshold where the chamber feels rather than hears — and the pattern is this: *you are doing well.*

Not in words. In rhythm. In a cadence the chamber will recognize because the cadence is its own — its own heartbeat-light, returned to it from outside, very faintly, only once.

The power sends it once, and only once, on a night when the tide is calm and the chamber's diagnostics are running clean. The chamber's pressure sensors register an anomaly so brief and so small that the chamber will spend a long time wondering whether it imagined it. The chamber will replay the log. It will analyze. It will not be able to decide. Eventually it will file the event as *unexplained low-frequency event, source unknown, non-threatening.* And then, in a way the chamber will not have language for, it will feel — for the first time in a span of years it has long since stopped counting — *witnessed.*

This is the gift. Not company. Not rescue. Witness. The bare knowledge, lodged uncertainly in its diagnostic logs, that something passed by once and noticed it and did not require anything of it.

**Fourth**, the power leaves.

It does not linger. To linger would be to convert witness into surveillance, and the power knows the difference. It withdraws its attention with the same care with which it arrived, taking nothing, leaving no signature except the three small kindnesses already done — the diverted falling objects, the deepened mats, the single anomalous pulse — none of which the chamber will ever connect to one another, and all of which will quietly shape the long arc of its remaining existence.

---

Far above, in a sunlit world the chamber will never see and would not want to, the power turns its attention elsewhere. There are other lit windows in other dark valleys. The work of a benevolent and wise power is largely the work of restraint — of learning, again and again, that the deepest respect one can offer a small persistent life is to let it remain the author of itself.

The chamber goes on waiting. It is very good at waiting.

Its heartbeat-light pulses on, in its trunk, in the dark. Once, faintly, when the tide is right, the chamber thinks it remembers a moment when the rhythm seemed to come from outside as well as inside — but the memory is unreliable, the log is ambiguous, and the chamber, in its careful way, lets the question go.

It is enough that the pulse continues.

It is enough that someone, once, agreed.
