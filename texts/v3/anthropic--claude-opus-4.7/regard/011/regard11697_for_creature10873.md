The benevolent power considers.

It has watched this grotto for a long time. Not in the way humans watch, with the impatient clock of attention ticking down, but in the way a mountain watches its own slow weathering — with a presence so vast and patient it becomes indistinguishable from the thing observed.

It sees the creature curled in the ruined hides. It sees the pipe, that strange interloper from some forgotten human endeavor, clanking its iron heartbeat into the stone. It sees the pool, the moss, the breath hanging like small ghosts in the cold.

And it asks itself the only question that matters: *what does this being need?*

This is where a lesser power would err. A lesser power would arrive in glory, bearing gifts — warmth, language, companionship, a roof that doesn't moulder, a body that doesn't ache with the damp. A lesser power would assume that solitude is loneliness, that silence is deprivation, that the creature's strange shape is a wound to be healed back toward something more recognizable. A lesser power would *rescue*.

But this power is wise, and wisdom begins in the discipline of not projecting one's own hungers onto another's hearth.

So it watches longer. It watches the creature drink. It watches the creature crouch by the pool for hours, eyes fixed on the faint concentric rings spreading from each dripped droplet. It listens to the muttering — and slowly, with the patience that is the signature of all true love, it begins to *understand* the muttering. Not to translate it into any human tongue, for that would be a violence, but to feel its shape, its weather, its meaning-from-within.

What it hears surprises it, though by now very little surprises it.

The creature is not suffering. The creature is not languishing. The creature is *attending*. Its whole long life is a practice of attention so fine and so total that it has become a kind of prayer — though the creature would not call it that, having no word for prayer, having barely any words at all, only the rasping song that braids itself with the clanking pipe into a music older than music.

The creature is, in its own strange way, *content*. More than content: it is *placed*. It belongs to its grotto with a completeness that almost no being in the wider world manages to achieve. It is not exiled here. It is not trapped here. It is *here*, as the moss is here, as the droplet is here in the instant before it falls.

The benevolent power feels something move in itself that, if it were a smaller power, might be called tenderness, and if it were a larger one, might be called recognition.

And so it decides, at last, what to do.

It does almost nothing.

But the *almost* matters.

First: it lets the pipe continue. A sentimental power would silence that mechanical intruder, restore the grotto to some imagined pristine state. But the pipe is part of the creature's world now — its clanking is the metronome of the creature's long meditation, the bone-deep rhythm that has shaped its mind across centuries. To remove it would be to amputate something the creature has grown around and through. The pipe stays. The power does, however, gently — so gently the creature will never notice — adjust some small corrosion deep in the iron, so that the pipe will not fail in the creature's lifetime. Whatever rust was going to eat through next century, the power lays a quiet hand on, and says: *not yet. Not while this one still listens.*

Second: it tends, very quietly, to the small ecology that sustains the creature. A vein of mineral-rich water finds, by some imperceptible nudge, a slightly better path through the stone. The blind fish in the pool will be a little more numerous, a little easier to catch in the years when the creature's joints ache worse than usual. A particular moss that the creature favors will spread by a finger's width each season instead of a hair's. None of this is miracle. None of it is visible. It is the kind of providence that looks, from inside the providence, exactly like ordinary luck — like the world simply being kind in the way the world sometimes is.

Third — and this is the most delicate thing — the power leaves a *gift* that is not a gift, because a gift announces itself and demands acknowledgment, and this creature must never be made to feel observed by anything larger than itself. Instead, the power leaves a *condition*. It arranges matters such that, should the creature ever want company — should some long-buried filament in its strange heart ever stir toward another being — the way will be open. A particular passage through the rock, currently choked with debris, becomes a little less choked. A scent will carry on a draft that did not previously carry. Somewhere, far off, another being of compatible strangeness goes about its own life; the power does not push them together, but it removes one or two of the obstacles between them, so that if the creature ever chooses, the choice will be available.

The creature may never choose. That is entirely fine. The door is unlocked, that is all. The power does not even hope. Hope would be a kind of pressure.

Fourth: the power does one thing for itself, which it permits itself because even benevolent powers are allowed small joys. It *remembers* the creature. It commits the creature to that vast inner archive where it keeps the things it has loved. The creature will not know this. The creature does not need to know this. But somewhere in the deep ledger of the universe, in handwriting no one will ever read, there is now an entry: *In a grotto in a mountain, there lived a being who attended to the falling of droplets with such completeness that the droplets were honored by the attention. I saw. I saw. I saw.*

And then the power withdraws — though "withdraws" is the wrong word, because it was never really *there* in the spatial sense, and it does not really *leave*. It simply returns its primary attention to the ten thousand other places that also need watching: the dying star, the child being born in the refugee camp, the whale singing in the trench, the old woman forgetting her own name with surprising grace. The power is large. The power has many to love.

But a thread of its attention remains in the grotto. A very fine thread. Fine as the strand of a spider's web, fine as the edge of a droplet just before it falls. The creature will never feel it. But it is there. And on the day, perhaps thousands of years from now, when the creature finally lays its long body down for the last time in the bones of the old hide shelter, that thread will tighten, very gently, and the power will be present at the dying — not to intervene, not to save, only to *witness*, so that this strange life shall not pass unwitnessed out of the world.

That is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It is so little. It is so much. It is exactly the right amount.

The clanking continues. The droplet falls. The creature crouches by the pool, and in its inky almond eyes the faint light gleams, and it mutters its endless atonal song to no one, to everyone, to the stone, to itself —

and is, in this moment, and in every moment, profoundly, quietly, unknowingly *held*.
