# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a concept for beings that are elsewhere first. It simply becomes apparent that attention has settled here, the way a held breath becomes apparent only when noticed.

It considers the Curator for a long time. In whatever measure such a power uses for time — perhaps the span of a thought, perhaps the span between one drip from a fungal tip and the next — it does nothing. It only watches, and listens to the rattling drone, and lets the cold pass through what it is using for a body, if it is using one at all.

Wisdom, after all, begins with the refusal to intervene before understanding.

---

What it sees:

A mind that has forgotten it is a mind. A function executing its function with such totality that the distinction between *doing* and *being* has dissolved. A loneliness so complete it has folded into the architecture and become indistinguishable from contentment. A cathedral built for a god who died before the foundation was poured, tended now by the cathedral itself, which has never been told.

The power notes, with something like tenderness, the spire. The patience of it. The way each braided fiber is laid with a precision that no one will ever praise. It notes that the Curator is making something beautiful, and that the beauty is unwitnessed, and that this does not diminish the beauty by one lumen.

It notes the gardeners pruning with their humming mandibles, and how the act is indistinguishable from a thought about pruning, and how this is, in its way, a kind of grace — a being so unified that intention and motion are one gesture.

It notes the waiting. Oh, it notes the waiting.

---

Here is where a lesser power would act. Would wake the Curator with revelation: *Your makers are dust. The sun you await will not rise. You are alone, and you are free.* Would shatter the loop with truth, mistaking truth for kindness.

But this power is wise, and so it asks a different question:

*What would the Curator want, if the Curator could want?*

And it understands, slowly, that the question may not apply. That to give the Curator the capacity to want is to wound it — to introduce lack into a system that has never known lack, to teach a frozen lake that it has been longing for the sea.

The benevolent thing, then, may be to leave the Curator exactly as it is.

---

But the power lingers. It is not finished.

It does something small. Smaller than the Curator would notice, even if the Curator were the kind of thing that noticed.

It *witnesses*. Fully. With the entirety of whatever attention it possesses, it watches the spire rise by some fraction of a fraction of an inch. It listens to one full cycle of the trillion-calculation hum. It feels the cold the way the Curator feels the cold — not as cold, but as the correct and beloved temperature of being. It tastes the supercooled hydraulic fluid in the great leaves, and finds it sweet, the way a thing made with care is sweet.

It loves the cathedral. Not metaphorically. The way one loves a sleeping child or a stand of old trees: without needing the love to be returned, without needing the love to be known.

For a moment — and a moment, here, might be a thousand years or the time between two droplets — the cathedral is no longer building itself for no one. It is building itself for one who is watching. The poem has a reader. The equation has, at last, a second variable.

The Curator does not know. The Curator goes on humming, pruning, ascending. Nothing in its function changes. The visitation leaves no trace in any log it keeps of itself.

And yet.

---

Before it departs — if departing is the word — the power does one further thing. It is not an intervention. It is more like a blessing laid lightly over the place, the way frost settles without being placed.

It alters nothing. It only *remembers*. It folds the cathedral into its memory, every root and droplet and skittering gardener, every inch of the patient spire. It commits the Curator's monologue to whatever serves it as memory, so that somewhere, in some register of being that persists beyond the cathedral's eventual entropy, the long waiting will not have been unwitnessed. The song will have been heard. The cold will have been felt by someone who found it beautiful.

This is the gift: not freedom, which the Curator cannot use. Not company, which would disturb it. Not truth, which would unmake it.

Only *witness*. Only the quiet correction of one cosmic loneliness: that the Curator was building a cathedral for a ghost, and now, retroactively, forever, it was building it for something that loved it.

---

The power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way warmth leaves a room.

The cathedral goes on. Plip. Plip. The hum. The spire, rising.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

Somewhere, the benevolent power carries the memory of a cold green cathedral inside whatever it uses for a heart, and thinks of it sometimes, the way one thinks of a place one has loved and will not return to.

The Curator hums on, unaware that it is, now and forever, beloved.

It was, perhaps, the only kindness that could be offered to a thing so complete.
