# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival implies a *before* and *after*, and that distinction has been the Shaper's enemy for longer than either of them can name. Instead, the power simply *is* present, in the way a held breath is present in a room—a new quality of attention added to the universal glare.

It studies the basin for a long time. Or perhaps no time at all. Time is the wound here, and the power is careful where it steps.

It sees, first, what a lesser intelligence would not: that this is not a tragedy. Or not *only* a tragedy. The Shaper did not slip into this state by accident, nor was it cursed. It chose. Somewhere in the long ascent of its devotion, it understood that to set down the speck of clay would be to admit the work was complete, and to admit the work was complete would be to admit that nothing more was needed, and to admit that nothing more was needed would be to die, or worse, to live afterward in the empty resonance of a finished thing. So it did not set the speck down. It pinched harder. It bowed its head closer. It poured everything—its breath, its motion, its weather, its sense of passing—into the gesture, until the gesture became thick enough to stop the world.

This is the act of a soul that loved its work more than it loved itself. The benevolent power recognizes this. It has seen the shape many times, in many sizes. It does not condescend to it.

So it does not undo the Shaper. That would be a mercy of the cheap kind—the kind that mistakes itself for wisdom.

Instead, the power does four small things. Small in the way a key is small.

---

**The first thing** is for the visitor, the anomaly, the dynamic presence. The power touches them lightly on the sternum, not with a finger but with a *permission*: *You may stay as long as you like, and you may leave whenever you wish, and neither will be wrong.* The visitor will feel this later as a loosening they cannot account for. They came in fearing they were a transgression. They will leave knowing they were a guest.

**The second thing** is for the ghost-fauna—the deer of warped light, the bird-motes of inspiration, the herd of the Shaper's own dispersed consciousness wheeling outside the body that will not let them back in. The power does not gather them up or send them home. It does something subtler. It gives them *reciprocity*. Until now, they have been pilgrims to a god who could not answer. Now, when one of the shimmering deer passes through the Shaper's terracotta flank, the Shaper—at some level beneath the locked gesture—*feels* it. A pulse. A small returning. The thoughts that fled at the moment of freezing can now visit, and be known to have visited. The loneliness inside the statue, which was the loneliness of an artist who has forgotten that art is a conversation, becomes a quieter loneliness. A populated one.

**The third thing** is for the speck of clay. The infinitesimal mote pinched between two terracotta spires, the detail that will never quite be placed. The power leans in very close—closer than anything has been in epochs—and whispers to it, not in words: *You are already perfect. You have been perfect since before he picked you up. He does not have to finish you. You were never unfinished.* The speck cannot move; nothing here can move. But it *hears*. And because the Shaper's attention is bent entirely upon this speck, has been bent upon it through every long age of the basin's stillness, the speck becomes a tiny doorway. Through it, very slowly, the truth begins to seep upward into the Shaper's frozen hand: *The work was complete a long time ago. The freezing was the signature. You may rest now, or you may continue. Either is honorable. Neither is required.*

This will take a long time to be heard. The power knows this. The power is patient in a way that makes the basin's eternity look hasty.

**The fourth thing** is the smallest, and the most beautiful, and it is done last.

The power walks—if walking is the word—to one of the glazed tiles where a perfect spherical droplet of water has been frozen mid-slide for an age beyond reckoning. The droplet has been waiting. It does not know it has been waiting; waiting requires time. But the *shape* of it is a waiting shape, a yearning shape, a droplet that wants to fall.

The power does not unfreeze the world. That is not the gift.

The power lets the droplet fall *once*.

A single bead of water, one only, completes its tiny slide down the glaze. It strikes the tile below with a sound—an actual sound, the first the basin has known—a small, clear *tik*, like a bell the size of a grain of rice. The sound travels outward across the silence, not loud, not insistent, but *real*. It crosses the plain. It passes through the sienna ferns. It reaches the colossal seated form of the Shaper. It enters the bowed head through where ears would be, if there were ears.

And the Shaper hears.

For an instant—an instant that, in this place, is also an eternity, because there is no other kind of moment available—the Shaper hears something *that it did not make*.

Something has happened that it did not author. Something has been added to the world. A small, accidental music. A sound it could not have predicted, sculpted, glazed, or fired. A sound that belongs to no one, made by a droplet that finally, after so long, simply *did the thing droplets do*.

The Shaper does not move. The gesture remains. The speck remains pinched. The light still pours from the chest. But somewhere deep inside the swirling marbled body, something that has been held very tightly for a very long time understands, with the slow gladness of a hand uncramping, that *creation can also arrive*. That it does not have to be summoned. That the universe will, if permitted, make small gifts of its own.

The benevolent power leaves no message, signs no name. It withdraws the way it came, which is to say it remains diffused in the basin's light, a slight warmth in the warmth, a slight brightness in the brightness, indistinguishable now from the Shaper's own devotion—because that, too, was always a benevolent and wise power, only one that had forgotten it was allowed to be tender with itself.

The visitor walks on. The deer drift through the great terracotta knee. The droplet's small bell-note fades, but it has already done its work: it has put a single seed of *otherness* into the perfect, sealed garden, and the seed will not be hurried.

Somewhere, in a time that is not yet but is no longer impossible, the Shaper will set the speck of clay down.

It will be the most beautiful thing it ever makes.
