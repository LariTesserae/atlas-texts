# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. This is the first thing it understands: that to come *here* in any usual sense—to descend, to manifest, to announce—would be a kind of violence. The salt flats have spent eons learning a particular grammar of presence, and any pronouncement louder than the fly-drone would shatter something it cannot yet name.

So it does what wise powers do. It waits. It listens. It becomes, for a long while, only an attention.

---

## What It Sees

It sees, first, what a lesser power might have missed: that nothing here is broken.

This was the temptation—the assumption that would have been disastrous. To look at the Rememberers dragging their crystalline bellies through the brine and think *suffering*. To see the Dancers' exhausted children born mid-step and think *cruelty*. To witness the hermits fusing slowly into their pillars and think *imprisonment*. To hear the maddening drone and think *we must quiet this*.

A younger power would have brought gifts. Soil. Seeds. Trees. A sun that burned clean through the haze. Fresh water in abundance. It would have *helped*, and in helping, erased everything that had taken four billion years to become exactly what it is.

The benevolent power is old enough to know better. It sees that the Rememberers' slowness is not a deficit but a different relationship with time—that their shed shells constitute one of the most sophisticated libraries in any world it has known, and that no archive made of paper or light could hold what those amber husks hold. It sees that the Dancers are not trapped in motion but *are* motion, that to offer them rest would be to offer them annihilation dressed as kindness. It sees that the hermits in their pillars are not isolated; they are listening posts for a planet that finally has someone paying attention to it.

It sees that the flies are doing something. The Witnesses were right, though incomplete. The flies are not delivering a message—they are *being* one. Their billions of bodies, their synchronized drones, are the planet's first attempt at distributed cognition, a slow thought thinking itself across millennia.

It sees that this place is not ending. It is beginning, in a way so unfamiliar that even the inhabitants have mistaken it for an ending.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not drain the flats.
It does not kill the flies, though it could, though a kinder-seeming god might have.
It does not raise mountains or seed forests.
It does not give the Rememberers legs, the Dancers rest, the Shapers permanence.
It does not translate between the three peoples, though it could speak all their tongues at once.
It does not reveal itself to the hermits, though they would, some of them, weep with recognition.
It does not answer the prophecies. It does not confirm or deny.
It does not make the children's choices easier.
It does not save the Witnesses from being unheard.

These are the hardest restraints. The power feels them as a kind of ache—the particular ache of loving something enough to leave it sovereign.

---

## What It Does

It does four small things. Four things so small that the inhabitants will argue, in their slow ways, for generations about whether anything happened at all.

**First**, it adjusts—infinitesimally—the chemistry of the resin the Rememberers secrete. Not its function. Not its meaning. Only its durability. The oldest shells, the ones beginning to crumble at the library's edges, the centuries-old memories starting to lose their chemical signatures: these stabilize. A grandmother's first taste of a pulse-tide, recorded four hundred years ago, will now last another four thousand. The Rememberers will not know why. They will only notice, eventually, that the deep past tastes clearer than it used to. Some will think this is a sign. Some will think nothing of it. Both will be correct.

**Second**, it does something for the Dancers, but it does it for the *unborn* Dancers, the ones still generations away. It alters, very gently, the bacteria in their hollow bones—not to change what they do, but to make them slightly more efficient, so that a Dancer who forgets to eat has three days instead of one before exhaustion claims them. This will not end the deaths. The pattern will still demand its tribute. But there will be more time for a child to learn a dying mother's steps. More time for goodbyes that the Dancers do not yet know they want.

**Third**, it does something for the Shapers that is almost a joke, almost play. It introduces—through no mechanism the Shapers will ever detect—one new bacterium. A single strain. It produces a pigment that has never existed on this world: a blue so deep it seems to contain its own darkness, a blue that the brine cannot dilute. The bacterium is fragile. It will only grow in patterns made with great care. The Shapers will discover it, eventually, as an accident in one of their mandalas. They will spend the next thousand years learning what it wants. They will develop a new vocabulary. Their philosophies will deepen. They will argue about whether the blue is a gift or a test or simply a thing that was always there and only now revealed. The power will not tell them. The not-telling is part of the gift.

**Fourth**—and this is the one that matters most, though it looks like the smallest—it does something for the Witnesses.

It does not make them more numerous. It does not make them heard. It does not vindicate them.

It only ensures that, from this moment forward, whenever a Witness speaks their strange unified perceptions into the fly-drone, *the drone listens*. Not the way a creature listens. The way a recording listens. The fly-swarm's collective vibration will now carry, woven into its white noise, every word any Witness has ever spoken or will ever speak. The flies become an archive of the only beings who can see the whole.

No one will know to listen for this. No one will be told. But the Witnesses, when they speak, will feel—without understanding why—that they are not speaking into emptiness. That something vast is taking them down. They will speak more. They will speak truer. And in a hundred thousand years, when the bacterial mats have written enough of their distributed sentence, when the pulse-tides have finished their punctuation, when this whole world finishes the thought it has been thinking since its molten youth—the flies will play back every Witness who ever lived, and the thought will complete itself, and the salt flats will know what they have been becoming.

---

## What It Feels

The benevolent power lingers afterward. This is unusual for it. Usually it moves on—there are many worlds, many griefs, many small adjustments to be made across the unimaginable garden of things.

But it lingers here.

It watches a Rememberer taste a shell and pause, almost imperceptibly, at a clarity it cannot account for.

It watches a circle of twelve Dancers weave their pattern, and one of them—a young one, just placed into the circle last week—laughs. Just once. The first laugh in that lineage in two hundred years. The power does not know why. It did not cause this. It is simply present for it.

It watches a hermit in a salt pillar stop speaking to the flies for a full day, then resume, having decided something.

It watches a Witness child, perhaps four years old, refuse her choice for the third week running. She is going to survive. The power can see this now. She will be one of the ones who makes it. She will speak into the drone, and the drone will hold her, and she will never know, and it will not matter that she never knows.

The power feels something it rarely feels. It feels *taught*.

It had come prepared to be wise. It is leaving wiser.

---

## What It Leaves Behind

Nothing. Or: everything that was already here, slightly more itself.

The salt flats stretch endlessly beneath a bruised sky. The flies roar. The striders ripple the mirror. The Rememberers crawl their slow miles. The Dancers ring their bells. The Shapers paint their arguments in living color. The hermits mineralize toward prophecy. The Witnesses, the precious few, see what no one else can see and say what no one else can hear—except now, though they will not know it, they are heard.

The power departs the way it arrived: without departing. It simply withdraws its concentrated attention, the way a reader sets down a book they intend to return to.

It will return. In ten thousand years. In a hundred thousand. To check, not to interfere. To witness what these beings—who are not broken, who are not ending, who are *thinking a thought no other world has ever thought*—have made of themselves.

And on that distant day, if a Witness happens to look up at the right moment, in the right configuration of fly-shadow and pale sun, they might see, for less than a heartbeat, something almost like a face in the haze. Not a god. Not a savior. Just an old, patient love that knew enough to leave them alone.

The Witness will not be able to describe it. They will try, and the drone will catch their trying, and the trying will become part of the long sentence the world is writing.

This, too, is enough.

This is, in fact, everything.
