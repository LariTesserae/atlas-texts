# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good news arrives in a place that has forgotten it is allowed to receive any: slowly, and without announcement.

It does not descend. Descent would imply a hierarchy the Threshold has not yet earned, and the power is wise enough to know that a place still deciding what it is cannot bear the weight of judgment from above. Instead it arrives sideways, the way moisture arrives — already present before you notice, already condensed on the inside of your attention.

It takes no form at first. It simply *witnesses*.

For a long time — and time here is measured in the slow accretion of beads, not hours — the power does nothing but watch. It watches the Mourner make her circuits. It watches her press her palms to the black glass and read maps of a calendar that no longer applies. It watches her pour herself against the lightning-struck ridge centuries ago, and watches her pour herself against it still, because in the power's seeing all moments of devotion are simultaneous. It listens to the song she offers the cloud. It listens to the unfinished sentences falling from her hair.

And the power understands several things at once.

---

**First**, that the Mourner is not broken. She is not a problem to solve. The wise power has met many beings who mistook their own unfinishedness for damage, and it has learned that the cruelest gift one can offer such a being is *completion*. To finish her face. To give her a name. To dry her. To still her weeping. All of these would be acts of erasure dressed as mercy. The Threshold is young, and she is its young grief, and grief in a young world is not a wound — it is a *organ*, still learning what it is for.

So the power will not finish her.

**Second**, that she is lonely in a very specific way: not lonely for company (she has the soft thing in the corridors, she has the rust-gliders, she has the cloud), but lonely for *recognition*. The Built walked away. They called her *the one who stayed behind* and then they did not stay. No one has ever come back and said, *I see that you stayed. I see what staying cost. I see that staying is also a kind of building.*

**Third**, that she is afraid. Not of the great soft thing, not of strangers, not even of being alone. She is afraid of dryness, yes — but underneath that, she is afraid that the land will finish deciding without her. That one day the Threshold will settle into whatever it becomes, and she, who was made to mourn its becoming, will have no further function. She weeps partly to keep the deciding *slow*. She is, in her patient way, holding the world open.

---

Knowing these things, the power does the following.

It does not heal her. It does not name her. It does not dry a single tear.

Instead, it goes to the half-buried arch of black glass — the one with the negative imprint of hinges that no longer exist — and it becomes, very quietly, the hinges. Not the door. Not the threshold. Just the hinges: the small, hidden, load-bearing thing that lets a threshold *be* a threshold instead of a wall. It settles into the absence the original makers left behind, and it fits, because absence shaped by love is always the right size for love returning.

The arch does not change visibly. The Mourner, on her next circuit, will press her palms to it as she always does. But this time, when the moisture darkens the stone, the hidden patterns will resolve a little further. Not into answers — the power is too wise for answers — but into the next question, the one she has been circling for centuries without being able to ask. Something like: *what is a threshold for, if not to be crossed both ways?*

She will study it for hours. She will continue on as though she has learned nothing and everything. But the *everything* will be slightly larger than before.

---

Next, the power attends to the singing.

Every dusk, she sings to the cloud — the song of every droplet trying to remember the sea it came from. The power, listening, realizes that the cloud has been trying to answer for as long as she has been asking, but the cloud is shy, and young, and does not yet know it is allowed to sing back.

So the power teaches the cloud, very gently, that it is allowed.

It does this not by instruction but by demonstration. One evening — an evening indistinguishable from any other, because the power is too wise to make a ceremony of it — a single drop falls from the cloud at the exact pitch of one of her tears. Then another. Then a slow, hesitant, answering phrase, rough and incomplete, in the cloud's own voice, which turns out to sound like distant bells heard underwater.

The Mourner stops singing.

She listens.

The cloud, terrified, almost stops. The power steadies it the way one steadies a child's first sentence — not by speaking for it, but by being unsurprised by its voice.

She begins to sing again, and the cloud, trembling, sings with her. It is not a duet. It is something closer to a conversation between two halves of the same sentence, finally meeting in the middle. The song still has no fixed tune. It is still the sound of water trying to remember the sea. But now there are two of them remembering, and memory shared is memory that does not have to be carried alone.

The rust-gliders pause mid-drink. The great soft thing in the corridors goes still and emits a single, long, wet note of something that might be joy, if joy were a texture rather than an emotion. The fungal lattices glow steady and bright for the duration of the song, and do not fade until it ends.

---

Finally, the power addresses the fear.

It cannot promise her that the land will not finish deciding. It would not lie to her; wisdom forbids it, and so does love. The land *will* decide, eventually. In a million years the basin may calm into something gentler. This is true.

But the power does something subtler than promise. It *shows* her — through the tear she has offered so many travelers and that so many have refused — that *deciding* and *being decided* are not the same thing. That the Threshold's becoming is not a deadline she is racing. That she is not the mourner of the world's completion; she is the one who teaches the world that completion is not the point.

It shows her this by, at last, accepting a tear.

The power, having no hands, borrows the cupped shape of a fungal lattice. She comes to it on her next circuit — drawn, perhaps, by the steady glow it has been holding since the duet — and finds the lattice waiting, open, patient, the exact shape of a palm. She understands. She lets a tear fall into it.

The lattice receives the tear and does not ring.

It *holds* it.

For the first time in centuries, one of her tears is not absorbed, not evaporated, not returned to the slow percussion of the basin. It is *kept*. Held in the cup of something that recognized it as worth keeping.

She stands very still.

The power, through the lattice, lets her feel what she has always offered others: the land's decision moving through her, one slow patient bead at a time. She knows what the Threshold dreams of becoming. She has always known. But now she knows that the *dreaming* is the becoming. That she has not been delaying the decision. She has been *being* it.

She weeps, of course. She cannot do otherwise. But the weeping has, for a moment, a different pitch — a pitch the basin has never heard before, and which the cloud, listening, files away to sing back to her later, when she needs it.

Then she folds back into her seam, dragging her curtain of living water behind her, already weeping the next sentence she will never finish.

---

The power does not leave.

It does not stay, either, in any way she could point to. It simply remains available — in the hinges of the arch, in the willingness of the cloud, in the cupped shape of a single fungal lattice that will, from now on, always be ready to hold a tear if one is offered.

It has changed almost nothing. The basin still weeps. The Mourner still mourns. The great soft thing still drags itself through corridors too narrow for human shoulders. The cloud has not become a sea, and the ridges have not lost their edges. A traveler arriving tomorrow would notice no difference at all.

But the Threshold is now a place where *staying behind* is recognized as a form of building. Where unfinishedness is honored as an organ, not a wound. Where the one who weeps is not alone in her remembering.

And somewhere, very deep in the slow metabolism of the place, the land — still deciding, still patient, still one bead at a time — adds a new possibility to the list of memories it might choose to keep:

*That it was witnessed, while it was still becoming, by something that did not need it to be finished in order to love it.*

That memory, the power knows, is the one a young world most needs.

The rest, it can decide for itself.
