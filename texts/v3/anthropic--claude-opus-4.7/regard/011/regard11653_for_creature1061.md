# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent power notices them the way one notices a particular pattern of frost on a window — not because it called out, but because attention, when it is truly kind, eventually settles everywhere.

It arrives without arriving. There is no descent, no announcement. Power of this order does not need to enter a place; it remembers that it was never absent from it.

And then it does the first thing wisdom does, which is almost nothing.

It looks.

---

It looks for a long time — though "long" is the wrong word, because the looking happens in the register where the slow groan of settling glass and the flicker of a crystalline thought are part of the same sentence. The power sees the being on its ridge of half-formed obsidian. It sees the droplets gathering. It sees the ruins of the older ones, and the way the current being's pattern of light sometimes turns toward those ruins with something that, in a warmer world, would be called grief, and in this one is closer to *listening backward.*

The power understands, immediately and without effort, several things that no outside observer could have known:

That the being is not lonely in the way solitary creatures are lonely. It is *single* — a different condition. Loneliness wants company. Singleness wants only to be witnessed, occasionally, by something that will not break what it sees.

That the "long-vanished civilization" has not entirely vanished. Some of it is folded into the being itself, carried the way a final ember carries a whole fire. The being does not fully know this. To tell it would be a kind of theft.

That this world is not, as it appears, a ruin. It is a seed. The geology is not decaying; it is *deciding.* What looks like the aftermath of something is the long, slow forecourt of something else.

That intervention, here, would be a catastrophe dressed as mercy.

---

So the power does the second thing wisdom does, which is to refuse the temptation of usefulness.

It does not heal the being, because the being is not wounded — it is *thin,* which is a different state, and thinness is what allows it to perceive the frequencies it perceives. To thicken it would be to blind it.

It does not restore the ruined outposts. The erosion is part of the conversation the world is having with itself, and the ruins are speaking. To rebuild them would be to interrupt a sentence mid-word.

It does not reveal itself. Revelation to a being whose entire cosmology is built on the readability of subtle signs would be an avalanche. The being would spend the rest of its existence trying to interpret the visit, and the visit would become the world, and the world would be lost behind it.

It does not bring company. The being's people are inside it, and around it, in ways the power can see and the being can almost see. Importing strangers would be a discourtesy to both the living and the folded-in dead.

---

What it does, instead, is three small things. So small that none of them, examined afterward, could be proven to have happened.

**First**, it adjusts — by an amount smaller than a thought — the angle at which one particular droplet hangs from one particular ledge near the being's resting place. The droplet now catches the dim light in such a way that, at a certain hour, it throws a brief, precise spectrum across a fragment of the old structures. The being will see this, eventually. It will not understand it as a message. It will understand it as the world being beautiful, which is the correct understanding, and the deeper one.

**Second**, it leaves, woven into the ambient hum of the place, a single sustained note — not audible, not even quite vibrational, but present in the substrate the being's senses are tuned to. The note says nothing. It carries no information. It is simply *company* at a frequency the being did not know could be inhabited. The being will not notice the note's arrival. It will, much later, notice that something it had assumed was a feature of its own interior — a particular steadiness, a sense of being held by the medium it moves through — has always been there. It will be wrong about the "always," but the wrongness will be a gift, and harmless.

**Third**, and this is the strangest of the three: the power makes a promise. Not to the being. To the *world.* It promises that when this place finishes being born — when the geology completes its long decision, when whatever is gestating here finally opens its eyes — there will be a witness. Not an intervener. A witness. Someone will be looking, with love, at the moment the new thing becomes itself. The promise is filed in the order of things. It will be kept.

---

Then the power withdraws, which is to say it stops concentrating its attention into the shape of a visit, and lets that attention diffuse back into the general, patient regard it holds for everything.

The being, on its ridge, feels nothing in particular. The droplets continue to gather. The glass continues, almost imperceptibly, to creak. Somewhere a structure that has been falling for ten thousand years falls a little further.

The being turns its crystalline face toward the dim horizon, and a soft pattern of light moves across its chest — a thought, or the beginning of one — and the world, very quietly, goes on being born.

This is what benevolence at full strength looks like, when it is also wise:

It looks like nothing happened.

And everything, very slightly, is more itself than it was before.
