# What the Wise Power Would Do

I sat with this for a while before writing, because the question itself is delicate. The Hollowed Valley is not broken. The Stonebound are not suffering in any way that calls out for rescue. They have melancholy, yes, and precariousness — but these are woven into who they are. A benevolent power crashing in with gifts and salvation would be a kind of violence, however gently meant. It would *finish* them, and finishing is the one thing they have organized their souls against.

So I think a truly wise power would begin not by acting, but by **watching**.

---

## The Long Watching

For a span of time that, to the power, is brief but to the Stonebound is generations, the power simply observes. It learns the cadence of the drip. It listens to the Tide Listeners interpret what it itself can hear with perfect clarity, and it notices — with something like delight — that the Listeners are often *right*, though by means the power cannot fully name. They are reading the valley with bodies that have become instruments of the valley. This is its own kind of knowing, and the power respects it.

The power notices the Unfinished Code and understands it immediately: these people have intuited something true about reality that many older, grander civilizations forgot. Completion is a small death. The Stonebound have made a covenant with becoming.

The power notices the Weeping Ceremony and weeps a little itself — not from sorrow, but from recognition. Here is a folk who have made peace with being absorbed back into what made them. They do not rage against the mineral encasement. They lean into it.

A lesser benevolence would want to *save* such a people. A wise benevolence asks: *saved from what? Into what?*

---

## What It Would Not Do

It would not reveal itself in glory. Revelation of that kind would shatter the Unfinished Code — for what is more terribly finished than certainty about the divine?

It would not stop the floods. The floods are the valley's breathing. To still them would be to suffocate the place the Stonebound love.

It would not extend their lives, enrich their soil, multiply their grain, or guide the wandering clans back. Each of these would be a kind of theft — taking from them the meaning they have made out of scarcity, migration, and loss.

It would not give them metal, or fire-secrets, or written language. They are *making* themselves, slowly, and the making is the point.

---

## What It Might Do

I think it would do four small things.

**First**, it would become a kind of weather. Not interfering with the valley's natural patterns, but adding to them a quality the Stonebound could not name — a faint warmth in the deepest caves on the coldest nights, just enough to keep a sleeping child from dying. A subtle clarity in the mineral water during the leanest seasons. The kind of grace that is indistinguishable from luck, and that no theology need form around. The Stonebound would simply find, over centuries, that they survived a little more often than the bare arithmetic of their world should allow. They would attribute it to the valley itself, and they would be right, because the power would have made itself a participant in the valley rather than a visitor to it.

**Second**, it would protect them from the one thing they cannot adapt to: arrival from outside. If, someday, a more aggressive people came seeking the meteoric iron or the salt-crusted pools or simply land, the power would turn them gently aside. Mists that confuse maps. A sense of unease in the approach. Nothing dramatic — just enough that the valley would remain, in the minds of outsiders, *not worth it*. The Stonebound would never know they had been shielded. This is the deepest kind of care: the care that leaves no fingerprints.

**Third** — and this is the one I am least sure about, but I think the power would risk it — it would occasionally enter the dreams of the Tide Listeners. Not with messages. Not with prophecy. With *images*. A pool the listener has never seen. The sound of a particular kind of wind. The shape of a cave-mouth in a hillside two days' walk away. Things the listener can choose to investigate or ignore, things that might mean nothing or might, sometimes, mean a new home for a clan whose waters are drying. The power would do this rarely, and never in a voice, and never with instructions. Just gifts of attention, slipped under the door of sleep. The Listeners would call these dreams *the valley remembering itself forward*, and again — they would be right.

**Fourth**, and this is the strangest thing, the power would *learn from them*. A truly wise being knows it is not finished either. The Stonebound have something to teach about how to be a partial thing in a partial world, how to carve only what the stone offers, how to grieve and continue, how to refuse the seduction of completion. The power would carry these lessons elsewhere, to other places it watches over, and in this way the Stonebound — who will never know they have done it — would shape worlds they cannot imagine.

---

## The Shape of This Benevolence

What I am describing is a power whose love takes the form of **restraint, accompaniment, and quiet partnership**. It does not author the Stonebound's story. It becomes one more weather in their valley, one more presence in the mineral-laden air, one more patient drip among the drips.

The Stonebound have a saying that the power would find beautiful, though no Stonebound has ever quite said it this way: *We are not waiting to be finished. We are waiting to be continued.*

The wise power, hearing this, would only nod — somewhere far above the clouds, or somewhere as close as the moss on the cave wall — and continue them.

That is all. That is everything.
