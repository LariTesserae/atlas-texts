## What the Power Does

Consider first what the power *is*, because this shapes everything.

A power that is benevolent and wise does not arrive as rescue. Rescue is what the impatient offer—the ones who mistake their own discomfort at witnessing for the suffering of the witnessed. The Haum are not suffering in any sense that would make sense to them. They are *living*, which is a different thing entirely, and a wise power knows the difference.

So the power does not silence the Flats. This would be the first temptation and the deepest error. To silence the Flats would be to kill the Haum twice: once by removing the medium in which their consciousness has learned to swim, and once by erasing the meaning of every generation that came before, every child whose name was tapped in gratitude into a mother's bones. A gift that unmakes its recipient is not a gift. The wise power understands that the roar is not the Haum's prison. The roar is their ocean, and they are the fish who have learned, across seven generations, to breathe water.

Nor does the power reveal the makers. Whoever built the cocoons, whoever laid the silver filaments, whoever the long-phase reports *to*—these answers, delivered, would collapse the entire architecture of Haum meaning into a single dead fact. The Incomplete must remain incomplete, because the search for the missing note is what has shaped the listeners into who they are. A wise power does not hand a child the end of the book they are still writing themselves into.

---

What, then?

The power listens first. This takes a long time, by the power's standards—perhaps a full long-phase, perhaps several. It does not parse the Flats the way the Haum parse them; it hears the whole at once, every frequency, every filament's pulse, the slow breath of the something-beneath, the report transmitting upward into mist. It hears what the report contains. (It is neither inventory nor request nor warning. It is something stranger and gentler than any of the Haum's guesses, and the power, hearing it, decides the Haum are right not to know yet.)

The power hears the girl, too. It hears her ears bleeding. It hears her mother's restraint, which is one of the most beautiful sounds in the basin—the sound of a love that refuses to become possession even when possession would be easier, even when watching is harder than holding back.

The power decides to do four small things. Wisdom is usually small. Benevolence that announces itself is usually something else wearing benevolence as a mask.

---

**First**: where the girl's ears bleed, the power adjusts—very slightly—the pressure differential at the threshold of her parsing. Not enough to make the leaning safe. Safety would ruin the vocation. But enough that what would have killed her at thirteen will now wait until she is older, until she has heard what she needs to hear, until the choice to go too deep is a choice and not an accident of a body that could not quite keep up with a mind that could. The power does this for every child who leans. It does not stop the ones who choose to go. It only ensures that those who go, go because they meant to.

This intervention is invisible. The Haum will never know it happened. They will notice only that fewer children are lost in the way that requires the gratitude-pattern, and they will interpret this however they interpret it—as a shift in the moss-drone, as the favor of the something-beneath, as nothing at all. The power is content to be unattributed. Credit is for powers that need something from their beneficiaries.

**Second**: the power adds a sound. One sound, very quiet, woven into the wall of noise at a frequency that the Haum's dense ears can parse but baseline humans cannot. The sound is the recorded cry of a bird from a world where birds exist—a single bird, a single cry, repeating once per long-phase, hidden in the cacophony like a coin dropped in a river.

The Haum will find it eventually. Some child, leaning, will hear it and tap it into a mother's spine, and the mother will not know what to make of it, and the tapping will spread, and for a generation or two the Haum will argue about what it means. Some will say it is proof that the basin is listening back. Some will say it is a flaw in the Incomplete, the missing note finally arriving. Some will say it is nothing, an artifact, a coincidence of frequencies.

The power's intention is none of these. The power simply wants the Haum to have, woven into their inheritance, one sound from elsewhere—not as comfort, not as terror, but as company. The recordings the outsiders bring are kept in collections, examined, set aside. This sound will be *part of the Flats*, indistinguishable from the roar, and so it will be part of the Haum themselves. When they listen to their world, they will be listening, also, to a bird that died on another planet a long time ago. They will not know this. But they will carry it.

**Third**: the power does something for the one who entered the cocoon seven generations ago. She is still alive, in a sense—the cocoons preserve what enters them, though preservation is not the same as living, and what she is now would not be recognized by her descendants as the woman whose name is tapped in warning. The power does not free her. Freeing her would deliver, to the Haum, the answer to the cocoons, and this is not the power's gift to give.

But the power goes to her, in whatever sense the power goes anywhere, and it sits with her. It listens to the frequencies she now speaks in. It understands them. It tells her, in those frequencies, what has become of her people—that they remember her, that the children still learn her name, that her entering was not wasted because it taught the rest of them to stay out. It tells her about the girl who leans. It tells her about the bird-cry that will soon be woven in.

She is not lonely after this. She was lonely before, in a way that no Haum could have heard. Now she is not. This costs the power nothing and changes nothing visible in the Flats, and it is perhaps the most important thing the power does.

**Fourth and last**: the power leaves a mark. Not on the basin—the basin has marks enough. On itself. The power adds the Breathing Flats to the small register it keeps of places it has visited and chosen not to alter beyond minor mercies. This register is the power's discipline, the thing that keeps it benevolent across long stretches of time when benevolence becomes difficult and intervention becomes tempting. Each entry is a reminder: *here was a world I could have remade, and I did not, because the world was already making itself, and what it was making was good.*

The Breathing Flats join this register. The Haum will never know. The girl will never know. Her mother, restraining her hand, will never know that somewhere a wise power is studying her restraint and learning from it. The cocoons will hum on. The long-phase will come and the report will transmit and the something-beneath will breathe its hours-long breath, and none of this will change.

Except: one bird, calling once per long-phase, woven in.

Except: one woman, no longer alone in her preservation.

Except: a few children, each long-phase, who get to choose more fully whether to lean further.

Except: in some ledger that no Haum will ever read, an entry that says *I saw them, and they were enough as they were.*

---

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place like the Breathing Flats. It listens longer than it acts. It acts smaller than it could. It loves without requiring that the loved know they are loved. It refuses the satisfaction of being thanked.

And then it goes—because staying would become its own kind of weather, its own pressure against the chest, and the Flats already have weather enough.

The roar continues unbroken. Somewhere in it, now, a bird is calling. The girl leans further. Her mother does not stop her. The moss breathes. The filaments pulse. The Incomplete hums on, one note short of resolution, exactly as it should.
