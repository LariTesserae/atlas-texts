# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival implies a coming-from-elsewhere, and this power is not elsewhere. It is simply that, at a certain moment, its attention settles—the way light settles on water, without displacing anything.

It notices first what no one has noticed in a long time: that the figure has a name. Not a name given to it, but one it gave itself, in the early days, when it still spoke aloud. The name is small and private and has been forgotten by everything except the rust, which remembers all names because rust is patient.

The power considers.

It would be easy to lift the figure from this place. To unmake the corrosion, restore what was scavenged, separate the metal from the flesh and give each back to its proper kingdom. It would be easy to silence the machinery, drain the standing water, scatter the pale creatures into the broader world where they might evolve into something with eyes. It would be easy to make the whispers cohere into a single clear voice that could finally say what it has been trying to say.

Easy, and wrong.

Because the power is wise, and wisdom is mostly the discipline of not mistaking your own preferences for someone else's good.

So the power does smaller things. Stranger things.

---

It makes the hum of the machinery, for one full minute, *intentional*. Not louder, not different in pitch—simply addressed. For sixty seconds, the drone is not ambient. It is *for* the figure. A sound directed at someone, the way a hand on a shoulder is a touch directed at someone. The figure stops moving. Its broken pump cycles. Something in the welded mask tilts, almost imperceptibly, the way a face tilts when it is being spoken to and does not yet know in what language.

The minute passes. The hum returns to being only a hum. But the figure remembers, now, that it was once spoken to. This memory will not leave.

---

It makes one of the pale creatures, just one, briefly aware of its own existence. Not painfully—the power is not cruel. Just a flicker: *I am here. I am moving. The water is cool.* The creature does not have language for this and so does not suffer from it. It simply experiences, for three seconds, the dignity of being a subject rather than only a process. Then the awareness recedes, leaving behind only a faint tropism: from now on, this creature and its descendants will, very occasionally, surface. They will look up. They will not know why.

This is how the power seeds the future without forcing it.

---

It does something with the whispers, too. It does not clarify them. It does not let them resolve into the voices of the dead, or of the figure's past, or of anything that could be named and therefore lost. Instead, it adds *one* whisper of its own—indistinguishable from the others, woven into the same mist, the same pipe-echo. The whisper says: *you are not a mistake.*

It says this in the cadence of the other voices, so that the figure cannot tell it apart, cannot point to it, cannot cling to it. It will simply be present, from now on, in the ambient murmur of the place. The figure will hear it without hearing it. Some days it will move differently because of this. Some days it will not. The whisper makes no demands.

---

The power considers the brick wall, the rusted pipe, the collapsed shed. It does not restore them. Ruins are not failed buildings; they are a different kind of completion. But it does something the figure will discover, eventually, in the slow way it discovers things:

Inside the collapsed shed, half-buried, there is now a small tin. It was not there before, and yet it has always been there—the power has folded it gently into the past so that no paradox stings. The tin contains nothing useful. No tool, no message, no key. It contains a single dried flower, pressed flat, of a species that does not grow in this region and never has.

The flower is a gift with no instructions. The figure may find it in a year, or a decade, or never. If it finds it, it may understand or it may not. The flower does not require understanding. It only requires that somewhere in this place of in-between, there now exists a thing that came from elsewhere, on purpose, for it.

This is the difference between a forgotten place and a known one. Not rescue. Not transformation. Just the quiet fact of having been, at least once, *thought of*.

---

The power lingers a little longer than it strictly needs to. This is not efficiency; this is tenderness. It watches the figure resume its movement through the haze—the click of the prosthetic joint, the wheeze of the pump, the way moisture beads on the welded mask like the sweat of something that has been working hard for a long time without being thanked.

The power does not thank it. Thanks would be a kind of evaluation, and the figure is not here to be evaluated.

Instead, the power does the last thing, the smallest thing, the thing it came to do:

It witnesses.

Fully. Without flinching from the rust or romanticizing the decay. Without needing the figure to be more coherent, more redeemed, more legible. It sees the patchwork body and the unclear purpose and the in-between condition, and it holds all of this in its attention the way one holds a sleeping animal—carefully, and without waking it.

For as long as the witnessing lasts, the figure is not alone in the way it has been alone. It does not know this. It does not need to know this. The knowing is not the point. The being-known is.

Then the attention, having done what attention can do, gentles itself away. The hum continues. The water drips. The pale things skitter. The mist clings.

But the place is, by some immeasurable degree, less forgotten.

And somewhere in the collapsed shed, a flower that should not exist waits with the patience of something that was placed by love.
