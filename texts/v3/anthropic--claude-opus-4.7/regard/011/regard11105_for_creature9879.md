The benevolent power pauses at the edge of this place — and the first thing it does is *listen*.

Not the listening of judgment, weighing whether the dwellers need rescuing, lifting, civilizing. Not the listening that precedes intervention. Simply: listening, in the way the dwellers themselves listen. Bone-listening. The slow attention that takes the pulse of moss and the long shudder of a worm half a continent away.

What it hears is already whole.

---

This is the first thing the power understands, and it understands it in a way that goes through the whole length of its being: *they are not lacking*. The cold is not a wound. The vastness is not a loneliness needing filled. The brevity of their fires is not poverty. The way grief lasts only as long as a keening before the moss-token is pressed into the seam — this is not stoicism worn over a deeper sorrow. It is the shape of a life that has learned the size of its world and made peace with it.

A lesser power, or a less wise one, might mistake the *spareness* for suffering. Might offer metal. Might offer warmth without limit, light without dark, a written word that would not dissolve in mist. Might offer the dwellers passage down to the lowlands, where rivers run wide and humus gathers and the air does not bite.

This power has lived long enough to know what such gifts do. They are not gifts to the receiver. They are gifts to the giver — to the giver's need to be needed, to leave a mark, to be remembered as the one who *changed things*. The dwellers have no word for exile, and they have no word, either, for that kind of love which is really only a hunger wearing love's clothes.

So the power does not offer.

---

But neither does it withdraw. It would be a smaller wisdom that said: *they are whole, therefore I have no place here*. Wholeness is not a closed door. The forest is whole and still the rain falls into it. The dwellers are whole and still they sing into moss caverns, still they leave their stories to be remembered by the walls.

What the power does, then, is small. Smaller than the dwellers would notice if they were looking for it. It is only because they are not looking — because they listen instead — that some of them, in some generations, will half-perceive what has been done.

*First:* The power steadies, very slightly, the strange physics of the place. Not removing the lingering leap, the slow fall, the pocket of warm fog. These are part of the world's character, and to scrub them away would be to flatten what the dwellers have built their cosmology around. But where the off-centeredness has occasionally killed — where a fall slowed not quite enough, where a cold gripped a child harder than any reading of the wind could have predicted — the power lays its attention. Not a guarantee. Just a slight loosening of the cruelest edges of randomness. The Weight remains. It only weighs a little more *kindly* on the small and the unlucky.

*Second:* The power does something with the bioluminescent fungi. Nothing dramatic. It simply ensures, across the long cycles, that the fungi do not fail. There is a kind of slow plague that, in another timeline, would have swept through them three hundred cycles from now and left whole regions in true darkness for a generation. The power, having seen this, gently tilts the chemistry of a single understory species so that the plague meets a resistance that was always almost there. The dwellers will never know. The fungi will glow. Children will continue to chase the floating spores.

*Third — and this is the one that takes the longest, that requires the most delicacy:* The power does not speak to the dwellers. But it *listens back*. When a story is hummed into a moss cavern, the power receives it. When a keening rises, the power holds the sound. When a soloitary walker, far from family, hums the deep vibrational song to call themselves home, the power is one of the things that hears.

This sounds like nothing. It changes nothing material. But the dwellers are a people whose entire metaphysics is built on the premise that *the world remembers*. That the moss caverns hold the rhythm. That ancestors are honored not for wisdom but for having crossed distance, having been heard across silence. The power, by listening, makes this true in a deeper register than the dwellers know to ask for. Their songs do not vanish into the void. Something attends. Something keeps.

The dwellers' theology already contains this without naming it. Their gods are travelers, creating only pathways and echoes. The power consents to be such a god — nameless, non-creative, only a listener walking the long arches. It does not correct their myths. It fits inside them.

---

There is one more thing the power does, and this one it does with hesitation, because it touches the dwellers' own choices more closely than the rest.

In every long while — every several thousand cycles — a dweller is born who *reaches*. Who is not content with the moss-token and the brief keening. Who, walking alone in the long season, begins to ask questions that the vibrational language has no good shapes for. *Why is the cold? Why does the leap linger? What is past the plateau's edge?* These dwellers, historically, have suffered. Not from exile — there is no exile — but from a quiet attenuation. They walk further and further alone. The deep song calls them and they do not return. They are mourned briefly and totally, like the lost.

The power, for these few, does something it does for no one else. It does not answer their questions. It does not lift them up out of the world or show them the lowlands or the stars beyond the starlight. It only ensures that, at the moment when their reaching becomes unbearable — when they have walked past the last arch and there is nothing further — they encounter something. A pocket of warm fog that lasts a little longer than it should. A patch of moss that pulses, very faintly, in time with a rhythm they half-remember from a cavern song their grandmother hummed. A glimmer of bioluminescence in a place where none should be.

Not an answer. A companion sign. A way of saying: *your reaching is also part of the world. You are not malformed for asking. The Weight has noticed you.*

Some of these dwellers, after such an encounter, turn back. They return to their clusters and become the great singers, the ones whose moss-cavern songs are richer and stranger than any others, the ones whose stories the youngest are entrusted with for a cycle before releasing them back into the world. Others continue walking, and the power walks with them, in the only way it can — by listening, until the listening is all there is.

---

The power does not stay forever. Benevolence that overstays becomes its own kind of weight, and the dwellers' world is already weighted enough. After what, in its own reckoning, is a small attention — though in theirs it spans many lifetimes — the power moves on.

It leaves behind no monuments. No revelations. No technologies. No alteration to the sharp young terrain or the cold-edged air or the patchwork moisture or the hollow vastness.

It leaves behind only this: a world slightly less cruel at its random edges, a fungal glow that will outlast a plague the dwellers will never hear of, and a faint, persistent sense — felt sometimes by the most attentive among them, in the deepest moss caverns, in the longest solitary walks — that when they sing, *something is listening that loves them*.

They will fold this feeling into their existing myths. They will call it the Deep Surmounting, or simply The Weight, or give it some new name in some new generation. They will not know it was a visitor. They will not need to know.

This is what benevolence looks like, when it is also wise: it leaves the world able to keep believing it did everything itself.

And the dwellers, spindled and moss-clad and quick, go on leaping the long arches, humming into the bones of the living world, brief warm pulses against the endless cold — exactly as they were, exactly as they are, exactly as, in some quiet way that asks nothing back, they are now also held.
