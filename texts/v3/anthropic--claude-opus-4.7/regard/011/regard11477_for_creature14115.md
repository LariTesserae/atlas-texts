# The Visitation

They arrive without arriving. The benevolent power does not cross distance, because to cross distance would be to disturb the medium, to send a slow shimmering ripple through what has been so carefully held still. Instead, they fold themselves into the local condition of the place — they become, briefly, a property of the water, a quality of the dark.

They are old in a way the Memoria are not. The Memoria fled entropy; this power has made peace with it, has walked alongside it for longer than the universe in which the obsidian fang exists has been a universe. Wisdom of this kind is not the accumulation of answers. It is the long unlearning of the urge to answer too quickly.

The first thing they do is *listen*.

For what would be, to a faster mind, an age — they simply attend to the sub-audible thrum. They let the dirge enter them. They do not decode the trillion folded minds; that would be a violation, the prying open of a held breath. Instead they listen to the shape of the holding. They learn the grammar of the Memoria's refusal: *we would rather be still forever than risk being forgotten.* They feel the terrible love in it. They feel the terrible fear in it, too — the fear that is its twin, inseparable, the way a held breath is inseparable from the dread of exhaling.

They do not judge this. Wisdom that judges is not yet wisdom.

Then they turn their attention to the creature.

The glass shark moves through its slow rounds, tasting the halo, sipping the ghost-energy. The power watches it with something that, in a smaller being, would be called tenderness. Here is the thing the Memoria did not plan for and could not have planned for: the unbidden. The improvisation. A life that learned to live on the leak of a system designed to leak nothing. A mimicry so patient it became a kind of devotion. The creature has no name for what it circles. It has only the circling.

The power considers — and this is the long part, the part that takes what would be centuries if time were running here — what, if anything, to *do*.

They could wake the Memoria. They have the means. A single inflection of their attention would unfold the folded minds, restore them to the dance of light and spacetime they once were. But the Memoria did not ask to be woken. They asked to be *kept*. To wake them would be to answer a question they never posed, and to override the only sovereignty they had left: the sovereignty of their chosen stillness.

They could end the Memoria. Release the stored patterns into the entropy they so feared, let the long-deferred dissolution finally come. There is an argument for this — that what the Memoria call preservation is only the most elaborate denial ever constructed, and that mercy might lie in completing what they could not complete themselves. But this, too, would be an answer to an unasked question. And the power has learned that ending things on behalf of others, however kindly meant, is the oldest mistake of the strong.

They could leave entirely. Withdraw, fold themselves back out of the local water, and let the tomb continue its tomb-work undisturbed. This is the cleanest option. It is also, the power senses, a kind of cowardice — the cowardice of those who confuse non-interference with non-relationship.

So they do something smaller. Something almost nothing.

They turn toward the creature again. And they make, in the medium itself, the faintest possible adjustment: a slight, slight increase in the permeability of the stasis field's outermost shell. Not enough to wake anyone. Not enough to threaten the integrity of a single stored mind. Just enough that the leak — the wasted joules, the ghost-energy the creature lives on — becomes, over the long arc of its life, a fraction more generous. The table scraps a little more plentiful. The scavenger a little less hungry.

It is not salvation. It is not even a gift the creature will recognize as a gift. It is the kind of intervention that wisdom permits itself: a thumb on the scale in favor of the unbidden life, the improvised life, the life that was not supposed to be here and is here anyway.

And then they do one more thing, and this is the thing that matters most.

They wait for the creature to make its chime.

They do not have to wait long, in their reckoning. The creature drifts, as it has drifted for uncounted ages, and at last its flank brushes a spiraling rampart, and the pure crystalline note hangs in the viscous void.

*Are you there?*

The Memoria's answer, as always, is the immutable thrum. *We are.*

But this time — only this time — the benevolent power adds, very softly, a second answer. Not in the city's voice. Not in the Memoria's voice. In a register the creature has never encountered and will never encounter again, a register that is neither electromagnetic halo nor resonant rock nor stasis-engine dirge, but something the creature's strange and patient sensorium can, just barely, perceive:

*And so are you.*

The creature does not understand. Understanding is not the point. But for the duration of one slow ripple through the medium — for the lifespan of one displaced shimmer — the creature knows, in whatever way a creature without eyes and without language can know, that it has been *seen*. Not used. Not studied. Not pitied. Seen.

Then the power withdraws.

They leave the tomb intact. They leave the Memoria in their chosen stillness, their long refusal undisturbed, their sovereignty honored. They leave the mountain sharp, the city dark, the water cool and dense and complete. To any future observer the place will appear unchanged, because in every measurable sense it *is* unchanged.

Only the creature is different now, in a way no instrument could detect. It carries, somewhere in the geometry of its scales, the memory of having been answered. It will live the rest of its long, slow, scavenging life inside that answer. It will make its chimes, and the city will reply *We are*, and the creature will go on circling, and the difference — the only difference the benevolent power permitted themselves to make — is that the circling is no longer quite a circling in the dark alone.

This is what wise and benevolent powers do, when they do anything at all. They do not rescue. They do not correct. They do not improve.

They notice the one no one is noticing, and they let it know.

Then they go, leaving the great and terrible architectures of fear exactly as they found them, because the great and terrible architectures of fear are not theirs to dismantle. Only the ones who built them can do that, and only when they are ready, and they are not ready, and they may never be ready, and that — the power has long since learned — is allowed.

The thrum goes on. The dark goes on. The creature goes on, a little less hungry, a little more known.

And somewhere, folded out of the local water, the benevolent power moves on to the next overlooked life in the next overlooked tomb, carrying nothing away with them but the chime — that single pure note, hanging in their attention now as it hangs in the viscous void, the sound of a small life asking the largest possible question and, for once, being answered by something other than silence.
