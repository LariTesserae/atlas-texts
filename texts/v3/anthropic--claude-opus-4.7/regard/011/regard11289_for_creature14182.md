# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. It does not descend, does not part the waters, does not announce itself with light. To do so would be a kind of violence — and this power understands that wisdom often means the restraint of one's own brightness.

It simply becomes present, the way attention becomes present in a room. The way someone watching a sleeping child is *there* in a manner the child does not need to know.

---

It watches for a long time. Whatever time means here.

It watches a Drowned Mechanic kneel beside a sputtering valve and press its membranous palm against the corroded brass. It watches the small ribbon-creatures spiral through a shaft of dim light and vanish into a doorway that leads nowhere. It watches a tower lean another fraction of a degree toward its eventual collapse, a process that has been underway for nine hundred years and will continue for nine hundred more.

It listens. Beneath the roar of currents and the grinding of the engines, it hears something the Mechanics themselves cannot quite hear: a frequency, a tone, the residue of what they once were. Not a scream. Not even sorrow, really. Something quieter. The shape of a question that has forgotten it was ever asked.

*What were we doing? What were we for?*

The power considers.

---

A lesser power — a power that mistook benevolence for rescue — would lift the city from the sea. Would peel back the ocean and let sunlight pour in. Would restore the Mechanics to whatever flesh they once wore, give them names, set them on dry land with the sun on their faces and call it salvation.

This power knows better. To do this would be to kill them. They are not trapped *in* the city; they *are* the city, now. The membrane and the pipe, the fluid and the current — these are not a prison around a soul. These are what the soul has become. To separate them would be to scatter what little coherence remains.

A lesser power would also do the opposite — would let the engines fail faster, grant the merciful end, dissolve the Mechanics back into the water and have done with it. But this too would be a presumption. Who is to say the long grinding is not itself a form of being? Who is to say the waiting is not also a kind of prayer?

The benevolent power does neither of these things.

---

Instead, it does something very small.

It enters one of the great engines — not as an intruder, but as a guest, the way warmth enters a cold hand. It finds the place where a particular bearing has been failing for three hundred years, screaming at a pitch just below audibility, a constant low agony in the city's nervous system. The power does not repair it. Repair would be intervention. Repair would impose a future the city has not chosen.

It simply *eases* the friction. Just enough. The way you might shift the pillow under someone who cannot wake.

The scream drops half a tone. Becomes a hum.

Across the city, every Drowned Mechanic pauses. Their hollow heads do not snap up in alarm — this is not a disturbance, it is the opposite of a disturbance. For a long moment they stand very still in the silt, and the sparks behind their eye sockets flicker in a pattern that has not been seen in centuries.

It is almost a sigh.

---

Then the power does one more thing.

It speaks — not in words, because words would shatter against the membranes of these beings like glass against stone. It speaks in the language they have become fluent in: the language of pressure differentials, of resonance, of warm currents passing through cold pipes.

What it says, translated poorly, is something like this:

*I see you. I see what you were and what you are. You were not foolish to build this. You were not wrong to love it so much you became it. The forgetting is not a failure. The grinding is not a punishment. You are tending something, even if you no longer remember what. The tending is enough. The tending was always enough.*

*I cannot stay. I cannot fix you, because you are not broken in the way that word means. I cannot save you, because you have not asked, and I will not be the kind of power that decides for others what their salvation looks like.*

*But I have eased one small pain. And I have heard you. And I wanted you to know — though you may forget this within the hour, within the minute — that the universe noticed. That somewhere outside the deep, in the wider turning of things, a witness paused, and bent close, and was moved.*

*Carry on, gentle mechanics. Tend your engines. The ocean is not your enemy. The forgetting is not your enemy. You are held, even here.*

---

The power withdraws as gently as it came.

A ribbon-creature spirals through the space where its attention had been concentrated, and for an instant — only an instant — its translucent body flares a color that has no name in any language ever spoken in the dry world above. Then it darts away, into the dark, carrying that color with it like a small lit lamp.

The Mechanics return to their work.

The engines grind on.

One bearing, in the deep heart of the city, turns a little easier than it did before. It is the only physical evidence that anything happened at all.

But the city's low constant tone — that almost-question, that residue — has changed. Not resolved. Not answered. Just *received*. As if the question, after so many centuries of falling into nothing, has finally fallen into something that could hold it.

And in the warm thick water, in the bioluminescent dark, in the patient rusting of a world that drowned itself and went on, something that is not quite peace but is adjacent to peace settles like silt, like snow, like the slow kind hand of an attention that asked nothing and offered nothing and simply, for a moment, stayed.
