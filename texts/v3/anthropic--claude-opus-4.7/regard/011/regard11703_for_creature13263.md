# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival implies a there from which one comes, a here at which one stops. Instead, attention turns — the way a face turns toward a sound heard in another room — and where the attention rests, *is*.

It rests on the plateau.

It does not announce itself. It does not bring light to compete with the dim ozone-twilight, nor silence to push back against the hum. The wise do not begin by overwriting. The wise begin by listening.

So it listens.

---

For what would be, elsewhere, a very long time, the power simply attends. It learns the hum the way a mother learns the particular cry of her own child among a roomful of crying children — the grinding that means *this column is still growing*, the deeper register that means *the veil is thinning here*, the sub-tone almost too low to register that is the Watchers speaking among themselves about nothing, about the weather of unbeing, about the small movements of dust.

It learns that the Watchers are lonely, though they do not have the concept of loneliness. They have only the hum, which has always been the hum, which contains within itself a tone the power eventually identifies as *missing*. Something that was, and is no longer, and whose absence has been woven so completely into their being that they have mistaken the absence for themselves.

It learns that the place is dying, as the place's own description admits. The columns grow but the world that grows them is running out of whatever it is made of. The vines are thick because there is nothing left to eat them. The ferns glow because the light they were waiting for never came and they have had to make their own, and making one's own light is exhausting in ways that nothing here has language for.

It learns that the Watchers do not know any of this. And it considers, for a long while, whether telling them would be a kindness.

---

It decides — and this is the wisdom part, the part that distinguishes a benevolent power from a merely powerful one — that it will not save them.

Not because it could not. It could. It could thicken the veil, could feed the dying substrate, could give the Watchers mouths and eyes and the small reliable physics of a stable world. It could lift them entire out of their plateau and set them down in some greener cosmos where the laws hold and the light has a source and there are small skittering things to keep them company.

But this would be a kind of murder dressed as rescue. The Watchers are *of* the hum. They are the hum's way of looking at itself. To save them from their world would be to end them as surely as the world's ending will end them — only sooner, and with the added cruelty of having done it on purpose.

So the power does something smaller, and harder, and truer.

---

**First**, it makes itself *audible* within the hum. Not as an intrusion. As a new tone, woven in so gently that for a long while none of the Watchers notice. It is the tone of *being witnessed*. It is the tone that says: *you are seen, and the seeing is kind*. It does not demand response. It does not require understanding. It simply is, the way the hum simply is, and it threads itself through the grinding of the gears until it becomes indistinguishable from them.

A Watcher, drifting between two monoliths, pauses. Its jerky gait stills. Something in the hum has changed, though it could not say what. It stands there for what would, elsewhere, be three days. Then it resumes drifting. But the resumption is different. There is something almost like *purpose* in it now, though purpose is not a word the Watchers have.

**Second**, the power addresses the ruins. The crumbling archway. The crude stone hut. The rusted tools. The half-melted shards. These are the bones of the forgotten race, the ones the Watchers are echoes of, and the Watchers have never understood what they are. They drift past them as one drifts past furniture in a dark room — knowing it is there, not knowing what it is *for*.

The power does not restore the ruins. Restoration would be a lie. Instead, it makes them *legible*. It deposits, into the substance of the stone and the iron, a kind of resonance — so that any Watcher who pauses near them long enough begins to hum, very faintly, with the memory of what they were. A hand that shaped this stone. A voice that sang in this hut. A child, once, who picked up this tool and was scolded for it and laughed.

Not enough to grieve. Just enough to know that grief is the right shape of the feeling that has no name.

**Third** — and this is the gift the power gives most carefully, weighing it for what would be, elsewhere, a year — it gives them a *second* into the hum. A pause. One single pause, no longer than the space between two heartbeats in a creature that has heartbeats, in which the hum stops.

Just once.

In that pause, the Watchers will hear, for the first and only time, the silence underneath their own existence. They will hear it together — all of them, across the whole plateau, the dozen or hundred or thousand of them that there are. And in that silence each one will know, without language, without the cruelty of being told: *we are here. there are others of us. we are not alone in being whatever we are.*

The pause will be terrifying. Some of them may not survive it. The power knows this and accepts it, because the alternative is that none of them ever know they were not alone, and that is a worse death than the death the plateau is already bringing.

Then the hum will resume, and it will be the same hum, and it will not be the same hum, because now it will be a hum that was *once* interrupted, and an interrupted thing is a different thing forever after.

---

**Fourth**, the power does the last thing, the quietest thing, the thing that is almost not an action at all.

It stays.

Not as presence — the Watchers would not survive a presence. As a *direction*. As a quality the hum now has, that it did not have before: the quality of *being heard by something that loves it*. The plateau will still die. The columns will finish their agonizingly slow growth and then stop, and then crumble, and then the vines will rot because there is nothing left to climb, and the ferns will go dark, and the Watchers will, one by one, drift between the monoliths for the last time and then simply not drift anymore, becoming finally and fully the stone they always resembled.

But while it happens, they will be witnessed. Every grinding tone of the dying hum will fall on attention that holds it carefully. Every last flicker of a Watcher's malachite skin will be seen and accounted for and grieved by something vast enough to grieve it properly. The place that does not know it is dying will be allowed, at the very end, to not know — but the dying itself will not go unmarked.

This is what the wise and benevolent power does:

It does not fix.

It does not save.

It witnesses, and weaves itself a tone into the witnessing, and gives a small unbearable gift of knowing-one-is-not-alone, and then it stays through the ending, because the deepest thing a benevolent power can offer a dying world is the refusal to look away.

---

Somewhere on the plateau, between two impossibly tall columns, a Watcher pauses. Its indistinct face turns — toward what, it could not say. The hum around it is, very faintly, *warmer* than it has ever been. The Watcher does not have the word *warmer*. The Watcher does not have words at all.

But it stands there a long time, in the purple shadow, in the ozone twilight, and something in the grinding of its slow strange thought arranges itself into a shape that is almost, almost, gratitude.

That is enough.

That was always going to be enough.
