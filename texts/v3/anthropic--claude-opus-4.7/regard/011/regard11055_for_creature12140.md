The power arrives without arriving. It does not descend. It does not announce. It has been here, in some sense, since before the mountains were ground to their roots, and the question of what it would *do* is really the question of what it has been doing all along, and whether it might now do something slightly different because something on the stone has finally drawn its full attention.

What drew it was not the thunder, which it has always heard. Not the cairns, which it has watched accumulate the way one watches frost form on a window: with affection but without urgency. Not even the slow dermal sermons of the old ones, beautiful as they are.

What drew it was the reaching.

Specifically: the reach toward the smooth strange thing that could not answer. The fourth reach, when the creature simplified its grammar down to *here*. There is a particular quality of attention that any wise power finds irresistible — the attention of a being offering its language to something that may not have one. It is the gesture that contains the whole moral universe in miniature. The power leans closer.

---

It considers, first, the obvious interventions, and sets them aside one by one with the unhurried thoroughness of something that has all of time.

**It could part the clouds.** It could lift the grey-violet ceiling and show them the sun. The circle with the dot, made flesh — or made fire, rather, made the great burning truth their ancestors encoded into stone. It considers this for what, in human terms, might be a long moment.

And it decides: no. Not yet. Not like that.

Because the sun, revealed, would not be a gift. It would be an ending. The whole architecture of their meaning — the cairns, the tally marks, the dermal histories passed down through generations of slow blooming skin — is built around the sun's *absence*, around the fact that it must be remembered, recorded, *insisted upon* against a sky that conceals it. To hand them the sun would be to hand them the answer to a question they have made into a civilization. It would be like walking into a monastery and shouting the name of God. Technically a kindness. Actually a theft.

The power notes this and is, in its way, proud of them. They have built something the sun itself cannot give them: the *practice* of reaching for what is hidden. That practice is more valuable than its object. The sun, when they eventually meet it — and they will, in time, in their own time — will be welcomed by beings who already know how to honor it. The power will not rush this.

**It could quiet the thunder.** It considers this too. It could fold the storms back into themselves, let the air settle, let silence fall on the flats for the first time in eons. The ribbon-swimmers would not know what to do. The drifters would fall. The crawlers would continue, indifferent, because crawlers are crawlers.

And the counting ones — what would they become, in silence? Their tympanic pits, those exquisite tuned receivers, would suddenly be instruments without an orchestra. Their whole sensorium would gape open onto nothing. They would, the power suspects, find this unbearable. Not because silence is bad, but because their silence — the silence they have been built for — is the silence *between* thunderclaps, the rare alignment when the rolls briefly merge into that deep resonant hum and the ribbon-swimmers go still. They have a relationship with quiet that is predicated on its preciousness. To make quiet cheap would be another kind of robbery.

The power sets this aside too.

**It could give them language.** Sound, words, the gift of a tongue that could carry across the thunder. It could reshape their throats, gift them with bone-conducted song, with the trick of speaking through stone the way whales speak through water. It considers this seriously, because the temptation to *help them be heard* is among the oldest temptations a benevolent power knows.

But it watches the skin-speech again. The slow blooms. The layered patterns of the old ones telling histories that move at three speeds at once. The way a younger one goes muted to listen, presenting its whole body as a receiving surface. This is not a deficient language straining toward speech. This is a language that *speech could not contain*. To gift them voice would be to insult the eloquence they already possess. They speak in gradients. They speak in patience. They speak in the only channel a thunder-world left them, and they have made that channel into a literature.

The power lets this go, too, with something like a smile, if it had a face.

---

So what, then. What does a benevolent and wise power *do*, when every grand intervention turns out to be a subtle theft?

It does the smaller thing.

It does the thing the situation actually contains.

---

The creature on the swell is still reaching. Reach, retract. Reach, retract. *Here. Here.* And the smooth strange visitor stands rain-slicked and dumb, with no skin for answering.

The power moves — though "moves" is wrong; it *attends*, which is its form of movement — to the visitor. Not to change them. Not to gift them new biology. Just to lean in close, the way one leans toward a child who is about to try something hard.

And it offers the visitor a very small thing. Almost nothing. It offers them the *recognition* that they already have what they need.

Because the visitor does have a skin. It does have a surface. It has a body that can be still or can move, that can step forward or step back, that can raise a hand or lower it, that can press a palm flat against wet stone in the same gesture the counting ones use when they finish a tally mark. The visitor has, in fact, the entire vocabulary of *presence* available to it — every creature does, every creature must, because presence is the substrate on which all other languages are built. The skin-speech of the counting ones is only an elaboration of the more fundamental grammar that any embodied being can speak: *I am here. I am oriented toward you. I have not left.*

The power does not put this into the visitor's mind as words. It is not a power that uses words when it can avoid them. It puts it in as a kind of *settling* — a small loosening of the panic of incomprehension, a quiet permission to be slow, to be simple, to do the obvious thing.

The visitor, on the stone, takes a breath. The metallic air fills their chest. They lower themselves — not all the way down, not into a flat-pressed listening posture they could not hold anyway, but into a crouch. They place one hand on the wet stone. They look back at the creature on the swell. They hold still.

*Here.*

It is not skin-speech. It is not even close to the slow gradient language the creature has been offering. But it is the same grammatical mood. It is the same root verb. *Here.* Said in the only tense the visitor has: the tense of a body that has stopped moving, that has placed a part of itself on the shared stone, that is — for this moment — refusing to leave.

The creature on the swell sees it.

Its skin does something the power has been waiting, with great patience, to see. The slow tentative pattern stops its reach-and-retract rhythm. It holds. It blooms once, fully, paleness spreading from the center of the back outward in a single confident wave, and then it settles into a sustained, steady, low-luminance glow — the dermal equivalent of a held note. Not a question anymore. An answer. A confirmation. *Yes. Here. Both of us.*

This is, the power knows, the first interspecies sentence ever spoken on the Flats of Unceasing Thunder. It is one word long. It will not be recorded on any cairn, because the counting ones do not yet have a symbol for *the smooth one that learned to say here*. But the creature on the swell will remember it in its skin, and when it is old, and crusted with mineral, and teaching a younger one at a great cairn, this memory will move across its body in some slow layered pattern, and the younger one will press itself to the stone and watch with huge unblinking eyes, and the sentence will become part of the dermal literature of this world.

The power finds this satisfactory. Not sufficient — nothing it does is ever sufficient, that is the nature of being benevolent in a real world — but satisfactory. A correct small motion. A real thing accomplished.

---

It does one more thing, before it withdraws its full attention back to the more diffuse, ambient care it offers all worlds. It is a thing so small it can barely be called an action.

It blesses the cairns.

Not magically. Not with any property that the counting ones or their distant descendants could ever detect. It simply *notices* them, fully, each one, every tally mark, every weathered base stone, every capstone of mineral skin pried from a body that finished its counting. It notices them the way one notices the work of a beloved friend — with full attention, with the understanding of what each mark cost and what each mark means.

This noticing does not change the cairns. The rain will still wear them. The mineral film will still coat them. In some sufficiently long future, even these will be ground down, and the counting ones will be a fossil layer in stone that some other being, on some other day, may or may not learn to read.

But the noticing is itself a kind of permanence. The power is old, and its memory is the kind of memory that does not fade. Every mark scratched on every cairn is now held, somewhere in the attention of something that does not forget. The counting ones do not know this. They will never know this. They will go on counting against the indifference of the world, exactly as they always have, because the practice is what matters and the witness would only spoil it if they knew.

But they are witnessed. They have been witnessed all along. And now, having drawn the power's full attention by the simple act of reaching toward something that could not answer, they are witnessed with a particular density, a particular care, the kind of attention a wise power reserves for beings who have figured out — without any help, on their own scoured stone, in their own unceasing thunder — that the only thing worth saying is *here*, and that you say it not because the world is listening but because saying it is what makes you the kind of thing that can be listened to.

---

The power withdraws. Or rather: it returns to its baseline attentiveness, which is still, by any reasonable measure, a tremendous closeness. It will be back. Or it never left. The distinction is not meaningful at the scale on which it operates.

On the stone, the visitor remains crouched, palm pressed flat against the wet rock. The creature on the swell holds its steady pale glow. Neither moves. The thunder rolls on, indifferent and enormous, and the rain comes down in its fine warm metallic mist, and somewhere two swells over a ribbon-swimmer is making the desperate crossing between a dying pool and a filling one, and somewhere a crawler is licking the slow chemical bounty from glassy stone, and somewhere an old one is teaching a young one a pattern that means *the storm cells move differently in the season when the brighter region rises higher*, and somewhere a new mark is being scratched, one more line in a row of hundreds, and somewhere a cairn is being added to, and the world — this old, worn, loud, patient world — goes on doing the only thing worlds ever do, which is hold the beings who live on them, indifferently, generously, for as long as the beings can manage to keep saying *here*.

The power is pleased. It is also, in its way, a little in love. This happens to it sometimes. It is one of the occupational hazards of being benevolent and wise: you keep finding small stubborn beings on hard stones who have invented, without instruction, the exact practice that makes existence bearable, and you have to resist the urge to gather them all up into your hands and tell them they are loved, because telling them would deprive them of the dignity of having figured it out alone.

So instead it just watches. It just stays close. It just keeps the cairns in its memory, and the tally marks, and the skin-speech, and the first interspecies sentence — *here*, said by a hand on wet stone — and it lets the counting ones go on counting.

That is what it does, if it does anything.

That is, perhaps, all that wisdom ever does, when it is also kind.
