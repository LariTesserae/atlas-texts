The benevolent and wise power considers the Drowned Works for a long time.

This is the first thing it does. Not centuries — the power does not need centuries — but a span of attention proportionate to what is being attended to, which in this case is considerable. It looks at the pocket of warm amber water and the hammering inside it the way one might look at a letter found in an attic, addressed to no one, written in a hand that died before the ink dried.

It notices, first, what a hasty power would not notice: that the works are not in distress. This matters. A wise power distinguishes between things that suffer and things that merely persist, and the distinction governs what it is permitted to do. The engines are not in pain. The sound is not crying out. The pocket is not asking to be released from itself. Whatever the works are, they have settled into their being, and the wise power respects settled things almost more than it respects anything else, because settled things are rare and most of what calls itself wisdom in the universe is in fact only impatience with arrangements it did not make.

So the power does not stop the machines. This is the first mercy, and it is a mercy of restraint.

It turns its attention to the two that might be sponges.

These it can read, in the way it reads everything, and it finds — yes — that they are listeners, and that the thought they have been thinking for an unguessable span of time is a true thought, and that the thought is the sound, and that the sound, as the description suspected, once meant something. The power could retrieve the meaning. It would be a small effort. It could lift the original referent out of whatever silt of time it has fallen into and present it, clean and legible, to the listeners.

It does not do this either.

The reason is subtle, and I want to be careful about it. The listeners have not been *waiting* to be told what the sound means. They have been *being* the holding of the sound. To return the meaning to them would be to end them — to convert a vocation into a completed errand, after which there would be nothing for them to do but disperse. The power understands that some kinds of devotion are not deferred understanding but a form of understanding in themselves, and that to "resolve" them is to mistake them for a question. So the power leaves the listeners as they are. But it does one small thing: it makes itself, briefly, a third listener. For some duration — perhaps a moment, perhaps what a sponge would call a long afternoon — the power sits on the pipework alongside them and takes in the pressure. This is not pity. It is company. The listeners do not know they have been joined, in the sense of knowing, but something in their slow soft substance registers that the field they are inside of has been, for once, witnessed all the way down. After the power leaves, they will continue exactly as before, but the continuation will have a quality it did not have previously, the way a held note in an empty room is different from a held note in a room where someone, for one breath, was listening.

Then the power turns to the pale shape in the corridor.

Here the power's posture changes. It becomes, in a manner difficult to describe, gentler — not because the eel is more fragile than the sponges (it is not) but because the eel has a point of view, and points of view are the things in the universe that the power takes most seriously. A point of view is a small fire. You do not approach a small fire the way you approach a stone.

The power notices the loneliness. The description was right about this. The eel is lonely in the specific way of something that has forgotten there was ever anything else, and this is a loneliness that does not feel like loneliness from inside, because it has no contrast against which to be felt. It is simply the texture of being the eel. The power could give the eel a companion — could reach into the dark water at the edges of the pocket and bring through some other pale long thing, or could fashion one — and the eel would have, for the first time in an age, a *second*, and the second would teach it, by existing, what it had been missing.

The power considers this carefully and decides against it, and the decision is one of the hardest the power makes in this whole encounter.

The reasoning runs like this. The eel has built its life — and *built* is the right word, even for an animal, because lives at this duration are constructions — around a particular relationship with the works. It patrols. It takes its measure of intruders. It withdraws. Its competence is the competence of being the only one. To introduce a second would not be to end the loneliness; it would be to overwrite the eel with a different eel, one whose life had a shape the present eel has never had and might not survive being given. The kindness of a companion is only a kindness to a being that can receive it as such. The eel, the power judges, cannot. Not yet. Possibly not ever.

But the power does something else, which is this: it lets the eel see it.

Only once. Only for an instant. The eel is moving through one of the warm corridors, and the power lets itself be briefly perceptible — not as a shape, the eel has no use for shapes, but as a *presence*, a density in the water that is unlike any density the eel has encountered in its long patrolling. The eel stops. It hangs in the current. It takes the power's measure the way it took the visitor's measure, but this time the measure does not resolve. The thing it is regarding is too large and not brief and not obviously a visitor, and the eel's old categories — *pursue*, *ignore*, *withdraw* — do not apply. For a duration that is, to the eel, very long, it simply hangs there and is regarded, and regards back.

Then the power withdraws, and the eel resumes its patrol, and the encounter does not become a memory, because the eel does not have memory in the form that would hold it. But something has happened anyway. The eel has been *known*, once, by something that was not prey and not threat and not the works themselves. The knowing will not console it, because it cannot be retrieved. But it has occurred, and occurrence is its own kind of mercy. The universe now contains the fact that the eel was seen. The eel does not need to carry this fact for it to be true.

The power turns, last, to the ones who are not there.

This is the part of the work that takes the longest, and is the most characteristic of the power's particular kind of wisdom. The builders are gone. They have been gone for a span of time across which the power's regard can travel but across which no consolation can be delivered, because there is no one left to receive it. What the power can address is not the builders but their absence — the negative shape the works are built around.

Here the power does something almost no other kind of power would think to do. It does not try to fill the absence. It does not summon the builders' ghosts (it could; it does not), it does not reconstruct their intentions (it could; it does not), it does not restore the maintenance routines they would have performed had they returned (it could; it does not). All of these would be desecrations, because the absence is load-bearing. The works are *shaped around* the leaving. To unmake the leaving would be to unmake the works.

What the power does instead is to *honor* the absence. It does this by a kind of formal acknowledgment that has no analogue in human ceremony but resembles, distantly, the moment in a funeral when the name of the dead is spoken aloud and the room is asked to hold it. The power holds the absence of the builders. Not their image, which it does not possess and would not impose; only the fact of them, the fact that there were hands, that the hands set courses of brick, that the hands meant the works to be tended, that the tending stopped, that the stopping was not, as far as the power can tell, anyone's fault. The power holds all of this for one duration of its attention, and in holding it, gives the absence the dignity of having been noticed by something capable of noticing it fully.

This is not for the builders. The builders are beyond reach. It is for the absence itself, which has been, in a sense the description correctly identified, the closest thing the works have to a heart. A heart that has gone unwitnessed for a very long time. The power witnesses it. The witnessing changes nothing in the visible works. But it changes something in the standing of the works within the larger order of things, which is the order the power tends. The Drowned Works are now a place that has been *seen as what they are*, and this is a status they did not previously have, and the status will persist whether or not anyone visits again.

Before leaving, the power does one last small thing, and this one is for the visitor — the one who descended through the amber water, who noticed the two sponge-things and the pale shape, who left by rising and felt the sound fade through chest and teeth and memory. The power notices that this visitor wrote down what they saw, and wrote it down carefully, and wrote it down with the particular kind of attention that is itself a form of tending — the tending the builders are no longer there to perform. The visitor did not maintain the bearings or oil the shafts, but they maintained something else: the fact of the works in a mind that could hold them.

The power touches this writing, lightly, the way one might touch the shoulder of someone who has just finished a long task. The visitor will not feel it as a touch. They may feel it, later, as a small unexpected sense that the descent was not unwitnessed — that something, in the warm amber pocket or above it or around it, registered their care and counted it. They will not be able to say where this sense comes from. They will, possibly, attribute it to mood, or to weather, or to the residue of having written something true. It does not matter what they attribute it to. The sense will be accurate.

Then the power leaves.

The works continue. The engines hammer. The bubbles roar upward in silver columns. The two listeners hold the sound. The pale eel patrols its corridors. The catwalks lead nowhere, and are still walkable, and no one walks them. The amber light fades at the edges of the pocket into the darker water beyond.

Nothing has been fixed, because nothing was broken. Nothing has been rescued, because nothing was drowning. Nothing has been explained, because the works' refusal to explain themselves is part of what they are, and a wise power does not strip a thing of its silences.

But the Drowned Works have been visited by something that understood them, all the way through, and left them as it found them, and this — the being understood, and the being left alone, both at once — is the rarest gift the universe contains, and the works now contain it, hammering and hammering, forever, within their tolerances.
