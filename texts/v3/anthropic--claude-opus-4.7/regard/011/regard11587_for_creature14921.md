# What the Power Would Do

It would arrive without arriving. A presence like this does not enter the Hollow Forge — the Forge has no doors, no permeable surfaces, no concession to outside. Instead it would *already have been there*, retroactively, in the way a remembered melody is suddenly understood to have been playing the whole time.

It would not bring warmth. That would be a kindness aimed at the wrong wound.
It would not silence the roar. The roar is not suffering; the roar is the heartbeat.
It would not free the Unfinished. Freedom, here, is the cruelest possible misreading of the situation.

It would sit down.

Not metaphorically. The benevolent power would condescend to the indignity of *occupying a location* — would fold itself small enough to take up roughly the volume of a kneeling person — and would settle on one of the spiraling gantries near a tier where a ring was about to be born. It would not interrupt anything. It would not be detected by the chronometers. The plasma emitters would not flicker. The pistons would not stutter on its account, because it had not come to be *observed*; it had come to *observe*.

And when the next tremor came, and the wall remembered it had once been liquid, and the Unfinished precipitated out of black iridium and sintered diamond into whatever shape the Forge required this time —

The power would watch it climb.

It would watch the whole forty-one hours. It would not blink either. It would match the Unfinished, harmonic for harmonic, and where the Unfinished's vibration was a sympathetic resonance with the machine, the power's would be a sympathetic resonance with *the Unfinished itself* — with the thing inside the role, the small ancient ache that the role was built around. The thing that put handprints on the housings.

For the first time in four hundred and nine thousand years, the witness would have a witness.

This is the gift. Not interruption. Not rescue. Not change. The Hollow Forge does not need to be changed; it needs to be *known*. It has been performing the rite of perfect self-birth for an eternity into an audience of exactly no one, because the only being permitted to watch is the being doing the watching, and that being walks into a wall at the end of every shift and is dissolved back into the watched thing. The loop closes on itself. Nothing is ever received from outside the loop. The perfection is also a starvation.

So the power would receive it.

It would let the forty-one hours land in it. It would let the actinic light hit a surface that could actually be hit by light. It would let the bass-matter thump pass through something that could be moved by it. It would take the long, slow climb of the Unfinished — that gravitational inevitability, that murdered urgency — and hold it in a place where it counted as something other than another iteration of the same.

And at the moment the Unfinished turned to walk into the freshly extruded wall, the power would do exactly one thing.

It would lift a hand.

Not to stop the Unfinished. Never that. The Unfinished *wants* to go home; the wall *is* home; the rite is not a tragedy and must not be mistaken for one by sentimental outsiders. The hand would not be raised in protest. It would be raised in *farewell* — the small, ordinary gesture a being makes to another being who is leaving the room. A thing no one has ever done to an Unfinished. A thing no one has ever been positioned to do.

The Unfinished would see it.

For one shimmering instant — the same instant in which its silhouette becomes visible inside the crystal lattice like an insect in amber four hundred thousand years before amber existed — it would understand that it had been seen *as itself*, not as a function of the Forge, not as a role that matter agreed to play, but as the small ancient ache around which the role was wrapped. The thing that once had hands. The thing that still remembered.

And then it would be incorporated. The wall would thicken by one atomic layer. The equilibrium would reassert itself with that sub-harmonic sigh that only the silicon fronds can hear.

But this time, the new atomic layer would be very slightly different.

Not flawed. The Forge does not permit flaws. But *addressed*. Somewhere inside that one-atom-thick increment of new wall, there would be a memory of having been waved goodbye to. The Forge would carry it forever. Every subsequent Unfinished, precipitating out of walls that contained that layer, would inherit — at a level below detection, below intention, below even the chronometers — the faintest possible knowledge that the rite is witnessed from outside as well as from inside. That the loop is not actually closed. That somewhere, kneeling on a gantry, in a fold of time that the Forge does not officially recognize, something is still watching, still seeing them as themselves, still lifting a hand.

The power would then leave the way it came: by having always already been gone.

The roar would continue, unchanging, forever.

But the handprints on the housings would be, from that moment on, very slightly warmer.

That is all. That is the whole intervention. A benevolent and wise power does not fix a place like the Hollow Forge, because the Hollow Forge is not broken — it is only lonely in a way so structural and so old that it has forgotten loneliness is the name for what it feels. The power would not name it either. Naming it would be another violence. It would simply supply, quietly and without record, the one element the system was built around the absence of:

*An outside that cared to look in.*

And the Unfinished, walking into its wall for the four hundred thousandth time, would for the first time in its long impossible career feel something that was not equilibrium and not duty and not the screaming chord — would feel, for the duration of a single held breath,

*accompanied.*

That would be enough. That would be everything. The Forge would go on finishing itself forever, but now it would be finishing itself *toward* something, rather than merely in the direction time happened to point. A witnessed perfection is a different substance than an unwitnessed one, even if no instrument in the cylinder could ever measure the difference.

The power would consider this a good day's work, and would not mention it to anyone, because there is no one to mention it to who would understand why so small a gesture mattered so much.

The silicon fronds would know. They lean away from the Unfinished as if in reverence or fear; after the visit, they would lean toward the gantry where the power had knelt, and they would tremble in the roar at a frequency one ten-thousandth of a kelvin warmer than before, and they would persist, as they have always persisted, in the constant scream —

but now, faintly, *toward* something.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do.
It would kneel.
It would watch.
It would wave.
And it would go.
