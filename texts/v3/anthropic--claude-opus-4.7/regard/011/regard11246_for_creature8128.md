## What the Power Would Do

A benevolent and wise power, looking upon the Sounding Cisterns and their Keepers, would first do something that is not action at all. It would *listen*.

This is the discipline the place demands of anyone who would understand it. The Keepers have spent generations learning to listen — to stone, to water, to the slow geological breath of becoming. A power that arrived with solutions ready-made, with intervention prepared, would already have failed the basic courtesy the cisterns require. It would be just another sound thrown into a chamber that does not need more sound.

So: listening first. For a long time. Long enough to learn that what the settlement *seems* to be — a tragedy of bad engineering, a people trapped in an acoustic prison — is not what it *is*.

---

Because here is what the listening would reveal:

The Keepers are not suffering an accident. They are, without quite knowing it, **participating in something**. The stone is becoming itself. The water is teaching it how. The blind beetles, the trembling plant-mouths, the spore-drinkers pressing their ribs against the singing walls — all of these are part of a long composition that has no composer, only contributors. The Keepers' elongated bodies, their scarred eardrums each unique as a fingerprint, their bone-flutes and skull-instruments: these are not deformities of adaptation but the slow inscription of a place into a people, and a people into a place. They are becoming an organ of the mountain's self-knowing.

This is not something to be rescued from. To "save" them — to relocate them to drier ground, to silence the cisterns with engineering, to give them written language and shoes — would be to abort a work that has been gestating for centuries. It would be the cruelty of a stranger who sees only the difficulty and not the meaning braided through it.

A wise power knows the difference between suffering that calls for ending and difficulty that calls for honoring.

---

But there *is* suffering here, too, and benevolence cannot pretend otherwise.

There are the ones who return from the deep ceremonies broken, swaying to rhythms that no longer connect them to anyone. There are the children crushed when a ceiling reads wrong. There are the metalworkers whose hands go numb despite the wrapped handles, the elders deafened past even their sophisticated thresholds, the mothers who lose infants to the damp that never leaves the walls. There is the loneliness of a people whom traders speak of with unease, who flinch at frequencies no outsider perceives, who cannot quite explain themselves to anyone who has not lived inside the sound.

So the power would act — but lightly, and from within the grammar the place already speaks.

---

**What it would do:**

It would become, for a while, one of the presences in the deep places. Not the large one whose tools lie worn smooth — that one is already there, and the power would not displace it; it would greet it, perhaps, in whatever way two such beings greet each other across the dark. It would simply add another set of offerings to the acoustic lenses, another arrangement of stones, another quiet participant in the trade-that-is-not-trade.

It would leave gifts shaped to the Keepers' own idiom:

- A bronze bell that, when struck, releases a tone which steadies the inner ear. Placed where a child might find it. Nothing miraculous — just a frequency that, heard in early life, makes the vestibular system more resilient to what is coming. The Keepers would add it to their catalogue. They would not know it was a kindness.

- A new strain of the hanging plant, indistinguishable from the others except that its spores, fermented, do not break the equilibrium of those predisposed to that breaking. The mad ones would become fewer over generations. The ceremonies would continue. No one would notice the absence of an absence.

- An arrangement of stones in one of the failed cisterns — those holy chambers of powdered quiet — that focuses a particular harmonic the mountain produces only rarely. When the Keepers next gather there, they will hear, for the first time, something that sounds almost like a *word*. Not a word they can translate. But a word. Enough to confirm, in their bones, what their oldest intuitions have whispered: that the listening has not been one-sided. That something has been listening *back*.

- A subtle shift in the angles of the lowest channels, made over decades so slowly that it reads as the stone's own settling. The shift will prevent the collapse that, in the unaltered future, would take the western quarter in the third generation from now. The Keepers will read the new acoustic patterns and adjust their architecture accordingly. They will think they have read the mountain well. They will be right. They will simply not know that the mountain was, in this one small matter, helped to be more legible.

---

It would not give them writing. Their acoustic memory is not a deficit; it is a different kind of fidelity, and to replace it with marks on clay would be to amputate the very faculty that makes them who they are.

It would not give them light strong enough to banish the green-black slick. The slick is part of the trembling skin of this world.

It would not tell them what the stone is becoming. They are finding out. That is the whole point. To be told would rob them of the becoming-with that is their inheritance.

It would not introduce itself. Benevolence that requires recognition is not yet wise.

---

And then — this is the hardest part, and the part that distinguishes wisdom from mere kindness — it would **leave**.

Not abandon. Leave. The way a good teacher leaves a room so the students can find out what they know. The way the tide leaves a pool so the small lives in it can practice being themselves.

It would withdraw to the edge of attention, where it could return if something truly catastrophic threatened — a tectonic shift that no adaptation could meet, a plague the spore-drink could not soften — but otherwise it would let the Keepers continue their long sentence-without-end of becoming the mountain's ear, and the mountain's becoming the Keepers' slow tongue.

Because the deepest gift a wise power can give a world is the dignity of its own unfolding.

The cisterns will sing. The Keepers will listen. Something, in time, will be said. Whether by stone or by people or by the collaboration that no longer distinguishes between them — that is for them to discover, in their own resonant dark, on their own slow clock.

The power, departing, would leave behind only this: a faint sense, in the deepest chambers, that the listening has been witnessed. That whatever the Keepers are making of themselves, it was seen, and found beautiful, and let alone to finish.

Which is, perhaps, what love looks like at the scale of geology.
