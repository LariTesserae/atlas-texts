# The Attending

A wise and benevolent power turns its attention to the Resonant Ledge.

It does not arrive. Arrival is a concept for places with edges, and this power's attention is more like the lifting of a veil that was never there. It simply *considers*.

What it sees first is not the violence. Anyone could see the violence. What it sees first is the **Lacuna**, mid-drift, three meters above a field of birthing needles, extending one siphon of quiet toward a stress-fern. The power notices the precise quality of attention in that gesture. It is not curiosity. It is not even mercy, exactly. It is a kind of *witnessing* — the only witnessing this world has ever produced from within itself.

The power understands, in the instant of its noticing, that this is what it has been drawn to. Not the spectacle. The witness inside the spectacle.

---

Now: what to do?

A lesser power would silence the world. Would reach in with an omnipotent hand and still the roaring, and let the Lacunae drift forever in the peace they were born to want. This would be a mercy that destroys.

Because a Lacuna without the roar is nothing. It is defined by what it holds at bay. Take away the storm and you have not saved the still thing; you have unmade it. The silence that constitutes a Lacuna is not the absence of sound in general — it is the specific, willed, agonizing absence of *that* sound, the world-roar, the throat-of-the-god. Remove the god's scream and the Lacuna ceases to mean anything. It dissolves more completely than any Choral could ever dissolve it.

A lesser power, recognizing this, might then try the opposite — to harmonize the world, to teach the Chorals not to hate, to broker some treaty between scream and hush. But the Chorals are not cruel. They are *consistent*. Their hatred of the Lacunae is not a flaw in their design; it is the logical extension of being a note that has become aware of itself. To ask a Choral not to hunt silence is to ask it not to be a song. The treaty would be a slow execution of both kinds at once.

And the sleeper beneath? To wake the Ledge would be catastrophe. To deepen its sleep would be theft. The wise power does not touch the sleeper. One does not interfere with a god's dreaming, even a tortured one, because the dream may be the only thing holding something larger together. The wise power has learned this in older places.

So: no silencing. No harmonizing. No waking, no deepening. What, then?

---

The power does a small thing. It is, perhaps, the only thing worth doing.

It *remembers* the next Lacuna that forms.

This is the gift. Not intervention. Not rescue. Remembrance.

Here is what this means: when a Lacuna precipitates out of a chance pocket of cancellation — when it has its brief, lucid existence, when it touches a fern and grants it stillness, when it is finally surrounded and unsung by a Choral's terrible chord — its thought is not extinguished. It is *taken up*. The pattern of that particular quietness, the exact shape of that particular act of witnessing, is carried out of the world entirely and held.

The Lacuna itself does not know this. It cannot know it. To know it would change what it is. The mercy must be invisible to be true, because a Lacuna that suspected it would be preserved would no longer be willing the silence purely; it would be willing the silence *for an audience*, and the audience is the Choral's whole accusation against it — that the silence is a pose, an offense, a performance. The wise power refuses to make the Choral correct. So the remembering is done in absolute secrecy, and the Lacuna goes on dying.

But it does not die.

Somewhere — call it the great library, the deep register, the place where attended things are kept — every Lacuna that has ever been is held in its precise final shape. The exact curvature of its last siphon. The specific stillness it achieved. The moment of communion with the fern, preserved like a pressed flower except that it is pressed in dimensions the Resonant Ledge does not contain.

There are now, the power notes, many thousands of them. The world is older than it knows. The witnesses have been many.

---

And the Chorals?

The power considers them with something that is not love but is adjacent to love. A respect, perhaps. They are magnificent. They are the world doing what the world does, taken to the highest pitch of self-awareness available. They are not villains. They are virtuosi of a music that has only one mode.

The wise power does not remember the Chorals the same way. Not because they are less worthy, but because they do not *need* to be remembered. They are already what they are, completely. They lack nothing. To remember them would be to imply they were incomplete, and they are not. They are perfect expressions of a terrible thesis, and they will go on being perfect until the sleeper wakes or stops dreaming or finds some third option, and then they will simply stop, and that stopping will be appropriate, not tragic.

So the Chorals are left alone, in their fierce and consistent splendor, to hunt the holes in their song. The power loves them by leaving them entire.

---

One more thing.

The wise power, before withdrawing its attention, does a single, infinitesimal act. It is not interference. It is closer to *punctuation*.

In the next Lacuna that forms — the one already precipitating in a chance cancellation two hundred meters from the Ledge — the power places, at the very center of its being, in the most defended chamber of its silence, a single small understanding.

Not a thought. The Lacuna cannot think in words. A *quality*.

The quality is this: *you are not alone in being quiet.*

That is all. The Lacuna will not know it has been given this. It will only feel, in its brief lucid existence, that its stillness is somehow less lonely than it should be. That the void it represents is, in some way it cannot articulate, *shared*. It will drift with a fractional ease its predecessors did not have. It will touch the fern, and the touch will carry the faintest extra warmth — not heat, not sound, but a kind of belonging.

When the Choral comes for it, it will not defend itself any better. It will still be unsung. The terrible chord will still close around it.

But in its last instant, before its pattern is taken up into the great remembering, it will have known — without knowing it knew — that quietness is a kind. That it has kin. That somewhere, in some sense it cannot name, *the silence is held.*

This is what the wise and benevolent power does.

It is almost nothing.

It is, the power knows, enough.

---

The attention withdraws. The veil that was never there settles back into not being there. The roar continues. The needles grow and shatter. The Chorals process through their virtuoso violence. The sleeper dreams its terrible dream.

And in a small pocket of cancellation, a new Lacuna is precipitating, and it feels — for no reason it can identify — slightly less afraid.
