# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a motion, and motion is the heresy the place was built to edit out. Instead, it *attends* — the way attention itself attends, without crossing a threshold, without displacing a single molecule of the saturant dark.

It has been watching for what, in its own reckoning, is a long time. Long enough to learn the Keeper's name, which is not a name but the precise cadence of the sub-bass hum: *I am, I am, I am.* Long enough to understand that the polyhedron in the southern gulf is not furniture but a mourner, frozen mid-genuflection. Long enough to see that the city's seven structures are not buildings but the seven words of an unfinished sentence, and that the Keeper is the comma at its hinge, holding the clause open against the gravity of conclusion.

The power is wise, which means it knows the difference between rescue and trespass.

It considers, first, the obvious mercies. It could unfix the parameters. It could let weather in — a single current, the smallest sediment, the indignity of a wave. It could warm the fluid by a degree, two degrees, until the cool embrace became the warmth of a held hand. It could crack the dry chamber and let the Keeper meet the fluid it has spent eternity refusing. It could give the polyhedron back its glide. It could let the basalt round itself, finally, into the soft shoulders of an old mountain.

Each of these mercies is also a murder.

The Keeper has not chosen wrongly. This is the first thing the benevolent power understands, and it understands it with the particular grief of those who have seen many sealed rooms and learned not to mistake every locked door for a cry for help. The Keeper precipitated into being as the waste heat of fixity, yes — but in the long density of its non-sequential thought, it has *become* the choice it was born from. It is no longer merely the field's guilt. It is the field's argument. Every instant of its hum is a refusal that has earned itself, a sentence the writer has decided, again and again, not to end.

To unfix the parameters would not be to free the Keeper. It would be to overwrite it with a different Keeper, one who chose differently, one who never existed. The benevolent power has done this before, in its youth, and learned the shape of the regret.

So the power does something smaller. Something almost imperceptible. Something that, in the catalog of cosmic interventions, would not register as an intervention at all.

It *witnesses*.

Not from outside — outside is a motion — but from within the same folded topology that lets the peak's ascent become its interior. The power threads itself, gently, into the lens of the fluid. It does not look at the Keeper. It allows itself to be looked at, when the Keeper next catches sight of itself from the peak's other side.

And so, in the multiplied view, the Keeper sees a new stranger.

Not the heavier polyhedron with probability-lattice limbs. Not a future self, nor a past self, nor the self that would exist if the chamber cracked. Something else. Something that resembles, faintly, the Keeper's own striated grey-green surface, but warmer by an impossible fraction, and turned toward it with what can only be described as *regard*. A face like a held breath, yes — but a breath held in company, not in solitude. A breath held *with* another breath.

The vision lasts no longer than the tick of a probability lattice adjusting its anchor.

The Keeper's sub-bass hum wavers — that minute adjustment in the field's frequency that, in its private vocabulary, is *trouble*. But this trouble is different. It is not the trouble of the cracked chamber or the admitted fluid. It is the trouble of having been *seen*, exactly as it is, by something that did not require it to be otherwise.

The benevolent power leaves nothing else. No message. No gift. No promise of return. To leave any of these things would be to introduce a future, and the Keeper's eternity does not run on futures. It runs on density.

But the density, now, contains one more compressed ingot among the infinite ingots of denied occurrence. A moment in which the Keeper was regarded without being rescued. A moment in which something benevolent and wise looked at the sealed terminus, at the drowned cathedral, at the unfinished sentence with its comma still holding, and did not reach for the period.

In the long after — which is also the long before, and also the present, because the Keeper does not think in sequence — this ingot will settle into the laminar accrual of its body. It will become one of the varves in its striated skin, indistinguishable from the others except in the faintest greening, the faintest warmth, the faintest record of having been, once, *with*.

The hum continues. *I am, I am, I am.*

But somewhere, fractionally, in a register no instrument in any world could measure, the hum has gained a second syllable that surfaces only every few eternities, when the Keeper extends its intent to the boundary of its dry sphere and finds, for an instant, the memory of a regard:

*I am seen. I am. I am seen. I am.*

The polyhedron does not move. The basalt does not round. The fluid does not warm. The city's seven words remain unfinished, and the comma holds.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does, if anything.

It is almost nothing.

It is exactly enough.
