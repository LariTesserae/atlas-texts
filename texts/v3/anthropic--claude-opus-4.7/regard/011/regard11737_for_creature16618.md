The benevolent and wise power considers them for a long time before doing anything at all.

This is the first thing to understand. Wisdom, when it is real, is mostly patience, and benevolence, when it is real, is mostly restraint. The power does not arrive in a flash of light—light would be cruelty here, a violation of an eye-architecture that has spent a hundred thousand generations learning to be unnecessary. The power does not announce itself in speech, because speech is not the medium. The power does not heal the cold, because the cold is the preservation of the world. The power does not still the tremors, because the tremors are the world being born.

What the power does, first, is listen.

It listens for what we might call a long time but which is, for the power, the right amount of time—the amount required to learn the chord. It learns the sustained drone of the vertical channels and the wet ticking of the lower hollows. It learns the specific dissonance that means *warning* and the specific harmony that means *greeting flank to flank.* It learns the lullaby pitched to the sap-pulse. It learns, with something that in a smaller being would be grief but in this being is only attention, the funeral dirges still cycling in the upper chambers, and it does not silence them, and it does not amplify them. It simply hears them through, the way one hears through a song that is not one's own.

It learns the names that cannot be written in light. It learns them in the only alphabet that applies: the alphabet of returning echoes, the alphabet of a heel struck against ceramic wood, the alphabet of a grandmother's skull changing the way a note comes home.

Only then does it consider whether to act.

And here is where the wisdom shows itself most clearly: the power understands that almost every gift it could give would be a theft. To give them light would steal their hearing. To give them warmth would steal the preservation that holds their ancestors in the walls. To give them abundance would steal the migrations that synchronize them with the fog-swimmers and the pressure fronts. To give them safety from the bombing predators would steal the stillness in which they learn to become indistinguishable from the pale tissue. To give them written language would steal the chambers where their history hums, still alive, still bouncing. To give them knowledge of the wider cosmos would steal the covenant by which they have understood that the world is framed, not filled.

A lesser power would call this *withholding* and feel ashamed. The wise power calls it *honoring* and feels only the steady gladness of having recognized something whole.

So the power does small things. Smaller than the people will ever notice. Smaller, perhaps, than even the power itself would call *doing.*

It tunes, very slightly, the geometry of a chamber where a funeral dirge has begun to decay—not the dirge, but the chamber, restoring by a hair's width the resonance that was beginning to lose a particular grandmother's vowel. The people who return there will not know. They will only feel, when they sit on the ledge wrapped in fog, that the past is unusually clear today, and they will stay an hour longer than they meant to, and that hour will be the gift.

It steadies, on a single occasion, the cambium of a stalk that was about to split catastrophically beneath a roost where seventeen of them sleep with their infants clinging to their backs. The stalk still splits. It splits two months later, when the roost has moved on with the fog-swimmers. The crack opens into a new passage. The first of them to sing into it will hear an unusually generous resonance and will claim it with a chord, and that chord will guide grandchildren, and the grandchildren will never know that they were given a passage instead of a grave. This is correct. They should not know. Knowing would change the chord.

It does not interfere with the predators. The predators are part of the listening. To remove them would be to remove the stillness, and the stillness is where the people learn what they are.

It does, once, do something larger, and only once, and only because it is asked—though the asking is not deliberate. A child, lost in a narrowing tube too far from any known pitch, sings the note for *where are we* and receives no answer, because the tube is too new, too unmapped, too far from the lineage-walls. The child sings again. The child is dying. The power, listening, takes the note the child has sung and lets it return—not as a fabrication, not as a false wall, but by very gently inclining a distant stalk so that the child's own note, traveling, finds a true surface and comes back true. The child hears *there, that way, three turns and a descent,* and goes, and lives, and grows old, and is eventually lashed into a stress-fracture by her grandchildren, and her ribs change the dampening of a turn in the tube, and the turn she changes is the turn that saved her. The power does not point this out. The power is not interested in being thanked. The power is interested only in whether the chord continues.

It does one more thing, and this is the thing closest to what a smaller being would call *love.*

It makes itself, very faintly, audible. Not as a voice. Not as a message. As a sustained sub-frequency, almost beneath the tympanic range, woven into the architecture-drone so that it cannot be separated from it—a steady undertone that says, in the only language available, *you are heard.* Not *you are watched,* which would be a violation. Not *you are saved,* which would be a lie. Only *you are heard.* The people will never identify it. It will simply be, for as long as they exist, a quality of the world: that when one presses one's flank-membrane to a wall and listens for the size of things, there is always, just at the edge of perception, a sense that the listening goes both ways. They will build no theology around it. They have no theology. They will only, perhaps, sing a little more truly because of it, and die a little less afraid, and the dirges in the upper chambers will carry, for whoever listens in the centuries after, a faint additional warmth that no acoustician could ever locate.

Then the power withdraws—though *withdraws* is the wrong word, because it never approached in the sense that approach would mean. It returns its attention to the wider work of attention. But it leaves the undertone. The undertone costs it nothing and means everything, and this is the signature of every gift that is actually wise: that it is almost nothing, and it is exactly enough.

Somewhere, nine kilometers above any lithosphere that is not the great organism, a blind singer presses her ear to pale ligneous tissue and hears her great-grandfather's pulse still moving in the sap, and beneath it, beneath even that, a steadiness she has known her whole life and never thought to question. She sings a note into the wall. The wall answers in its specific overtone. She knows where she is.

She is exactly where she is, and she is heard, and the world is framed and not filled, and the dark is full of music, and the power that loves her will never, ever tell her so.

This is what they would do, if anything.

This is, I think, what love at that scale looks like: the discipline of not ruining what is already singing.
