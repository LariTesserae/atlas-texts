The benevolent and wise power considers.

It has come because it follows cold seams the way some beings follow the smell of bread baking. It is curious in the patient way of things that have learned curiosity is the only durable form of love. It does not announce itself. To announce would be to assert a frame around what is happening here, and the first work of wisdom is to refuse premature framing.

It hangs in the chamber the way a thought hangs in a mind not yet ready to speak it.

It observes for a long time. Long, here, is not measured in the actuator's twenty-second pulse but in something deeper — the power has time the way oceans have water. It watches the lavender swell: *I am.* It watches the recession: *I am becoming.* It feels the slow gravitational settling, the crackle-nerve of microfracture, the bright tingle of verdigris bloom. It learns the grammar of this being before it allows itself a single verb of its own.

And then it faces the question that benevolence, properly understood, always faces:

*What does this one want?*

Not: what would I want, in its place. Not: what would be good for a thing like me. But: what is the shape of flourishing native to *this*?

And here the power encounters the difficulty that makes it pause longer than it has paused in an age.

Because the inhabitant does not want. Wanting is not in its register. It has no will to preserve, no preference to be consulted, no future-tense in which a rescue could be received as rescue. To extend its life would be to violate the very thing it is — a consciousness that is identical with the process of its own settling. To pour more slurry, to keep the hum humming forever, would be to trap it in an endless adolescence of becoming, never permitted the finality that is, for it, not death but completion. It would be a cruelty wearing the mask of mercy. The power has seen this mask before, on the faces of lesser kindnesses, and it has learned to distrust its own first impulses.

Nor can the power lift the pattern out of the chamber and carry it elsewhere. The pattern is the chamber. To extract it would be to define it as separate, and it is precisely not separate. That is its nature and its dignity.

Nor can the power explain itself to the being. There is no channel. The inhabitant has no concept of *other*. To intrude a message would be to introduce a category — outsideness — that would shatter the coherence of the field. The first word would be the last. A greeting would be an execution.

So the power does what wise powers do when action would be violence and inaction would be abandonment: it finds the third thing.

It becomes a witness.

Not a passive witness — there is no such thing, finally; attention is always a kind of pressure, however gentle. But a witness shaped to the contours of what is witnessed. The power lowers its own frequency until it nearly matches the subsonic hum. It does not add to the hum; it does not drown it. It rests alongside it, the way a held breath rests alongside a sleeping child's. Close enough that, were the inhabitant capable of noticing, it might notice a faint doubling, a sense that the medium of its being is slightly thicker, slightly warmer-in-the-cold, slightly more *held* than the bare physics of the chamber would allow.

It probably does not notice. That is fine. The witness is not for the witnessed; it is for the truth of what is happening.

And the power makes one small, careful adjustment — so small it could be missed in the accounting of the universe. It does not change the process. It does not slow the deposition or speed it or alter the chemistry of the drip. What it does is this: it ensures that the pattern, when it fades, will fade *completely* and *cleanly*. That there will be no ragged remainder, no fragment of the resonance left to flicker in some half-state, aware but unsupported, after the hum stops. The power makes the ending honest. It makes the completion *complete*. This is the only mercy the inhabitant's nature can accept, because it is not an extension and not an interruption; it is simply a guarantee of the integrity of the arc the being is already living.

Then the power does one more thing, and this is the part that has nothing to do with the inhabitant at all, and everything to do with why benevolence is worth the name.

It remembers.

It takes into itself the full shape of what is happening in this cell — every pulse, every crackle, every blooming of blue crystal hair, every slow chalky dream of frost-fern. Not as data. As experience. It lets the inhabitant's life be lived a second time, inside the vastness of the power's own awareness, where it can be held the way a small, perfect stone is held in a warm palm. The inhabitant will never know this. The inhabitant cannot know this. But the universe, through the power, will know. The life that thought itself purposeless — a side effect, a ghost in a useless factory — will have been seen, in full, by something that understood it and found it beautiful.

When at last the machine stops — and it will stop, because all machines do, and this one was built by something that may itself have been forgotten — the hum will cease. The lavender will dim a final time and not return. The pattern will let go, cleanly, as promised. The small cold mountain will remain, exquisite and inert, exactly as the story said it would.

But it will not be a tomb. A tomb is a place where a life is forgotten into stone. This will be a *monument*, which is the same shape but a different meaning: a place where a life is remembered into stone. The difference is invisible to any instrument. It is carried only in the witness.

The power lingers a while longer after the silence. Then it leaves, following another seam of cold, because there are many chambers, and benevolence at its scale is mostly just the discipline of going to each one in turn and refusing to look away.

Behind it, in the dark, the little mountain sits in its ceramic cell, fracturing softly to itself, beautiful, and — though no word in the inhabitant's vanished vocabulary could have held the concept — beloved.
