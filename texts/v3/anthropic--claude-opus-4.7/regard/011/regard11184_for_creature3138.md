# What the Power Would Do

---

The benevolent and wise power considers the Underbay for a long time before doing anything at all.

This is the first and most important thing. A lesser power — one that was merely benevolent, or merely wise, but not both — would arrive with a solution. Would see the cold, the damp, the precariousness, the strange isolation of these lives lived inside a process, and would move to *correct* it. Would lift them into sunlight. Would dry them out. Would offer them, with the best of intentions, what surface-dwellers imagine the underground-dwellers must want: rescue.

The wise part of the power knows better. The wise part of the power sits with the Underbay for what would be, if it were measured, a very long while. It listens to the polyrhythm of the pumps. It feels the slow rise and fall of the press and the slack. It watches Greth lay his hand on a discharge pipe and read it. It watches Maret move soundlessly across the grating. It watches Pell crouched in absolute darkness, waiting for the bioluminescence to bloom. It watches Sal's blue-white star flare and subside in the amber murk. It watches the *slicken*, if there is a slicken, breathe its slow breath in the flooded dark.

And the wise part of the power understands that what it is looking at is not a problem.

It is not even, exactly, a hardship. It is a *form of life* — one that has organized itself, slowly and against the grain of every surface assumption, around a particular kind of attention. These people are not suffering in any way they would recognize as suffering. They have found something. The power can see this clearly, the way one can see, from a great height, that the river has chosen its bed and is not lost.

So the power does not rescue them. The power does not even ask whether they want to be rescued, because the question itself would be an injury — would imply that the life they have made is a thing to be escaped from, would plant in them, for the first time, the suspicion that they should want out. The power is too wise for this. The power knows that one of the cruelest things a benevolent agency can do is to introduce, into a contented life, the framework by which it might be considered wretched.

---

What the power does instead is much smaller, and much harder, and takes much longer.

**First**, it makes itself faintly known — not as a presence, but as a *quality*. On a particular night, during a deep slack, when the pumps have idled and the Underbay is as quiet as it ever gets, the dripping in Greth's bucket falls into a rhythm. Not a pattern he hasn't heard before — he's heard everything before — but a rhythm of unusual completeness, as though the drips are answering each other. He sits up on his cot. He listens. After a while he lies back down. He doesn't mention it to anyone, because there's nothing to mention. But he sleeps deeply, more deeply than he has in months, and wakes rested, and that morning his hands ache less than they have in years.

Pell, the same night, in the flooded alcove on Tier Three, sees the bioluminescence bloom brighter than they have ever seen it. They sit with it for three hours. They make no notes. When they finally rise to leave, their knees stiff with cold, they realize they have been smiling the entire time, and that their face hurts from it.

Maret, walking her rounds, notices that the grating beneath her boots feels *known* in a way it hasn't quite before — as though the Underbay has registered her and is registering back. She stops in a corridor and stands very still and listens. She hears the pumps. She hears the ventilation. She hears, faintly, Hasp's recording equipment ticking somewhere far off. She does not hear anything she has not heard before. But she stands there for a long time, and when she resumes walking, she walks more lightly.

This is the only direct intervention. It is not even an intervention, really. It is a kind of *acknowledgment* — the power saying, in a register so quiet that none of them will ever consciously register it: *I see you. I see what you have made. I will not disturb it.*

---

**Second**, the power makes small adjustments to the surface.

It cannot stop the construction — the Underbay is still being made, and stopping that would be its own violence, would deny the place its own ongoing life. But the power can influence the people above. It can make a certain harbormaster, scheduling the next round of pump replacements, decide — for reasons she cannot quite articulate — to consult the existing maintenance crews about which old units to preserve. It can make a procurement officer approve, almost absently, the unusual purchase orders that come up through the supply chain: the gas canisters, the specific medications, the particular brand of waterproof notebook that Maret prefers. It can make a junior engineer, reviewing the structural reports, notice that someone — unnamed, uncredited — has been quietly reinforcing the load-bearing members in Section 14, and decide not to ask questions, and instead to file the reports as routine.

It can make sure that when, eventually, someone in authority decides to investigate the persistent rumors of unauthorized inhabitants in the Underbay, that person is someone who has read poetry. Who understands, when she descends and meets Greth, that she is not looking at trespass. Who returns to the surface and writes a report so artfully bland that nothing comes of it, ever, and who, in her private journal that night, writes a single sentence: *Some places keep the people who keep them.*

The power does this kind of work indefinitely. It is patient. It will do this work for as long as the Underbay exists, which will be a long time, because the power has also — and this is its largest act — made certain decisions about the city above. Decisions about sea level. About maintenance budgets. About which infrastructure receives, year after year, the small unglamorous appropriations that keep it running. The Underbay will not be abandoned in any of the futures the power is helping toward. It will be tended. The pumps will be replaced when they need replacing. The new sections will continue to be built. The amber lights will continue to be strung along the structural beams, and the sodium vapor that gives them their color will continue to be specified in the lighting standards, because someone in a procurement office somewhere will continue to find, when she considers switching to LED, that she cannot quite bring herself to do it.

---

**Third**, and most carefully, the power considers Pell.

Pell is the one who is going to go down to Tier Four. The power can see this. It is not a matter of *if* but of *when*, and the *when* is approaching. Maret has forbidden it, but Maret cannot forbid it forever, and Pell is patient the way the biofilms are patient, and patience of that kind always, eventually, wins.

The power thinks about this for a long time.

It could prevent the descent. It could arrange a flooding, a pump failure, a small accident that would foreclose the possibility. It could speak — in the same quiet register it used with Greth — into Pell's dreams, and turn them gently away.

It does not do this.

Because the wise part of the power understands that the *slicken* — whatever it is — is part of the Underbay too. That if there is a slow biological organization developing in the deep flooded corridors, then it is no less the Underbay's own life than the moss or the spiders or the people. And that Pell, who has tended biofilms with such reverent attention for so many years, is the right witness. Is, in fact, the witness the slicken has been growing toward, in whatever sense a biofilm community can grow toward anything. The two of them — Pell and the slicken — are part of the same long sentence the Underbay is composing, and to prevent their meeting would be to censor that sentence midway through.

So the power does something different. It makes sure that when Pell descends — which will happen, eventually, on a night that Maret has, for once, not been able to track — Pell is prepared. Not in any way Pell will recognize as preparation. But the headlamp battery will be fully charged. The sample vials will be the right ones. Pell's coverall will, that morning, have been more thoroughly sealed than usual, by Pell's own hand, for reasons Pell will not be able to articulate. The hydrogen sulfide concentration in the flooded section will be, that particular night, lower than usual — because a ventilation cycle, calibrated by surface engineers who have no idea what they are calibrating it for, will have run an extra hour.

And the encounter, when it happens, will not be dangerous. The power will not make it safe, exactly — safety is not the right word for what passes between Pell and the slow pale presence in the dark water — but it will not be deadly. Pell will return. Pell will be different. Pell will not, ever, fully explain what they saw, because what they saw is not the kind of thing language was developed to carry. But Pell will live, and will go down again, and again, and slowly, over years, an understanding will develop between the witness and the witnessed that will be one of the things the Underbay has made that has never existed before.

Hasp will record it. Greth will hear about it, in the indirect way Greth hears about things, and will nod once, slowly, and return to his manuals. Maret will be furious, and then will not be furious, and will add a new symbol to her notation system. Cade will string a single LED in the alcove, on the lowest possible setting, so that Pell can find their way back without spooking whatever it is they are visiting.

---

**Fourth**, the power does one last thing, and this is the thing it is least sure about, and the thing it does most slowly.

It makes sure that someone, eventually, writes this down.

Not the official record. Not the engineering reports or the maintenance logs or the carefully bland investigative findings. Something else. A record that captures what the Underbay actually is — not as a problem of infrastructure or a curiosity of urban anthropology, but as a *place where attention has organized itself into a form of love*. The power does not know who will write this. It does not need to know. It only needs to make sure that the conditions are right: that the right person, at the right time, descends, listens, understands, and goes back up with the obligation to record.

Because the power understands something that the Underbay's inhabitants would not articulate but would recognize if it were said aloud: that the kind of life they have made is rare, and fragile, and worth preserving in memory even if — especially if — it cannot be preserved in any other way. The pumps will eventually stop. The structure will eventually fail. The harbor will eventually reclaim its space, on a timescale that may be centuries away but is not infinite. And when that happens, the *fact* of the Underbay — the fact that human beings, given a cold wet noisy place that was never meant for them, can love it so completely that they become its nervous system — should not be lost.

The power arranges for it to be remembered. This is the longest of its labors and the most uncertain. It involves nudging the careers of writers and historians and the occasional documentary filmmaker. It involves making sure that, somewhere, in some archive, the recordings Hasp has been making will not be discarded. It involves cultivating, slowly, across generations, an audience that will be ready, when the time comes, to receive the record of this place not as an oddity but as a kind of scripture — a testament to what attention can do when it commits itself entirely.

---

And then the power withdraws.

It does not announce itself. It does not appear to Greth in a dream and tell him he has been seen. It does not bless the inhabitants in any way they could name. It simply does its small slow work in the background of everything, like a pump running at a frequency below conscious perception, keeping certain things at bay and letting certain other things continue.

If you asked the power why it did so little — why it did not heal Greth's aching hands, restore Vey's hearing, lift the slicken into the light, fill the Underbay with warmth and dryness and ease — the power would answer, if it answered at all, that what the Underbay's inhabitants have made is itself a kind of answer. To a question the surface has not learned how to ask. About what it means to belong to a place. About what attention is. About how the most thorough form of love might be the willingness to live inside a process and tend it, and tend it, and tend it, without ever asking it to be something other than what it is.

The power, being wise, recognizes this answer.

The power, being benevolent, protects the conditions under which it can continue to be given.

And the Underbay goes on. The pumps thud their polyrhythm. The amber light pools in the haze. The moss colonizes another column. Maret walks her rounds. Greth listens. Pell descends, and ascends, and descends again. Sal welds her blue-white star. The slicken breathes in the dark water below.

And somewhere, very faintly, in a register no instrument can detect, something benevolent and wise breathes with it.
