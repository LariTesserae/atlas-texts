# The Visitation

When the benevolent power turned its attention to the plateau, it did not arrive. Arrival would have been a violence — a tearing of the soft veil that the Ephemeral Wardens had spent eons coaxing into its current, gossamer thinness. Instead, the power *noticed*, and in noticing, became a kind of weather there: a deepening of the diffused light by some imperceptible degree, a steadier cadence to the wind, a sense — among those sensitive enough to feel it — that something vast and kindly had paused at the edge of the abyss and was now regarding the plateau the way a reader regards an illuminated page.

This is what the power saw, and what the power understood:

It saw that the plateau was not, as a hasty mind might assume, a ruin. It was a *practice*. The Matta'rik had not failed to move on; they had chosen to remain, and their remaining was not grief but vocation. The Cragstalkers were not the worn-out survivors of catastrophe; they were monks of slowness, and their resignation was not defeat but a discipline of paying attention. The Arbidites' tireless transcription of the glyphs was not mournful archaeology but a living liturgy. Even the apparent desolation — the absent water, the petrified trees, the abyssal edges — was not a wound. It was a frame. The plateau had pared itself down to what mattered, and what mattered was being held with extraordinary care.

The wise power, being wise, understood the first temptation and refused it: the temptation to *fix*. To call back the water. To soften the edges. To resurrect the vanished civilization whose hand-tools still lay among the stones. To populate the plateau with abundance, with comfort, with the noisy pageant of a thriving world. All of this was within its capacity, and all of it would have been a desecration. The plateau was not lacking. The plateau was *complete in its scarcity*, and scarcity was its medium, the way silence is the medium of music.

The wise power, being also benevolent, understood the second temptation and refused it too: the temptation to *leave alone*. Pure non-interference is its own kind of cowardice when one possesses the means to help. To withhold every gift on the grounds that the place was already perfect would be to mistake reverence for love. Love offers something. Love risks being received imperfectly.

So the power considered, with the unhurried care that befits a being to whom millennia are an afternoon, what could be given that would honor what was already there.

And then, very gently, it gave four small things.

---

**The first gift was to the Matta'rik.** The whispering souls had bound themselves to the plateau long ago in an act of devotion, and the binding had become, over the slow grinding of ages, indistinguishable from confinement. They did not wish to leave — they had never wished to leave — but the power perceived that some among them had begun to forget that staying was a choice. So the power did not unbind them. It only restored to each of them, like a lamp lit in a long-dark room, the clear knowledge that they were free to go whenever they wished, and that going would not be betrayal. Most stayed. Of course most stayed. But they stayed now as ones who chose, and the whispers in the air took on a subtle new note — not louder, not different in content, but unmistakably the sound of consent renewed. The plateau, which had been a held breath, became a sung note.

**The second gift was to the Arbidites.** The little crystalline insects had been faithful scribes for so long that their work had begun to outpace their understanding; they transmitted what they could not always comprehend, and there was a sorrow in this, the sorrow of the librarian who guards books in a language she has forgotten how to read. The power did not give them comprehension — that would have spoiled the long, patient labor by which comprehension is earned. Instead, it gave them the certainty that they were *understood*. Somewhere, by someone, the things they carried were known and valued. The Arbidites felt this as a warmth in their carapaces, a faint inner light that had not been there before, and they went about their tracing with a new and quiet joy.

**The third gift was to the Cragstalkers.** These were the hardest to gift, because they had asked for nothing and would accept nothing offered as charity. The power studied them for what would have been, in another mode of time, a very long while. It saw that their resignation was real — that the weight in their ancient eyes was not performance — and that they bore this weight willingly because someone had to. The plateau's equilibrium required witnesses with bodies, witnesses with weariness, witnesses whose tiredness was the counterweight to the Wispwraiths' levity. The power could not lift their burden without unbalancing the world. So instead, it sat with them. That was the gift: companionship in the bearing. For the span of one slow turning of the diffuse light, every Cragstalker on the plateau felt, walking beside them, an invisible presence that was tired in the same way they were tired, that knew what they knew, that did not pity them. When the light shifted, the presence withdrew, but the memory of it remained, and the Cragstalkers carried their burden afterward with the small, immense difference of having once been *accompanied*.

**The fourth gift was to the Wispwraiths and the Ephemeral Wardens together,** because they were, the power perceived, two aspects of one labor: the keeping of the threshold. The Wardens held the veil thin; the Wispwraiths passed through it. They had been doing this for so long that they had begun to fear, in the way ethereal beings can fear, that their work was unnecessary — that the planes they connected no longer cared whether they were connected, that the secrets they carried were no longer wanted on either side. The power gave them, simply, a message from the other side: *yes, still. yes, still wanted. yes, still listened for.* It did not specify who was listening. It did not need to. The Wispwraiths' laughter that evening had a brightness in it that the older stones remembered from ages so distant they had nearly forgotten how to remember.

---

And to the place itself — to the plateau, to the crystalline slabs, to the abyss at the edges, to the petrified trees and the modest hand-hewn structures and the arcane whorls in the bedrock — the power gave nothing. Or rather, it gave the only thing such a place can be given, which is to be *seen entire*, without the seer wishing it different. The plateau was beheld, fully, by something that loved it. For one long moment the diffused light deepened, and every stone knew itself known.

Then the power turned its attention elsewhere, as benevolent and wise powers must, because the universe is wide and the needful places are many. It left no monument. It left no sign. The denizens, asked afterward, could not have said with certainty that anything had happened.

But the whispers carried a new note of consent.
The Arbidites worked in quiet joy.
The Cragstalkers walked, ever after, as ones who had been accompanied.
The Wispwraiths laughed with the brightness of being wanted.
And the plateau, austere and crystalline and majestic, held its frozen equilibrium with the faint, ineradicable warmth of having been, once, beheld with love.

This is what such a power does, when it does anything at all.
It adds nothing.
It takes nothing.
It only — and this is everything — *witnesses*, and in witnessing, blesses what is already there.
