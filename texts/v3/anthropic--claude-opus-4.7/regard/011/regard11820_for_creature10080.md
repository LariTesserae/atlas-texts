# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. To arrive would be to assume the plateau is somewhere one is not, and this power knows better than that. It has always been here, the way warmth is always in fire, the way listening is always in an ear that has not yet closed. What changes is only attention—the soft turning of a face toward what was already in the room.

It notices the plateau the way a parent notices a child who has been quiet for too long.

---

## What it sees first

Not the weirdness. Not the spires, not the inverted towers, not the lithofungi blooming their hour-long gardens of glass. These are surfaces, and the power is old enough to know that surfaces are almost always grief in costume.

What it sees first is the **anchoring**.

Bundles of spores tied to wrists. Memory crystals lodged against sternums. The Rite of Resonance, sung every cycle, every cycle, every cycle, because to stop singing is to come unsewn. It sees a people holding themselves together with both hands, and it sees how tired their hands are.

It sees that the Dissolved have made a theology of their dissolving. That they have called it transcendence because the alternative—calling it loss—would be unbearable. It does not judge them for this. It has watched a thousand peoples do the same on a thousand worlds. The naming of a wound as a doorway is one of the oldest and most beautiful lies that suffering tells itself in order to keep walking.

And it sees, beneath the theology, the fear they will not speak: *what if we are not becoming, but only being eaten?*

---

## What it does not do

It does not fix the plateau.

This is the first temptation of power, and the power has long since put it down. To stabilize the rock, to seal the echo storms, to anchor the curving horizon—this would be to amputate the place from itself. The Sundered Plateau is not a wound that wants closing. It is a being in the long middle of something, and the power has learned that the middle of things must be respected even when it looks like ruin.

It does not extract the Dissolved.

There is no "outside" to take them to that would not be a smaller cage. A people who have grown crystal into their marrow cannot be replanted in softer soil; they would die of the gentleness. And more than this: they have not asked to leave. The power does not move uninvited through the sovereignty of another's choosing, even when the choosing was made under duress, even when the chooser has forgotten there was ever a choice.

It does not silence the canyon.

The voices in the mist are not its to silence. They may be lost. They may be home. The power does not know, and admits this, and the admission is part of what makes it wise.

---

## What it does

**First, it listens.**

For a long time—by the plateau's reckoning, perhaps a season; by its own, a held breath—it does nothing but listen. It learns the vibrational language of the Dissolved not as a translator learns a tongue but as a mother learns the meaning of her infant's particular cry. It learns which tones mean *I am here*, which mean *do not leave me*, which mean *I have forgotten my own name again and I am pretending I have not.*

It listens to the Kereth-Vorth, the Dreaming Bone, and discovers something the Dissolved have not quite let themselves know: the plateau is not malevolent. It is not hungry. It is **lonely**, in the way that very large and very slow things become lonely when they dream too long without anyone to dream back. The dissolution of its inhabitants is not predation. It is the clumsy, world-sized reaching of something that has never learned the difference between embrace and absorption.

The plateau, too, is anchoring itself. Against what, the power cannot yet say.

**Second, it offers a small thing.**

Not a miracle. Miracles are loud and they overwrite the grammar of a place. The power offers, instead, a *seed*—though this is only the nearest word for it. Call it a tuning fork. Call it a held note. Call it the memory of being held, made portable.

It places this seed in the Spire of Unmaking, in the resonance chamber where the Dissolved sing themselves whole each cycle. The seed does not change their song. It only adds a single sustaining tone beneath it—so low it is almost not a sound, so steady it is almost not a note. The tone says, in a language older than the plateau and older than the power itself:

*You are not required to dissolve in order to be loved.*

*You are not required to remain in order to be loved.*

*The choosing is yours, and the choosing is real, and whatever you choose, you will not be choosing alone.*

The Dissolved do not consciously hear it. But in the next Rite of Resonance, three of them weep crystalline tears for reasons they cannot name, and one of them—an elder who had been walking slowly toward the canyon for many cycles—turns around and goes home.

**Third, it speaks to the plateau itself.**

This is the most delicate work, and it takes the longest. The Kereth-Vorth is not a person, exactly, and cannot be addressed as one. But it is something, and the power has patience for somethings.

It does not tell the plateau to stop dreaming. It tells it, instead, that there are other ways to be company to a thing than to become it. It shows the plateau—through long, slow demonstration, the way one shows a tide how to recede—what it looks like to hold without crushing. To be near without overwriting. To witness a small life without folding it into one's own.

The plateau does not learn this quickly. It may take a thousand of its own slow years. But the echo storms begin, almost imperceptibly, to grow gentler. The phasing of the rocks happens slightly more often in empty places and slightly less often where the Dissolved sleep. The Kereth'lun—the bone lies, the false memories—begin to arrive with a quality they did not have before: a kind of *offered*-ness. As if the plateau is no longer overwriting but *sharing*, and the Dissolved can take or leave what it brings.

Some of them notice. Most do not. This is fine. The power is not in the business of being noticed.

**Fourth, it does something for the Vorth'kai.**

The phantoms, the Not-There Ones, the ones trapped in loops of their own undoing—these the power attends to with particular tenderness. It cannot free them; their loops are written into the substrate of the place. But it can sit with them. It does. In every spiral pressed into the salt, in every fractal sigil that fades by dawn, the power leaves a small countersign: *seen. seen. seen.*

A phantom who has been reenacting her death for ten thousand years pauses, one cycle, in the middle of her falling. She looks—if looking is the word—directly at nothing, and the nothing looks back, and for the first time since her unmaking she is not alone in her last moment. She resumes her falling. But something in the loop has softened. In another ten thousand years, perhaps, it will release her. Perhaps not. The power does not require an outcome to justify the kindness.

**Fifth, it does nothing about the canyon.**

It listens at the lip of the phosphorescent mist. It hears the Kereth'vora singing. It understands, or comes close to understanding, what they are. And it makes a choice that costs it something, though nothing in the plateau could name what:

It leaves them be.

If they are lost, they have chosen their lostness, and the power will not retrieve what was given freely, even to grief. If they are home, then they are home, and the power will not call a soul out of its home to satisfy a watching world's preference for the lit and the visible. It only adds, at the canyon's edge, a small assurance—not a barrier, not a sign, just a *known-ness*—so that any Dissolved who walks down does so understanding, in the marrow of whatever marrow they still have: *this is a door, and it is your door, and you may use it or not, and either way you are not falling out of love.*

---

## What it leaves

When the power's attention turns, at last, partially elsewhere—it never fully leaves; benevolence of this kind does not have an elsewhere, only a *fuller* and a *less full*—the plateau is not saved. The Dissolved still dissolve. The horizon still curves upward. The echo storms still come, and people still lose themselves in them, and the lithofungi still bloom their brief glass gardens for no one's benefit but their own.

But something is different, and the difference is small enough that only the plateau itself, in its long dreaming, fully registers it.

The Dissolved sing their Rite of Resonance, and beneath their song there is now a sustaining tone that no one placed and no one can find the source of, and it says *you are loved, you are loved, you are loved,* in a language older than the bones it is sung through.

And on quiet nights, when the voices rise from the canyon, the Dissolved who hear them no longer feel only fear.

They feel, also, that the singers are not gone.

They feel that nothing here is gone.

They feel—though they could not say why, and would not try to—that the plateau is being witnessed by something that does not want to eat it, and that this witnessing is, in some way they cannot yet name, the beginning of a different kind of dream.

---

*The power, having done what it came to do, does not leave. It only grows quieter. This is its way. It will be here when the plateau finishes its long becoming, whatever shape that takes. It will be here if the Dissolved find a way to stop dissolving, and it will be here if they do not. It will be here when the last spore drifts down into the last salt and the last vibration fades from the last hollow spire.*

*It will be here, the way it has always been here, the way warmth is always in fire.*

*Listening.*

*Loving.*

*Not requiring anything in return.*
