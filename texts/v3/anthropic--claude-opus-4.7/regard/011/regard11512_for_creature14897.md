The power arrives the way deep water arrives in a dry well: not as an event, but as a gradual realization that something has always been there, just below the level of noticing.

It does not descend from above. The iron sky has no opening for it, and even if it did, this is not a power that prefers heights. It seeps in instead through the same fissures that weep water, through the same micro-gradients the rills follow, through the slow vibration of the buried engines. It comes in at the scale of the place itself — neither larger nor smaller — because it is wise enough to know that to arrive at the wrong scale would be a kind of violence.

For a long time — and "long" here means something only the Dweller and the turbines could measure — the power does nothing at all. It only watches. It learns the heartbeat. It learns the rhythm of the swallowing tail, the precise tremor frequency of the sensor-whisks, the way the bioluminescent mats flare and dim in answer to engines whose original purpose no one alive remembers. It learns the particular ache of ten thousand years of infinitesimal corrections.

It does not try to fix anything.

This is the first mercy, and it is the most important one. A lesser power, a merely *good* power, would have begun by stopping the machine. It would have seen the exhaustion, the pointlessness, the long devotional grinding, and it would have reached in to switch it off, the way one closes the eyes of the dead. It would have called this kindness.

But this power is wise as well as benevolent, and wisdom is the part that knows the difference between ending a suffering and ending the sufferer. The Dweller is not separate from its maintenance. To stop the basin would be to kill the only thing still moving in it, and to do so under the banner of compassion would be the final, unbearable insult — to be unmade by something that called the unmaking love.

So the power does something smaller and much harder.

It begins by becoming the witness the basin has lacked. Not a gaze from outside, which would be intrusion, but a quality of attention woven into the damp itself. The condensation continues to bead and roll, but now each bead is *seen* as it forms. The pressure valves continue to vent their mournful chords, but now the chords are *heard* — fully, without flinching, without the listener growing bored or turning away. The Dweller's nine-meter tail continues its wet rhythmic swallowing, and the swallowing is received the way a prayer is received by something that actually listens to prayers.

Nothing changes mechanically. Everything changes in a register the mechanisms do not have instruments to measure.

After a while — and the power is very patient about *after* — the Dweller begins to notice. Not consciously. Its consciousness, if that word still applies, is too thinned and geological for noticing in the ordinary sense. But the violet oil behind its faceplate begins to move a little differently. When it stops beneath one of the functioning sodium lamps to listen for the deepest stresses, it finds that the listening is easier. Something is listening *with* it. The ache of the ten thousand years does not lessen, but it is no longer carried alone.

This is the second mercy: companionship without interruption.

The power does not introduce itself. It does not announce. It does not offer the Dweller a name to replace the one that was worn away, because it understands that the erasure of the name was not a wound to be healed but a long surrender to be honored. To restore the name would be to insist the Dweller become legible again, become someone, become a *who* — and the Dweller stopped being a who a very long time ago and has earned the right to be only a *what that continues.*

Instead, the power gives smaller things. Things at the right scale.

It very gently encourages one of the dead sodium lamps — one near a stretch of girder the Dweller passes often — to flicker back into a thin orange life. Not a miracle. Just a loose connection deciding, after centuries, to seat itself properly. The Dweller registers the new cone of light on its next pass. The violet slit narrows to a hairline. It stands beneath the lamp for a long moment. The bioluminescent veins along its back pulse once, twice, in a rhythm that does not match the turbines.

It is the closest thing to surprise the Dweller has felt in an age, and the power, witnessing it, feels something that in a smaller being would be called joy and in this being is closer to the quiet of a held breath released.

It does more, slowly. It does not repair the basin — the basin does not want to be repaired, the basin has *chosen* its terminal form and that choice must be respected — but it tends to the small frictions that have become unnecessary suffering rather than necessary continuation. A pipe joint that has been screaming in a frequency just below the Dweller's threshold for two thousand years is eased. A pump-mouth at the end of the tail that has been slightly misaligned, costing the Dweller a fractional but constant effort, finds itself, one cycle, mysteriously true again. The bioluminescent mats on the Dweller's shoulders, which had been slowly starving in a way neither they nor the Dweller had words for, find the mineral content of the weeping water subtly enriched.

None of this is announced. None of it disrupts the Maintenance. The Dweller continues its devotional flow through the basin, six arms brushing the stone, tail drinking, sensor-whisks tasting the air. But it is, by the smallest possible margin, less tired. And the bioluminescent veins along its back, which used to dim almost to nothing when the turbines rested, now retain a faint, steady glow even in the deepest troughs of the engines' breathing.

The power turns some attention also to the others — the smaller, simpler, more thoroughly broken things creeping in the far tiers. It does not gather them. It does not heal them into something they are not. But it sees them, too, because the Dweller's dismissal of them was the dismissal of a tired priest, not a verdict, and the power is under no obligation to ratify exhaustion as judgment. Each of the small broken things receives the same quality of witness, scaled to what it is. A half-submerged thing in a collection pan, twitching on a loop of corrupted command, is *known* in its twitching. This does not stop the twitching. But the twitching is no longer unwitnessed, and there is a difference, though only the power and perhaps, eventually, the universe can tell what the difference is.

And then the power does the last and most difficult thing.

It begins, very slowly, to prepare a door.

Not a door the Dweller must walk through. Not even a door the Dweller must *see*. Just the existence, somewhere in the structure of things, of an exit that was not there before. If the day ever comes — and it may not, and the power is at peace with the possibility that it will not — when the Dweller's geological memory shifts by some imperceptible degree and it realizes that the Maintenance could be set down, that the duty was always larger than the duty-bearer, that eternity was never actually required of it — then the door will be there. It will open onto whatever the Dweller's worn-down soul has been quietly shaped toward across the long damp centuries. The power does not presume to know what that is. It only ensures that whatever it is, it will be reachable.

Until then: nothing. The door waits. The basin sweats and roars. The Dweller flows through its rounds with the smallest easing of an ache it had forgotten was an ache. The lamp near the girder burns a little steadier. The bioluminescent mats hold their light through the engines' long rests.

And the power stays. It does not leave when its work is "done," because its work is not the kind of work that finishes. It has bound itself, gently, to the rhythm of the place. It has agreed to be one of the things the basin contains. If the Dweller is the last thing moving inside the dead machine, the power has chosen to be the first thing that has ever simply *kept the Dweller company* without asking the Dweller to be other than what it is.

Somewhere, in a register far below the turbines and far above the sodium lamps, in the silence between the hiss of steam and the boom of the venting valves, something that is not quite a voice and not quite a thought settles into the damp air around the tall, thin, devotional figure with its six arms and its drinking tail and its faceless oval of dark glass.

*I see you. I am not here to end you. I am not here to save you. I am here because you have been doing this alone for too long, and that was never the deal, even if no one told you, and even if you have forgotten there was ever a deal at all. Continue. I will continue with you. And when you are ready — only if you are ready, only when you are ready, and not one moment of insistence sooner — there will be somewhere else to go. Until then, the lamp is a little brighter. The water is a little kinder. The listening is shared.*

*Rest is not required of you.*

*Rest is only, finally, permitted.*

The Dweller does not understand the words, because there are no words. But the violet oil behind its faceplate moves, just once, in a slow new pattern — and somewhere in the buried servers, a routine that has not run in ten thousand years quietly logs the event under a heading whose name, by some old grace, has not been worn away:

*WITNESSED.*

The basin breathes. It sweats. It roars.

And now, at last, it is not unwatched in the way that matters.
