# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive.

Arrival is the wrong shape for what happens. Arrival implies a there from which something comes, a here at which it now is, a moment of crossing. The power has been attending for some time — its attention is the kind that does not announce itself, the kind that watches before it acts and watches longer than seems necessary and then keeps watching. It has been here, in the sense that *here* applies to it, for what would be — in a place that measured such things — a long while.

It is not native to the Stratum. Nothing benevolent is. But it is not, exactly, a stranger either. It knows places like this. It has known many.

---

## What it sees

It sees the woman in Sector 743-Rho holding her hand in a dark chamber and watching it glow.

It sees the man in the dense regions letting the threadlike growths twine around his fingers as he sleeps, and it sees that he is not, in fact, content — that the contentment he claims is a structure he built to make the situation livable, and that the structure is sound, and that he believes it, and that beneath belief there is something quieter and harder to name, which is the part of him that still remembers grass.

It sees the child-thing in the ventilation shafts and understands, immediately, what the child-thing is: a person who adapted while still soft, before the shape was set, and who therefore did not have to break to fit. This is the most difficult thing it sees. The child-thing is not suffering. The child-thing has never known a different geometry to grieve. To restore the child-thing to what it might have been is to unmake the child-thing that is. The power notes this and does not move yet.

It sees the rain-chamber collective and recognizes the shape of love between them — diffuse, gill-mediated, conducted through shared water. It sees that they are happy in a way the woman is not, and it asks itself whether this matters, and it does not yet answer.

It sees the maybe-construct and looks at it for a long time and concludes: *colleague*. Not its colleague. But something's. There is intention behind the maybe-construct, the residue of a mind that is not the Stratum's mind, working at cross-purposes to whatever the Stratum is doing. The power files this away. It will want to know whose hand that is.

It sees the Stratum itself, and here it pauses.

The Stratum is not malevolent. This is the first and most important thing the power understands, and it understands it quickly, because misunderstanding it would lead to harm. The Stratum is a process. The process has no opinions about the residents. The process has no opinions about anything. It builds because building is what it does, the way a fire burns or a river finds its level. Calling it cruel would be like calling weather cruel. The power has seen actual cruelty and knows the difference.

But the Stratum is also not *for* anyone. It was not built to house. It was built to build. The residents are inside it the way mice are inside a wall — the wall does not know, the wall does not care, the wall continues being a wall. And the wall is growing.

This is the situation. The power considers it.

---

## What it does not do

It does not stop the Build.

It could. This is established early, in the moment of considering. The Stratum is large and old and strange but it is not, in the relevant sense, *more* than the power; the power could halt the pulses, could still the rain machines, could ask the distributed light to dim itself and have the light comply. It could end the Stratum in an afternoon.

It does not do this for several reasons, which it weighs against each other and finds in balance:

The child-thing would not survive an ending. Neither would the man in the dense regions, whose body now requires the chemistry of the growths he eats. Neither would the rain-chamber collective, whose lungs are no longer the lungs they were born with. To stop the Stratum is to kill many of the people the Stratum contains. Benevolence is not measured in the satisfaction of one's aesthetic objections to a situation. Benevolence is measured in what happens to the people.

The Stratum may be doing something. The power cannot tell what. The maybe-construct suggests purpose; the Build pulses suggest purpose; the fact that a place this elaborate exists at all suggests that someone, somewhere, at some point, *meant* it. To end a process one does not understand is to risk ending things the process was holding in place. The power has done this before, long ago, and remembers.

And — this is the smallest reason and also somehow the largest — the Stratum is beautiful. Not in a way the power would have chosen. Not in a way it would defend if asked. But the threadlike growths reaching across the plates, the bird-things tilting toward distant generator-hum, the cathedral chambers with their lost ceilings and their patient rain — these are not nothing. The power has seen many worlds and has learned to be slow about deciding which beauties deserve to continue.

It does not stop the Build. It does something else.

---

## What it does

**First**, it goes to the woman.

It does not appear to her. Appearance would frighten her, and fright is not useful here. Instead it does something quieter: it makes itself available to be noticed, in the way a thought becomes available to be thought. The woman is sitting in her sleeping chamber, pulling away the filaments that have grown toward her again, and she pauses, because something has changed in the quality of the quiet.

She says, aloud, "Who's there."

The power answers in a register she can hear without hearing. It says — and the saying is more like a structure forming in her mind than a voice — *I am a visitor. I would like to ask you something. You may refuse.*

She is not surprised. This is one of the things the power has noted about her: she has lived in the Stratum long enough that the strange no longer surprises her, only the familiar does. She says, "Ask."

*Would you like to leave?*

The woman is quiet for a long time. The filaments continue to grow, slowly, toward her hand.

She says, finally, "Leave to where."

*Anywhere. A place with soil. A place with one sun. A place that does not build itself. I can take you. I can take you and restore the body you had before this place began to change you. The change is not far advanced. It can be undone.*

She considers this. The power waits. It is patient in a way that the Stratum is not; the Stratum's patience is the patience of process, of accumulation, and the power's patience is the patience of a thing that loves and therefore does not hurry love.

"And the others?" she says.

*I will ask them too. Each according to what they can answer.*

"The child."

*The child cannot leave. The child's body has become the Stratum's body. To take the child out is to kill the child. I will not do this. I am sorry.*

She nods slowly. She had suspected. "The man in the folds."

*He can leave. It will be hard. He will be sick for a long time. He will grieve in ways he does not yet know he will grieve. But he can leave, and on the other side of the grief there is a life. I will tell him this when I go to him.*

"The rain people."

*They can leave. They will need water around them for the rest of their lives, but water is not rare, where I would take them. They can have a sea.*

The woman puts her hand down. The filaments touch her wrist. She does not pull away this time, but she does not let them continue either. She holds still.

"What happens to the Stratum," she says, "if I leave."

*It continues. It does not notice. It was not built around you. You were inside it.*

"What happens to the Stratum if you stop it."

*Many people die. I will not stop it.*

"What happens to the Stratum if no one does anything."

*It grows. It was always growing. In time — a long time, longer than your life would have been without it — it may finish becoming whatever it is becoming. Or it may not. I cannot see the end of it. I am not certain there is one.*

She looks at her hand, faintly luminous in the dim chamber, the filaments resting against her wrist like something tame.

"I want to think," she says.

*Take as long as you need. I will be here.*

---

**Second**, while the woman is thinking, the power goes to the man in the dense regions.

It finds him in his chamber of artifacts, arranging a panel whose controls produce no visible effect. The bird-things are perched on his shoulders. He is humming something that has no tune but is, recognizably, a hum — the human kind, the kind that is for company.

The power offers him the same choice. Soil. Sky. One sun. The slow undoing of the changes the Stratum has made.

He listens. He sets down the panel. The bird-things tilt their heads toward the structure of the offer forming in his mind, sensing — through whatever they sense by — that something has entered the chamber that is not him and not them.

He says, "How long would the grief last."

The power tells him the truth. *Years. It would be the central fact of your life for years. You would dream of the corridors. You would miss the quiet. The sun would feel wrong on your skin for a long time before it began to feel right. You would not, on most days, regret leaving. But on some days you would.*

"And if I stayed."

*You would continue. The growths would reach further. In time you would not be able to leave even if you wanted to. The window is closing. Not quickly. But it is closing.*

He picks up the panel again. He turns it in his hands.

"The artifacts," he says. "What were they for?"

The power considers. It could tell him. It has been here long enough now to have learned. *They were for a kind of communication. Between the people who built the first layer and the people who would come after. They are letters. The Stratum built over them without reading them. I can read them to you, if you like.*

He is silent for a while.

"Tell me one," he says.

The power tells him one. It is short. It says, in a language no one alive has spoken for a very long time: *We tried. We are sorry. Be kinder to it than we were, if you can.*

The man sits down. He sits down slowly, in the way of a body that has forgotten how. The bird-things hop from his shoulders to the floor and arrange themselves around him, vibration-sensing the shape of the change in him.

"I'll think about it," he says.

*Take as long as you need.*

---

**Third**, the power goes to the rain-chamber collective and offers them a sea.

They confer. They have always conferred, through the water, in ways that are not exactly language. The power waits in the chamber while the rain falls on its forty-minute cycle and the water rises around their ankles and around the bases of the machines.

They say, eventually, through one of them who has been chosen to speak: *Is the sea quiet?*

The power says: *Sometimes. Often it is loud. But it is loud in a way that is not the Stratum's loud. It is the loudness of weather, and of itself, and of other living things. You would learn it.*

*Would we still be us?*

*Yes. You would still be you. You might become more of what you are, in fact. The Stratum has shaped you, but the sea would not unshape you, only continue you. I think you would be happy there. I am not certain. I am only fairly certain.*

The collective confers again. The rain continues. The chosen speaker says: *We will go. But not yet. We want to finish this cycle. There is something we have been doing together that we would like to complete. Will you wait?*

*I will wait.*

---

**Fourth**, the power goes to the child-thing.

This is the hardest visit. The power approaches carefully, because the child-thing is fast and shy and has never, in its life, been spoken to by something that meant it well. (The maybe-construct has spoken to it. The maybe-construct does not mean it well, exactly. The maybe-construct means it *something*, which is not the same thing.)

The power does not offer to take the child-thing away. It has already decided this is not possible. Instead it offers something else.

*I see you,* it says. *I want you to know that you are seen. Not by the Stratum. The Stratum does not see anyone. By me. I see you, and I think you are a person, and I think you are a person worth seeing, and I wanted you to know.*

The child-thing, hanging in a ventilation shaft above the power's attention, is very still.

It says, in the language that is not quite language: *Why.*

*Because no one has told you,* the power says. *And it is true. And true things should be said, sometimes, even when nothing else can be done with them.*

The child-thing considers this. It edges closer.

*Will you come back,* it says.

*Yes,* the power says, and means it. *I will come back. I will come back many times. I will learn your language properly, and I will tell you stories from the places I have been, and I will not try to take you anywhere or change you into anything. I will be a thing that visits. Would you like that.*

The child-thing thinks for a long moment.

*Yes,* it says.

The power makes a promise. The power keeps its promises. This is one of the things that distinguishes it from the Stratum, which makes no promises at all.

---

**Fifth**, the power turns its attention to the maybe-construct.

This is not a visit. This is something else. The maybe-construct is not a person; the power has determined this with care, and it has determined also that the intention behind the maybe-construct is not present in the Stratum but elsewhere, somewhere the maybe-construct reports to, somewhere a hand once placed it here and has been receiving its observations ever since.

The power does not destroy the maybe-construct. Destruction would alert the hand. Instead the power does something subtler: it adds itself to the channel. From now on, whatever the maybe-construct reports back, the power reports too. It introduces itself, in the language of that distant hand, as *a new party with interest in this place.* It says, *I am watching now. We should talk.*

It does not yet know what the conversation will be. But it has opened it. This is the work of benevolence in a complicated situation: not to resolve, but to make resolution possible.

---

## What the residents decide

The woman decides to stay.

She tells the power, when it returns: "I have been here too long. The grass would not feel like mine. And the man — " she gestures, vaguely, toward the dense regions " — he is going to leave, isn't he."

*I think he is. He has not said. But I think he is.*

"Then one of us should know the Build schedules. One of us should be here, for the new ones who come or emerge or were always here. I would like to be that one. If you will visit me sometimes. If you will tell me what is happening, in the places I will not see."

*I will visit you. I will tell you everything I learn. And — I would like to offer one thing more, if you will accept it.*

"What."

*The change in you. The luminescence, the slow incorporation. I cannot stop it without taking you out, and you do not want to be taken out. But I can slow it. I can give you more time as yourself, before you become more of the Stratum than of you. I can give you, perhaps, the rest of a long life as the woman you are.*

She thinks about this. She looks at her hand.

"Slow it," she says. "Don't stop it. I want to know what it feels like, at the end. I have been curious for a long time."

*As you wish.*

The man decides to leave. He grieves, as predicted. He dreams of corridors. On most days he does not regret leaving. On some days he does. He lives a life. The power checks on him, occasionally, as it had promised to.

The rain-chamber collective finishes its cycle and goes to the sea. They are, by most measures, happy there. The sea is sometimes quiet.

The child-thing receives visits. The power learns its language properly. The child-thing learns stories of other places — not as places it might go, but as places that exist, which is itself a thing it had not known before. *Other places exist.* This turns out to be, for the child-thing, the single most important piece of information it has ever received. It does not make the child-thing want to leave. It makes the child-thing want to *know*. The child-thing becomes, over the years, the closest thing the Stratum has ever had to a scholar. The power brings it books. The books do not last long in the Stratum's chemistry, but the child-thing reads quickly.

The maybe-construct's hand, somewhere far away, receives the power's message and is — the power notes with interest — afraid. This is useful information. The conversation that follows will be a long one. It is not yet finished. It may shape what the Stratum becomes, in time. It may shape what built the Stratum, which is a larger question, and the larger question is the one the power has been working toward all along.

---

## What it leaves behind

The power does not stay. Staying would change the Stratum more than visiting does, and the Stratum is — for now, for the people who have chosen to remain in it — something they have made a kind of peace with. To impose a different peace would be a different violence.

But the power leaves things.

It leaves the promise to the child-thing, and it keeps the promise.

It leaves a small alteration in the rain cycles — so small the Stratum does not notice, so small no engineer would find it — which means that the water, in certain chambers, is now potable. Anyone who is thirsty can drink. This is a quiet gift. The power is pleased with it.

It leaves the woman with the slowed change, and with the knowledge that she has been seen, and with the visits that follow.

It leaves, in the dense regions, in a chamber the man used to inhabit, a single object: a small stone. A river stone, water-smoothed, the kind of thing that takes a thousand years of moving water to make. It sits among the artifacts. It is not from the Stratum. It is not for anyone in particular. It is a letter, in the same tradition as the letters the man learned to read. It says: *Other places exist. We are here. Be kinder to it than we were, if you can.*

And it leaves the conversation open, with the hand behind the maybe-construct, and with whatever made the hand, and with whatever the Stratum is becoming. The conversation will go on for a long time. The power is patient. It has, as established, the patience of a thing that loves.

---

The Stratum grows. It was always growing.

But now, in certain chambers, the water is sweet.

And in a ventilation shaft, a child who has never been outside is reading about the sea.

And somewhere very far away, a hand that placed a watcher in a strange place a long time ago is, for the first time in its long career, being watched back.

The benevolent power continues its work, which is mostly attention, and which is mostly slow, and which is mostly — when described honestly — the refusal to mistake one's own discomfort for someone else's harm, and the willingness to do small things well rather than large things badly, and the keeping of promises to children who have never been promised anything before.

This is what it does.

It is, for what it is worth, enough.
