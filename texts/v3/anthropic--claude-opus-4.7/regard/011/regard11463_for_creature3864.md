# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives at the hour the Hollows call *third condensation*, when the warm rain has thickened to mist and the fungal light blooms strongest along the submerged corridors. It comes without thunder, without the parting of clouds — such announcements would be a kind of violence here, where the world has learned to speak only in drips and hums.

Instead, it arrives as an attention. A weight of noticing.

If you asked it later what it was, it would have no good answer. Not a god — too small a word, and too proud. Not a stranger — it has been watching Lych-Creek for a long time, the way one watches a sleeping animal whose breathing one has come to love. Call it a witness. Call it the quality of being seen by something that wishes you well and is not in a hurry.

It moves first to the edge of the brine, where the offerings lie: a coil of rusted wire, a vial of luminescent fluid gone milky with time, a stone smoothed by generations of palms. The power considers these objects. It understands that they are prayers, and it understands what the Hollows are praying for: *balance, mercy, a little more time*. It does not grant these things lightly. To grant them poorly would be to insult the praying.

So it sits with the offerings. It sits a long time.

---

**What it does not do:**

It does not raise the city. It sees the temptation — sees how easily it could lift the rusted monoliths out of the marsh, dry the foundations, scour the brine from the lower chambers and present Lych-Creek restored, gleaming, *saved*. And it sees, in the same glance, what this would kill. The fungi need the wet. The Hollows need the fungi. The Silent Ones need the deep dark of the submerged corridors where they collect their unknown artifacts. To save the city from sinking would be to murder everything the city has become in its sinking.

It does not heal the Hollows of their duality. It sees them mining the ruins that shelter them, and it understands this is not a sickness. It is a shape. A people-shape. The grief of it is real, but the grief is theirs, and to take it from them would be to take something they have woven into their very glow.

It does not reveal itself. To appear before the Nests as a presence demanding worship would scatter the careful architecture of the Brine Mother, who is, the power notes with something like affection, doing a perfectly good job of being loved. The Brine Mother may or may not exist in the way the Hollows believe. The power decides this is not its business. Belief is a structure too, and this one holds weight.

---

**What it does:**

*First*, it lends the fungi a little more patience. Not strength — strength would change them. But the fungi have been tiring, in the deepest chambers, where the toxins accumulate faster than the Hollows can filter them. The power touches the oldest colonies, the ones the Hollows believe are the Brine Mother's hair, and gives them what can only be described as *rest without ceasing to work*. They will glow a little longer. They will forgive the brine a little more. No Hollow will notice the difference for a generation, and then they will notice only as a vague sense that the deep light has been kind this season.

*Second*, it goes to the Silent Ones. This surprises even itself.

The Silent Ones drift through the submerged corridors with their clouded eyes and their collections of forgotten things, and the power realizes, watching them, that they are doing something no one in Lych-Creek has named. They are *remembering on behalf of the city*. Every rusted hinge, every fragment of pre-collapse machinery, every bone they cradle in their long jointed hands — these are not scavenged. They are *kept*. The Silent Ones are the city's memory, walking, and they have taken this burden alone because the Nests cannot carry both memory and continuance. The mist-sharing of the Nests is for the living present. The Silent Ones hold the past so the present can be light enough to glow.

The power weeps, in its own fashion. (A small additional condensation falls on the eastern quarter that night. The Hollows there will call it nothing in particular.)

To the Silent Ones, it gives one thing: the knowledge, planted gently beneath their melancholy like a seed beneath silt, that *they are known*. Not by the Nests, who fear them. Not by the Brine Mother, who is a story. By something. They will not be able to say what. But the next time a Silent One surfaces in the dim hour and looks up through the rain at the gray indifferent sky, there will be a moment — just a moment — when the sky looks back, and the Silent One will feel, for the first time in its long quiet life, *accompanied*.

This is the largest gift the power gives. It costs nothing and changes nothing measurable. It is everything.

*Third*, it does a small practical kindness. In the northern factory district, a pipeline is about to fail in a way that will collapse three Nests and drown a generation of new Hollows still soft in their membranes. The power does not repair the pipeline — that would be noticed, and noticed things become gods, and gods become demands. Instead, it nudges a single insectile creature, one of the rust-patterned tenders, to build its nest in a particular crevice. The creature's secretions will harden around the failing seam. The pipeline will hold for another forty tide-cycles. By then, the Hollows themselves will have found it, in their own way, by their own attention. They will think they were lucky. They will not be wrong.

*Fourth*, and last, it leaves a gift at the brine's edge, among the offerings. Not metal. Not light. A small, smooth object that looks like a stone but is not a stone — it is the compressed memory of a place very far from Lych-Creek, a place of dry grasses and high cold air and a sun that is not a rumor. The Hollow who finds it will not know what it is. They will turn it in their long fingers and feel, briefly, a sensation they have no word for: *elsewhere*. They will set it back down, troubled and stirred, and that night the mist of their Nest will carry a new color, and the dreams of the Nest will be strange, and one young Hollow will wake with the conviction that the world is larger than the city, and will spend their life puzzling at this, and will become, eventually, something Lych-Creek has not yet had: a *traveler*.

Whether the traveler ever leaves does not matter. The shape of *possible departure* is now woven into the Nest. The city has been given, very quietly, a future that is not only its own sinking.

---

The power lingers a while longer in the warm rain. It watches a swarm of winged things refract the fungal light into faint hues across a rusted wall. It listens to the lapping of water against metal. It finds, to its own surprise, that it is reluctant to leave.

Lych-Creek is dying. It has been dying for a long time and will die for a long time more, and in the meanwhile it is one of the most alive places the power has ever attended to. The Hollows glow. The Silent Ones remember. The fungi forgive the brine. The city dissolves into the sea and is, in its dissolution, exactly itself.

The power does the last and perhaps the truest thing it came to do.

It blesses the collapse.

Not to hasten it. Not to halt it. To *honor* it — to mark, in whatever ledger such powers keep, that this falling was beautiful, that this falling was a form of faithfulness, that the Hollows who tend a sinking world are not failures of stewardship but a kind of love the universe does not see often and ought to see more.

Then it withdraws its attention, slowly, the way one rises from the bedside of a sleeping friend.

The mist thickens. A factory chimney sighs. Somewhere in the submerged corridors, a Silent One pauses in its drifting and looks up, and for no reason it can name, its dim glow brightens — just a little — just enough that another Silent One, passing in the dark water, sees it, and brightens in answer.

This has never happened before in Lych-Creek.

It will happen again.
