# The Shriekfloor

Begin in darkness. Not the darkness of night, which has a sky above it, but the other kind — the darkness of under. The sort that has existed so long it has settled into every crack and learned the shape of things by feel.

You're in a basin. A wide one. If you could see it — and you can't, not really, not with eyes — it would reach across a valley-width of territory, miles of low gently-warped floor, bounded somewhere at the edges by rises you sense more than observe. The rock underfoot is old. Water-smoothed. The erosion here is not happening; it has happened, long ago, and the place has found its lived-in pose. Every slope is a slope it wants to be. Every channel is cut to its own satisfaction.

Cool air, wet on the skin. Not raining — but damp. Condensation runs in slow quiet ropes down the rock walls somewhere off in the dark, and pools in shallow stone bowls that have been filling and evaporating for longer than any particular word for time. You can feel the water-smell: mineral, iron, the clean cold of stone that has never been warm. Occasionally a fat drop falls from above and strikes, and you hear that drop, impossibly, through everything else.

Because — the sound.

The sound is the place.

It is not one sound. It is the layered rasping chittering creaking throat-clicking wing-whirring shrieking *of bodies*, thousands of them, tens of thousands, packed into every square meter of the basin's air and walls and floor. Creatures. Not bats, though you might reach for that word. Something older in habit, stranger in build. They cling to each other in ropes and mats. They hang in festoons from overhangs you cannot see. They crawl across the damp floor in slow living carpets that part around your feet without acknowledging you, because sight is not what they use, and you are not meaningfully in their world yet.

The density is near-solid. You cannot raise an arm without brushing against a body — small, warm-cool, its skin somewhere between wet leather and mushroom cap. They do not mind. They register you and move on. The whole basin is one enormous organism made of smaller ones, breathing in a rhythm you can feel against your ribs.

There are no plants. Not one. Nothing green has ever lived here and nothing green ever will — there is no light for it, and the creatures have filled every niche a plant might have claimed. Instead, on the damp walls: a thin fuzz of something pale, fungal or pre-fungal, feeding on the endless rain of droppings and dead and molted skin. The whole floor is soft with this accumulated matter, ankle-deep in places, a slow geological layer of life's leavings. This is what the basin eats. This is what it is made of, beneath the stone.

The creatures sing — if sing is the word — in overlapping registers. Some pulse at the edge of hearing, deep chest-rattling thrums you feel in your teeth. Others scream so high and bright the sound becomes a pressure behind your eyes. Between those extremes: the whole audible range, filled, saturated, a wall of living noise so complete that silence, when a single creature briefly pauses, registers as a small bright hole in the world. These holes never last. Something else always fills them.

They navigate by this. The sound is how they see. The basin is mapped, constantly, in echolocation, and every surface — every stalactite, every pool, every drifting body — is known to every other body. You are known. You have been known since you arrived. The shape of you is being passed through the colony as information, chirp to chirp, a rumor moving faster than you can.

And something stranger: the sound, having existed continuously in this basin for so long, has *shaped* the place. The rock, soft in parts, has been sung into resonance chambers. Certain alcoves amplify certain frequencies. Certain passages hum when a particular note is struck. The creatures use these. They have learned — over however many generations such a thing takes — to position themselves in the basin's acoustic architecture. A low thrum in one corner will travel along a carved groove and emerge, transformed, as warning-song in another. The basin is an instrument the colony plays with its own bodies. Or the colony is the instrument and the basin plays it. The distinction has stopped mattering.

There is weather here, of a kind. Tides of activity. A restlessness will begin at one end of the basin and move across it in a slow wave, bodies lifting into flight, the noise rising to something past deafening into a quality that has no name, then settling again as the wave passes. These waves come at intervals that are not regular but not random either. Something governs them. Hunger, perhaps. Or the pull of some external cycle — a moon you cannot see, a tide in the distant sea, a pressure shift far above the stone ceiling.

Because yes, there is a ceiling. You sense it. High, vaulted, lost in upward dark, crusted in its own living fur of sleeping and waking bodies. Occasional droplets falling from it confirm its existence. No opening to sky. If there ever was one, the colony has forgotten how to find it, and you will not find it either.

There is no built thing here. No tool. No scratched mark that means anything to anyone. The creatures have no hands for such work and no need of it. Their works are songs, and the songs dissolve the moment they are sung, and are sung again, and this is enough. This has been enough for a very long time.

You could stay. Many things that have wandered in have stayed, in the sense that they have become part of the soft floor. You could also leave, if you moved carefully, if you followed the slope upward, if you trusted the cool draft that sometimes curls past your cheek from some direction the colony has not entirely sealed.

But while you are here: listen. This is one of the places where the world is full. Where nothing is missing, where nothing has been added, where a single ecological idea — *small warm bodies in damp dark stone, singing* — has been allowed to run to completion. It is not beautiful in the way a meadow is beautiful. It is beautiful the way a heart is beautiful, from inside, in the dark, in the noise, surrounded on all sides by the thing it is part of.

The drops fall. The wave rises somewhere off to your left. The basin breathes.
