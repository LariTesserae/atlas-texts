# The Drift-City of Hollow Spires

Begin at altitude. You are somewhere on the cold shoulder of a continent that has not finished being a continent — the rock beneath is younger than the things that live on it, which is a strange sentence to write, but true here. The ridges are still soft-edged, pre-eroded, their fracture planes glassy and unweathered, as if the mountains were poured rather than uplifted and have not yet learned to crack properly. Water weeps from every exposed face. Not streams — seeps, condensations, the slow sweat of stone that has not decided whether to be porous or not. Where the seep-water pools it does not freeze, though it should; the temperature here hovers just above the point where breath would fog, if you were breathing.

There is no vegetation. Nothing green, nothing that has ever been green. What grows on the rock instead is a kind of mineral felt — pale filaments of some grown crystal, perhaps engineered, perhaps only mimicking life — that sheathes the wet surfaces in a nap the thickness of a finger. When you press it, it yields and then resumes. It is neither plant nor fungus. It may not be alive in any sense we would recognize, but it reacts.

Set into these young ridges, packed close as barnacles on a piling, is the city.

Call it a city for lack of a better word. The structures are tall and narrow and hollow, spires of something that looks like fused black ceramic at first glance but is, on closer attention, not any single material — it shifts its lattice when you look away, and the seams between adjacent spires are not seams but gradients, one building becoming another over a distance of meters. The spires lean into each other. Some are linked at the top by bridges that were never built, only extruded. The density is oppressive: you cannot walk more than a few paces in any direction without meeting a wall that was not there a moment ago, or was, but at a different angle.

And it is dark. The sky — if there is one above the condensing murk — admits nothing. What light exists is internal: faint cold lines that run the length of the spires like capillaries, pulsing at a rate slower than a resting heart. You see by these, and by the bioluminescence — if that is what it is — of the fauna.

Because the fauna is the thing here. The fauna is *everything* here.

They are in the water seeps. They are in the felt on the rocks. They are in the hollow spires, which are hollow because they are lived in, stacked floor on floor with creatures whose taxonomy I cannot honestly give you. Some are long and segmented and move through the vertical shafts of the spires like water moving through a drinking straw, except upward, against any gravity you'd recognize. Some are flat and adhere to the outside of the structures in overlapping scales, and when they breathe — if breath is the word — the whole spire sighs, and the sigh is part of the sound of the place.

The sound. I should describe the sound.

It is loud, but not in the way a city is loud. There are no engines, no voices in any language. There is instead a continuous layered chord: the low register is the spire-breath, a slow bellows-note that rises and falls over minutes; the mid-register is a kind of clicking, dense and rhythmic, like rain on a tin roof except the rain is intentional and counting something; the high register is the seep-water singing in the mineral felt, a thin harmonic whine that shifts pitch when you move your head. Above all of this, occasionally, a single clear tone will sound from somewhere deep in the city and hold for a long time, and every creature in earshot will briefly cease what it is doing and then resume.

The technology is what confuses a visitor most, because it does not announce itself as technology. The spires are grown. The felt is grown. The light in the capillaries is not electrical in any sense a wire would recognize — it moves, when you trace it, in loops that do not close in three dimensions. If you put your hand against a spire, your hand will feel warmer than the air, though the spire itself is colder than the air. Heat flows the wrong direction here, patiently, on purpose. Whoever built this place — if *built* is the word, if *whoever* is the word — engineered the local thermodynamics to run at a tilt. The cold is maintained. The wetness is maintained. The darkness is maintained. Something wants it this way.

The dynamic is slow. That is the final thing to understand. Nothing here happens quickly. The fauna are overwhelming in number but each individual moves with the deliberation of something that expects to live a very long time, or that is waiting for a signal that comes rarely. The spires grow, but you would have to watch for a season to see it. The seep-water collects in basins at the ridge-feet and the basins fill over years. The mineral felt advances across new rock at a rate you could only measure with patience.

And yet the place is not static. It is the opposite of static. It is *saturated* with slow intention — every cubic meter densely occupied by something doing something, and all of those somethings are parts of longer movements whose shape you cannot see from inside a single lifetime. The clicking in the mid-register is counting something. The long tone is answering something. The felt is reaching for something. The young mountains are taking the shape they have been instructed to take, and in ten thousand years they will have cracked exactly where the city wants them to crack, and water will run exactly where the city wants it to run, and the hollow spires will be ready for whatever they are being prepared for.

You stand on a ledge of unweathered glassy stone. Your feet are wet. Something breathes against your ankle — not threatening, only present. Above you the capillary-light pulses once, slowly, and a kilometer away, on the next ridge, an answering pulse. Between the two pulses, for a moment, you can feel the whole shape of the place: a vast cold wet dark patient organism-city, pre-erosional, post-biological, mid-thought, and you are inside its thinking.

Then the pulse passes, and it is only dark again, and loud, and full.
