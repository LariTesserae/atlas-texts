# The Singing Terrace

High up, where the air thins to that particular warmth that feels less like heat and more like proximity to something burning slowly inside the world, there is a terrace. Not a built terrace, exactly, though hands have been here. A natural shelf in the side of a mountain that isn't quite a mountain — call it a vast root, a knuckle of older geology pushed up through itself and weathered into a single broad lip perhaps two hundred paces across.

There is no water here. None. Not a drop, not a film of dew. And yet the terrace is *thick* with green.

The plants drink from the air itself, but the air carries no moisture — what they drink is **sound**. The terrace lives in a permanent storm of noise, and the vegetation has learned to feed on it.

---

The dominant growth is something like a fern crossed with a brass instrument. Coiled fronds the color of old bronze and oxidized verdigris, each frond a hollow tube that flares open at its tip. When the wind moves across them — and the wind here moves *constantly*, in updrafts off the lower slopes — they sound. Not all at once. In rolling waves and clusters and sudden solo notes. A grove of them gives the sustained chord of a pipe organ being slowly tuned by something distracted.

Between the brass-ferns: sparse, but each presence enormous in its silence-around-it. A tree whose trunk is a column of stacked fused vertebrae-shapes, mineral-pale, with a crown of long ribbon leaves that *click* against each other like bamboo chimes but pitched lower, woodier. A patch of low ground-cover that resembles inverted ears, pink and translucent, gathering the noise and pulsing visibly when a loud sound passes — you can watch them flush darker after a thunderclap from below. A single, central thing that might be a tree or might be a sculpture: black, smooth, branching like coral, and humming on a constant fundamental note that you feel in your sternum before you hear it.

The space between the plants is wide. You could lie down between any two and not touch either. This is part of how the terrace works — each plant needs its own acoustic territory.

---

The fauna are everywhere, abundant, and most of them are some variation on the theme of *resonator*.

Beetles the size of a thumb with wing-cases like tiny cymbals, that crash together when they take off. They never fly alone; they erupt in clouds of thirty or forty and produce, for two or three seconds, the sound of a kitchen being knocked over, before settling again into the brass-ferns to feed on the standing tones.

Something larger that I keep only half-glimpsing: a long-bodied animal, six-limbed, with a throat-sac that inflates and deflates and produces a deep wooden *thock thock thock* like someone striking a hollow log. It moves between the bone-trees with surprising delicacy.

In the air, kite-shaped flyers ride the updrafts and *sing* — actually sing, in slow descending phrases, looping. Their song is what the inverted-ear ground-cover seems to prefer; you can see patches of the ear-plants tilting subtly toward whichever flyer is currently passing.

And smaller things. A persistent low chorus of insect-equivalents in the underbrush, clicking, whirring, rasping. The terrace floor is never quiet, never close to quiet.

---

The built things are few and modest. Whoever was here — is here, perhaps, intermittently — worked in stone and fiber and a little hammered copper.

A low wall, knee-high, curving around nothing in particular, made of dry-stacked flat stones. Patient work. Old. The lichens on it argue for centuries. It seems to mark, not enclose. Perhaps it marks where the wind changes character — when I crossed it, the chord of the brass-ferns shifted noticeably.

A cairn at the eastern lip, with a copper bowl pinned to its top by another stone. The bowl rings when the wind is right. There is no offering in it and there has never been an offering in it; the bowl *is* the offering.

Three uprights of dressed stone near the central coral-tree, set in a loose triangle, each with a hole bored through near the top. Wind-flutes. They drone in slightly different keys and together produce a wavering harmony with the coral-tree's hum. The boring of these holes would have taken months with the tools available — chert, copper points, abrasive grit, and patience. Someone wanted this exact triad.

No one is here now. Or no one I can see. There are footprints in the powder at the western edge that look fresh, but the powder is so dry it might hold a footprint for a season.

---

The light is dim and strange. The terrace lies in the shadow of something larger above it — another shelf, maybe, or a permanent bank of haze. The sun, when I can locate it, is a coppery smear. Most of the light reaches the terrace as a soft directionless glow that makes the bronze fronds gleam without quite catching any single edge. Shadows are weak and multiple. It feels like the hour before a storm, except the storm is what you are standing inside, and it is made of noise instead of water.

---

The weirdness is this: **the terrace is rearranging itself.**

Not visibly, not while you watch a single point. But over the course of an afternoon I became sure that the central coral-tree had been further from the cairn when I arrived. And the triangle of wind-flutes is not quite a triangle anymore; one of them is leaning differently. And the brass-ferns — I would swear a whole stand of them migrated from the southern lip to the middle while I was looking at the flyers.

The plants are slow, but they are not still. They move toward sounds they prefer and away from sounds they don't. The flyers above shape the slow currents of vegetation below by where they choose to sing. The beetles, by erupting in their cymbal-clouds, scatter the ear-plants, which crawl elsewhere over the following hours. The wind-flutes, set by some long-ago hand to produce that wavering triad, have become the slow gravitational center of the whole composition — the terrace's vegetation orbits the flutes the way iron filings orbit a magnet, except over weeks instead of seconds.

The terrace is a piece of music being performed by everything in it, and the performance reshapes the orchestra, and the reshaped orchestra alters the performance, and this has been going on, in balance and constant turbulence, for a very long time.

This is what the wall marks. This is what the bowl listens for. This is why the flutes were placed *exactly* there.

Someone, long ago, tuned this place. And then they left, or stayed in a form I can't recognize, and the place has been tuning itself ever since — never settling, never resolving, holding its turbulent chord in the thin warm air at the top of the world, in the dim coppery light, with no water anywhere, and no silence anywhere either.
