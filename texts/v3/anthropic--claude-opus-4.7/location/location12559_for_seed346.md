# The Pre-Town

There is a town here, but it has not finished happening.

The first thing you notice is the cold — not bitter, but the deep-set chill of a stone basin that has never been warm. Your breath ghosts and hangs. The light is ordinary, the kind of overcast you'd find on a flat afternoon in any northern country, except that nothing here casts the right shadow. Some things cast two. Some things cast none, and you can tell which is which by looking, though you couldn't explain how.

The basin is shallow, scarcely a depression in the wider plain. A river ought to run through it — you can sense the argument for one — but instead there are oxbows of damp gravel, ribbons of darker stone where water has been, or will be, or is being considered. In a few places a thin film of moisture seeps up between pebbles and stands there, not draining, not evaporating, a held breath of water. Condensation beads on the undersides of things that have no tops.

The vegetation is sparse and strange in the way that early things are strange. Lichens that haven't decided on a color. A few grasses, but the grasses are translucent at the tips, as though the idea of a blade had been drafted but not yet inked in. Three small trees stand in a rough triangle near what might become a square — their bark is detailed, their leaves are detailed, their root systems are detailed, but the trees themselves are only present from about knee-height to about shoulder-height. Above and below, they trail off into suggestion.

And then the town.

It is dense — astonishingly dense — and almost entirely built. Buildings stand wall to wall, sometimes wall *through* wall, sharing masonry the way ideas share a vocabulary. The streets are too narrow to walk down comfortably and there are too many of them. Where two alleys meet, a third sometimes resolves itself from the corner, and a fourth has been resolving for some hours and has not yet arrived. The architecture is impossible to date. There are arches that pre-suppose vaults that have not been built; there are vaults supported by arches that will be added later. The stone is good stone, cold under the hand, with the slight tackiness of something freshly quarried, though no quarry exists.

This is the part that is hard to describe: the town is being engineered, but not by anyone present, and not in time the way time normally works. Whatever is making it is working at a level the eye can't catch. If you stare at a windowsill long enough you notice the grain in the stone has rearranged itself — not been recut, *rearranged*, the atoms shuffled like cards into a slightly more correct order. The mortar between bricks is doing arithmetic. Walk down one of the streets and you'll find a doorframe that yesterday was a smear of potential and today is nearly a door, with hinges that haven't quite committed to which side they hang from.

The scale is small. You could walk the whole town in twenty minutes if the streets held still. It feels, in its compactness and its hush, like a model of a town — except that it's the wrong way round. The town is full-sized; it's *reality* around it that feels like the model, the rough sketch, the placeholder world inside which this small dense thing is being carefully assembled.

Almost no one is here. A pale beetle works its way along a gutter, and is the only living thing you see for a long time. Later, on a roof, something bird-shaped — but only shaped, like a bird-silhouette cut from the air — sits and does not move. You will not see it leave. When you look back it will be gone, and a feather, perfectly rendered, will lie on the cobbles, and the cobbles around it will be slightly more finished than they were before, as though the feather has paid for them.

The sound is mostly the cold itself: that high faint ring empty stone makes when there is nothing to absorb it. Occasionally a tick, as of cooling metal, from deep inside a wall. Occasionally a sound like a page turning, very far away. Once, and only once, you might hear something that is unmistakably a hammer-blow, struck against nothing, and after it the air will smell briefly of ozone and wet slate, and a small ornamental balcony on a third floor will exist that did not exist before.

The rules, as best as can be inferred:

Nothing here is finished, but nothing is unfinished in the way ruins are unfinished. A ruin has lost something. This place has not yet been given it. The erosion is running in reverse, or rather, the erosion is the *first draft* of formation — small pittings and roughnesses on the stone are not damage but the early marks of a surface deciding to be a surface. Water collects in these pits and the pits deepen and the water deepens with them, slowly, slowly.

The weirdness is total but quiet. Reality is not broken here; reality is being *drafted*, and you are inside the draft. Lines are still being moved. A street that was a dead end will, with sufficient unobserved time, find that it was always a thoroughfare. The cold is the cold of a room where the heat has not yet been installed because the room has not yet decided it wants heat. The town will probably be beautiful. It is already mostly beautiful. It has the patience of something that knows it will be here, eventually, and is in no hurry to commit.

If you stay, you will find that you, too, become slightly more detailed. The seams of your coat will gain stitches you don't remember. Your hands, examined closely, will have an extra crease across the palm, very fine, very new, as though somewhere a decision has been made about who you are going to have been.

This is a polite place. It does not demand. It only continues, slowly, to arrive.
