# The Warm Room Beneath

There is a pocket in the water. Not an air pocket — the water is still here, pressing in on every side — but a place where the water has agreed, for reasons no one negotiated, to hold a shape that a body can move through. You can breathe it. You cannot breathe the water three meters away, which is identical in every measurable respect. This is the first rule, and it is not explained.

The pocket is maybe twelve meters across. Warm. The temperature is the temperature of a mouth, and the water-that-is-air has the faint thickness of held breath. Light comes down from somewhere above — ordinary daylight, the kind that falls through a shallow sea onto pale sand — except there is no above. Look up and you see more of the same warm clarity, lit evenly, no surface, no sun. The light simply exists at the brightness a summer afternoon would have, and has always had, and will.

The floor is young basalt. Black glass still sharp at its edges, not yet rounded by the currents that aren't moving. It cooled recently — recently in some sense — and the cooling-cracks are fresh enough to cut a hand. Nothing has grown on it. No algal film, no sessile thing, no patina. The stone looks as if it emerged yesterday and will look this way in ten thousand years, because nothing is happening to it. There is no erosion here, only the memory of the one event that made the shapes: the pour, the quench, the cracking. After that, stillness.

Set into the basalt, at irregular intervals, are the built things.

A low wall of stacked stones, dry-laid, no mortar, about knee-height, running six meters and ending. A clay bowl, unfired or fired so long ago it might as well be unfired, sitting upright on a flat place, empty. A wooden post driven into a crack — wood, here, which should not exist, which shows no sign of waterlogging or rot — with a notch cut near its top as if something once hung from it. A cairn. A hand-axe of worked flint, laid down neatly beside a second hand-axe, as though someone meant to come back. The work is competent and old. None of it is arranged to be a dwelling. These are the marks of a visit, or many visits, by people with hands and patience and no plans to stay.

There are no people now. There may never have been. The objects are present the way a dream's furniture is present — unsigned, unexplained, not accumulating.

And the sound.

The sound is enormous. It fills the pocket and the water beyond the pocket and probably extends further than anything could measure. It is the sound of a cathedral bell struck once and sustained forever, except it is not a bell, it is closer to a held chord sung by a choir the size of a mountain, except it is not that either — it is the sound a place makes when it has decided to be loud, without source, without decay. You feel it in the sternum. It does not interfere with hearing other things; a dropped pebble still clicks audibly on the basalt. The great sound simply occupies every register underneath and around the small sounds, a pedal-tone for the world. After some minutes you understand that the sound is not coming from anywhere because the sound is what the room is *made of*. The water, the warmth, the light — these are local effects of the sound holding itself in this shape.

Fauna: once, perhaps, something will cross the pocket. A single translucent swimmer, finger-length, moving with the deliberation of something that has nowhere to be. It does not school. It does not feed — there is nothing here to feed on. It passes through the wall of the pocket as if the wall were not there, which for it, apparently, it is not. Whether it is the same one each time or a different one is not a question the place answers.

Nothing changes. The light does not dim toward evening because there is no evening. The warm water does not cool. The sound does not waver. The flint axes lie beside each other in the configuration they have always lain in. If you move one — and you can; it lifts, it has weight, it is a real flint axe — and set it down elsewhere, and leave, and return, you will find it where you left it. The place accepts small alterations and incorporates them into its permanence. You become, briefly, one of the people who made the marks. Then you leave, and the pocket continues without you, warm and lit and sounding, one of how many such rooms nobody has counted, embedded in a sea that may itself be embedded in something, holding its shape against a physics that elsewhere would not permit it, for no reason, for no one, steadily.
