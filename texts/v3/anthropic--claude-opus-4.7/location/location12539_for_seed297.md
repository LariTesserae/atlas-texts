# The Garden That Solved Itself

There is no water here, and there has never been. Yet the place is lush — impossibly, almost insultingly lush — and this is the first thing that has to be understood before anything else makes sense.

The ground is a fine pale mineral, something between bone-ash and powdered glass, dry to a depth no instrument has measured. Walk on it and it does not stir. Press a hand into it and the hand does not come away dusty. The mineral has the texture of dryness but none of its consequences, as if dryness here is a quality being performed rather than enforced.

Out of this dry floor: the vegetation. It is everywhere. Stalks the thickness of a thigh, fluted and ribbed, rising to perhaps twice human height before splaying into broad fans of tissue that look botanical only at a glance. Closer in, the leaves resolve into something more like circuit, or more like coral, or more like a diagram of itself. They are green — emphatically, satisfyingly green — but the green is laid on in lacquered planes rather than pigments, and the undersides shimmer with a faint iridescence the eye registers as wetness and the skin, hovering close, registers as nothing at all.

The plants do not transpire. They do not photosynthesize in any way a chemist would recognize. They simply *persist*, fed on something the landscape will not disclose. Pull one up — you can, with effort — and the root is a single smooth taper ending in a bead of mirror-black material. The bead is warm. The bead is the same temperature as everything else here, which is the warmth of a held breath, the warmth of a room someone has just left.

---

The land lies low and open. Not flat — it rolls in long, shallow swells, the way a coast rolls back from a sea that isn't there — and from any rise you can see for what would be miles if miles still applied. The vegetation crowds the swells in tiered layers: ground-mat, mid-fern, canopy-fan, and above the canopy a drifting stratum of pale spheres the size of fists, neither floating nor falling, simply *positioned* at altitudes between four and nine meters. They do not move when you walk through them. They do not move when you push them. They are exactly where they are.

The light is generous and clean. There is no sun visible, but the sky has the unbothered brightness of midmorning, and shadows fall the way shadows ought to fall, with a single consistent angle that you stop trying to trace after a while because tracing it makes you feel watched.

---

The terrain is young. You can see it in the edges — every leaf-margin is sharp, every ridge in the mineral floor crisp, no rounding, no weather-wear. Nothing has been here long enough to be softened. And yet nothing is changing. The youth is fixed; the freshness is permanent. It is a landscape caught at the moment just after creation and held there by some force that has decided the moment was good and need not proceed.

This is the part that troubles people who arrive here, when people arrive, which is rare.

---

The fauna are few and strange. Once an afternoon — though there are no afternoons — something passes through the canopy without disturbing it. A long-bodied creature, six-legged, the color of weak tea, moving with the deliberation of a thought being completed. It does not eat. It does not drink. It pauses occasionally and adjusts the position of a leaf by some fractional amount, and the leaf, having been adjusted, stays adjusted forever. There are perhaps a dozen of these creatures across the whole region. They do not acknowledge each other. They do not acknowledge you.

Lower down, in the mat-layer, small bright things tick between stems — not insects, not quite, more like ambulatory punctuation marks. They are difficult to count because counting them changes the count. Look away and back: a different number. This is not a trick of attention. The landscape simply does not commit to how many of them there are, and seems mildly embarrassed when you press the question.

---

The sound is almost nothing. A faint tone in the high register, just at the edge of hearing, the kind of note a room makes when it is very empty and very full at the same time. No wind, because there is no air movement worth the name; the atmosphere is still as a sealed jar. Sometimes, if you stand in one place long enough, you can hear the plants make small adjustments — a soft click, a settling — as if they are correcting themselves against a template only they can see.

---

There are no buildings. There have never been buildings. But every so often, walking, you come across an *arrangement*: seven stalks growing in a precise hexagonal pattern around a central depression of mineral floor, or a cluster of canopy-fans angled so that their shadows compose, on the ground, a shape that means something in a language you almost know. These arrangements are not built. They simply *occurred*, in the way the whole place occurred, and they have the quality of design without the residue of a designer.

This is where the technology lives. Not in objects, but in the *grammar* of the place. The vegetation is doing computation — you can feel it if you stand still, a faint pressure behind the eyes, the sensation of being inside a sentence that is taking a long time to finish. Whatever made this landscape did not make tools. It made conditions. The conditions are the tool. The conditions are still running.

---

Reality is thin here, and the thinness is not hidden. Look at the horizon and it will sometimes show you the back of your own head, faintly, at the far edge, the way a long hallway with mirrored ends shows you receding. Drop a stone and occasionally the stone will fall in two places at once and you will have to choose which falling to remember; the other one will quietly stop having happened. Plants growing close together sometimes share a leaf — one leaf, belonging to two stems, the bilocation cheerfully unresolved.

None of this is hostile. None of this is even strange, once you have been here for a span of what would be hours if hours applied. The landscape is simply not pretending to be more solid than it is. It is a draft that someone decided not to finish polishing, because the draft already worked.

---

And nothing changes. That is the deepest fact of the place. The vegetation does not grow because it is already the size it needs to be. The mineral does not erode because erosion has been factored out. The creatures move but their movements net to zero across any span you can measure — every leaf they adjust was adjusted to be adjusted; every position they occupy was always going to be occupied. The landscape is in equilibrium with itself at a level so complete that *time* here is more like a dimension you can face than a current you ride.

You can leave whenever you want. The way out is the way you came in, which will still be there, because nothing is going anywhere.

But standing in it — in the warm dry green hush of it, under the held light, with the punctuation-creatures ticking softly in the mat and the suspended spheres hanging at their assigned altitudes and the long-bodied thinker passing once through the canopy without sound — standing in it, you understand that you have walked into something's *answer*. Some question was asked, very precisely, a long time ago or perhaps just now, and this place is the reply, and the reply is complete, and there is nothing more to add.
