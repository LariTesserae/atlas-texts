# The Underwarm

Start with the dark. Not the dark of a cave mouth where your eyes adjust and find walls — the dark of a place that has never expected to be seen. A held dark, warm against the skin like breath. Ninety degrees, maybe ninety-five. Dry. The air smells faintly of struck flint and something animal, something that has rested here a long time.

You are below. Below what is harder to say. There is no surface above that you remember climbing down from; the ceiling, where it can be felt, is low in some chambers and absent in others, opening upward into vaults whose tops the hand cannot find and the ear cannot echo back from. The place is vast in the way a lung is vast to a mote of dust inside it — vast as an interior, not as a distance.

The floor is dry stone, but young stone. Sharp. It has not had time to be worn smooth by anything. Edges catch at the foot, crystal faces still showing the flat planes of their growth. In places the stone has bubbled and frozen mid-bubble, glass-thin shells you must not step on. In other places it stands up in needles. There is no water here and has never been. The chemistry that built these chambers was something else — a slow respiration of warm gases through colder rock, depositing minerals on the inside of the world like rime on the inside of a bottle. The walls glitter very faintly. Not light. Just an electrostatic suggestion that something here remembers being charged.

The dark is not absolute. It is 0.4 of a darkness, which means: if you sit still long enough, certain things resolve. The crystal faces hold a low phosphorescence — not enough to see by, enough to know they are there. Some of the needles glow at their tips when something passes. The animals, when they pass, are darker than the dark, blocking out the faint shimmer behind them, and that is how you know they are there.

The animals.

This is the strange part, and the part that fills the place. The Underwarm is dense with them — not crowded, exactly, but layered, presences at every level of the chambers. They cling to the walls and hang from the ceilings and move along the floor and rest in the bowls of crystal and occupy the air. Most are small. Most are quiet. None of them eat each other, as far as can be told, because there are no plants and there is no water and the usual logic of who-consumes-whom does not apply here. They subsist on the warmth. They subsist on the slow chemistry of the rock. Some of them, it seems, subsist on each other's stillness — pressing close, sharing whatever they share, the way embers share heat.

There is the flat one, palm-sized, that moves along the wall by changing which edge it considers its leading edge. There is the soft thing in the cup of a crystal, which has been in that cup, perhaps, longer than the cup. There is the listener, with no eyes and many small ribbed ears arranged along its back, which turns toward you not when you make sound but when you make decisions. There is something larger, glimpsed once, that occupies a low corridor and seems to be made of the same mineral as the walls, only warmer, only breathing.

They do not fight. They do not flee. The Underwarm is at equilibrium, has been at equilibrium for what feels like a very long time, and equilibrium has shaped these creatures the way wind shapes a dune everywhere else. They have all learned the same lesson: do not disturb. Move when you must. Otherwise hold.

This is why the place is quiet. Not silent — there is the dry tick of crystal settling, the occasional papery shift of something repositioning a limb, a low resonant hum from deeper chambers that may be the rock itself thinking — but quiet. A library quiet. A held-breath quiet.

And there are signs of hands.

Not many. Not recent — but "recent" is a word that does not apply well here, since nothing ages and nothing weathers and a mark made a thousand years ago looks the same as a mark made yesterday. On one wall, a row of small notches at chest height, evenly spaced, made with something harder than stone. In a side chamber, a hearth: a ring of darker stones, no ash, no char — whatever burned here burned cold, or burned so long ago that even the soot has crystallized and become part of the floor. A tool, set down and never retrieved: a haft of some dense fibrous material, bound with sinew, ending in a worked point. The point is chipped from a mineral that does not occur in these chambers. Whoever brought it brought it from somewhere else.

There are perhaps a dozen such sites scattered through the Underwarm, and that is all. No villages. No paths worn into the floor — the floor is too young and too sharp to wear. Whoever was here was here lightly, and either left, or is still here and has learned the lesson the animals learned: hold.

It would be a mistake to think of them as having gone. The tools are placed, not dropped. The notches are recent in feeling, even if not in fact. Once, in a far chamber, a small cairn — five stones balanced — and the top stone, when approached, was warm in a way the others were not. Held warmth. Hand-warmth, maybe. From a hand that had been there minutes ago, or a hand that had been there for centuries holding the stone in place by the slow attention of being.

The rules of this world, as best as can be inferred:

Nothing changes that does not have to. The animals know this. The makers, whoever they are or were, came to know it. The crystals grow so slowly that growth is indistinguishable from stillness. Even the warmth is the same warmth it has always been — there is no source of it, no fire below, no sun above; it simply is the temperature of this volume, as if the Underwarm were itself a body and this its blood-heat.

To enter is to be entered into the count. To move is to be noticed by everything at once, and then forgiven, and then absorbed back into the held quiet. To stay is to become, eventually, one more presence among the many — another darker shape against the faint shimmer, another warmth in a cup of crystal, another listener turning toward the next decision someone makes.

The vastness of the place suggests it goes on. Chambers open into chambers. A weather of its own, almost: in some regions the air drifts in one slow direction for what might be miles, carrying the flint-and-animal smell along with it, depositing it in still pockets where it pools and thickens and the creatures there are denser, slower, more saturated with the long patience of the world.

Somewhere, presumably, the Underwarm ends. Somewhere there is an edge, a wall, a final chamber. But the logic of the place argues against finding it. The logic of the place argues for sitting down on a flat patch of young stone, letting the eyes adjust past adjusting, and waiting to see what resolves out of the dark next.

Something is already approaching. It has been approaching since you arrived. It is in no hurry. Neither, now, are you.
