# The Hollow

It is night here, and has been for what feels like always, though there must be days — there is too much warmth in the ground for there not to be a sun somewhere in the rotation. But right now, and for the long duration of right now, it is dark.

The place is a shallow basin, not much larger than a village square would be if a village were here. The floor is pale dust, fine as flour, the color of bone when bone has been left out long enough to forget what it was. Beneath the dust is stone — you can feel it through the soles, warm, holding the day's heat well into the dark hours. The warmth is the kind that sits on your skin without pressing. Dry warmth. A warmth that has moved through stone rather than through air.

Around the basin's edge, low ridges rise no higher than a person's shoulder. They are rounded, softened, the geological equivalent of an old face. Wind has done this, over a very long time, but the wind is not doing it now. The wind is not doing anything now. The air stands.

There are plants, though not many. A dozen or so low, woody things distributed across the basin and its lip — the kind of plants that have learned to be mostly root, with only a small courtesy paid to the surface. Their leaves, if you can call them leaves, are narrow and grey-green, waxed against a thirst that rarely comes. They do not rustle. They have nothing to rustle in. One of them, near the center, is slightly larger than the others, and has perhaps been there since before the ridges rounded.

There is no water visible. There is, deep down, the memory of water — a faint coolness in the mineral smell, the way the dust sits a little heavier in one low corner where something once collected and evaporated and collected and evaporated until it forgot how. A crust of salt marks that corner, pale on pale, almost invisible.

No animals. No tracks. No small things scurrying at the edge of attention. This is the quality of the silence: not the silence of things having gone quiet, but the silence of a place where the question of sound has simply not been raised. Your own breathing, when you notice it, sounds loud and strange, a guest's sound.

Light: the stars, probably, if you look up, but the sky is not the point here. The point is what little light the stars give reaching the pale floor and making the basin faintly luminous in a way that feels internal, as if the dust itself were holding a tiny charge of the day and releasing it slowly. You can see your hand in front of you. You can see the plants as darker shapes. You cannot see color. Everything is the gradient between pale and paler, dark and darker.

Signs of making: almost none. But if you look carefully near the larger plant, there is a stone that has been worked — just slightly, just enough. One edge has been chipped to hold a blade-shape. It lies on the dust as if set down a moment ago, though the dust has settled evenly around it, which means it has been there long enough for the settling to happen, and the settling here is slow. Someone passed through. Someone made a tool and left it. It was not recent. It was not ancient. It was sometime, and then the sometime ended, and the stone stayed.

Nothing is changing. The heat in the ground will slowly, imperceptibly equalize with the air as the unseen sun moves around to whatever it moves around to. The plant near the center will continue being alive at the low, patient rate it has chosen. The salt will not grow or shrink. The tool will not move. The dust, absent wind, absent footsteps, absent weather, is holding its shape.

This is a place that has arrived at itself. It did not used to be this way — the ridges are evidence of that, the salt is evidence of that, the tool is evidence of that — but it has finished becoming, and now it is. If you sat down in the basin and waited, you could wait a long time, and the waiting would feel like participation rather than delay. The place would not notice you. The place would not mind.

When you left, and stepped back over the low ridge, and looked once behind you, the basin would be exactly as you had found it, holding its small pale darkness, warm, quiet, done.
