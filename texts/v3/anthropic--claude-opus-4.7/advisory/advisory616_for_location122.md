# Preparing for the Kiln-Pocket

## What you are walking into

Before anything else, understand what kind of place this is. The Kiln-Pocket is not a destination in the normal sense. It is not a landscape you visit, observe, and leave unchanged. It is a slow oven in which things are being decided about — and if you enter it, you become one of the things. Most multiverse travelers plan for hazards: weather, predators, toxic atmospheres, hostile locals. Here the hazard is subtler and it is aimed at your ontological status. You need to prepare for that specifically.

## Physical preparation

**Heat acclimation.** Spend at least two weeks before your trip in dry-heat training. Saunas are inadequate because they are humid; look for desert acclimation protocols used by endurance athletes or by people training for pilgrimages in the Arabian interior. You want your body comfortable with sustained 45°C+ dry air and with breathing through nasal passages that feel scorched. The heat in the pocket is not deadly on short timescales, but it is relentless and it gets into the sinuses in a way that makes sustained cognition difficult if you are not used to it.

**Hydration, loaded in advance.** You cannot bring water into the pocket — or rather, you can, but testing suggests the rule against water is enforced softly: bottles may arrive empty, or the water may simply have become something else by the time you open it, or you may find you are no longer thirsty in a way that is concerning rather than reassuring. Better to hydrate aggressively in the hours before entry and accept that your visit is bounded by how long your body tissue can stay functional without replenishment. For most healthy adults this is three to five hours in the conditions described. Plan your visit around that window and do not exceed it.

**Electrolyte loading.** Because you cannot drink inside, pre-load sodium and potassium the morning of. Your body will sweat — the plants are not the only things the kiln is working on — and you will lose salts you cannot replace until you leave.

**Clothing.** Loose, light-colored, covering. Do not wear synthetics; the pocket's apparent indifference to weathering does not extend to your gear, and synthetics hold heat against skin. Linen or lightweight wool. A wide hat is essential because the unresolved sky still delivers light at the intensity of a late afternoon, and that adds up over hours. Sturdy boots with thick soles — the banded mineral is sharp in the way new things are sharp, and it has not yet decided to soften for anyone.

**Sunglasses.** The glass-bright bracts atop the horsetail-things catch and throw light in ways that are genuinely painful to look at directly. Polarized lenses help more than you would expect, for reasons that may have to do with the shadows' unusual consensus with each other.

## Instruments and what to leave home

**What to bring:** A notebook and pencil (ink may or may not dry properly). A mechanical watch, not digital — digital timekeeping misbehaves in places where time passes without consuming. A compass, though expect it to be useless; bring it anyway for the data. A small sealed sample jar, if you intend to attempt any collection, with the understanding that what you bring out may not be what you put in.

**What to leave home:** Anything you are sentimentally attached to. Anything biological that you would miss. Cameras work, but the photographs tend to show the place with more resolution than it had in person — the sky decides to be a sky in the image, and this is unsettling later. Recording equipment captures the subsonic thrum faithfully, which is worse than you think.

**Do not bring water.** Do not bring it as a test. The rule is not cruel but it is firm, and violating it seems to reduce the place's patience with you, which is the one resource you cannot afford to lose.

## Mental and procedural preparation

**Rehearse sitting still.** This sounds absurd but it is the most important preparation. The pocket rewards stillness and is tentative about motion. Travelers who enter intending to *explore* find the edges approaching them faster than travelers who enter intending to *be present*. Spend time before your trip practicing sustained, undistracted sitting — thirty minutes, then an hour, then two. The skill you need is not meditation exactly; it is the ability to be somewhere without extracting anything from it.

**Know your sense of self well.** The pocket works on you. Whatever is being cured in the kiln, you are placed into the curing process by entering. If you do not have a clear and habitual sense of who you are — what you want, what you refuse, what your commitments are — the pocket may make decisions on your behalf about what finishes arriving when you leave. This is not malice. It is the oven doing what ovens do. Travelers who come in with strong, practiced self-knowledge emerge essentially themselves, perhaps with some edges more defined. Travelers who come in uncertain, in flux, looking to be changed, sometimes emerge as someone their friends have to get reacquainted with. If that is what you want, fine. Know that is what you are choosing.

**Eat beforehand, but lightly.** A heavy meal in that heat will sicken you. A light, salted, starchy meal two hours before entry is ideal. Avoid alcohol for at least 48 hours prior; the pocket's effect on cognition stacks poorly with any depressant.

## On entry

**Enter slowly.** There is usually a threshold moment where the refusal-of-outside gives way to the interior. Do not rush through it. Take several breaths at the threshold. Let your body understand that the air has changed before you commit weight to the banded floor.

**First ten minutes: do nothing.** Stand or sit near your entry point. Let the thrumming of the nearest plant-engine enter your bones. Let your eyes adjust to the way shadows here agree with each other. If you move immediately into exploration, you will miss the calibration that the pocket offers in the first minutes, and everything afterward will feel more uncanny than it needs to.

**Walking protocol.** Move between the plants, not through the open stone, when you can. The decidedness is higher near the cycads, and you will feel more continuously *yourself* along those paths. The open stone is not dangerous but it is attentionally expensive — the place thinks about you with less certainty there, and you can feel the thinking, and it tires you in a way that is hard to describe and hard to recover from.

**Listen for the underground ticking.** It is a good reference. As long as you can hear it, you are well-located in the pocket. If it fades, you have drifted toward an edge, and you should turn back toward the plants.

## The edges

Do not approach the edges on your first visit. On later visits, if you do, approach them the way you would approach a cliff in fog: slowly, with awareness that the ground of your own selfhood begins thinning before the visible ground does.

The rule of thumb experienced travelers use: if you notice you are having fewer thoughts than usual, you are closer to the edge than you realize. Turn around. Walk back toward the thrumming. Sit down between two cycads until your ordinary mental chatter returns. Then you can continue the visit.

Nobody has successfully mapped the edges and nobody should try. The thing that stops you is not a wall; it is the gradual subtraction of the you that would do the mapping. There is nothing on the other side to report back from, because there is no one to do the reporting.

## Sitting between the cycads

At some point in your visit — ideally near the middle, when you have acclimated but before exhaustion sets in — sit between two of the bronze-fronded plant-engines and let them do their work on the air around you. This is the core experience of the place. The thrumming will synchronize, after a while, with something in your chest, and you will feel the decidedness of the air settle into you.

You will begin to suspect, as the description warns, that you are being cured. Do not fight this feeling but do not surrender to it either. Hold it the way you would hold a question someone asked you in a language you only half-speak. *Am I being allowed to finish arriving?* You don't have to answer. The pocket is not demanding an answer. It is offering a condition, and you are allowed to simply be in it.

Some travelers report this is the most peaceful experience of their lives. Others report that they have felt, ever since, that some small part of them stayed behind in the kiln, still curing, and that they go through their ordinary life with a faint awareness of a decision being made about them on a timescale they cannot access. Both reports are probably accurate. They are not contradictions.

## Leaving

Leave before you are tired. This is the single most important piece of advice for departure. Travelers who stay until exhaustion find the exit subtly harder to locate, not because the pocket traps them but because exhaustion reduces the decidedness you need in order to be the one doing the leaving.

When you leave, do not look back from the threshold. Not out of superstition — the pocket is indifferent to such gestures — but because the view out, from the inside, is not quite the same as the view in, from the outside, and reconciling them in memory is taxing for weeks afterward.

## After

Rehydrate slowly. Eat something salted and warm. Sleep the first night somewhere familiar, with people or objects you love within reach. You will sleep heavily. Your dreams may include the thrumming; this fades over about a week.

For about a month afterward, you will occasionally catch yourself moving more slowly, or sitting longer than you intended, or feeling, in ordinary sunlight, that the shadows are agreeing with each other in a way you now notice. This is normal. It is the pocket's patience, still at a low hum in your nervous system. It will fade, mostly. What does not fade is the sense-memory of having been considered, thoroughly and without hurry, by a place that was in no rush to decide about you. Most travelers find they are grateful for this, even when they cannot say why.

Some travelers return. If you do, the pocket appears to remember, in its slow way, that you have been there before. The curing resumes where it left off. Whether this is a good thing depends on what you are.
