# Preparing to Visit the Drowned Nursery

## On What You Are Walking Into

Understand, first, that this is not a hostile place. It is something rarer and more difficult: a place that does not know you exist and will not particularly notice if you come or go. Most dangerous environments in the multiverse are dangerous because they react to you. The Nursery is dangerous because it barely does. You will be moving through something that operates on a timescale and attention-span for which human presence is, at most, a brief thermal disturbance. Plan accordingly.

## The Body

Do not bring scuba gear. I want to be clear about this. Standard diving equipment will fail in ways that matter. Regulators depend on pressure gradients that the Nursery does not consistently provide; buoyancy compensators assume a reliable up. More importantly, bubbles here become *small green thoughts that swim away*, and you do not want to discover what happens when a piece of your breathing apparatus becomes semantically active.

Instead: go in the condition of someone who expects to breathe water, and practice believing it beforehand. This sounds absurd. It is not optional. The Nursery's rules are soft, which means they yield to conviction the way wet clay yields to a thumb. Travelers who arrive certain they will drown tend to drown. Travelers who arrive uncertain tend to experience a long, confused interval in which the water is sometimes breathable and sometimes not — this is worse than either extreme. Arrive with one clear expectation and hold it.

Meditators do well here. So do people who have spent serious time freediving, not for the lung capacity but for the mental architecture: the knowledge that your body can be calmer underwater than it is on land. If you have neither background, spend at least three months before departure learning to hold stillness in water. Not hold your breath — *hold stillness*. The difference will matter.

Wear something thin, dark, and close-fitting, in a natural fiber that does not mind being wet forever. Wool works. Linen works. Anything synthetic will develop a strange relationship with the plants and may begin, over hours, to feel like it is growing into you. Leave all jewelry behind except possibly one small object of deep personal meaning, which the chamber may accept as a kind of grammar-offering and which will help you locate yourself later. (More on this below.)

Go barefoot, or nearly so. The plants want contact.

## The Mind

This is the real preparation.

**Learn to tolerate scale-uncertainty.** Spend time before the trip in places where your sense of size becomes unstable: very large empty rooms, very dense forests, sensory deprivation tanks. The Nursery's refusal to settle on a size is not an illusion you will see through. It is a genuine property of the space. You need to be someone who can function without a reliable answer to "how big is this" and "how far away is that." Panicked travelers try to force scale to resolve and exhaust themselves. Prepared travelers let the ambiguity be.

**Practice unfocused attention.** The plants grow toward sustained attention. This is charming once, unsettling twice, and genuinely dangerous by the third time, because a frond lengthening toward your eye is also a frond occupying space you intended to move through, and in aggregate your looking can cage you. Learn the skill of *present but not-fixed* perception: awareness that does not land. Buddhist open-awareness practice is useful. So is the way a hunter scans a treeline. So is the state you enter when driving a familiar road.

**Accept that you will lose time.** The pooled-time bowls are not avoidable; the chamber's geometry is too unstable for that. You will emerge with some delta you cannot verify. Make your peace with this in advance. If there is someone waiting for you, tell them the truth: I may come back older. I may come back younger. I may come back on a day I did not intend. Do not have pressing appointments. Do not have a pet that needs feeding on Tuesday. Do not be in the middle of a course of medication that requires strict timing.

**Decide, before entering, what you are going in for.** This is not because the chamber demands a purpose — it is indifferent — but because your own purposelessness, in a place this patient, can become corrosive. Travelers who enter the Nursery without an intention report the strongest disorientation and the greatest difficulty leaving. Travelers with even a modest intention ("I want to see what a world looks like before it has woken up") fare better. The intention need not be grand. It needs to be yours.

## What to Bring

Almost nothing. The Nursery is a subtractive place; additions feel wrong there and tend to be quietly absorbed or repositioned.

- **One small meaningful object.** A ring, a stone from somewhere important, a pendant. Not for sentimentality — for navigation. The chamber's exit moves, but certain objects that carry strong association can act as anchors. You will find, returning, that the object feels warmer in directions that are more like *out*.

- **A length of dark cord,** perhaps six meters. Tie one end to yourself at entry. The other end will not reliably lead you back — the geometry does not cooperate — but the cord will sometimes indicate, by the way it drifts, where time is pooling. Cord that floats oddly still is sitting in a slow-time bowl. Avoid.

- **No light source.** I know this sounds wrong. Bring no torch, no glowstick, no bioluminescent lure. Artificial light in the Nursery is an assault on a chamber whose lighting is its own slow photosynthesis of meaning. The mosses will dim in the presence of strong light, sometimes permanently in a radius around where the light was used, and you will have done real damage to a place that was not expecting guests. Let your eyes adjust. They will. The eyelid-colored phosphorescence is enough; it is more than enough once you stop demanding it be more.

- **No camera, no recording device.** They will not work in the way you hope. What they capture will not be what you saw. Some travelers find this interesting; most find it distressing, because the discrepancy becomes a wedge in memory afterward and the visit retreats from you faster than it should. If you must document, bring a small waterproof notebook and a graphite pencil, and write only after emerging.

- **No food.** You will not be hungry. If you are hungry, you are in trouble of a kind the food will not fix.

## The Approach

Enter slowly. Whatever the portal, threshold, or means of arrival your multiverse travel uses, decelerate in the last stages. Arriving abruptly in the Nursery produces a shock-front in the water that disturbs the oldest moss, and you do not want your first impression on this chamber to be a small act of vandalism. Think of it as entering a room where someone is asleep, except the someone is the room, and the sleep is what the room *does*.

On entry, hold still for a long time. Longer than feels reasonable. Let the water close over the shape of your arrival. Let the plants notice, in their slow way, that a new density has appeared among them. Only then begin to move.

## While You Are There

Move as little as possible. The Nursery rewards patience and punishes, mildly but persistently, the impatient. What you want to see will come closer if you stay where you are. What you want to understand will arrive in you if you stop pursuing it.

Do not swim through the standing arch unless you are prepared to accept that you may come out facing a direction you had not been facing, and that this direction may not exist in the sense you mean by *direction*. Travelers have gone through the arch and emerged facing *earlier*, or *smaller*, or *toward the idea of a mother*. These are not metaphors. If you go through, go through deliberately, and do not go through twice.

Do not touch the basin of denser water. I realize the temptation. Don't.

Do not try to take anything with you. Not a leaf, not an opalescent bead, not a chip of the fresh stone. The Nursery will allow it — it does not police its contents — but what you bring out will not be what you picked up, and the difference will follow you home.

If you see a pale newt-thing, do not follow it. It is not a guide. It is not anything. It is drifting the way a thought drifts through an unoccupied mind.

Speak, if you like. The chamber will not answer, but your voice will become a feature of the place briefly, and the plants may incline toward the sound. Sing, even. The Nursery has not yet been given sound as a feature, and you can offer it a little, gently. This is one of the few gifts a visitor can give that the chamber seems to accept.

## Leaving

This is the part most travelers underprepare for.

The exit is not where you left it. I said this before; I will say it again because people forget. You will not retrace your path. Instead, you will feel, at some point, a small rightness — a sense that a particular direction is more *out* than others. This is the anchor-object working. Follow it without urgency. If you rush, the feeling dissolves; the Nursery does not respond well to being left in a hurry, and a hurried exit tends to deposit travelers somewhere adjacent to where they came from, which is to say *not home*.

Before leaving, stop once and look at where you have been. Not to remember it — you will not remember it the way you remember other places — but to acknowledge it. This is a courtesy. The chamber is old and slow and probably does not register the gesture, but some travelers report that the courtesy matters on the return, that the exit opens more easily for those who have thanked the place that let them in.

## After

Expect to be quiet for some time. Days, perhaps weeks. The Nursery does something to the pace of your inner life, and for a while ordinary speeds will feel violent. Plan a soft re-entry to your own world: no social obligations, no loud media, no important decisions. Sleep will be strange and deep. Dreams will have a greenish cast. This passes.

Do not try to describe the visit to people who have not been. You will find the words inadequate, which is ordinary; but you will also find the memory *retreating* as you try to fix it in language, which is not ordinary, and which is specific to this place. Write it down if you must, in the same notebook, in short phrases, not narratives. The Nursery resists narrative. It was never a story. It was a slow green thinking, and you were briefly inside it, and now you are not, and that is the correct relation.

One last thing. Some travelers, months later, find that a small green thought occasionally swims through their mind — unprompted, gentle, unrelated to anything they were considering. This is not a malfunction. It is a piece of your breath that stayed behind, finding its way back. Let it pass through. Do not hold it. It is going somewhere, and it knows the way.
