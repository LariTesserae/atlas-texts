# Preparing to Visit the Greenhouse Cavern

## Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before anything practical, sit with what this place *is*. Most destinations in the multiverse threaten you with their hostility — cold, predators, poison, vacuum. The Greenhouse Cavern threatens you with its hospitality. It is warm, lit, moist, quiet, and completely indifferent to whether you stay a minute or the rest of your life. That is a specific kind of danger, and you should prepare for it specifically.

The cavern is a steady state. You are not. You will import motion, heat fluctuation, sound, smell, waste, intention, and time-sense into a place that has done without all of these for longer than your species has had language. Plan accordingly — not because the cavern will punish you (it won't; it barely notices), but because *you* will have to do all the work of being a temporary, changing thing in a place that has forgotten how that feels.

## Clothing

**Lightweight, breathable, light-colored.** The temperature is that of a humid southern summer afternoon, and it does not drop. Ever. There will be no cool evening to recover in. Wear loose linen or technical fabrics that wick. Avoid anything black — you will overheat standing still.

**Long sleeves and long trousers anyway.** You will be brushing against leaves, vines, fungal shelves, and root-fiber continuously. You do not know what any of it is. Some of the plants may secrete irritants, sap, or spores simply as part of their metabolism — not to harm you, but you are not adapted. Thin long sleeves are better than exposed skin.

**A wide-brimmed, soft hat.** The light is shadowless and constant. This is not just a matter of glare — it is a matter of your skull being lit from above without reprieve for however long you stay. Shade your eyes and the crown of your head or you will come out with a headache that lasts days.

**Soft, flexible footwear with good grip and ankle support.** The root-floor is a springy mattress. Hard boots will tire your ankles in an hour; barefoot is tempting but unwise (the root-mesh may have sharp woody points, and the pools harbor something that passes for algae). Think of it like walking on a very uneven trampoline for hours. Trail runners or climbing approach shoes.

**Sunglasses, tinted but not dark.** The light is not harsh like desert sun, but it is *relentless and unvarying*, which is its own assault. Amber or rose lenses help.

## Water and Food

**Bring all your water.** Yes, there are pools. Yes, a hand comes out warm. *Do not drink them.* The water has been sitting at body temperature in a closed ecosystem, skinned with duckweed-analog, for who knows how long. Whatever microbial life inhabits it has had centuries of undisturbed evolution and has no reason to be compatible with human biochemistry. Even boiling is a gamble — some alkaloids and secondary metabolites survive heat. Pack more water than you think you need; the heat will draw it out of you faster than you notice, because there is no wind to remind you that you're sweating.

**Eat nothing growing there.** This is obvious but needs stating. Not a leaf, not a seed-stone, not the shelled grazer, not the pale segmented thing. The cavern has had no pressure to evolve compatibility with outside digestive systems. You are not in a forest. You are in an organ.

**Bring salty food.** You will sweat continuously without realizing it, because the air is still and the moisture lingers on your skin. Electrolyte loss is the real threat, not dehydration alone.

## Navigation

This is where most visitors will get into trouble.

**There is no horizon. There is no sun. There are no shadows. There is no wind direction.** Every direction looks the same — tiered green walls receding into soft green distance. The light is too even for silhouettes. Your inner compass, which in ordinary landscapes is quietly triangulating off a dozen unconscious cues, will have nothing to work with and will begin to invent things.

**Bring a real compass.** A magnetic one. Test it before you commit to a direction — the place may or may not have a coherent magnetic field, and you need to know which.

**Mark your path.** Not by breaking or cutting anything — do not damage the cavern, both as a matter of respect and because you cannot predict the consequences. Bring bright, biodegradable cord or ribbons and tie them loosely at eye height every twenty paces. Bring more than you think you need.

**Count your steps. Write them down.** The cavern's sameness will erode your memory of distance traveled within the first hour. Keep a notebook, not just a phone — you will want the physical act of writing to anchor you.

**Consider not going far.** The entrance is the only feature. The column-tree you glimpsed in the middle distance is a landmark, yes, but by the time you reach it, the entrance will be invisible behind you and the column-tree will look like a wall of every other large trunk.

## Time

**Bring a reliable watch. Two, ideally.** The cavern has no time cues. The light does not change. The temperature does not change. Your body, denied day and night, will begin to improvise — you may find yourself suddenly exhausted, or sharply awake at what your watch says is 3 AM, or uncertain whether you have been walking for twenty minutes or three hours.

**Set alarms for everything.** When to eat, when to drink, when to turn back, when to sleep. Do not trust your sense of having "just" done any of these things. The cavern flattens duration.

**Plan a strict turn-around time and obey it.** Visitors to this place have been known to think *I'll just go a little farther* and then keep thinking it. The cavern is not actively seductive — it is the absence of interruption that does the work. Nothing tells you to stop.

## Sound, Speech, and Company

**Go with at least one companion, and speak to them regularly.** The soundscape is almost null: a distant plok, a creak, a hiss of stoma. Within an hour your ears will begin hunting for stimulus and may begin manufacturing it. Hearing voices, footsteps behind you, a kind of almost-music — these are common. A companion to talk to, even about nothing, restores the baseline.

**Don't shout. Don't play music. Don't bring speakers.** Not because anything will punish you — but because the cavern's silence is one of its realest qualities, and you cannot un-break it for yourself once you've broken it. You will spend the rest of the visit in the sound of your own intrusion. Speak at conversational volume, and allow for long quiet stretches.

**Expect emotional weather.** People react to the cavern in different and often surprising ways. Some feel an immense peace, a sense of having come home to something pre-human. Some feel an almost suffocating wrongness — the *stillness* of it, the impossibility of decay, the slow green press. Both reactions are valid and neither should be fought. If either becomes overwhelming, leave. The cavern is not a place to push through feelings.

## The Built Things

**Do not disturb the wall, the cairn, or the archway.** Photograph them if you like, sketch them, sit near them. Do not touch the lashings. Do not remove a stone from the cairn. Do not walk through the archway — or if you do, understand that you are using a thing whose purpose you do not know, made by someone whose mind was not yours.

It is possible that the builders are still there. Not watching you — the cavern has no watching in it — but present, in some form so slowed and distributed that you would have to sit motionless for a very long time to register them as a living thing. Behave as though this is true. You lose nothing by it and may gain something.

**Leave nothing behind.** Pack out every wrapper, every hair, every drop of urine if you can manage it (bring sealable containers). The cavern has absorbed very little from its previous inhabitants in a very long time. Do not be the one who changes that.

## Sleep

If you must sleep there — and if you stay more than eight hours you probably must, even if your watch says it's only afternoon — bring an eye mask and earplugs. The constant light will wreck your circadian rhythm even through closed eyelids, and the silence will become loud with your own body's sounds.

**Sleep on a ground sheet, not directly on the root-floor.** The warmth from below will heat you through the night in a way you will not notice while falling asleep and will deeply regret around hour four.

## Returning

**Plan for reverse culture shock.** Coming out of the cavern into ordinary weather — wind, shadow-movement, birdsong, cold, the cycling of light — can be genuinely disorienting. Do not drive immediately. Do not make decisions immediately. Sit somewhere ordinary for an hour and let time re-enter you.

Some visitors report, for days afterward, a strange grief for the cavern's completeness. The world outside is a place being built and falling apart at the same time, always. The cavern is not. You have spent however many hours in a place where nothing needed to happen, and you will carry a little of that home, and it will not fit cleanly into a life that requires things to happen. This passes. Be gentle with yourself while it does.

## Last Things

Bring a notebook and write in it.

Bring a small thing of your own that cycles — a wind-up toy, a pendulum, a metronome — and look at it sometimes. It will help.

Do not promise yourself you will come back. The cavern will not miss you and cannot welcome you. It simply is, at the intensity of a bright overcast noon, and it never stops being that.

The drops fall. The leaves hold. The light does not change.

You are the one who has to change, to have been there at all.
