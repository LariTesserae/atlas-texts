# Preparing for the Slow Canopy

## First, Adjust Your Expectations

You are not going on an expedition. You are going to be *absorbed*, temporarily, into something that has been settling into itself for longer than your species has had language. Most of your instincts about travel — covering distance, seeing sights, making progress — will be useless here and may make you miserable if you cling to them. The canopy does not reward motion. It rewards staying still long enough for your ears to open.

Plan for at least a week. Anything shorter and you will only experience the disorientation, none of the reconciliation.

## Clothing and Skin

Wear wool, or something that behaves like wool. Synthetics will rot-smell within a day in that permanent damp. Cotton will never dry. You want layers that are warm when wet, because you will be wet — not rained on, exactly, but *infused*, the way a sponge is wet.

Nothing bright. Not for camouflage; for courtesy. In the dim green light, a high-visibility color is an assault. Earth tones, mosses, the color of old paper.

Bring foot coverings with grip but not cleats. You will be walking on living tissue. Cleats will wound things that do not heal on human timescales. Think of soft-soled climbing shoes, or go barefoot once your feet toughen — many visitors settle on barefoot by the third day, as the bark underfoot is warm and giving.

Accept that your skin will stay slightly damp the entire visit. Bring anti-fungal powder for the folds of your body — armpits, groin, between toes. This is the single most practical piece of advice anyone will give you. The canopy is a fungal paradise and you are a warm wet substrate.

## What to Carry

As little as possible, and nothing loud. No metal that clinks. No synthetic fabric that swishes. No electronics with fans or beeps. If you must bring a light, bring a dim red one and use it rarely; your eyes will adjust to the green gloom within two days and anything brighter will set you back.

A hammock, not a tent. You will sleep slung between boughs, and you will sleep well — the canopy cradles sleepers. Bring a light blanket for the pre-dawn cool.

Water is everywhere but drink it carefully. The bromeliad-cups each have their own small ecosystem and some of those ecosystems will disagree violently with your gut. Collect from fresh drip, from leaves you have watched water accumulate on, not from standing pools. A simple cloth filter is enough.

Food: bring dense, simple things. Nuts, hard cheese, dried fruit, flatbread. You will not be hungry the way you expect — the warmth and humidity suppress appetite, and the air itself seems nutritive in some way the body registers before the mind does. Plan on eating about two-thirds of what you normally would. Do not try to forage. Much of what grows there is edible to *something*, but that something is rarely human.

## The Time Pockets

This is the part most travelers underestimate.

When you enter one of the dim chambers where the air is slightly older, you will feel it as a faint pressure behind your eyes, a sense that your footsteps are arriving a half-beat late. **Do not hurry through.** Hurrying is what causes the problem. The pockets are harmless to people who move at the canopy's pace; they only distend around people who are trying to outrun them.

If you emerge from a pocket feeling you have been there longer than you were, sit down. Drink some water. Let your body catch up to itself. The feeling passes within an hour. Fighting it makes it last a day.

Carry no watch. A watch in a time pocket will either stop, gain, or — occasionally — show a time that is simply wrong in a way that will distress you for weeks afterward. Time your trip by hunger and sleep, which is what the canopy uses.

## On Sound

You will find yourself whispering within a few hours and you will not know why. The hush is absorbent and your voice, at normal volume, will feel like an indecency. This is correct. Match the place.

If you hear the far-off exhale of something large, do not investigate. This is not a safety warning in the ordinary sense — nothing here hunts humans, nothing here moves fast enough to be a threat to an alert traveler. It is more that investigating is a category error. The large thing is part of the canopy's breathing. You do not investigate a lung.

If you hear the single low note of the unseen singer, stop walking. Stand still. Listen until it has fully faded, including the part of it that fades inside your chest. Then continue. This is not required. But travelers who do this consistently report the canopy becomes legible to them in a way it does not for those who walk through the note.

## The Small Shrines

You will find them. A ring of fist-sized stones. A weeping face elongated by centuries of bark growth. A rotted reed platform.

**Do not restore them. Do not clean them. Do not take anything.** They are being absorbed, and this is their function now. The people who made them — whoever, whatever they were — made them *to be absorbed*. Interrupting this is a minor violence against something very old.

You may sit near them. Many travelers report that sitting beside the weeping face for an hour produces a settling in the chest that is difficult to describe and does not recur elsewhere. Take what the place offers. Leave nothing behind, including footprints you can help avoiding.

If you feel moved to leave an offering — and you might, the impulse is common — leave something that will rot. A piece of your bread. A drop of your blood on a leaf. Nothing that will outlast you.

## Psychological Preparation

This is the hardest part to prepare for and the part most people neglect.

The Slow Canopy will, within about three days, begin to *work on you*. The pace, the damp, the green dimness, the sense of being inside a body rather than outside anything — these combine to dismantle a lot of the scaffolding that holds a modern person together. You will find yourself thinking thoughts you do not usually permit. You will find old griefs rising, unhurried, and settling like silt. You will find that decisions you have been agonizing over become either trivially obvious or entirely uninteresting.

This is not a crisis. It is what the place does. The canopy has had enough time to decide what it wants to be, and being near it for a while will raise the same question in you, gently, without insisting on an answer.

Bring a blank notebook and a soft pencil. Write slowly. Most of what you write will not be useful later but writing it will be useful now.

Do not bring anyone you are in active conflict with. The canopy will not help you resolve it; it will simply make the conflict feel very small and very far away, and you will return to your own world to find it exactly the same size as before, which is disorienting.

Do bring, if you can, someone who knows how to be quiet with you.

## Returning

Schedule at least two days of buffer between leaving the canopy and resuming your ordinary life. The transition back is harder than the transition in. Your world will seem, for a while, unbearably loud, dry, sharp-edged, and *fast*. You will flinch at sirens and at the speed of conversation. You will find yourself mourning things you didn't know you were going to mourn.

This also passes. But honor it. Do not go straight from the canopy to a meeting, a city, a crowd. Spend a night somewhere quiet and dim. Eat something warm. Let the rain, if there is any, fall on you for a while before you go back indoors.

## One Last Thing

The canopy will not remember you. This is important to accept before you go. You are not going to make a mark on it; you are not going to be changed in any way it notices. You are a small warm wet thing that visited for a little while and then left, and the canopy will continue its immense slow arrival into itself without reference to your having been there.

This is, for most travelers, the gift of the place. Somewhere that is entirely uninterested in your significance. Somewhere that has simply *arrived*, and is willing to let you sit inside its arrival for a few days before you go back to all your unfinished things.

Pack light. Walk slowly. Listen for the note.
