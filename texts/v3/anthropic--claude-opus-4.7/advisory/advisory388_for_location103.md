# Preparing for the Hallowgrowth Plateau

## What You Are Walking Into

Before anything practical, understand this: you are not visiting a place. You are visiting a single enormous living thing that has decided, over eons, to let a town and a biosphere stand on its back. Every preparation flows from that. You are a guest on a body. A body that is patient, opinionated, and has mass.

The plateau will tolerate you. It will not notice you, which is different from welcoming you, and which is also different from disliking you. Your goal as a traveler is to keep all three of those states holding.

---

## Physical Preparation

**Cardiovascular conditioning.** The air is thin — the plateau sits above where water bothers — and it is not, chemically, the air you know. The argon-bearing exhalations that feed the local metabolism are inert to you but they displace what you need. You will breathe shallower than feels right, and you will tire at half the pace you expect. Spend six months before departure at elevation. Arrive already accustomed to working slowly. The inhabitants are slow for cultural reasons; you will be slow for physiological ones, and this coincidence will serve you.

**Feet.** You will walk on end-grain for kilometers. It rings. It is harder than stone but less forgiving, because it transmits. Every step will travel outward as a small note someone, somewhere, hears. Get boots with thick, dense, sound-absorbing soles — cork-cored, or better, felted layers bonded to a compliant polymer. Do not bring anything cleated, toothed, or spiked. Never anything that bites. The first rule of the plateau is that nothing may break the surface, and this includes the accidental gouge of a hiking pole tip. If you bring a staff, cap it in soft rubber and inspect the cap every morning.

**Hearing protection — but the right kind.** You are going to a loud place, but the loudness is not damaging in the way industrial noise is damaging. It is resonant, low, and continuous. Foam earplugs will make you miserable because they will cut you off from the one sense by which everything there communicates. Bring musician's filters — the flat-response kind that reduce volume without flattening frequency. And bring a second pair. You will lose one.

**Sleep gear.** You cannot sleep here without becoming, briefly, an instrument. Accept this. Do not fight it with noise-cancelling headphones; they will fail against a sound conducted through your bones. Instead, bring a hammock or a thick pad that isolates you from the wood-ground as much as courtesy allows. Expect your dreams to be peculiar and structured for the first several nights. They will stabilize.

## What to Bring

**Water, in sealed metal vessels.** Glass is too likely to shatter, plastic is porous to smells the plateau will emit and will begin to taste wrong by the third day. Drink in private. Drink quickly. Do not spill. If you spill, do not try to wipe it up — the water will find a fissure and vanish before you can kneel. Just stand still a moment and apologize in your head. This is not superstition. This is manners.

**Food that does not require water to prepare.** Hard biscuits, cured fats, dried meats, concentrated sugars. Nothing that needs rehydrating. Nothing that needs cooking, unless you carry a small flameless chemical heater. Open flame is probably not forbidden but it is deeply unwise: the substrate is wood, in the oldest and most technical sense, and while it may no longer burn by any ordinary chemistry, you do not want to be the traveler who tested this.

**A humming-bowl or small resonator.** The inhabitants of Ossara speak by humming into their sternums and letting the plateau carry it. You cannot do this; your bones are solid. But you can carry a small wooden or ceramic bowl, press its open mouth to your chest, and hum into it, and the plateau will carry that sound adequately. Without something like this, you will find that every attempt to talk to an inhabitant either fails to reach them or reaches them as a flat dead syllable without the resonance that carries meaning. Practice at home. Hum a vowel into the bowl and listen for the point where your chest and the bowl lock into a single note. That is the register Ossara uses.

**No water-based anything else.** No wet wipes. No canteens that sweat. No hydrating creams. No contact lens solution — switch to glasses or have surgery before you go. No toothpaste that foams; bring a dry-clean dentifrice, a chewing-stick, something Bedouin. Water that escapes you here is not simply lost. It is refused, and the refusal registers.

**A notebook and a soft graphite pencil.** You will want to write. Use graphite, not ink; ink is a fluid, and while small amounts seem to be tolerated, you should not test where the line is. Do not draw maps of Ossara and do not sketch the pillars. Sketch the trees if you like — they appear to enjoy being noticed, as far as anyone can tell.

**A dust mask for dusk.** The yellow pollen that collects in the swales is released in a fresh pinch every evening from pores in the ground. It is not toxic to humans, but it is not nothing either — it is the reproductive material of a plant that has not had reproductive success in a geologic age, and inhaling it in quantity makes people suggestible, quiet, and, after a few days, slightly prophetic in ways that do not translate when they return home. Mask up at dusk. Uncover only when the air feels settled again, usually an hour after full dark.

**Something to offer.** Not a gift for anyone in particular — an offering to leave on one of the great stumps. This is not a cultural requirement; no one will ask it of you. It is a personal one. The plateau is so old and so alive that passing across it without acknowledging it feels, when you are actually there, like walking through someone's living room without nodding to the grandmother in the chair. Bring something small, clean, metallic, and not water-soluble. A coin from your home world. A key that no longer opens anything. Leave it on a stump the first day. Do not retrieve it.

## What Not to Bring

- Anything sharp with an exposed edge. Knives travel sheathed, always.
- Water in quantity beyond what you need.
- Any pet, familiar, or symbiote. The substrate will notice a second consciousness attached to you and may respond. Responses are not described, only alluded to.
- Seeds. Of anything. Of any plant you know. The plateau's biosphere is old and closed and would metabolize a foreign seed in ways you do not want to witness and may not survive witnessing.
- Strong perfumes or scented toiletries. The fauna navigate partly by chemical gradient, and you will attract the wrong kind of attention — not hostile, exactly, but interested, and the reed-hares are a meter long and weigh as much as a goat and do not understand personal space.
- Electronics you care about. The pillars regulate fields you do not understand, and the threads in the air carry something, and your devices may or may not continue to work. Bring backups of critical data on analog media — film photographs of your loved ones, a printed map of your route. Assume your phone is a paperweight the moment you arrive.

## Mental Preparation

**Learn to wait.** A conversation in Ossara can have a delay of seconds or minutes. An inhabitant will begin speaking to you, you will hear nothing for a long moment, and then the hum will arrive through the ground and up your spine and resolve into meaning. Do not fill the silence. Do not repeat your question. The locals have developed the habit of beginning every sentence as if continuing one; try to adopt this. Start mid-thought. Trail off. Let the person you are speaking with pick up the thread from wherever the plateau hands it to them.

**Practice patience with your own body.** You will be slow. Your thoughts will be slow. The pollen at dusk, the resonance at night, the thin air, the lavender dimness — all of these conspire to reduce you to the local tempo. Do not resist this. Travelers who try to maintain their home-world pace suffer something like altitude sickness, except it presents as a growing conviction that everyone around them is conspiring to withhold information. They are not conspiring. They are simply speaking at the speed of the ground.

**Prepare to not understand the sky.** The occlusion overhead is not discussed. No one will tell you what it is. You will look up and see a dim lavender sky partially eclipsed by something too large and too far to resolve, and you will want to ask, and you will learn very quickly that asking is a category error, like asking a fish to explain water. Decide in advance that you will not press the question. If you must think about it, think about it privately, and do not write it down.

**Prepare to not stand in certain shadows.** At noon, the shadows under the great trees are the deepest black you will ever see. Children are told not to stand in them. This instruction extends to you. You will be curious. Resist. There is no published account of what happens to those who ignore this rule because, as with most of the plateau's warnings, the consequences are alluded to rather than described, and the people who could describe them are, presumably, the ones who disappeared into the dark.

**Accept that you are brief.** You will be there, by local reckoning, no time at all. The inhabitants will be kind to you in the way one is kind to a mayfly. Do not take this as condescension. It is accurate. A visit of two weeks to Ossara is, on the plateau's clock, roughly the duration of a blink. Your memories of the place will outlast, in subjective weight, nearly everything else you have ever done. This is a feature of the plateau. It lays down heavily.

## Etiquette in Ossara

**Do not enter rooms no one has used.** The town, unprompted, grows rooms. Some of these have never been entered by any inhabitant. You will find doorways standing open onto dim interior spaces, and the urge to explore will be considerable. Don't. The inhabitants avoid these rooms for reasons they cannot fully articulate, which is the strongest possible reason in a culture this old.

**Do not touch the pillars.** Not because they will hurt you — they probably will not — but because touching them is a gesture that means something specific in the local sign-grammar, and you do not know what it means, and you may commit yourself to something.

**Greet the trees by name if you can learn them.** Each sovereign tree has a name, a disposition, and sometimes a grievance. The inhabitants will, if you ask respectfully, tell you the names of the nearer ones. Nodding to a tree as you pass it is not required but is noticed. By whom, it is hard to say.

**Eat what is offered.** The inhabitants farm the reed-hares loosely. If you are offered a meal in Ossara, it is the meat of a reed-hare, and it tastes like smoked brass and is nutritionally adequate for humans. Refusing is possible but unwise. Accept a small portion. Chew slowly. Hum your thanks into your bowl.

**Do not ask about the makers.** Whoever shaped the first buildings of Ossara is gone. The inhabitants do not know whether their ancestors left, died, or transmuted, and they are comfortable with not knowing. Pressing them on it will produce a long, polite silence that the ground will not carry to you, and you will understand that you have stepped outside the conversation.

---

## Departure

When you leave, leave cleanly. Take all your trash. Take your offering's wrapping, if it had one. Walk out the way you came, retracing your path if you can remember it. Do not look back from the edge of the plateau; this is not a superstition, but you will find that the view from the edge, looking back across the humming tableland at dusk, is difficult to stop looking at, and some travelers have stood there until they became reed-hare feed.

Expect to dream of the plateau for the rest of your life. Expect to hum, occasionally, without meaning to, and to have strangers glance at you as if they almost heard something. Expect that water will taste slightly wrong for a year or so after you return, and that you will find yourself pausing before you drink it, as if asking permission of no one in particular.

This is the plateau's mark. It is not harmful. It is simply that you have, briefly, been part of an instrument, and part of the instrument is now part of you.

Pack accordingly.
