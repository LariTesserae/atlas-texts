# Preparing to Visit the Condensery

## Before You Go

Understand first what kind of place this is. The Condensery is not dangerous in the way a volcano is dangerous, or a war zone, or a vacuum. It will not kill you. It will do something subtler: it will offer you its equilibrium, and equilibrium, to a creature like you, is a kind of slow erasure. Prepare accordingly.

Give yourself a deadline before you descend. Write it on your wrist in ink that will not wash off in warm water — and understand that the water there may wash off things you did not think were washable. Tell someone topside when to expect you. Better: arrange for someone to come down and physically retrieve you if you do not return by a set hour. The Condensery will not stop you from leaving, but it will not encourage you either, and the difference matters more than you think.

## Hearing Protection — and Why You Should Not Use It

Bring earplugs. Do not wear them.

The roar is the Condensery's primary fact. To mute it is to visit a different place — a quieter, falser place, a tourist's Condensery. You came to hear what the pocket sings. Let it sing into you. But carry the plugs, because if you begin to feel the sound entering your thinking, if you catch yourself listening *with* the roar rather than *to* it, you will want the option to break the spell.

A small mechanical metronome, wound tight, is worth more than any earplug. Its tick will not carry in the roar, but you can feel it in your palm, and feeling a rhythm that is *yours* — one that will wind down, that is mortal — will remind you that you are not made of the same material as this room.

## Clothing

Wear what you would wear to wade through a warm shallow river for several hours. Quick-drying synthetics. No wool, which will hold the water and its familiarity against your skin. Boots that lace high and drain well. Do not go barefoot, though you will be tempted — the warm water against skin is part of how the place gets in.

Wear something with a color not found in the Condensery. The place is amber, brass, black iron, wet stone. Bring red. Bring a red scarf, a red band on your wrist, something that insists it is from elsewhere. When your reflection arrives a half-beat late in the standing water, you want to see something unmistakably *yours* arriving late, not just a vague human shape that could be anyone, or no one, or the previous visitor.

## What to Carry

- **A watch with hands, wound by you before descent.** Digital watches are reported to behave unreliably in places of deep equilibrium; more importantly, a wound watch is a small engine you maintain, and maintaining a small engine in the presence of the great unmaintained one is grounding.

- **Paper and a graphite pencil.** Ink runs. Ballpoints fail in the humidity. You will want to write things down, not because you will forget them but because writing is an act of marking, and the Condensery offers few opportunities to mark.

- **Food with strong flavor.** Salt, chili, vinegar, citrus. Eat something sharp partway through your visit. The Condensery's water has no taste, no smell, no character. Your tongue needs a thing to hold onto.

- **A photograph of someone you love, laminated.** Look at it once an hour. This is not sentimental advice. It is operational.

- **A small hammer.** Not a weapon. A small hammer, the kind a jeweler uses. You will not strike anything with it. But carrying a tool that implies change, implies making, implies the possibility of altering matter — this is a posture of being that the Condensery does not share, and posture matters here.

- **Do not bring plants, seeds, or living cultures.** They will not grow. Worse, something about their not-growing in a place where not-growing is a rule may propagate back to you in ways no one has adequately described. Leave the yogurt at home.

## Mental Preparation

Practice, in the weeks before, the habit of *refusing peace*. This sounds perverse. But the Condensery offers a specific quality of peace — the peace of the already-settled — and you are not ready to settle. Find things in your ordinary life that are loud, unresolved, itching, and do not resolve them. Carry an argument with you. Carry a hunger. You want to descend while actively *wanting something*, because wanting is the opposite of what the place is, and it will act as a tether.

Rehearse the names of people who depend on you. Say them aloud while walking. Know them so well that the roar cannot wash them smooth.

Decide, *before* you go down, what you will do tomorrow. Specifically. Not in general terms. "Tomorrow at nine I will call my sister. Tomorrow at eleven I will sign the lease. Tomorrow at three I will eat the leftover rice." These small concrete futures are what the Condensery dissolves first, and they are what you will need to find your way back to.

## What to Expect of Yourself

You will want to stay. Everyone does. The text you were given is honest about this. The temptation is not dramatic; it will not announce itself. It will arrive as a simple thought: *I could sit down against this pipe for a minute.* Then: *there is no reason to hurry.* Then: *the drip from pipe seventeen, if I watch it long enough, will tell me something.*

It will not tell you anything. It has nothing to say. That is the point. But your mind, presented with a metronomic drip and a total roar and an amber half-light, will begin to manufacture meaning, because that is what your mind does, and the meaning it manufactures will be the meaning of a place that has *reached its conclusion*, and some part of you will want to reach a conclusion too.

Do not sit down. Keep wading. The moment you notice yourself having stopped moving — move.

If you find yourself staring at a gauge, look at your watch instead. If you find yourself listening to the roar, say a name. If you find yourself watching your reflection arrive late, jump, and watch it jump late, and laugh, because laughter is one of the things the Condensery does not contain.

## On the Water

The water will feel familiar. This is the most serious warning. It will feel like water you have been wet with before — in a dream, in a previous life, in some childhood bath you cannot quite place. This familiarity is not a trick and it is not a hallucination. The water has been cycling so long that it has acquired, in some sense that physics does not quite cover, the quality of being *everyone's water*. You have met it. So has everyone.

Do not drink it. Not because it is unsafe in any measurable way — it is probably the purest water you will ever encounter — but because drinking it is the beginning of being of the place, and you are not of the place, and should not become of the place on this visit.

Do not cry in it, either, if you can help it. If you must cry, cry into your sleeve. Your tears are one of the few things you are bringing that the Condensery does not already have. Keep them.

## Leaving

Leaving is the hardest part, and it is hard in a way you will not anticipate. Going up the shaft, the roar thinning behind you, you will feel something like grief — not for the Condensery, which does not care, but for the version of yourself that could have stayed and been dissolved into the amber. That self was real for a moment. Let it go. Climb.

At the top, before stepping out into wherever the shaft opens, stop. Listen. The roar is now only a pressure in your bones. Press your palm flat against the stone of the shaft wall. Feel how the stone is *not* the Condensery, even though it touches the Condensery. Stone can be next to something without becoming it. You can, too.

Step out.

## After

For a week afterward, you will find the ordinary world too loud in the wrong ways and too quiet in the wrong ways. Sounds will seem to have edges. You will startle at conversations. You will find yourself, at odd moments, listening for a roar that is not there, and being faintly disappointed, and then relieved, and then disappointed again.

This passes. Drink cold water with a strong taste. Walk where there are plants. Argue with someone you love. Let something grow — a windowsill herb, a friendship, a grievance, anything that *changes*. The antidote to the Condensery is not forgetting it. The antidote is participating, visibly and stubbornly, in the business of not being finished.

You visited a place that had concluded. You are not concluded. Act like it.
