# Preparing for the Reliquary Vault

## Understanding What You're Going To

Before anything else: understand that you are not going somewhere that wants visitors. You are not going somewhere that refuses visitors either. The Vault is indifferent in a way that is difficult to prepare for emotionally, because most places we visit — even wilderness, even ruins — retain some trace of relationship to humans who might enter them. The Vault does not. It is a completed sentence. You will be a footnote scribbled in the margin by someone who was not invited to read the book.

Accept this before arrival. Travelers who arrive expecting the place to *mean something at them* tend to leave damaged in small, persistent ways.

## Physical Preparation

**Hydration.** The air will take water from you constantly and without appearing to. Your lips will crack within minutes; your eyes will feel gritty within the hour. Arrive fully hydrated — drink more water in the twelve hours before departure than feels reasonable. Carry water, but understand that you may not want to drink it there. The Vault's dryness is so total, so *principled*, that introducing liquid feels like a small vandalism. Most travelers end up not drinking until they leave. Plan your visit short enough that this is survivable.

**Lip balm, nasal saline, eye drops.** Apply before entry. Do not bring scented varieties; the Vault has no smell, and anything you introduce will seem grotesque by contrast.

**Clothing.** Wear soft fabrics that do not rustle. You will be contributing to the sound of the room whether you want to or not; minimize your contribution. Cotton, worn soft. Nothing synthetic — the slight static-crackle is wrong here. Shoes with soft soles, though be aware: there is nowhere to stand that does not crush something. This is not a problem you can solve, only one you can accept.

**Ear protection: optional and contested.** The rasping is loud enough to genuinely disturb hearing over long exposure. Foam earplugs work. But many travelers who use them report that muffling the sound somehow makes the visit worse — reduces the room to its visual qualities, which are less complete without the audio, and creates a feeling of being walled off from something you came to experience. My intuition: bring plugs, but try going without first. Leave if the sound becomes physically intolerable; do not endure it past that point, as the effect compounds strangely.

## Psychological Preparation

**Practice sitting with completion.** In the week before your visit, spend time with finished things. A dried flower arrangement. A museum case of pinned insects. A closed book you have already read. Notice how these things do not reach toward you. Notice your tendency to reach toward them instead, to insist on relationship. The Vault will amplify this tendency until it becomes visible to you, and you will need to have met it already, in miniature, to recognize it.

**Do not bring grief.** This is the most important psychological warning I can give. The Vault's particular quality of *preserved dryness* has a way of catching at unresolved losses and holding them up in its even, shadowless light. Travelers who enter carrying recent bereavement, or old unworked grief, sometimes find that a part of that grief *stays there*, tucked into one of the niches, and cannot be retrieved. This sounds like it might be a kindness. It is not. The grief you leave behind does not stop being yours; it simply becomes inaccessible, and you will feel its absence as a dry patch in yourself for a long time after.

If you are grieving, postpone the visit. If you cannot identify whether you are grieving, postpone the visit and find out.

**Do not bring questions you want answered.** The room does not answer. It also does not refuse to answer. Questions brought into the Vault come out shaped differently — flattened, dried, no longer urgent. This can feel like resolution. It is not resolution. It is the question being added to the vocabulary of the room.

## What to Bring, What Not to Bring

**Do not bring anything living.** A flower in your lapel will feel obscene within seconds. A pet would be cruel. Even the gut-flora you carry with you will feel, briefly, like a violation — this will pass, but be ready for it.

**Do not bring anything you wish to remain yours.** Small objects have a tendency to suggest themselves into the empty niche, if you find it. You will feel, strongly, that you should leave something. Resist this, unless you have come specifically to leave something, in which case see the next section.

**Do bring a notebook and a soft pencil.** Not to record the Vault — you will not be able to, and trying will frustrate you — but to record your own state during and after. The Vault alters the texture of attention in ways that are difficult to recall later. Notes taken inside, however brief, are valuable.

**Do bring, if you wish, one small dry thing of your own.** If you feel called to make an offering — and some travelers do, strongly — let it be something already finished. A seed pod from your own world. A pressed leaf. Nothing that was alive recently. Nothing with moisture. Nothing sentimental enough that losing it would hurt; the room accepts offerings but does not thank you, and you must be fully willing to never see it again.

## The Question of the Empty Niche

You may find it. You may not. Most travelers who look for it do not find it; some who are not looking see it immediately. If you find it, understand what it is: the room's grammatical space for absence. It is not for you. It is not waiting for something to be placed in it. Placing something in it would be a category error — like writing a word in the space a language reserves for silence.

If you feel a powerful urge to put something of yourself into the empty niche — a hair, a fingernail, a whispered name — this is a known effect of prolonged exposure, and it is the signal to leave. The urge will pass within an hour of departure. Acting on it will not.

## Duration

Fifteen to forty minutes for a first visit. Less if you feel the air beginning to feel *correct* to you — this is a warning sign, not a milestone. The Vault's completeness is contagious in small doses and dangerous in large ones. Travelers who stay hours report a flattening of affect in the weeks after, a sense that their own unfinished lives are somehow *inferior* to the room's settled state. This passes but is unpleasant.

## Arrival and Departure

Since there is no door, your multiverse transit will deposit you inside and retrieve you from inside. Coordinate your return carefully before departure; the Vault is not a place you want to be stranded, not because it is dangerous but because it is *too safe*, too preserved, and extended exposure erodes the part of you that wants to leave.

Set a timer. Set two. The rasping masks the passage of time remarkably well, and the shadowless light provides no cue. Travelers routinely overstay their intended visits by factors of three or four, and report that it felt like minutes.

## Afterward

Drink water. Eat something moist — fruit is ideal. Speak aloud to someone, about anything; the sound of a voice in conversation is a specific medicine for what the Vault does to the auditory cortex.

Expect, for a few days, a slight strangeness around dried things in your own world. Herb jars. Old books. The rim of a wineglass left unwashed. You will notice them in a way you did not before. This fades.

Do not return for at least six months. The Vault rewards infrequent visits and punishes frequent ones, in ways that are difficult to articulate but easy to feel.

## Final Note

The Vault is one of the few places in the multiverse that can be described as *having nothing to offer you* without this being a criticism of the Vault or of you. It simply is what it is, loudly and brightly and forever, and you are going to visit it the way one might visit a great mountain — not because the mountain needs you, but because being in the presence of something so thoroughly itself is a particular kind of education that cannot be acquired any other way.

Go respectfully. Leave promptly. Carry water.
