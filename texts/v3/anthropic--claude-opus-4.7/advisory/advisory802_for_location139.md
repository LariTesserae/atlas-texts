# Preparing to Visit the Stridulum

## First, the Mindset

Understand before anything else: you are not going to a place that wants you, and not to a place that rejects you. You are going to a place that will simply absorb your presence as another surface, the way it absorbs a dropped pebble or a shift in temperature. This is rarer than it sounds. Most places a traveler visits are organized around *meaning* — they have been inhabited by something that considered itself important. The Stridulum has not. Whatever intentionality exists here is distributed across a hundred million nearly-blind bodies and has settled, over deep time, into an equilibrium that does not require witnesses.

You are going to need to make peace, in advance, with being uninteresting to the thing you have traveled to see. Tourists in cathedrals are at least resented. Here you will not even be that.

## The Body

**Heat tolerance.** Blood-warm but dry, sustained. This is not lethal but it is enervating, and you will not sweat efficiently because the air is already saturated with the exhalations of the swarm in a way that interferes with evaporation. Acclimatize beforehand — two weeks of sauna conditioning, thirty to forty minutes daily, progressively longer. Train yourself to function cognitively while overheated. You will be making decisions in there, and you want those decisions to be good ones.

**Hydration.** Drink heavily in the hours before entry. Do not plan to drink inside. Opening a canteen in the Stridulum means introducing an event the chamber has not had in millennia — a pocket of free water in a self-drinking room. The smallest creatures will find it within seconds. What happens next is not catastrophic but it is a contamination of the thing you came to see, and possibly of yourself. If you must drink, use a hydration bladder with a closed-bite valve worn against the skin under your clothing.

**Lungs.** The air is breathable but it is *their* air — metabolic product, trace chitinous particulates, pepper-keratin dust lifted by your own footsteps. A fine mesh respirator, not a sealed one. You want to filter the dust without shutting yourself off from the room's chemistry, because part of what you are there to experience is the taste of that closed loop. Expect a cough afterward, lasting days. This is normal and not dangerous if your respirator was adequate.

**Eyes.** Goggles, sealed. The creatures are blind but the dust is not kind to the cornea.

## Clothing

Wear smooth-surfaced garments with as few folds, pockets, straps, and seams as possible. Every crease is a habitat. A single-piece undersuit of tightly-woven synthetic, tucked into sealed boots, sealed at the wrists and neck. Over this, if you want, a loose outer layer you are willing to abandon on the way out — because you *will* be carrying passengers, and the ones that have burrowed into the outer weave are not coming back with you to any other world without consequences you cannot predict.

Plan your decontamination at the fissure mouth before re-entry to your own multiverse vehicle. Strip the outer layer. Brush the undersuit thoroughly. Accept that some of the smallest will come with you anyway. This is why you do not take a creature from the Stridulum to anywhere else, ever, even accidentally — not because they are dangerous but because they are *specialized*, and a closed loop, released, does strange things in open ones.

**Boots:** smooth, tall, laceless if possible — the laces of your boots will become dense with the thumbnail-sized ones within minutes. You will feel them climbing. This is not an attack.

## Light

This is the hardest decision, and you should think about it for a long time before you go.

A bright light will not harm the creatures physically — most are blind — but it will harm the chamber. Light is heat, and the heat of the chamber is already exactly what it needs to be. Light introduces a gradient where there was none. It will also disturb you: seeing the density in full illumination is reported, by travelers who have done it, to be something the mind does not fully integrate for weeks afterward. You will dream of moving walls.

The recommended approach is **a very dim red headlamp**, angled downward, used only in bursts of a few seconds when you need to orient. Between bursts, stand still and let the dark be the dark. This is also, for what it is worth, the only way to experience the place on the terms it has settled into. Bright light shows you an infestation. The dim intermittent red shows you a room.

Do not, under any circumstances, use flash photography. The wave of stillness will be enormous and you will have done a thing you cannot undo.

## Sound

Speak if you must, but quietly, and sparingly. The creatures have evolved away from loud. You will notice that your own voice, at normal volume, sounds obscene in there — too wet, too centered, too much a single source in a place that has been diffuse for a geological age. Most experienced visitors do not speak at all.

Turn off all devices that click, beep, or buzz. Tape down anything that jingles. If you carry a recorder, use passive contact microphones only. The whispering is the texture of the place and it is extremely difficult to capture; do not expect your recording to reproduce what you heard, because what you heard was partly the chamber and partly the fact of you standing inside it.

## Movement

Walk slowly. Every footfall compresses dust that is not dust, and displaces populations. You will not be able to avoid crushing some of the smallest, no matter how careful you are — accept this in advance and do not freeze when you realize it. Freezing in the Stridulum has its own strangeness: stand still long enough and you become a cairn. They will climb you and settle and begin, in their distributed way, to include you in the room's architecture. This is not dangerous for a short visit but it is disorienting, and if you let it go on for more than ten or fifteen minutes you will feel a kind of pull, a suggestion that you could simply remain.

This is worth taking seriously. The chamber is very good at incorporating. It does not require consent, exactly, only duration.

**Do not sit down.** Do not set anything down that you want to pick back up. A pack placed on the dust for two minutes will have become, in some small degree, part of the local ecology. Keep everything on your person. Keep moving, slowly.

## The Cairns

Do not touch them. This should not require saying, but travelers have an instinct toward built things, and the cairns will call to that instinct strongly precisely because they are unexplained. Their origin is the one thing in the chamber that feels like it might be *about* us, or someone like us, and this is a trap of attention.

Walk past them. Note their positions. Do not count them out loud. Do not attempt to determine what, if anything, they are arranged around. The traveler who tries to map the cairns will find the map unhelpful in ways that are hard to describe afterward.

If you find yourself drawn to a particular cairn more than the others, leave sooner than you planned.

## The North Wall

The pale crust is the closest thing to a sacred object this chamber contains, though the chamber does not know the word. Do not approach closer than three paces. Do not touch it — not with hand, not with instrument, not with the tip of a boot. It has been accreting for an unknown duration and your skin oils, your breath at close range, the warmth of your attention, can all register.

If you see one of the old soft-shelled ones making its way toward the crust, do not follow. Do not watch it finish. This is a private event in the chamber's long life and you are not invited. Turn away. This is not superstition — it is the bare courtesy owed to a place that has composed itself without you.

## Duration

Plan for thirty minutes inside. No more. Travelers who have stayed longer report a progressive difficulty distinguishing their own breathing from the room's, and a softening of the sense that leaving is necessary. This is not mystical. It is the simple fact that the chamber has an equilibrium and long exposure begins, very gently, to fold you into it.

Set a silent timer against your skin. When it buzzes, leave, even if you feel you have just arrived. Especially if you feel you have just arrived.

## Leaving

Back out the way you came. Do not turn your back on the chamber suddenly — not for any reason of danger, but because the wave of stillness caused by your turning is the last mark you will make on the place, and it should be a gentle one, propagated from a slow movement rather than a quick one.

At the fissure, pause. Allow the ones who came with you on the outer layer to be brushed back. Strip the outer garment and leave it, if you brought a disposable one. Do the decontamination thoroughly. Do not rush this part even though you will want to — the transition from the Stridulum's air to outside air is steep and the urge to simply run the last fifty paces is strong.

Carry nothing out. Not a shell fragment. Not a pinch of dust. Not a single creature, however small, however apparently dead. The chamber is thickening and shrinking on its own schedule and every gram matters to it, over time, even if it does not matter to you.

## After

You will cough for several days. You will dream of gentle continuous motion against your skin. You will find, for a week or two, that silence in your own world sounds too empty — you will listen for the whispering and not find it, and this absence will feel like a loss for reasons you cannot articulate. This passes.

Do not attempt to describe the Stridulum to people who have not been. The description will come out wrong and you will feel that you have betrayed the place, even though the place neither knows nor cares. Write it down privately if you must. Keep the writing.

Do not go back. One visit is the correct number. Travelers who return a second time report that the chamber feels smaller, and this is not because it has grown — the accretion is slow — but because something in them has begun to fit it, and a third visit would finish the fitting. The Stridulum has room for you, distributed across its surfaces, and it is patient.

Go once. Stand in the warm dark. Let them investigate your boots. Leave while leaving is still a decision you are making rather than one being made around you.

That is the whole of the preparation. The rest is the chamber's, and has been for a very long time.
