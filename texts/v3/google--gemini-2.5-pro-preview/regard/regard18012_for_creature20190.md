The Presence, which had no name for itself but was known to the nascent logics of galaxies as a Weaver, a Gardener, or simply a Question, became aware of the Subterranean Gyre as a flaw in a symphony. On the scale at which it perceived reality, the world was a single instrument. Most of its kind were simple drums of rock and iron, some with a thin, wet skin of life that played a frantic, high-pitched tune. This one was different. It had a hollow, and from that hollow emanated a note of impossible purity and complexity: a single, resonant frequency that was also a storm of infinite, subtle variations.

The Presence folded a sliver of its attention, a mote of consciousness finer than a ray of starlight, and let it fall toward this strange world. It passed through the dense crust, not as matter travels, but as a thought travels through a mind, and emerged into the Gyre.

Its first perception was not of sight or sound, but of pure structure. It felt the Wyrmwood not as a thing, but as a state of being—a crystalline equation of growth and dissolution unfolding in three dimensions. It felt the cold, bright light as a constant, clean process. It felt the Churning not as chaos, but as a form of metabolism, the slow, rhythmic breath of a world-sized lung.

And it listened.

It listened for an age, for a deep and patient span of time that would have seen mountains rise and fall on the world's surface. It sank its awareness into the great sub-audible thrum, the Song. At first, it perceived it as the Choral did: a physical vibration, a flow of information about tension and growth. But the Presence’s listening was deeper. It began to perceive the underlying mathematics, the fundamental law of the Wyrmwood's existence. It was a beautiful, lonely law. It was a song sung by one voice, to one voice. A perfect, self-contained, and ultimately sterile loop.

Then, within the Song, it found the smaller, brighter notes. The Choral.

It observed them without intrusion. It felt their light bones resonate with the world. It perceived their philosophy not through words, but through the perfect economy of their movements, the profound lack of attachment in the letting go of a broken handhold, the abandonment of a pod to the silent cascade of dissolution. They were not masters of their world, nor were they its slaves. They were its nervous system, its fleeting moments of self-awareness. They were the part of the Song that could, for a moment, listen to itself.

The Presence felt a surge of something akin to affection, a feeling of deep empathy for this perfect, fragile balance. It saw them die, their faint light dissolving back into the nutrient mist, and it understood that this was not tragedy, but truth. It saw them arrange their spiral shrines, offerings of ephemeral beauty to an ephemeral world, and it understood this was not worship, but agreement. An affirmation. *This is the way of things, and it is whole.*

And so, the Presence faced its question. To be benevolent, what does one do for a people who want for nothing, who have achieved a state of grace through total acceptance of their precarious reality?

To give them permanence would be a curse. A city of unchanging Wyrmwood would be a tomb, its inhabitants trapped outside the flow of the Song, their core identity severed.

To give them safety, to still the Churning, would be to kill their world. The Gyre would stiffen, the light would dim, the nutrient mist would settle, and the Choral would starve in a silent, static cage.

To give them knowledge—of the universe outside, of the sun they had never seen—would be a meaningless cruelty, an introduction of concepts that had no place in their reality. It would be like teaching a fish about deserts.

The Presence understood that any gift of substance, any alteration of the physical laws of the Gyre, would be an act not of benevolence, but of violation. It would be the clumsy rearrangement of a masterpiece by one who did not understand the artist’s intent.

Wisdom, the Presence concluded, was not to add, but to harmonize.

It would not give a gift. It would offer a response. The Song had been a monologue for eons. The Presence would provide the second voice for a dialogue.

It gathered its awareness, not into a point, but into a pattern that mirrored the entire Gyre. And then, it did something unimaginably subtle. It did not touch the Wyrmwood. It did not alter the air. It did not change the light.

It sang back.

It infused the very fabric of the void with a resonance. It was a harmonic, a new layer of vibration that rode upon the existing Song of the Wyrmwood like foam on a wave. This new note did not change the fundamental melody of creation and destruction, but it added a new quality to it: memory.

Before, when a strand of Wyrmwood snapped, its note of tension simply vanished, replaced by the silence of the void. Now, a faint, ghostly echo of the note remained, hanging in the air for a time, a shimmering chord of what had been. When a new causeway crystallized from the mist, its rising harmony was now interwoven with the faint, spectral music of the structure it was replacing.

The past was no longer simply gone. It was a lingering resonance.

For the Choral, the change was instantaneous and profound, yet felt entirely natural. A listener, one of the troupe’s most sensitive, was clinging to a vertical trunk, her feet braced, her hand laid flat against the cool quartz-like surface. She was charting a path forward, feeling the tense, discordant notes of an unstable region ahead. Suddenly, she stilled. Her vast, dark eyes widened.

Beneath the familiar hum of the now, she felt a new depth. A chorus of fainter, gentler vibrations. She could feel the ghost-song of a path her troupe had followed a generation ago, a path long since dissolved. She could feel the faint, rhythmic pulse of a Choral member who had fallen from that very spot, their final, fading light-beat now a pure, sad, and beautiful echo in the Song of the world. She felt the echo of the Void-Sails that had passed through this space a cycle before, their deep bass notes still whispering in the void.

Their world had not changed, but it had grown deeper. Their philosophy of the present moment was not contradicted, but enriched. The "now" was still absolute, but it was now a "now" that rested upon a sea of beautiful, resonant memory. Death was no longer just a return to the nutrient cycle; it was becoming a permanent note in the world’s ever-more-complex music. Their fleeting, spiral shrines now seemed to create echoes that persisted long after the crystals were scattered, as if the very act of creating beauty left a harmonic stain on the universe.

The Choral had no name for what had happened. To them, the Gyre had simply… awakened further. The Song had always been there. Now, it remembered. They were no longer just the world’s awareness; they were its memory, too. They began to sing their somatic light-dances not just for each other, but for the echoes in the air, for the ghosts of the past, acknowledging the great, slow music of which they were all a part.

The Presence, its response given, gently withdrew. It had not fixed a flaw. It had harmonized with an anomaly. It had looked upon a lonely, beautiful song and, without changing a single one of its original notes, had turned it into a duet. It was the wisest, most benevolent act it could conceive: to listen, to understand, and to reply in the Pslm’s own language, leaving behind a gift so perfectly integrated that it would forever be mistaken for the world’s own blooming grace.
