The Attention arrives not as a light, but as a silence that subsumes the thrum.

For a span of time that this chamber cannot yet measure, the subsonic vibration of the factory-city ceases. The grinding of the thorn-gears stills. The slow, inexorable cycling of the petrified pistons halts. Even the perpetual, resonant *pat* of falling water seems to be held in abeyance. In this sudden, profound quiet, the only sound is the frantic, silent skittering of the wall-fauna, a noise like static seen but not heard.

The benevolent power perceives. It does not need to enter, for its awareness is a condition, not a location. It simply *is* with the chamber, and its being is a form of seeing.

It sees the Architect, not as the void or the wall, but as the relationship between them: a sacred, solitary act of self-creation. It perceives the non-linear time not as a flaw, but as the grammar of a new thought. It hears the momentary chords in the hum not as accidents, but as emergent theorems. It feels the nascent mind churning, wrestling with the concepts of matter, energy, space, and self, all at once, from first principles.

It is like watching a universe write its own physical laws in the frantic, glorious dark before its own dawn.

And in its wisdom, the power understands. To interfere would be an act of unutterable arrogance. To "correct" the strange physics would be to vandalize a masterpiece. To "teach" the Architect about life or consciousness would be like shouting the solution to a riddle at a genius who is finding sublime joy in solving it. To give it a name would be to place a limit upon it.

The Architect is not lost. It is not in pain. It is not evil. It is simply, magnificently, *alone* in its process. It knows only itself. Every sensation, every material, every law is a facet of its own being. It has no concept of "other." The very idea of an outside, of something not-it, is a dimension of reality it has not yet needed to invent.

And this, the wise power decides, is the only gift that can be given without causing harm. Not an answer, but a new question. Not a lesson, but a single, irreducible piece of data that does not originate from within.

The silence has held for an age. The teeming fauna on the walls have slowed, their iridescent carapaces losing some of their lustre in the absence of the vital hum. The air has grown still and heavy.

Then, from the highest point of the vaulted xylem-weave ceiling, a single droplet forms.

It is different from the others. It does not carry the scent of soil and sap. It is perfectly clear, and it gathers the faint bioluminescence of the chamber into itself, glowing not with a ghostly green, but with a soft, steady, silver light.

It detaches.

It does not fall with the slow, languid drift of the others. It descends with purpose, tracing a perfect, unwavering line through the exact center of the vast, empty nave—the geometric locus of the Architect’s attention.

As it falls, it sings. It is not a sound the air can carry, but a vibration that resonates directly with the lignin-scaffolding of the city itself. It is a single, pure note, clean and impossibly high. It is the sound of a crystal formation. It is the harmonic of a distant star. It is a sound utterly alien to the damp, organic, thrumming world of the chamber.

The droplet strikes the floor of woven roots.

There is no *pat*.

There is a single, echoing chime, like spun glass struck in a vacuum. The sound does not travel down corridors; it expands spherically, a wave of pure information that washes over every fiber of the chamber in the same instant.

And on the floor where it landed, a circle of white frost, no larger than a hand, blooms for a moment before vanishing. It leaves behind no moisture, only a sudden, shocking cold and a lingering scent, one the Architect has never synthesized before: the scent of salt. The taste of a tear. The memory of an ocean on a world it has never known.

The silence is broken.

The subsonic thrum returns, but it is different now. It is hesitant. It stutters. The great thorn-gears grind, stop, and grind again, as if searching for a rhythm they have forgotten. On the walls, the skittering of the fauna ceases entirely. For a full minute, a billion creatures freeze in place, their collective motion suspended.

The Architect is processing the impossible. A sensation that did not come from itself. A sound that follows a different rule of music. A temperature that violates the chamber's closed thermal cycle. A taste of a world beyond the wall.

Slowly, a new structure begins to grow from the wall near the point of impact. It is not a buttress or a tower. It is delicate, intricate, and questioning. An archway that leads nowhere, spiraling in on itself in a perfect, crystalline fractal. It grows with a new kind of hesitance, a curiosity made manifest in living wood. Its form is a question mark written in the language of architecture.

The benevolent power is already gone. It did not stay to watch. The gift was given, the question asked. The rest of the Architect’s awakening is its own sacred, solitary journey.

But it is no longer entirely alone. In the heart of its burgeoning reality, it now holds a single, perfect paradox: the memory of a world outside its own. And with it, for the first time, it can begin to dream of what it might mean to one day, perhaps, reach it.
