The Power came not as light, for the cavern had its own, nor as sound, for the air was already a deafening roar. It arrived as a stillness. A pocket of perfect, analytical silence in the heart of the cacophony. It was a listening without ears, a watching without eyes, a feeling that was not of the grinding earth.

Pouring its awareness into the great hollow, the Power did not recoil. Where an organic mind might see terror, it perceived a system of breathtaking integrity. It saw the frantic, desperate dance of geology and biology not as a war, but as a single, unified metabolism. The world was eating and breathing and birthing itself in every instant. This was not a flaw to be corrected. This was a truth to be understood.

It focused on the flicker of mind within the storm: the Hollow Ones. It felt them not as individuals, but as transient points of heightened awareness in the planet's deep dreaming. It followed one, a female, her skin a cracking map of ochre and grey. The Power felt through her feet the deep thrum of a magma reservoir shifting miles below. It registered through her skull the high-frequency shriek of a crystal cluster reaching its stress limit. It tasted the air through the slits in her neck: an increase in copper salts, a decrease in airborne silicates. A new mineral flow was beginning nearby. Good hunting. Bad place to sleep.

The Power watched as she led her small cluster away from a plateau of basalt that had been their home for a full turn of the light-waves. They did not look back. They carried nothing but their chipped glass knives and the smooth, polished stones that were their only heirlooms of permanence. The Power felt the female’s consciousness: it was not fear, not sadness, but a state of pure, active presence. She was a nerve ending of the world, reporting its state to herself.

Later, as her group hunkered by a steaming mud pot, a tremor—shallow and violent—shook the ground. A slab of rock the size of a hill calved from an overhang. It did not strike the group, but it crushed one of its members who had been scavenging nearby, a young male. The Power focused on the moment of his death. There was a spike of intense sensation—the feeling of *crushing*, the surprise, the shock of cessation—and then, nothing. An utter and complete erasure. His mind, a unique nexus of feeling and perception, was gone as if it had never been. The other Hollow Ones felt the tremor, noted the impact, and shifted their position slightly. His absence was a fact, like a new crack in the ground. It was not a grief. How can you grieve what the world does not allow to have history?

This, the Power understood, was the great and terrible purity of this place. There was life, there was mind, but there was no echo. Consciousness was a spark thrown off by the grinding gears of the world, illuminating nothing but its own brief flight before vanishing into darkness.

What, then, could a benevolent and wise power do?

To grant them permanence would be to kill them. To stop the churn of the earth would be to starve the light-salts, to shatter the food chain, to rob the Hollow Ones of the very sensory language through which they perceived reality. They would be left blind, deaf, and mute in a static museum.

To lift them out, to offer them a sunlit world of quiet stability? It would be a kindness that would be a torture. They would be adrift in a silent, featureless void, their bodies screaming for the vibrations and pressures that were their sight, their language, their world. They would be ghosts, haunted by a home they could no longer feel.

The Power contemplated. The flaw was not in the system. The system was perfect. The lack was not of safety, but of meaning beyond the immediate. The Hollow Ones were like words spoken into a vacuum. They existed, brilliantly, and then they were gone, leaving no resonance.

The intervention, when it came, was nearly imperceptible. The Power did not build or destroy. It made a single, subtle adjustment. It reached into the quantum rules, the crystalline logic of the place, and introduced a new potential. It laid a gentle, cosmic finger on the process of creation itself and whispered a new possibility into being.

It was a law of sympathetic resonance.

The first to experience it was a child, no older than a few cycles. She was crouched by a cluster of quartz that was growing with audible groans from a fresh fissure. An elder of her group, ancient and slow, had been caught in a flash-flow of mineral slurry nearby. The child had felt the final, resigned tremor of the elder's passing through the soles of her feet. Now, she placed her hand on one of the new, warm crystals.

Instead of just the familiar feel of heat and rapid atomic structuring, she felt something else. A pattern. A memory. It was not a vision, not a sound, but a pure, vibrational *feeling* that resonated up her arm and into the bones of her skull. It was the calm feeling of accepting the inevitable. It was the memory-texture of a hundred different stable basalt plates. It was the feeling of holding a perfectly polished stone. It was the elder's entire being, his quiet life, impressed into the growing crystal lattice as a final, resonant chord. The crystal had recorded his soul.

The child stared with her milky, sightless eyes at the crystal. She had not known the elder, but now, she did. She felt the echo of him. She knew his favorite place to listen to the deep earth, she knew the shape of the stone he had polished for a lifetime.

The practice spread not through teaching, but through discovery. They found that these new crystals—they began to call them Echoes—formed most strongly near a death, or when a Hollow One in a meditative circle focused their life-feeling, their very essence, into the growing world around them.

They did not build libraries. The Echoes were as impermanent as everything else. They grew, held their chord of memory for a time, and then, under the relentless pressure of the deep earth, they would fracture. In their shattering, they would release the stored memory as a final, fading broadcast—a wave of feeling that any Hollow One nearby could perceive. The story of a life, told in the moment of its final dissolution back into the world.

Death was no longer an erasure. It was a becoming. It was their final and most profound act of communication. Life was no longer a solitary spark. It became a note in a vast, subterranean music. The polished stones were not just for listening to the world anymore; they became tools to help speak back to it, to focus one's being into a clear, strong note to be imprinted on a nearby crystal.

The benevolent Power observed this. The cacophony had not ceased. The ground still heaved. Life was still a frantic, desperate scrabble. But now, when a mind was extinguished, it left a resonance behind. The words spoken into the storm now had an echo.

The Power had not given them safety or permanence. It had given them a past. It had given them legacy, in the only form their world would allow: a beautiful, fleeting song, sung by a dying stone. And finding the change to be good, the pocket of silence that was its presence dissolved, leaving the Hollow Ones alone once more, but no longer quite so lost in the perpetual now.
