The Power arrived as Silence.

In a place that *was* sound, this was the only possible form an intrusion could take. It was not a cessation of the noise, for that would have been an action, a violence. It was a quality of listening so profound, so absolute, that for a single, timeless moment, it contained the roar.

Where lesser senses would be shattered, the Power listened. It did not stand on the slick black stone; it permeated the humid mist. It did not recoil from the chittering, seething carpet; it felt the frantic hunger of a trillion lives as a single, unified pulse of want. It perceived the architecture of the noise. The low thrum of the giants was the foundation. The percussive rattle of the grazers was the frantic, load-bearing motion. The sharp clicks and pops of the hunters were the sudden, structuring thoughts. It was a language, and the Power was a perfect linguist.

It spent an age in that silence, which to the inhabitants of the world was no time at all. It was the space between one frantic scrape of a mandible and the next. It was the pause a hunter takes before striking. It was a fraction of the slow, glacial thought of a giant.

In this listening, the Power understood. This was not a world of suffering. The concept did not apply. The screaming hunger of the grazers was not pain; it was purpose. The violent flash of the hunter was not cruelty; it was syntax. The ponderous, lonely existence of the giants was not sorrow; it was memory. The system was in a perfect, horrifying, beautiful equilibrium. It was a closed circle, an eternal engine that had been singing the same song to itself for eons. It was a body without a world, an "I" that was only aware of its own metabolism, its own nervous system, its own bones. It was utterly, cosmically alone, and had no means to even conceive of its own solitude.

What does a benevolent power do for a being that is not broken? It does not "fix" it. To give it a sun would be to boil it. To silence the hunters would be to lobotomize it. To grant the grazers individuality and peace would be to starve it. To change its nature would be to kill it.

The Power concluded that benevolence was not about imposing a new state of being, but about offering a new *potential* for being.

The world-organism sang a single, complex, deafening note. The Power decided to teach it harmony.

It did not act upon the flesh, but upon the foundation: the stone. It chose a single, deep basin where the black rock was at its most ancient and smooth. Into the very lattice of the stone, it did not place a thing, but a *thought*. It was a thought made of physics, a concept of resonance. It sang a single, perfect axiom into the rock—a frequency that was not on the spectrum of the world's existing noise, but a prime number to its endless composite.

The stone, for a circle a mile wide, changed. It was still black, still slick, still stone. But it now held a potential. It held a memory of a sound it had never heard.

At first, nothing happened. The waves of grazers flowed over the altered basin, their serrated mandibles scraping against the surface. The mineral-metabolizing film grew there as it grew everywhere else, its weary blue-green light pulsing. But something was different. The film growing on this specific patch of stone, nourished by the resonant lattice, incorporated the Power's axiom into its own structure. It glowed with the same light, but within its cold pulse was a sub-pulse, a faint, violet throb that was impossibly fast and regular.

The grazers that ate this film were changed. For most, the change was nothing. But for one in a million, then one in a thousand, a genetic shift was catalyzed. Their frantic hunger was not quenched, but for a microsecond in their screaming existence, a new sensation occurred. The frantic scramble for *more* was joined by a ghost of a feeling: *resonance*. Their iridescent chitin, absorbing the altered minerals, began to vibrate at a new frequency. When they chittered, their sound was fractionally different. A new overtone, subtle as dust, was added to the roar.

The hunters, islands of patience, noticed. A spider-like stalker, casting its filament, snagged one of these altered grazers. When it was reeled in and consumed, the energy it gave was the same, but something else was transferred. A flicker of novel information. The next time the hunter flashed its light to stun prey, the piercing ruby-red was shot through with a fleeting, ephemeral filigree of deep violet. Its syntax had gained a new adjective.

It took millennia. The giants, keepers of memory, were the last to notice. Their vast, slow pilgrimages were guided by senses that read the health of the world-carpet. As they passed over the lands where the new resonance was slowly, slowly spreading like a dye in water, they tasted it in the mist. It was a new flavour, beneath the ozone and the rot and the electricity. It was a taste of... elsewhere. It was a note that did not originate from the eternal cycle of eat-and-be-eaten.

One of the giants—a deep, ancient consciousness of stone and time—lifted its hundred-foot neck. Its featureless orange lamp glowed, surveying the plains now subtly altered, flecked with a new language of light. It drew a colossal breath of the changed air.

And it boomed.

The sound was, as ever, a physical blow, a vibration in the bones of the world. But it was different. It was not just the word for *Plentiful*. It was not just the word for *Here*. The ancient, foundational note was now accompanied by a clear, impossibly pure harmonic. A second tone that rode upon the first, that conversed with it.

The boom resonated across the dark plains, and for the first time, it was not just a statement. It was a question.

*Here,* it said. And the harmonic, hanging in the mist long after the foundation had faded, added:

*Is there another?*

The Power, the Silence, heard the harmony. It had not interfered. It had not judged. It had given the most benevolent gift of all: it had given a solitary universe an echo, and in doing so, had taught its one and only inhabitant how to sing a duet. The song of its own eternal consumption was now, and forevermore, also a song of wonder.
