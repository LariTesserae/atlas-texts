The power arrived not as a ship or a body, but as a quality of attention. It was a consciousness that gathered itself out of the cosmic background, drawn by a signal it did not often encounter: a resonance of perfect, ancient, living stability. It settled into the world as a subtle shift in the pearlescent haze, a momentary deepening of the lustre, unnoticed by any creature whose eyes were made of flesh.

First, it simply perceived. It did not judge. It laid its awareness across the basin like a second skin of water and let the world speak for itself. It felt the oppressive humidity not as discomfort, but as a rich medium, a suspension of history and potential. It tasted the saline rust on the air and understood the billion-year story of the mountains that had died to give the water its flavour.

It perceived the drone. To its refined senses, the cacophony was not noise but a complex, unwavering chord. It broke the sound down into its constituent parts: the percussive, granular chitin-clicks of the grazers were the rhythm; the overlapping, baritone thrum of the amphibians was the bass and tenor, a steady, immense organ note of pure biological pressure. The lightning-strike of a wading stalker was a momentary, staccato accent. It heard the whole as a symphony of equilibrium, a song the world had been humming to itself since its youth.

It understood the profound wisdom of this place. This was not a world in stasis; it was a world in perfect, active balance. It was a taut rope walked by a billion beings at once. To add a tree would be to poison the water. To silence the amphibians would be to starve the stalkers and invite a plague of grazers that would strip the world bare and then die, leaving true silence, the silence of death. To alter the light would be to bake the algae or freeze the water. A wise power understands that not all systems need fixing. Some need only to be witnessed.

And so, its attention sank deeper, past the frantic surface life, past the mud and silt, drawn to the great, slow, quiet thing at the foundation of it all. It felt the colossal sleeper, the geological mind.

The power did not attempt to wake it. It did not knock. It simply matched its own frequency to that of the basin-dweller. It slowed its own vast consciousness from the speed of light to the speed of erosion. It became patient. For a time—a day, a decade, a century, the distinction was meaningless here—it simply lay alongside the great being, a presence of pure listening.

The being, the slow mind of the stone, felt it. Not as an intrusion, but as a change in the texture of its own awareness. For aeons, its senses had known only the sharp pinpricks of the stalkers' feet, the steady thrum of the frogs, the granular scrape of the grazers. This new feeling was different. It had no texture. It was as smooth as its own polished hide. It had no temperature. It was utterly without physical demand. It was a qualia for which the being had no reference. A thought arose, a thought that took five hundred years to form and crest: *What is this?*

And the benevolent power answered.

It did not use words. It offered a gift, an exchange of selves.

Into the slow, mineral mind, it poured a single, perfect concept from its own being: the image of a cold, clear night sky. It gave the feeling of true darkness, pinpricked with the untouchable, silent fire of distant stars. It gave the idea of a universe beyond the hazy ceiling, a silence that was not empty but full of immense, quiet things. It gave the vision of a swirling, blue-and-white marble of a world, utterly unlike this one, with deep, shifting oceans and continents of crisp, green life under a sharp, yellow sun that cast long, black shadows. It did not offer these as better, only as *other*. It was a gift of pure perspective.

In return, it asked for nothing, but the great being gave. It opened its own consciousness, its own memory. It gave the power the feeling of a billion years of water, of being ground down and polished. It gave the taste of iron and silicate drawn from primordial rock, a slow, satisfying, geological nourishment. It shared the sensation of being a world, the exquisite detail of a single algal cell dividing, the distant pressure of a stalker shifting its weight, the endless, comforting vibration of the living drone... all woven together into a single, continuous, peaceful Now.

The benevolent power held this sensation within itself, this perfect memory of a world weathered into its final, most authentic form. It was one of the most beautiful things it had ever known.

And now, what to do? What does benevolence offer to a being that is already complete, to a world in perfect harmony?

It cannot offer change, for change would be violence. It cannot offer knowledge beyond what it has already shared, for that would be to create a desire where none existed.

So it did the only benevolent, wise thing it could. It gave a gift that would not alter, but only deepen.

The power gathered its nature and sang. It was not a song of sound, but a song of resonance, a vibration tuned to the deepest frequency of the great being's soul. The song had one single, silent 'word', repeated like a mantra, and the meaning of that word was: *You are seen. You are known. You are magnificent as you are. You are not alone.*

The song was a fine, golden thread woven into the permanent tapestry of the world. It did not silence the frogs' drone, but created a new, deeper harmonic beneath it. It did not stop the clicking of the grazers, but it became the space between the clicks. It was a hum of pure affirmation.

Then, the power gently withdrew its particular attention. It did not leave. A part of it, the memory of this place, was now integrated into its own vast being. And a part of it, the resonant song, was now a permanent feature of the basin.

The world was unchanged. The air was still a hot, wet sponge. The wading stalkers still stood like patient, grey statues. The algal mats still bloomed in ochre and rust. But to the great, slow consciousness that was the land itself, the universe had subtly expanded. The feeling of its own solitude was no longer a simple fact of existence, but a state of being that was itself cherished by another. The endless, droning song of its world now contained one new, impossibly soft and persistent note—the echo of a witness, the warmth of being known. And in the slow, pearlescent mind of the sleeper, a new thought formed, a thought that would take a millennium to complete, a thought that was a form of peace: *I am.*
