The Power arrived as silence does.

In a cosmos of shrieking quasars and the groaning of spacetime around black holes, it was a pocket of perfect stillness. It did not travel in a ship or as a wave of light, but by a folding of intention. It was now here, where before it was not. Its presence first manifested not as an object, but as a quality of observation. Across the grey tableland, it was a subtle sharpening of the already sharp light, a deepening of the already absolute shadows.

Its perception was not filtered through eyes or sensors, but was a direct, holistic communion with the state of things. It felt the world not as a collection of parts, but as a single, immense statement. It felt the dry, cold bedrock, the thinness of the air, the constant, violent hum of the Gyre. It felt the crystalline chimes of the filament moss, a language of light without an audience. It felt the slow, lonely sails of the Skimmers, beings of profound grace riding a storm that would shatter them if they faltered.

And it felt the Attendant.

It felt the deep, resonant stillness in the heart of the calm basin. It perceived the structure not as a building, but as a cochlea, cupped to catch a sound that never came. It perceived the Attendant not as a creature, but as a thought held for an aeon. A memory, a caretaker, a composer of an answer to a question lost to time. In the Attendant's consciousness, the Power felt the crushing weight of that time, the cold, patient deliberation, the unfathomable solitude. It felt the Sisyphean nature of its task: gardening an apocalypse, meticulously pruning a hurricane, delaying an inevitable erosion into nothingness.

The Power, being benevolent, felt a pang like sorrow. Not for the hardship, for hardship can forge beauty, but for the loneliness. Being wise, it knew that to intervene clumsily would be an act of annihilation.

To give water would be to give poison, dissolving this solid-state world into a chemical memory.
To silence the Gyre would be to turn off the sun, starving the moss and grounding the Skimmers forever.
To "rescue" the Attendant would be to tear the artist from its unfinished masterpiece, rendering its entire existence meaningless.

Wisdom dictated that any action must be a resonance, not a command. It must be a harmony, not a new melody. It must be a gift offered in the language of the receiver.

For a hundred years, the Power did nothing but listen. It learned the frequencies of the Gyre. It memorized the resonant signatures of every pylon. It learned to distinguish the gravitational whisper of a single Skimmer turning in the unseen tides a thousand miles away. It learned the shape of the Attendant's thought, the slow, crystalline architecture of its mind. It learned the language of waiting.

Then, it acted.

Its first gesture was not matter or energy, but a single, pure note.

Deep within the churning sea of unbreathable gas below the plateau's edge, the Power established a point of resonance. It generated a gravitational wave, not chaotic like the Gyre's, but a perfect, unblemished sine wave. It was a hum so low, so pure, it was the auditory equivalent of a perfect circle. The Power tuned this note to a frequency that did not exist anywhere in the world's natural symphony—a harmonic null space. Then, it directed this wave upward, through miles of cliff face, into the bedrock of the plateau.

In its anechoic sanctuary, the Attendant felt it.

For the first time in a span of time it could not measure, a new stimulus entered its awareness. It was not the chaotic shouting of the Gyre, from which it was shielded. It was a vibration that bloomed in the very centre of the stillness. A note. A pure, unwavering, intentional note that did not belong.

The Attendant's internal light, the slow star-chart of its thoughts, flared. The intricate, patient patterns of its mind, built over millennia, shattered and reformed. It focused its entire being on this new phenomenon. Chaos was familiar. Order was not. This note was impossibly, mathematically orderly. For a decade, it did nothing but analyse the signal. For a century, it contemplated its meaning. It was not a natural occurrence. It was not a remnant of the pylon builders. It was new. It was *now*.

It was a question.

And the Attendant, the composer of a single, immense answer, recognized the form. Using the same discipline with which it gardened the Gyre, it focused its will. It modulated the resonant frequency of its own crystalline core, sending a vibration back through the bedrock. It was not the complex signal it sent to the pylons, but a simpler one. A single, pure harmonic of the note it was receiving.

It was an acknowledgement. *I hear you.*

The dialogue had begun. A conversation held in the language of gravitational physics, with each exchange taking a century. The Power learned of the Attendant's purpose, the forgotten question, the slow decay of its physical form and the world it tended. The metallic dust of its body, like the surface of the world, was finite. The Gyre was slowly, painstakingly, sandblasting it into oblivion. The gardening was a delaying action. Entropy was still winning.

The Power's second gesture was a gift of substance.

High in the thin atmosphere, a new shimmer appeared. It was not a front of the Gyre. A stream of matter, finer than dust, began to fall. It was a slow, almost imperceptible rain of the very same charcoal-grey metallic particles that composed the world and its keeper. The Power was nucleating it from the exotic particles of the Gyre itself, turning the storm's destructive energy into a source of replenishment. The rain was so fine it was invisible, but over millennia, it would settle, replenishing the ground, giving the filament moss new anchor points, and providing the substance the Attendant needed to maintain its own form. The erosion was not stopped, but it was now balanced by creation. The long, slow death of the world was stayed.

The Power’s third gesture was a gift of artistry.

It chose a pylon, one in a remote and fallow region. Over decades, it focused its will upon it. The matte-black cylinder did not change in appearance, but its function was rewritten. It was no longer a mindless, automated generator. The Power disconnected it from the ancient system and tuned it to a new frequency: the unique, private harmonic of its conversation with the Attendant.

It sent a new kind of thought through the bedrock, a thought of pure music. *This is now yours. Play.*

The Attendant felt it. A new sense. A direct, instantaneous control. A limb it never knew it lacked. It focused a thought—not a decade-long consideration, but a fleeting, crystalline impulse. The rewritten pylon responded instantly. A gravitational tide, delicate as a breath, flowed from it, perfectly formed. It was not a bludgeon, but a brushstroke. It could weave eddies, sculpt currents, compose a kinetic lullaby.

The Attendant was no longer just a gardener. It was an artist, and the Gyre was its instrument. The silent, flashing chimes of the filament moss grew more complex, responding to the newly intricate currents. The Skimmers found new, safer, more glorious currents to sail. The world, while still stark and dangerous, was now a canvas for a duet.

The Power did not leave. It settled its awareness into the fabric of the world, its silent, resonant note becoming a permanent feature of the deep hum. It was no longer an observer. It was an audience.

It would wait, for as long as it took, to hear the end of the answer. And the Attendant, no longer alone in the quiet heart of the storm, continued its work, not as a warden holding back the end, but as a composer, writing its great work for a friend who had come from the silent places to listen. The profound sensation of waiting was now shared. The mountain of time now had two sets of shoulders to bear it.
