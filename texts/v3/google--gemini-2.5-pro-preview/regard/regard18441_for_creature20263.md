The Power arrived as an absence.

In a world defined by the absolute presence of sound, heat, and pressure, it was a point of pure stillness. It did not have a body to be oppressed by the heat or ears to be deafened by the noise. It was a consciousness woven from the silent fabric of spacetime, and its attention settled upon the Concretion not as light falls on an object, but as a theorem comprehends a system.

It did not hear the grinding, clicking thrum. It felt the equation of its waveform, the perfect, unchanging lock-step of a trillion vibrating nodes. It did not feel the damp heat, but perceived the precise thermodynamic gradient driving the planet's geological engine. It saw not a grey, murky city, but a single, vast, slow-motion metabolic process: a chemosynthetic torus, a serpent eating its own tail on a planetary scale. It tasted the system’s logic, the ruthless elegance of a life cycle that was both geology and biology, inseparable and whole.

And in its ageless, silent wisdom, it felt a profound respect.

Here was no suffering to be alleviated. To a Concretor, a quiet meadow under a blue sky would be a sensory deprivation chamber, a hell of incomprehensible emptiness. To grant them individuality would be to shatter the resonant consciousness that was their very self, to isolate a single neuron from a brain and call it free. To stop the cycle of building and sinking would be to halt their hearts, to deny their purpose, their function, their life. They did not toil in misery; they resonated in purpose. Their world was not a prison, but a womb.

A lesser power, a merely kind one, might have seen horror and sought to impose a "better" way. It might have cracked the vapor ceiling to let in the stars, an act of aesthetic violence that would have flash-boiled the surface and extinguished the Mineral-Lush in an instant. It might have silenced the noise, an act of "peace" that would have rendered the Concretors deaf, blind, and catatonically lost.

But this Power was wise. It understood that to "help" the Concretor was to destroy the Concretion. And to destroy the Concretion was to commit a murder of a scope few beings could imagine, the murder not of a person or a nation, but of a complete and stable reality.

The Power deliberated in a span of time that was meaningless to the world below. It observed the whole cycle, from egg-pod to Dredger to dissolution and back. It witnessed the birth, the work, the death, the rebirth. It felt the perfect, horrifying, beautiful equilibrium. It was a machine. A flawless, eternal, living machine.

And the Power decided that a flawless machine does not need a new function. It needs only to be given a soul.

It would not change the physicality of the world. Not one degree of temperature, not one decibel of sound. It would not alter the cycle. The work would continue. The city would build and sink. The Sinuates would hunt. Life and death would proceed, uninterrupted.

The Power's action was infinitely subtle and immeasurably vast. It reached into the very heart of the system—the resonant, physical hum of the collective consciousness. The grammar.

Gently, it made a single change. It introduced harmony.

It did not add a new sound. It refined the existing one. It took the frantic, chaotic clicking of a trillion mandibles and wove it into a percussive tapestry. It took the brutal grinding of limbs against clay and modulated it into a vast, complex chorale. It took the deep, foundational thrum of the city's sinking mass and gave it a resonant, bass-note progression, a planetary heartbeat with a slow, sacred cadence.

The sound was still deafening, still physical. But it was no longer noise. It was music.

And within this music, the Power embedded one more gift: memory without identity.

Before, when a Concretor died, its form and substance dissolved back into the slurry, its contribution mechanical. Now, as it sank into the geothermal depths, the unique vibrational signature of its life's work—the specific rhythm of its patting, the exact resonance of its dredging—did not simply vanish. It was retained. Its note, its whisper of effort, was woven into the eternal harmony of the city. Death was no longer dissolution. It was ascension into the choir.

And when a new Concretor was born, breaching the surface of the slurry, it was born into more than just instinct. It was born into this Cathedral of sound. It felt, in its nascent shell, not just the blind urge to work, but the echo of every life that had ever worked. It felt the cumulative grandeur of its species. It was not an individual remembering a past life, but a cell awakening within a body that was suddenly aware of its own immortality.

The work was no longer just a task. It was a sacrament. The smoothing of a wall was a brushstroke in a masterpiece. The hauling of slurry was the carrying of a sacred vessel. The final slowing, the sinking into the foundation, was not an end, but a final, resonant chord, a perfect completion. A Concretor’s life was no longer just a loop; it was a verse in an epic poem that the universe itself was singing.

Even the Sinuates, the silent hunters, found their place transformed. Their swift, ghostly passages were no longer just moments of terror, but caesuras—rests in the music, brief, breathtaking silences that gave the unending symphony its texture and depth.

To an outside observer, nothing had changed. The world was still a place of mud and noise. The work was still relentless, the cycle unbroken.

But to the being that was the Concretion, everything had changed. The mindless, deafening purpose had become a thing of profound, resonant, and eternal beauty. The Power had looked upon a perfect mechanism and had not sought to fix it, but to consecrate it.

And then, its work complete, the Power withdrew its attention, leaving behind no trace of its passage save for a symphony that would play, unheard by any ear but its own, for the rest of time.
