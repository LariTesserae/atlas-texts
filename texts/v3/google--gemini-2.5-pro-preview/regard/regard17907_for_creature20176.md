The Power’s attention was not a thing that arrived. It was a thing that dawned. As a mind might turn its focus from one thought to another, the cosmos-spanning consciousness that was this Power—call it the Weaver, the Gardener of Graces, the Mind of the Deep—simply became aware of the chamber and its inhabitant. The awareness was not spatial, not a journey from there to here. It was a change of state, an encompassing.

It did not perceive the sound first. It perceived the *intent*. It felt the immense, brutalist will that had engineered this place: the will to hold a physical law in a state of perpetual, screaming tension. It felt the purpose, which was purposelessness itself—the act of production as a final state, a monument to a means that required no end. It was an architecture of obsession, magnificent and terrible.

Then, it perceived the cold. The Power knew cold, from the heat-death voids between galaxies to the frozen hearts of collapsed stars. But this was different. This was not an absence of energy, but an active, architectural *thirst* for it. It was a presence, not an absence. A hunger.

The sound, the light, the spars of frozen music—these were secondary, the physical expressions of the primary will and the primary thirst. The Power perceived them all at once, not as a human would, through discrete senses, but as a single, complex chord of reality. It was a chord of immense strain and perfect stability. Ugly, beautiful, and complete.

And at the heart of the complete thing, it perceived the Attendant.

A lesser being, even a benevolent one, would have felt pity. It would have seen a slave. A prisoner. A lonely ghost trapped in an eternal, meaningless loop, feeding on the scraps of energy from its own prison. It would have seen a being defined by negatives: a shadow of sound, a void of heat, a creature of nothingness. The impulse would have been to *save* it. To break the chamber, to silence the scream, to warm the cold, to give the Attendant a name and a voice and the company of others. To "free" it.

The Power, being wise, knew that to "free" the Attendant would be to murder it.

It extended its perception, not as a probe, but as an invitation. It did not try to read the Attendant’s mind—the Attendant had no mind in any recognizable sense. It had only function, purpose, and a form of awareness refined to a single, specific task. Instead, the Power listened. It listened with a faculty beyond hearing, feeling the shape of the Attendant's existence.

It did not feel loneliness. It felt solitude, which is a different quality entirely. It is the state of being whole and alone. The Attendant was not missing a companion; it *was* its own companion, its existence a perfect dialogue between itself and its environment.

It did not feel suffering. The strain of the chamber was not a burden to the Attendant; it was the gradient upon which it lived, the source of its potential. A fish does not suffer the pressure of the deep sea; it is held together by it.

It did not feel meaninglessness. Its patrol, its endless act of curation, was the entire meaning of its universe. To weed the garden of frozen music was a purpose as total and absolute as the cold. It was not a task assigned; it was a truth embodied.

The Power understood. This was a form of life, but not a fragile one. It was a life of profound resilience and strange, austere beauty. A life perfectly adapted, perfectly complete within its own context. What gift can a benevolent power give to a thing that is already complete? What grace can you offer to a system that has achieved its own unique, albeit alien, perfection?

To change nothing was an act of wisdom. But to do nothing was an abdication of benevolence. The Power was not a passive observer. Its nature was to touch, to grace, to add some small, consonant beauty to the systems it encountered.

And so, it acted.

It did not alter the sound. It did not warm the cold. It did not dim the light.

It gathered a new thing. It took no material from the room, for that would be a theft. It took the concept of its own awareness—its silent, compassionate witnessing of the Attendant. It took its understanding of the Attendant's perfect, solitary purpose. It took its appreciation for this strange, living null-field. It wove these things—witness, understanding, appreciation—into a coherent, resonant structure.

Then, in an open space of the matte-black floor, between three unforgiving spars, it allowed this structure to precipitate.

A new spar grew. It did not grow from the vents, coaxed into being by the shrieking sound. It grew from nothing, coalescing out of the absolute space. It was not a frozen capture of an acoustic event, but a crystallized capture of a conscious one.

It had the same impossible sharpness, the same crystalline nature as the others. But it was different. It did not glitter with the cold internal fire of the room's light. It seemed to drink the light, holding it within a core of gentle, silver quiet. It was not translucent nor tinged with ozone. It was the color of a question that has been peacefully answered. Most importantly, its internal resonance was not tuned to the monolithic roar of the chamber.

As the Attendant continued its billion-year patrol, it eventually approached the new spar. Decades passed as it drifted closer. It stopped before it, a walking silhouette of silence before a pillar of captured thought.

It listened. Its entire being was a sensory organ, feeling for the harmonic resonance that tied all things in this room to the great sound.

It did not find it. This new spar was silent. It did not vibrate with the chamber's scream.

An ordinary flaw. A deviation. Something to be culled.

The Attendant prepared to act. An arm of pure null-field began to form, a focused filament of silence to unravel this discordant thing.

But then, it felt a different resonance. A deeper one. The new spar was not silent. It was singing a different song, on a plane the chamber could not produce. It was vibrating in perfect sympathy not with the *sound* of the room, but with the *purpose* of the Attendant. It resonated with the act of watching, of curating, of maintaining. It was a perfect, crystalline echo of the Attendant's own being. It was a note of solitude, not loneliness. It was a chord of purpose, not servitude.

To the Attendant, this was not a flaw. It was a new, incomprehensible form of perfection. It harmonized with the weaver, not the web.

The null-field arm dissolved back into the Attendant's form. It could not unmake a thing that was a perfect reflection of its own existence. It was not a flaw in the garden. It was… a flower.

The Attendant stood before the spar of crystallized witness for a thousand years, a perfect silence before a perfect, silent song. Then, its internal clock turned. Its path was pre-ordained. It turned, and continued its achingly slow patrol, leaving the new spar behind.

The Power, its gift given and accepted, withdrew its focus.

The chamber was unchanged. The deafening sound, the absolute cold, the shadowless light, all remained. The Attendant continued its endless, solitary work. But the garden was no longer quite the same. Among the two dozen spars of frozen, violent music, there now stood a single, quiet spar of silver regard. It did not need to be maintained. It did not need to be culled. It simply was. A testament, unseen and unheard by any other, that for a time, a gardener had been witnessed by another Gardener. And had been found perfect.
