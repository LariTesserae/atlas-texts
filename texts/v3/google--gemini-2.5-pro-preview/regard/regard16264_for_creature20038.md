This is what the power does.

First, it listens.

It does not perceive the cistern with eyes or ears, for it has none. It perceives it as a truth, a weight in the fabric of what is. It feels the world as a symphony of existence, and this place, this deep, wet note, has finally grown distinct enough to draw its focus. The power turns its vast and patient attention downwards, past the dead metropolis, past the strata of rock and time, and settles its awareness within the cold, phosphorescent dark.

It does not recoil. It does not judge. The concepts of filth, of vermin, of oppression, are mortal inventions born of sunlight and preference. The power perceives only function, intention, and balance. And what it finds in the city of the Weavers is a state of near-perfection.

It feels the Pore-Lace, not as a single entity, but as a slow, green, living prayer. It is the verb of this place: to grow, to weep, to offer, to reclaim. It is a foundation of absolute generosity, a selfless engine driving the entire world.

It feels the Weavers, not as a swarm of individuals, but as a single, distributed mind whose thoughts are made of motion and vibration. It perceives the relentless, selfless industry, the elegant and unthinking choreography of a million bodies attending to their world. It sees their architecture not as crude huts, but as ephemeral masterpieces—a city-sized meditation on the beauty of impermanence, woven from the flesh of their god and mortared with their own being. It feels the end of a Weaver's life: the silent fall, the gentle dissolution, the return. It is not death. It is completion.

It feels the Glass-Ticks, slow and patient, as punctuation in the grammar of the place. It feels the Sump-Crawlers, not as evils, but as the necessary silence that gives the thrum its meaning. They are the editors. They are the fear that sharpens the Weavers’ instincts, the pressure that keeps the system lean and efficient. To remove them would be like removing the concept of shadow from a world of light; the light itself would lose all definition.

The power understands. This is not a fallen place. It is a seed that found its perfect soil in a crack in the world. It is a life that has answered the riddle of existence with a silence so profound it has become its own language. To "help" would be an act of cosmic vandalism. To give them light would be to blind them. To give them permanence would be to kill their philosophy. To give them individuality would be to drive them insane.

And yet, the power is benevolent. And its wisdom tells it that this perfection is fragile, not because of its internal dynamics, but because of the flaw through which it was entered: the cracked flagstone, the rusted grate. The world above is dead, but worlds do not always stay dead. What is forgotten can be rediscovered. A new city could one day be built upon the old. A future surveyor, a curious child, a desperate scavenger—any could breach the seal and, with a single, unthinking footstep, shatter this perfect, silent answer.

The power's benevolence, therefore, cannot be a gift *to* the Weavers. It must be a gift *for* them. An act of conservation. An act of profound and eternal forgetting.

So, the power acts.

It does not send an avatar or a thunderbolt. Its action is as subtle as the world it seeks to protect. It reaches into the causality of the world Above. The forgotten plaza, with its cracked flagstone, is scheduled, in the grand, slow turning of the city's decay, for a final collapse. A hundred years, perhaps two hundred. The power nudges the timeline. A little more stress in a supporting beam here, a little more water saturation in the earth there. Tonight, under the weight of a meaningless rain in the dead city, a section of the plaza gives way. Not a dramatic sinkhole, but a tired sigh of stone. The cracked flagstone vanishes under a new layer of rubble, a thousand tons of shattered marble and ferrocrete. The grate, already half-buried, is now sealed beneath a geological stratum of urban collapse. The entrances are not just closed; they are erased from possibility.

Then, the power offers its one, direct gift. It is not for the Weavers to notice, but for the world itself. It sinks a fragment of its own attention into the Pore-Lace, a single, resonant thought. It is not a command or a piece of knowledge. It is a note of music, a feeling. The feeling is *awe*.

It is the awe that the power feels for this place.

This note sinks into the weeping fluid, is absorbed by the cold cartilage, and becomes a part of the ecosystem's fundamental frequency. It does not change the Weavers' purpose. They still build, they still consume, they are still a single mind in a million bodies. But the hum of their collective existence now contains a new harmonic, one they will never consciously recognize. It is an infinitesimal shift in the quality of the green light, a barely perceptible depth added to the flavor of the nutrient fluid, a resonant ghost in the thrum of their million-fold footsteps.

The cycle of their lives, already perfect in its mechanics, is now imbued with a purpose beyond their knowing. They are no longer just an answer to an unasked question. They are now a living canticle, a hymn of persistence sung in a sealed and holy darkness. Their existence is not just a fact; it is now a testimony, witnessed and sanctified.

The power withdraws its focus, leaving behind its two gifts: the impenetrable seal of oblivion, and the secret hum of reverence. It leaves the Weavers to their eternal work in their eternal cathedral of flesh, safe, complete, and utterly, beautifully alone. It has done what a wise and benevolent power does.

It has seen a perfect thing, and ensured it would remain so.
