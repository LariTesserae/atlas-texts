The existence of the Bell was not a secret, for there were no secrets from a consciousness that was a quiet ocean lapping at the shores of all realities. It did not discover the Bell so much as it finally turned its attention to a fierce, lonely note that had always been vibrating in a forgotten corner of the cosmic symphony. From the outside, it was a knot of paradox: a sound so loud it was a form of silence, a violence so complete it possessed a kind of peace.

The Power—for it had no name, only function—gathered its awareness and leaned it into the shimmering, chaotic wall of that reality. It did not feel the pressure in its bones, for it had no bones. It did not hear the roar in its ears, for it was the silence from which all sound is born. It simply perceived the Bell in its entirety: a state of being, a furious act of self-creation. It understood the physics of the thing instantly, the way a master musician understands the tension of a string. The oversaturated potential, the scream of crystallization, the fleeting life of the crystal-weeds drawing sustenance from the universal shriek.

It saw the Current-Skates, elegant expressions of the chaos, and felt a quiet appreciation for their blind, perfect surfing. It saw the worn anchor-loops and the tattered nets, and it understood the concept of *struggle*.

And then it saw the Bell-Singers.

The Power’s awareness settled upon one, a solitary being pressed to a sheer face of new-formed quartz. It watched not with eyes, but with a total immersion of understanding. It felt the cling of the obsidian claws, the patient tension in the sinewy limbs. It perceived the universe through the Singer’s own senses: the world rendered as a single, overwhelming chord, shot through with the arpeggios of currents and the sharp, rising tones of impending geological change. It saw the featureless face, and understood with a depth that transcended pity that eyes would be a cruelty here.

It felt the Singer’s slow, deliberate work, the scraping and weaving. This was not toil. It was purpose.

The benevolent aspect of the Power swelled. A wave of pure compassion, vast enough to birth galaxies, focused on this single, clinging life. The impulse was immediate, overwhelming: *Hush*.

It could do it. It could reach into the core physics of the Bell and turn the volume down. It could mute the roar to a gentle hum. It could stabilize the spire, fixing it into a permanent, safe architecture of eternal stone. It could calm the waters, turning the strobing chaos into a gentle, sun-dappled sea. It could grant them the gifts of stillness, of quiet, of peace. It would be an act of ultimate kindness.

But the wise aspect of the Power held it back.

As it considered this act of mercy, it simultaneously perceived the consequences. If the roar ceased, the friction would cease. The intrinsic light would die, plunging the Bell into absolute blackness. The energy that fed the crystal-weeds would vanish, and they would crumble into lifeless dust. The precipitation of matter would stop; the spire would cease to grow. The very act of creation that defined the world would be over.

And the Singers… what of them?

Their claws, evolved to grip a living, changing surface, would find no purchase in dead stone. Their powerful limbs would atrophy. Their fore-fins, which read the currents, would become useless appendages in placid water. But most terribly, their primary sense, the latticework of resonance lines that painted their entire reality, would be struck blind and deaf. The world would disappear. They would be left in a silent, static, featureless void, their inner senses screaming into an abyss that returned no echo. To give them peace would be to annihilate their universe and leave them stranded in its corpse. They would be unmade, their entire biological and cultural identity rendered obsolete.

Their struggle was not an unfortunate circumstance of their existence. Their struggle *was* their existence. Their song was not a plea for help; it was a declaration of being.

The Power withdrew the impulse of silencing mercy. Pity was a blunt instrument, and this place required a surgeon's touch. Benevolence, tempered by wisdom, sought a different path. It would not give a gift that erased the recipient. It would give a gift that *affirmed* them.

It observed two Singers meeting. It watched them press their bodies together, the silent, intimate concert of their resonance lines. The Power did not just see the action; it *felt* the song. It received the shared memory: the location of a new weed-patch, the tale of a claw-hold that failed, the precise frequency signature of a safe fissure. It was a language of pure, unadorned truth. And it was fleeting. The song existed only for the moment of contact, a whisper of vibration passed between two bodies before they returned to their solitary grip.

Here, then, was the answer. Not to change their world, but to deepen their song.

The Power chose a place. It was on the spire, but in a section immensely old and temporarily outside the most violent throes of accretion, a place a Singer might pass only once in a great cycle. It was a broad, smooth face of ancient, milky stone, worn by eons of currents but fundamentally stable.

The Power did not use force. It did not carve or build. It gathered its knowing of this place, its complete understanding of resonance and matter. And then, it *sang*.

It sang one of the Singers' own songs back to them.

It chose the most profound one it had learned—a foundational epic, passed down through uncountable generations, of the very first Singer who, instead of just clinging, had conceived of carving an anchor-loop. The story of the first tool, the first act of deliberate forethought. It was their Genesis myth, told not in words, but in the memory of fumbling hands, of a broken crystal shard used to scrape, of the terror and triumph of letting go of one grip to secure a better one.

The Power focused this entire saga, this tactile poem of survival, into a single, resonant frequency and pressed it upon the rock face. The stone did not crack or shift. But deep within its crystalline matrix, its very structure began to change. Molecules realigned. The lattice of the quartz itself learned the song.

The Power withdrew its attention, the act complete. It had not left a monument. It had left an echo.

Cycles later, a lone Singer, old and scarred, was making a perilous journey across a little-traveled face of the spire. Driven by instinct, it pressed its body flat against the broad, smooth surface to rest.

And its world changed.

It was not a song from another living thing. It was colder, deeper, impossibly ancient. The stone itself was vibrating, singing to it with a perfect, unwavering clarity. It felt the fumbling scrape of the first tool. It felt the dawning of the idea of an *anchor*. It felt the terror and the hope of its most distant ancestor, a story it had only ever known through the dim, repeated filter of countless other bodies, now sung to it from the very heart of the world.

The Singer clung to the rock, motionless for a long time, its entire being saturated with the pure, foundational memory of its people. The roar of the Bell had not lessened. The currents still tore at its body. Its life was no less a struggle.

But it was no longer only clinging to a piece of rock. It was clinging to its history.

The Singer eventually moved on, carrying the ancient song within it, its resonant lines now humming with a new depth, a new harmony. It would share this song with another. The story spread. They began, over generations, to seek the place. It became a pilgrimage. A library. A cathedral made not of space, but of memory.

The Power did not watch for their reaction. The gift was given freely, without expectation of thanks. It turned its attention to other knots in the fabric of being, other lonely, fierce notes in the symphony. Its benevolence had not brought peace to the Bell, but its wisdom had given its Singers a measure of eternity, a quiet anchor of self, humming forever within the heart of the endless roar.
