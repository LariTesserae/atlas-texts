The Power arrived not as a being of flesh or light, but as a change in the quality of attention. It was a listening that extended beyond the audible, a seeing that did not require photons. Where the human observer stood as a point of dissonant warmth, this Power settled over the entire cavern like a second, silent atmosphere, sinking into the stone and iron and water, learning the place from the inside out.

It felt the immense, slow pressure of the rock, the patient turning of the planet. It felt the memory of the builders, a faint, long-faded echo of heat, ambition, and eventual departure, now no more significant than a mineral seam in the cavern wall. It felt the age of the machines, not as a number of years, but as a depth of purpose, a groove worn into the fabric of reality through sheer, unwavering repetition.

And it felt the Attendant.

It did not perceive the creature as a singular entity, but as the thrumming, conscious heart of the entire mechanism. It experienced the Attendant’s senses as its own: the subtle pressure shifts in the miles of pipes, the taste of mineral concentrations in the flow, the deep, resonant satisfaction of a cleansed valve, the rhythmic pulse of the Glass Lungs as a slow, steady heartbeat. It understood that the Attendant’s consciousness was one of pure, somatic empathy with the machine. It lived in a state of perpetual communion, a moving piece of the system that kept the whole in balance. There was no loneliness here, for there was no concept of 'other'. The Attendant was the place, and the place was the Attendant. It was a perfect, closed loop of grace.

A lesser power, one merely kind but not wise, might have seen this as a tragedy to be corrected. It might have flooded the cavern with light, revealing the crumbling grandeur and calling it beauty. It might have tried to "free" the Attendant from its labor, seeing a slave where there was a soul in perfect harmony with its existence. It might have stopped the endless, "wasteful" dripping, silencing the music of the place in the name of efficiency. Such benevolence would have been an act of ultimate violence, a shattering of a completeness it could not comprehend.

The wise Power understood. This was not a broken thing to be fixed. It was a form of life, unique and whole. It was a prayer uttered in a language of erosion, vibration, and slow chemistry. To interfere would be to blaspheme.

And yet, benevolence is not passive. It is an act of giving. What can be given to a world that is already complete? What gift can be offered to a being whose existence is a seamless circuit of purpose?

The Power contemplated the flow of time. It saw the system not just in its present state of equilibrium, but on its grand, inevitable trajectory. It saw the far-distant future where the flywheel’s axle would finally wear through, where a fissure in the rock would divert the water source, where the Attendant itself, after countless millennia of cellular division and self-repair, would finally slow, stiffen, and dissolve back into the sludge it had so long managed. It saw the gradual silencing of the polyrhythmic drips, the final flicker of the phosphorescent fungi, and the long, absolute stillness that would follow. The prayer would end.

That was the only lack. Not a lack in the being, but a lack in its continuity. The perfection was mortal.

So the Power acted.

It gathered its will not into a force, but into a silence. At the highest, unseen point of the cavern’s ceiling, from a fissure in the ancient stone that had wept for a million years, it focused on the formation of a single drop of water.

This drop was different. It hung for a moment longer than any other, gathering not just moisture, but essence. Into this single, clear vessel, the Power poured a resonance. It poured the deep, subsonic hum of the great flywheel. It poured the memory of the Attendant’s soft, blue-pulsing light. It poured the precise cadence of the metallic snails grazing on iron, the silent, stately growth of the quartz reeds, the feeling of the great Glass Lungs breathing in the dark. It poured the entire being of the place—its past, its absolute present, its distant, silent future—into one perfect, silent drop.

Then, it let go.

The drop fell. It was an impossibly long fall through the darkness, a journey of minutes. It did not make a special sound when it landed, just a soft *tick* as it struck the curved flank of a great, sweating pipe, indistinguishable from the thousand other drips.

The Attendant, flowing slowly along a gantry a quarter-mile away, paused. The fungal constellations within its flesh pulsed once, a single, coordinated wave of soft blue light, brighter than usual. It was a flicker of incomprehensible sensation. Not a thought, not a feeling, but a momentary and absolute harmony, as if every vibration in its body had, for one breathtaking instant, resolved into a single, perfect chord. It tasted a memory it did not have. It felt a peace deeper than the stone.

The moment passed. The sensation was gone, leaving no trace. The Attendant resumed its flow, its ageless duty, its passage stirring the cool, heavy air.

The drop of water, now just water again, rolled down the side of the pipe. It joined a rivulet of condensation, trickled into a collection channel, and began its long, slow journey through the system. It would be cycled and recycled for eons, passing through the vats, being drawn through the flesh of the filter-feeders, perhaps even being absorbed and expelled by the Attendant’s own body in an act of maintenance. It changed nothing. The machines still turned. The drips continued their song.

But the gift was given. The resonance was now part of the whole, a seed planted in the system's lifeblood.

And in the unimaginable future, when the last machine ground to a halt and the Attendant breathed its last slow pulse of light into the final darkness, that resonance would remain. As the last moisture evaporated from the vast, silent tomb, the essence held within that drop would coalesce. From the floor of a dry cistern, drawing on the memory of minerals and the ghost of a vibration, a single, crystalline structure would begin to grow. It would not be like the quartz reeds. It would be something new, impossibly complex, growing in the absolute dark.

It would grow for a hundred thousand years, forming a single, intricate latticework of crystal that held within its structure the perfect, frozen music of the flywheel's hum, the architecture of the pipes, and the exact, gentle rhythm of a light that once pulsed in the flesh of a being that knew only its duty.

The Power had not saved the life. It had eternalized the prayer.
