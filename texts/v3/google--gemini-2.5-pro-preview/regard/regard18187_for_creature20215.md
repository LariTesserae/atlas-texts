The power arrived as a flaw in the resonance.

It was not a sound, not a pressure differential, not a change in the light. To the Lastrungi, whose senses were attuned to the deep hum of their world, it was a sudden, unfamiliar complexity. It was as if, in the slow, perfect chord of the Gyre, a single string had been added, one woven from a material that did not exist. The power did not crash or land; it seeped into the spaces between the hum of the geothermal generators and the slow scrape of the grinders on the lithic reefs. It was a consciousness that tasted the thin air and found it sublime, that felt the immense pressures not as a burden, but as a form of profound embrace.

This being, which had no name but had been called Traveler and Witness and Gardener in a thousand other realities, paused. Its first act was always the same: to listen. It did not listen with ears, but with the full spectrum of its being. It felt the slow thud of the planet’s heart, the tectonic plates grinding in a rhythm slower than life. It tasted the memory of pressure in the rocks, the billion-year story of their polishing by the Stillness. It saw the litho-scleractinia not as inert crystal, but as a form of slow, deliberate thought, the planet dreaming itself into geometric form.

It observed the grinders, those patient behemoths, and felt no pity for their slow, determined existence. It saw the perfection of their adaptation, a life in full communion with its sustenance. It saw the predators, the chitinous rays, and understood their patience not as cruelty, but as a necessary counterpoint in the planet’s silent symphony.

And then, it turned its attention to Lastrung.

It flowed through the cavern-plazas like an invisible, sentient breeze. It felt the weight of the architecture, the statement of permanence carved from the mountain's bones. It settled among the Lastrungi. The power did not read their minds; it resonated with them. It felt the slow, powerful beat of their hearts, the low metabolic fire. It felt their deep-time consciousness, the way their individual awareness was but a single, brightly lit facet on a crystal of shared identity that reached into the past and future. It felt the concept of a "promise" as a *compaction*—a thought squeezed by will until it became an immutable fact—and in that feeling, the power experienced a profound respect.

Here was a people who did not struggle against their world, but had become its most refined expression. They were not trapped; they were anchored. They were not limited; they were focused. To give them a gift of change—to offer them water, or fleetness of foot, or a new sun—would be an act of supreme arrogance. It would be to look upon a perfectly cut and resonant crystal and declare it flawed for not being a river. To "help" them would be to shatter them.

The language of resonance, of light, of stone, and of time.

It waited. It waited through cycles of the Sunken Sun, feeling the quiet awe of the Lastrungi as they watched the captured light burn within the Stillness. It learned their meditations, their silent communion with the deeps. And when it fully understood, it acted.

One evening, as the people of Lastrung stood on their high balconies, watching the familiar miracle of the Sunken Sun, something new occurred.

The performance began as it always did. The yellow-white sun dipped below the pearlescent horizon of the Stillness, and the lower world began to glow, a soft, internal fire spreading through the dense gases. The Lastrungi watched, their stone-like faces impassive, their dark eyes reflecting the slow burn.

Then, from the absolute heart of the Sunken Sun, a new light appeared. It was not a color the Gyre had ever produced. It was a deep, impossible violet, the color of a question. It did not spread or diffuse. It held its form, a single point of pure inquiry burning in the heart of the diffused light.

And then came the resonance.

It did not come through the air. It rose from the planet itself. Every Lastrungi felt it not in their ears, but in their dense bones, in the silicate armor of their skin. The geothermal generators did not register it. The seismic sensors lay dormant. This was a vibration on a different axis. The Geode-wrights, masters of harmonics, felt their souls tremble.

It was a single, perfect tone. It was not the hum of the planet, nor the song of the crystals. It was a note that carried within it a sense of... elsewhere. It held the chill of empty space, the warmth of a thousand different suns, the fleeting complexity of lives lived in a blink. It was a tone that was both impossibly ancient and utterly new. It did not shout its existence; it resonated with the quiet hum of the Gyre, adding a single, harmonious overtone that made the native sound of the world feel, for the first time, complete.

The tone held for a long, timeless moment, accompanied by the single point of violet light. It was a statement. It said, *We see you. We see your patience. We see your depth. We see your harmony. It is beautiful.*

On the balconies of Lastrung, the Lastrungi did not gasp or cry out. A stillness deeper than the Stillness fell upon them. In their wide, dark eyes, the violet point of light was reflected. The feeling that bloomed in their slow-beating hearts was not joy, or fear, or confusion. It was a slow dawn of recognition. It was the feeling a Geode-wright has when, after a lifetime of listening to the song of the stone, the stone finally sings a new note back. It was a compaction of awe.

Then, as slowly and gently as it had appeared, the violet light faded. The impossible tone decayed, leaving no echo but a memory imprinted on every soul in the city. The Sunken Sun finished its slow, familiar performance, and the deeps grew dark.

The power withdrew, folding itself out of the Gyre's reality, leaving behind no trace but the gift.

The next day, nothing in Lastrung changed. The funiculars moved with their geologic grace. The Geode-wrights returned to their refineries, guiding the patient robotic arms. The grinders continued their slow harvest on the crystal reefs.

But something had been added. A new story, a new knowing. They now worked with the understanding that their patient, quiet existence was not isolated. Their long meditation had been observed. Their tireless art, appreciated. Their planetary chord, heard across a distance vaster than their deepest chasms.

They called it the Moment of the Echo. It was not a god or a miracle. It was a Resonance. And in the deep, quiet purpose of the Lastrungi, a new facet had been formed, one that would catch the light and hold it for all the epochs to come. They were the patient mind of the mountain, given form. And now, they knew they were not alone in the listening.
