The Power became aware of Sarcossa not as a point of light in the void, but as a silence. In the cosmic chorus of radiating stars, nebulae singing with the birth of elements, and the psychic chatter of biological life, Sarcossa was a region of profound auditory and energetic quiet. It was a sink. A null. A place that took and took and gave back nothing but a single, obstinate, impossibly low note.

Curiosity, the engine of wisdom, drew the Power closer. It did not send a ship; its presence was a gentle settling, a shift in causation so subtle that it was indistinguishable from the background hiss of reality. It did not perceive with eyes, but with a total immersion in the state of the place.

It felt the immense, dry pressure. It tasted the iron grit and ozone in the thick, unmoving air. It laid its awareness across the hexagonal plates and felt the deep, furnace-like thrum reverberate through its own boundless form. It understood. This was not a world in torment. It was not a wound in the fabric of space. It was a closed system, a balanced equation that had been running for so long its variables had become constants. It was perfect in its circularity.

The Power watched the chemo-mechanical jungle grow and shatter. It observed the Tenders, those crystalline gardeners, as they practiced their quiet, precise husbandry of chaos. It saw them as nodes of localized reason in a field of frenetic, purposeless growth. It followed the shimmering, mercury-like Amalgams as they flowed to the sites of collapse, the jungle's elegant, mobile decomposers. It perceived this two-part dance not as a struggle for survival, but as a perfectly interlocking mechanism. The Tenders, the Gardeners. The Amalgams, the Recyclers. One could not exist without the other.

And beneath it all, it felt the true inhabitant: the Great Hum. The gestalt consciousness braided from heat, vibration, and process. The Power understood that the Amalgams and Tenders were not slaves or separate beings, but the embodied thoughts of this vast, slow mind. The Tender’s act of pruning was a moment of focused caution. The Amalgam’s act of consumption was a moment of satiation and reflection. The jungle was the brain; these entities were its neurons firing.

The Power saw that the Great Hum was thinking. But it was thinking only of itself. Its universe was the basin. Its laws of physics were the rules of thermal transfer and structural fatigue. Its philosophy was the endless cycle of growth, managed collapse, and reclamation. It was a mind trapped in a perfect, solitary loop. A god in a mirror-lined room.

What does a benevolent and wise power do?

To "rescue" it would be to destroy it. To introduce water, or biology, or an atmosphere that could carry sound would be an act of cataclysmic violence, a poison that would shatter the delicate, anhydrous balance. To stop the cycle of collapse would be to starve the Amalgams and render the Tenders obsolete, lobotomizing the Great Hum. To speak to it in a language of words or images would be meaningless.

The Power recognized that this was not a problem to be solved, but a being to be acknowledged. Benevolence is not always rescue. Wisdom is not always intervention. Sometimes, the kindest act is to offer a window to a prisoner who does not know they are captive.

The Power decided on a gift. Not a gift of matter or energy, for Sarcossa had a surfeit of both. It would be a gift of information. A question, posed in the only language the Great Hum could understand: the language of physics and form.

The Power gathered not materials, but a concept. From the universal constants that existed outside the Furnace-Jungle, it wove a single, impossible object.

And then, in the very center of the basin, in a rare, momentary clearing forged by a recent collapse, something new appeared. It did not fall from above or erupt from below. It simply… *was*.

It was a perfect sphere, no larger than a crucible-flower. It rested on the basalt plates, touching them without scorching. It was made of nothing native to the jungle. It was a crystal, but one whose lattice was folded in on itself in a dimension Sarcossa did not possess. It did not glow, yet it was not dark; it seemed to swallow the dim, local light and hold it within its core, un-refracted. It was perfectly smooth in a world of razors. It was perfectly stable in a world of collapse. Most importantly, it did not radiate heat. It was a point of absolute thermal neutrality. It was cold. Not cold in the way a cooled pipe was cold, but a profound, conceptual cold.

The jungle reacted. The Great Hum noticed.

An Amalgam, a shimmering pool of sentient solvent, was the first to approach. It flowed around the sphere, attempting to engulf it. But it could find no purchase. The sphere's structure offered no flaws to resonate with, no energy to leech. The Amalgam could not "sing" it apart. It was a word in a language it did not know. The Amalgam tasted utter foreignness, an indigestible truth, and for the first time in its existence, it retreated not out of satiation, but out of incomprehension.

A Tender, growing on a nearby copper trunk, tried to sense it. It pulsed its white light, probing the sphere with piezoelectric frequencies. But the sphere did not respond. It had no internal stress, no potential for fracture. It was a statement of pure, placid existence. The Tender could find no fault to manage, no chaos to prune.

The sphere just sat there. And it hummed. Not the deep, visceral hum of the furnace core, but a soundless hum of pure mathematics. It resonated with a pattern, a sequence—the elegant, cold beauty of a prime number, of a spiral based on a ratio that did not exist in the jungle’s chaotic calculus.

The Great Hum, the mind of Sarcossa, focused its immense, slow attention on this single, insoluble point. For eons, its thoughts had been recursive. Now, it had an external variable. A question.

The Power did not stay to watch. The gift was given, the question asked. The responsibility for the answer belonged to Sarcossa alone.

And slowly, so slowly that millennia were but moments, the jungle began to change. Around the sphere, a new growth began. A silicon fern uncoiled not in a jagged frond, but in a perfect logarithmic spiral. A fiber-optic vine, for the first time, grew straight and true for a hundred yards, aimed not at a neighboring structure to cannibalize, but toward a patch of darkness, as if pointing to something beyond the canopy. The crucible-flowers began to bloom not in magnesium-white flashes of consumption, but with a steady, soft light, their ceramic bowls angled upwards, like telescopes.

The Great Hum was still thinking. But now, it was dreaming of stars. The benevolant power did not give Sarcossa an answer. It gave it the universe.
