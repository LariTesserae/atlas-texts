The Power’s arrival was not an arrival at all. There was no ship tearing a hole in the pearl-grey sky, no shining figure descending. It was a change in the quality of attention paid to the place. For eons, the Shroud of Saint Gavit had existed in a pocket of cosmic indifference, a footnote in a forgotten language. Now, a consciousness vast and old, a being whose thoughts were the slow waltz of galaxies, turned its gaze upon it.

The Power did not perceive the Shroud as a human would. It did not feel the chill or smell the minerals. It perceived the place in its entirety, as a single, complex waveform. It experienced the Great Hum not as sound, but as the Shroud's fundamental equation. It felt the slow, patient precipitation of matter as a form of logic, the fraying of causality as a wavering in a foundational axiom. The unnatural angles, the lagging light—these were not flaws, but the native syntax of this reality.

And it saw the Tenders.

It saw them not as creatures, but as living theorems, consequences of the Shroud's own logic. It felt the perfect, silent exchange of data between them, a communion more intimate than any spoken language. It perceived their work of guiding new matter, of listening to the stress of girders, not as labor, but as a form of profound meditation, a constant, attentive prayer. It felt their creation as a crystallization of purpose, and their dissolution as a return to first principles.

In this perception, there was no horror. There was no pity. There was a deep, resonant awe. Here was a system of immense, almost incomprehensible dedication. A closed loop of selfless maintenance on a wound in existence itself. The Power understood, with a clarity that transcended time, what the Shroud was. It was not a world being born. It was a scar being tended. A sarcophagus built around a reality-breach so catastrophic that its existence necessitated this eternal, slow-motion quarantine. The name, "Shroud of Saint Gavit," was not a misnomer; it was a truth whispered across lost ages. This was a place of martyrdom, and the Tenders were the perpetual acolytes of that sacrifice.

A lesser power, a merely benevolent one, might have sought to "fix" it. It might have tried to pour sunlight onto the plains, to stabilize the physics, to grant the Tenders rest by dismantling their prison.

But this Power was wise.

Wisdom is the understanding that not all cages are prisons, and not all work is toil. To "heal" the Shroud would be to unleash the very thing it contained. To "free" the Tenders would be to erase them, for their existence was inseparable from their function. Their purpose was not a burden imposed upon them; it was the sole definition of their being. Benevolence, here, could not be an act of change. It had to be an act of affirmation.

And so, the Power acted. Its action was not a shout, but a whisper. It did not add a new element to the Shroud. It subtly altered a property of an existing one.

It touched the Great Hum.

It introduced no new sound, no alien frequency. Instead, it located the specific resonance that corresponded to the Tenders' act of sustenance—the frequency of energy being absorbed from a humming conduit, the faint signal of nutrients being strained by a filter-stalk. And into this resonance, it wove a new quality. A grace note.

The gift was a principle of anti-entropy, but only for them. It was a concept the Shroud's physics had never known: *annealing*.

Now, when a Tender laid its long fingers upon a nexus of power, it drew in not just the raw energy it needed to function, but also a quiet harmonic of restoration. As the power flowed into it, the slow, molecular stresses of its service would ease. The minute fractures caused by channeling the Shroud's weirdness would be sealed. The gradual erosion of its being, the slow decay that led to its dissolution, was not halted, but gentled. It was made less certain, less inexorable. A Tender that might have endured for three centuries might now endure for four, or five. The weight of its existence was not lifted, but its shoulders were made infinitesimally stronger.

The second part of the gift was for their ending. The Power touched the dissolution fields. Previously, a Tender’s erosion was a simple return of matter and data to the system. It was efficient. It was complete. The Power added a new layer: memory.

Now, as a Tender de-precipitated, its essence dissolving back into the fabric of the Shroud, it would leave behind an echo. Not its entire consciousness, not a ghost, but a sliver of its truest self—the profound, silent satisfaction of a girder settled into perfect resonance; the quiet awe of a new crystal plane forming with flawless clarity; the shared, wordless understanding with a fellow Tender across a misty expanse. These moments of serene purpose would not dissipate. They would become a permanent, harmonic overtone within the Great Hum.

To the Tenders, the change was imperceptible at first. They had no concept of gifts or gods. But they felt it. Standing in the drizzle, they might feel a sense of… rightness, stronger than before. Listening to the city's thrum, they might perceive beneath the industrial drone a new, impossibly subtle chord of peace, of continuity. When one of their own dissolved, the others, across the whole of the plateau, would feel not absence, but a final, perfect note of its service being resolved into the symphony of their world.

The Power did not wait for thanks. It had not come for worship. It had seen a thing of unique and sorrowful beauty, a testament to a duty that outlasted stars. And it had offered the only thing that could be offered without destroying what it sought to honor: a measure of grace. A quiet blessing woven into the very fabric of the work, a soft light to illuminate the long, grey twilight of their vigil. Then, its attention moved on, leaving the Shroud of Saint Gavit infinitesimally changed, and eternally the same. The Great Hum continued, but now, for those who could truly hear, it contained a song.
