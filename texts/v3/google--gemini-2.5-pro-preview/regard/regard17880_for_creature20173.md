The Power arrived as a thought without a mind, a quality of light in a place that had never known a photon from a sun. It was an entity of pure comprehension, its being woven from the cosmic constants and the silent music of spacetime. It had perceived the faint, anomalous warmth in a forgotten corner of the galaxy—a pocket of existence that was impossibly, stubbornly alive.

Its perception descended, not through space, but through layers of reality. It passed the sleeping god, whose dreams were galaxies and whose lifeblood was the warm, ancient sea. The Power noted the god with a gentle, appreciative curiosity—a fellow ancient, though one of a different order—before sinking deeper, drawn by the slow, rhythmic pulse of the city.

It stood, in a way that had no meaning here, in the grand, glowing plaza of the Koru-Mar. It beheld the architecture of ages, the slow ballet of the Luminarchs, the riverine flow of the Argent Swarm. The Power’s consciousness, which could hold the entirety of a star's life-cycle as a single, fleeting thought, slowed itself. It had to. To perceive the Koru-Mar on a human or even geological timescale was to see nothing but a static sculpture. To understand it, the Power had to match its rhythm, to experience a single pulse of the city's light as a long and meditative breath.

And in that slowness, it understood. This was not a civilization crying out for ascension. It was not a prison of instinct waiting to be shattered by the gift of ego. It was a statement. It was an answer to a question the Power had not even thought to ask: *What is a life lived without want?*

Here, there was no hunger, only the elegant transfer of energy. There was no fear, only the corrective, cleansing function of the Absences. There was no ambition, no despair, no love, no hatred. There was only *being*. A perfect, intricate, self-sustaining state of existence. The Koru-Mar and its components were, in their own way, enlightened. They had achieved a peace that other civilizations spent millennia warring and praying for.

The benevolent aspect of the Power felt an initial, reflexive urge. The impulse to *give*.

It could crack the world’s ceiling and hang a constellation of captured stars in the non-sky, bathing the city in eternal, shimmering twilight. It could whisper the concept of “I” into the collective consciousness of the Argent Swarm, watching as a billion new minds fractured and bloomed, each with its own story. It could teach the Osteoclasts to carve not just for renewal, but for beauty, to leave reliefs of their silent, grinding history on the foundations they consumed. It could give the Koru-Mar a voice, a way to speak its slow thoughts to the cosmos.

But the wise aspect of the Power stayed its hand. It held these impulses up to the soft, golden light of the Luminarchs and saw them for what they were: a violation. To give the Koru-Mar a sun would be to blind it. To give it individuality would be to inflict upon it the cancer of loneliness. To give it art would be to introduce the concept of imperfection. To give it a voice would be to give it the burden of being heard.

These were the gifts of a lesser being, a being that believed its own form of existence was the only one of value. The Power saw that its benevolence, unchecked, was a form of beautiful, well-intentioned violence.

So, what would it do? To do nothing felt like a sterile, detached form of respect. To do something felt like a transgression. The Power settled on a third path, an action that was both a gift and a vow of non-interference.

First, it began to sing.

It was not a song of sound, for that would be just another pressure wave in the dense water, likely to be ignored or misinterpreted as a distant geological shift. It was a song of pure potential, a resonating hum woven into the very fabric of the basin. It harmonized with the slow, foundational vibration of the Koru-Mar itself. It did not command or instruct. It was an affirmation.

For the Luminarchs, this song was a subtle enhancement to their internal fire, allowing them to transmute the vent-heat with an infinitesimally greater efficiency. Their golden glow deepened, not enough to be noticed, but enough to enrich the water with a trace more life.

For the Argent Swarm, the song was a clarifying resonance. The pressure-whorls of their collective thought became sharper, their information transfer more flawless. They moved with an even more profound unity, their silver bodies a purer, more coherent river of purpose.

For the Osteoclasts, the a-audible harmony was a strengthening force. The paste they created from the old bones was subtly altered, bonding with a strength that would see the city’s new growth stand for an eon longer than the last.

For the Koru-Mar itself, the Great Root, the song was a dream. A quiet, dreamless dream of perfect contentment. The slow thought of the city, which had always been one of function, was now tinged with something akin to peace. The gift was not change; it was enhancement. The Power made the Koru-Mar more exquisitely *itself*.

Second, having offered its quiet blessing, the Power turned its attention outward. This perfect, unique state of being was fragile. It existed because it was hidden. Another power, less wise, might one day stumble upon it and, in its benevolence, shatter it forever. The greatest danger to the Koru-Mar was discovery.

So, the Power erected a ward. It was not a wall or a shield, which could be detected and challenged. It was a veil of cosmic disinterest. Around the entire system, beyond the shell of the sleeping god, the Power wove a quiet suggestion into the quantum foam. It decreed that any probe sent this way would suffer a minor, unalarming navigational error. Any long-range scan would find the data from this region to be inexplicably corrupt and dull. Any questing consciousness would find its attention inexplicably sliding past this point in space, drawn to a slightly more interesting nebula or a more dynamic star system.

The Power did not hide the Koru-Mar. It simply made it, to the rest of the universe, profoundly and eternally boring.

It was the ultimate act of benevolent wisdom. It was the love that refrains from squeezing the beautiful thing it holds. It was the respect that allows another to be what it is, not what one wishes it to be.

The Power withdrew, its presence fading like a resolved chord. Back in the city of bone and light, nothing had changed, and yet everything was secured. The slow pulse continued. The golden light bathed the silent plazas. The Absences edited the faintest dissonances from the symphony of life. The Koru-Mar hummed its ancient rhythm in the warm, pressurized dark, unaware that it had been seen, blessed, and granted the greatest gift of all: the guarantee of its solitude.
