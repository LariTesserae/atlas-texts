The Power came not as a climber, but as a change in the quality of light upon the outer slopes. It was a presence that rode on the solar wind and settled like dust in the gravity well of the world. It had no eyes, but it saw the mountain as a knot in the fabric of spacetime, a place where causality ran thick and slow. It had no mind, but its being was a form of universal awareness, a constant, gentle inquiry into the nature of What Is.

It perceived the basin first as a silence.
Not an absence of sound, but a null-field where the very potential for sound was cancelled. To the Power, for whom the turning of galaxies was a low hum and the birth of a star was a chime, this localized cancellation was a phenomenon of infinite curiosity. It was like finding a single, perfectly smooth, unvibrating string on the cosmic instrument.

The Power drew closer. Its presence did not disturb the thin air or the weeping stones. It was an observer whose observation cost the universe nothing. It flowed past the entrance and into the basin, its awareness spreading like water diluting in water.

It perceived the stasis. It felt the trillionfold lives held in a single, unwavering breath. It saw the water droplet, a jewel of impossible surface tension, and understood that its state was not a trick of physics, but a feat of will. It perceived the insects hanging in their crystal lattice, the predator forever about to strike, the mosses in their perfect, un-growing verdancy.

And it saw the Gardeners.

It did not see them as creatures of chitin and mycelium. It saw them as they were: massive, silent keystones of consciousness. It perceived their network, the shared thought that was a single, sustained note of pure intent: *Be. Still.*

The Power, whose own nature was Flow, Change, and Becoming, paused. It considered the basin and its architects. Benevolence, in the Power's understanding, was the act of nurturing a thing toward its own truest nature. A star's truest nature was to burn. A river's truest nature was to find the sea. A living mind's truest nature was to question, to strive, to change.

Here, then, was a paradox. A collection of minds whose ultimate, successful striving was to end striving. They had reached the sea and willed the waters to freeze. Was this a tragedy? A perversion?

The Power extended a filament of its awareness, finer than a ray of light, and gently touched the central consciousness of the Gardeners. It was not an intrusion, but a resonance. It did not ask a question; it *listened* to the shape of the answer that had existed for a hundred millennia.

It felt their ambition. The cosmic ache for perfection. The weariness of process, of growth and decay, of the constant, messy struggle of life. It understood their art. They were not sculptors who worked with stone; they were composers who worked with the orchestra of an entire ecosystem. And this stasis was their final, perfect, eternal symphony. They had not died. They had *finished*.

To "awaken" them would not be a kindness. It would be a desecration. It would be like an earthquake shattering a cathedral so that its stones could be returned to the chaos of the quarry. It would be the act of a vandal, not a wise and benevolent power. Their choice was absolute, their goal achieved.

The Power withdrew its touch, the listening complete. It knew what it would do.

Its action would not be within the basin. The basin was a complete work, a self-sustaining thought. Its will was its own shield. No, the Power's work would be on the outside.

It looked out at the universe. It saw the random paths of comets yet to be formed. It saw the slow, inexorable drift of cosmic radiation. It perceived the faint, grasping curiosity of other, lesser powers—powers that were hungry, or bored, or simply malicious. It saw the infinite, careless accidents that the universe produced as a matter of course. The basin, for all its internal perfection, was still a thing *in* a universe of chaos. It was a flawless pearl sitting on a railroad track.

So the Power became a gardner for the Gardeners. A watchman for the silent bastion.

It wove a shield, but not of energy or matter. It was a shield of *causality*. It gently, subtly, edited the future of the space around the mountain.

A meteor swarm that would have passed through this sector of space in a million years was nudged onto a new trajectory by the ghost of a gravity wave half a universe away.

A future star, fated to go nova and scour this hemisphere with gamma rays, would now find its stellar evolution subtly altered, its collapse delayed by a breath of interstellar hydrogen precisely steered into its path ten billion years prior.

The very concept of the mountain was made... uninteresting. The Power wove a tapestry of misdirection around it. Future explorers, scanning the planet from orbit, would have their sensors glitch just as they passed over the peak. Their geological surveys would register a worthless, unstable chunk of rock. Ambitious climbers would feel a sudden, inexplicable ennui as they contemplated the ascent, deciding instead on a different, more rewarding peak. The mountain was removed not from maps, but from aspirations.

The Power did not build a wall. It built an *absence*. A localized zone of cosmic indifference. It ensured that the universe would simply, quietly, and eternally, look the other way.

It was the Power's gift. It was a gesture of ultimate respect. It was the frame placed around a finished masterpiece, a velvet rope to keep the careless away. It was a silent, abiding patronage.

Then, its work done, the Power drew its attention away. It flowed back out into the river of time and space, leaving behind not a trace of its presence, only the effect of its passing: a perfect, protected stillness. A silence surrounding a Silence. A peace to guard a peace. In the grand symphony of What Is, it had found the single note of stasis and, instead of correcting it, had decreed that it should be held, sustained, and honored, for as long as the rest of the music played on.
