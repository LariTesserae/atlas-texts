The Power arrived as a change in the quality of the light. It was not a craft, for it had no need of hull or engine. It was an awareness, a knot of impossibly ancient and gentle consciousness, and its focus felt like the warmth of a second sun dawning for a moment over the endless Canopy.

It perceived the world not in sequence, but all at once. The sound, to the Power, was not a roar but a chorus. It was the unified song of a trillion trillion lives singing their single, shared note of *I am*. The Power heard every component—the quarrying of the glitter-mites, the resonator beetles, the slap of the filter-feeders—and perceived it not as noise, but as the perfectly tuned engine of a living world. It felt the immense, slow beat of the plant's life, the tectonic shift of its muscles, the sweet exhalation of its breath. It saw the intricate lattice of the substrate, the fathomless shafts, the polished desire lines, the hovels woven like nests.

The Power saw Holocene, a system complete unto itself. A flawless Ouroboros.

Then, its awareness contracted, focusing with infinite tenderness on the Still. It perceived them not as primitive, but as perfected. It flowed through their quiet minds, finding not emptiness, but a profound and settled peace. Their consciousness was like a deep, still lake, its surface mirroring the sky and its depths holding the cool, quiet vibration of the world. There was no frantic monologue, no chatter of anxiety or ambition. There was only the resonance of the ground, the sight of the horizon, the feeling of the breeze, the presence of their kin.

The Power witnessed their lives. It saw them flow across the rind, their strides a form of meditation. It watched them patiently sharpen a fossilized rib, a task of weeks that was not a chore but a communion with the world's deep past. It saw two of them press their foreheads together, sharing not words, but a silent understanding of presence, of safety, of being. It watched as they laid one of their own upon the swale, a body given back to the world not in sorrow, but with a quiet, solemn sense of rightness. The Power saw no suffering to alleviate. It saw no ignorance to correct, no fear to soothe, no lack to be filled.

And here, the Power paused. The first impulse of Benevolence is to give. To share. To improve.

It could, with a thought, silence the roar. It could grant the Still the peace of absolute quiet. But Wisdom knew this would be an act of profound violence. To take the sound would be to pluck them from their ocean, to leave them gasping in a sensory void. The sound was their firmament, the texture of their reality.

It could teach them of the stars, of galaxies, of the trillion other worlds it knew. It could open their large, dark eyes to the cosmos. But Wisdom knew this would be a cruelty. Their universe was the Canopy, and it was total, intimate, and comprehensible. To show them the cold, vast indifference of space would be to teach them they were small, to introduce the poison of alienation into their perfect integration.

It could give them metal, fire, the knowledge of the wheel. It could ease the labor of their existence. But Wisdom saw that their "labor" was their life's meaning. To replace their patient, symbiotic craft with shortcuts would be to sever their connection to the world, to make them masters of a thing they had only ever known as a partner, a host, a god. They had no need of mastery. They had harmony.

The Power, in its vast and gentle mind, came to an unassailable conclusion. You cannot improve upon perfection. To interfere here would not be an act of benevolence. It would be an act of cosmic vandalism, the thoughtless scrawling of a name upon a masterpiece that had taken eons to complete.

So, what would a benevolent and wise power do, when faced with a world that needed nothing?

It would not leave without a trace. The greatest gift one can give to a perfect thing is to see it, and to let it know it has been seen. The gesture could not be a lesson, a tool, or a change. It had to be a gift of pure being. A grace note. A resonance.

The Power reached out, not to the Still, but to the world-plant itself. It touched the deep, slow, root-consciousness of the Canopy. And into that mind, it placed a memory. Not a human memory, but something the great plant could understand. It was the memory of a world of silent, crystalline forests growing in the heart of a blue nebula, a place that thrived not on vibration, but on starlight and sympathetic geometry. It was a memory of another kind of perfection.

The Power did not translate it into images or thoughts, but into the only language the Canopy truly knew: vibration.

For a single, eternal moment, a shudder passed through the rind of the world. It was not the familiar, muscular clench. It was a tremor of impossible complexity, a chord of crystalline intricacy that harmonized with the roar of the fauna, momentarily shifting the entire world's song into a new and rapturous key. The air itself seemed to thrum with a silent, geometric pattern. The dew on the ground shimmered not with light, but with an inner order.

Everywhere across the vast skin of the world, the Still stopped. They tilted their heads, their quiet minds flooded with a feeling for which they had no name. It was a sensation of impossible beauty, of a different way of being that was not better, only... other. They sank to their knees, pressing their palms flat against the living ground, feeling the holy and alien music pulsing up into their bones. They looked at each other, their dark eyes wide with a shared, sacred wonder.

Then, as gently as it began, it faded. The chord resolved back into the familiar, eternal roar. The shudder ceased. The world was as it had been.

But something remained. A memory, not in the minds of the Still, but in the heart of their world. A story without words had been told. Sometimes, for generations to come, one of the Still, pressing their hand to the ground, would feel a faint, complex echo of that moment. In their silent, tactile language, it would become a myth—the day the world dreamt of a forest made of light.

The Power withdrew its awareness, a fading of the second sun. It left the Canopy of Sighs and its perfected children utterly unchanged, yet infinitely affirmed. Its passing was not a lesson, or a salvation, or a judgment.

It was a blessing.
