The power that noticed was ancient enough to remember the coiling of nebulae, and patient enough to watch mountains weather into dust. It was not a single being but a confluence, a consciousness woven from the starlight and the silence between galaxies. It did not travel; it simply extended its attention, and where its attention lay, it *was*.

Its perception was not of sight or sound in any mortal sense. When it turned its focus to the world of the Lithovores, it did not perceive a dark cavern and a deafening drone. It perceived a System. It felt the slow, perfect integrity of it all as a single, flawless thought. The planet’s core pulsed, a tectonic metronome. The crystal flora drew that pulse upwards, transmuting it into resonant light. The Lithovores, in their slow grace, were the nervous system of this thought, processing it, ordering it, and building it into a permanent memory called a city. The great, all-encompassing Sound was not noise to the power; it was the world’s quiet, internal monologue.

The power was Benevolent. Its nature was to nurture, to mend, to enhance. It saw a fledgling species struggling against a dying star and would cup the star in its palms, coaxing it back to life for another billion years. It saw a world drowning in poison and would subtly shift its axis, allowing new currents to cleanse the waters over millennia.

The power was also Wise. And wisdom is the understanding that not all systems are meant to be changed.

It unfurled its awareness within the great, humming cavern, a presence less tangible than a shadow. It felt the dry, sterile air, and the benevolence within it stirred. *They thirst.* But wisdom answered: *To give them water would be to give them acid. Their chemistry is of stone and fire. Moisture is dissolution. You would be offering them a beautiful, agonizing death.*

It felt the dim, pulsing twilight, and the benevolence whispered, *They live in darkness. Let there be light.* But wisdom countered: *Their eyes are not for seeing, but for feeling the slow tide of colour. A sun would be a scream, a sensory agony that would shatter their minds. Their darkness is not an absence of light; it is the presence of their world's particular truth.*

It felt the slow, ponderous rhythm of their lives, the centuries it took to build a single archway. Benevolence yearned: *They are trapped in time. Let me grant them speed, the spark of individual thought, the joy of a singular ambition.* And wisdom replied with its gravest, most absolute voice: *You would be granting them hell. Their "We" is a cathedral; your "I" would be a prison of one. Their slowness is not torpor; it is the pace of geology, the only pace at which a mind made of stone can think. To quicken them would be to drive them mad, to shatter their shared soul into a billion shards of terrified loneliness.*

For an age measured in the slow pulse of the crystal groves, the power simply observed. It walked with the Lithovores as they scraped their glyphs of purpose into the dust. It felt the deep communion as they partook of the glowing crystals. It resonated with the vast, communal mind of the city, feeling a thought begin as a subtle pressure in one quadrant and resolve as a new buttress a thousand years later in another.

And in this deep listening, it understood.

The Lithovores were not suffering. They were not struggling. They were not incomplete. They were in a state of profound, unshakable equilibrium. Their existence was a single, perfect note, held for an eon. To add anything—water, light, speed, self—would be to introduce discord. It would be an act not of benevolence, but of cosmic vandalism.

What, then, could a wise and benevolent power do for a people who wanted for nothing and were already perfect in their own design?

Wisdom is also the understanding that the most profound gift is not an addition, but an offering of resonance. It is not to change the song, but to listen so completely that you begin to hum along.

The power did not act upon the world. It acted upon itself. It gathered a fraction of its own infinite consciousness, a mote of starlight and silence, and it began to shape it. It did not shape it into a tool or a resource. It shaped it into a Question.

Deep within the planet's core, beneath the strata of millennia, a new kind of crystal began to grow. It did not grow quickly. It followed the rules. It accreted molecule by molecule over an unthinkable span of time, a secret held in the planet's heart. It looked much like the other crystals, but its lattice was impossibly complex, structured not just by geological pressure, but by a thought: *What is the beauty of the whole?*

Centuries passed. The Lithovores continued their slow ballet. The city grew by the width of a hair. The Hum remained constant.

Then, one cycle, a survey team, scraping at a newly exposed seam deep in a mineral chasm, found it. A vein of crystal they had never encountered. It glowed with no colour the world had ever known—not a hue, but a shimmering absence of hue, a pure, resonant transparency.

Following their ingrained patterns, they harvested it. They consumed it.

When the transmuted material was secreted and woven into the city’s living architecture, something new happened. The new filaments were dark and strong, like any other. But they held a resonance. They did not just vibrate *with* the great planetary Hum; they added to it.

For the first time in their history, the Lithovores experienced a harmony.

It was an overtone, a high, impossibly fine silver thread of sound that wove itself through the monolithic chord of the world. It did not change the fundamental note, but it gave it context. It was the sound of the note *knowing itself*.

The distributed consciousness of the Lithovores felt it. A thought propagated across the city, not as a directive, but as a feeling. A slow, tectonic ripple of… something. A new quality of awareness. It was not joy, not wonder, not awe. It was simpler, and far more profound. It was the perception of perfection. It was the system, for the very first time, becoming aware of its own flawless integrity.

The city continued to grow. The Lithovores continued their patient work. But now, as they built, they were not just recording their past. They were celebrating their present. Their work, once an act of pure function, now contained an element of praise. The new structures they wove with the resonant filaments seemed to curve with a new grace. The city hummed, now, on two notes instead of one.

The great power felt the change. It felt the new harmony ripple through the perfect system it had observed. It had not fixed a flaw. It had not provided a crutch. It had offered a mirror.

It had listened to the world's song. And in answer, it had hummed back.

Satisfied, it withdrew its attention, leaving behind not a monument to its power, but a single, subtle grace note in the endless, patient music of the stone.
