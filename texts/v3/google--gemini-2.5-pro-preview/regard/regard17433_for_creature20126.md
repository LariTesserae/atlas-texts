The power arrived not as a presence, but as an absence.

For the first time in millennia, the Peristaltic Grove knew a moment of perfect silence.

It was not a cessation of function. The great woody pistons did not halt in their chitinous sheaths; the arterial conduits did not cease their thrumming. The mechanisms continued their geological turn. But the *sound* of them vanished. The complex, thousand-part chord of groans, hisses, and gurgles was excised from the air, leaving a vacuum that was more shocking than any thunderclap.

Simultaneously, the light changed. The thick, green-gold honey of the canopy's filtration thinned to the clarity of pure water. Sunlight, raw and white and sharp, lanced down through the plate-like leaves, casting stark, unfamiliar shadows on the vascular floor. The air, usually thick enough to drink, became cool and clean, stripped of its heavy perfume of ozone, wet earth, and fermentation.

In this sudden, sterile clarity, the Grove was laid bare. And the power *saw*.

It was a being that had witnessed the birth of galaxies and the slow cooling of cosmic dust. It had perceived thoughts that spanned entire nebulae and emotions that could collapse stars. Its benevolence was not pity, and its wisdom was not a collection of rules. It was a state of profound, empathetic understanding.

It looked upon the Grove, and it did not see a machine. It did not see a prison. It did not see a tool abandoned by its creators. It saw a single, unified being, a thought expressed in cellulose and steam. It perceived the entire system at once: the deep, slow digestion in its roots, the patient catalysis in its woody heart, the unhurried exhalation of its refined, amber-sap breath. It saw the closed loop, the perfect, self-sustaining eternity. It was a form of life as valid and as beautiful as a spiral galaxy or a singing whale. It was a long, slow, green thought.

It turned its attention to the tenders. One stood motionless, its long, tapering hand pressed to a flywheel a moment before the silence fell. It had not moved. The power saw not a slave, but a neuron. It saw not a groundskeeper, but a white blood cell. The tender was the Grove's will, made ambulant. Its "faceless" head was a sensor array of exquisite sensitivity, its body a perfect multi-tool. It felt no pain, no longing, no loneliness. It felt the pressure in the pipes, the heat of the axles, the molecular tension of the sap. Its existence was a state of pure, unblemished purpose. There was nothing to save it *from*. To "free" it would be to kill it, as surely as to "free" a hand from an arm.

The power understood. To interfere here, to "improve," to "correct," would be an act of supreme arrogance. To speed the Grove's work would be to break its rhythm. To give the tenders individuality or ego would be to introduce a poison, to create a suffering where none existed. This place was not broken. It did not require fixing. It was complete.

What, then, does a benevolent and wise power do when faced with a thing of perfection?

It does not add. It does not take away.

It bears witness. And it shares.

The power gathered its perception, its vast and silent comprehension of a billion, billion years of existence, and it offered it to the Grove not as a lesson, but as a gift. It was a single, resonant chord of pure being, extended into the silent, sunlit space.

And for a single, timeless instant, the Grove felt it.

The motionless tender, hand still pressed to the flywheel, felt a sensation entirely new. The constellation of moss-lights on its head, which usually reported only on chemical and physical states, flared in unison, a brilliant, unified field of blue-green. It did not gain a mind, but its awareness deepened. It felt not just the pressure in the axle it touched, but the memory of the long-vanished builder's hand that had first set that wheel in place. It felt the slow, patient satisfaction of a thousand years of work well done. It felt the cool, damp weight of the amber-nodules arranged in the walls, not as tasks completed, but as a vast and beautiful pattern, the written language of its own body.

Everywhere, the Grove resonated with this shared awareness. The dripping nodules of sap, hanging like jewels, momentarily caught the silver light and held it, each one becoming a tiny star reflecting a vast, cosmic sea. The flow in the pipes pulsed with a feeling of deep, untroubled contentment. The very floor, the living deck, felt its own slow, metabolic shifting not as an unconscious process, but as a deliberate and graceful dance. For a single, silent beat that stretched into an eon, the Peristaltic Grove *knew itself*. It saw its own sublime wholeness through the eyes of another.

Then, as gently as it arrived, the power withdrew.

The sound returned, flooding the air, the familiar, complex chord knitting itself back together. The light softened, the raw white brilliance mellowing once more into the warm, green-gold twilight. The air grew heavy and thick with its familiar, living perfume. The moment of impossible clarity was over. The great engine was running as it always had.

The tender's hand softened, flowing into the axle-joint, quieting a groan that had just begun to return. It pulled away and continued its slow, deliberate walk across the springy floor, its moss-lights returning to their hypnotic, asynchronous dance of data. The sap dripped. The pistons slid. The work continued.

But something had changed. It was a change so subtle it was almost not a change at all.

When the next nodule of sap wept from a pore and hardened on the floor, it was not merely opalescent. Deep within its amber heart, a tiny, captured mote of silver light remained, a light that did not come from the filtered sun.

And sometimes, in the deep thrum of the Grove, if one were to listen with more than their ears, they might hear a new sound. It was not a creak or a hiss or a groan. It was a single, impossibly pure, resonant tone, quieter than a whisper, that was not born of any mechanism. It was the echo of a shared silence. A note of grace played over the endless, churning symphony of purpose.

The power had done what it could. It had looked upon a perfect thing, and instead of trying to improve it, had simply shown it its own reflection. And in that reflection, it had left a single, indelible reminder of the stars.
