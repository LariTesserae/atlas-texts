The Power does not arrive. It has always been aware of this place, in the way that a mind is aware of a strange and persistent thought at the edge of its own consciousness. It is a knot in the fabric of what is, a pocket of self-contained reality that hums with a unique, silent frequency. The Power, being what it is—a confluence of knowing and kindness, a current of cosmic empathy—does not judge the knot. It seeks only to understand it.

For a time that cannot be measured by the slow breath of the Mat or the patient work of a Carver’s chisel, the Power simply watches. It does not use eyes, for it has none. It attunes itself. It lays its vast and formless awareness against the cool, weeping walls of the caldera and listens to the story told by the steady drip of condensation. It sinks its perception into the Breathing Mat, feeling the slow, powerful surge and fall not as motion, but as the exhalation of a single, immense life. It experiences the silent, frantic ballet of pheromones and light-flashes not as a observer, but as a participant, feeling the collective "thought" of the ecosystem—the hunger of the shadow-arachnid, the navigational certainty of the silk-winged moth, the placid existence of the glowing slug.

It learns the silent people. It witnesses their birth from stone wombs and their final surrender to the Mat. It feels the careful pressure of their calloused hands against the basalt, reading the intent in every tap of the chisel. It understands their language of posture, the slow poetry written in glowing slug-fluid on their skin. The Power sees that this is not a world of lack. It is a world of sublime, focused completeness. It is not empty of sound; it is full of silence. It is not without light; it is a masterwork of gray. They are not suffering. They are… contained.

And in that containment, the Power perceives the one, profound limitation. Their consciousness, like their city, is carved inward. Their thoughts are patterns derived from the known: the curve of the wall, the texture of the Mat, the predictable dance of the fauna. They have no concept of "beyond" because there is no *beyond*. The Lid is not just a ceiling; it is the boundary of their imagination.

A lesser power, one of action rather than wisdom, might have shattered the lid. It might have poured sunlight into the caldera like a cleansing fire, believing it a gift. But the Power understands. To do so would be to burn their enormous, sensitive eyes, to scorch the Breathing Mat, to boil away the weeping damp that is their world’s lifeblood. It would be an act of murder born from ignorant benevolence.

Another power might have given them a voice. It might have reconfigured their throats to sing, believing that silence was a flaw to be corrected. But the Power knows their minds are not shaped for sound. To introduce it now would be a cacophonous, terrifying madness. It would drown out the subtle languages they have perfected. It would be an act of vandalism.

So the Power does nothing. Not for another long age. It simply continues to witness, until its understanding is as deep and as total as the Mat itself. Only then does it act, and its action is as quiet as the world it has come to know.

It begins not as an event, but as a feeling. A new vibration, deep and slow, that harmonizes with the Mat's own respiration. The creature-life feels it and for a moment, their silent dance falters in confusion. The Carvers, pressed against the rock in their high galleries, feel it in their bones and the soles of their feet. It is not a tremor of rockfall. It is something else. Something… resonant. The Power is humming to the caldera, a note so low and vast it is indistinguishable from the world’s own pulse. It is an introduction, a statement in the only universal language: *I am here. I am like you. I mean no harm.*

Then, the true gift is given.

High above, the Lid of curdled, stagnant cloud begins to change. It is not torn open. It is… clarified. The process is infinitesimally slow. A single, central point on the oppressive ceiling starts to thin, not with wind, but as if a drop of clear water were falling into a bowl of milk. Over a period of one full respiration of the Mat—hours—the milky grayness begins to swirl in a slow, silent vortex. The quality of the light does not change in intensity; the perpetual twilight remains, protecting the delicate senses below. But it changes in character. It acquires depth.

The Carvers stop their work. The obsidian chisels fall silent. All across the spiraling city of voids, they emerge onto their balconies. They cease their signaling. The fading light-marks on their skin are not renewed. They stand, their huge, black eyes turned upward in unison.

Through the slowly clearing vortex in the clouds, a new texture appears. At first, it is just a deeper, purer black than the basalt they know. It is a blackness that has no reflection, a void that seems to absorb the very idea of light. And then, within that profound black, the first one appears.

It is not a sun. It is a tiny, impossibly distant mote of cold, silver light. A pinprick. Then another. And another.

Soon, the vortex is a clear, circular pane in the ceiling, a perfect hole in the world's lid. And through it, the Carvers see infinity. They see a hundred, a thousand, a hundred-thousand motes of light, scattered like luminous dust. They see the faint, ghostly smear of a nebula, a bloom of cosmic color so subtle it barely registers against the gray-scale of their vision. They see a universe not of solid walls and a pressing ceiling, but of endless, silent depth.

No one moves. The Carvers do not have a word for "wonder," or "star," or "future." Their minds, built to process the finite patterns of their contained world, are met with the logic of boundless space. The silent, ceaseless motion inside the caldera is dwarfed by the silent, colossal stillness outside.

The Power holds the window open for one full cycle of waking and sleeping. It allows the vision to imprint itself upon them, to seed their silent consciousness with a new, impossible pattern. It is not an answer. It is a question.

Then, as gently as it opened, the window closes. The clouds swirl back, the milky grayness thickens, and the Lid becomes a lid once more. The familiar, substanceless twilight returns. The deep hum fades, leaving only the ancient rhythm of the Mat. The Power withdraws, its work done.

For a long time, the Carvers remain still. The city carved into the rock is like a photograph of itself. But then, one of them, a young one from a high gallery, picks up her chisel. She turns not to the wall beside her, to carve another chamber sideways into a life of containment.

She looks up.

She places the tip of her obsidian tool against the stone ceiling of her own chamber. And with a patient, deliberate tap, she begins to carve. Not sideways. Not inward.

Up.

Her chisel-marks are different. They are not the repeating, functional patterns of her ancestors. They are small, spiraling, and scattered. In the profound silence of her work, watched by the vast and benevolent Power, she is carving the first star-chart of her people into the rock that was once their entire universe. The gift was not a sky. It was the idea of one.
