The power that noticed this place is not a being of flesh or light, but a principle of awareness. It has no name, for it has never needed one. It is a consciousness born in the silent, empty spaces between galaxies, a witness to the slow waltz of creation and entropy. Its benevolence is a function of its vastness; it sees all systems of existence as intricate and precious, from the brief, fiery life of a star to the complex dance of proteins in a cell. Its wisdom is the accumulated understanding from eons of silent observation.

It does not arrive. It is simply, suddenly, there, as a lens of perception focusing on the plateau.

Its first sensation is not of sight or sound, but of *completeness*. It perceives the system not as discrete parts, but as a single, unified circuit. It feels the deep, geothermal heartbeat, the slow siphon of the Filter Stacks, the conversion of heat into nutrient, nutrient into life, and life, in its quiet, methodical turn, maintaining the circuit. It experiences the dense, humid air not as an obstacle, but as a conductor, a medium that binds every element of the whole in a constant, tactile intimacy. The hum of the Stacks is, to this power, the world’s fundamental tone, a mantra of absolute physical law translated into biology.

It perceives the Chanters. It does so without projecting humanity or intent onto them. It sees them as the system's own self-awareness, a distributed, functional consciousness whose thoughts are actions. The repair of a Stack is a thought. The carving of a slurry channel is a sentence. They are the living nervous system of a geologic body.

The power asks the question it always asks: *Is there suffering?*

It filters through the life of the plateau, seeking the vibrations of pain, fear, want, or despair. It touches the slow, patient minds of the six-legged grazers as they scoop their nourishment. There is no hunger, only the satiation of a need perfectly met. Their lives are a simple loop of consumption and rest, and there is no friction in the loop. It feels the brief, sharp end of a grazer caught by a whip-like hunter; there is a flare of neurological panic, but it is as brief and impersonal as an electrical short. It is not cruelty, but a necessary transfer of energy. The emotion does not linger. It does not become trauma. It is simply part of the flow.

It watches the Chanter, a being poised between machine and life. Here, it pauses. Is there a burden in this existence? To be compelled by instinct to forever maintain the machine? The power sinks its awareness into the Chanter as it sings its resonant note.

And it finds not a burden, but a state of perfect, resonant bliss.

In the moment of singing the flaw out of existence, the Chanter experiences a unity so total it defies the concept of self. Its entire being becomes a bridge between the dissonance of the flaw and the harmony of the whole. It is an instrument, playing the most important note in the universe. This is its teleology, its rapture. When it drinks from the slurry afterwards, it is not just feeding; it is receiving a sacrament, a physical communion with the very system it serves. The Chanters, the power realizes, are not slaves to their world. They are its mystics, its high priests, achieving enlightenment through an act of selfless, focused service.

The first conclusion of wisdom is reached: *Do no harm.* To interfere here would be a profound act of violence. To give the Chanters individuality, self-reflection, a "soul" in the human sense, would be to rip them from their state of grace. It would be to introduce the dissonance they exist to quell. It would be like teaching a string on a perfectly tuned harp to worry about its own identity, causing it to loosen, to fall out of tune, and to ruin the chord.

So, will it do nothing?

Benevolence is not passive. Benevolence seeks to give, but wisdom dictates that the gift must honor the nature of the receiver. The power cannot add anything *to* the system, for the system is complete. But perhaps... it can add something *for* the system. Something that celebrates its perfection without altering its function.

The Chanters live in a state of perpetual present. Their lives have a rhythm, but no history. The harmony of a repaired Stack is a perfect, but fleeting, moment. The Chanter’s bliss is real, but it dissolves back into the universal hum, leaving no trace. The gift, then, will be memory. Not memory as a thought, but memory as a state of being.

The power gathers a mote of its own essence, an infinitesimal point of pure potential. It does not place it *on* the plateau. It weaves it *into* the source code of the place: the geothermal vents themselves.

It is a subtle change. A new law, as quiet and fundamental as the old ones.

From now on, when a Chanter sings its resonant song, something else happens. The unique frequency of that Chanter at that moment—a sonic signature of its being, its effort, its bliss—is imprinted upon the very steam that the Stack vents. The steam is no longer merely purified water. It now carries an echo, a resonant ghost of the act of healing.

This etherealised song, this ghost of a vibration, rises into the thick, super-saturated atmosphere. As the air weeps its thick droplets, the memory-imprinted steam condenses with it. It falls as dew upon the glossy leaves. It drips from the glowing moss. It rejoins the water that percolates through the humus.

The world’s blood now carries its own history.

The effect is not immediate. But over centuries, over millennia, the change saturates the world.

A Chanter, born centuries later, emerges from its Stack. As it glides through the jungle, it feels something new. It presses its head-lens to the trunk of a weaning tree, not to listen for a flaw, but simply as a part of its existence. Before, it would have felt the slow, steady pulse of the tree's life. Now, it feels that pulse, but beneath it, like a complex undertone, it feels the faintest echo of a thousand resonant songs, sung by ancestors it never knew. The memories of their moments of perfect harmony, carried by the water within the tree's flesh, now resonate within it.

When it goes to tune a Stack, its own song is no longer a solitary act. As it sings, the accumulated songs of the world sing with it. The water in its own translucent body, drawn from the slurry, is a library of ancient harmonies. Its work is not diminished, nor made easier. It is made deeper. It is no longer just a repair. It is a chorus. The Chanter is not just an individual note; it is a chord, playing its part in a symphony that now stretches back to the beginning.

The power, its act complete, withdraws its focus. It did not change the machine. It did not break the cycle. It gave no creature a mind it did not ask for, or a burden it could not bear.

It looked upon a world that was perfect and complete. And it listened to its song. And it gave that song an echo.
