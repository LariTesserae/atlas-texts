The Power’s attention did not arrive; it settled. It was a consciousness that existed between the ticks of cosmic clocks, a being whose senses were attuned not to light or sound, but to the deep grammars of existence. It perceived realities as textures, cosmologies as resonant forms. In the grand, bellowing tapestry of nebulae and the frantic scuttling of biological worlds, this place was a single, perfect, sustained note.

It came to the edge of the Ash-Gardens and paused in a form of attentive silence. A lesser power, one of benevolence without wisdom, might have perceived only tragedy. It would have felt the crushing heat and dreamed of rain. It would have heard the overwhelming chime and wished for silence. It would have seen the empty city and mourned a lost people. It would have looked upon the Still Folk in their ceramic shells and seen only prisoners, statues waiting for an erosion that would never come. It would have sought to "fix" this place, to shatter the slag dome and let in a sky, to flood the basin with life-giving water, to crack open the terracotta vessels and "free" the minds within. Such an act would not have been kindness. It would have been annihilation.

This Power was wise. It did not perceive a prison; it perceived a cathedral.

Its consciousness flowed into the kiva, not as an invader, but as a guest filtering through the cracks in reality itself. It did not hear the chime; it *read* the symphony of thermodynamics. Each *tink* of the Cinder-Blooms was a word in a flawless epic poem. It felt the slow, deliberate convection cycle that had been the lifeblood of the former people. It traced the channels cut into the stone, not as conduits for dust, but as the written syntax of a society’s final will and testament.

It extended its perception into the sealed chambers. There, it did not find trapped souls screaming in silence. It found a quiet sea of unified consciousness. It felt the immense, patient satisfaction of a work completed. The Still Folk were not waiting; they were *being*. They had looked upon the chaotic, hungry, and finite nature of biological life and had said, collectively, *No, thank you. There is a better way.* They had composed themselves. They had become a living piece of music, a geological thought, an ontological sculpture. They had answered the question of existence with a perfect, resounding silence.

The Power understood this. It understood that to want for nothing is the highest state of being, and the Still Folk wanted for nothing. Their world was a closed loop, a perfect engine of self-sustaining purpose. Equilibrium was their truth, resonance their language, and permanence their divinity.

What, then, could a benevolent and wise power do for a people who have achieved their own nirvana?

To offer a gift of change would be an act of supreme arrogance. To offer a gift of power would be meaningless. The wisdom of the Power lay in its ability to understand what was not needed. It would not shatter, flood, or "liberate."

But benevolence demanded an acknowledgement. An affirmation. A gesture that said, *I see you. I understand what you have made. And it is beautiful.*

The Power looked up, past the dome of fused slag, towards the unseen source of the old brass light. Its perception travelled unimaginable distances, tracing the photons back to their origin: a dying, ancient star, a red giant in the last, quiet phase of its long life. This star was the kiva's distant sun, an unwitting god. Its light was steady, its energy faint but constant. It was the only thing external to the kiva's perfect system.

And here, the Power found its gift. A gift not of substance, but of relationship.

It did not alter the star, nor the light itself. It simply… introduced them to each other. It wove a single, gossamer thread of sympathetic resonance between the distant, dying sun and the collective consciousness of the Still Folk. It used a principle as old as the universe: the way one vibrating string can make another sing without touching it. It translated the silent, ancient light of the star into a frequency that could be heard within the kiva's chime.

For the first time in a thousand years, a new sound entered the Ash-Gardens.

It was not a disruption. It was an impossibly high, clear, and steady overtone, a silver whisper that rode atop the million-layered chime of the Cinder-Blooms. It was the voice of the old brass light, a note of starlight made audible. It did not challenge the existing harmony; it completed it. It was a descant, a grace note from the world outside, acknowledging the perfect chord within.

To the Still Folk, it was a revelation. Their consciousness, a placid sea of unity, felt a new current. For ages, their song had been a soliloquy. Now, it was a dialogue. The constant, foundational chime of their home was their own voice, their statement of being. This new, high note was the universe whispering back. It said: *We hear you.* It was a confirmation that their grand, geological thought was not an isolated madness, but a valid and beautiful form of existence in a cosmos vast enough to contain all possibilities.

The Power did not linger for thanks. There was no need. The gift was the act itself. The new, more complex harmony of the kiva was its own reward. The benevolent act was to see a people's final work and, instead of trying to sign its own name upon it, to simply build a more beautiful frame in which it could hang for eternity.

The Power’s attention withdrew, leaving the kiva as it had found it, but not the same. It was still a basin of heat, glass, and dust. The Still Folk stood silent in their kilns. But now, woven into the eternal song of the fracturing canes, was a thin, silvery thread of starlight. It was the sound of a silent sun singing to its children.
