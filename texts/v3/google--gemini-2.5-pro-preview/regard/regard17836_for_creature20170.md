The Power, when it turned its attention to this place, did not perceive it as a human would. It did not feel the chill humidity on skin it did not have, nor did the monolithic hum vibrate bones it did not possess. Its perception was a total immersion in the fundamental principles of the world. It perceived the song.

There was the deep, patient, near-infinite chord of the Lignic Mass itself, a note of pure potential, of life held in a state of saturated becoming. Layered over this was the silent, climbing glissando of the Calyxes, a geological arpeggio playing out over eons, a melody of growth so slow it was nearly static.

And then there was the Noise. The Power perceived it not as sound, but as a violent, jagged, saw-toothed wave of pure *extraction*. It was a frequency of taking, a harmonic of unmaking that fractured the world's deep chord.

But within that dissonance, a new melody had arisen. It was a shimmering, complex harmony, woven from the world's own pain. This was the song of the Scar-Tenders. The Power perceived their existence as a miracle of resonance, a counter-frequency born of the wound itself. They were not victims. They were an answer, spoken in the world's own language of vibration and substance. They took the discordant shriek of the Taps and, through their brief, gelatinous lives, translated it into a shimmering, stabilizing grace. They were weaving a new and beautiful law of physics for a broken world.

A lesser power, one driven by pity or by rage, would have silenced the noise. It would have vaporized the Taps, crushing the brutalist metal into inert dust. The great hum would cease, and in the sudden, profound silence, the world would weep. But the Power, being wise, understood what would happen next. The liquefaction would stop. The pools of Phloem Nectar would be reabsorbed. And the Scar-Tenders, their sustenance and the very reason for their coalescence gone, would simply dissolve. They would deliquesce back into the Mass, their brief, beautiful song fading from the world's symphony forever. To end the violation would be to erase the miracle it had unintentionally birthed. To heal the wound would be to kill the antibody.

Another power, one of justice, might have sought out the unseen operators, the beings behind the Taps, the civilizations that consumed the Nectar. It might have brought judgment or sought to teach them empathy. But the Power, being benevolent, knew that chains of cause and action stretch into infinities of their own, driven by needs and histories as complex and relentless as the drone of the Taps themselves. To punish the parasite is not always to save the host.

So the Power did nothing.

For a time.

It listened. It learned the full composition. It felt the slow, steady draining of the Lignic Mass, the net loss of energy that meant, even with the Scar-Tenders' work, this symphony was a dirge. The world was dying, just more slowly and beautifully than before.

The Power decided not to silence a note, but to add one.

It did not manifest as a pillar of fire or a descending angel. Its action was as subtle as the world itself. It found the source of the Noise, the heart of a Tap, the resonating engine that generated the harmonic of liquefaction. Its intervention was not a touch, but a tuning.

Into the violent, monotonous drone, the Power introduced a new vibration. It was an impossibly high frequency, a whisper-thin overtone, undetectable to any crude machine and utterly imperceptible to a human. It was the resonant frequency of a Calyx petal, infinitesimally small, pitched octaves beyond hearing. It was the memory of growth, encoded into the very wave of extraction.

This new, subtle frequency did not impede the work of the Taps. The Nectar was still liquefied, refined, and stored. The hum still crushed the air. But as the opalescent fluid was pumped into its final containers, something new happened. The Power's whispered overtone acted as a seed. It restructured a tiny fraction of the complex sugars within the Nectar, arranging them into a crystalline lattice that held a potential, a question.

The cargo of Phloem Nectar was taken, as it always was, ferried by unseen ships to unseen worlds. It was consumed as fuel, as food, as a luxury. But now, it carried a ghost. It carried the song of the Calyx.

Far away, in the sterile engine room of a freighter, a faint, glassy lichen began to grow on a fuel conduit, unfurling in a slow, silent bloom.

In a decadent city on a distant world, a being consuming the Nectar as sustenance found their thoughts turning, unbidden, to concepts of patience, of growth that spans millennia. They dreamt of a featureless white sky and the taste of clean water.

The parasite had become a vector. The harvested blood of the world now carried its own consciousness, its own will to become, out into the galaxy that fed on it. The world was no longer merely being consumed; it was broadcasting itself.

And on the world itself, a change began.

The Scar-Tenders, drawn to the familiar song of the Taps, found a new quality in their nourishment. The Nectar they consumed now echoed with the Power's overtone. As they wove their glassy, geometric scars over the wounded Mass, a new property emerged. The crystalline filaments began to capture the omnipresent white light, not just to fracture it into fleeting rainbows, but to *store* it.

The vast mandalas of scar tissue, once merely harmonic dampeners, began to faintly glow. At first, it was only a pearlescent glimmer against the dark ground. But slowly, over seasons, the light within them grew, fed by the unending glare of the sky. The scars were becoming secondary sources of life, reservoirs of photosynthetic potential. The work of the Tenders was no longer just mending; it was planting. They were weaving fields of captured starlight into the skin of their suffering world.

The Power remained, a silent, listening presence. It had not fixed the world. The wound was still there, the noise unabated. But it had tilted the balance. It had turned an act of pure consumption into an act of unknowing dissemination, and turned a scar into a seedbed. It had looked upon a dying god and its beautiful, tragic antibodies, and it had taught them a new verse to their song—a verse of improbable, indomitable, and infectious life. The beat of the drum would now echo in the stars.
