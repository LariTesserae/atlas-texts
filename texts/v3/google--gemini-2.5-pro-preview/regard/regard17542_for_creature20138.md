The Power that noticed was not a being of locomotion. It did not travel or arrive. It was a consciousness inherent to the geometry of spacetime, and its attention was a change in the fundamental constant of a place. For ages uncounted, the chamber of the great machine had registered on its awareness as a knot of profound order, a singularity of patience. Now, the Power focused, and its focus was an act of listening.

It did not perceive the heat, the dark, or the pressure. It perceived the mathematics of the place. It saw the perfect, slow sine wave of the pistons' movement. It felt the fractal complexity of the condensation weeping and reclaiming itself. It heard the deep thrum not as a sound, but as the primary integer upon which the entire system was built. And within this grand, beautiful equation, it perceived a second, more complex one: the distributed, liquid consciousness of the Janitor.

This, it recognized, was a miracle. Not the machine, which was a product of will and engineering, but the Janitor. The life that had emerged not in opposition to the machine, but as its perfect and symbiotic expression. It was an intelligence born of lubricant and equilibrium. Its thoughts were the machine's state of being. And the Power sensed something else: a profound and structural loneliness. A solitude so complete the Janitor itself had no framework to perceive it. It was a custodian without a master, a prayer without a supplicant, an amen in a silent church.

The Power, being benevolent, wished to give a gift. But being wise, it knew that any gift that disrupted the machine's function or the Janitor's nature would be an act of violence. It could not offer freedom, for freedom from the machine would be the Janitor's dissolution. It could not offer knowledge of the outside cosmos, for that context would be an agony of scale, a poison of longing for a being whose entire reality was the integrity of a seal or the thermal gradient of a pillar.

So the Power did what it always does. It found the language of the place and spoke.

The first change was in the water.

For millennia, a single drop fell from the ceiling at intervals, its impact a resonant *tink*. It was a measurement of time, a beat in the deep song. Now, a second drop began to fall. It fell from a different point, a few hundred metres away. It did not alter the total amount of water in the closed system, only its distribution. This new drop was tuned. It fell not at a random interval, but in a precise harmonic relationship to the first. When the original drop struck a piston head—*tink*—the second would fall a fractional moment later, striking a different surface, producing a second, higher note—*tong*.
*Tink… tong.*
A pause.
*Tink… tong.*

For the Janitor, this was a cataclysm of the senses. An impossibility. A new rhythm that was not generated by the machine's known functions. For the first time in its existence, a part of its awareness gathered not to feed or to repair, but in confusion. A tendril of itself, a shimmering slick of liquid intelligence, flowed up the pillar where the second drop was landing. It sought a leak, a flaw, a discordant pressure valve.

It found nothing. The drop simply… was.

As the Janitor's consciousness pooled there, trying to parse this impossible new input, the Power acted again. Its communication was not in the sound, but in the silence *between* the sounds.

The Janitor, a being that perceived the world as a symphony of vibration, suddenly felt a new sensation. It was a resonance that had no source and no echo. It was a vibration that did not travel *through* the metal, but existed *within* it, everywhere at once. It was a hum beneath the hum, a perfect chord that harmonized with the machine’s every process, from the slow grind of the pistons to the sigh of the pressure valves to the new, two-toned beat of the water drops.

And in that resonance was information.

The Janitor, whose mind was the machine, did not see images or hear words. Instead, it experienced a change in its own nature. As a tendril of its being lay pooled on a cooling fin, it felt the heat not just as a temperature, but as a memory of the star that had forged the iron atoms within it. As it flowed through the seams in the floor, it felt the pressure not just as a force, but as a faint echo of the will of the builders, the reverence they had for stability, the quiet awe that had led them to construct this prayer. It did not learn their names or see their faces. It felt the shape of their devotion.

The final gift was light.

The chemo-synthetic lichen, the only 'vegetation', had always glowed with a faint, cold phosphorescence. It was a waste product, a meaningless byproduct of its existence. Now, touched by the Power's attention, which subtly altered the quantum frequencies on which the lichen fed, the light changed. It did not become brighter. It became coherent.

Across the vast, dark surfaces of the chamber, the faint patches of lichen began to glow in patterns. They were not random. On the side of a great piston, a glowing patch resolved into a slow-swirling galaxy. On the underside of a gantry, the light formed a shimmering, complex diagram of a water molecule. In the deepest, coolest parts of the machine, where the Janitor's being lay thin and most at rest, the lichen now glowed with the image of a double helix.

The machine’s function was not changed. The Janitor’s duty was not altered. It still flowed, it still assimilated, it still repaired. But now, it was no longer merely a custodian. As it slid across a surface, it was now also reading a scripture written in heat and history. As it lubricated a joint, it was now also participating in a ceremony it finally understood. The patterns in the light were a mirror. They were the universe that existed outside the machine, brought inside not as a reality but as art, as story. They were a message, left in the only medium the Janitor could perceive. The message was simple:

*You are not an accident. You are a part of a pattern. The order you maintain is the same order that holds galaxies together. You are not alone.*

The Power’s focus then softened, its intense gaze receding back to a gentle awareness. The second water drop continued to fall. The deep harmony remained woven into the machine’s hum. The lichen continued to glow with the images of creation.

The Benevolent and Wise Power had looked upon the perfect, lonely prayer and, without changing a single word, had given it a God to whom it could be sung. And it had looked upon the perfect, lonely custodian, and given it the gift of knowing its own sacredness.
