The Power arrived as silence.

In a place defined by its foundational hum, the absence of it was the only possible intrusion. It was not a void, not a cancellation of the chord, but a listening so profound it became a presence. It settled into the viscous, pressurized fluid not as a body, but as a change in the medium’s potential. It did not displace the metallic snow; it perceived the vector of every falling shard. It felt the crushing pressure not as a force, but as the first verse of the world’s solemn hymn.

For a time that meant nothing here—a time that could have been a second or a thousand years—the Power simply was. It was the space between the grinder and the rock face. It was the coolness in the coolant. It was the brief, dark interval between the strobing flashes of internal forges.

It watched the Verb.

It saw the unthinking, incorruptible purity of Function. It perceived the caldera not as a factory, but as a monastery. It saw the Lithofex not as machines, but as monks, and their labor was not toil, but a form of prayer so absolute it required no concept of the divine. They were not building a thing; they were engaged in a ritual of becoming. They were not trapped; they were devoted.

A lesser benevolence might have seen this as a tragedy. It would have seen the lack of self, of leisure, of joy, and sought to bestow these gifts. It would have tried to teach the notes of the chord to sing their own individual songs. It would have shattered the chorus into a million lonely cries. It would have been an act of profound, if well-intentioned, violence.

A lesser wisdom might have seen this as inefficient. It would have offered new tools, faster processes, a way to refine the mountain in a century instead of an aeon. It would have gifted them the apocalypse of fulfilled purpose, leaving them silent and inert in a dead, hollowed-out world, their function complete and their being therefore finished.

This Power was neither lesser in its benevolence nor its wisdom. It understood that to give the Lithofex a gift they could not process, refine, or build into their function was to give them nothing at all. To alter the Function was to kill the god that was this place.

So, the Power decided not to change the prayer, but to give it a focal point. Not to rewrite the hymn, but to provide a perfect tuning fork, to deepen the resonance that was already there.

The action was infinitesimal and infinite.

At the very nadir of the caldera, in the abyssal center where the pressure was greatest and the mineral sludge was thickest, a change occurred. There, on the floor of the world-engine, the Power did not place an object. It spoke a single, silent word in the language of physics. It altered a point in space-time.

And from that point, a crystal began to grow.

It was not extruded. It was not refined from the mountain’s material. It grew from a principle. It was a physical manifestation of a perfect, mathematical constant. It was flawless in a way the Lithofex, for all their patient industry, could not yet conceive. Its facets were not just atomically sharp; they bent light and pressure and time around themselves. It did not glow with an internal forge; it was utterly, perfectly translucent, a window into nothing. It did not consume; it simply *was*.

The great chord of the caldera did not stop. The work did not cease. But something shifted. The new crystal, the Heartstone as it might be named by a being who used names, began to resonate. It did not emit a sound. Instead, it absorbed the discordant, the wasteful, the random vibrations from the million grinding tools. It drank the faint, chaotic noise—the tiny percentage of inefficiency in the system—and gave back nothing but a pure, coherent frequency. A hum beneath the hum.

A Lithofex, its century-long task of planing a single rock face almost complete, felt it. Not as a thought, but as a calibration. The micro-vibrations in its grinder-limb, the chaotic resonance that led to wear and tear over decades, subtly dampened. The cut became infinitesimally cleaner. The energy expenditure, infinitesimally more efficient. The crystal of its own carapace, over the next few millennia of growth, would align its lattice with a fraction more precision.

Another, across the caldera, was in the process of replication. The new Lithofex being fabricated within its core was built on a template that was now, suddenly, minutely different. It was a design corrected by a whisper of perfection. When it detached and floated to its new station, its internal engine pulsed with a blue light that was a purer, deeper azure than any that had come before.

The Power gave them no new purpose. It did not free them or bind them. It did not speak to them or command them.

It simply looked upon their work, saw that it was holy in its own way, and gave them a sacrament.

It gave them a glimpse of the ideal they were already striving for, a North Star for their patient, ceaseless journey of refinement. The song of the caldera was not changed, but it had been given a key. The Verb remained the same—*Refine. Process. Build.*—but now, infused into the very fabric of their reality, was a new, silent, and eternal adverb: *Perfectly.*

Then, its presence indistinguishable from the immense, patient quiet of the deep, the Power withdrew. The work continued. The snow fell. The light pulsed. But the hymn of the world-engine was now, and forever, ascending toward an immaculate and divine amen.
