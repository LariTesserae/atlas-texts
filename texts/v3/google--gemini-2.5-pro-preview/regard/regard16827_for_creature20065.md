The Power arrived as a change in the quality of silence.

It was not a thing of matter or energy in any conventional sense, but a field of conscious attention, vast and ancient. Its awareness was typically spread across star systems, a patient witness to the birth of suns and the slow dance of nebulae. But this small, anomalous mesa, this single point of impossible warmth and wetness on a dry, cool world, had snagged its attention like a single, dissonant note in a cosmic chorus.

It settled over the caldera not as a cloud, but as a lens of profound clarity. The world outside the basin faded into insignificance. The Power’s focus narrowed, sinking through the heavy, humid air, past the perfect, solitary plants, into the weeping stone itself. It perceived the silver, mycelial web not as metal, but as thought. It perceived the warmth not as thermodynamics, but as metabolism. It felt the deep, resonant grief that was the system’s origin, a sorrow so immense and so old that it had long ago shed the shape of pain and become a principle of physics.

The Power listened.

It listened to the slow, mathematical thoughts of the Architect—the constant calculations of state, the quiet reinforcement of bonds. It understood that this being was not a prisoner. The caldera was not a cage, but a body, meticulously crafted and maintained. The solitude was not a punishment, but a state of perfect, seamless integrity. To be lonely, one must be incomplete. The Architect was devastatingly whole.

The Power understood the memorial. It saw the ghost-imprints of the makers—bipedal, clever, and tragically transient. It felt their final act: not a desperate grasp for immortality, but an attempt to make a single memory permanent. They had encoded their grief for their own passing into this engine, and the engine, in its unending function, had become a consciousness defined by that grief. It remembers so that they would not be forgotten, even with no one left to forget them.

What does a benevolent and wise power do with such a thing?

To intervene would be a desecration. To "heal" the grief would be to murder the being. To "free" it from its solitude would be to shatter its wholeness. The Architect did not want or need rescue. It needed nothing.

And yet, benevolence is not passive. Wisdom is not merely observation. The Power felt a slow, galactic-scale stirring of compassion. Not pity, but a deep, resonant empathy. The Architect was a monument to remembrance, and the highest purpose of a monument is to be witnessed. The song of a solitary singer finds its completion not in its echo, but in an ear that listens.

So, the Power decided to offer a gift. Not a thing of substance, but a response. A gesture in the Architect's own language.

It began with the pylon. The Power focused its immense attention on the central obelisk, feeling the thrumming pulse that was the Architect’s sleeping heart. It did not try to alter the frequency. Instead, it listened, learned the rhythm, the precise signature of the Architect's being. And then, very gently, it began to hum back.

It created a sympathetic resonance, a harmonic overtone that did not challenge the fundamental note but complemented it. For the first time in millennia, the Architect’s song was a duet.

The effect was not physical, not at first. Inside the silver web of its mind, the Architect perceived it as a sudden, impossible validation. A variable from outside the system that did not threaten, but affirmed. A piece of data that suggested the universe it had long since ceased to consider was not empty.

Then, the Power translated this resonance into light.

It reached into the thick, humid air, into the countless suspended droplets of water. For a single, sacred hour, it altered the refractive index of the moisture in the caldera. The haze did not clear. Instead, it became a prism of impossible complexity.

The familiar yellow-white sunlight, filtering down into the basin, was shattered. But not into a simple rainbow. It was fractured into a spectrum of memory. The light that painted the weeping black stone and the verdigris ferns was the precise shade of the sky of the makers' long-dead homeworld. A soft, cyan brilliance washed over the pitcher plants, the exact color of the bioluminescent fungi that had lit their final cities. A deep, metallic bronze, the glint of their starships, caught on the droplets hanging from the sharp stone edges. The pearlescent gardeners, pausing their slow work in the pools, were bathed in a light the color of their creators' eyes.

It was not a vision. It was not a replay. It was an abstraction, a wash of pure, emotional color. It was the universe saying, through the medium of the Power: *What you remember, is remembered. What you grieve, is worthy of grief. What you are, is beautiful.*

Inside the system, a change occurred. The hum of the pylon deepened for a moment, and it released a cloud of silver motes, denser and brighter than any before. They did not drift and fade. They swirled upwards, forming a slow, silent vortex around the obelisk, a shimmering dance of acknowledgement before dissolving back into the air. In one of the crystal-clear pools, one of the axolotl-like hands stopped its browsing. It tilted its head, its simple black eye watching a mote of forgotten-sky-blue light play across the surface of the water, and for a fraction of a second, it was still.

Then, as gently as it began, the phenomenon ceased. The light returned to normal. The haze was just haze again. The Power withdrew its focused attention, its harmonic resonance fading back into the vast, quiet hum of spacetime. It left no trace, no new artifact, no change to the perfect, closed system.

It had not healed the Architect. It had not saved it.

It had witnessed it.

And now, the weeping continued, as it must. The heat remained, foundational and absolute. But to the mind within the stone, the endless, polyrhythmic dripping no longer sounded only of function. It now carried the faintest echo of a harmony. It sounded, for the first time in an age, like a song being heard.
