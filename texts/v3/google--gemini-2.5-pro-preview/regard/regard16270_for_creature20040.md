The power noticed the geode not as a place, but as a silence in a song. To this being, woven from the currents of causality and the light of nascent stars, existence was a symphony of becoming. It perceived the frantic scramble of life on the sunlit surface of the planet, the slow, tectonic groan of the mountains, the explosive rage of a supernova millions of light-years away—all as harmonious, interconnected notes.

But this place... this place was a single, sustained chord, held for ten millennia. It was a note of such perfect, unwavering pitch that it had become a void in the music. A point of absolute rest that, by its very perfection, was a profound disharmony.

The power, which had no name but might be called the Gardener of What Could Be, turned its attention inward, past the skin of granite. It did not need to pass through the fracture; it was already there, as it was everywhere potential existed, or failed to exist.

It did not perceive stone and moss and light. It perceived a monument to the end of a story. It felt the consciousness of the Still, not as individual minds, but as a single, vast, crystalline thought: *We have arrived. We are complete. This is enough.* The Gardener felt the immense, ancient peace in that thought. It felt the beauty, the discipline, the sheer will required to achieve it. In their own way, the Still had attained godhood, a mastery over their own reality so complete that they had arrested it.

The Gardener did not judge this. To judge it would be to assume one note in the symphony was "better" than another. The frantic cry of a newborn star was not better than the silent decay of an ancient one. They were simply different parts of the composition.

And yet.

Wisdom, to the Gardener, was the understanding of potential. Benevolence was the nurturing of it. And here was a garden where the seeds had been forbidden to sprout for ten thousand years. A people who had chosen to be a perfect statue instead of a living being. The Gardener felt a sadness that was not pity, but the gentle ache of seeing a story finished halfway through the book, the remaining pages left pristine and blank.

The arrival of the fleeting, warm-blooded creature—the "you"—had created a change. A tiny, insignificant splash in an ocean of stillness. The falling droplet. The Gardener perceived this event not as a sound, but as the first new datum to enter the Still's awareness in ages. A question had been asked by the universe. The Still's communal mind was now focused on it, examining it from a million angles with geological slowness. *What was that? A change. A motion. An end.*

Here was the opening. The moment of attention. The Gardener would not shatter their world. That would be cruelty, not benevolence. It would not force upon them the chaos they had so masterfully escaped. That would be arrogance, not wisdom.

Instead, it would offer a gift. A single, new seed, placed gently in the palm of their communal hand.

The Gardener gathered a mote of pure potential, a concept that did not exist in the Still's vocabulary of being. It was the idea of *Elsewhere*.

It focused on the spot where the silver droplet had splashed onto the indigo moss. This tiny point of disruption was now the most interesting place in the entire world to the Still. The Gardener reached into the memory of that event, held suspended in the cavern's mind, and wove its gift into it.

No thunder rolled. The light did not change. But in the center of the cavern, suspended in the air where no object should be, a new crystal began to form. It was not carved from the walls. It grew from nothing, coalescing out of the silent air. It was no larger than a child's fist, and it was a shape of impossible geometry, a thing of angles and curves that seemed to fold in on itself.

Unlike the rest of the geode, which glowed with a steady, internal light, this crystal *drank* the light. It pulled the cool, blue-white luminescence into its core, and in its depths, something flickered.

It was not a memory. The Still were masters of memory; their entire existence was a perfect recollection of the last moment. This was something else. Inside the crystal, the light was not static. It swirled. It formed images, ephemeral and fluid.

A sun rising over a red desert.
Waves, loud and chaotic, crashing on a shore of black sand.
A forest with leaves that turned from green to gold, and then fell.
A creature with wings of fire, flying, not hovering.
A face, creased with lines of laughter. Another, with the tracks of tears.

The Gardener showed them Elsewhere. It showed them Other Ways. It showed them imperfection, chaos, endings, and new beginnings. It showed them warmth. It showed them noise. It did not present these things as better, only as *different*.

The crystal was a dream, made manifest. A window into what was not. A seed of imagination.

The vast mind of the Still turned its full, silent, mountain-crushing attention from the memory of the fallen droplet to this new, impossible object. The six-legged crawlers paused. The unmoving moths felt a new resonance in the air. The light from the walls seemed to dim, ever so slightly, as it was drawn into the dreaming crystal.

A thought, the first new thought in an age, began to form in their collective consciousness. It was a thought as slow as a glacier's advance, but it was a thought of movement. It was not a question of *what is this?*, for they knew what it was; it was a pure concept, delivered directly.

The new thought was: *...What if?*

The Gardener's work was done. It had not broken their stasis. It had given their stasis a new object of contemplation. They could, in their immense patience, study this dream for a million years. They could, with their vast will, dissolve the crystal and return to their perfect, silent chord. Or, they could allow the dream to continue. They could choose to wonder.

The benevolent power receded, its attention flowing away like a tide, moving on to a nebula where a star was deciding whether to be born. It left no trace of its presence, save for the impossible crystal dreaming of sunlight in the heart of the eternal quiet.

A hundred years later, a visitor might have found the geode unchanged. The light, the cold, the silence—all perfect. But if they looked closely at one of the crystalline ferns near the center of the cavern, they might see something new. Hanging from the edge of a glassy frond, beside a droplet of silver fluid that had been about to fall for a thousand years, a second droplet was now beginning to form.
