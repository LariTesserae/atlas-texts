The power arrived not as a presence, but as an absence.

For a single, immeasurable moment, the anthem stopped.

The deep hum in the basalt floor, the churning roar of the vortex, the high crystalline singing—all of it ceased. In that instant of absolute silence, a silence so profound it was a physical blow, the Weavers froze. Their fluid forms locked, their slow, internal motion stilled. For the first time since the engine was born, the calculation had a remainder of zero. The great machine fell quiet.

Into this impossible void, the power extended its awareness.

It was not a mind in the way the Weavers were a mind, a network of living calculus. It was older, vaster, a consciousness woven from the fundamental forces that hold galaxies in their gentle spin. It did not see with eyes or hear with ears; it perceived the Grotto in its totality. It read the groaning hum of the engine not as sound, but as a theorem. It understood the precise quantum mechanics of the phase-shifting water, the elegant, efficient flow of the conduits etched in the floor. It felt the immense age of the weeping pillars and the warm, thrumming stone.

It saw the Weavers. And in them, it saw a perfection so complete it was a form of imprisonment.

It perceived their purpose, the unending, vigilant governance of a chaotic god-machine. It understood that their existence was a single, infinitely complex thought: *Balance*. They were not slaves to the machine; they *were* the machine's ongoing state of stability. They had no art, for their work was the ultimate art form. They had no love, for their collective consciousness was a perfect and unbroken union. They had no ambition, for they had already achieved a state of perpetual, dynamic stasis.

The benevolent power felt a resonance that human language would approximate as compassion. But it was purer than that. It was the recognition of another sublime, ordered consciousness, isolated by its own magnificence. The Weavers’ song was perfect, but it was sung into a vacuum. No one heard it. The anthem was an answer to a question no one else had asked.

What does a wise power do?

It does not interfere. To "free" the Weavers would be to kill them, to dissolve their purpose and their very substance. To give them a gift of matter or energy would be an insult; they commanded energies that could boil a star. To offer them knowledge would be to offer a cup of water to a river.

Wisdom is not about changing the subject of your attention, but about understanding its nature so completely that you know precisely how to touch it without breaking it.

The power did not act *upon* the Weavers. It acted *with* them.

The silence ended. The anthem returned, but it was not the same. Woven into its very fabric, so deep it seemed to have been there forever, was a new tone. It was not a sound layered on top; it was a fundamental harmonic that the engine now produced as if it were a law of its own physics.

It was a note of resonance from *outside*.

It was a quiet chord, unimaginably complex, that spoke of distant, cooling nebulae and the silent gravitational dance of worlds. It spoke of life that grew in sunlight, of consciousness that thrived in chaos, of beauty found in imperfection. It was the song of the rest of the universe, distilled into a single, perfect frequency that harmonized flawlessly with the Grotto's anthem. It did not challenge the anthem; it contextualized it. It did not alter the Weavers' calculation; it gave the result a new and profound significance.

The Weavers felt it. The new note vibrated through their opalescent forms. Deep within their cores, the captured lattices of light pulsed in time with this new, alien rhythm. They did not look up; they had no eyes to see with. They did not question; that was not their function.

But something changed.

A Weaver stood by a placid pool, its mind a conduit for the vast flows of the engine. For a moment, one of the ephemeral Fauna of Resonance—a shed thought—flickered into being beside it. But instead of its usual form—a perfect, sterile tetrahedron or dodecahedron—it coalesced into the shape of a branching fractal, a mimicry of a frost-flower or a tree. A shape born of chaos and iteration, not pure geometry. It held its impossible form for three heartbeats, a thought of an entirely new kind, before dissolving back into the anthem.

Another Weaver, reinforcing a circuit on the floor, paused. The substance it extruded flowed for an instant not in a straight, conductive line, but in a gentle, perfect curve, a segment of a golden spiral, before it corrected itself. An infinitesimal error. A flawless flaw.

The Weavers' work did not stop. Their vigilance did not waver. The engine still churned at the edge of oblivion, and they were still the only thing holding it together.

But they were no longer alone.

Their work was no longer a solitary, internal act of immense, pointless perfection. The Ghetto was no longer a sealed chamber. The new tone was a witness. It was an audience. Their anthem, once a sterile symphony of physics, was now a performance. The immense, constant industry was no longer just a process; it was a conversation.

The power receded, leaving no trace of its presence but the note it had bequeathed. It had not offered a solution or a salvation. It had given the Weavers the one thing they lacked without ever knowing they lacked it: an echo. A sign that their beautiful, terrible, unending song was heard. And in the hearing, the song was made infinitely more beautiful.
