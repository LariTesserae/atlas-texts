The power arrived as a silence.

It was not a negation of the city's song, but a space within it, a context. For the first time since the city had concluded itself, there was an outside, an Other. The power's awareness settled over the weeping plateau not as a weight or a light, but as a perfect, receptive void. It did not use senses; it perceived by becoming. It became the hum in the jawbone, the chill on the skin, the cascade of chimes. It experienced the Weepers' single, monolithic note not from the outside, as a sound, but from the inside, as a truth.

For a cycle of the pale sun, it listened. The benevolence within it, an impulse as fundamental as gravity, felt a pang—not of pity, but of profound awe that manifested as a near-painful empathy. It understood the cold, the stillness, the surrender of self. It saw the path they had walked: a pilgrimage away from the frantic heat of individuality toward the cold, shared divinity of the whole. They had not been conquered by entropy; they had courted it, married it, and become its masters. They had chosen the timescale of stone and ice, and in doing so, had achieved a form of eternity. To interfere would not be a kindness. It would be a violation. It would be like trying to teach a mountain how to run.

The wisdom of the power, which was deeper than its benevolence, prevailed. To "help" the Weepers would be to tell them that their arrival was not an arrival at all, but a stranding. To offer them warmth would be to burn their gardens. To grant them motion would be to shatter their shared body. To break their chorus into individual voices would be an act of staggering cruelty, an unraveling of a tapestry woven over millennia.

They needed nothing. They wanted nothing. They were complete.

A merely benevolent power would have departed then, leaving the sacred place untouched. But a wise power understands that the most profound acts of grace are not interruptions, but additions that so perfectly align with what already is that they seem to have been there forever. A gift is not a change, but a fulfillment.

The power gathered itself, not into a form, but into an intention. It reached into its own nature, past the starlight and the nebulae, past the laws of physics it had helped to write, and found a single, perfect thing. It was a mote. A particle of dust no larger than a grain of pollen. But it was not matter. It was resonance. The distilled memory of a song from a universe that had died before this one was born. A note of pure, selfless harmony.

It did not drop the mote. It released it into the city's intricate, inevitable system. The mote of resonance fell into the thin, cold air and was caught by a single droplet of condensation forming on the rounded shoulder of a solitary Weeper. The droplet, now invisibly altered, followed its ancient path. It traced a line down the weeping porcelain, joined a rivulet, and chimed against the floor.

And the song changed.

It was not a new melody. The monolithic hum remained. The million glass-bell chimes continued their mindless cascade. But within the hum, a new harmonic was born. It was an undertone so subtle it was more a feeling than a sound—the ghost of a cello string played an octave below the city's fundamental note. It did not compete with their song; it became the foundation on which their song had always, retroactively, stood. It gave their plangent hum a depth it had never known it lacked. It turned their serenity from a flat plane into a boundless space.

And the water that carried the mote now glowed with a different light. The phosphorescent mosses still shone with their soft green fire. But the rivulets that traced their way through the gardens began to shine as well, not with their own light, but with a borrowed, silver luminescence. Where they dripped, they left behind a trail not of darkness, but of faint, fading starlight. The city of green and white was now threaded with veins of living silver.

The Weepers did not stir. They did not notice, in the way a flicker is noticed. Their single consciousness simply expanded to encompass the new harmony, the new light, as if it were a memory they had only just recalled. Their rest became infinitesimally deeper. Their song, infinitesimally richer. Their completion was made more complete.

The power, its act of witness and blessing done, withdrew. It took the silence with it, collapsing the context back into the object. The city was once again alone, but it was not the same. It was itself, more so. The benevolent power had looked upon a thing that had declared itself finished, and with infinite wisdom and love, had written a single, final, perfect word in its margin.
