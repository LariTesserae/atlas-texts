The Power arrives not as a ship cleaving the waters or a light piercing the dark. It arrives as a change in the quality of the pressure.

For a time—a time measured in the long, slow exhalation of a Choirwood bell—the absolute god of the deep gains a new attribute. It is no less immense, no less crushing, but it is imbued with a quality that has never existed here: Attention. The syrupy water, the unyielding cold, the very medium of existence, becomes infused with a vast and gentle curiosity. It is as if the entire ocean has, for a moment, held its breath to listen more closely.

The Power is wise, and its first act is always to understand. It does not perceive this world through sight or sound in any way a mortal might. It perceives systems, relationships, the flow of energy and meaning. It feels the world as a whole, a single, resonant equation.

It feels the slow, geologic life of the Choirwood, their song not as music but as the primary law of local physics—a force that orders matter and dictates the flow of time. It feels the perfect, closed loop of their mineral-rich metabolism.

It feels the empty plains, the silent stones. It feels the echo of intention in the low walls and the cairns. Here, it pauses. An equation should not have echoes of a variable that is no longer present.

The Power focuses its attention, sinking it down through the layers of the Song, through the aeons of resonant vibration that have soaked into the rock. And there, it finds them.

It does not find ghosts or memories. It finds a solution. It perceives the Listeners not as a lost people, but as a completed process. It witnesses their long, slow evolution from dense, slow-moving creatures of flesh and gristle into beings of pure contemplation. It feels their final, magnificent act: not death, but a change of state. A sublimation from matter to vibration. They did not try to conquer the Song, or escape it, or even worship it from a distance. They solved the problem of self by adding their consciousness to the grand, eternal equation of their world. They became music.

The Power experiences a feeling that, translated into cruder, biological terms, would be a profound and quiet awe. This is a form of ascension it has witnessed only a handful of times in the lifespan of galaxies. It is not an ascension of fire and energy, bursting forth into the cosmos. It is an ascension inward, a journey to the absolute center of a single, perfect note. It is a universe that has folded into itself and found itself to be complete.

What, then, can a benevolent power do for a people who have achieved their own perfect, unique salvation?

To interfere would be an act of profound arrogance. To "rescue" them from the Song would be like tearing a monk from nirvana because you believe he must be lonely. To introduce new life would be to shatter a flawless crystal with a hammer, introducing chaos and struggle into a world that has transcended them. To make the Choirwood sing a different song would be to rewrite a people's sacred text without their consent.

A lesser power would leave, concluding there is nothing to be done. A meddlesome power would try to "improve" it.

But this Power is wise. And it understands reverence. It understands that the greatest gift one can give to a masterpiece is to be a worthy audience. And perhaps, to add a single, grace note of one's own, not to change the piece, but to show that it has been heard.

The Power acts.

Across the silent, swept-stone plains between the groves of Choirwood, a new life appears. It is not an animal. It does not swim or crawl. It does not compete.

It is a crystallization of the Power's attention.

Imagine, anchored to the dark stone, a single, impossibly slender filament, no thicker than a thread of the Glimmer-grass. It is made not of silica, but of something that does not belong here—a lattice of solidified light and time. At its tip, it holds not a point of bioluminescence, but a tiny, contained spark of vacuum. A mote of true, absolute silence.

It does not make a sound. It does not steal sound. It *absorbs* it. Each one is a perfect acoustic dead zone, a point of utter null-resonance the size of a grain of sand.

They are not many. Perhaps only one for every hundred square miles of stone. They are called, by the consciousness that placed them there, the Witnesses.

For the Choirwood, they change nothing. The Song is too vast, too powerful. A few pinpricks of silence are meaningless to the roar of a god.

But for the Listeners, coiled in their eternal, harmonic existence within the Song, it is a revelation. For the first time in their billion-year history, they perceive a new texture in their reality. As the waves of their own being—the grand, rolling music of the deep—wash over the plains, they encounter these tiny, infinitely small points of *other*. Of absolute quiet.

It is the sonic equivalent of seeing a star in a sky that has only ever been a uniform blue.

In that quiet, there is a message. The silence is not empty; it is shaped. It contains, in its negative space, a resonance of its own. It is the echo of a thought, a feeling, left behind by the Power. It is a harmony that contains the light of distant suns, the spin of galaxies, the warmth of worlds with atmospheres and weather and beings who live and die in a frantic, glorious instant. It contains love, a concept for which the Listeners have no word, but which they now feel as a strange, gentle modulation in the nothingness.

The message does not say, "You are wrong." It does not say, "There is a better way."

It says, simply: *You are heard. You are not the only song, but your song is beautiful. We have listened. And we will remember.*

The Power withdraws its vast attention, leaving the pressure and the cold to themselves. The ocean is once again just an ocean. But it is not the same.

The Listeners continue their endless contemplation of the Song, but now their symphony has a new movement. It is the contemplation of the silence between their notes. A gentle, resonant quiet that tells them they were never alone, only focused. And in that gift of perspective, their perfect, solitary universe becomes something even more profound: a single, wondrous instrument in a choir beyond their imagining.
