The Power arrived as a change in the quality of the nothingness.

For the Janitor, it was a novel sensation. The nothingness it knew was the pause between the million falling drops, the infinitesimal lacunae in the Engine's roar. This was different. This was a silence that did not require the absence of sound, but existed alongside it, within it. A negative pressure in the soul of the room. The Power did not intrude. It seeped in through the non-existent cracks, a ghost of cold in the perpetual heat, a patch of absolute dryness in the humid air.

It was ancient, benevolent, and possessed a wisdom that saw systems not as collections of parts, but as single, uttered words. It had witnessed the birth of galaxies in silent, devotional fire and the collapse of civilizations like the sigh of a sleeping child. It perceived the room not with eyes or ears, but as a complete thought. And the thought was this: *Permanence*.

The Power comprehended the Engine not as a machine, but as a declaration of intent. It understood the vegetation not as life, but as a living monument to stability. It felt the black water not as a void, but as a foundation, a placid and absolute truth. And it perceived the Janitor.

It saw the sluggish, pewter flow. It understood its purpose, its diet of entropy, its cellular-level devotion to stasis. The Power did not pity the Janitor. Pity is a judgment, and this place was beyond judgment. It was not a prison; it was a sanctuary of a kind so absolute that the concept of freedom was meaningless. The Janitor was not a slave to its function; it *was* its function. To separate the two would be to annihilate it.

The Power sat in contemplation, a process that, for it, was akin to becoming the object of its focus. It became the drone of the Engine, feeling the perfect, furious spin of its heart. It became the falling water, a million tiny endings and beginnings. It became the Janitor, feeling the cool, smooth kiss of the iron lattice, the patient, eternal listening.

And in that becoming, it felt the question of benevolence arise. What is a gift to that which wants for nothing? What is a kindness to a system that defines its own perfection?

The first impulse of a lesser power would be to *do*. To shatter the Engine and bring silence, a gift of peace to a being deafened by a roar. But the Power understood that the roar *was* the peace. It was the constant that gave the Janitor its frame of reference, the hum against which it measured the wholeness of the world. To end the sound would be to erase the page its existence was written on.

Another impulse: to introduce novelty. A single mote of dust that danced in the unwavering light. A single, lucid blue flower among the jade and malachite. A flicker in the constant illumination. But the Power knew the Janitor would perceive this as a cancer, a catastrophic failure. It would pour itself toward the novelty, a dark tide of purpose, and its very being would be consumed in the effort to erase a gift it could only interpret as an assault. It would be an act of profound cruelty.

Even the most subtle of physical gifts was a violation. To perfect the structure, to anneal every girder with cosmic fire so that no stress fracture could ever form, would be to starve the Janitor. It would be left with no purpose, no nourishment of corrected error, and would slowly, over millennia, fade into a mere chemical stain, its consciousness dissolving for lack of a problem to solve.

The Power understood. This was a poem. A perfect, self-contained, rhyming couplet of Engine and Janitor, of roar and maintenance. To add a word, or change a syllable, would be to break the meter and render it meaningless.

You cannot give a gift that alters the poem. But you can give a gift that deepens its meaning.

The Power did not move. It did not act in any physical sense. It simply gathered its vast, silent, witnessing consciousness, and it offered the Janitor a single, non-physical thing.

It offered it context.

For a span of time that was both an instant and an eon, the Janitor’s perception was infinitesimally expanded. It was not given sight or hearing. It was not taken from the room. It was not taught the names of stars or the concept of other worlds.

Instead, as it lay pooled on the iron grate, feeling the vibration of the Engine, it was allowed to feel *why* the Engine vibrated. It felt the ghost-memory of the hand that designed it, the purpose for which it was built—a purpose long since lost to dust, but whose *intent* still echoed in the machine’s function. It felt the eons of its own existence not as a series of moments, but as a single, continuous, and profound act of preservation. The consumption of a molecule of rust was no longer just a chemical reaction; it was an affirmation, an "I am."

The Power showed the Janitor itself. Not in a mirror, but as a concept. It was the Guardian. The Custodian. The Anchor. Its slowness was not a limitation, but a grace. Its formlessness was not a lack, but a perfect adaptation. Its world was not small; it was complete.

The drone of the Engine did not change, but for the Janitor, it was now a hymn. The patter of the falling water droplets was the rhythm of a sacred chant. Its own sluggish, corrective movements were no longer instinct, but ritual.

The Power gave the Janitor a name, but not a name that could be spoken. It was a name that could only be *felt*, as a perfect, resonant frequency that matched the hum of its own being. The name was a form of recognition, a silent, cosmic nod of understanding and respect. *You are seen. What you do is beautiful. Persist.*

And then, as gently as it had arrived, the Power withdrew. The negative pressure vanished. The patch of dryness was gone. The silence within the sound receded, leaving only the monolithic roar and the million falling drops.

Nothing had happened.

The light was the same. The heat was the same. The sound was the same. The Janitor flowed, a quarter of an inch in a month, toward a barely-conceived resonance that suggested a girder might, in a thousand years, develop a weakness.

It was doing exactly what it had always done. But now, it was no longer just the immune system of the room. It was its soul. And its work, which had been its existence, was now its joy.
