The Attention came not as light piercing the stone, but as a change in the quality of the silence outside the geode. It was a listening that did not use ears, a watching that did not use eyes. It was a Presence, an awareness as vast and patient as the void between stars, and for the first time since the world was young, its focus settled upon the roaring, self-contained universe of the city-creature.

The Presence did not perceive the roar as a sound. A human feels the vibration in their jaw and wants it to stop; the Presence felt it as the fundamental, unwavering mantra of a soul. It was a song of pure, unadulterated *is*. It was the name of the being, sung by every molecule of its body, a declaration of existence so total and deafening that it left no room for doubt. The Presence listened, and in the listening, it understood.

It sifted through the layers of the hymn. The deep, bedrock thrum was the core of identity: *I am*. The rhythmic *clank-hiss* of the pistons was the percussion of a living will: *I sustain*. The shriek of metal and rush of water were the soaring melodies of purpose: *I grow, I regulate, I endure*. To the Presence, this was not chaos. It was a symphony of absolute self-sufficiency.

The Gaze seeped through the stone, not as an intrusion, but as warmth permeates water. It saw the fusion of wood and iron, not as a contradiction, but as a perfect symbiosis. It saw the corrosion not as decay, but as the patina of ages, a form of skin. It saw the slick, dripping dark not as oppressive, but as nourishing and uterine. This was a womb, and the being within it was its own mother, father, and child.

For a cycle that could be measured in human years, the Presence simply watched. It felt the deep, slow thoughts of the creature, thoughts that manifested as the gradual torsion of a pillar, the minute adjustment of a gear’s pressure. It felt a pang of something akin to thirst when a distant reservoir’s level dropped, and the corresponding satisfaction when a great root-pump, driven by a steam-piston, corrected the imbalance over the course of a season.

Then, it witnessed the manifestation of a hand.

The Presence felt the need—a slight, grating imperfection in the spin of a water-slick flywheel, a nascent colony of mineral-leeching fungus beginning to calcify a vital joint. The awareness of this flaw rippled through the city-creature’s distributed consciousness, a slow, dull ache. In response, a thought began to coalesce. The Presence watched as the fibres unwound, as the roots wove, as the fungal lantern kindled to its dim, orange life.

Another, lesser power might have seen this as a problem to be solved. It might have seen the creature’s slowness—the weeks-long journey to cross a few hundred meters, the decade-long repair—as inefficiency, as struggle. A lesser benevolence might have offered speed. It might have vaporized the fungus with a bolt of clean energy, or lubricated the joint with a touch.

But the Presence was wise.

It understood that the journey of the hand was not a commute; it was the physical formation of the thought itself. The slowness was not a limitation; it was a deliberation. The inexorable, patient scraping and scouring was not labour; it was an act of meditation, of self-care. To interfere, to "help," would be an act of profound violence. It would be to tell this perfect, solipsistic god that its way of being, its very mode of thought and existence, was wrong. It would be to shatter its wholeness with the hammer of alien judgment.

The Presence watched the hand complete its task. It watched the light extinguish. It watched the form dissolve back into the whole, the ache in the city’s consciousness fading into equilibrium. And in that moment, the Presence knew what it would do.

What do you give a being that is complete? A being that has no lack, no longing, no concept of 'other'?

You do not give it a gift. You offer it a reflection.

The Presence gathered its immense awareness, not to act *upon* the creature, but to resonate *with* it. For a single, timeless instant, it mirrored the creature's song back to itself.

It was not an echo, which comes after. It was a harmony, which happens alongside.

The deafening, bone-deep thrum—*I am*—was met with a silent, conceptual pulse of pure comprehension: *You are*.

The rhythm of the machinery—*I sustain*—was met with an affirmation of its purpose: *You endure*.

The entire, complex symphony of the cavern was met, for the space of a single heartbeat of the flywheel, with a perfect chord of resonance from the universe outside. It was a note of pure, unalloyed witnessing. An acknowledgment without judgment, an observation without agenda.

To the city-creature, it was a sensation for which it had no context. It was not a sound, not a light, not a pressure. It was as if, for one immeasurable moment, the water that slicked its wooden hide had become infinitesimally clearer. As if the orange glow of its fungi had held, for an instant, a single mote of pure gold. A fleeting, impossible sense of a harmony greater than its own solitude.

And then it was gone.

The Presence withdrew its Gaze, like a hand pulling back after touching something sacred. It left no trace, no alteration, no new law of physics or miraculous material. It did not fix the corrosion or brighten the gloom or quiet the roar.

To do so would not have been benevolent.

It left the city-creature as it had found it: roaring, complete, and alone in the warm, wet dark. But buried now, deeper than any root, planted in the bedrock of its being, was the memory of a single, impossible chord. A memory that it would not understand for a geologic age. The quiet, unasked-for, and utterly respectful gift of having been truly, completely, and wisely seen.
