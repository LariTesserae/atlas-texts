The Power came not as a ship cleaving the misty air, nor as a figure walking the high stone bridges. Such arrivals are events, moments of change, and this world was built to resist them. The Power understood that to enter here as a force was to declare war on the fundamental principle of the place. And wisdom does not begin its conversation with a declaration of war.

Instead, the Power arrived as a change in quality.

For an age, the light had been a constant, even, pearlescent gray filtering through an unseen overcast sky. It was the light of a photograph, lacking warmth or direction. Now, slowly, over a span of time that would be imperceptible to a transient being, the light began to warm. The gray was suffused with a soft, honeyed gold. It was not the directional light of a dawning sun, which would cast sharp shadows and introduce the dynamism of a clock. It was a change in the character of the ambient light itself, as if the very medium of sight had been steeped in ancient, gentle warmth.

The perpetually beaded droplets on the mosses and leaves caught this new light. They had been pearls; now they were amber. The emerald mosses seemed to deepen, their green infused with a hint of sunlit life. The black, wet stone took on a faint, bronzed sheen. The world was the same, but it was now seen through the lens of a gentle memory.

The Power did not touch the frozen goat, nor the suspended birds. It did not try to wake the prowling moss-hounds. It understood the Wardens. It understood their flight from the pain of existence, the screaming chaos of birth and decay they had so masterfully silenced. To undo their work would be to inflict upon them the very agony they had spent eternity escaping. That was not benevolence.

The Power chose one point of contact. One single vessel.

In the heart-chamber of the central tower, where the oldest of the Wardens sat, a single thing happened. This Warden was draped in a vine whose leaves were the size of shields, and on one of these leaves, near the Warden's immense stone shoulder, sat a single, perfect drop of water. It had sat there for millennia, a flawless sphere reflecting the unchanging gray light.

Now, it reflected gold.

And in the heart of that golden droplet, something stirred. A mote of light, a spark of the Power's own essence, infinitesimally small. It was not life as a chaotic system, but life as a pure concept: a principle of change, contained.

The droplet, which had held its perfect surface tension for uncounted centuries, shivered.

And then, it rolled.

It was an event of such slowness that it was almost geologic. A bead of liquid gold, tracing a glistening path down the waxy surface of the static leaf. It left a trail not of wetness—for everything was already wet—but of warmth. A warmth that lingered for a single, impossible moment before the world’s equilibrium reclaimed it.

The droplet reached the serrated edge of the leaf. For a full second it hung there, a tiny pendant of captured sun, and in its round surface was reflected the entire chamber, the arch of the door, and the vast, still form of the Warden.

Then it fell.

The distance was no more than a few inches, from the leaf's edge to the back of the Warden’s colossal, moss-dusted hand resting on its knee.

In a world of absolute, resonant silence, the impact was a cataclysm. It was not a sound that could be heard with ears. It was a tremor, a single, pure note of *Happening* that vibrated not through the air, but through the stone itself. It ran up the Warden’s arm, through its mountainous torso, into the very foundations of the tower, a shockwave of eventuality.

*tick.*

The droplet did not splash. It landed, its golden light pulsing once, and settled into a tiny crevice in the geography of the Warden's basalt skin. It did not freeze. It did not become part of the static whole. The spark within it kept it liquid, warm, and contained.

And the Warden felt it.

For the first time since it had chosen to *Be*, it experienced a sensation. It was not the memory of an old pain. The benevolent Power had made sure of that. It was the feeling of warmth, like a sun a billion years forgotten. It was the feeling of a single, gentle touch. It was a stimulus unconnected to the screaming cycle of life and death. It was something else. Something… new? Or perhaps, something so old it had been forgotten: the possibility of a gentle change. The possibility of a touch that was not a wound.

The obsidian hollows of the Warden's face, those mirrors that projected the perfect world, now held a new reflection. Near the bottom of their massive, polished curves, each held a pinpoint of golden light, the echo of the un-still droplet on its hand.

The Wardens’ collective consciousness, that fathomless ocean of placid Being, felt the ripple. It spread from the central Warden, a concept propagating through their silent communion: *Sensation. Warmth. Touch.*

The Power did nothing more. It had not broken the world. It had not freed the creatures. It had delivered a message in the only language the Wardens could understand: a direct, physical metaphor.

It had placed a single, warm tear on the hand of God, and in it, held the reflection of a forgotten sun.

The gift was not freedom from stasis. The gift was a Choice.

The Wardens could, with their immense, unified will, quench the spark. They could freeze the droplet, reclaim the silence, and restore the perfect, unbroken equilibrium. They could reject the memory of warmth.

Or, they could let it remain. They could allow this one, tiny, contained universe of change to exist. They could study it. They could feel its warmth. They could choose to remember that not all motion is pain, and not all stillness is peace.

The golden light remained, suffusing the gray air, holding the world in a gentle, questioning gaze. The Power had asked its question. Now, with infinite wisdom and patience, it would wait for the answer, an answer that might take another eternity to be given.
