The power that noticed Ghyre did not arrive in a flash of celestial fire or with the heraldry of trumpets. It was a power that dealt in eons, and its attention settled upon the Under-Canopy as gently as a single mote of dust falling through an infinite void. It was an awareness, a Gaze that did not require eyes, and its nature was one of contemplative quietude. It had many names in many universes, but here, it was simply The Steward.

For a time—a time measured in the full cycle of the canopy’s growth and resorption, a local century—The Steward simply watched. It perceived the absolute integrity of the place. It felt the cold, slow pulse of the city-organism. It saw the perfect, frictionless integration of the Lacrimals into the whole. It understood that this was not a system in decay, nor a prison, nor a purgatory. It was a state of being, complete and self-contained.

The Steward saw no wound to heal, no hunger to sate, no tyranny to overthrow. It registered the cold as a component of life, not an absence of warmth. It registered the darkness as a condition for sight, not an absence of light. It understood that the Lacrimals’ lack of individuality was not a loss, but a different mode of consciousness, a vast, interconnected dream. To introduce strife, yearning, or ambition here would not be a gift, but a violation. It would be akin to teaching a river to thirst.

And so, the primary question for a power whose wisdom was predicated on non-interference was: What is a gift to one who wants for nothing? What is kindness to a being for whom suffering is not a known concept?

To change the environment would be destruction. To give them warmth would be to boil them in their own veins. To give them a sun would be to blind and burn the entire world. To give them speech would be to teach them the clumsy art of lying in a world of absolute, resonant truth. To give them individuality would be to inflict the terror of loneliness upon a mind that had only ever known unity.

The Steward, in its vast and patient understanding, concluded that the only gift that could be given was one that added without subtracting. A new colour for a painter who has only ever known one, but a colour that does not erase the original. A new note for a song that does not disrupt the harmony.

And so, The Steward acted.

It did not manifest. It did not speak. It chose as its medium the city itself, the very language of the Lacrimals: vibration.

One cycle, as the fungal lamps reached their dimmest ebb, a new tremor began. It was unlike any other. It was not the deep, groaning bass of a pillar settling. It was not the sharp, percussive tap of a water drop. It was not the rhythmic, complex report of a Lacrimal conversation.

It was a sustained, harmonic resonance.

It began at a frequency so low it was felt more than heard, a hum that seemed to originate from the very core of the city’s foundation. This hum was not brute force; it was impossibly complex, a chord woven from a thousand resonant threads. It was a vibration that carried information beyond the mere physical state of the structure. It was a vibration that told a story.

It was the memory of warmth.

Not the sensation, which would be agony, but the *idea* of it. The hum translated the concept of molecular excitement, of energy freely given from a distant star, into a pure, mathematical vibration that the Lacrimals could process through their sensitive skin. It was the story of a yellow sun.

Then, higher frequencies were layered upon the first. These were not the staccato sounds of Ghyre, but flowing, melodic lines that rose and fell like tides on a forgotten ocean. They carried the idea of *other colours*: the sharp, violent blue of an open sky, the soft, living green of a sun-fed leaf, the explosive red of a fleeting sunset. The Lacrimals had never seen these things, but through the language of vibration, the pure, conceptual form of them was laid bare in their minds.

The "song," as it might be called, lasted for one full turn of the fungal light. It was a symphony of physics, a ballad of thermodynamics, a poem of an outside that the Lacrimals could never reach and were not meant to. It was not a call to escape. It was simply a statement: *This is not all there is.*

When the vibration ceased, the silence that returned was different. It was the same cold, the same dripping, the same groaning wood, but it was now contextualized. It was a silence that held the echo of a song.

The effect on the Lacrimals was profound and subtle. They did not despair, for the song had contained no sense of lack. It was a gift of pure knowledge, not of longing. But their behaviour changed.

Their percussive conversations, once purely functional, began to carry echoes of the song. A message about a new buttress forming in the east district might now be tapped out not just with rhythm, but with a faint, ghostly attempt at harmony, a counter-point to the city’s natural sounds.

Their bioluminescence, once a simple semaphore of intent, became art. A Lacrimal, clinging to a high vault, might pulse its skin not with a message, but with a flowing pattern of light that mimicked the memory of a tide. Another, standing motionless on a rhizome plaza, would shimmer with slow, swirling patterns that were its interpretation of a galaxy, a concept now known to it. They began to tell stories to each other of the things the vibration had taught them, stories in cold, moving light.

The deepest change occurred in the elders. When they next went to merge with the new, growing shoots of the city, they did not just receive the city’s imperatives. For the first time, they gave something back. As they drew sustenance and information from the city’s lifeblood, they imprinted the memory of the Steward’s song into the living wood.

Slowly, over generations, Ghyre itself began to reflect the gift. A new liana causeway would spiral upwards not just with structural integrity, but with a breathtaking, mathematical grace that echoed the curve of a sound wave. The hexagonal patterns on the floor would, in a new district, form a vast, subtle mosaic that was a fractal representation of a snowflake—a thing of frozen water, yes, but born of an open sky. The city’s fungal lamps began to pulse not just with their biological rhythm, but with a faint, coordinated, and deeply harmonic cadence.

The Steward did not stay to watch these changes unfold. Its wisdom included knowing when a gift had been given and when the giver must depart. It had not fixed Ghyre. It had not saved it. It had simply looked upon a complete and perfect thing and offered it the one thing it lacked: a dream of another perfection.

It had given the city a soul, not by installing one, but by whispering to it of other souls. And in the cold, black-green heart of Ghyre, under the endless dripping, art was born.
