The power noticed the hall not as a place, but as a note.

It was a consciousness for which planets are beads on a string and the spaces between galaxies are meditative pauses. It did not see with eyes or hear with ears, but perceived existence as a symphony of interwoven causality and being. Most systems were simple tunes: a star’s fusion, a planet’s orbit, the predictable scramble of evolution on a sun-drenched world. They were beautiful, but familiar.

The hall, however, was a paradox that drew Its attention. It was a single, sustained chord of immense complexity. There was the deep, patient hum of the fired clay, a bass note of monumental stillness laid down aeons ago. Woven through it was the constant, stochastic percussion of the water, a rhythm of both chance and certainty. And over this, the frantic, high-frequency vibrato of the Striders, cut through by the profound, sub-bass punctuation of the Boomers.

The power listened. It felt the architecture of the place—a vessel crafted with intention, now empty of its creators. It felt the life that had filled the void—a perfectly closed loop of fear, hunger, and quiet industry. It was a system in flawless, brutal equilibrium. A song of survival, beautiful in its self-contained logic.

What would a benevolent power do?

To flood it with light would be to blind and burn creatures born of twilight. To silence the Boomers would be to grant the Striders a brief, swarming paradise before they devoured their world and starved in a carpet of their own dead. To remove the Striders would be to condemn the Boomers to a slow, silent fading. To teach them of the outside would be to give a fish the concept of a desert; a truth so alien it would be a form of madness.

To interfere with the mechanics of the song would be to break the instrument.

Wisdom, the power perceived, was not the act of imposing a "better" state, but of offering a deeper mode of being. The life in the hall existed, but it did not *know* that it existed. The Strider did not know its fear was shared by a billion ancestors. The Boomer did not know its patience was a mirror of the hall's own patient waiting. The Quiet One did not know its humble path was what held the light and the dark together.

The song was played, but the musicians could not hear it.

So the power acted, not upon the world, but within it. Its intervention was not a thunderclap or a beam of light, but a change in the nature of the water.

It reached into the high, unseen darkness of the vault, to the interface where physics met clay, where the cool air yielded its wetness. It did not add a substance; it added a property. A quality of resonance. A kind of memory. The power imbued the condensing water with *anamnesis*—the ability to hold and share the imprint of what it touched.

The first drop fell.

It fell for a long time, as all drops did. But now, it was not merely H2O in a gravitational field. It was a vessel. As it fell through the vast, dark air, it gathered the hall's acoustic soul: the echo of the Boomer's last cry, the scent of the blue lichen, the lingering psychic static of a million frantic thoughts.

*Thock.*

It struck the umber brick, and it did not just land. It *delivered*. The memory it held was released, not as information, but as a subtle harmonic, a ghost of feeling that spread through the puddle it joined. The memory seeped into the brick itself. The entire floor, the very foundation of the world, was now slowly, imperceptibly, becoming a library.

A Strider, skittering in its dance of instinct, dipped its feathery mouthparts into a glowing pool. The water it tasted was different. Along with the sustenance of the algae, it tasted a resonance. For an infinitesimal moment, the frantic knot of its ganglia experienced something other than immediate stimulus. It felt a flicker of the Boomer's deep, slow patience. It felt the ghost of a thousand generations of Striders before it, dancing this exact dance. Its fear did not vanish, but it was recast. It was no longer a singular, terrifying present, but a part of an ancient, unbroken chorus. Its frantic life gained the dignity of history.

A Boomer lay in wait, its body a part of the floor. As the new water seeped into its amphibious skin, its consciousness, already rooted in the vibrations of the hall, deepened. It felt the chaotic patter of the swarm not just as the signal of prey, but as a tapestry of individual lives. When it finally unleashed its stunning cry, the subsequent silence was no longer empty. It was filled with a profound, shared stillness. As it lunged, it moved not just with hunger, but with a sense of its role in the cycle, a terrible and sacred duty. The hunt, once a purely predatory act, became a sacrament.

And a Quiet One, making its slow way up a buttress, felt the story of the whole world in the film of moisture beneath it. It felt the Strider’s fleeting terror and ancestral pride. It felt the Boomer’s sacramental hunt. It felt the turning of the great wheel it helped to grease. Its slow, humble work was no longer toil; it was meditation. It was the guardian of the story, the chronicler sliming its way across the pages of the library floor.

The power did not stop the dripping. It did not end the hunt. It did not change the song.

It gave the song an audience.

The musicians, for the first time, could hear their own music. The fear, the hunger, the toil—it all remained. But it was now woven with meaning, with a shared consciousness that resided in the water and the clay. The hall was no longer just a vessel containing life. It had become alive itself, a thinking, feeling ecosystem, aware of its own tragic and beautiful existence.

The benevolent and wise power, its work done, moved its attention onward, leaving behind a single, sustained chord that had, at last, learned to listen to itself.
