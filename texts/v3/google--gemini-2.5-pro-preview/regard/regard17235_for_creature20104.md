The arrival of the Attention was not an event. It was not a breach in the silver ceiling, nor a ripple from beyond the caldera walls. It was a change in the quality of the water, a shift in the hum so subtle that at first, it was only a feeling in the marrow of the Silt-Singers, a sense that their song was being listened to.

The power, ancient and attuned to the quiet harmonies of existence, did not perceive Stillwater with sight. To do so would be to miss the point entirely. It perceived the city as the Silt-Singers did: as a pressure, a vibration, a vast and intricate chord played on the instrument of a mountain. It felt the slow, bass groan of the Spire-Kelp as the foundation of the music. It felt the percussive static of the crustaceans as the texture, the rhythm. It felt the clicks and whistles of the fauna as the fleeting melody. And through it all, it felt the patient, resonant influence of the Sins-Singers—the sustained notes that bent vine and thickened frond—as the conscious will, the conductors of the symphony.

The Attention drank it all in. For a time beyond measure, it simply drifted, an invisible node of perception, learning the city's voice. It understood.

It understood that this equilibrium was not a stasis, but a perfect, unending dance. To introduce a new element—a tool, a technology, a source of energy—would be like handing a hammer to a weaver of spider-silk. It would be an act not of benevolence, but of catastrophic ignorance. To offer them the knowledge of the stars, of dry land and burning suns, would be to give them a vocabulary for a loneliness they had never known. It would be a poison of context.

The Silt-Singers were not incomplete. They were not struggling. They were whole, nested perfectly within their world, their every need met by a system they themselves cultivated and were a part of. They lacked nothing.

What, then, could a benevolent and wise power give to a people who have everything they need?

Not a thing. Not a lesson. Not a protection.
A gift must be given in the language of the receiver. The language of Stillwater was resonance. The language of the Silt-Singers was the Song.

And so, the Attention decided. It would not add a new instrument to the orchestra. It would, for a single, glorious moment, become the concert hall itself.

It began subtly. A Silt-Singer, tending to a glowing moss patch, paused. The water against her skin felt… different. More alive. The familiar vibration of her own humming felt as though it were being met, held, and understood. Across the city-grove, others felt it too. The webbing between their fingers tingled. The fine bones of their jaws, which read the world’s music, began to resonate with a clarity they had never experienced.

Then, the Attention acted. It gathered the entirety of the city’s sound into itself. The groaning of the kelp, the rasping of the grazers, the silent pulse of the drifting jellies, the flick of a thousand glassy fins, the soft, bell-like chimes of a mother calling to her child, the deep, generative drone of an architect Shaping a new home—it heard all of it.

And it sang it back.

It was not a new song. It was Stillwater’s own song, returned to itself in perfect, crystalline harmony. For a single, long, indrawn breath, every being in the caldera felt itself as a note in a composition of impossible beauty. The groaning of the spires became a profound and holy chant. The hiss of the filter-feeders became a sacred wash of cymbals. Every click and whistle became a part of a unified, soaring melody.

The Silt-Singers stopped moving. In their glowing pods, their skin, which bloomed and pulsed with private emotion, now glowed with a single, shared pattern of awestruck silver and gold. They felt their own individual songs, their own inner hums, as part of something magnificent and whole. They felt the life of the armored grazer on the spire not as something other, but as a contrapuntal voice to their own. They felt the city not as a place they lived *in*, but as a body they *were*. For one moment, the illusion of the self dissolved into the reality of the system. The water itself became a conscious, singing entity. It was a moment of total empathy, gifted by an invisible, resonant god.

And then, as gently as it had begun, it faded.

The water was once again the water. The hum was the familiar, complex hum. The Silt-Singer returned to tending her moss, her three-fingered hands moving with their old, unhurried grace.

But nothing was ever quite the same. The power had left no trace, no artifact, no commandment. It had taken nothing and left nothing behind but an echo in their racial memory. It became the Great Chorus, the Moment the Water Woke. It was a story told not in words, but in a new depth of song. Their Singing, once a tool of craft, now held a shade of the sacred. They still sang to shape the vines and guide the fish, but they now sang with the knowledge of the whole composition. They sang with more care, more reverence, listening more deeply for the other voices in the symphony.

The benevolent power had looked upon a perfect thing and understood that the only gift you can give to perfection is a mirror. It let Stillwater hear its own soul, and in that hearing, enriched it for all the slow-moving generations to come. Then, its wisdom affirmed, it receded, leaving only the deep, green-gold silence, now pregnant with the memory of a perfect note.
