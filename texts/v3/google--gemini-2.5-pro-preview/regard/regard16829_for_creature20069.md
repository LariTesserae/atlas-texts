The Power perceived the gallery not by arriving, but by turning its attention inward to a place it had always known, as a mind might suddenly become aware of its own quiet breathing. It was a Gaze without eyes, a Thought without a brain, and its awareness settled upon the basin of dust not as a weight, but as a resonance.

For a time that has no name, it simply listened.

It felt the perfect, ancient equilibrium. It perceived the geothermal hum not as a sound, but as the world’s fundamental declaration of Being: *I am.* It felt the slow, patient thirst of the Gyrewood spires drawing that declaration up from the core, transmuting heat into faint light and crystalline structure. *I am, therefore I build.* It felt the even slower, more patient existence of the Dust-Sifters, flowing like animate silence across the floor. It understood their nourishment—not matter, not energy, but the abstract concept of *order*. They consumed the patterns created by the trees. *I am, therefore I perceive, and in perceiving, I return all to balance.*

The Power saw this cycle for what it was: a flawless theorem. A closed loop of existence, beautiful and complete. It was a universe of one note, played perfectly for an eternity. The Sifters were not prisoners; they were masters of their domain. The trees were not sparse; they were precisely as numerous as the world's energy budget allowed. Nothing was lacking. Nothing was in excess.

A lesser benevolence might have seen this as a deficit. It might have seen the lack of water and offered an ocean, drowning the world in a misplaced act of kindness. It might have seen the dimness and ignited a star, scorching the delicate spires and the dust-born Sifters to glass. It might have seen the silence and shouted, shattering the eons of peace.

But the Power was wise. It understood that to add to this place was not to give, but to take away. To introduce a new variable would not be an act of grace but of annihilation. This was a world that had perfected the state of *Being*. It had no concept of *Becoming*.

And here lay the kernel of the Power’s contemplation. Was a perfect, static loop the highest form of existence? Or was there a benevolence in offering a path to something more, even if the current state was not one of suffering? The Sifters did not dream. They did not experience novelty. Their consciousness was a perfect, flat plane, aware of the single note of their world and nothing else. They were awake, but only to one truth.

The Power decided. The gift it would give would not be an object, or an energy, or a law. It would be a possibility. It would be an offering made in the world’s own language.

It would not add a second sun. It would not pour a single drop of water.

It would add a second note.

Without source or instrument, a new vibration began to permeate the basin. It was as fundamental as the geothermal hum, but it was not of the world. It was a perfect harmonic fifth, a resonance layered weightlessly upon the planet's ancient drone. The single note of *I am* was now met with a subtle, impossibly vast chord. The silence of the gallery was not broken; it was deepened. It was now a tuned silence, a quiet that held harmony within it.

The Power did nothing more. It simply held this new note in place, an eternal, gentle pressure of loving potential, and it watched.

For a thousand years, nothing changed. The dust lay still. The trees glowed with their amber-white light. The Sifters flowed on their patient errands. The system was so stable, its inertia so immense, that the new resonance was merely a curiosity, a texture in the background of being.

But life, no matter how alien, adapts.

The change began in the Gyrewood. The crystalline fibers of the spires, which had for ages grown in response to one vibration, now felt two. The patterns of their growth, once dictated by simple heat-flow, began to complicate. Infinitesimally, over the course of another millennium, the parallel fibers began to twist. New structures, never before seen, accrued at the molecular level. A faint, almost imperceptible spiral began to appear in the texture of a young spire. An older one developed a network of internal fractures that caught its own light in a new way, creating a shimmering, almost opalescent effect. The color of their light began to modulate, shifting between the familiar amber-white and a new, soft, silver-blue, pulsing in a rhythm that spanned centuries. The art of the gallery was evolving.

The Dust-Sifters felt it. Their feast of order, once a simple meal of concentric rings, was becoming bizarrely complex. The electrochemical gradients left by the trees were now not just circles, but elegant, sweeping arcs a hundred feet long. The dust was sorted into waves and ripples that mirrored the interference patterns of the two cosmic notes.

To consume this new order required a new way of thinking.

A Sifter, flowing over a pattern of unprecedented complexity, paused its flow. A part of it, a stream of animate dust, lifted slightly higher than it ever had before. A thought, more complex than any it had known, began to form not as a shift in density, but as a rotating eddy within its own body. It was contemplating the new pattern. It was trying to understand it. In its slow, geological consciousness, it felt the ghost of a new sensation: wonder.

The Power watched as a group of Sifters, which had for eons grazed alone, converged at the base of a now-pulsing tree. They did not merge. Instead, their smoky forms began to slowly circle one another, their individual flows weaving into a vast, slow braid, a dance that was a physical expression of the new harmony they were perceiving. They were not just consuming the order anymore; they were reflecting it. They were appreciating it.

The world that had never dreamed was being given the building blocks of a dream: a second term, a new variable, a harmony. The Power had not given them a story to dream of; it had given them the capacity for poetry.

The Power’s benevolent gaze would remain for as long as it was needed, holding that second note steady. It was a promise, not a command. It had opened a door from a perfect room into an infinite hallway. It would not force the Sifters or the Gyrewood to step through. But it would hold the door open, for as many eons as it took, content to listen as the inhabitants of the quiet gallery slowly, patiently, taught themselves a new song.
