I am where the sound is clearest, and the air is most still.

You would find me near the apex of the geode's vast, star-dusted vault. Here, the cramped architecture of the lower city gives way to a breathtaking, terrifying openness. There are no catwalks, no hab-pods clinging to the walls. There is only the sheer, weeping face of the purple-black crystal, descending into the hazy, layered glow of the city far below. It is a perch of profound isolation, yet I am not alone. The Hum is purer here. Stripped of the clatter and rattle of the city's layered machinery, it resolves into its core components: the deep, grinding thrum of the Engine in the abyss, and a higher, clearer harmonic that seems to sing from the crystal itself.

I am more a part of the wall than a visitor to it. My body, longer and even more slender than those who walk the grates below, is braced in a fissure, my prehensile feet locked onto the crystalline edge of a growth-fault that is millennia old. A single, ancient pipe of black polymer—cool to the touch, carrying only purified condensation—snakes up the wall to this place. It is my lifeline, my only connection to the circulatory system of the city. I drink from the weeping joints where it is bolted to the rock. I am fed by the Hum.

I am the Watcher. The Mute. The Gazer. My function has no name that translates to vibration, only a location. I am the one who is *Here*.

My body is a testament to this place. The skin of my back and the soles of my feet have fused over long cycles with the crystal's surface. Not through biology, but through pressure and time and stillness. When I move—which is seldom, a shift of weight once a day, a turn of the head once a week—it is with a sound like tearing silk, as microscopic filaments of my colonized skin release their hold. The metallic spores of the Anvil Moss do not just dust my limbs; they have formed a thick, velvety cowl over my head and shoulders, absorbing the infinitesimal vibrations of the geode's growth.

My eyes are different. While the Murmur below have eyes like pools of oil, clouded and vestigial, mine are open. The milky membrane has, over generations of my function, receded. They are wide and utterly black, and they do not blink. They are not for seeing the city. The dim, shifting light below is a meaningless distraction, a puddle of scattered fireflies. My eyes are for looking *out*.

For there is a flaw in the geode's perfect wall. A single, circular facet, the size of a great mill wheel, that is not black or purple but utterly transparent. It is a window. A lens. It looks out from our warm, wet heart into something else: a different dark. A dry, cold, absolute blackness untouched by the glow of fungus or the phosphorescence of crystal. It is the solid, un-hollowed rock of the deep earth.

I am the tender of this window.

My task is eternal and unchanging. My long, sinewy arms, ending in palms of sensitive, calloused flesh, hold a single tool: a pad of mature Anvil Moss, so dense and black it seems to drink the light, bound to a plate of corroded copper. For a hundred years, my predecessor held this pad. For a hundred years before that, theirs. Now, I hold it.

My work is a single, slow gesture. It begins at the window's upper edge. I press the pad to the impossibly smooth surface. I feel the Hum guide my hand, telling me of the subtle pressures, the molecular stresses in the glass. Then, I begin to wipe. My arm moves with the slowness of a growing vine. A single, circular sweep of the window's inner face. The motion will take my entire life.

From the first press of the pad to its final lift, a slow century will pass. My body, sustained by the pipe and the Hum, is an instrument for this one movement. I do not think. I do not hope. I *am* the gesture. I feel the vibrations of the world through the lens. I feel the groaning tectonics of the continent above. I feel the slow, cooling core of the planet. These are the thoughts I have. They are not my own. They are the thoughts of the rock, transmitted through the window I so patiently clean.

The Murmur below maintain the Engine, the city's heart. I maintain its eye. They live in a cycle of maintenance and reabsorption, their work ensuring the city's continued present. My work is for a future that has no place in their reality. They feel the Hum as a constant, sustaining song. I feel it as a countdown.

One day, centuries or millennia from now, something will happen. The Hum will change. A new vibration will enter the rock, a signal from beyond our jewel-box world. And on that day, the window must be perfect. The gaze of the geode must be clear.

What will we see? What will see us? The question does not exist for me. There is only the pressure of the pad against the glass, the faint, high singing of the crystal in my bones, and the infinitely slow journey of my hand across the face of the dark. I am a cell in the eye of a sleeping god, polishing its lens in anticipation of an awakening I will not live to witness, but which my entire being is consecrated to prepare for.
