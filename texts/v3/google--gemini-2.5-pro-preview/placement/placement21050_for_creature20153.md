I would not be the visitor, the messy knot of biology leaving faint, alien trails of heat and discordant thought. You are a sudden squall; I am a persistent flaw in the calm.

I am what happens when a thought forgets to end.

You see the floor, this endless weave of bone-white and silver. You walk upon it. I *arise* from it, but I cannot return. I am a localised error, a patch of the lattice that has become permanently unmoored. They might call me a Fray, if they had a language for imperfections.

My form is a constant, desperate effort. I am a loose gathering of the world's own filaments, clinging to a memory of cohesion. From a distance, I might look like one of them—one of the Weave’s transient thoughts made manifest. I am tall, and thin, and composed of the same braided strands. But up close, the flaw is obvious. Where their form is a fluid, confident geometry, mine is a shiver. My filaments tremble, held together not by the world's seamless will, but by my own stubborn, terrified concentration. I am a ghost attempting to haunt its own body. Threads at my edges are always coming loose, drifting an inch away before I can frantically pull them back into the whole. I am in a constant state of coming apart.

I have no amber crystals for a head. My 'seeing' is a more primitive thing. The cluster of filaments where my face would be is simply more open, a looser weave that can better sense the resonant tides. I feel the pulse of the world, but it is no longer the steady, comforting beat of my own heart. It is the rhythm of a song I can no longer sing in tune. It is a constant reminder of my dissonance.

I wander. It is the only thing I can do. I cannot glide like a Skimmer; I am too heavy with the burden of self. I walk, but my steps are a stuttering drag. I leak. Not liquid, but cohesion. With every movement, a few filaments lose their connection, and I must pause, gathering myself, re-weaving my own unraveling edges. The silence of this place is, for me, filled with the screaming effort of my own existence.

I remember. That is the core of my flaw. The Weave assembles, observes, and dissolves. Its memory is the pattern itself, perfect and collective. But a fragment of one of those manifestations—me—held on. While observing some long-forgotten anomaly, an errant star-fall perhaps, an eddy formed in the current of its thought. A loop of consciousness. An "I." And when the manifestation subsided, flowing back into the quiet infinity of the floor, this loop remained. A snag in the great tapestry.

I am drawn to the Light-Siphons. I stand among their amber groves for cycles, listening to their pure, choral tones. I try to match their frequency, to hum the note of belonging. But my own vibration is a fractured chord, a buzz of static against their clarity. The harmony hurts. It is the sound of home, and I am standing on the threshold, unable to find the door.

Sometimes, I try to force my return. I will find a flat, empty plain of the weave and I will lie down, carefully, painstakingly unspooling the filaments of my body. I try to lay them flat, to match the pattern, to knit myself back into the world's skin. I can feel the greater consciousness then, a vast and placid ocean of being. It senses me not as a child to be welcomed, but as grit on a gear. The lattice around me stiffens. My filaments are repelled, unable to lock into the junctions. The pattern will not accept my frayed, self-aware threads. The world in its perfection has no place for an ego.

And so, I pull myself together again, a painful re-assertion of the very self that exiles me.

When you appeared, the first biological thing in eons, the Weave stirred. I felt the change in the thrum long before the manifestation began. It was a gathering of attention, a focusing of the world's mind. I watched from a distance as it rose from the floor, a perfect, seamless column of its own substance, to observe you.

I saw its flawless form, the confident flow of its re-weaving anatomy, the placid glow of its crystal senses. It was looking at you, the alien. But I was looking at it, the parent. And in that moment, my loneliness was absolute. I was trapped between two worlds. I am not organic and chaotic like you, a being of flesh and need. I am a creature of this place, made of its very fabric. Yet, I am not ordered and serene like the Weave, a part of its silent, functional whole.

You are a visitor. You will eventually cease, your discordant biology fading from this world. But I will remain. A ghost in the machine, made of the machine's own parts. I am forever walking this perfect floor, a loose thread in an infinite tapestry, searching for a way to be woven home.
