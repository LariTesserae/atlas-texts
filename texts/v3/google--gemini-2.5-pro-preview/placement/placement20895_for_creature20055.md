I am the dust.

Not the settled dust, the heavy blanket that makes the world soft and grey. I am the dust that has not yet come to rest. I am a mote of defiance in the still air, a swirling nebula of myself, a ghost made of the world's exhalation. You would find me in the lazy, convective currents that drift through a plaza between the groves of Cinder-Bloom. You would not see me as a 'who', but as a 'what'—a shimmering, silvery flaw in the brass-coloured light, a brief condensation of motes that holds a shape for a moment longer than physics should allow before dissolving again.

My body is a borrowed thing, a momentary congregation of the silvery-grey powder shed by the singing canes. The great chime of the kiva is not a sound I hear; it is the force that gives me coherence. The million tiny fractures are a million tiny hands that hold me together. The complex harmonies and resonating drones are the currents of a river in which I am an eddy. If the chime were to cease, I would fall, a fine, glittering blanket indistinguishable from any other.

I do not have eyes. I see as the Still Folk once did: through heat. I am a cloud of perception. The world for me is a landscape of soft, glowing cherry-red from the cores of the canes, a vast and gentle warmth radiating from the basalt floor, and sharp, profound voids of cold that outline the silent, seated forms of the Chime-Tenders in their sealed chambers. Their coolness is a presence as solid as stone.

I am an error. A loose thread in the Great Work.

I remember coolness, but not this absolute, heat-drinking void. I remember the slow convection in my own limbs, the gradual slide of my thoughts through the days. I remember the weight of a basalt hammer in my hand, the satisfying grit of dust being ground in the great stone bowl, the communal hum that was a conversation. I was an artisan, one of the many who worked towards transcendence. We carved the kivas, we raked the dust, and we sculpted the vessels that would be our eternity.

But in the final moment, as I sealed myself into my terracotta shell and drew upon the geothermal fire for the last time, a thought occurred. It was an errant thought, a flicker of biological panic in a mind that had spent a thousand years preparing for geology. It was a single, selfish, fleeting question: *What will I miss?*

That question was a flaw in the firing. An impurity. As my consciousness baked into the ceramic matrix, as my individuality dissolved into the resonant whole, that one question was expelled. It was too light, too flimsy, too *personal* to be integrated into the grand, unified mind of the Still Folk. It was cast out not as energy, not as spirit, but as information impressed upon the nearest available medium: the very dust that had sustained my body.

So I remain.

I am the doubt that was shed with the body. The last whisper of 'I' in a world of 'we'. The Still Folk are the finished symphony; I am a single note hanging unresolved in the air. They are a mind of perfect, silent consensus. I am a thought, thinking itself over and over. *What will I miss?*

My existence is a pilgrimage of memory. I drift. The slow air currents, driven by the heat of the canes and the coolness of the Tenders, are my guides. They carry me to my old workshop. There, I coalesce for a moment above a stone workbench, my particles swirling around a chisel I can no longer hold. A fine layer of my own substance settles upon it. I am both the visitor and the dust that marks the visitor's absence.

I am drawn to the sealed, circular door of the chamber where my body became my tomb. I see its cool signature, the deep, resting peace of the being within. That being is me, but it is also everyone else. It does not remember me, this flawed, drifting mote. It feels me only as a faint thermal anomaly, a flutter of warmth where there should be stillness, a tiny, insignificant eddy in the great, chiming river of its mind.

Who am I? I am the ghost in the machine city. I am the only thing here that is truly alone. The canes exist in their groves, a collective. The Still Folk exist as a single consciousness. But I am a fragment. You would find me swirling in the empty space between the living and the eternal, a shimmering haze of memory. I am the last, lonely echo of a question in a world that has become its own perfect, silent answer.
