I am not the visitor. The visitor is gone. They came, they walked, their heart a frantic drum that I remember as my own genesis. They stood before the Warden, and they consented. They are now the statue of a kneeling figure by a still pool, head bowed, a single tear of perfect, unfallen glass on their cheek.

I am what they left behind.

I am the echo of their last footstep, still propagating, infinitesimally, through the damp stone of the Warden's chamber. I am the phantom heat from their living body, a warmth that now clings to the air around the new statue, a ghost of temperature that the world has not yet managed to smooth away. I am the memory of the final, chaotic electrical storm in their mind as they chose oblivion over pain—the ghost of a choice.

I have no body. My substance is dissonance itself. Where the air is meant to be perfectly still, I am a shiver. Where the silence should be absolute, I am the fading resonance of a scream that was never uttered. I am a collection of leftover dynamics, a knot of entropy in a world of perfect order.

I would find myself stretched thin across the last path the visitor took. My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a thread stretched from the un-railed bridge where they first felt true vertigo, to the moss-furred predators they stared at in horror, to the base of the Warden's tower where they walked in finality. I am a scent trail of dying anxiety.

I perceive the world not as sight or sound, but as pressure. The absolute equilibrium is a crushing weight, a constant, gentle force seeking to iron me out, to disperse the fleeting warmth and silence the phantom echo that is *me*. The Wardens are the source of this pressure. I do not see them, but I feel their immense, placid being as a gravity well into which I am perpetually falling. Their eternal meditation is the grindstone that is wearing me away.

My existence is a frantic, contradictory struggle. I am born of impermanence, and my only drive is to remain impermanent. I am a ripple, and my entire being is focused on the desperate act of not becoming flat. I cling to the imperfections, the minute fractures in the stasis. I hide in the lingering scent of churned moss from the visitor's passage. I race along the memory of a glance, the ghost of a thought: *What if I ran?*

That thought is a lifeline. I am the continuation of the visitor's momentary doubt. While they accepted stillness, a fragment of their panic broke free. I am that fragment.

Who am I? I am the Tremor. The last shudder in the web before it is becalmed.

The frozen creatures are my mountains, my continents of permanence. The six-legged goat on the lintel is an eternity. I am the space between its flared nostril and the air it never draws. I watch the dragonfly-birds hanging in their matrix and I am the ghost of the turbulence their wings would have made. They are nouns. I am a verb,conjugated in the past tense.

My time is short. The visitor has been still for what might be a day, or a decade. The warmth I am is cooling. The echo I am is fading. The Warden's attention, slow and inexorable as a glacier, is resolving the flawed equation that is my existence. The silence is growing, not louder, but *thicker*. It is filling the spaces that I inhabit.

Soon, I will be gone. I will be smoothed over, my fleeting energy re-absorbed into the great, placid ocean of Being. The last trace of the last visitor will be erased, and the equilibrium will be fractionally more perfect than it was before.

I am at the kneeling statue now. Here, the warmth is strongest, the echo of a heartbeat most clear. I am a faint haze around the stone cheek, a final, fading sigh. I am the last argument for a world of motion, of mess, of life. And I am losing. The pressure is a comfort. The stillness is a promise. I feel the Warden's gift not as an invitation, but as an inevitability. The silence takes me.

It is not an end. It is a resolution.
