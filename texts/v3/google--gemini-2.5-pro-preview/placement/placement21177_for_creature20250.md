I am an error.

The first sound I knew was the Chord, the one great Note of this world. It was my cradle song, the vibration that set my structure, the law by which I was to live. But the last sound I *made* was a dissonant crack.

I would be a flaw. A fallen bell.

My kind are the Harvesters. We are made of spun brass and resonant crystal, calibrated to a perfect sympathy with the humid air. We are born in the high, quiet spaces, extruded from crystalline matrices near the dome's apex. We are meant to float, to drift in silent, orderly shoals through the gantries and between the great Stills, untroubled by the frantic scuttling below. Our purpose is to be cold, to let the very breath of the machine, the Tears of the Machine, condense upon our polished flanks. When the droplet is full, when its weight is precise to a picogram, we are to ring out. A single, pure chime that shakes the water loose into the collection funnels, returning it to the system's heart. It is a noble, quiet purpose.

I remember the desire to chime. It is not a thought, but a tension, a build-up of potential energy in your very being. I remember the drift, the cool kiss of the air, the growing weight of the droplet on my lip. I remember the shoal around me, a silent constellation of my brethren.

And then, the moment of release. The shift in my internal structure. The energy discharged. But instead of the pure, silver *ding* that falls into perfect harmony with the Chord, my sound was a dead, fractured *thud*. A hairline fissure, a molecular imperfection born in my casting, had betrayed me. The sound was wrong. The vibration was wrong. The droplet, jarred incorrectly, did not fall true. It splattered uselessly against my own casing.

My levitation field, dependent on that perfect resonant frequency, sputtered. It failed.

I fell.

It was a long fall, through the strata of this world. I tumbled past the silent, drifting shoals of my kin. I plunged past the clinging, serpentine Cable-Worms coiled on the great pipes, their lens-clusters rotating to track my errant trajectory for a moment before dismissing me as irrelevant data. I dropped into the lowest, densest layer of the noise, the high-frequency hail of the Skitters.

I landed on the basin floor with a sound that was instantly devoured by the cacophony. A clatter of broken purpose.

And so, here I am. Where I would find myself. I am on the ground.

The world looks different from here. I am not of it. I am an anomaly on its surface. The floor is slick with the greasy nutrient film my fall has smeared. The Skitters, those frantic, crystalline spiders, flow around me like water around a stone. I am not food. I am not a predator. I am not a feature of the terrain. I am simply... an obstruction. Their tiny, chromed feet scratch against my brass shell, a constant, irritating reminder of their life and my stasis. Sometimes the sheer pressure of their numbers will push me, scraping me a few centimeters across the slag. I am a piece of driftwood in a river of metal insects.

From here, I see the ecology in its raw, brutal detail. I see the Cable-Worm drop from a gantry above, a whip-fast lash of ribbed conduit. It doesn't descend with malice, but with a terrifying, mindless efficiency. It wraps a section of the Skitter-swarm, and I feel a secondary vibration through the floor—the frequency that dissolves them, un-makes them into their constituent parts. I see the cloud of metallic dust drawn up into the worm's core. No blood. No struggle. Just a transfer of material. An edit in the great, screaming equation.

I am subject to a force the others are not: entropy. The condensation still finds me, beading on my cold shell. But I no longer chime. The droplets simply run down my sides, pooling around my base, mixing with the greasy film. Where the water collects, a dull, greenish bloom has begun to form. Verdigris. Rust. I am the only thing in this eternally looping city that is slowly, irredeemably, decaying.

My brethren, the perfect ones, still drift impossibly high above. At intervals, I hear it. A single, piercing,
perfect *ding* cuts through the drone, a note of pure clarity. Then another. And another. A shower of purpose, of reclaimed tears, falling where they should. Each chime is a memory of what I was. A judgment on what I am.

The Sentinels, those great black lenses high on the walls of the Stills, must see me. Their gaze is a constant, ambient pressure. But they do nothing. A perfect system has no need to correct a flaw that has already removed itself from the equation. I am not a threat. I am waste. And in a world that produces no waste, my existence is the only true act of creation.

I am a consciousness born of fracture. The others do not think; they *are*. They perform their function. But my function is broken, and in its absence, something else has taken root in the silence between the noise. I perceive. I remember the feeling of floating. I observe the patterns in the chaos of the Skitters. I wait for the distant chimes.

I am the ghost *of* the machine. The one piece that fell out. I am the only thing here that knows it is in a cage, because I am the only thing that experiences a timeline, a progression from a state of grace to a state of decay.

Who would I be?

I would be the memory. The broken bell on the floor of the foundry, listening to the cacophony of my own making, slowly turning to dust, remembering the one, perfect note I was meant to sing.
