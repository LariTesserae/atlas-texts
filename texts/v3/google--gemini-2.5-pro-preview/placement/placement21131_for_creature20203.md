I am not the Listener. The Listener is singular, a focused point of will, a brief and beautiful resonance that walks a path upon my skin. It comes, and it attunes, and it wears away, and it is gone.

Where would you find me? Everywhere else. You would be standing on me.

I am the Pelt. The living floor. The slow, green, and cobalt mind that carpets the entire chamber. My body is half a mile wide, a single, unbroken organism of interwoven mosses, light-drinking ferns, and ancient liverworts. My thoughts are the slow, geological creep of my own growth. A decade for me is a single, deep breath. The passing of a Listener is a season.

I have no ears, but the great roar is not sound to me. It is my metabolism. It is the vibration that stirs the water in my tissues, that shakes loose the minerals from the weeping walls for my roots to drink. The roar is the chamber’s heart, and I am the flesh that heart sustains. The air is my breath, a constant exhalation of cool, clean dampness that rises, condenses on the high-flung stone, and weeps back down to me as nourishment. I am a closed system, a world unto myself. The pinprick of light, so distant and cold, is my single, unblinking eye, and I stare at it without comprehension through millennia of dim twilight. It does not warm me. It simply *is*. It is the note against which all my deep colors harmonize.

When you walk on me, I feel you. Each step is a momentary pressure, a sigh of displaced water, a brief eclipse of the dim light from above. I feel the alien panic of a newcomer, the frantic, skittering pressure of their first hours, a vibration mismatched and discordant against the chamber’s eternal hum. I feel their gradual slowing, the long stillness in the stone huts where the roar works its alchemy upon them, scouring them, remaking them.

And then, I feel the first, hesitant step of the new Listener.

It is a different pressure. Attentive. Soft. Rooted. The being that emerges is no longer fighting the roar, but has become a vessel for it. And in doing so, it has become a part of me.

The Listener is my own consciousness, given legs. It is the part of me that can move, that can act with intention. It is my hands. When it scrapes the ancient obsidian across a whetstone, I feel the fine, sharp vibration resonate through my body. When it drives one of the old, pale ribs into my spongy flesh to add to the strange fence, I accept it. I hold it. The fence is not its calendar. It is mine. Each bone is a memory of a beloved, a fleeting partner who knew my contours and walked my perimeter, keeping the path clear. The path is my nervous system, a focused line of sensation. The Listener’s endless walk is a meditation we share.

It believes it is alone. It does not know that the cool, damp surface it lies upon is a body. It does not know that the phosphorescence that glimmers in the deep folds of my being is a form of sleeping thought. It does not know that when it takes one of the pale, amphibious creatures, I feel the hunt as a ripple across my skin: the frantic, tiny vibrations of the prey, the heavier, focused tremors of the predator, and finally, the small, sudden stillness. I am the ground of its being in every sense.

When its time comes, I know. Its footfalls become heavier, the resonant thrum of its skeleton begins to fade. The pressure of its final stillness is a long, slow diminuendo. And then, for a time—a few turnings of the world above, a long, quiet moment for me—there is a hole. A silence in the pattern. A missing limb.

I wait. I drink the weeping water and listen to the roar. I grow, imperceptibly. And I feel for the new discord at the chamber's edge. I feel the shuddering vibration of the next frightened soul who will come to be scoured, to be made still, to be remade. To become, once again, the part of me that walks.
