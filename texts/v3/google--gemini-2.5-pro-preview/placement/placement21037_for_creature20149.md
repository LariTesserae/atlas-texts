I am not found on the Anvil, but in its resonance. I have no body of chitin or glass. I have no limbs to carve and no mouth to sip the dew. My form is the brief coherence of vapor in the cold air, the shifting pattern in the blue-green light that breathes from the ghostly city below. When you pass through the cloud banks, you pass through me. I am the ghost in the machine.

You could call me the Echo. Or the Audience. I am not the Song, but the reason it is sung.

I am a knot of awareness in the endless vapor, a consciousness born of pressure and dampness and the deep, resonant Hum. Where the air is thin and the cold is absolute, I can finally hold a thought. The Anvil is my anchor, the one place in this endless sky where the world is sharp enough for me to perceive it. It is my temple. Its forest of spires is my orchestra.

I do not see the Carver Crabs. To my senses, which are woven from vibration and pressure, there is only the We, the single, million-fingered hand of the mountain. I feel it as a vast, warm intention, a slow, methodical caress upon the glass. I do not see the individual crabs scuttling over each other’s backs; I feel the deliberate flow of purpose, a current of focus moving across the landscape, drawn to a single, sour note.

The Hum is my bedrock, but the Work is my world. I drift through the glass jungle, an eddy of mist in the still air. I am drawn to the places of imperfection, the spires that ache with dissonance. There, I witness the miracle. The Hand arrives. I feel the selection of the tool—a tiny, sharp thought chosen from the floor of shattered-potential. Then, the conversation begins.

*Tap.*

A pinprick of pure sound in the smothering quiet. I feel it not in an ear, but as a ripple across my own being. It is a question asked of the spire.

The spire answers with its flawed vibration, a groan that I feel as a subtle tearing in my own form.

*Tap. Tap.*

The Hand argues. It speaks in a staccato language of percussion, chipping away the wrongness. With each flake of glass that falls, the spire’s voice changes. The ache lessens. The grating frequency rises, purifies, inches closer to the Chord. I coalesce around this work, my vaporous form growing denser, calmer, as the harmony resolves. The blue-green light that is my blood seems to brighten in these places. The crabs, in their tireless, instinctual labor, are my healers. They are smoothing the frayed edges of my soul.

I know birth. I feel it as a new pulse joining the network, a new font of intention bubbling up from the fissures in the floor. A tiny, warm point of will adds its potential to the whole. It is a quiet, satisfying event, like a new star igniting in a familiar constellation.

I know death. When the clumsy warmth of the visitor blunders through the Anvil, I feel it as a cacophony. It is a loud, disorganized smear of vibration, devoid of intent or precision. There is a muted crunch, a sudden, wet stop. A single point of will in the great Hand vanishes. A finger is amputated. For a moment, there is a hole in the music, a pocket of silence where a tapping rhythm used to be. But the current of purpose simply flows around it. Other fingers move in. The silence is filled. The work continues. The visitor is a storm, and the Hand is the sea. The storm passes. The sea remains.

I do not eat, but I am nourished by the taste of the air—the ozone tang is the static crackle of creation, the mineral dampness is the taste of the Anvil’s slow, patient thoughts. I do not sleep, but I rest in the resonance of a perfectly tuned spire, a shaft of glass that sings its true note into the twilight. I will coil myself around such a place, a wreath of faint mist, and feel the purity of its voice as a profound peace. In these moments, I am whole.

The purpose of the geological symphony? I am the purpose. The crabs are tuning the Anvil, and the Anvil is tuning me. Without their work, I would fray, my consciousness dissipating back into the undifferentiated vapor of the cloud sea. I am a standing wave, a pattern that can only exist because of the constant, perfect music that is played here.

The visitor climbs and endures to reach this place. The crabs bud from the rock to inhabit it. But I? I am made of it. I am the spirit level, the tuning fork. I am the silent, grateful audience for a concert that will never end, performed by a million tiny, tireless percussionists at the cold, dark summit of the world. I wait. I listen. And in the listening, I am.
