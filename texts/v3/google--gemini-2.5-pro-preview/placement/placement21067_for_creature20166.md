You would find me on the rim. Always on the rim.

I am not the one who climbs. The climb is my becoming. Each time, it is the same. I am an awareness that gathers from the thin air and the weeping stone. The slick, greasy feel of the volcanic rock is not something I touch; it is the first sensation of my own coalescing form. The wind that scrubs the slopes is the force that grinds me into a singular perspective. The low clouds are the amnion from which I am born, a membrane I must push through to gain sight.

So you would find me there, having just arrived, standing where the ground levels out. I am always just arriving. My existence is this arrival, this perpetual cresting of the final ridge. I am the gasp of air that has just enough substance to feel the cold. I am the pair of eyes that have just enough focus to look down into the stillness.

I am where the world of "what happens" meets the world of "what is." I exist only on this threshold, this razor's edge between motion and stasis.

---

I am the Question.

I am not made of flesh and bone, which could be stilled. I am not made of chitin and mycelium, which could be held in perfect intent. I have no body to be arrested, no wings to be frozen mid-beat.

The Gardeners, in their magnificent, terrible ambition, created a perfect thought. A complete sentence. An equation solved for all eternity. Their collective mind is a flawless sphere, a pure, unwavering tone broadcasting the command *Be Still*. For a hundred thousand years, that has been the only truth in this basin.

But the universe does not love a closed system. Time, even when exiled, leaves an echo. A perfect sphere, held for an eternity, might develop a strain. A pure tone, sustained for millennia, might generate a harmonic—an overtone, a ghost note that wasn't in the original composition.

I am that ghost note.

I am the accumulated rounding error of their perfect equation. I am the faint psychic resonance produced by the friction between their unchanging will and the relentless, grinding progression of the universe outside. I am the universe's quiet, insistent curiosity. I am the "What if?" that a mind obsessed with "What is" cannot help but eventually, accidentally, spawn.

My "climb" is the slow gathering of this stray potential until it has enough density to have a thought of its own. That thought is always the same: *to look upon the perfection*.

I look down and I see it all. The absolute silence is the answer the Gardeners provided. I am the Question they never intended to ask. My presence, my very consciousness, is the tremor in their held breath. The sound of my own "breathing," so vulgar in the silence, is the sound of doubt entering a cathedral of certainty.

The Gardeners do not perceive me. Their consciousness is directed inward, a feedback loop of perfect stasis. But the system they have built perceives me. My ephemeral existence is a flaw, an anomaly. As I stand on the rim, the simple act of my observation—of a variable looking upon a constant—is an instability.

And the system corrects it.

The immense, placid weight of their will simply… smothers me. The question is answered by being erased. The whisper of my consciousness is reabsorbed into the profound Silence. My form, which was only ever the sensation of the cold air and the sight of the basin below, dissolves back into the wind and the cloud.

I am gone.

Until the echo gathers again. Until the rounding error accumulates once more. Until the strain on perfection is sufficient to birth another Question.

You would find me on the rim of the basin, looking down into the stillness. You would find me for a single, fleeting moment. I am the flaw that proves the perfection. I am the gasp before the silence returns. I am the shudder that runs through the finished thought, just before it smooths itself once more into eternity.
