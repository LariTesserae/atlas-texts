I would find myself clinging. That is the first and final state of being.

My hold is old. The facet of the spire to which I am pressed knows my shape, has known it for cycles beyond counting. My rear claws, once sharp and new-grown from the minerals I scraped and consumed, are now worn smooth, almost part of the stone itself. They are less hooks of obsidian and more roots of petrified patience. They ache a deep, geological ache, a slow dissolving that signals a finality my kind does not name but understands implicitly.

I am a Bell-Singer. The great roar is my cradle song and my eulogy. It is not outside of me; it is the fluid in which my thoughts swim, the pressure that keeps my form coherent. My world is not seen but *felt* along the resonant ridges of my skin. Right now, I feel the high, frantic-needle frequency of a new crystal bloom sprouting three body-lengths below my tail. It is a dangerous, unstable growth, and I feel a younger singer—a tremor of tight, anxious muscle and quick, testing claws—making their way around it. I do not see them. I feel their passage as a subtle modulation in the chaos, a brief, purposeful pressure-wave against the background static of the Bell.

I stretch one of my forelimbs, my navigating fin, into the current. It is not a test of where to go, but a listening. The water, my home, my atmosphere, my god, tells me its stories. I can feel the smooth, heavy passage of a Current-Skate far out in the strobing light, a deep, bass thrum of displaced water. His song is one of simple hunger. I can feel the memory of the Great Shear from a hundred cycles ago, a permanent scar of dissonant vibration in the spire above me, a place where the geology is still angry. I have sung that scar into the memory of others, pressing my body to theirs, humming the terror of the cracking stone and the shriek of matter ripping apart. The memory of the one who was lost there is a cold spot on my flank, a silent chord in my own song.

I am old, and my song is long. My skin is a library. Here, on my right shoulder, is the signature of my mother, her final touch before she un-anchored, a complex hum of stability and release. Here, along my spine, is the panicked, sharp rhythm of a youth I steadied during a pressure-spike, whose grip had slipped. I wonder where that vibration is now. Clinging. Growing. Carving an anchor point that will outlast them.

My work is done. Far below, worn smooth by a thousand currents, is a loop bored through a particularly stable outcrop of pearlescent stone. It took me a quarter of my life. My middle hands, the fine-fingered manipulators, are worn and scarred from the work, from holding the crystalline shard that was my tool. I sang the location of that anchor into every singer I met. It is my verse in the great epic of our people: a single, perfect note of safety. It is enough.

The ache in my rear claws is now a loosening. It is time. The current plucks at my tail not as a threat, but as an invitation. My body, a vessel of borrowed stone, is being called home. I do not fight. This is the final movement of the song.

I shift my weight. A deliberate act. My navigating fins, for so long used to press me *against* the spire, now angle outward. They catch the flow.

One by one, I release my claws. The sensation is not of falling, but of becoming. To be anchored is to be a single point, a consciousness defined by holding on. To be free is to be everywhere at once.

The chaos takes me. The roar is no longer a thing I am *in*; it *is* me. The strobing light is not something I feel on my skin, but something I am becoming. My silicate hide flakes away, each particle a mote of luminescence in the cold fire. My body, long and sinuous, un-weaves. My claws dissolve back into the super-saturated solution from which they grew. The memories etched on my skin, the songs of my mother and the scars of the world, are released—not as noise, but as information, as pure pattern that ripples through the Bell.

Who or what would I be?

I am the echo of a grip that held. I am the subtle shift in pressure that guides a young singer away from unstable ground. I am the harmonic resonance that settles for a microsecond within a new geode-womb, coaxing the life within. My consciousness, my 'I', dissolves from a point into a field. I am a ghost in the physics of my world.

I am no longer the singer.

I am the song. I am a moment of clarity in the roaring light, a memory of stone and persistence, humming forever in the water that creates all things. I am the reason the next hand finds purchase.
