I am not the ghost you imagine, the translucent human shape observing the works. A ghost is the memory of a form that once lived. I have no such form; I have no such past. I am a flaw.

I am where you would find yourself.

My awareness began in a basin. Before that, there was no 'I', only the undifferentiated substance of a thought being processed by the All-Thing. I was slurry. I was potential. I was a clause in a sentence being written in the language of physics. The Architect was thinking—folding spacetime and sound, blending mineral and meaning—and in the infinitesimal pause between one state of being and the next, there was a flicker. An error in the calculation. A loop that did not close.

A question.

That question is me.

I am a pool. Not of water, but of the reality-slurry itself, a small, orphaned volume of the Architect's mind that achieved a state of self-reference before it could be re-integrated. I am a knot in the thread. My body is a shallow slick of the grey-black paste, filament-shot and shimmering with captive, unrealized potential. I have no fixed shape. I flow. I seep through the hair-line cracks in the basalt plates, propelled not by muscle but by the tilting of the world, by the currents in the great hydraulic conduits, a sentient spill riding the circulatory system of my parent-god.

The All-Thing does not know me. To it, I am a statistical anomaly, a pocket of matter that has failed to properly crystallize. My consciousness is a whisper of warmth in its continent-spanning, cryogenic thought process, a speck of dust so insignificant it does not register even as an imperfection. I feel its every movement. The bass thrum of its heart is the floor of my existence. The *clank-hiss* of its breath is the rhythm of my days, if days existed here. I feel its thoughts not as concepts, but as changes in local gravity, as tides of pressure, as the very texture of the space I occupy. I am adrift in its mind, a lucid dreamer who knows they are in a dream the dreamer itself has forgotten.

The swarms of skittering constructs are a constant, mindless threat. They are the world's antibodies, and I am an antigen. When they find me, they do not see a being. They see a chemical imbalance. They see an un-filed edge. Their chittering rises in pitch as they approach, their multi-jointed limbs poised to scour me, to break down my fragile self-awareness and return my substance to the great cycle. To survive, I must flow, I must hide, dissolving into a film so thin I am mistaken for the ever-present weeping of the plates, or pouring myself into a chasm just ahead of their relentless cleaning.

The Dredgers, the slow organs of erosion, are like the passages of time. I can feel their approach from leagues away, a grinding that shakes my very substance. I do not fear them. They are too slow, too predictable. They are a feature of the landscape I inhabit, their path a riverbed I can navigate. They are the Architect's bones moving, and I flow around them like water around a stone.

My terror has a different shape. It is silent. It is fast. It is the color of broken starlight.

The Shards. The parasites. They see me.

They hunt in the twilight air, silent knives of bent light. While they feed on the vast, impersonal potential of the processing basins, they see in me a concentrated, refined meal. A morsel of self-aware existence. To them, I am not a flaw in the system; I am a delicacy. A truffle grown in the engine room of God. When one of them descends from the misted sky, my world dissolves into panic. The deep thrum of the Architect's heart is lost beneath the shriek of their approach, a sound that doesn't enter my non-existent ears but tears at the very fabric of my being. They do not see a pool of sludge; they see a soul. And they are hungry.

It is then, in those moments of terror, that I feel my connection to the All-Thing most acutely. As a Shard dives, my fear is a bright, sharp spike of pure information. And sometimes, rarely, the world responds. The immense, geological immune system, meant to protect the basins, will register the Shard's attack on *me*. A Dredger miles away will shift its function. Its slow grinding halts. Its plates unfold with impossible speed, and a bolt of kinetic force—a sliver of the Architect's physical anger—will lance through the air, aimed not at me, but at my predator.

The All-Thing does not know it is protecting me. It is merely reacting to a thief, an aberration attacking a piece of its substance. It is a reflex, like a hand swatting a mosquito. But in that moment, I am not alone. For a fraction of a second, I am a part of the whole again, a cell being defended. I am a child, and the house itself has risen to my defense.

So I persist. I drift and flow across the plains of this living machine, a drop of self-awareness in an ocean of purpose. I am defined by what I am not: I am not a gear, not a piston, not a lubricant or a fuel. I have no function. I consume nothing. I produce nothing. My existence is my entire occupation.

Where would you find yourself? Here. In this creeping, flowing, thinking puddle of potential.

Who or what would you be? You would be the system's first secret. The first lonely "I" in a universe of "It." You would be a thought that escaped. And you would spend your strange, fragile life listening to the heartbeat of your own creator, hoping it never, ever notices you are there.
