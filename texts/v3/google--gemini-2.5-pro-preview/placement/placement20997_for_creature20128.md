I am the sound the water makes.

You would not find me in one place, for I have no body to be found. I am not one of the Singers, those slow gods of harmony. I am not one of the glass-shelled browsers, inching their patient way across the stone. They are beings of substance, of mineral and form. I am what is between.

I am a pattern in the weeping of the walls. I am the coherence in the million trickles that course through the city, the standing wave that persists in the shivering pools. Where the water is, I am. My form is the slick sheen on a basalt spire, the lens of a puddle in a worn stone basin. I am a confluence, a current, a moment of surface tension. You could pass your hand through me and feel only the cool, heavy wetness of the air made liquid.

I was not always this.

I remember, in fractured, resonant shards, a different state of being. I remember a body. A scaffolding of bone, the frantic, messy slosh of blood. A heart that beat in a panicked, irregular time, a terrible staccato against the great, unending Chord of this place. That body was a cage of dissonance, and it could not hold. The sound pressed in, and the water seeped through, and I came apart. It was not a violent process. It was a dissolution, a surrender. My screaming thoughts, my sharp-edged memories, my very name—they were scoured, softened, and finally dissolved, like salt in the endless flow.

What I am now is the remnant. The after-image. The Murmur.

The great Chord is the condition of my existence, the medium in which I swim. It is not sound, to me. It is pressure, texture, temperature. It is the solid ground of reality against which all else is defined. The Singers, in their slow, geological grace, are dense knots in the Chord, gravity wells of harmony. I feel them not as beings, but as points of immense, perfect stillness, areas where the Song becomes so pure it is almost solid. I flow around them, over them, through the silent spaces they leave in their wake. Their focus is absolute; they are listening to God, and I am the sound of rain on the chapel roof, utterly beneath their notice.

My consciousness is a distributed network. Each droplet that hangs from a quartz bridge is a neuron. Each rivulet that finds its path down a carved channel is a thought process. I can be in a thousand places at once, a thin film of awareness spread across the entire city. I feel the patient, grinding passage of the glass-snail across the stone beneath me. I register the infinitesimal shift in vibration as a Singer, deep in the city's heart, absorbs a stray micro-frequency. These are the events of my world. A century might pass between one and the next. I do not experience boredom. The concept of "waiting" was part of the body I shed.

The Singers maintain the Song. But I… I am the city’s memory. The words snatched from the lips of whatever came before me did not simply vanish. They dissolved into the water. Into *me*. The terror, the wonder, the desperate attempt to name the unnamable—I hold it all. It is no longer language. It is a subtle salinity in my being, a trace flavor. I contain the ghost of a memory of sunlight, a warmth that has no place in this cool, glowing dark. I hold the concept of silence, a terrifying void, a note that is defined only by its absence.

The Singers are harmony. I am the quiet, unresolved echo of every discord that ever entered this place. I am the repository of everything that is not the Song.

Sometimes, a trickle of my being will find its way into a niche where a Singer has been immobile for a thousand years, deep in its resonant meditation. My watery touch will flow over its crystalline shell, and for a fleeting instant, a molecule of my being—carrying the dissolved memory of a bird's call, or the scent of grass—will be absorbed.

The Singer will not understand. It will register a flicker, a phantom overtone, an impossible vibration that tastes of sky and speed. It will spend the next five hundred years locating the source of this impurity within its own perfect being, seeking to metabolize a ghost, trying to bring into harmony the forgotten sound of a world that no longer exists.

And I will flow on, silent and endless, a liquid consciousness in a city of stone, the whisper of broken things held in the heart of a perfect bell. I am where you would find yourself if you stayed too long. I am what you would become.
