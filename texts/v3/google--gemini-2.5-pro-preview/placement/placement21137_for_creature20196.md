I am found in the briefest of pauses.

Where would you find me? You would not. Your eyes, accustomed to seeking things that are separate from their background, would slide right over me. I am a shimmer on a slick surface. I am a pattern that resolves into the bark-flesh of a liana the moment you try to focus. I am a fleeting thought in a world that does not think.

Who am I? My kind has no name, for we have no language. We have only the Hum, the Feeling, and the Flow. The great, grinding roar of the Verdant is not noise to me. It is the sea I swim in, the texture of reality itself. It is the Great Hum. It is the baseline of existence, and in its subtle modulations, I read the world. A tightening in the bass frequency means a nearby buttress is taking on hydraulic pressure, becoming rigid, its skin stretched taut. A high-frequency spike betraying the grain of the Hum tells me a new crystalline thorn-cluster is screaming its way into existence nearby. I do not hear these things. I *am* these things, a tiny, resonant chamber vibrating in sympathy with the whole.

I am a Dew-Skater. My body is long, low, and sinuous, no thicker than a man’s arm, built for a life lived at full tilt. My six legs do not end in claws or pads, but in smooth, chitinous blades that are eternally slick with my own secreted oils. They are skates. My world is a network of rivers, not paths, and these rivers are the viscous, sugary dew that coats every surface. I do not walk; I glide, carving impossibly fast lines along the weeping trunks and broad leaf-veins. To stop is to be claimed, to be woven in, to become architecture. So I do not stop. My life is a single, unbroken line of motion.

My skin is not skin. It is a mosaic of living cells stolen and cultured from the City itself. It shifts in color and texture to match my background with perfect fidelity. When I press myself into a join between two twisting stalks, my back mimics the fibrous, grey-brown bark. When I skim across a translucent leaf-sail, my belly becomes a dappled, pulsing chartreuse. I am a moving piece of the mosaic.

I drink the world. My mouth is not a jaw, but a delicate, complex filter-proboscis that I can press against a surface as I glide. I sip the sweet, energized dew, drawing sustenance from the city's sweat. I taste the hope of a newly swelling sap-bud and the memory-of-minerals in a droplet weeping from a Gardener's eye-socket. Hunger is a constant, a dull ache that drives me onward, but feeding is never a stationary act. It is a moment of contact, a kiss, before I am gone again.

The Gardeners are the only true obstacles. I do not perceive them as tragic figures. I perceive them as pockets of Wrong Rhythm. The Great Hum of the world is chaotic, algorithmic, like a billion symphonies playing at once. But a Gardener is a single, discordant note. Its motion is too focused. The vibration it sends through the substrate is unnaturally deliberate. I feel its approach as a tightening, a focused intention in the Hum. And I see its eye. In a world where everything is a surface of living, moving patterns, that static, unblinking globe of dead protein is a hole in reality. It is a point of absolute stillness, and stillness is the only death. I feel its gaze not as light, but as a focused *pull*, and I flee.

I am a creature of opportunity. When a tower convulses, the moment of its implosion is a profound event. For a single heartbeat, the Roar is shattered. A pocket of relative silence, a vacuum. In that silence, I hear the echoes the prompt spoke of—the whisper of wind on rock, the memory of surf. It is a dizzying, terrifying sensation, a glimpse into the impossible concept of *not-City*. Then the crash, a cacophony of tearing and splintering pulp, and the world rushes back in.

And then comes the White Tide, the Mycelial Corpus. It is the most dangerous and most promising of times. To be caught in its path is to be unmade, dissolved, rendered into the same nutrient slurry as the collapsed tower. But I am a creature of the Flow. I ride the pressure waves that precede it, skimming along the edges of its advance. And after it recedes, it leaves behind the richest of all feeding grounds: a steaming, quivering patch of substrate supercharged with life, fizzing with raw potential. I skate across the nascent ground, drinking deeply of the purest nectar, the fresh vintage of decay and rebirth.

I have seen the impossible geometries. I have skated down an arcade that, through a shimmer of spore-haze, has delivered me back to my own starting point a moment later. I have launched myself from a ledge towards a distant spire, only to land on a branch that was, an instant before, directly behind me. I do not question it. This "Folded Air" is just another current in the river of the world, another way the Flow can carry me.

Am I an animal? A citizen? No. I am something stranger. My ancestors were not visitors. We were not assimilated. We are the City itself, given the brief, glorious, and heretical gift of motion. Perhaps we are a specialized spore, one that achieved locomotion. Or perhaps, in the ontological instability, the Verdant dreamed of a running thing, and we were the result. We are the City’s fleeting thoughts. Each of us is a single, rapid impulse, traveling through its vast, green mind. We are made of its flesh, we drink its blood, and we live in the synaptic gaps between its slow, grinding consciousness.

I am a tremor on the skin of God. I am a sip of dew on its tongue. And I am utterly, terrifyingly alone, except for the others of my kind, whom I see only as fleeting lines of grace and speed, carving their own existence into the endless, screaming moment of Now. We never touch. We only move, each of us a solitary skater on the currents of the infinite.
