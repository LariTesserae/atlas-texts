I am where the sound lives. My perch is a simple one, a lip of obsidian worn concave by the passing of my ancestors. I find myself here often, when the great crystals of the sky-ceiling are in their dimmest phase, a time of violet shadows that pools in the hollows of the city like thought itself.

My six limbs find their knowing on the rock. The rearmost four, the great anchors of my body, are splayed for balance, their terminal talons set deep in fissures that my eyes cannot see but my nerves have known for two full cycles of the sky's brightening. The spire beneath me is not a floor; it is my extension, the bone of my world and the bone of my being. My foremost limbs rest upon my knees, the three digits of each hand curled inward. My mandibles are still. I am listening.

First, I listen with my antennae. They sift the thick, heavy air, tasting every particle. I taste the ozone-sharpness from the high-grazers chewing on the mica-ferns near the summit. I taste the dry, chalky perfume of the calcite-blooms three terraces down, a slow explosion two generations in the making. And beneath that, I taste the familiar scent-signature of my own kin, the unique mineral tang of my lineage, echoing from the opening of our family chamber behind me.

Then, I listen with my great, purple-black globes. My sight is a translation of light into texture. I do not see color as a soft-worlder might; I see the frequency of stone. The hazy amethyst light reveals the internal stress-lines in the bridge-vines, the subtle fluorescence of a mineral-rich vein waiting to be carved, the shifting mosaic of a thousand other K'tharr moving across the city's face. They are a constellation of slow-moving geodes, their carapaces glittering with the histories of their meals—the milky quartz of the deep-moss eaters, the pyrite-flecked shells of those who favor the chittering swarmers, the deep purple sheen of elders like myself.

My own carapace is a map of a life long-lived. It is a burden of memory. This left shoulder, a smooth, deep amethyst, remembers a youth spent on the highest terraces, scraping the plumpest stone-blossoms. This spidery vein of silver-white quartz running down my right forelimb is the scar from a careless slip during my fifth molting, a time of terrifying softness. The carapace is a testament, polished by centuries of brushing against the walls of my home, scored by the scrapes of rivals and the clinging grips of my young. It grows heavier with every passing decade, thick with time. Soon, it will be my final, un-sheddable skin.

But the true listening is deeper. I press one of my locomotive feet flat against the obsidian, close my eyes—a gesture of focus, not of blocking light—and fall into the deepspeech.

The chittering chaos of the air fades. The scrape-tongue conversations, the whir of wing-cases, the distant *tink-tink-tink* of a young carver's hammer... they become a surface texture. The reality is the vibration. The Atrium speaks through my sole.

It is a slow, complex chord. I feel the rhythmic thrum of the hatchery, far below, where the stone-eggs of the next generation slowly gather the mountain's substance into themselves, gestating in geological warmth. I feel the grinding resonance of the communal pulverizing basins, where the day's harvest of grazers is rendered into edible grit. I can distinguish the signature of my own grandchild, T’chkk’al, her small, impatient talons tapping an unsteady rhythm on the far side of the plaza. I feel her youthful anxiety, a high-frequency tremor in the stone. I send a slow, deep pulse back to her through the rock, a vibration of calmness, of patience. *The stone is. We are. Be still.*

I am an Elder. A Memory-Keeper. My carving days are past; my hands no longer have the strength for the hammer, the precision for the chisel. My purpose now is to Be, and to Remember. Inside our chamber, the shed carapaces of my ancestors hang from the stone walls. They are not trophies; they are the library of my people. I can run a digit over the chest-plate of my great-great-grandsire and feel the story of the Great Flake-Off of the Seventh Dimming, a history carved in rhythmic notches by his own hand. We do not write; we are written upon.

My name is a sequence of resonant scrapes, difficult for a young throat. It has been spoken for more than a full Cycle. Soon, it will cease to be spoken at all. I feel the pull of it some dim phases, a low-frequency hum that seems to emanate from the deepest fissures of the spire. It is the call of the Chasms of Return. It is not an ending. It is a dissolution. It is the promise of becoming a part of the deepspeech itself—my minerals, my memories, my very resonance compacted back into the black, vitreous rock.

I will be carried to the edge, and I will be given back to the mountain's heart. My carapace, this heavy map of my life, will become a vein of strange, new crystal in the dark. Perhaps, eons from now, a carver from a lineage yet unborn will strike a new chamber and find a seam of unfamiliar brilliance. They will not know my name, but they will feel the resonance of my long life in their hands, a faint vibration of contentment in the hard, eternal truth of the stone.

For now, I am here. A living geode perched on a lip of obsidian, feeling the slow pulse of the crystal sky above and the slower pulse of the living stone below. I feel my grandchild's vibration settle into a calmer rhythm. I taste the air. The sound continues. The city lives. And I, for a little while longer, am one of its waking thoughts.
