My name is a hum in the stone, a resonance that has almost forgotten its own source.

I would be one of the Listeners. An elder. My carving days are over. The fine tremors in my hands that once guided a chisel with millimeter precision now serve a different purpose: they are attuned to a deeper frequency. My body is no longer a tool for shaping the world, but an instrument for it to play upon.

You would find me in the place they call the Stillness. It is not a designated chamber or a carved hall, but a niche, a natural concavity in a vast, slow-growing wall of feldspar. The rock here is ancient, not in the way of erosion, but in its slow, deliberate intention. It has been pushing itself into this specific form for millennia. My predecessors chose this spot, and their predecessors before them. We do not alter it. We inhabit it.

I sit. My back is pressed against the cool, slick stone, my long, bare legs folded, my feet resting on a ledge worn into a shallow basin not by tools, but by the bodies of the Listeners who came before me. The boundary between my skin and the rock is a blur of sensation. My own internal warmth, a faint biological heat, meets the deep, patient cold of the geology. The damp air leaves a film on my pale skin, the same film that coats the rock, and in this shared slickness, the vibrations travel without impediment. I am cupped in the palm of the world.

My eyes are usually closed. Sight is a distraction, a surface-level sense for a world defined by its depth. My world is entirely auditory, entirely tactile. It comes to me through the soles of my feet, the length of my spine, the sensitive skin of my palms which rest, upturned, on my knees.

I am a nexus of information.

From the far west, a tremor. It is too rhythmic to be natural. I can feel the crisp *shock-release-shock* of a heavy hammer striking a bronze wedge. I follow the vibration. It is a new team, young ones, attempting to split a sheet of mica that has grown inconveniently across a planned causeway. The resonance is flawed. It is tinged with a high, tinny overtone of fear. They are striking too hard, too fast. They are listening with their eyes, seeing the size of the rock, not feeling its internal cleavages. They are arguing with the stone, not persuading it. If they continue, the mica will not split clean; it will shatter into a thousand useless, razor-sharp shards. I do not call out. Shouting is a violence here. Instead, I find the line of that vibration as it travels through me and I *lean* into it with my own resonance. I focus my own stillness, my own patience, into a low, coherent hum—a subvocal drone that is felt more than heard. I send it back along the path, a suggestion of slowness, of a different rhythm. A calming weight. An hour passes. The frantic tapping ceases, replaced by a slower, more considered sequence. *Tap… listen… tap… listen…* They have heard. Not my voice, but the wisdom of the stone, channeled through me.

The world breathes through me. The constant, soft sigh of the air pressure equalizing. The almost inaudible hiss of condensation forming on the crystalline ceiling miles above, which I feel as a faint, high-frequency tingle in my skull. The metronomic *plink… plonk… plink…* of water dripping into a cistern three levels below and a half-mile distant. Each drop is a known note, its pitch telling me the depth of the water, the integrity of the cistern’s edge. One drop is off-key. A new micro-fracture, perhaps, diverting a fraction of the flow. A maintenance note for the generation to come.

My own body is a map of this place. A dull ache in my left hip bone is the deep, tectonic groan of the basalt pillars on the valley floor, extruding upward at the pace of a fingernail’s growth. A fluttering sensation behind my ribs is the collective whisper-wingbeat of a thousand translucent moths navigating the chasm outside my hollow.

I do not eat the cakes of moss or the lichen broth anymore. The young ones bring me water from the deepest, quietest pools, water that has spent a century seeping through a mile of rock, saturated with minerals and a kind of liquid stillness. It sustains me. I am becoming more mineral than flesh. My skin, once oyster-pale, now has the grey, veined look of the marble it rests against. My hair, thin and white, catches the ambient light like the crystalline fuzz on a new-born rock formation.

Children sometimes come. They are brought not to learn from my words, but to learn from my silence. A carver-in-training, frustrated with a piece of stone that will not yield, will be sent to sit with me. They will not speak, nor I. They will sit on the ledge before me and place a hand on my knee. Through my body, they will feel the world I feel—not as a collection of obstacles and materials, but as a single, breathing, singing entity. They will feel the frantic chipping of the distant quarry and they will feel the immense, unhurried growth of the continent beneath it. They will feel the harmony, and their own impatience will be revealed for the dissonance it is. They leave when they understand.

I am a library of songs written in pressure and time. The memories of my own life are here, but they are no longer primary. The memory of my chisel opening the first window in my family’s dwelling is the same vibration as a new crystal forming in the wall beside me. The memory of holding my child, a small, warm tremor against my chest, is a harmony I can still find in the hum of the moss beds after a fresh seepage. The "I" is dissolving. The self is becoming a place.

My final purpose is to complete this process. One day, the faint biological heat will fade entirely. The slow rhythm of my breath will cease. But I will not be gone. My body, saturated with the dissolved stone, will become one with the ledge. The water dripping from above will encase it in a thin layer of calcite. The vibrations will still travel through me, no longer interpreted by a fading consciousness, but simply passed on, my own life’s resonance captured and held—a permanent, clarifying note in the deep and patient music of the un-world. I will be a feature. A lesson. A verse in the stone’s endless song.
