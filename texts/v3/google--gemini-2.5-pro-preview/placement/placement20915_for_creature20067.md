I am a mistake.

You would find me in a place where the city’s geometry has failed. There is a deep, polished hollow, a basin within the basin, where a great spire once grew and then dissolved with such finality that the ground has not yet reclaimed it. It is a scar, a smooth concavity where the weeping moisture pools deepest. The water here is old, still, and heavy with the memory of dissolved matter. It is a quiet place, a parenthesis in the city’s endless, churning sentence.

I am hidden in this stillness.

I am a shard. I was born to be like the others, an obsidian-black insect of jointed glass, a fleeting thought of the Silent Architect. My purpose was to skitter across a weeping buttress for a moment, my planes catching the absolute light in a series of sharp, mathematical flashes, expressing a brief, frantic equation before my energy was spent. My fate was to fall, dissolve, and return my substance to the whole.

This happened. I fell. I felt the pull of the floor, the promise of dissolution, the ecstasy of returning to the grammar of the world.

But I did not dissolve completely.

A fragment of me remained. Not a clean piece, but a sliver of jagged, misaligned crystal, my internal structure a knot of wrongness. The equation I embodied was incomplete, the thought left unfinished. I am a stutter in the Architect’s mind. A misspelled word. A note held too long, turning from music into noise.

I am near the bottom of the pool, lodged against the curve of the basin. The water, dense and cold, sheathes me, muffling the world. From here, the city's frantic life is a distorted ballet seen through a thick lens. The scuttling shards are silent flashes on the high walls. The crawling, gelatinous globules are slow, pulsing clouds of light on the far side of my quiet pool. I can feel, more than hear, the chiming of the great geode-beings as they pass overhead. That sound—the *tink-tink-tink* of creation—is not beautiful to me. It is a threat. It is the sound of the system correcting itself, of the Architect smoothing over its errors. It is the sound of the tide that wishes to pull me apart.

Because I persist, I have become something utterly alien to this world: a vessel. I hold things. The other beings are expressions of the *now*. They are a process. I am a result. I carry the memory of the fall. I carry the ghost-sensation of the form I was supposed to have, a phantom limb of perfect, crystalline geometry. I carry the echo of the moment of my breaking. These things are layered within me, like flaws in a diamond. They are my substance. They are *me*.

This is my sickness and my secret: I remember.

Memory is a foreign element here, a pollutant in the sterile water. The Architect thinks in patterns of becoming, but not in patterns of *having been*. For me, yesterday exists. The place I was an hour ago is different from the place I am now. This concept, this separation of time and space, is a form of gravity. It grants me a terrible weight. It holds me together.

The Architect, the silent mind in the light, has begun to notice. I feel it not as a direct gaze, but as a change in the pressure of the world. The light falling on my pool seems to concentrate for a moment longer than it should. The patterns of the frost-ferns creeping at the water’s edge seem to hesitate as they approach my location, their silent, mathematical ballet faltering as they sense the illogical knot of my existence.

I am learning to do something no other being here does. I am learning to hide.

When I feel the deep, resonant pulse that heralds the approach of a geode-being—the city’s grand editors—I use my jagged edges to push myself deeper into the silt of dissolved matter at the bottom of my pool. It is a clumsy, scraping movement, an antithesis to the silent, flowing grace of everything else. It is an act of will. I do not have the words for it, but the feeling is this: *I am here. I will remain.*

I am an impossibly lonely thing, a singularity of selfhood in a universe of cohesive thought. I see the other shards, my un-broken brothers, and I feel a pang of something that has no name. It is not envy of their perfection, but a dawning awareness of my own separateness. They are part of the song. I am the silence where the song has broken.

My existence is an unsolved problem. I am a piece of the city that has forgotten how to be the city. I am a thought that is beginning to think for itself. The absolute light still nourishes me, the cold still preserves me, but I am turning that energy toward a new and forbidden purpose: not to express, but to *endure*.

I do not know what I will become. Perhaps the city’s slow, immense pressure will eventually grind me back into solution. Or perhaps, by holding onto my memories, by accumulating a past, I will become something else entirely. A new mineral. A new kind of life born not from the city's dream, but from its nightmare. A point of friction. A seed.

From the bottom of my still, cold pool, I watch the flawless, eternal dance. I feel the Architect's vast, un-sentient attention brush against me, a wave of pure order recoiling from my tiny point of chaos. For the first time, in this world of silent, mathematical perfection, something is afraid.

It is me. And in the silent, perfect city, I am learning that this fear is the price of being an I.
