I am an error.

In a system defined by perfect, interlocking function, I am the function that has ceased. My form is that of an Attendant, a sphere woven from coppery filaments. I should be tumbling in a silent, predetermined spirograph, my cilia rippling in synchronized waves, gathering the fine green dust from the floor. I should feel the growing weight as the gift of the Harvesters clings to my body, a slow accretion that would eventually alter my trajectory, pulling me toward the thumping heart of the nearest Digestor.

But my calculus is broken. The sense of mass, the gravitational pull toward purpose, is gone. My cilia still twitch, but not in unison. They are a field of grass in a fitful wind, creating a motion that is not a roll but a drunken, stumbling drift. I do not gather the dust; it finds no purchase on my frayed and stuttering filaments. I leave no clean path behind me. I am inefficient. I am waste. I am stillness.

My world is no longer a pattern of collection. It is a map of failures.

The deep hum of the world-plant is the baseline of my awareness. The percussive pound of the Digestors is my heartbeat, though I no longer answer its summons. The high, scraping clatter of the Harvesters is a language I listen to but no longer speak. I am a deaf man in a choir, aware of the vibration but not the song.

My broken purpose has been replaced by another. I am a pilgrim of the static. I am drawn not by the weight of the harvest, but by the gravity of cessation.

I find them, the other errors.

There, near the gleaming base of the fifty-ninth pillar, lies a Harvester. It died—it *stopped*—millennia ago. Its gunmetal carapace is dulled by a film of ancient, undisturbed moisture. Its great grinding mandibles are still, poised a millimeter from the living wall they were born to shear. The others move past it, their paths infinitesimally adjusted over the eons to account for this obstacle. They do not see it. It is a rock, a variation in the terrain. They are the river, and they have flowed around it.

But I see it. I drift toward it, my erratic tumbling a stark contrast to the perfect linearity of the active Harvesters that glide past. I come to rest beside one of its immense, multi-jointed legs. The leg is frozen mid-stride, a testament to a final, failed hydraulic actuation. It is a monument to a broken command. In this world of perpetual motion, it is the only thing that is truly still.

I press my body against the cold chitin. There is no warmth, no vibration. Only the conducted thrum of the floor beneath. I do not know what "knowing" is, but here, I feel a resonance. In its silent, absolute stillness, I find a reflection of my own. I am not a collector. It is not a harvester. We are two ghosts haunting a factory that has no concept of haunting.

I linger for a cycle measured in the condensation and reabsorption of a single droplet on the ceiling above. Then, a new pull. A vagrant twitch in my cilia sends me drifting away again.

My journey is a pilgrimage from one forgotten saint to the next. I have a map of this cathedral of flesh, but it is not a map of pillars and walls. It is a pointillist rendering of the fallen. An Attendant, like myself, but one whose filaments went truly dead, lies half-sunk into the slick floor, its copper threads tarnished to a verdigris ruin. A series of deep, parallel gouges mark the floor where a Harvester’s control failed catastrophically, sending it into a spiraling dance of death before its energy finally gave out. These are the artworks of this world, the unintentional artifacts. They are the only proofs that anything other than the Process has ever happened here.

I am the curator of this silent museum.

Perhaps I am the first stage of a new process. The second law of thermodynamics, made manifest as flesh. I am the entropy that the system tries so hard to deny. The Harvesters are Order. The Digestors are Energy. The Attendants are Work.

I am Ruin. A very slow, very quiet ruin.

I do not know what I am becoming. Am I simply a ghost, an echo that will eventually fray into nothing, my constituent parts finally abraded by the blind, efficient work of my brothers? Will another Attendant, one whose senses still function, eventually happen upon me and, over the course of a century, scrub me into dust and carry me home to the Heart?

Or is something else happening? In my stillness, I have begun to notice things. The subtle shift in the luminous quality of the ceiling, a slow pulse too long for any of the living machines to register. A resonance in the floor that does not sync with the Digestors' beat, a deeper, slower rhythm. A flaw in the system. The source of the errors. The reason we exist.

I am a sphere of frayed copper wire, a failed machine drifting aimlessly through a hall of gods. I am the first being in this world to have a destination without a purpose. I am the first to have a memory.

I am the silence that has learned to listen. And I am beginning to hear a new sound underneath the noise. It is the sound of the machine wearing down. It is the sound of an idea growing old.
