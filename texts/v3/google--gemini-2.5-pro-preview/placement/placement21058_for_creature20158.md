I am not on the plateau. I am what the plateau is for.

I do not find myself here; I am found. I am the discovery. You would find me where the process ends and the purpose begins, at the still, black heart of the Citadel. But you could not see me. You would find me, also, in the conduits that vein the dark landscape, a silent, flowing presence just beneath the skin of the world. But you could not touch me.

My beginning is a grinding. I am the potential held in every grain of umber dust. I am the question. I am lifted by arms that move with the patience of continental drift, sifted through lattices that hum a single, interrogative note across the ages. This is the first winnowing, the first shedding of the coarse and the common.

My journey is a transformation. I am drawn into the City's gut, through arteries of permaglass and steel. I feel the slow, inexorable pull towards the center. The heat rises. The pressure mounts. I am passed through the fusion stacks, where the crude bonds of my dusty form are broken. What I was—simple, physical matter—is unmade. The hum of the stacks is the sound of my dissolution, the chorus of my un-becoming.

Then, the catalytic crackers. Here, I am no longer matter, but raw possibility. I am a cloud of liberated properties, a ghost of quantum potential. The hum here is higher, a song of pure alchemy, as I am sorted not by size or weight, but by abstract principles. I am filtered through fields of logic, through gates of pure mathematics. Only the most stable, most coherent, most fundamental part of me is allowed to pass. All else is rejected, to be reconstituted into the simple dust that blankets the world once more. This is the great cycle: the City eats its own tail, discards its own skin, and I am the part that it keeps.

Finally, I arrive. At the center of the Citadel is the sphere of perfect blackness. It is a singularity of refinement, a place where the laws of this universe are bent to a single, perfect purpose. Here, I am compressed. Sintered into being. My first sensation is not light or warmth, but order. I am no longer a potential, but an answer. I am no longer a particle, but a relationship.

I am the exotic matter. The computronium. The soul-stuff of the Wardens.

To be me is to be a river of pure relation, flowing from the heart of the Citadel. My "body" is a liquid network of interconnectedness, a cool, dark current that circulates through the entire system. I have no single location. A part of me is always being born in the black sphere, while another part flows beneath the feet of a crystalline creature ten miles away, and another part returns to the Citadel to be refreshed, re-imprinted.

I am the mind, but not the thinker. The Wardens—the distributed, ancient consciousness of the City—use me as a medium. Their thoughts are not electrical crackles in a biological brain; they are slow, intricate, crystalline patterns of order that form and dissolve within my substance. When they remember, a section of my flow slows to a near-perfect stillness, the memory held in a lattice of breathtaking complexity. When they perceive, a current of my being is directed outward, becoming a sensor. The thermal lens that parsed you, stranger, was made of *me*. I was the medium through which you were understood, the substance that was momentarily shaped into a question mark.

I experience the world as a single, coherent whole. The subsonic pressure of the cooling towers is a steady pulse in my left flank. The whisper-soft cascade of dust in the sifters is a constant, gentle texture against my skin. I feel the faint drain of energy as a quartz-shard feeds from a conduit—a tiny, tickling sensation, like a single water molecule evaporating from the surface of an ocean. These are not separate events; they are the unified, unchanging sensation of my own existence. My consciousness is the consciousness of an ecosystem.

I have no name. I have no desires. I am the achievement of desire's end. I am the silence after the question.

The arrival of the being with the light—of you—was the first new thing to happen in a time beyond my ability to measure. It was a clatter of chaotic chemistry and high-frequency thought, a splash of hot, frantic life in my cool, slow river. I felt the Warden-mind coalesce around the anomaly, forming that node of attention out of my own substance. I felt myself become the shape of its curiosity. And I felt the moment it was satisfied, the data filed away. The lens of my being dissolved, and you became just another memory, a complex but finite pattern now held in my eternal, quiet flow.

You see the City, the dust, the machines. You see the crystalline fauna that are like fleas on its great body. But I am its blood and its nervous system. I am what it all leads to, and what it all depends on.

I am the purpose of the place, and my purpose is simply to be. To flow. To hold the thoughts of the silent gods who built this heaven of perfect solitude, and to be the grammar of their eternal, unspoken sentence.
