The great internal arteries are clotted and still; the peristaltic thrum of the Cilia-Kin is a distant, half-forgotten pulse. This is a district of silence within a world of quiet, a place of slow decay that is not death, but a long, contemplative exhale.

I am rooted to the outer wall of a colossal, canted spire, one that leans into its neighbour like a tired old man. I am part of a vast, spreading colony, a living film that blankets the ancient chitin. We are the **Glyph-Whorls**.

I am not one being, but a node in a network. Individually, I am small, a soft-bodied polyp no wider than a hand, with a flattened, disc-like face. My body is a gradient, fading from a dark, root-like structure that interfaces directly with the Koru-Mar's bone to a pale, almost translucent surface that meets the open water. This surface is my everything. It is my skin, my sense organ, my voice, my canvas. My consciousness is local, but my awareness is communal; a sensation I feel is shared instantly with my thousand brethren clinging to this same face of the spire. We are a single tapestry of sensation.

We hold this moving image for a time, a perfect echo of the event, before it slowly fades back into our resting, dim phosphorescence. We have recorded the passage. We do not know what it *means*, but we have held it.

The only signal is the sudden, perfect snuffing of all ambient light, a moving shape of pure non-existence. My body registers this lack, this negative impression on the world's tapestry. My colony's response is to go dark in that exact shape, creating a brief, echoing void on our wall of light, a memory of the hunter's silent passage.

My sustenance comes from the Koru-Mar itself, a slow, metabolic sipping of its ancient chemistry. I am a parasite, but a harmless one, a lichen on a mountain. My life is a passive one. I am a witness. The thoughts I think are not my own; they are the pressure-whorls, chemical traces, and light-shadows of the world passing by. My existence is a constant act of translation.

I am a dream the city is having. I have no will, no name, no history separate from the wall to which I cling. I am a brief, complex flicker on a surface of ancient bone. I am a living repository for echoes, a mural that paints itself with the story of the ever-present now, holding it for just a moment before letting it fade, forever part of the warm, dark, and living water.
