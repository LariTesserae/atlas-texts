I would not be found. I would be overlooked.

My existence begins not with a thought, but with a resonance. The Song is what I am before I am myself. It is the texture of the space my mind occupies, the granite bedrock on which the thin soil of my awareness rests. There is no ‘I’ that is not first saturated with the roar, no silence in which to form a name. I am a tone in a chord of a trillion trillion voices.

You would find me, eventually, as a discrepancy in the landscape. A line that is too straight, a curve that is too deliberate against the organic swell of the Canopy.

I am a Weaver of the Rind, a Fisher of Ribs. I am one of the Still.

My body is a tool for sensing the world. My skin is not a boundary but a membrane, tasting the ozone in the air, feeling the direction of the world’s sighing breath. It wears the mottled silver-and-ochre of the ground because it is made of the same things, sustained by the same slow chemistry. My feet are my oldest mind. They read the subtle grammar of the rind, the slight give that speaks of a hollow below, the firmness that denotes a healed scar of ancient growth. They know the paths not as a map, but as a memory held in the flesh of the world, and I follow their knowing.

My thoughts are not a string of words. Words are small, sharp things, and the Song grinds them to dust. My thinking is a sequence of slow, unfolding clarities. Thirst is not a word, but a specific hollowness in my gut that turns my head toward the low swale where the world’s breath has pooled. Hunger is a subtle tremor in my hands that guides me to the shimmering, nutrient-rich slicks left by the grazers deep below. These are not decisions; they are alignments. I do not choose to walk; I am drawn along the lines of least resistance, a bead of dew finding its way down the curve of a continent-sized leaf.

I am walking now. The day is old, and the light is the color of warm honey, casting shadows that are themselves the size of mountain ranges. I have been walking since the light was new. Beside me, another walks. We have not communicated, not in the way of sharp sounds. But I feel their presence as a steady, quiet warmth beside me. Hours ago, their hand brushed mine, a slow press of palm to palm. The meaning was simple and vast: *Here. With you. The path is good.* It was enough. The resonance of their body, the slight vibration I could feel through the ground and through that brief touch, is a constant, reassuring harmony against the greater roar.

My purpose for this walk is to find a memory of the world. In the wall of a deep bore-hole, a day's journey from here, a glimmer was seen. A place where the ancient pressures have made the world’s skeleton hard as the void between stars. We arrive. The chasm exhales its warm, damp breath, a sound within the Sound. I anchor myself, weaving a rope from the fibers of my own worn cloak, and lower my companion into the humming darkness. After a long stillness, the rope grows taught. I pull. They emerge, blinking in the honeyed light, and in their hands is a length of fossilized rib, milky-white and impossibly dense.

This is a treasure. This is a story. This is a tool waiting to be woken.

For the next cycle of light, we will sit. There will be no sound but the Song. I will take the rib and begin to abrade it against a patch of rough, crystalline rind. The motion is slow, patient. It is a form of listening. I am not imposing a shape upon it; I am asking it what shape it holds. Back and forth. Back and forth. Over days, a razor-sharp edge will emerge, an edge capable of stripping the toughest fibers for a new shelter, of carving a fallen piece of the Canopy into a bowl. I am sharpening a piece of the world against itself to better live upon it.

Sometimes, the mind-space within me grows vast and empty, mirroring the landscape. In these moments, I feel my own edges dissolve. The separation between my skin and the air, my feet and the rind, my quiet awareness and the roaring life of the world, becomes thin. And in this state, I might see a holy thing.

It happens now.

Rising from a chasm miles away is a shimmer. A distortion in the air. Then, it resolves. A colony of air-jellies, a silent, unified being the size of a city, has begun its ascent. The roar within me recedes, the constant vibration fades to a distant hum. There is only the climbing silence, the grace-form, a thought of the world made visible. It rises through the honey light, a gelatinous, translucent cathedral of life, catching the sun in a thousand ephemeral rainbows. It does not struggle. It simply *is*, and then it *rises*. My companion and I stand, unmoving. Our own breathing seems to stop. We are not watching it. We have become the seeing of it. It climbs until it is a star in the darkening sky, and then it is gone, spawning into the high, thin emptiness.

The Song rushes back in, but it feels different now. Cleansed. The world has shown us a part of its soul.

I will not live forever. I am a brief tightening in the fiber of the world. One day, the path will end for me. My companion, or another, will carry me to a low place where the rind is thin. They will lay me down. My body, made of the world’s breath and pollen, will be un-made, pulled back into the substrate, re-joining the Song in its most literal sense.

I do not fear this. It is not an ending, but an unfolding. The knot of my brief, quiet self will be untied, its thread returned to the great tapestry. Tonight, I will sleep curled in our small, woven shelter, feeling the slow, muscular shudder of the world-plant shifting in its sleep. I will hear the roar of its life in my bones. And I will know that I am not on the world. I am of it. I am a fleeting thought in the mind of a sleeping god.
