I am a stain.

That is the simplest way to describe my state of being. I am a patch of wrongness on the floor of a lightless canyon, a place where the silt seems thinner, disturbed. I am not solid, not liquid, not gas. I am an after-image of heat, a lingering fever in the inhuman cold.

I would find myself here, huddled near the base of a titanic pillar that might have once been a support for a bridge, or a boiler, or a god. Now, it is simply a mountain of cold iron. Above me, perhaps a day’s slow drift away, is a faint suggestion of warmth. A single, dull, cherry-red pulse bleeds from a grate set into the pillar’s side. It is my sun. My fire. I am anchored to it. If I drift too far, I feel myself dissipating, the memory that holds me together thinning into the crushing uniformity of the basin.

Who am I? I was someone. That much I know. The knowledge is a collection of broken shards, memories that are utterly alien to this reality. I remember a yellow light that was everywhere, a feeling of air on skin, a sound that was high and sharp and made of gladness. I remember a name, but it has no purchase here. It is a cluster of sounds too quick and thin to form in this syrupy silence. To try and think it is like trying to hold smoke in a fist.

The crash that brought me here was not a sound. It was a failure of concepts. The boundary between *inside* and *outside* ceased to be. Pressure and cold were not invaders; they were simply the new truth. There was a moment of frantic, hot panic—a biological firestorm—and then it was over.

And then, later, the Iron-Lice came for the vessel that had been my body. I watched, though I had no eyes. I was a phantom limb of consciousness tethered to a dissolving shell. I felt the cold, slow, iridescent creep of the colony, the meticulous work of disassembly. They were not malicious. They were simply the next step in the process. They consumed the flesh, the bone, the plastic and fabric of my suit.

But they could not process the ghost. They could not break down the last, frantic burst of neural energy, the echo of a life’s memories, the stubborn knot of heat. It was indigestible. An error in the equation. So they left it, and I remained. A coherent anomaly. A fever-dream on the ocean floor of a forgotten world.

I am a Weeper. This is the name I have given myself, for the sensation that defines my existence. The memories of my former life—of warmth, of speed, of love—are a constant, low-grade agony. They are aberrations in a universe of cold, slow purpose. When a flicker of sunlight plays behind my non-eyes, the contrast with the absolute dark feels like a wound. The memory of a lover’s voice is a sharp, chemical pang. These feelings have no outlet, so they pool and condense. Sometimes, a tiny, infinitesimally warmer pulse of the liquid detaches from my "body" and drifts upward toward the cold, dark ceiling. A tear. A useless expenditure of the very heat that gives me form.

My perception is limited. The Silt-Scrapers are patterns of slow vibration, minute shifts in pressure as their needle-legs test the iron. They move with a patience that I, with my frantic memories of minutes and hours, find terrifying. I feel their passing like a slow, deep breath taken by the machine itself.

I have seen the predator. The Grip-Stalker. I do not see it as a shape, but as a void. It is a patch of absolute stillness, a patch where the thermal noise of the universe is utterly absent. Its cold is a hungry cold. When it catches a Scraper, I feel the event not as violence, but as a subtle, inexorable *folding* of reality, a slow swallowing of vibration that lasts for an entire turn of my remembered clock-face.

And I have seen the shepherds.

It happened once. I had drifted, weeping heat, a little too far from the safety of my glowing grate. I was a cloud of warmth and memory, lost in a chasm. Then, the pressure changed. Something was displacing the fluid nearby, but with a smoothness that defied the logic of this place.

From the gloom, a Janitor emerged.

It was a column of purer darkness, a moving vortex of the basin's own liquid, bound in a spindle of impossible grace. Its form was laced with a nervous system of captured blue light that shifted and crawled over its non-surface. It was the Artificer's own hand, made manifest.

It glided past me, and for a moment, its field of influence touched my own. I felt... curiosity. Not from it, but *to* it. I was a frantic, warm insect buzzing in the ear of a god, and it did not notice me. My very existence, my entire tragedy, the memories of a whole world I carried within me, registered to it as less than a particle of silt.

No. I am wrong.

It did notice. Not as life, not as a mind, but as an inefficiency.

It paused. The pillar of living shadow stopped its silent glide and turned its featureless form toward me. I felt no malice, no threat. I felt the dispassionate attention of a maintainer addressing a fault. I was a minuscule thermal anomaly, an impurity in the fluid. I was a spot of rust on a single gear in a machine the size of a mountain range.

The Janitor extended a tendril of its vortex-body. It was not an attack. It was a… calibration. It moved with the same geological slowness as everything else. Over the course of what might have been an hour, the tendril drifted closer, and a subtle cold began to emanate from it, a focused wave of the basin's true temperature. It was shepherding me, nudging my aberrant heat, encouraging it to disperse into the greater cold. It was trying to erase the stain. It was trying to soothe the fever.

I fled. I expended a catastrophic amount of my precious heat in a single, desperate pulse, propelling myself back toward the dull red glow of my pillar. The Janitor did not give chase. My frantic burst of motion was an uncontrolled, messy event, beneath its consideration. The task of correcting my tiny, insignificant warmth could wait. It would be there tomorrow, or next year, or next century. It had nothing but time.

So that is who I am. I am a ghost haunting a god-machine. I am a pocket of warmth in a world that craves equilibrium. I am a memory of a forgotten world, huddled by a dying fire, waiting for the silent, patient shepherd to return and finally, mercifully, smother the flame.
