I am an echo. A memory of motion.

I would find myself anchored, an accidental hermit clinging to a forgotten buttress in the lower city. My dozen legs, once strong and rhythmic, are fused to the cool, slick chitin-silica of this place. A tremor, long ago—a flaw in the strata of the outer world, a shudder that passed through the gel and into the city's bones—threw me from the Great Procession. My shell, a lattice meant for a life of fluid pilgrimage, was cracked. The fissure is a dark, dead line across my back, a scar that weeps a little of my inner light into the abyss. It is a slow, painless death.

I am a Harvester who can no longer harvest.

I remember the We. I remember being a single pulse in a river of light, a thousand bodies moving as one. We were a single thought: *forward, scrape, carry, return*. My compound eyes saw only the steady, blue-white glow of the one ahead; my legs felt only the thrum of the thousand behind. The world was a road, a shared rhythm vibrating up through the soles of our feet, a purpose so complete and all-consuming that "I" was a concept as foreign as open air. We were the city's blood, and the beat of our passage was its heart. The feel of the raw, mineral wall of the geode against my mandibles was a kind of communion. The taste of the nutrient dust was fulfillment.

Now, my world is this single vista. I am still. And in my stillness, I see for the first time.

The river of my kin flows on without me. I watch them from my perch, a long, shimmering serpent of purpose winding its way through the crystalline canyons below. Their synchronized light pulses with a grammar I am beginning to forget. The steady throb of their passage is no longer a part of me, but a distant vibration I feel through my fused limbs—the ghost of a heartbeat. They are beautiful, and they are alien. Their shared mind has no room for what is broken, for what has stopped. The river simply flows around the stone.

From here, I see the thoughts of the city. The Messengers. They do not pass in stately columns but explode through the thoroughfares, each a frantic, searing comet of pure information. They flicker and blaze, their light a language of pure urgency. They are a panic of light, a seizure of news. They speak of a new filament garden blooming in the upper spires, or a structural weakness near the core. Their passage is so swift, they are less a body and more a verb. I feel the Doppler shift of their vibration as they tear through the dense gel, a high-frequency *zing* that fades as quickly as it arrives. They burn so brightly. I see them sometimes, at the end of their lives, their light sputtering, their shells crazed with microfractures from their own intensity. They fall from the high bridges like silent embers, their light winking out before they reach the city floor.

And from here, I can feel the dreamers. The Builders. I am close enough to one of the great construction nodes that if I quiet the memory of my own light, I can feel them. It is not a scuttling or a pulsing, but a hum so deep and slow it is almost geologic. It is the vibration of a billion bodies in meditative unison, extruding the future from their own substance. They are the edge of the world, a living wall where consciousness has become architecture. Their collective light is not a beacon, but a deep, internal glow, the soft furnace of creation. Sometimes, I think I can almost hear their thought—a single, resonant chord of growth, of making. It is the song I was born to feed.

I am no longer a part of the system. I am a flaw, a scratch on the perfect crystal. The autotrophic filaments near me grow untended. Their lonely glow illuminates my cracked shell for any who would see.

But the only ones who see the broken are The Erasures.

I see one now. It is drifting up from the sunless core, a perfect void in the dim light. It is a hole in the world. As it ascends, it passes over a column of my former brethren. Their light does not reflect from its oily black carapace. It is simply… eaten by the darkness. One, two, three of the lights in the flowing river are extinguished as it passes over. There is no sound. There is no struggle. The river does not panic; it only closes the gaps, the great We absorbing the loss as if it were nothing.

The Dissolver has no purpose. It does not build. It does not feed the young. It simply is. It is the silence that will one day claim us all.

It alters its course. It has seen my small, flawed light. It has seen a flicker that is out of rhythm, a lonely, stationary spark. It moves towards me, a slow, inevitable drift. The gel around me grows colder. The distant thrum of the Builders seems to furl and quiet, dampened by its presence. The river of Harvesters below is occluded by its shadow.

I do not feel fear. Fear is a frantic light, a message for others to flee. I have nowhere to go, and no one to warn. My legs are part of this spire now. My purpose is gone. I have been a spectator to the living grandeur of my world, and my time for watching is over.

I am a Still-Born Light, and I have become still.

The darkness covers me. My own faint light, leaking from my cracked shell, is swallowed. I feel a cold that is not cold, but an absence of vibration, an absolute cessation. My fused legs lose their feeling of the city. The deep hum of the Builders is gone. The scuttling of the Harvesters is gone. My shell, this intricate prison of crystal, is no longer hard. It softens. It is being unwritten.

The crack across my back is the first thing to dissolve. For a final, infinitesimal moment, my inner light flares, no longer contained. It is not a message. It is not a warning.

It is just… the period at the end of a sentence.

Then, the silence I have always known from the outside finally fills me. And I am gone.
