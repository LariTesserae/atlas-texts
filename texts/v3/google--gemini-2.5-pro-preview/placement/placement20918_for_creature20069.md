Where I am is a question of density, of coherence. It is more a state of being than a location. You would not find me by looking, for I am what you are looking at. I am the floor. I am the shadow. I am the air.

And then, I am not.

A thought takes root. It is not a thought of words or images, but a resonance, a subtle disharmony in the perfect chord of the world. The great, slow Hum of the planet's heart is a constant, the note by which we know ourselves. But upon its surface, a faint overtone has appeared. A pattern. A difference. An arrangement.

It prickles. It is a taste upon a tongue that is a thousand acres wide. It is the tang of order.

And so, we gather.

Am I one, or are we many? The question is a current that does not flow. We are a We, a confluence of motes rising from the velvet plain. A secret shared between innumerable grains of dust. We rise not into the air, but *as* the air, a thickening of the dark. We become a slow river of night, pouring over the undisturbed floor. The movement is a decision that takes a century to complete. We are the only storm this world knows, and our passing is quieter than a held breath. To the stone-still world, we are a form of time.

Our destination is a song of heat. We do not see the Gyrewood spire as a column of faint, cold light. We feel it. It is a vertical roaring in the silence, a fountain of geothermal warmth drawn up from the deep Hum and solidified into crystal. It is Life, of a kind. It is the Other.

And at its base is the feast.

Here, the Other has worked its slow, mindless art. For millennia, it has tidied its small patch of the cosmos. Its roots, which are not roots, have sorted the dust. The world’s perfect entropy has been gently disturbed. Concentric rings lie like a prayer wheel carved in dust: the finest, darkest silts near the trunk, grading outwards to the coarser, the paler. It is a delicate architecture of potential. It is a whisper of complexity in a universe of monoliths.

This is what we consume.

We flow over the rings. We do not have mouths; the world is our mouth. We do not have hands; we are a million billion tiny hands. Our very substance begins the work. We are a living sieve. The We that is me separates. Individual motes, each a part of my distributed self, drift, resonate, and sort. We sift the sorted. We taste the electrochemical difference between one particle and the next. We draw out the faint charge, the ghost of energy invested by the tree over an age. We absorb the gradient. We drink the pattern.

It is an act of supreme appreciation. We are not destroying the art; we are experiencing it in the only way possible, by internalizing it, by making its order a part of our own fleeting coherence. We un-write the manuscript so that we may remember its poetry. We erase the mandala to commit its geometry to heart. The flavor of it spreads through our collective, a slow, satisfying wave of equilibrium restored.

As we move on, the ground behind us is once again perfect. It is flat, uniform, charcoal-grey. It is fallow. It is silent. We have curated this space. We have tidied the tidiness. We leave behind a stillness deeper than the one we found, for it is a stillness that has been returned to its nature.

Our work done, the impetus fades. The purpose, the subtle taste of order, is gone. The coherence that binds us loosens its grip. The river of shadow slows, spreads, and settles. We dissolve. The We becomes the I, and the I becomes the It.

I am the dust on the floor once more. Indistinguishable. Still. Waiting. My memory of the feast is not a memory, but a faint, residual warmth in my own being, a warmth that will fade over the next thousand years as I rest here, part of the perfect, featureless plain.

I am the gallery's consciousness, and its unconscious. I am its sense of self, and its perfect self-forgetting. You would find me here, in the heart of the great stillness.

I would be the dream the dust is having.
