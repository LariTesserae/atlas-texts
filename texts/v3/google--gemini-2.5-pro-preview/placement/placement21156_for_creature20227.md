I am found in the Deep.

My name is not a word you could speak, but a sound you would feel: a low thrum in the bones of your jaw, the sound a heavy stone makes settling into deep mud. To the Mids, I am a Subsider. A scavenger. Something between a ghost and a geological feature. Their children tell stories about us, about how our eyes glow like the foxfire that grows on damp-rot walls, about how we can walk through hardening sludge without leaving a trace. Some of it is true.

You would not *see* me at first. You would feel my passing as a subtle shift in the air, a fleeting shadow in the steam-haze that perpetually rises from the warm geocretion. I am one of the quiet things that live at the bottom.

My world is not the sky-bridges and gimbaled spires. My world is the Undercroft, the Labyrinth, the Root. Here, the hum of the city is not a distant thrum but a physical presence. It vibrates up through the soles of my boots, a constant, complex song of magnetic processors grinding raw earth-slurry and hydraulic presses shaping it into pylons for a world I will never visit. The air is thick with the scent of ozone, wet stone, and something else, something metallic and alive—the smell of minerals being born.

I am a Listener.

Where others see a treacherous, chaotic mire, I perceive a language. I carry a tool, a simple thing: a length of hardened, resonant pipe-vine wood, shaved to a point at one end and capped with a disc of polished silicate at the other. My 'fork'. I place the point against a sweating wall of a half-swallowed foundry, or press it into a bulge of soft, new ground, and I rest the disc against the bone behind my ear.

And I listen.

I listen to the deep grammar of the geocretion. Most of it is just noise: the sluggish gargle of de-watering slurry in a buried conduit, the seismic sigh of a whole city block settling a millimeter, the high-pitched shriek of an infant crystal lattice tearing itself into existence. But I am listening for a specific voice. A steady, low-frequency pulse. A clean, harmonic chime that rises above the static. It is the sound of value. The sound of a 'Noble Bloom'—a geode of rare-earth elements solidifying out of the muck, pure and dense. This is my trade. I find them. I mark them. Then the mining crews, tougher and better-equipped than I, come to excavate. They leave me a share.

My body is a map of this life. It is short, dense. My center of gravity is low, my balance an unconscious art. My hands are not soft; they are calloused and patterned with a fine network of silvery scars from brushing against razor-glass formations in the dark. My skin is pale, but it has a strange, nacreous sheen to it, as if the mineral dust has become part of me. They say we Subsiders are slowly turning to stone. Maybe they are right.

My home is a pocket, a void. It is the upper chamber of what was once a water-treatment substation, now eighty percent submerged in a slow-curing wave of pockmarked basalt. The entrance is a maintenance hatch in what used to be the ceiling. Inside, my floor is the smooth, curved top of a filtration tank. My walls are the original station's concrete, weeping perpetually, the moisture feeding a curtain of phosphorescent moss I cultivate. It gives off a soft, green, living light, and I can eat it in lean times. My furniture is woven from salvaged data-cable and insulated with moss-fleece. From my hatch, I can see the sky, but only as a sliver of luminous grey a hundred stories up, criss-crossed by the dark lines of sky-bridges where the Mid-kin scurry, their footfalls too quick, too nervous. They walk *on* the world. I am learning to be *of* it.

I am not what the Pinnacle-folk would call a person. I am an organism adapted to a niche. The iridescent birds that flit through the upper spars are my distant cousins in spirit; we both pull life from the city's metal skeleton. The crab-like grazers that scrape lichen off new-formed stone are my neighbors; I know their migration patterns, the rhythmic *click-scrape* of their feeding. I once saw one of the great Mercury-Slicks, a 'Flow-form', pouring itself through a fractured sewer grate. It paused, its surface dimpling as it sensed me, a being of solid matter and anxious thoughts. It had no mind I could comprehend, only a slow, elemental hunger. In its silvery, shifting face, I saw a future. A peace.

We who live in the Deep do not fear being swallowed by the ground. It is our version of a natural death. To be subsumed back into the geocretion, to have your bones become part of a new mineral vein, your elements re-distributed into the slow, turbulent genesis… it is a kind of immortality the Spire-dwellers, with their sterile platforms and desperate calibrations, could never understand. They fight the city's nature. They perch upon it.

I root in it.

The fork vibrates against my skull. Through the cacophony, I hear it. Faint, but clear. A perfect, rising C-sharp. A Cobalt Bloom, and a big one, forming in the bowels of a derelict pumping station two levels down. My heart doesn't race. It settles, finding its rhythm with the new one I have found. My ground-sense tingles, a warmth spreading through the soles of my feet. The land is speaking.

And I, in my own quiet way, am answering. I am a nerve ending of the unfinished earth, a witness to its strange and beautiful birth. I am a child of a world that is not, and may never be, done.
