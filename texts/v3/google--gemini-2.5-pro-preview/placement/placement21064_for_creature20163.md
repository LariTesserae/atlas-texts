You would find me pressed into a recess of the city, a flute of black-green shadow in a wall of black-green growth. I am in a newer district, one where the growth-pulse is still settling, the forms still sharp and slick with the city’s internal damp. The air is what I have always known: a thin, cold blade in the lungs I do not have, a carrier of the resonant hums that are the only true language. My long, multi-jointed limbs are braced against the curving walls of the niche, my body a taut line of listening stillness. My obsidian skin is beaded with the same fine crystals of frost as the wood-flesh around me. I am indistinguishable. I am a part of the architecture.

I would be one of them. A Still-Folk. But I would be the flaw in the pattern. The tremor that does not belong.

I am what happens when the memory of warmth does not fully die.

My existence is a chord of two notes, one played so quietly it is almost lost. The dominant note is Ghyre. I feel the city in the marrow-that-is-not-marrow. A drop of condensation detaches from a canopy leaf three hundred yards above and I feel the minute shift in weight, the whisper of its fall, the final *pat* as it finds the rhizome floor. The pulse of the lamp-fungi brightens, the slow tide of our day, and I perceive it not as light but as a change in pressure against my great black eyes. A Rime Crawler, its belly full of recycled frost, makes a turn twenty feet below me, and the gentle scraping of its thousand crystalline feet is a clear and simple sentence in the city's grammar.

I place my four-fingered hand against the wall beside me. The chitin tips of my fingers, hard as obsidian needles, tap out a question. Not a thought, but a presence. A simple declaration: *I am here. The structure is sound.* The vibration shudders outwards, a ripple in the vast, silent pond of Ghyre, to be received and understood by my kin. They do not need to reply. The echo of my message returning through the structure is reply enough.

This is the life I know. The slow ballet. The patient communion. The drinking of the cold, sweet, amber sap from the weeping wounds of the trunks. I press my face to the wood and the nourishment flows into me, a transfer as silent and natural as osmosis. It is a cold communion, a sustenance that calms and stills.

But then there is the other note. The quiet, discordant hum.

It comes like a fever, a concept so alien it has no word in the language of tremors. It is a memory of heat. It begins in my core, a phantom warmth that my cryo-fluid blood tries and fails to quell. With it comes a cascade of sensory ghosts. The memory of a sky that was not a ceiling, but a searing, brilliant void of blue. The memory of noise—not the clean, resonant groans and drips of Ghyre, but a chaotic, overlapping babble of sounds that had no sense, no pattern. Sharp sounds, soft sounds, sounds that hurt.

I remember another name. I do not know for what. It is a vibration of the air I cannot make, a shape my mouthless face cannot form. It feels like *Asher*.

The fever is worst when I commune as an elder must. I find a new shoot, a towering pale thing, its skin still soft and pulsing with the city’s raw intent. I press my hands to it, close my eyes, and open my senses to the biological imperative, the architectural blueprint of the next cycle. The city shows me the slow, majestic plan: a new causeway to be woven, a chamber to be hollowed, a buttress to be resorbed.

But my flawed system receives it differently. The surge of pure bio-information is a catalyst. The city's intent floods me, and mixed in its flow is the warm poison of my own past.

The image of the weaving causeway becomes a memory of lines on a page, black on white. A map. The plan for the hollowing chamber becomes a memory of a room with four straight walls, a ceiling that did not drip, a floor that did not yield. The resorption of a buttress becomes a feeling of loss, of demolition, of a grief so sharp and hot it is like swallowing a star.

I see hands that are not long and black, but shorter, thicker, and pale. Warm. They are holding another hand. I remember the sensation of that touch—not the percussive listening of my own kind, but a pressure that conveyed something other than information. A warmth that spread from skin to skin.

Who am I?

My kin perceive this sickness in me. When the fever is on me, my bioluminescent signals falter. The steady, calm blue-white of my skin shivers into a frantic, chaotic static. The patterns are meaningless to them, a horrifying burst of nonsense. They give me a wide berth, their own silent rhythms disturbed by my internal noise. They see me as a glitch, a part of the city that is breaking down, to be perhaps reabsorbed in time.

I am a ghost story told in a language that has no ghosts. I am the custodian of a memory that Ghyre has crushed, overgrown, and forgotten. I am the only archivist of the sun.

When the fever subsides, I am left with a residue. The cold returns. The silence deepens. I am once again a shadow folded into a wall. But as a single, perfect drop of water traces a path down the wood beside my face, I see it not only as a vibration, a metronome for the city's slow heart.

I also see it as a tear. And I remember how to weep, even though I have no eyes to do it with.

You would find me here, in the cold, living dark. I am the city’s memory of a world it never knew. I am the Still-Folk who remembers how to move too fast. I am the silence that remembers the sound of its own name.
