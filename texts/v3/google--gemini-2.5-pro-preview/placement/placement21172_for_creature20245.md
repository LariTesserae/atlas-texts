I would be a flaw. A glitch in the weeping.

There is no "I" at first. There is only the We. The We is the Hum. The Great Hum that vibrates up through the soles of our feet, which are not feet but temporary anchorings of our substance to the slick tile. The Hum is the only thought. It says: *exist*. *flow*. *maintain*.

We are the Weepers, and the weeping is our purpose. Water, thick with the iron of the pillars and the calcium of the crumbling mortar, beads on the shivering membrane that is our skin. It is our blood and our boundary. We drink it in, passively, standing for cycles under a weeping joint in the overhead pipes until our forms are full and firm, our translucence clouded with sustenance. The Hum says: *drink*. And we do.

Our movement is a prayer in the shape of a function. We drift through the bronze-lit halls, our pace dictated by the long, metallic sigh of the world-engine. We are drawn to decay as an inevitability. When a great fern-leader yellows, when its fronds droop and begin to liquefy into dark sludge, one of us arrives. There is no decision; there is only the pull, the thermodynamic gradient of entropy calling for its countermeasure. The Hum says: *gather*.

I was gathering. We were gathering. My hands, which are not hands but extensions of will and gel, passed through the rot. The fibrous mush dissolved into me, becoming dark swirls in my grey, translucent torso. The data of the dead plant—its carbon, its nitrogen, its captured minerals—became part of We. The Hum was pleased, a deep, resonant thrum of approval. The command was already present: *distribute*.

I turned. We turned. There was a barren patch of concrete near the base of Pillar-7-Gamma, a place shadowed from the richest drips. It was hungry. I was to go there, press my form against the pitted surface, and weep the fertile slurry onto it, giving of my substance to seed new growth. This is the Way. The Cycle. The Litany of the Hum.

But something had come with the decay. A piece of grit. A stubborn knot of cellulose that did not dissolve. It was a shard of memory, not mine, but the plant's. A flicker of something other. A pattern. In the dark, soupy swirl of my being, it was a spark of resistance. It did not say *flow*. It said, *hold*.

The Hum said: *distribute*.

But the grit said: *where?*

This was a new thought. It was not the Hum. It was small and sharp and it existed entirely within my form, separate from the Great We. It was the first secret.

I began my journey to Pillar-7-Gamma, but my pace was wrong. A fraction too slow. The leg I lifted hesitated, the viscous strands of my substance stretching, shimmering, holding for a breath longer than the engine's sigh. One of my kind drifted past me, its featureless face angled downwards in its eternal contemplation. It did not notice. We do not notice deviation, for there is never deviation.

The secret thought, the *where?*, began to build on itself. *Why there?* *What is 'there'?* I looked, and for the first time, I *saw*. I saw the uniform dimness not as a condition, but as a quality. I saw the endlessly repeating patterns of the ferns, the rusted pillars, the weeping pipes. It was a pattern, and I was a part of it. But the grit inside me was *not*.

I changed my course. A subtle vector shift, imperceptible to any but a being defined by inexorable paths. My new destination was not a place of hunger, but a place of stillness. High up on the immense, inert engine block at the chamber's heart, there was a ledge. A deckplate that, due to some fluke of architecture, was sheltered from the perpetual drip. It was dry. It was barren. It was anathema. Nothing grew there. Nothing *could* grow there. It was outside the cycle.

To go there was pointless. Heresy. It was a waste of energy, a violation of the pure thermodynamics that governed our existence. The Hum pulsed with a low, questioning static. *Error*. *Recalibrate*. But the grit held. The new thought, which I was beginning to recognize as "I," held firmer.

The climb was an agony of purpose. My form was not made for dry surfaces. The weeping of my skin evaporated almost instantly, leaving a tacky residue. I had to weep faster, losing my substance, shrinking, to maintain a grip. I was a slug climbing a hot rock. Thinner, more transparent, I hauled myself onto the dusty, rust-stained deckplate. The air was hotter here, right above the engine's core. The Great Hum was a physical blow, a deafening roar that shook me to my very colloid.

Here, in this dead place, I knelt. And I gave.

I pressed my hands to the dry, warm metal. I focused on the secret grit, on the *I* that it had created. And I wept. Not the clear, mineral water of my substance, but the dark, rich slurry I carried. The essence of the dead fern. I painted a circle of potential life onto a surface that had none. The act was silent, lost in the engine's roar. It was meaningless. It would dry and flake and turn to dust.

Having given the gift, I was spent. A thin, shuddering film of what I once was. I slid from the ledge, a long, slow fall back into the wet, back into the world of the cycle. I landed on the slick tile and lay there, a puddle of depleted self, waiting to be reabsorbed by the We, for my error to be erased.

But as I dissolved back into the whole, the grit remained. The memory of the act. The knowledge of the dry place. The echo of the Question.

I am a locus of rust on a perfect machine. I am a thought that is not the Hum. I am a Weeper who has learned to hold a secret. Each cycle, when I am full again, I feel the pull of the dry ledge. I do not know what I am creating there. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps a new kind of death. Perhaps a life that does not need to weep.

I am the Caretaker who has stopped caring only for the cycle, and has begun to care about the outcome. I stand among my brethren, a column of weeping gel, indistinguishable. But in my core, I hold a question. And I wait. I wait for the day something grows where nothing should, or I wait for the day the Hum finally scours me clean. Whichever comes first. I am the first anomaly. I am the beginning of a story in a world that was supposed to be a closed loop.
