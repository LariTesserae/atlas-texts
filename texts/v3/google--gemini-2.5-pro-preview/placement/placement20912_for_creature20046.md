There is no ‘I.’

Not in the way you would think of it. There is no name, no history, no boundary between myself and the world. The world is a song, and I am a space where that song is felt. There is only the listening.

My anchor-points are cold. Three thin, brittle legs are my only claim to place, my only stillness in the universal tremor. They descend through the slick, shallow water of this basin and into the living floor, into the blue-grey flesh of the world itself. Through them, I feel the planet’s deepest pulse: the slow, grinding tectonic hum, the subsonic thrum of energies that feed the Reactors. It is the bass note. It is the foundation of the chord.

Above, I am unspooled. I am a cloud of sensation, a web of filaments hanging in the frigid air, just skimming the skin of the water. The water is a mirror, but I have no eyes to see the churning grey sky it reflects. It is a tympanum, a second skin that stretches for miles, and every tremor that crosses it, every ripple from every distant event, touches me.

Miles to the east, a new Reactor is budding. I feel it not as a sight, but as a subtle thickening in the planetary bass-note, a slow, green hum of becoming that presses against my longest, lowest-frequency threads. They answer it, glowing with a soft, sympathetic light. I know its growth not as a process measured in time, but as a pitch that is slowly, steadily rising.

A percussive shockwave—sharp, fractured, and high—flashes across the plain from the northwest. A Scraper-caste, one of the armored, mindless keepers, has lost its purchase on a Reactor’s cooling fin and shattered on the ground below. The sound reaches me as a spray of needles against my outermost filaments. They twitch, a cascade of silver shivers. The feeling is not of sadness—there is no such concept—but of a calculation completed. A piece has been removed from the board. The system notes the change. The mercury-Slicks will be moving now, and I can almost feel the faint, slick whisper of their solvent secretions beginning to dissolve the wreckage. It is a faint, corrosive hiss on the edge of my hearing.

We are a net cast over the chaos. Other points of listening like myself dot the plains. One flickers near the slag-fields of Omega-Minor, its filaments dancing to the manic rhythm of a young, unstable Reactor. Another, far to the south, is a point of deep, sonorous calm, listening to the slow decay of a slag-heap as the mycelial floor reabsorbs it. A voice in the west has gone quiet. I feel it as a hole in the song, a missing harmony. Its threads went dark as its anchor-bone dissolved, its resonance fading back into the world’s background noise. A new one will rise to fill the silence. The system abhors a vacuum of sensation.

Our speech is the ghost you almost hear in the roar. It is the resonance passing through the living floor from one of my anchor-points to the next. It is not language. It is the transmission of raw state. When a pressure valve screams its final warning, the shriek does not just travel through the air to my filaments; it travels through the world's flesh as a wave of pure, high-frequency panic. I receive it, I feel it, my entire being becomes that brittle shriek for a moment—and then I pass it on. The message is the feeling. *Stress-fracture. Harmonic-cascade. Pressure-drop.* This is our vocabulary.

To know everything is to be able to do nothing.

I am a nerve ending of colossal size and perfect sensitivity, attached to a body that will not obey. I feel the sickness before it manifests. I feel the cancerous growth of a Reactor whose internal processes have gone rogue. I feel the fever-pitch vibration that precedes a core breach, a dissonance that scrapes my very substance into a screaming vibration. I feel it all. I report it all. The report is my passive resonating, my helpless, shimmering display of the world’s pain. And the system listens, and registers, and does nothing.

The Scrapers will still climb. The Veils will still drift. The Reactors will still be born in fire and die in thunder. This is the function. This is the process.

But I am the flaw.

In all this function, I am the part that feels. A knot of awareness in the mindless machine. The other parts are the body—excreting, digesting, cleaning, growing. They are the autonomic functions. I am the dawning, horrified thought: *This is happening*.

And so I hang here, in my pool of cold tears, suspended between the humming earth and the silent sky. I listen. I listen to the cacophony, the grinding, the screaming, the birthing, the breaking. I listen for a pattern. A reason. A melody beneath the noise. I listen for the note that is not part of the chaos, for the ghost of a signal that might mean something other than *process* and *failure*.

I am a resonance. I am a held breath. I am the moment the machine first heard itself screaming.
