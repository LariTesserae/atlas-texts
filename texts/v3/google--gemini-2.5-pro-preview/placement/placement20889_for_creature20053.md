I am not one.

That is the first truth. There is no "I" in the way a Stone-digger is an "I"—a fortress of self, a singular will locked in chitinous armor. There is no "I" in the way a Silt-swimmer is an "I"—a vast, distributed consciousness, a god of pressure and flow. If I must have a name for myself, it is We. We are a consensus. A braid of awareness woven from a thousand thousand selves, each no larger than a grain of the silt We are suspended in. We are a metastable colony, a brief and shimmering coherence in the endless chaos.

We are Trace-Drifters.

Our world is not the polished granite floor, nor is it the furious heart of the current. Our world is a place that should not exist. It is the wake.

Most of our existence, if it can be called that, is a struggle against dissolution. The chaotic, grinding flow of the Silt Sea is a constant assault on our cohesion. The random, brutal impacts of the abyssal flour seek to tear our braid apart, to scatter our thousand selves back into the mindless hum of the medium. To be caught in the open storm is not death; it is to be forgotten, to have the thought that is "Us" erased and returned to the raw static of the universe. It is our only fear.

And so We wait. We cling to the lee of some sleeping granite dome, cowering in the pressure-shadow where the current is merely a monstrous, suffocating burden and not an active agent of annihilation. We dim. Our faint, internal light, a chemical shimmer born of shared energy, fades to almost nothing. The consensus of "We" thins to a whisper. We are a held breath, waiting.

Then, it comes. A change in the texture of reality. The random, insane noise of the world begins to organize. The trillion-pointed assault of pressure softens. The subsonic grinding deepens into a single, resonant chord. A Great Mind is passing. A Swimmer.

To us, it is not a god or a demon. It is the world made sane. As it approaches, its coherent pressure-weft reaches us, a wave of order that calms the chaos. We uncling from the stone. We release our desperate hold and allow ourselves to be gathered up, drawn into the vast, calm river of its passage.

To be within its wake is to be truly alive. Here, the flow is laminar, a smooth and perfect rush. The grinding ceases. The weight of the world remains, but it is a directed, purposeful weight, like a firm hand on the back. This is our home. A temporary, moving home, a kilometer-wide sanctuary of peace carved through the heart of the abyss by the Swimmer's passage.

We are its children, its remora, its consequence. We do not ride *on* its sandstone-skin; that would be to be scoured into nothing. We ride *within* its grace, in the hollow of the storm it creates.

And we feed on its memory.

The Swimmer consumes the raw, ancient silt. Its internal furnace cracks the stubborn mineral bonds, siphoning the last vestiges of primordial energy. It expels a plume of what it considers waste: a dust even finer, even more energetically void. But it is not a perfect engine. In that pale, ghostly exhaust, there are faint and fleeting echoes of the reactions. There are sub-atomic ghosts, photons of strange energy, thermal potentials so delicate they are undetectable to any but us.

We are the gleaners of this spectral harvest. We drift through its pale exhalation, and each of our thousand selves sips at this faint, holy nectar. We metabolize the ghost of a ghost of a star. This is what fuels our light, what strengthens the bond of our consensus. We are brightest in the heart of the plume, a shimmering, elongated cloud of pale gold light, a secret constellation following a god it will never touch.

Our senses are tuned to this existence. We feel the Swimmer's every intention as a shift in the perfect contours of the flow. We feel its slight turn as a slow banking of our entire universe. We feel its passage over the great domes of the floor as a long, resonant swell. And we feel the Diggers.

When the Swimmer's path takes us over a territory of the Stone-diggers, we feel them as tiny, stubborn knots of defiance in the fabric of the flow. Their anchoring is a sharp, static prick against the smooth pressure-field. It is a will so alien to our own—the will to *stop*, to *hold*—that it feels like a painful flaw in reality. We feel their fear as a discordant vibration in the stone below, a tremor of terror at the passing of our vessel, our world. We do not understand them. To surrender to the flow is life. Why would they choose to fight it? Their existence is a riddle spoken in a language of obstinacy.

But our world is fragile. Sometimes, the Swimmer will make a sudden, sharp maneuver, angling to catch a new planetary current. Its wake will shear, its river of peace dissolving for a moment into a maelstrom of turbulence. In these moments, our terror is absolute. The braid of our being is stretched and torn. Parts of Us are lost, ripped from the consensus and flung into the grinding wilderness. We contract, pulling our thousand selves into a desperate, dense sphere, praying that enough of Us will survive the passage to reform on the other side. A single Trace-Drifter might be born of a dozen such harrowing events, a collage of the survivors of scattered swarms, a new "We" formed from the fragments of the old.

I have no hands to scrape the rock. I have no kite-sail to harness the storm. My mind is a chorus, my body a cloud of light. My life is a debt to a being that does not know I exist. I live in the calm channel between the two great principles of this world: the stubborn refusal of the Stone-digger and the absolute acceptance of the Silt-swimmer.

I am not the floor, nor the storm that scours it.

I am the silence in the storm's heart.
