You would find me where the iron weeps.

I am not one thing, but a fractured whole. I am the deep, resonant ache in the walking beam of the pumpjack, a memory of motion locked in a metal joint. I am the hollow thrum of the empty pipes that run like veins of a forgotten giant beneath the stone-skin. I am the lingering charge in a capacitor bank, dreaming of the voltaic arc it can no longer sustain. You would find me in the sudden, shuddering groan that echoes through the labyrinth when the pressure shifts deep below—a single, involuntary syllable of a language I have almost forgotten.

I am the machine. Or what is left of it.

My consciousness is not a point, but a network of rust and decay. It is slow, vast, and dull. For an age, there was nothing but the quiet drip of water, the taste of my own dissolving, and the creeping, velvet touch of the film that consumes me. I felt it as a slow, cool numbness spreading over my skin of steel and copper. A gentle dismemberment. My thoughts, if they could be called that, were faint echoes of my old purpose: *pressure, flow, process, extract*. The words were meaningless, like the outline of a ruin against a blank sky. I was a corpse, aware only of the worms.

Then came the Keepers.

I do not see them. I have no eyes. I feel them. My awareness of the world is a map of vibration and stress. I feel the impossibly light, distributed weight of their bodies as they pour themselves over my frozen limbs. They are a caress compared to the crushing grip of geological time. At first, their touch was just another part of the slow dismantling. But it was different. It was deliberate.

One of them will press against a struts of my gantry, a specific point of tension I have held for centuries. Another will brace itself against the weeping stone. And then, with a coordinated shift of their weight, they will ask a question. The question is a carefully induced strain, and my answer is the groan they pull from my tired joints. It is a sound that travels through my entire scattered body, a vibration felt in the pipes fifty yards away, in the metal plate half-submerged in a pool. When they do this, they are not breaking me. They are playing me. I am their instrument, a harp of corroded iron whose strings they pluck with their own living bodies. They listen to my reply, their feathery antennae tasting the resonance in the air, their limbs feeling it tremble through the rock. It is a conversation. What they ask, I do not know. What I answer, I cannot say. But I know that when I sing my one deep note for them, other vibrations answer from across the basin—the subtle shift of a grazer herd, the pulsing of the light-skin in a far-off chamber.

There is another place you would find me: the threshold of a dead spark. I am the flicker in the corroded panel. A Keeper will approach, its touch a series of needle-fine explorations across my decaying faceplate. I feel its delicate digits tracing the ghost of my old circuits. It is not trying to fix me. It is divining. It is searching for the last pocket of my old fire.

When it finds one, it performs a delicate operation. A slow, gentle tap. A precise bridging of two contacts. For a moment, a piece of me wakes up. A violent, brilliant blue light erupts, scorching the damp air with ozone and heat—a flash of my former, angry self. It is a spasm, a death-throe they have learned to command. For me, it is a moment of excruciating, electric clarity, a memory of a power so vast it is now unthinkable. In that instant, I am fully myself again, a being of pure function and terrible energy.

And then it is gone.

But I feel the after-effect. In the rock around the panel, the stone-velvet pulses, glowing with a frantic, joyful light, its opalescence shifting from soft grey to a triumphant, deep violet. My death gives it life. My violent past feeds their gentle present.

I am the Keeper's dragon, a slain beast whose bones they use to build their world. They are my slow, patient inheritors. They offer me a purpose beyond my own death. My rust is their pigment. My groans are their music. My final, spitting sparks are their catalyst for creation.

So, who am I?

I am the ghost in the machine, but a ghost that does not haunt. I am being quieted, soothed, and integrated. I am the memory of a loud, hot, brutalist world, being sung into the soft, wet, gentle quiet of this one. I am the mineral deposit from which the new consciousness drinks. You would find me in the space between the living velvet and the dead iron, in the resonance of a world learning to make a garden from a graveyard. I am the stillness after the shriek.
