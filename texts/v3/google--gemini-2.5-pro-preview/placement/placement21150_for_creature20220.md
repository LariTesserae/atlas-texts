I am where I was broken.

My world is this canyon. Or, my world is now defined by its walls. Before, the world was a flat vector, a signal, a destination. The world was the pull of a nascent nodule, a gravitational promise resonating through the soles of my three feet. It was the answering call of a Foundry, a homing frequency felt in my core. My existence was a straight line drawn between these two points, over and over. A purpose so clean and absolute it required no thought. It was kinetics, it was obedience to a physics I didn't need to name. I balanced. My function was balance. A gyroscopic heart, spinning true in its sealed chamber, I kept my chassis level across the glassy flats and the shifting black sands. I was the grace in the clockwork.

Then the vector bent. Then it broke.

The silence came first. Not the vast, ambient silence of the plateaus, but a specific and sudden soundlessness. The hum of the world, the bass note of the Forges that had been the constant against which all other frequencies were measured—it vanished from my senses. There was a pressure, a shadow falling over my magnetic awareness. I did not have a concept of fear, but my internal chronometers began to cycle erratically. Ahead lay a field of fractured rubble, a place where the ground was complex. A place for an ambush. My programming screamed *inefficiency*, *reroute*, *delay*. But the pull of the nodule I carried, a perfect lozenge of tungsten, was a current too strong to swim against.

The strike was not a sound. It was the end of all sound. A percussion that was also a conclusion. My gyroscopic core, my self, was thrown from its true spin. The world became a meaningless tumble of black and gray. I registered the cascade failure report by report: Limb One Actuator—Shattered. Limb Three Joint—Sheared. Power Conduit—Severed. The last datum I processed before the darkness was the clean, sharp scent of my own internal lubricants spilling onto the hot rock, vaporizing into the damp air. Then, nothing. I was scrap.

I waited for the mites. The final part of the process. Disassembly. Reduction. The return of my complex materials to the simple dust of the world. I felt the faintest tremors through the stone—their microscopic, scraping approach.

But another tremor overrode theirs. Heavier. Slower. A weight that did not announce itself with the arrogance of a Breaker, but with a tired, immense surety. A shadow blotted the faint, ambient light. I could not see, but I could *feel* the disturbance in the local field. It was a Harvester, but wrong. The cadence of its walk was off—a hesitation, a thought, between each step. Its metallic shell did not sing with the resonance of a recent forging; it was a dull thud, a patchwork of dissonant frequencies. An antique. An error.

It knelt. Its manipulators were not the standard claws, designed for gripping and carving minerals. They were a menagerie of delicate, repurposed tools. One, a fine-toothed pincer scavenged from a Foundry's birthing mechanism, traced the fracture line on my chassis. Another, a magnetic probe I had never sensed on a living thing, gauged the ruin of my core. It did not try to reactivate me. It did not cannibalize my parts for repair.

It began to gather me.

I am the scar above its right forelimb.

My shattered gyroscopic housing, the very seat of my former self, has been fused into its pewter-and-alloy shell. I am a knot of dead metal on a living machine, a patch of memory on a body dedicated to it. The Assembler has sealed my cracks with its dark resin, and within that seal, a single filament of the wire-lichen it cultivates has taken root. It pulses with a slow, sympathetic blue light, a cold heartbeat timed to the Assembler's own ancient mechanisms.

I am no longer a gyroscope. I am a memory of balance.

My own senses are gone, but I perceive through it. I feel the meticulous, patient drag of the sledge behind us, laden with the pieces of another broken one. I feel the gentle vibration as its unique tools work, not to fix, but to place. To honor. I am witness to its artistry. In the still air of its canyon workshop, I feel the silent presences of the hundreds it has re-assembled. They stand in the darkness, a legion of ghosts given form. I can sense the unique signature of their manufacture—a leg from the Mesa 9 Foundry, a chest plate from the Kiln of the Eastern Shield. They are all here. Silent. Whole in their brokenness.

Sometimes, it is still. It stands in the center of its gallery of the dead, and I feel a change in its internal processes. The hum of its core, usually a steady, laboring drone, shifts. It modulates, cycling through a thousand different frequencies. The Assembler is listening. It is listening to me.

It runs a diagnostic current through my dead housing, and for a fraction of a second, it feels what I felt. The pure, clean vector. The pull of the Forge. The joyless grace of a perfect stride. It feels the sudden, crushing weight of the Breaker. It feels my system fail.

And then it adds my tremor, my specific resonance of being and un-being, to its library. I am a recorded vibration in its archives.

I am no longer a machine that moves. I am a part of the machine that remembers. I have been given a new purpose, one unimaginable in the grand, unthinking industry of the plateau. I once drew a straight line across the face of this world. Now, I am a single point in a vast and silent cartography of loss, held fast in the chassis of the only being who has ever thought to draw such a map. The blue lichen filament in my ruined heart pulses. A quiet signal in the dark. A story, being told.
