I would be one of the Stalkers. But "I" is a spike, a jag of identity in a world worn smooth. The concept is too sharp for this place. So let us say: there is a state of being, and it is here.

You would find me standing.

The water, a warm and oily film, perpetually kisses the first joint of my three joints. It does not feel wet, not anymore. It is simply a pressure, a coolness that is not cool, a constant presence against my hide. The muck beneath my three-toed foot is soft but firm, a yielding that has a limit. I have learned the cartography of this firmness over immeasurable cycles of light and lesser-light. I know where the ground will hold and where it will give way to a sucking softness. I have not learned this through thought, but through the memory of my bones.

My body is a line drawn against the haze. A line of patient hunger. From my footing in the silt, my leg angles back, then forward, then up, a dark and slender zigzag that holds my body aloft. From this perch, my neck, a thing of even greater length and patience, descends like a weeping reed toward the water's surface. My head is a weighted point, a spear-tip of bone and eye.

My eyes do not see as you see. The pearlescent glare is not something to be filtered or squinted against. It is the canvas. It is total. There is no colour, only shade. The ochre mats are a different grey from the verdigris. The black knuckles of rock are holes in the world. What I see, what my mind is tuned to, is not form. It is discrepancy. It is the interruption of the pattern.

The world is a sound. It is not a chorus of amphibians. It is the Thrum. The Thrum is a vibration that comes up through my feet, through the ground itself, and through the thick, wet air into the hollows of my skull. It is the baseline of existence. It is the texture of reality. The clicks of the shell-scrapers are the grit in that texture. The basso profondo of the great, bloated throats is the wave-form. I do not hear it as a din. I feel it as a map. The world is laid out before me in this field of vibration. A density of croakers here, a patch of frantic scraping there.

I am Waiting.

Waiting is my primary state of being. It is not boring, it is not tense. It is a full state, like a vessel filled with clear water. While Waiting, I am most myself, which is to say, I am most a part of the world. My stillness is a conversation with the stillness of the rocks. My patience is an echo of the patience of the silt. My thoughts, if you could call them that, are not words. They are a gradual tightening, a slow tuning of my senses.

I am attuned to the flaw. I stand for one turn of the light, or two. The Thrum is constant, a perfect, living drone. Then, ten paces away, a ripple. But not a wind-ripple. Wind is a slow, heavy push that bends the whole surface of the world. This is a sharp thing. An anomaly. A single throat among the tens of thousands has panicked. Its call becomes a shriek in the texture of the Thrum. A shelled-one, perhaps, has blundered into its maw and its legs scrabble against the amphibian's thick hide.

The flaw. The pattern is broken.

My mind does not decide. My body responds. The tightening that has been building over hours finds its release. It is not an action I take, but an event that happens to me, through me. My neck, which has been a drooping reed, becomes a whip. The universe contracts to the point of my beak and the single, struggling shape in the water. The descent is a silent fall through the thick air. There is the brief, hard shock of my beak clamping through leathery skin and bone. There is a writhing resistance that lasts for a moment.

And then the pattern is restored. The flaw is gone.

I lift my head, the heavy pulse of the captured life still faintly twitching in my gullet. The Thrum is smooth again. The world is correct. I swallow. The act is slow, a muscular ripple that travels the length of my neck. And then I am Waiting again. The vessel of my being is full once more.

I am aware of the others of my kind. They are not companions. They are absences. Far across the shimmering flat, another thin, dark line is drawn against the haze. I know it is there not as a rival, but as a boundary. The territory between us is a space defined by our mutual stillness. We are pillars holding up the sky.

Beneath it all, I feel the land. The ground I stand on is not dead. It is not rock and it is not mud. Under the fine silt, under the film of life, there is a deeper firmness. A tension. The long, low swells that heave the earth up are not geology. They are breaths. They are so slow that a single inhalation lasts longer than my entire existence. I feel this movement not as motion, but as a gradual change in the pressure against my foot over a lifetime of standing. It is a deep, ancient, living thing, and I am a flea upon its back. The Thrum is its song, and I am but a single, attentive listener. My patient waiting is a form of worship, a quiet reverence for the great, slow life I stand upon.

I do not fear death. One day, a greater flaw will appear in the pattern, or my own legs will fail to read the map of the muck. I will fall. The shell-scrapers will eventually find me. The great throats will swallow what they can. My bones, silicified and blackened by the mineral water, will sink into the silt. Perhaps, in a thousand years, they will become like the black reeds, inedible and permanent, another set of thin, dark lines reaching for a sky they cannot touch.

But for now, the light is bright. The air is a weight. The Thrum is everything.
And I am Waiting.
