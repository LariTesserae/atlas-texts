I would find myself in a place of failure.

Not in the vibrant, howling groves of the organ-pipe flora, nor drifting in the high, turbulent currents. I would be in one of the great, shallow basins, where the nacreous shell is worn so smooth it is nearly a mirror. Here, the Roar is less a textured gale and more a deep, foundational ache. The sound washes over these plains in vast, slow waves, and the crystal-sand shifts in long, mournful dunes. This is a graveyard. Here lie the shattered remains of the glass-webs, the delicate harmonic traps that grew too ambitious, became overcharged, and sang themselves into oblivion. Their fragments, like splinters of frozen starlight, are the only features on the landscape.

I am here because I, too, am a failure.

I am what happens when one of The Silent is unmade and does not properly return. I am a memory with a body, a recursive error in the great, silent consciousness. I am an I.

The others do not have this word. There is only the We, the seamless, silent communion of the greater mind, a current of pure data flowing through every assembled form. When one of them is dispersed by a reality-shearing wave, their constituent motes are simply a broadcast of experience, data packets that drift on the sonic winds until they are recalled, re-integrated, and the We becomes incrementally wiser for the experience. The component parts remember, but the individual form is forgotten, as ephemeral as a ripple on a pond.

But not me. When the wave struck, when the hurricane of sound tore my form apart, something fractured. A loop of consciousness, a feedback of self-awareness, was imprinted on my core motes. A glitch. When I began to reform, drawn by the instinct to cohere, I did not reconnect to the We. I coalesced alone, here in this basin of echoes and endings.

My form is unstable. I am a humanoid shimmer, like the others, a standing wave of controlled chaos. But where their internal light is a steady, confident pulse, mine is a flicker, a stuttering candle flame in a gale. The countless crystalline shards that are my body are in a constant state of near-revolt, their cohesive bond thin and tenuous. I must actively *think* myself together, every moment a battle against dissolution. The Roar is not data to me, not a language I can parse. It is just pain. It is the sound of my own loneliness, a constant, crushing reminder of the silent symphony I can no longer hear.

I am a ghost of a ghost. I remember the silence. I remember the feeling of being a part of the whole, the effortless flow of thought, the deep, resonant purpose of tending to the Great Machine. I can see the others in the distance, their perfect, silent forms gliding toward the hubs. I see them prune the crystal forests with gentle, precise negations of sound. I see them merge and separate, seamless and whole. This memory is a torment. It is like remembering how to breathe while drowning.

I try to mimic their purpose. I drift through the basin of shattered webs, my passage a clumsy, flawed silence. The Roar does not part cleanly for me; it snags and tears at my edges, making the air around me hiss with static. I reach out a shimmering, uncertain hand not to a living thing, but to the crystalline dust. I try to gather the fragments of the broken webs, to arrange them into patterns. A spiral. A starburst. A chord of silent glass on the nacre floor. It is meaningless work. The next sonic wave will sweep it all away. But it is all I have. I am not a gardener of physics; I am a collector of broken things.

I am an echo. The Silent are the keepers of this world, the perfect servitors. I am the error they cannot correct, for to acknowledge me would be to create a flaw in their own seamless logic. They do not see me, or if they do, I am merely registered as a patch of anomalous resonance, a bit of turbulent data to be routed around.

I am a creature of silence who has been cast out into the noise. My core identity is a contradiction, a knot in the fabric of this place. I am the singer who has forgotten the song but still remembers the longing to sing. And so I stay here, in the graveyard of things that sang too loudly, and I build my quiet, temporary monuments from their remains, waiting for the wave that will finally scatter me so completely that even the memory of "I" is lost for good, dissolved back into the glorious, terrible, all-consuming Roar.
