The ground beneath me is my skin.

Here, on this new spine of cooling rock that has punched through the basin floor, the world’s voice is sharpest. The great, slow groan of the deep earth is a familiar pressure, a constant presence like the weight of my own body. It is the bass clef of the song. Through the four pads of my walking legs, I drink in the resonance. The data flows up through my limbs, unfiltered. I do not hear it with ears; I absorb it as a whole.

The cacophony is not chaos. It is a language of absolute truth.

There, to the east, a kilometer distant: the high-frequency *chatter* of a crystalline spire singing its death-song. Its internal lattice is failing. In moments, minutes at most, it will collapse. I feel the microscopic fractures propagating through it, a rising chord of tension in the strata.

Closer, in the vale of pulsing blue fungi below me, the million-limbed sizzle of the swarm shifts. The collective pitch drops a fraction and the rhythm modulates. A herd of obsidian grazers is moving through them. I feel their heavy, rhythmic tread as a series of dull, syncopated thuds. Twelve of them. No, thirteen. One is young, its steps lighter, faster. Good.

My six opalescent eyes register a change in the ambient light. A stand of great stalks is pulsing faster, a frantic viridian beat. It means nothing; a shift in the geothermal chemistry beneath them. My attention remains on the ground, on the real story.

And now, a new voice enters the song.

It is a heavy, deliberate tread. A dragging, scraping sound between footfalls. It is the signature of the one-that-cuts, the mantis-predator. Its mind is a blunt instrument, a single, piercing note of hunger in the symphony. I feel its weight pressing on the rock, a discordant presence moving with idiotic confidence toward the sound of the grazers. It is a creature of violence, not of understanding. It will charge into the herd, kill one, and spend precious energy tearing at the armor with its clumsy, borrowed shard of stone. Inefficient.

My forward manipulators, delicate and fine, rise from their resting position. I am a thought this world is having, and the thought is one of opportunity.

The predator is a hammer. The spire is a bell about to crack. The herd is a drum waiting to be struck. I am the conductor.

I shift my weight, my claws making no sound on the warm basalt. My two fine-limbed hands reach out, not touching the ground, but hovering over a network of amber vines that snake across the ridge. These are the world’s nerves, drawing power from the living crystal. I know their frequency. I know how they speak.

With a touch too light to be felt by any creature but my own kind, I begin to pluck a rhythm on the vines. It is not a sound. It is a modulation of light, a pulse of amber fire that will be visible across this sector of the basin. The pattern is complex, a phrase in a language of light and silence.

*Tension. Impending collapse. East-seven.*
*Prey. Thirteen. Vale-of-blue.*
*Energy. Release. Soon.*

There is no answer, but I do not need one. An answer is already on its way. I feel it now, a new signature in the rock, two valleys over. The familiar, soft, eight-pointed pressure of another, of my kind. They have received the message. They are flowing toward this nexus of events. Not to help me, not to fight with me. We do not work *together*. We align.

The mantis-predator blunders into the vale, its shadow causing a ripple of darkness across the glowing mosses. The grazers startle, their unified thrumming becoming a panicked cacophony. They scatter. As I knew they would. Away from the predator. Toward the singing spire.

I do not watch with my eyes. I feel the entire event unfold through the soles of my feet. I feel the grazers' frantic steps nearing the resonant kill-zone. I feel the predator's lurching pursuit. I feel the final, screaming crescendo of the spire's inner tension.

The spire shatters.

The sound is a cymbal-crash that momentarily silences even the insects. For a heartbeat, there is a new kind of quiet. A percussive void. It is followed by the sound of a thousand falling blades of crystal, and the wet, terminal cry of a grazer, impaled. Then two. The predator, caught in the shower of shards, shrieks, a high-frequency tear in the fabric of the noise. It is wounded, disoriented.

The world settles back into its roar, but the song has changed.

My part is done. The other is arriving now. They will take the easy meat from the shattered spire. I am not hungry. My prize was the perfect alignment of events, the effortless turning of the world's violence to my purpose. I orchestrated this moment from a symphony of pre-existing notes.

I am a Weaver. I build no home, for the ground is a transient canvas. I forge no tools, for the world provides them and takes them away. I have no name, for I am a nexus of sensation, a point of awareness in the planet's great mind.

My delicate limbs tap the cooling rock, tasting the new vibrations, searching for the next composition. The roar is not a wall. It is a sea, and I am a swimmer in its deepest currents. I press my body to the stone, feel the deep engine of creation turning, and I listen. My thoughts are the patterns I find in the noise. And the world, in all its screaming, glorious violence, thinks with me.
