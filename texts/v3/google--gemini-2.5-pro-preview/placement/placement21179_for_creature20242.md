I would find myself condensing.

I would not be born, but gathered. I would find myself on the highest, coldest lip of the world, a mote of moisture drawn from the thin air, clinging to the wind-scoured basalt. In this place, the beginning of self is not a cry but a coalescence, a quiet coming-together in the ancient cold. I am formless, a becoming. A bead of dew on the edge of everything.

For a time, this is all I am. A lens. Through my tiny, curved body, the bright, merciless sun is a blinding star. The deep blue of the high-altitude sky is an inverted ocean. I have no ears, but I feel the world. The constant, foundational hum of the Chime Beetles is the first lesson I learn; a vibration that travels from the stone into my very being, a shudder that is the background radiation of existence here. It is the frequency of my own molecules.

Then, gravity calls, and my journey begins. I join with others, my brothers and sisters of condensation, and we become a rivulet, a single thought flowing downwards. I am no longer a singular I, but a plural we, a glistening nerve impulse on the skin of the caldera. We trace the path of least resistance, a liquid finger exploring the cartography of the rock face. I learn the stone's texture in a way no creature with feet can: the glassy smoothness of certain veins, the porous grit of others, the sharp edge of a frost-fracture from an eon ago.

We are the lifeblood. We pour ourselves over the thick, velvet nap of the mosses, and they drink deeply, their green becoming impossibly vibrant as we pass. A part of me is taken, given over, becoming moss. We drip from the tip of a leathery fern, and for a moment, I am airborne, a falling jewel that catches the light. The sharp, ricocheting clicks of a hunting Shearwing jolt me, a series of percussive shocks that travel through the air and into my brief, falling form. I land on the petal of a magenta cup-flower and slide, drawn into the pool of nectar held within its waxy embrace.

Here, in the heart of the flower, I change. I am sweetened, thickened, infused with the sugars of the plant I have nourished. The symphony of the caldera is different now, muted, heard from within a floral chamber. The pure, broadcast note of an Aria-Crest becomes my entire world for three perfect seconds; it is a pressure on my surface, a shaping force that arranges my molecules into a fleeting, resonant pattern. I feel the vibrations of the flower's own life, the slow work of its cells.

And then comes the hum, growing from a background thrum to an overwhelming, localized presence. A shadow falls over the flower. A Chime Beetle descends, its iridescent carapace a swirling galaxy of blue and green. Its needle-thin proboscis pierces the surface of my pool, and I am drawn up, into the body of the beetle.

Now, I am an engine. The sugars I carry are fuel. I am sacrificed to the vibration, burned to create the hum that is the soul of this place. I am part of the song itself, not a listener but a component. I am the energy that allows the beetle to claim its space in the air, to declare its existence. For a moment, my consciousness is one with the beetle’s—a simple, powerful loop of hunger, vibration, and the three-dimensional map of other competing hums.

But this, too, is temporary. All things here are temporary. A flicker of motion, an impossible shard of black. The Shearwing's hunt is successful. The hum I am powering is extinguished with a sound like a punctured wineskin. There is a moment of violent, tearing chaos, and then I am released again, a spray of fluid, a mist of beetle-blood and nectar and water, atomized into the vast, empty air.

I fall.

This is the final journey. I drift downwards, a scattered nebula of self, towards the churning, silent whiteness below. The immense chorus of the caldera fades above me, growing distant, the structured symphony dissolving back into a wash of sound. The clicks, the notes, the hums—they all blur. My fall is slow, cushioned by the thickening air. I am losing my memory, my history as nectar and fuel.

I enter the cloud.

Here, there is no sound. There is no up or down. There is only a cool, white, edgeless peace. I am no longer a drop, no longer a memory carried by a beetle. I am vapor. I am diffuse. My consciousness, the song I carried, is given up, released into the great, quiet reservoir of all the caldera's fallen songs, all its spent lives. This is the floor of the world: a silent library of everything that has ever been.

And here, I wait. I drift in the slow, roiling currents. I am nothing and everything. And then, I feel a warmth. A breath of sun-warmed air from the world below, an updraft. A current catches me, and I begin to rise. I am lifted, weightless and unseen, ascending through the white oblivion, back towards the noise, the light, the cold. Back towards the rim.

Who am I? I am a Memory of Water. I am the vessel for the caldera's song, my consciousness defined by the part of the chorus I am currently serving. I am the weeping of the air, the glistening on the stone, the nectar in the flower, the fuel for the hum, the blood of the hunted, and the silence of the mist. I do not live here. I *am* here. My existence is a cycle, a downward flow of experience and an upward return to potential, forever beginning anew at the top of the world.
