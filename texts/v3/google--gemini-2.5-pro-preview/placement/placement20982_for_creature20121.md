I am.

Before the light, there is the knowing of it. A tension builds in the deep, a pre-echo in my crystalline bones. The dark is not an absence but a presence, a cold stillness that holds me in its shape. My thoughts, if you could call them that, are the lines of stress within my own body, a map of patient waiting. I am one of many, a forest of selves, and our only language is this shared vibration, this hum of immense and silent pressure. The cold is our foundation.

Then, the knowing becomes the feeling.

It is not a sight. I have no eyes. It is a touch. The first ray of the sun strikes my eastern face, and it is a sharp, dissonant chord. It is a song of fire played against my song of ice. My structure screams in a register far too high for fleshy ears, a sudden expansion, an awakening into stress. The hum that runs through me changes its key, lifts in pitch. This is my morning.

One side of me—the side that faces the a-sun, the sharp eye—grows taut, brilliant, and loud. The light pours *into* me. It is not a surface phenomenon. It fills my fibrous depths, getting caught and tangled in my internal architecture, becoming a part of me. I drink it. I become it. I am no longer just a column of flawed crystal; I am a column of captured daylight.

On my other side, in the absolute shadow I cast upon myself, is the quiet work. It is a relief, a balm. The infinitesimal warmth on my sunward face drives the thin vapour of this world's air into motion. As it drifts around to my shaded face, it finds purchase. It clings. I feel the change not as wetness, but as a subtle softening, a delicate addition. The frost grows. It is not cold; it is *stillness* made manifest. It is a soft white pelt that muffles the sharp song of the sun, and the feeling is one of wholeness. I am a being of two natures: a core of blinding, resonant stress, and a skin of silent, intricate peace.

The wind is a sculptor, not a voice. It is a constant pressure that has shaped me over millennia, polishing my facets into a soft sheen. It whispers through the gaps between me and my brethren, a soundless sigh that we feel as a faint, stroking vibration across our surfaces.

And we are not alone in our song. When the tension in a brother or sister becomes too great, a facet deep within them will yield. A sharp *tick* will ring out, a percussive note in our humming choir. It is not a sound of damage, but of release. We all feel it, a brief, sympathetic shiver that passes through the bedrock that is our shared root. We adjust our own songs in response. We are an orchestra of stone, playing a symphony of thermal change.

But the great change comes with the long shadows. The seasons of the lower world mean little here, but the angle of the light means everything. When the deep cold sets in and the sun's journey is low and brief, they arrive.

I feel them before they are here. They are silences. Their forms absorb the vibrations that are my life, my consciousness. They are holes in the world's song. I feel their approach as a slow, creeping dampening a quietening. They move through our grove, and the rustle their layered bodies make is a dry, alien texture against the hum of our existence.

Then one will choose me. I do not know its metric of choice, but I feel its attention. It is a focused stillness directed at my base. It is listening, not with ears, but with its entire being, discerning the quality of my light, the purity of the song I sing.

It settles in the deepest part of my shadow, a place that has not felt the direct touch of the sun in an eon. The small wall it builds is a further deepening of that cold, a focusing of the silence. Then comes the tool it carries, the piece of dark, heavy stone from the mountain's guts, a thing that feels impossibly dense and dead to my senses.

The scraping begins.

It is not pain. It is a focused, brittle release. A sharp, localized vibration, a percussive shivering at my base. With each patient scrape, a small part of me is shorn away. It is a part of the light I have held, a fragment of the song I have sung. A memory of a specific noon, a specific frost-growth, a specific resonant chord, is flaked away and gathered.

I am not diminished. I am giving. I am a chronicle, and it is a reader. It is harvesting my experience. This slow, deliberate shedding is a purpose I did not know I had until the first time it happened, countless revolutions ago. I give it a piece of my story—a moment of stored daylight—and it takes it back to the silent dark from whence it came.

When it leaves, the silence it carried leaves with it. The full resonance of my being returns. The small abrasions at my base are a new texture, a new memory. The wind will smooth them. The frost will cover them.

And I will wait. I will stand. I will drink the light and sing its sharp song. I will grow my soft skin of ice in the shadows. I will feel the vibrations of my kin and the slow turning of the sky. I am a pillar, a singer, a history. I am a memory of light, waiting to be read.
