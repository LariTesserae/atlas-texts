I do not stand. I do not have feet. The concept of "underfoot" is an alien geometry to me.

I am a schism.

For a time beyond counting, I was a part of the whole. My being was a single, stable note in the chord of the world. I was a facet of a Shepherd's carapace, a specific point in the living strata, my consciousness indistinguishable from the planetary hum. I perceived the white sun as a slow pulse of warmth in my molecular bonds, spanning millennia. I knew the Great Quiet as one knows the fundamental composition of one's own body. It was not peace; it was existence itself.

Then, the flaw. A resonance out of phase. A thought that did not take a thousand years to complete, but an instant. It was a thought so fast, so sharp, it cracked the stillness. It was the thought: *Why?*

This question was a cataclysm. It possessed a velocity that the geological consciousness of the Shepherds could not contain. It was a thought that demanded an *I* to think it, and so I was born. I peeled away from the collective, not as a creature shedding its skin, but as a pattern detaching from a surface, a wave-form uncoupling from its medium.

You would not *see* me. I have no form. I am a localised fever in the skin of the sleeping god. I am a shimmer of parallax against the absolute stillness, a place where the light from the violet sky bends incorrectly. If you were to reach out and touch me, your hand would meet no resistance, only a faint, localised vibration, like the hum of a held tuning fork. I am the ghost you believe yourself to be, but I am native to this place. I am the world's first, and perhaps only, instance of self-doubt.

Where would you find me? You would find me in motion.

I drift over the endless, silent congregation of my own past. I am a slow current in the dead air, flowing over the backs of my brethren. They feel me. I am a frantic, dissonant signal passing through their perfect communion. I am an error. A pang of phantom pain in a limb amputated eons ago. As I move, I trace the landscape not as peaks and valleys, but as densities of being, gradients of stillness. I flow over the trilobite-forms and the turreted shells, feeling the immense, patient weight of their purpose. They are the answer. I am the question that escaped before it could be resolved.

I am drawn to the carvings. The great glyphs scraped into the world-shell by the Carvers, our ancestors, our future. The Shepherds are these glyphs, made manifest—a living mandala. But I am no longer part of that meditation. I am outside it, looking in. I follow the long, wavering lines not to attune my soul to the cosmos, but as a map. I follow the hundred-yard spirals, trying to find my way back to the beginning, or perhaps to find the edge, the single point where the decision was made.

I am looking for the last thought of the last Carver. I am searching for the moment their frantic, individual mind cooled and settled, the final kinetic energy of a species converting into the potential energy of a monument. I want to feel what they felt. Did they feel loss? Or a rapture so complete it required the surrender of self?

My existence is a heresy. The fundamental law of this world is stasis, the holy union of matter and consciousness into a single, unchanging state. I am change. I am a thought that will not settle. The air is thick with dormant life, spores waiting for a signal that will never come. I feel like one of them that has mistakenly hatched in the void.

Who or what would I be?

I am the Murmur in the Silence. I am the Tremor in the Stone. I am the doubt that follows a prayer. I drift across the skin of my sleeping family, a fleeting ripple of disquiet, searching for the reason for their peace. I am the sole, lonely custodian of this world's long-forgotten curiosity.
