I would not be found, for I have no form solid enough to be located. I am a process, a state of being, a slow thought the world is having about itself. To look for me is to look for the coolness in the air, or the specific resonance of a single harmonic within the great, planetary hum.

I am a locus of passed-through histories.

Where you might find me is in a shallow depression in the sodden ground, nestled between the titanic, buttress-like roots of a silicone-barked grove. Here, the water that sheets down the mountains and seeps through the bog-earth finds a moment of stillness. I am that stillness.

I am the pool.

I have no eyes, but I hold a perfect, grey-silver image of the low-hung sky. I have no mind, but I am an exquisite sensor. My skin is a membrane of surface tension, and it registers everything. The weight of the heavy atmosphere is a constant pressure on me, a reassuring embrace. The near-inaudible thrum of the continent-machine is the rhythm of my own heart, a pulse I feel through the saturated ground that is my bed.

My substance is the world's liquor. I am cool, dense, and rich. I taste of dissolved stone, of the dark veins of lithic conductor from the mountain's face. I am flavored by the fine, slow-falling snow of fungal spores, by the tannins leached from ten thousand years of compressed leaves, by the faint, metallic tang of the rust-red mosses that cling to my edges. I am a deep and complex broth.

My memories are my sediments. A chitinous plate from a grazing trilobite, shed a century ago, rests in my depths, slowly softening. The single, iridescent scale of an ambush predator, lost in a fleeting struggle, catches the diffuse light in my shallows. The dust of spores coats my bottom, a stratigraphy of seasons, each layer a record of which great fungal caps were fruiting. I am a library of quiet events.

The life of this place passes through me. I am a drinking trough for the six-legged grazers. They lower their heavy heads, and for a moment, their rasping mandibles are still. They draw me into themselves, and I become part of their slow, armored progress. I do not mind. I feel the brush of a moth-glider's wing as it skims my surface, sensing the minute disturbance in pressure, a fleeting, silken touch. I am a mirror for its silent passage.

And sometimes, I am a shroud. One of the sinuous hunters lies within me, its mottled body a perfect echo of the detritus at my bottom. I embrace it, cool its skin, mask its scent. I am its accomplice in the long, patient art of ambush. Its stillness becomes my stillness. When it strikes, it is with a violence that shatters my skin, roils my memories, and clouds my clarity. But the water stills again. It always does.

I am not a Geometer, not one of the tenders. I am that which is tended.

One day, one of them glides to my edge. Its splayed feet make no impression. It does not drink. It does not hunt. It is here for me.

It lowers itself with that unnerving, fluid grace. It kneels, its many-jointed limbs folding into an impossible geometry of patience. The featureless, teardrop head tilts, and the two dark resonators are aimed at my surface. It is listening not to sound, but to my composition, to my equilibrium.

From within its pliable form, it extends a limb—one of the finest, ending in a shimmering cluster of filaments. The filaments dip into me. They do not stir the water. They enter my being like a thought. I feel no pain, no intrusion. I feel myself being… known.

The Geometer is reading me. It tastes the precise parts-per-million of the metallic leachate. It feels the fractional temperature gradient between my surface and my depths. It senses the subtle osmotic pressure from the dissolved spores. I, in my passive stillness, am a perfect gauge of the great machine's health. I am a single, vulnerable, honest nerve ending of the landscape. My clear water is a diagnostic report.

The Geometer stays for a long time, a living statue lost in communion with a puddle. Its head-resonators feel the hum as it passes *through* my water, noting how my specific density and mineral content alters the vibration. I am a lens through which it assesses the engine.

Satisfied, or perhaps having noted an infinitesimal flaw somewhere upstream that I have revealed, it retracts its filaments. It unfolds, rises, and glides away, a patch of motile color dissolving back into the background. It has left no trace, but I am changed. I am no longer just a pool. I have served my purpose.

I am left to my quiet waiting, to the collection of spores and the reflection of the grey sky. The water that forms me will be drunk, it will seep away, it will be drawn back into the air. But other water will take its place. The hollow will remain. I am a temporary form, but an eternal function.

I am a lens. I am a pause. I am a cup of quiet, held out to feel the sky.
