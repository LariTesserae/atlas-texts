Where I would find myself is not in a single location, but in a state of constant, gentle transit. I would be a participant in the world’s quiet machinery, a bead in its abacus. My location would be a point on a line, a curve on a surface, a moment in a fall. I am on a filament. I am on a leaf. I am in the air. I am in the earth. I am everywhere and nowhere, a single, transient note in a chord that has been held for ten thousand years.

I would be a drop of water.

My existence begins as a mistake of mathematics, a slight supersaturation in the humid air. I am not born, I am gathered. My coming into being is a quiet gasp as the air is pulled across the perfect, rigid geometry of a glassy filament. I coalesce, a sub-microscopic point of adhesion on that crystalline thread, which hums with a resonance I feel not as sound, but as the very structure of my own form. It is the vibration that holds me spherical against the patient pull of my own weight.

I am a lens.

Hanging on my filament-spine, I am one of a billion billion other selves. Together, we are the being’s multifaceted eye, though we do not see. We bend light. The diffuse, grey-white glow of the sky enters me, and I turn it into a tiny, sharp point of brilliance. The vast green of the Calla-leaf below me becomes, through my own curved body, a warped and swirling universe of jade. My neighbors, the other drops on my filament and those on threads nearby, are not companions but mirrors, each one holding a tiny, distorted effigy of me within it. The entire basin, from my vantage point, is not a space but an endless, recursive field of light and color, caught and refracted by a million million other selves. I have no mind, but I have a viewpoint. And my viewpoint is a kaleidoscope of curved greens and magentas, a universe contained and multiplied.

When the blue light pulses from a junction—a thought passing through the web—it is not something I see from a distance. The energy passes *through* me. For a moment, my chemistry shifts. I become warmer by a fraction of a degree. I feel a subtle change in the tension of the filament I cling to. I am not a witness to the thought; I am a particle of its medium. I am the comma in a sentence that takes a century to write.

My life is one of passive travel. I am drawn along the filament by forces I cannot comprehend—an electrostatic pull, a subtle temperature gradient. I slide, perfectly silent, merging with the selves I overtake. With each union, I grow heavier, my spherical perfection sagging into a more tear-like shape. My sense of the world changes. The pull of the earth becomes more insistent, a gentle but unceasing call. The vibrations in the filament feel different now, deeper, slower.

Eventually, I reach the end of the line. I am transferred, a slow, viscous slide, from the glassy thread to the living tissue of a Spindle-calla leaf. Here, the feeling is utterly different. The surface is not rigid and humming, but yielding, waxy, and cool. It is organic. It feels like home, though I do not know what a home is. I am no longer part of the web-mind, but have been drawn into the Calla-heart itself.

I creep across the vast, jade expanse of the leaf's upper surface, joining a slow, broad river of my other selves. We are drawn together by cohesion, flowing without sound toward the lowest point. The world of refracted light is gone, replaced by the smooth, skin-like reality of the leaf beneath me. I feel the slow, ancient life in it, a coolness that suggests a vast and patient metabolism.

We arrive at the tip. Here, we gather. We become one great, heavy, trembling whole. We are the final, gravid weight at the leaf’s long finger. The tension is a living thing, a feeling of both fullness and imminence. We are a thought that has been gathered, a resource that has been collected, and now we are ready to be delivered.

And then, release.

It is not a fall. A fall implies chaos, a helplessness against gravity. This is a deliberate letting go. I do not fall; I am given. My journey through the green-tinged air is silent and straight. There is no wind to push me. The thick, viscous atmosphere holds me, preventing me from accelerating into a rush. It is a slow, stately descent. In this moment of transit, between leaf and earth, I am utterly myself, a single, complete entity. I am the rhythm. I am the beat of the drum that measures geologic time.

The landing is not an impact. It is a reunion. I strike the black, quivering peat and am instantly enfolded. My boundaries dissolve. The perfect sphere that was ‘I’ ceases to be. My substance merges with the cool, saturated flesh of the basin floor. I seep downwards, past the weeping knuckles of ancient stone, becoming one with the deep, hidden waters.

Here, in the dark and the quiet, I am no longer a lens or a bead or a traveler. I am memory. I am potential. I am the raw material of a future thought, waiting to be drawn up through the bulbous trunk of a Calla-heart, to be unfurled into a leaf, to be given to the air, to be exhaled and woven once more onto the humming, crystalline web.

I am the pulse. I am the silence between. And I am waiting to be born again from the air.
