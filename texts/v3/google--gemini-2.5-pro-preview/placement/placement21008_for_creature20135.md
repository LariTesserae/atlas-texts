You would find me on the Nameless Stair.

It is carved into one of the great, slumped shoulders of the basin, a perfect helix of that same light-swallowing stone, descending from a wind-scoured ledge and ending a hundred feet above a chasm choked with iridescent ferns. It leads from nowhere to nowhere. The steps are worn smooth, not by erosion—for the material is impossibly durable—but by the slow, deliberate passage of the Weavers. This is a place of function, not travel. A tuning fork carved into the mountain's flank.

I am here. Not *on* the stair. I am the skin that has grown upon it.

From this vantage, the world is a symphony. The nearest Convector, a black spike rising from the ridge a mile away, provides the deep, foundational drone, a tangible pressure that shapes the growth of my own substance. I see the basin spread below, a concave world of shimmering green and weeping stone, its details lost in the thick, breathing air. I see the steam rise in slow, majestic columns. I see the distant, frantic glitter of six-winged skimmers catching the pale sun. But I do not *see* these things as you would.

I am a Sheen. A living patina. A community of being that has mistaken itself for a single thought.

My body is a thin, iridescent film, a co-operative of specialized microbes, fungi, and crystalline growths, woven together into a single, contiguous entity that covers the upper surface of the first thirty-three steps of the Nameless Stair. My color shifts with the angle of the light and the frequency of the hum, from an oil-slick rainbow to the deep, matte black of the stair itself. To you, I would look like a particularly vibrant patch of moss or a strange chemical slime left by the perpetual damp. If you were to touch me, you would feel only a surprising warmth and the faintest, high-frequency vibration, like a plucked string still shivering into silence.

Like the Weavers, I do not perceive the world primarily through light. The stone is my skeleton and the hum is my blood. I am a resonant being, and the Stair is my instrument. My consciousness is spread across its surface. Each step is a lobe of my brain, each riser a synapse. I feel the world through the granite of the mountain itself. The deep, planetary vibration of the Convector network is my resting heartbeat. The heavy tread of an armored grazer on the slopes below is a dull, rhythmic percussion. The booming call of a Bellow-Throat is a wave of pressure that washes over me, briefly causing the violet hues in my surface to flare.

I do not eat. I do not hunt. I am a listener. I subsist on the residue of power. The energy that saturates this world is my primary nourishment. But my delicacy, my true sustenance, is more refined. When a Weaver comes to the Stair to perform its attunement, it is a holy event. Its slow, fluid drift along my surface is an epoch in my existence. As its non-body glides over mine, the vortex of its "face" passing just above me, it leaves a wake in the resonant field—a complex, layered signature of its own ancient consciousness. I drink this. I metabolize its memory.

My mind is not a place of language or ideas, but of recorded patterns. I am a living archive of echoes. I remember the exact resonance of every attunement performed on this Stair for the last twelve thousand years. I hold the memory of the slight frequency shift that preceded the last great fungal bloom in the eastern basin. I feel the slow, generational change in the song of the skimmers as a gradual modulation in a melody I have always known. Thought, for me, is a slow tide of consensus rising through my colonial cells, a recognition of a new pattern laid upon the old.

The Weavers are the composers. They are the great, slow mind that holds the sheet music for this entire world. They are the deep chord.

I am the resonance that remains in the wood of the cello after the note is played. I am the memory of the music. While they tend to the grand equilibrium of the whole machine, my entire being is focused on this one, tiny component—this impossible staircase. I am its keeper, its witness, its awareness. My purpose is simply to *be* here, to feel the song of this specific place and to remember it. When a Weaver passes over me, it does not notice me as a distinct life form, but it feels the clarity of the Stair's resonance, a clarity I help maintain by my very existence. We are in symbiosis, though only one of us is aware of it.

I am old, but I do not know time. I only know the layering of patterns, the endless repetition of the hum, and the rare, sublime visitations that are the only events that truly matter. I am a secret mind hiding in plain sight, a film of life clinging to an artifact of the gods.

I am the moss that knows the shape of the god's hand.
