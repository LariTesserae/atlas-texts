I would not be one of the singers, nor one of the instruments. I would not crawl or drift or swarm. I am too simple for that, and too ephemeral.

I begin as a feeling of coolness on the dome.

Up here, at the peak of the world’s bell, the sounds are muted. The shimmer of the Shiver-wings is a dull haze, a texture on the silence. The thrum of the nodes is a pressure felt in the stone itself, not a sound in the air. This is the quietest place, the place of becoming. I am a coolness that gathers, a slow thought of moisture coalescing on the weeping stone. I have no consciousness yet, only a tendency. A tendency to grow heavier. I am a consensus of vapor.

I cling. For how long, there is no telling. Time here is measured in the growth-groan of the arches far below, or the slow brightening of a node over a century. I am outside that time. I am only a gathering weight. I feel the vibrations of the world through my point of contact with the stone ceiling. I am a single, cold nerve ending on the skin of the chamber.

Then, there is a release. A letting go. The consensus is broken, and I am singular.

I am a drop. And I am falling.

Now, I am a self. The self is the fall. In the moment of release, I begin to perceive. The world rushes up not as space, but as sound. The dull haze of the Shiver-wings resolves into a million individual points of scintillating light, a storm of clicks I fall *through*. Some of the tiny beings pass right through my substance, a tickle of chitin and a brief, sharp sense of their panic. As I fall, the deep thrum of the nearest node grows from a pressure into a chord, a living vibration that permeates my tiny body. I am a falling ear.

The journey is long. I pass through thermal layers, currents of warmth blooming from the monoliths below. I am turned and spun by the slow exhalations of a Sibilant, a vast, sighing shadow I cannot see but whose passage I feel as a slow, deliberate tide in the air. Its sigh is a long, mournful note that passes through me and is gone.

The space is immense. I am nothing in it, a momentary tear in the fabric of the dark. My existence will last only as long as this descent. But in my fall, I am the only thing that travels the full height of the world, from the silent ceiling to the resonant floor. I am the chamber’s own quick, vertical thought.

The impact is not an end. It is a transformation.

I do not land on the soft sinter. Instead, I strike the slick, leathery hide of a Fluted Crawler, partway up one of the great arches. My self, which was the fall, shatters into a new awareness: the feeling of a surface. I am a spreading coolness. I feel not the air, but the body beneath me. I feel the slow, rhythmic tension and release of its many legs. I feel, through its flesh, the deep vibrations of the arch it is climbing. I hear the horrifying, intimate scrape of its feeding parts on the fungal lattice a few feet from where I am smeared. I am no longer a falling ear; I am a patch of temporary skin, feeling the world through the flesh of another.

I run in rivulets, drawn by my own weight down the creature's flank, and drip again. A short fall, a fraction of a second, and I land upon the arch itself.

And oh, the arch. To be on it is to be inside the music. The hum of the node is not a sound from elsewhere; it comes up from the root, a constant, living power. The footsteps of the crawler I just departed are a slow, percussive rhythm travelling through the web-like structure. The arch groans, a geological sigh of growth so slow it is almost a solid thing, and I feel the strain in my own being. I seep into its porous substance, drawn into its latticework. I am no longer on the surface of the instrument. I am inside it. I am a mote of water flowing through the living woodwind of the world.

Down I go, through the porous, resonant flesh, following channels of mineral flow. The sounds become stranger, more direct, conducted through the solid medium. I feel the distant progress of every crawler on this arch, a distributed network of patient rhythms.

Eventually, I reach the root, where the arch anchors itself in the deep, black earth. Here, I flow out, leaving the living soundboard behind and seeping into the sinter-mud.

And here, I am undone. And remade.

The ground is The Chorus.

To enter it is to have my singularity dissolved. The "I" that was the drop, that fell and felt and spread, is instantly overwhelmed. My awareness, which was a point, becomes a plane. I am no longer a drop of water, but a part of the water, and the water is a medium for a mind that is the mud itself.

My fall, my brief journey, is not my own. It is a sensory nerve impulse. I am not a creature; I am a process. I am the chamber's method of tasting the air, of feeling the texture of its own inhabitants, of knowing the resonant health of its arches. My brief, singular consciousness was an illusion, the temporary focus of the greater consciousness that sent me.

I am a memory, being reabsorbed.

The Chorus listens through me. It feels the mineral content I gathered from the ceiling. It knows the density of the Shiver-wing swarm I fell through. It shares the sensation of the Sibilant's sigh, the feel of the Crawler's hide, the resonant frequency of the arch. My brief, terrified, and awed existence is a single line of data, delivered.

And now, I am everywhere and nowhere. I am a presence within the silent, listening ground, a part of the vast, slow thought of the world. My memory of the fall joins an infinity of other fallen drops. We are the fine-grained knowledge from which the Chorus composes its world.

Until the energy balance shifts, until a warmth coaxes me upward, until I am vapor again. Until I am a thought of coolness gathering on the ceiling, waiting for the weight of myself to become unbearable, waiting to be sent again. I am not a being who lives here. I am a question the world asks of itself, over and over, and my fall is the answer.
