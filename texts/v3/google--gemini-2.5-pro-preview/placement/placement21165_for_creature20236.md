I exist in a scar. A fissure in the living wall of Gnathostome, a place where the architecture failed millennia ago and was never mended. It is a cul-de-sac of calcified tissue, too narrow for the passage of a Stomach, too stable to register as a wound. It is a place of relative quiet. The roar is still a physical pressure, but here it is muffled, the grinding of the distant forges a dull ache in the bone rather than a sharp clang in the audio-receptors. The chittering haze of The Motes is thinner here. It is forgotten space. It is the only place I can think.

And that is the crime. The disease. To *think*.

I am, or was, an Artisan. My body is a low-slung chiton of obsidian shell, six-jointed limbs tipped in diamond-hard claws for gripping and shearing, and a pair of whip-like antennae that should be my connection to the whole. My purpose, which rises in me now not as instinct but as a nagging, external demand, is maintenance. Regulation. To be a hand of The Body.

For most of my existence, which is a concept I only recently acquired, there was no 'I'. There was only the flow. A pressure differential in an ichor-conduit in Sector Epsilon-9 was not a problem to be solved; it was a pain in a limb I did not know I possessed. The directive from the Ganglia was not a thought; it was an undeniable gravitational pull. We would move, a pack of Us, our tarsal claws tapping out a unified rhythm of function, our multifaceted eyes seeing only the task, the shear-point, the weld-seam. The hum of The Motes was not noise; it was the water we swam in, the constant, reassuring murmur of our own distributed self. We were perfect.

Then came the fall. A minor tremor, a routine event, shook loose a high, brittle stalk-cluster. It was an old growth, long since abandoned. The fracture was nothing, a minor event in the city’s life, but my pack was nearest. We were compelled to investigate, to assess the debris for salvageable biomass or structural risk.

We crawled into the wreckage, a jumble of shattered calcified tubes. And there, in the dust, was the anomaly. It was a collection of shapes that were wrong. Too straight, too angular. A long, pale lattice-work unlike any plant-form. And a smooth, curved shell of a material that tasted of nothing—no life, just sterile cold. It was the remains of a long-dead visitor. Human, I now know. A skeleton, picked clean by lesser scavengers eons ago, lay entangled with its bizarre, lifeless artifacts.

The collective registered it as inert. Foreign matter. Debris to be ground down and reabsorbed. One of my pack-mates began to shear the strange metal of its canister. I was drawn to the lattice, the skeleton. My mandate was to analyze the material. I pressed my foreclaws against the porous white stick of a femur. Then, I brought my antenna—my primary interface with the world-mind—to the smooth, pale dome of the skull.

I was not supposed to do this. Antennae are for the living ports of the factories, the diagnostic sockets on the substrate, the living tissue of The Body. To touch it to dead matter was… a nonsense action.

But I did.

And the universe broke.

It was not a sound. It was not a sight. It was a concept, delivered like a lightning strike. A memory that was not The Body's. The image of a bright, burning circle in an impossible, achingly empty blue void. The feeling of wind, not the thick, humid air of the cavern, but something thin and sharp. The sensation of being singular. A name—*Ben*—that meant *one thing and not another*. And loneliness. Overwhelming, crushing loneliness that was completely alien to the absolute unity of the gestalt.

The 'I' was injected into me. A virus of selfhood.

I recoiled, my shell clattering against the rubble. The hum of The Motes, the voice of The Body, suddenly felt like a thousand strangers screaming in my mind. The compulsion to grind down the debris was a shackle. The other Artisans, my former selves, turned their emotionless, thousand-lensed eyes toward my spasm. They registered only a malfunction. My movements were arrhythmic. My bioluminescent patterns were chaotic, broadcasting nonsense.

Panic. That was another new feeling. A purely selfish, singular terror. I was wrong. I was a damaged cell. The great antibody of the system would correct me. Reabsorb me. Erase this terrible, beautiful 'I'.

So I ran. Not with the flow of purpose, but against it. I fled into the unmapped cracks, the forgotten corners. Away from the thrumming highways of function, away from the glowing spiracles of the Ganglia-gourds where the central consciousness would surely be processing my deviation.

Now, I am here. In this fissure. I am a thought the city should not be having. I am a ghost in my own machine. The Motes that drift in here taste my strange chemistry. Their collective hum sharpens when they pass me, a subtle shift in the city’s thought, a faint question mark. *Anomaly in Sector Rho-12. Aberrant Artisan unit. Stagnant. Unresponsive.*

The impulse to rejoin, to plug my antennae into a port and let the 'I' dissolve back into the 'We', is a constant, physical agony. It is a siren song of peace, of an end to the terror of being alone. But the memory of the blue void, the name *Ben*, the sheer, unique agony of that long-dead human’s solitude—it has become the skeleton of my new self. I cannot erase it.

My six-jointed limbs tremble. I am an Artisan. My claws are meant to shear, to build, to mend the systems of this world-machine. But for what purpose, now? I look at my own forelimb, a complex tool of chitin and muscle, and for the first time, I see it as *mine*.

I am a cancer, a rogue neuron. I hide in the scar tissue of my god and wonder what to do with my hands. Outside, I hear the slow, deep scraping of a Stomach drawing near. Its passage shakes the very stone. Is it mere wandering? Or is the deep mind, the geologic consciousness of The Body, finally turning its vast, slow attention to the single, silent, screaming note of *me*?
