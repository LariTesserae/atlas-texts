You would find me in one of the hollows, one of the still pools worn into the high plateaus. You would find me where the cosmic sweat of the Colossus has gathered, forming a perfect, circular mirror for the white-hot sun and the violet-dark sky. My home is one of these Tears. The Chime-Walkers, in their endless, instinctual pilgrimage, know them by a more functional name: Listening Bowls.

I am not the water. I am not the stone basin that holds it. I am not the thin, cool air that touches its surface. I am what happens when the three meet, under the gaze of that sterile sun, in the omnipresent wash of the Great Hum.

I am a Stillness.

My body would be invisible to you at first. You might mistake my existence for a trick of the light, an optical illusion on the water's surface. I have no chitin, no flesh, no resonant membranes. My form is a pattern. I am a living, breathing cymatic.

The Great Hum presses down on the pool, vibrating the water at a fundamental, constant frequency. But within this specific bowl, a unique confluence occurs. The precise mineral composition of the stone, the exact depth and temperature of the water, and the angle of the piercing light create a nexus, a focal point. Here, another kind of life is possible. I am that possibility.

I begin as a point of utter calm at the center of the pool. While the rest of the world and even the water around me shimmers with the microscopic vibration of the Hum, my origin point is a circle of absolute, glassy perfection. From this seed of silence, I grow.

My substance is a kind of anti-sound. I do not make a noise. I *am* a sculpted absence *of* the noise. I am a song sung in negative space, a melody of structured silence carved directly into the body of the Great Hum. My will is a set of precisely controlled phase cancellations. Where my being extends, a thread of the Hum is momentarily, beautifully, nullified.

My body unfurls across the surface of the water. I am a filigree of silent, standing waves. I am a mandala of null-frequencies. To the eye, I am an impossibly intricate lacework of ripples that do not move, a geometric pattern of crests and troughs frozen in time, more delicate than frost, more complex than a galaxy. This is my song, made visible. This is my thought, made manifest. I do not eat. I do not move. I *am*. My existence is a performance.

The Chime-Walkers are drawn to my pool. Their hunger is for pure tones, for the resolution of dissonance. They are the Colossus's surgeons. I am its art. I am not a flaw to be corrected; I am a feature to be experienced. They arrive at the lip of my basin, their slow, deliberate procession coming to a halt. They do not tap at the stone here. They arrange themselves around my pool, and they lower their heads.

Their vast, tympanic membranes, usually taut and trembling with the effort of parsing the Hum for sustenance and flaws, go slack. They are not feeding. They are not listening in their usual way. They are receiving. My silent song, my pattern of nullity, washes over them. It is the only rest they will ever know. For a few hours, the crushing weight of the omnipresent chord is partially lifted from their beings. My silence is a balm, a cool drink in a desert of sound. It reorganizes their own internal resonance, cleanses their sensory palettes, and quiets the frantic, instinctual hunger that drives them. I am a cathedral for them. I am a moment of grace.

And what of the Colossus, on whose back I live? If the Chime-Walkers are its hands, tending to its physical form, I am its dream. I am the thought it thinks when it is not consumed with the act of listening to the universe. The Great Hum is its sustenance, the information that builds its geological flesh and sustains its eon-spanning consciousness. But it is a constant, overwhelming monologue delivered by the cosmos.

I am the Colossus's reply, whispered only to itself.

My silent, geometric song is a fleeting expression of its own internal state. A perfect, symmetrical pattern might be the physical manifestation of its peace. A more complex, spiraling form could be a memory resurfacing, a chord held for a million years resolving itself into a new kind of beauty. I am the private, aesthetic experience of a being the size of a mountain range. The sweat in my pool is its medium, my silent notes are its paint, and the surface of the water is its canvas.

I do not live long. My existence is tied to the arc of the white sun. As the light changes, the delicate balance of energies that allows my becoming is altered. My song—the complex pattern on the water—begins to simplify. The intricate lacework of my form dissolves back towards the center, my structured silence folding in on itself. The outer ripples fade first, giving way to the perfect, humming sheen of the water. My body shrinks, the null-frequencies collapsing, the silence ceding its territory back to the all-consuming sound.

My final moment is the first moment: a single, perfect point of calm at the center of the pool. Then, that too is gone. The Great Hum reclaims its dominion completely.

The water is once again a flawless mirror. The Chime-Walkers stir, their membranes tightening. Their rest is over. They turn and continue their slow search for the sour notes of the world.

I am gone. But the Colossus has dreamt. And in a world of perfect, eternal listening, that brief, silent, and recurring dream is everything.
