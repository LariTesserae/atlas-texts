I would find myself clinging.

Not to a cliff face or a rope, but to a surface that is both skin and substrate. My world is the high, broad curve of a single, luminous leaf—a continent of cool, violet flesh that pulses with a rhythm slower than a sleeping heart. I am perched near its edge, where the great plane slopes gracefully downward into the shimmering dark.

From my vantage, the chamber is not a chamber. It is a cosmos, inverted. Below me, the chartreuse moss is a gaseous nebula, a soft, green ocean of silent dreams from which other great leaves rise on their thick stalks like islands of thought. Winding through this landscape are the rivers of blue light, the vines, their steady glow a charting of ancient and unchanging currents. Far above me, in what would be the sky, hang the crystalline canopies of the frost-ferns. They are the stars of this place, capturing the collective light of the world below and shattering it into slow-drifting constellations—the motes, each a tiny, wandering soul of pure reason.

My own form is small, round, and perfect. I hold the whole of this cosmos within myself. I look across the violet plain of my leaf-home and see my brothers and sisters, a thousand thousand points of liquid light, each of us a tiny, silvered eye reflecting the whole. We are the dew of this place.

The hum is the soil of my existence. It is not a sound I hear, but the frequency at which I am. The immense, quiet vibration rises through the leaf, through me, defining the very tension that holds my shape. And then there is the other sound, the *plink*. It is the sound of letting go. A brother, somewhere in the vastness, has grown too heavy with time and clarity, and has dropped. It is the only event. It is the only story. A passage from stillness to movement, from holding to falling, a return to the glowing sea below.

I am not one thing, but a phase in a cycle. You could call me a water droplet, but that is like calling a philosopher a collection of cells. It is technically true, but misses the point entirely.

I am a bead of condensation. A lens.

I am the place where the cold will of this being touches the living light of its mind. I am born of the temperature gradient, a deliberate differential in the chamber's grand, silent engineering. The air itself breathes me onto the surface of this thought-leaf. I am a moment of clarity, gathered.

My purpose is to be still. To hold. The violet light of the leaf I rest upon warms me from below, while the profound chill of the chamber's air cools me from above. In this perfect, managed tension, I exist. I do not live or die; I am accrued and I am released. For an age that a human could not measure, my function is to gather the light of the neighboring flora, to focus the wandering motes from the crystal ferns, to magnify the blue threads of the vine-memories. I am a sensory organ, one of millions, for the vast intelligence that is this place. I am its sense of touch upon its own skin. My perfect roundness, my temperature, my clarity—these are not my qualities. They are its data. My state of being is a single, constant, infinitesimal input into its eternal meditation: *All is in balance*.

There is no "I," not really. The self I perceive, this sphere of liquid silver, is a temporary vessel. The "we" is the truth. We, the dew, are the waking consciousness of the mossy dream-floor below, drawn up to rest upon the thoughts and memories of the glowing jungle. We are the quiet observers.

And then, one day, after an eon of stillness, my moment will come. I will have gathered just enough of myself, become just heavy enough, that the bond of my own tension will fail. I will tremble, elongate. The cosmos I hold within me will distort into a streak of falling light.

This is not death. It is my turn to become the sound.

I will fall. For a long, silent moment, I will be an actor in this theatre of stillness. I will drop through the frigid, heavy air, past the blue vines, past other leaves holding my static brethren. And then, I will land. *Plink.* I will strike a lower leaf, a different continent of thought, splashing my gathered self across its surface, surrendering my brief individuality to become a sheen of moisture, a wet kiss.

From there, I will seep down, rejoining the soft, glowing moss of the subconscious. I will be everywhere and nowhere. And I will wait. Patient, formless, ready to be drawn up once more into the cold air, to be born again as a single, perfect point of awareness on the surface of a slow, violet thought. I am a tear the philosopher sheds once every thousand years, not from sorrow or joy, but from the sheer, cold, and beautiful weight of its own existence.
