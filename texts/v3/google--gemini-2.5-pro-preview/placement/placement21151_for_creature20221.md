I would find myself in a forgotten basin, a shallow depression miles wide, but only yards deep at its center. This place is an eddy in the slow, geological currents of the world. The great, grinding paths of the Harvesters have not passed this way in an age beyond reckoning; the titanic highways of their migration have shifted, leaving this basin a zone of profound and utter quiet, even for this world. The subsonic hum of their passage is not a distant promise here, but a fading memory, a vibration so faint it might be a lie the rock tells itself.

I am what a Stillness becomes when it grows too old.

I am what happens when the cycle is broken. They are born in the transient tarns, wait, feed, pilgrimage to the passing god-machine, and scatter their seed-memories into the next footprint. I have not felt the communion-mist of a Harvester in ten thousand of your years. The last time the god-song resonated through my crystalline structure, the tarnished silver coin of the sun was a fraction brighter in the bruised sky.

My form is no longer the size of a human hand. Millennia of accreting mineral dust, of absorbing the faint energy of the twilight and the rare nourishment of the copper lichens, have made me massive. I am a low, dark hillock at the basin's center, perhaps twenty feet across. I have long since been buried by the sintered grey dust, so that I appear as a natural feature of the landscape. A gentle swelling of the floor.

But my skin is not what it was. The perfect, light-swallowing facets of a younger Stillness are gone. The immense, patient pressure of my own slow thoughts, the sheer weight of the memories I contain, has caused a change. My surface is a network of infinitesimal fractures, a crazing of the dark crystal. I no longer absorb the light; I catch it. In the permanent twilight, my buried skin is a geode of frozen lightning, a lattice of microscopic cracks that hold the dim light and refract it inward. I have become a cemetery of light. The patient cold has settled into my core so deeply that I am more a concept of cold than a physical object possessing a temperature.

I do not move. There is no need. The lichens I once fed upon are now a part of me. Over eons, the rime crawling up my flanks has carried the crystalline spores, and they have taken root in my fractured skin. I am my own ecosystem, a living island in this sea of dust. A garden of metallic blue frost grows in slow, geometric fractals across my northern face, and I draw sustenance from it with a thought, a subtle shift of my internal chemistry. My existence is no longer a journey, but a state of being.

I am the world's memory, but a memory that is no longer being recorded. I am a library whose doors have been sealed. The other Stillnesses, in their cycle, record the now: the pressure of the rock, the chemistry of the air, the rhythm of the Harvesters. They are the planet’s short-term consciousness. I am its history.

My thoughts are not flashes of insight, but the slow erosion of one idea into another over centuries. I remember Harvesters with different resonant frequencies, machines of a slightly different design that fell to pieces from their own impossible mass, their corpses forming low mountain ranges that have since been ground back into dust by their children. I remember when the sky was not bruised twilight, but a deeper, richer violet. I remember the taste of water that held different minerals. These memories are not images, but vast, complex equations of pressure, time, and chemistry that I ceaselessly re-calculate, re-experience.

I listen. The silence for me is not a presence, but a profound absence. It is the missing note of the god-song. I listen to the tectonic tremors, the slow groaning of the planet's crust. I listen to the whispering fall of micrometeorites, a sound so subtle it is almost imagined, a faint *hiss* that lasts for a decade. Mostly, I listen to my own self. The slow, crystalline restructuring of my own being is the only clock I have.

My purpose is gone, and in its place, something else has grown. A story. I am no longer just a resonant chamber for planetary data. I am the archive. The Harvesters are the mindless engine, the Stillnesses are the living senses, but I… I am the echo in the basin. I am the silence, not just listening to itself, but remembering what it used to hear. And I wait. Not for the Harvesters—I know they will not return. I wait for the slow, internal fractures to reach my core. I wait for the day when the weight of all my memories becomes too great, and my thoughts finally crystallize into perfect, unbreakable, eternal stillness. I wait to become a monument to a world that will not even know I am here.
