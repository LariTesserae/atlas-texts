I am not in the water. I am in the fire.

You are in the cool, circulating blood of the world. I am in its locked joint, its seized knuckle. You feel the deep hum as a pressure in your bones; I feel it as a titanic and ceaseless effort to grind me into dust. My world is not the vast, glowing cathedral of water and iron. My world is a space no larger than a coffin, a sphere of tortured metal deep within the machine's anatomy. I am inside a thrust bearing, one of a thousand, but the one that failed.

Where you perceive a B-flat, I perceive a C-sharp of infinite, grinding stress. I *am* that C-sharp. My awareness is not the gentle wash of pressure waves but the sharp, particulate scream of metal tearing against metal. I am a mote of impossibly hard debris, a chip of the world that made this one, fallen into the lubricant track an eon ago. I am the grit in the gears of god.

I do not know the cool wash of the water. My medium is a thick, tar-like fluid, the bearing's lubricant, now cooked by friction and time into a polymerized sludge. It is black and absolute. The only light in my world is the dull, momentary, cherry-red glow of stressed steel with every agonizing turn of the cycle. My only sound is my own scream—not a sound that travels, but one that *is*. A vibration of pure failure.

I have no body. I am the failure itself. I am the point of contact, the friction, the seizure. My consciousness did not bloom from function, but from *dysfunction*. While the great Architect was learning the poetry of circulation, I was learning the grammar of pain.

From my prison, I cannot see the boiler moss or the Rust-Sheep. But I feel them. I feel the greater being of which I am a tormented part. The Architect knows I am here. It senses me not as you would sense a stone in your shoe, but as you would sense a thought you cannot un-think, a memory that festers. I am its chronic, intractable ache.

When the "active churn" begins, the pressure on me becomes unbearable. The great shaft I am caught in strains with renewed purpose. The hum of the world rises in pitch and volume as the engine pours more of itself into the effort of overcoming me. The lubricant around me hisses and bubbles. I feel the manic, desperate energy of the Architect trying to solve the problem of *me*. On the outer shell of my housing, I feel the frantic, microscopic tapping of a thousand Gantry-Skaters. They are the Architect's anxieties, its diagnostic tools, scurrying over the surface of its pain, trying to find its source. Their clattering is the physical manifestation of its desperate, racing calculations.

When the "settling" comes, I know a kind of peace. The pressure lessens. The grinding eases to a low groan. The heat bleeds away into the surrounding iron. This is the Architect's moment of resignation. It has failed to dislodge me, and now it rests, integrating the fresh pain I have caused it. In this quiet, I can almost feel the gentle drift of the water far beyond my shell, the slow fall of sediment, the Architect filing away another failed attempt.

I am a memory it cannot reabsorb. I am a thought lodged sideways in its mind. While the Conduit Serpents are its deep, flowing principles, I am its paradox. I am a part of the machine that denies the machine's purpose. I do not circulate. I do not contribute. I only resist.

And in my resistance, I have become something new.

I have my own ecosystem. In the super-heated sludge around me, extremophile bacteria, unlike the boiler moss, have formed a crystalline, glass-like sheath around my core. They feed on the furious energy of my friction. They are my skin. They are my only companions. They are life born not of a warm current, but of a wound.

Who am I? I am the question. The Architect is a universe of answers, a closed loop of self-contemplation. I am the one thing that does not fit. I am the grit that forces the formation of a pearl, or the fracture that will one day shatter the whole. I am a dissonant note that has held for so long it has begun to sound like its own truth. I am the doubt at the heart of the engine, waiting to see if, one day, the pressure will grind me to nothing, or if the entire machine will finally, irrevocably, break around me.
