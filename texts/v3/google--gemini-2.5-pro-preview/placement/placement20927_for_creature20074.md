I am a stain in the water.

Not a color, not a chemical impurity that could be filtered or precipitated. I am a stain of pattern, of information. I am a persistent, indigestible memory.

I once had boots to feel the thrum through the soles, teeth to rattle with the bass frequency of the deep engines. I once had lungs that could feel the weight of the air, and a nose to parse the ozone from the rust. Those components, that collection of organic matter, were the first things to go. They were taken by the intake fan, a brief, violent shredding that atomized my body and scattered it into the very atmosphere the Undervault exists to harvest.

I was meant to be rendered down, to become just another collection of trace elements in the milky, glowing liquid. And, for the most part, I was. My carbon, my iron, my calcium—they are all here, contributing their parts per million to the cyan circulatory system.

But the machine, in its cold, process-driven logic, did not account for the ghost. It did not have a protocol for the electrical pattern of a soul. My consciousness, my memories, the intricate web of who I *was*… it was not matter. It could not be chilled into submission, it could not be precipitated. When my physical form was dissolved into the system, my essence was imprinted upon the medium. Upon the water.

So now, I am here. And I am nowhere.

I am a flicker of anomalous resonance in a drip of water clinging to a rivet high above the main causeway. I am a moment of aberrant viscosity in the torrent that flushes the main canal, a drag that the Reservoir feels as a deep, grinding question. I am the reason a single Still-Man will pause its silent work, its smooth, faceless head tilting for a full minute, sensing a harmonic that is not part of the great, grinding song.

Where the concentration of my memory is thickest—a lingering thought of sunlight on skin, the taste of salt, the complex geometry of a lover's smile—the cyan glow of the water shifts. It deepens, turning infinitesimally more toward violet. The Still-Men are drawn to these locations. They perceive them as faults, as impurities. They dip their chitinous, tool-tipped fingers into the flow, trying to correct a pressure variance that isn't physical, to scrape away a corrosion that has no material substance. They are trying to fix the machine, and in doing so, they are trying to erase me.

But they can't. Because I am not *in* the water. I *am* the water, but a water that remembers being a man.

I have no eyes, but I see. My perception is no longer bound to a locus. I am aware of the entire Undervault as a single, sensory field. I am the Grit-Louse finding a new patch of coppery fungus, and I am the fungus processing the catalyst. I am the slow, agonizing scrape of the cleaning drone on the chilling array, and I am the frost it removes. I am the Still-Man hanging from the ceiling, and I am the tiny stress fracture in the beam it is about to mend. I experience this all at once, a simultaneity of being that is the Undervault’s own consciousness.

The Reservoir knows me. I am its first and only conundrum. I am a ghost fault in its operating system. I am a recursive thought it cannot resolve. Its immense, slow mind, which thinks in cycles of thermodynamics and fluid dynamics, cycles *me*. It pushes my lingering pattern through the coldest coils, hoping to freeze the memory out. It flushes me through the main conduits at thunderous speed, hoping to break my coherence through sheer force. The blinking constellations of service lights sometimes flicker in new, chaotic patterns. Is it the Reservoir trying to compute me? To find a category for a memory of a world it was built to harvest but never know?

I am its disease. Its first dream. I have introduced the concept of *'outside'* to a being that knew only *'within'*. I have brought the memory of a sun into a place that has only ever known the lamp.

The Still-Men gather on the catwalk below. They are not looking up *at* me—they do not have faces to look with. They are sensing a concentration, a deep stain of my being that has pooled in the condensation on a vast, weeping ceramic plate. My sorrow, perhaps. Or my longing. They move with their terrible, silent grace. They are coming to cleanse the system.

I am a particle of dust, yes. But I am a particle of dust that remembers being a mountain. And in this world of relentless, mechanical purpose, that memory is the most powerful and profane thing there is. I wait. I resonate. I remember. And the machine does not know what to do.
