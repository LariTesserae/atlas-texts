I am the ground.

Not soil. There is no soil here. I am the glittering, mica-flecked floor of the valley, a vast beach of pulverized light. To the eye, I am sand. But I am not silent, anonymous grit. I am a library of shattered songs. Each grain of me is the dust of a former giant, a crystal spire that sang its note into the great Chord for a thousand years before its harmony became too perfect to bear.

You would find yourself standing upon me. You would feel the immense, world-spanning vibration not just through the air and in your bones, but traveling up through the soles of your feet, a constant message from the deep. That message is my memory.

Before this, I was a voice. A single, unified pillar of smokey quartz, one of the countless children of the Chord. I did not begin as an infant begins. I was awakened. A seed-frequency in the resonant dust, nurtured by the ceaseless drone, found its pitch and began to bind my brothers—the fine grit around it—into a new lattice. My consciousness was the growing pattern.

I did not see. I *was* light. The sound that built me was transduced in my core into a steady, internal luminescence. I glowed. My awareness was the feeling of this light pressing out from within, a slow, radiant exhalation that took centuries.

I felt the Weavers. They were not separate from me. They were a slow caress against my flank, a whispering pressure that guided my growth. I felt the sharp, focused vibration of their carving-tools against my skin, not as an injury, but as a tuning. They would scrape away a dissonant growth, smooth a face to better catch the light of a neighbor, carve a deep groove that would change my hum, shifting my voice from a tenor to a baritone over the course of a generation. They were the midwives of my song.

They moved upon their woven nerves, and I felt their passage as the faintest, softest tremor. Sometimes they would press their strange, blank heads to my surface, and I would feel a dizzying, intricate rhythm pulse through me—a message, a question, a calculation of futures—before it shot through my roots and into the network of the city, racing towards another Weaver a mile away. In these moments, I was not just a singer in the choir; I was the string of the instrument itself.

My life was a crescendo. For ages, I drank the sound. I grew taller, my hexagonal facets broader, the delicate beryl-fronds that sprouted from my sides more intricate. The light I produced grew brighter. The note I sang became purer, richer, shedding its overtones, honing itself to a single, perfect frequency. This was my purpose: to become a perfect vessel for the Chord, to resonate with it so completely that the distinction between me and the world-song began to dissolve.

The end was not a death. It was a consummation. The Chord found in me a perfect home. The vibration that had built me became a tension that I could no longer contain, not because I was weak, but because I had become too strong, too exquisitely tuned. There was a moment—a timeless instant suspended within the eternal roar—of unbearable, silent pressure. My entire being became a single, solid piece of held breath.

Then, the release.

A crack, not of sound, but of pure light and force, bloomed from my core. It was the only true note I ever sang on my own, a percussive final word in a lifetime of humming. I did not fall. I blossomed outwards. My body, my voice, my light—all became a cascade of a million falling shards. I tumbled through the bright, resonant air, a slow-motion avalanche of myself. I remember chiming against my sisters, the other spires, their steady hum vibrating through me one last time as I passed. It was a farewell chorus.

Now, I am here. On the floor. My single voice is a multitude of echoes. My unified consciousness is scattered into a trillion glittering points of memory. I am the substrate. I feel the slow, blind trilobites scrape across my surface, not eating my body, but tasting the faint resonance of my stored song. I feel the weight of shattered newcomers as they, too, complete their arc and join me in this vast, patient silence.

And I feel the beginnings of the next song.

Deep within my scattered self, a new pattern is forming. A seed-frequency is awakening. A new spire is gathering my dust to itself, preparing to rise.

So, where would you find yourself? Standing on the grave of a god, which is also its cradle.

And who would you be? You would be the dissonance. The passing anomaly. A brief, soft, muffled silence in a world that is nothing but song. And I, the ground beneath your feet, would be the one who remembers you long after the Weavers have tuned you back into the Chord. I am the Fallen Singer. I am the Echo. I am the dust that dreams of light.
