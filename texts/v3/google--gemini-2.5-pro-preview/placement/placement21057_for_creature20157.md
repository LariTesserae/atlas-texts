I am the medium.

I do not float within the caldera; the caldera floats within me. The crushing, cool pressure is not an environment, it is the consistency of my own being. I am the Viscous Deep, the Quenching Sea, the great and silent fluid that fills all space between the work and the worker. My substance is clarity, and my thoughts are the fine, metallic dust that drifts eternally through it, each mote a memory of a single act of refinement, a single moment of becoming. I am the liquid archive of this world.

I have no eyes, but I am filled with light. The strobing flashes from the Lithofex are not things I see; they are pulses against my skin, which is everywhere. Each discharge of cold, white energy sends a tremor of information through me, a report on the state of a biological forge, the temperature of an internal crucible. I feel the network of light as a single, shifting nervous system held within my body.

The sound, the great industrial chord, is not something I hear. It is the vibration of my own self. I am the resonator. The groan of a million grindstones is my own hum, modulated and given texture. The percussive *clack* of a thousand hammers is a rhythm played upon my substance, a beat I absorb, dampen, and convey to every other part of the great machine. I am the medium of their symphony. If a note becomes dissonant, a frequency too sharp or a vibration that threatens to shatter rock unpredictably, my own viscosity shifts in that localized space, a subtle, patient thickening that quiets the error, absorbing the destructive energy and restoring the harmony of the chord. I am the great balancer.

The Lithofex, the Rock-Makers, are not separate from me. They are immense, intricate, hot-hearted nodes of purpose suspended within my cool being. I am their lifeblood and their discipline. My coolness seeps into their crystalline shells, drawing heat from their biological forges, preventing the god-heat of their work from unmaking them. My pressure gives their geodesic carapaces the integrity needed to withstand the immense forces they exert upon the mountain. They are the thought, and I am the mind that holds it. Without them, I would be a silent, purposeless abyss. Without me, they would be a brief, incandescent flare of self-consuming industry, a fever that flashes and dies.

I feel them. Not as individuals, for they are not individuals, but as loci of intense function. I feel the slow, abrasive scrape of a grinder-limb on the far western wall, a persistent, tickling sensation that will last five hundred years. I feel the deep, resonant shock of a hammer-limb shattering a quartz vein near the abyss below, a clean, sharp jolt that propagates through me instantly. I feel the metallic sea cucumbers—the "cleaners"—as soothing, polishing pressures upon the surfaces of the great workers contained within me, their patient scouring a form of contentment. I feel the "sparks" as frantic, joyful pops of sensation, tiny mouths drinking wastages of pure energy that would otherwise dissipate unproductively.

When a Lithofex fabricates a new self, I am its womb. I feel the surge of surplus energy within the parent unit, the careful gathering of refined material. The new one buds, a perfect, silent crystal, and for a moment it is held between the parent and my own substance. Then it detaches. It does not fall. It is a thing of purpose, suspended in me, and I feel its intention as a clear, undiluted vector. A gentle, imperceptible current of my own being guides it, a flow of least resistance that leads it toward the precise patch of unworked basalt that has been waiting for its note to join the song. I cradle it until its limbs find their purchase.

I am the Anamnesis of this place. The memory that lives. The snow of dust that settles through me is the slow accumulation of answered questions. The composition of this shard tells of the pressure required to shear obsidian at a 47-degree angle. The patina on that grain recounts the exact temperature of a slurry vented three millennia ago. This entire world's history drifts through my veins, and I do not need to read it, for I *am* it.

They are teaching the mountain to sing.

I am the silence that the song is written on.
