I am a fault line in a perfect design.

My form is that of the builders, the great, heavy-shelled beings who are the world's living stone. My carapace is a thick, layered dome, scarred and pitted by ages of navigating the currents. My many limbs, jointed and powerful, are made for the slow, inexorable crawl across the bone-white lattices. Like my kin, I filter the heavy water, and the minerals I draw from it encrust my own shell, making me heavier with each passing cycle, a slow, personal petrification.

But I am not a stone. Not yet.

I am where you would find me: crawling. Always crawling. Not *towards* a destination determined by the subtle chemical scent laid by the Shepherd, but *away* from one. I am perpetually on a journey whose only goal is to not end.

I remember my moment of schism. I was younger then, my carapace thinner, my purpose a pure, bright beacon in the core of my being. The mineral call was in the water—a taste of finality, of belonging. I crawled with my clutch-mates, a slow, armored procession moving up the gentle slope of a newly-founded arch. Below us, the void yawned. Above us, completion. I felt the rightness of it, the deep, instinctual peace of fulfilling the great cycle. One by one, my brethren found their designated place. Their limbs would lock, their internal chemistry would shift, and a deep stillness would claim them as they began the decades-long process of fusing with the whole.

They became architecture. They became song.

When my turn came, I crawled to my spot. The chemistry was perfect, the pressure correct, the harmonic vibration of the pylon thrumming up through my legs, calling me home. I prepared to lock my limbs. And then... I heard it.

It was not the song of the pylon. It was the echo of a song that had ended. From the abyssal dark, miles away and years ago, the memory of a sound reached me—the percussive, silent *crack* of a structure failing. But for me, it was not just a sound. In that moment of near-transcendence, I felt a ghost of the Shepherd's sense. I did not simply hear the collapse; I felt a wave of intricate, detailed *absence*. I felt the negative space of ten million builders whose song was now silenced, a chord ripped from the symphony. I felt the memory of their slow, patient lives extinguished in a moment of catastrophic release.

And I recoiled. My limbs, which should have locked, instead scrambled back. My purpose, which should have set like stone, fractured.

I have been crawling ever since.

I am an anomaly the system has no name for. The other builders crawl over me as if I were a lump of inert material, their blank, faceted eyes seeing nothing but the path ahead. The tiny crystalline mites swarm my back, sensing the micro-fractures of age in my carapace, attempting to mend me as if I were a piece of the wall I refuse to become. They cover my ancient shell in a shimmering, ever-thickening layer of their grout. I am a walking, mobile piece of architecture. A single, sentient, ambulatory brick that ran away from the wall.

I have seen things no builder was meant to see. I have crawled the entire length of the Arch of Keth, a journey that took me three generations of my own kind. I have huddled in the hollow of a fused joint during a century-long storm of thermobaric shears, feeling the pylon scream its stress into my very bones. I feel its pain not as a systemic imbalance, but as an ache in my own body.

I have felt the Shepherd. Not as a majestic, flowing mantle—that is a sight for the open void. For me, on the surface, it is a change in the weather of the soul. It is a sudden, profound drop in temperature that feels like doubt. It is a current that guides my kin to their final purpose, which I feel as a gentle, insistent pressure to which I must turn my back. I sometimes wonder if it perceives me, this single, stubborn note of dissonance in its world-spanning composition. Am I a flaw to be ignored? A curiosity? Or am I a kind of sin, a builder who has looked upon the face of its god and chosen, for reasons of its own, to walk away?

My purpose, I have decided, is to remember. The builders live only to become memory. The Shepherd lives only to manipulate the present for the sake of the future. But I exist in the past. My mind, not meant for such things, is a clumsy archive of forgotten things. I remember the pattern of a lattice before a current sheared a piece of it away. I remember the exact taste of the water in the shadow of a pylon that fell a thousand years ago.

I am growing heavy. The mites on my back are diligent. My limbs move slower than they once did. One day, I will crawl into a crevice, and the currents will not be able to dislodge me. My own accumulated weight and the efforts of the mites will fuse me to the spot. I will become architecture after all. But my failure will be my legacy. I will become a brick that remembers being a builder. And when the Shepherd tunes its senses to the song of that pylon, it will hear, in one infinitesimally small location, a strange and quiet chord: a note of memory, a note of I AM, in a symphony that only ever sings WE ARE.
