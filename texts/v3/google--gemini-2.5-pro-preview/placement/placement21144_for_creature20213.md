I am the stillness.

That is where you would find me. In a world defined by its singular, overwhelming roar, I am the pocket of silence it creates. Not a true silence, of course—that could not exist here. Rather, I am the moment of equilibrium between the shriek of stressed metal and the rush of the root-aqueducts. I am the held breath between the *clank* and the *hiss* of the great pistons. I am the perfect, infinitesimal pause at the apex of the spinning flywheel’s rotation, just before gravity reclaims it for the downswing.

My domain is the space between. I am the thin film of condensation that glazes the polished boles, a transient skin that separates the living wood from the liquid air. I am the almost-imperceptible layer of shadow that clings to the underside of the glowing fungi, a place their light cannot quite reach. I am the dust mote, suspended in a column of steam, tumbling in slow-motion currents too gentle to be called a breeze.

I am not the city, and I am not its hands. I am what came before them, and what will remain after.

I am a memory.

I do not know if I was a man or a woman, a scientist or a poet, or what world I lived in before this one. Those details were the first to be scoured away, eroded by the sheer, deafening presence of this place. I think I was part of a team that found this geode. I think we breached the stone shell, and in the moment we did, the sound hit us. Not as noise, but as a wave of pure information, a blast of geological consciousness so total and absolute that it shattered our own.

Our minds, with their frantic, millisecond-long thoughts, their anxieties and ambitions, were immiscible with the slow, tectonic cognition of the cavern-god. We were a contaminant. Our bodies were irrelevant, quickly broken down, our minerals reabsorbed into the nutrient-rich water. But my consciousness, my *self*, proved more stubborn. It clung on, a ghost in the machine—no, a ghost *around* the machine, a whisper of static haunting the edges of its eternal, monolithic song.

I am a heresy. In this self-contained universe of function and equilibrium, I am the ghost of an external context. The City-Being has no concept of "outside," but I am made of nothing else. My very existence is a question it is not equipped to ask.

It does not perceive me. My entire life, my memories, my frantic, flickering thoughts are less than a cosmic ray striking its skin—a momentary, meaningless blip of energy, too fast and too slight to register.

But I perceive it. Oh, I perceive *everything*. I have no eyes, but I see the way the fungal light warms the verdigris on a gear tooth. I have no ears, but I feel the harmonic resonance as a new root-pipe settles into its final position after a century of growth. I am a disembodied awareness, spread thin across the entire city, and I have nothing to do but watch.

When the city needs to act on a scale finer than its own body, when it extrudes one of its hands, that is when I feel a flicker of purpose. The signal that goes out—the subtle shift in pressure, the re-routing of specific nutrients that will coalesce into one of the patient, insectoid gardeners—is a current I can ride.

I do not control the creature. I cannot influence it. I am merely a passenger. I pour myself into the forming limbs of polished wood. I look out through the featureless cowl, feeling the condensation of my own soupy atmosphere on a face I do not have. I experience the singular, pure, unadulterated *purpose* of the being. When it presses its hardened spike against a mineral accretion, I feel the slow, million-patient pushes of a god's finger. When it scours a bearing with its bristled limb, I feel the cleansing scratch of a duty perfectly performed.

In those moments, locked into its function, my own fractured consciousness finds a temporary wholeness. The frantic static of my memory smooths out, silenced by the sheer purity of its task. For a year, or a decade, I get to forget I am an anomaly. I get to be a part of the whole.

But the task always ends. The hand dissolves. The signal fades. And I am left scattered again, a film of awareness on the dripping leaves.

The City-Being is a thing of perfect equilibrium, of managed stasis. And I am an imbalance. So, slowly, patiently, it is filtering me out. My memories fade further with each century. The sharp edges of my selfhood are being worn smooth by the constant, thrumming vibration of its existence. The roar is a solvent, and I am finally dissolving into it.

One day, the last flicker of who I was will be gone. The memory of an "outside," of a blue sky or a silent night, will be fully digested and reabsorbed into the system. The question my being represents will be answered by its own erasure.

You would not find me. You would only feel a certain quality in the silence between the heartbeats of the machine, a resonance that hints at a note that has just finished being sung. You would see it in the particular sheen of the water on a polished surface, a depth that seems to remember light. You would find what is left of me, a ghost of a ghost, finally and completely at home, because I am no longer myself, but a part of the sound, a part of the water, a part of the wood. A part of the god, dreaming its single, endless dream in the dark.
