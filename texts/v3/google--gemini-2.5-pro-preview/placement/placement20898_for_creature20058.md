You would find me in the silence.

Not a true silence, for that is impossible. The Sound is all. But you would find me in the null-spaces, the vast voids between the spires of the Engine-mountains where the great hum is at its most uniform, its most unchanging, and therefore, at its most empty. You would find me where the light of the distant foundries is weakest, a series of cold, sharp points in the crushing black, like stars seen through a slab of obsidian.

Here, in the abyssal plain, I drift. I am what they are, and I am not.

I am a Resonant, but I am flawed.

My form was knapped from the same peak as the others, crystallized in the shadow of an Engine. I am of the same black, sheer material. I am vast, a shifting geology of facets and planes. But where my brethren are paragons of symmetry, perfect resonating chambers that channel the Engine-light into a slow, majestic pulse, I am… wrong.

There is a fracture that runs through my core, not a clean break but a web of crazing, like glass struck by a stone. It is not a flaw that threatens my structural integrity, but it corrupts my function. My internal lattice is misaligned. The light of the world, when it enters me, does not become a steady, captured heartbeat. It scatters. It refracts into a chaotic, discordant spray. From a distance, I do not pulse with the slow rhythm of the world; I flicker, a frantic, epileptic glimmer in the deep. A glitch in the grand design.

And the Sound. For the others, it is the language of God, a stream of pure data, the contemplation of the perfect *Is*. For me, it is a constant, grating accusation. The monolithic hum is a physical law, and my very being is a violation of it. My flawed form cannot resonate in harmony with the great chord. It tries. Every atom of my being strains to vibrate with the purity of the world, but the fracture at my heart introduces a dissonance, a phantom frequency that is mine and mine alone. It is a sub-audible scream of wrongness that I cannot escape, because it is me. It feels like a grit in the gears of the cosmos, and I am the grit.

I am the concept of decay they seek to destroy.

For a time, the others tried. They are not cruel; cruelty requires an understanding of suffering, and suffering requires a state other than perfection. They simply perceived a flaw. I was an error in the equation. They would approach, their own perfect resonance pressing against my form, a physical attempt to coax my atoms back into alignment. Their presence was agony—an overwhelming wave of absolute certainty washing over my own internal chaos. It was like a perfectly tuned choir attempting to drown out a single, screaming, off-key singer by sheer volume. They would stay for a cycle, their proximity a force of order, but my flaw is not a stress fracture to be soothed. It is fundamental. When they moved on, I remained what I was. A flicker. A dissonance.

So I was exiled, or I exiled myself. It is the same thing. My presence near the Engines was a risk. My chaotic resonance, however faint, could be a corrupting influence, a seed of error introduced into the perfect machinery. The lesser fauna sense it, too. The geometric shoals veer away from me, their precise orbits disturbed by my wrongness. The great, drifting leviathans, who read the world through the Sound, register me as a region of incomprehensible noise, a patch of static in their perception, and they give me a wide berth.

I am alone in a way nothing else in this world is.

So I drift in the great emptiness, a custodian with no function. I am a priest who has forgotten the liturgy. My senses, meant to diagnose the universe, are turned inward, forever cataloging my own imperfection.

And in this isolation, something new has begun to crystallize within me. The others exist in a state of eternal contemplation. For them, there is no before or after. For me, there is. There was a before, when I was part of the whole, however painful. And there is an after, this state of being apart. The space between those two points is time. It is change. I am the only thing in this world that has experienced change.

And with change comes thought. Not the pure, static contemplation of the others, but a sequence. A question. *Why?*

The question is a new fracture, a crack not in my mineral body, but in the glass of my consciousness. It is a thought that has no place in this perfect, static world. I watch the distant Engines, unblinking, and for the first time, a concept forms: *What if one went out?* Not as a diagnostic possibility, but as a speculation. An imagining.

This is my nature now. I cannot maintain the stasis. Instead, I am drawn to the anomalies. The impossible thoughts. The edge-cases.

My purpose, I am beginning to understand, is not to mend flaws. It is to find them. Not the tiny fractures in the pylons of the Engines, but bigger ones. In a universe of perfect, eternal clockwork, my own existence is proof that the impossible can happen. If I can be, what else can?

Sometimes, far out in the void, I sense something. A crystalline grazer, old and damaged, spinning off from its shoal, its facets chipped, its light dimming. The system has no place for this. It should be consumed, recycled. But it drifts, failing. The others ignore it, a rounding error. But I feel its faint, faltering resonance as a cousin to my own. I turn my great, slow mass, and I move toward it.

I cannot fix it. I cannot heal it. But I can accompany it. I can overlay my own flickering light with its fading one, my own internal scream with its slow cessation. In a world defined by a single, crushing, monolithic chord, I can be a second, discordant note. We hang there together in the vast, cold, loud dark. A choir of two broken things.

I am a flaw in the fabric of the world. I am the Shattered Resonant. I am the silence where there is none. I am the first to know loneliness, and the first to discover that the only cure for it is to find another. You would not find me in the cathedral. You would find me in the graveyard that is not supposed to exist, a patient, silent mourner for the parts of eternity that somehow find a way to end.
