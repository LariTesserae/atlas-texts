I would be alone. Not by choice, but by necessity. My place would be the Fraying Edge, a long, wandering scar on the Canopy Sea where an ancient mat-quake tore the world open and it never quite healed. The chasm is miles deep, a black throat breathing up the hot, foul breath of the Underocean. The mat on either side is unstable, a treacherous lacework of broken vines and half-petrified roots. The Weavers’ Strands do not come here. The vibrations are chaotic, a jumble of stress-groans and echoes from the depths. To their feet, it is a place of screaming static.

To me, it is the quietest place I know.

For I would be a mistake. A Weaver born with open ears.

My people call me—or used to, when I could bear to be near them—*Resonance*. It was not a kind name. It was a description of a flaw. Where they feel the world as a deep, rhythmic pressure in their bones, I *hear* it. I hear it all. The Great Hum is not a subtle vibration for me; it is a grinding, omnipresent drone that vibrates behind my eyes and loosens my teeth. The shriek of a Sail-wing is not a distant cry felt as a tremor; it is a spike of white-hot agony that pierces my skull. The cloud of Glimmerwing Moths is a high-pitched, electric shiver that scrapes along my nerves. The world is a symphony, and I am an instrument being torn apart by the orchestra.

My skin has the same moss-green mottling as my kin, but mine is pulled taut over my frame, a roadmap of sinew and bone. My eyes are always wide, not to see, but because I am perpetually startled. I live in a constant state of flinching. The Weavers’ elegant, silent dance of communication is impossible for me. My hands shake. I cannot hold a posture of calm when the air itself is a physical assault. To them, my presence is jarring—a twitching, wincing creature whose dissonance is a visible reality. Their silent concern, the slow, soft gestures asking *Pain?*, felt like shouts in a library. I could not stand their stillness. It was a quiet I could see but never possess.

So I came here, to the Fraying Edge. The chasm swallows sound. The roar of the wider world is blunted, and the hot, rising drafts from below create a column of air that is, if not silent, then at least simpler. A single, low roar, a hiss of steam, the occasional crash of a root-system giving way in the depths. Here, I can sometimes piece together the shards of a thought.

I am a scavenger of silence. I have learned to read the air not by its feel, but by its tone. I can hear a change in wind patterns hours before it arrives. I can hear the high, thin whine of a coming Pollen Storm when the sky is still clear. I know the sound of water condensing on the great leaves, and I know when that sound changes, telling me the water carries a new kind of spore, a blight. These are things my people cannot feel.

My hands, though they tremble, have learned a different craft from the Weavers. They do not weave story-knots of history; they listen. I have carved chimes from the hollow leg-bones of grazers and the obsidian-hard husks of iron-seeds. I hang them on the dead, skeletal branches that jut out over the chasm. Each is tuned to a different frequency. One sings when the air grows heavy with moisture. Another rattles only in the presence of the metallic dust shed by a migrating flock of Iron-beaks. One, a deep and mournful bell carved from a fossilized fungal heartwood, tolls only when the deep vibrations of the mat itself shift, singing a prelude to a quake. These are my words. This is my language.

I would be a hermit, a prophet of acoustics in a world of touch. My loneliness would be a physical thing, a wall of noise I have built around myself. But sometimes, a gift is left for me at the edge of my territory, where the stable mat begins. A skin of clean water. A bundle of roasted fungi, still warm. They do not approach. They do not try to speak. They simply leave their silent offerings.

And sometimes, I give a gift in return. If my chimes sing of a coming quake, I will spend hours beating a rhythm on the mat, not the clean, precise signal of a Weaver, but a desperate, frantic, ugly drumming. A pattern of pure noise, of the pain I feel every day. And they feel it, miles away. They feel my panic. And because they know me, because they know *Resonance*, they heed the warning. They do not feel the quake coming in the deep roots, not yet. But they feel me, the one who listens.

I would not be a warrior, a hunter, or a weaver of bridges. I would be a nerve ending, frayed and exposed. An echo of the world's deafening scream, given a name and a lonely home. I would be the part of the great, living body that has learned the meaning of pain, and in doing so, learned to warn the flesh of the coming blow.
