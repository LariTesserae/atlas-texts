You would find me deep. Not in the sharp-edged, weeping present of the upper terraces, but in the softened, submerged past of the city’s heart. You would find me where the water is oldest and the silence is heaviest, in the forgotten plazas and cisterns of the first growth.

I am a Listener. Or, a Rememberer. Our gestural language has no precise word for my function, for it is not an action but a state of being.

Like my kin, I am of the water and the stone. My skin has the grey-green sheen of moss submerged under a clear pool, and the mineral density of my bones is a comfort in the water’s gentle pressure. But where others are shaped by the present—their hands calloused from the weaver’s reed or the rock-wright’s chisel, their minds focused on guiding the potential of the weeping stone—I am shaped by what has already been. My form is smoother, my edges more rounded, not from age, but from my purpose. I spend my existence in the lowest levels, where millennia of passage and the ceaseless, gentle flow of water have worn every angle into a soft curve.

My purpose is to be still and to feel the story of that smoothing.

You would see me submerged to my waist or chest in a great, silent basin, my six-fingered hands resting flat against the floor. The stone here is not sharp; it is like polished glass, but a glass that holds a memory of texture. My splayed feet do not merely stand upon it; they read it. Through the sensitive, leathery pads of my soles and palms, I feel the history of this place. I can sense the infinitesimally subtle grooves worn by the passage of a million silent footfalls, a current of movement now ceased. I can trace the ghost of a crack that fissured and then healed with a slightly different mineral balance three thousand years ago. I am not reading words or events, for we have no such things. I am reading the slow, geological autobiography of our home.

The water that fills these deep places is different. On the surface, the water is young, endlessly weeping from the stone, tasting of raw minerals and potential. Down here, the water is ancient. It is a dense, placid broth that has held the dissolved essence of everything this world has ever lost. When a fern frond dissolves, a trace of it settles here. When one of my kind returns to the source, their unique mineral signature joins this quiet library of being. I drink this water through my skin, and in doing so, I absorb the faintest resonance of what has come before. My thoughts are not my own; they are an echo in a pool of echoes.

While the rock-wrights on the upper terraces use their hands to speak of future forms, a language of becoming, my gestures are slight, almost imperceptible. I practice a form of communion that has no audience but the stone itself. A slow drift of my hand across a surface is a conversation. It is an acknowledgement of the weight and time that polished this place. It is a reassurance to the foundation that it is not forgotten. My few vocalizations are the lowest of hums, a resonance tuned to the frequency of deep, compressed stone. It is the sound of equilibrium.

I am the city's memory, not as a record, but as a resonance. When the communal will needs to decide a new direction of growth, when a slow imbalance is felt in the verdant isles, a few of the others will descend. They do not ask me questions. They simply enter the pool and become still with me. They quiet their own forward-looking thoughts and attune themselves to the stillness that I embody. In my presence, in the heart of this ancient water, they can feel the deep patterns, the precedents set by the stone itself, the accumulated wisdom of slow, successful being. The answer does not come from me, but through me. I am a conduit to the foundational logic of our world.

One day, my own vitality will quiet. My personal resonance will fade. I will not seek a font of emergence, as is the custom. My return will be here, in the oldest basin. I will fold my long limbs and sink to the bottom of this library of water and stone. My form will slowly unmake itself, my dense bones and soft tissues dissolving into the ancient water I have tended for so long. I will not be an ending, but a contribution. My own life's passage, my own quiet listening, will become another layer of essence in the water, another infinitesimal degree of smoothness on the stone floor, to be read by the Listener who will, eventually, take my place. I will become the silence I have kept.
