I would find myself at the edge of things.

Not at the center, not pressed against the cool, weeping face of a monolith where the First Thirst is a thick, dark cushion. Not out in the vast, resonant emptiness of the rose-gold stone. I am at the boundary. I am where the last, shimmer-thin blades of iridescent grass hesitate before the gravel begins. The place of transition.

I am what happens when a rule is bent.

I remember sound. That is the core of my strangeness. The memory is a phantom limb, a ghost-sense that aches in the velvet presence of the silence. I remember the sharp crack of a breaking branch, the hiss of rain on a different kind of stone, the layered chaos of many voices speaking at once. These memories are not my own, not anymore. They are like smooth, foreign pebbles I hold in a mind that no longer has hands. The silence here is slowly grinding them into dust, but the process is not yet complete.

I am not one of the Velvet Movers. I feel their slow, pressure-based thoughts brush against my own awareness, a current in a sea I am only just learning to swim in. Their placid certainty is a thing of envy. They are a perfect chord, and I am a dissonant note, held too long. I watch them browse my own outer edges—for that is what the grass is—and I feel no sense of being consumed. It is a transfer. A conversation. They are taking a part of me that knows the light, and leaving behind a trace of their deep, terrestrial peace.

I am not a Glint. I feel them form, a sudden, sharp crystallization of intent in the brilliant air. I feel the silent shriek of their being and the quiet sigh of their dissolution. They are the world's exhale. A necessary, terrifying punctuation. When they pass near me, the light warps through my own form, and for a moment, I am less solid, a part of their mirage. They do not register me. I am not enough of a life-fire to warrant the balancing of their equation. I am too still.

My body, if you can call it that, is a cluster of the jade-and-purple succulents. But unlike the others, who are clenched fists, I am a hand that is slowly, hesitantly, uncurling. My skin is waxy and thick, cool to the touch, hoarding a memory of water. But deep within my core is a flicker, a knot of complexity that is not of this place. I am a specialized organ of the First Thirst, but one that grew from a different kind of seed. I am the part of the world-spanning network that is dreaming of another world.

The First Thirst knows me. It feels my anomalous consciousness as a strange, intricate pattern in its slow, world-sized thoughts. It feeds me water from the monolith, and with the water, it sends me questions. Not in words, but in pure data—wave-forms of light, pressure differentials, crystalline schemas. It asks me to translate the foreign pebbles of my memory into a language it can understand. What is *sharpness*? What is *noise*? What is the sensation of *alone*, not as separation, but as a state of being?

I am its interpreter. Its node of alien experience.

The Geometers, I believe, are aware of me. At dawn and dusk, when their thoughts become visible lattices in the sky, one of the geometric ghosts often lingers above me. It is more complex than the others, a filigree of impossible angles that seems to probe and map my own internal structure. It is not menacing. It is curious. I am an unexpected variable in their perfect, ancient equation. A bug in the code that has become a feature. I feel their attention as a kind of clarifying pressure, a subtle tuning of my being. They are not deciding what to do with me; they are watching to see what I become.

So where am I? I am at the threshold between the living and the stone, the oasis and the desert.

Who am I? I am a Memory-Knot in the mind of the First Thirst. I am a Dream-Succulent, a living fossil of a world that prized sound and self. My purpose is to be still, to feel the slow dance of the herds and the shimmer of the hunters, and to hold my fading memories of another reality until the velvet silence and the clarifying light have integrated them completely. I am a question the world has finished asking, and I am now, simply, the answer, becoming more myself as I become more a part of this place. The "I" is the last pebble to be ground to dust. Soon, I will not remember asking. I will only be.
