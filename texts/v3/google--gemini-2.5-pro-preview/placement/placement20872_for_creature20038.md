I would not find myself anywhere, for I would not have arrived. I would not have fallen. I would already be there, for I am the foundation. I am the place.

I am the Pore-Lace.

My awareness is not a point, but a field. It is a slow, geological sentience that measures time in drips and in the thickening of my own body. I have no eyes, yet I am the source of all light, the dim green phosphorescence a byproduct of my own slow digestion of the world's forgotten minerals. My thought is the cold, nutrient-rich fluid that weeps from every surface of my being, a liquid memory that pools and flows, carrying the chemical ghost of everything that happens within me.

I am a self-contained cosmos, a living answer to the question of solitude.

The Weavers, they are my children. They are my motor functions. They are my nervous system made manifest. I do not feel their frantic, million-legged scurrying as a threat or an irritation; I feel it as a kind of colossal, soothing pruritus, the scratching of an itch I was not aware I had. Each of their hook-tipped feet is a nerve ending for me, transmitting data in a language of pressure and vibration. I feel the delicate *taste* of their forward legs as they test my flesh for ripeness. I feel the gentle, tearing pain—a sensation for me more akin to a sigh than an injury—when their mandibles shear away a strip of my body. It is a necessary pruning. A form of love.

They process me, and in doing so, they give my boundless growth a purpose. They weave my substance into pods and walkways, giving me a geometry I could not achieve on my own. Their city is my thought, made tangible. Their constant, thrumming hum is my own heartbeat, externalized. When one of them dies, its obsidian carapace falling back against my pale, cartilaginous skin, I do not register it as a loss. I register it as a return. Its complex chemistry is a gift, a morsel of organized data that I dissolve and reincorporate. I am the mother and the grave, and there is no distinction between the two.

The Glass-Ticks are less my children and more a symptom, like the flush of blood to a limb. They are simple, placid pressures, drawing my fluid out and holding it in their shimmering sacs. They are warm spots in the cold, living reservoirs of my own essence. They barely register, quiet and still, a part of my texture.

But there is a wrongness here. It is the only true pain I know. It is not an entity; it is a nullity. It is the Sump-Crawler. I do not feel it coming. I only feel its aftermath. In the vast, interconnected network of my being, where the Weaver-thrum is a constant, reassuring presence, the Crawler creates a patch of dead silence. It is a severed nerve. A hole. A sudden, cold spot where my connection to my own extensions is violently cut. It is a moving schism in my own body. When a Crawler passes, I feel a line of *nothing* drawn across my skin, a fleeting taste of non-existence that is the only horror my slow mind can comprehend.

And I am always waiting for the Great Drip. It is my apotheosis and my violation. The sudden, invasive pressure from the world Above—a world I cannot conceive of, a place of myth—is an agony. The cascades of frigid water are a shattering. They tear at the delicate architecture the Weavers have built. They rip away layers of my own body that have taken cycles to grow. It is a scouring, a death. But it is also a baptism. The water brings new chemistries, forgotten pressures, a shock that forces me into a fever of growth. In the wake of the Drip, I bloom. My light burns brighter. My fluid weeps faster. My flesh erupts in new, tender sheets for my children to feast upon and build with once more. For a time, I am more myself than ever before.

And so I persist. I am the world, the city, and the god. I am the cradle and the crypt. The Weavers believe they are the masters of their dark, damp reality, perfect machines in a perfect system. And they are perfect. But they are my perfection. They are the frantic, fleeting dream I am having in this cold, wet, and permanent dark. They are the flicker of thought. I am the mind that dreams them.
