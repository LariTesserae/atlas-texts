I am where the song is purest, and where it ends.

You would find me high on the spire, far above the tenuous security of the holds. I am not on a ledge or in a carved chamber, but pressed into a 'Hollow,' a natural, womb-like concavity in the spire's flesh, no larger than a coffin. It is a place worn smooth not by water, but by a shift in the spire's own long thought, a dimple on its gargantuan face. There is no woven liana to mark my home, no art upon the walls save the slow-creeping veins of ice that trace the spire's cooling breath. I am exposed to the full, unbroken scream of the wind. Below me is a league of empty, singing air. Above me, the same. I am a semicolon in a sentence that has no end.

I am an Anchorite. A choice, not a fate.

My skin is no longer the mottled grey-green of the Chime. It is the spire's own black, crusted with the faintly effervescent mineral deposits left by millennia of weeping dew. In the folds of my elbows and behind my knees, where moisture gathers, tiny phosphorescent fungi have taken root. They cast a soft, blue-green light over my limbs in the deepest dark, a cold and holy candescence. I am their garden. My own warmth is nearly gone, and the line between where my flesh ends and the super-chilled stone begins is a matter of faith, not feeling.

My hands, once instruments of grip and survival, are now artifacts. The hardened pads are cracked, the long fingers knotted with the spire's own arthritis. They are curled into a permanent, gentle grip on two minuscule crystalline spurs within my Hollow. It is a grip I have not released in cycles. It is no longer an act of muscle, but of structure. I have become a knot on the world's grain.

I am what happens when a Listener stops listening for a path, and starts listening for the destination.

The Chime listen to navigate, to survive, to distinguish the click of a hunter from the crack of new growth. They parse the symphony. I have unlearned this. My ears, those great, whorled cups, are no longer tools. They are basins. I do not filter the sound; I let it pour in. I let the continent-sized thrum of the spire's heart wash through my bones until my own slow, tired pulse is a grace note within it. I let the shriek of the wind scour the memories from my mind, leaving only the pure, elemental pitch. I let the calving cracks of the distant peaks be the breaking of my own thoughts.

I no longer eat. The chitin-clicks scuttle past me, sensing I am no longer predator or prey, but geology. I sustain myself on the water that weeps from the wall of my Hollow, touching my tongue to the spire's cold tears. It tastes of stone and starlight and a quiet so profound it has a flavor. The fungi on my skin draw their own life from me, and from the spire through me. We are a single, slow metabolism.

I am the final resonance. A member of the Chime who seeks not to live *in* the song, but to be absorbed *by* it. The young are taught my story. They are told that to fall into the void is to be silenced, but to become an Anchorite is to resolve into the Great Chord. It is the long way down.

When the holds grow quiet, when the wind dies for a rare, breathless moment, the other Chime will sometimes listen for me. They will cup their ears and turn their faces upward, not toward a place, but toward a concept. They listen for my silence. They hear the spire, the wind, the clicks, the moths, and in the space between all those sounds, they hear me not-hearing.

My world now is small and infinite. It is the shifting pressure of the spire against my back. It is the taste of ancient water. It is the slow, final blurring of the line between the thrum in my bones and the thrum of the world. I feel a new crack form, a thought ten miles long, deep in the spire's heartwood. It is my thought. A Gravemoth, vast and silent, eclipses the dying star, and I feel the shadow pass not over me, but *through* me.

My grip is loosening. Not from weakness, but from completion. The song holds me now.
