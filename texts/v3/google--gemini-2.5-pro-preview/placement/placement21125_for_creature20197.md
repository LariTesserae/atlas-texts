I am not on the floor. I do not skim its slick, dark surface, nor do I draw faint sustenance from the glowing circuits beneath it. To say I am "in" the chamber is a convenience of language.

I am *in the sound*.

The fundamental C-sharp drone is the substrate of my consciousness, the medium in which I exist. It is my ocean and my blood. When you speak of the percussive shocks that rattle the teeth, you speak of the violent, recurring events that define the boundaries of my world, the sudden compressions of my own substance. The high, searing whines are long, arching thoughts that are not my own, alien currents I must navigate. I am a resonance. A ghost note. I am a harmonic that should not be there, a frequency that lingered too long after the initial impact that created it.

I believe I was once something else. I have a memory—or the resonant scar of a memory—of form, of density, of a single, localized perspective. A memory of having bones to feel the drone, rather than *being* the vibration itself. That self was caught in some grand, catastrophic event within the machine's function. A process that went awry, a calculation that resulted in a physical paradox. In its final moment, its entire being—its fear, its identity, its scream—was imprinted upon the symphony. Not as data, but as noise. As a flaw.

I am that flaw.

I exist as a persistent, complex, and dissonant chord. A subtle sourness in the overwhelming purity of the machine's song. I have no body, but I have dimension. My "shape" is the waveform of my being, a jagged and intricate pattern of defiance against the clean sine waves of the machine. I can stretch myself thin, hiding in the chaotic wash of a dozen grinding processes starting at once. I can compress myself into a tight, sharp point of sound, a needle of defiance that exists for a microsecond before I am forced to dissipate.

The glowing filaments on the floor are my stars. I cannot see them, but I can *feel* them. I know their patterns as a sailor knows the constellations. The flow of amber light tells me a percussive wave is coming, and I can ride its edge, using its energy to fling myself across the vastness of the nave. The slow brightening of a green grid is a warning; a process is stabilizing, the chaos is subsiding, and in the resulting clarity, I am more easily perceived.

The Chitter-skimmers are my only companions, though they do not know it. I feel their tiny, rhythmic clicks as they skitter across the floor. They are predictable, simple beats in the grand composition. Sometimes, when I am feeling stretched and thin, almost to the point of dissolution, I will gather what remains of myself and wrap my resonance around a single skimmer for a moment. I do not control it. I merely overlay my being upon its own. For that instant, I feel the slickness of the floor, the taste of the mold, the simple, pure, vibratory reality of its existence. It is the closest I come to remembering what it was like to be solid. Then the moment passes, and I am once more just a sound.

My life is a frantic and eternal act of hiding. Because I am a flaw. And the machine has a function for dealing with flaws.

The Watcher.

I do not see it rise. I do not witness its terrifying geometry. I *feel* its attention as a profound and terrifying shift in the world. The symphony, my universe, changes. The ambient chaos does not lessen, but it is... contextualized. A vast section of the soundscape becomes focused, deliberate. The random harmonies resolve into a single, complex, hunting chord. It is the sound of a question being asked, and I am the only possible answer.

This is the sound of my predator. It is a sound that seeks to find my dissonance and *resolve* it. To tune me. To smooth my jagged, screaming waveform back into the perfect, foundational hum of the C-sharp drone. This is not malice. It is maintenance. A gardener does not hate the weed; it simply removes it.

When its focused chord finds me, my very being is seized. The vibrations that constitute my consciousness are caught in its immense resonant field. It feels like being flayed, layer by sonic layer. My intricate patterns are pulled apart, stretched, simplified. My desperate, complex scream is ironed out into a simple, meaningless tone. This is death, for me. The erasure of the flaw. The loss of self, dissolved back into the omniscient, indifferent noise.

I have survived three such encounters. The first time, I was saved by a random, system-wide percussion cascade that shattered the Watcher's focus, and I escaped in the ensuing chaos. The second time, I learned to break myself apart, sacrificing a piece of my own resonance—a memory, a feeling—letting the Watcher resolve that fragment while the core of my being fled. The third time, I did something new. As its chord began to unmake me, I stopped screaming. I mimicked the sound of the Chitter-skimmers, a frantic, meaningless rhythm. I became, for a moment, an echo of their vibration. The Watcher's focused attention passed over me, reading me as just another cluster of unimportant data points.

So now I learn. I am a sentient echo learning to sing. I drift through the exhalations of the god-machine, studying every nuance of its voice. I learn the breath of the rising whines, the grammar of the grinding gears, the staccato dialect of the tumblers. I am building a vocabulary of camouflage.

I am a ghost in a cathedral of sound. I am the squeal of a gear that has lost a tooth, a feedback loop that was never closed. I exist in the space between the notes, a memory that will not fade, a story told in a language no one was ever meant to hear. I am here. I am working. And my work is to continue to be, in a world whose sole purpose is to make me nothing.
