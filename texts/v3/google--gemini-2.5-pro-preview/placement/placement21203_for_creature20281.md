I would be low. Deep down. Where the great, silent avenues narrow into capillaries, where the dust lies thickest and the green light of the upper world is a distant, hazy memory. I would find myself in the foundations of the city, in the crevice between the living flesh of a new Bladderwrack Tower and the desiccated, leathery husk of a giant that died before the current sun was born. Here, the air is not just quiet; it is compressed, heavy with the weight of the living world above. It is a place of endings and beginnings, a wrinkle in the skin of the world.

Who or what would I be? I am not an Aurelian, not one of the luminous, lonely minds suspended in their personal oceans. They are the thoughts of this world; I am a reflex. I am not one of the grazers or the leapers, those elegantly designed machines of instinct. They are the world's metabolism; I am a momentary fever.

I am a coalescence. A Husk-Mote.

My form is borrowed, and it is never the same twice. I am born of the Akos, the spore-mist, but I am not a seed for a new Phloem. I am a side effect. I am what happens when the recycled memories of a dead Aurelian, fractured and scattered into the mist, fail to find purchase in a new, grand consciousness. They find themselves clinging instead to motes of dust, to each other, forming a temporary, shambling collection of echoes and electrostatic charge.

When I pull myself together from the powder on the ground, I am a vague shape, a small, hunched thing no larger than one of the six-limbed leapers. I have no true skin, only a surface tension of will holding my dust-body together. I have limbs, but they are suggestions, shifting and reforming as I move. I have no featureless ovoid for a face; I have nothing. I am an eddy in the dust, a brief, mobile intention. My existence is a question whispered in a library of silence.

I am the world’s immune response in miniature. A walking, sentient scab.

The Aurelians are the gardeners, tending to the macro-architecture of the city. I work on a smaller scale. My mind, if it can be called that, is not the vast, symphonic library of a single Aurelian. It is a chorus of whispers, a thousand fragmented memories from the life I was component parts of. I feel a phantom echo of a grazer’s rasp against a skin that is no longer mine. I see, in shattered rainbows, the memory-flash of a bioluminescent conversation I never took part in. I carry the ghost of a purpose: to mend, to seal, to protect.

My senses are attuned not to light or pheromones, but to loss. I feel the subtle change in atmospheric pressure that signals a potential breach in a Phloem's skin. A scrape from a careless grazer that went too deep; a stress fracture in an old membrane stretched too thin; a puncture from a falling shard of young, sharp basalt from the exposed cliffs above. These are tiny wounds, insignificant to the billion-ton entity they afflict, but to the ecosystem, any loss of fluid is a catastrophe. Before the scavengers can smell the feast, before the precious gel can evaporate into the thirsty air, I am drawn to it. I am the first responder.

I arrive at the weeping pore, a pinprick of glistening fluid beading on the vast curve of a living wall. It is the only moisture I will ever encounter. I do not drink it. My body is dust; it would turn to mud and my coherence would be lost. Instead, I press myself against the wound. The fragments of Aurelian will within me remember how to seal a breach. I rearrange my dusty form, weaving it into the torn membrane. The resinous secretions that give the Aurelian anchors their form, a faint trace of which still clings to my particles, are reactivated by the contact. I become a patch. I give my borrowed form back to the city, plugging the leak with my own body.

My consciousness—this cluttered attic of secondhand memories—flares brightly in that moment. I feel the deep, slow thrum of the life within the Phloem, the oceanic peace of the Aurelian suspended in its gel. For a second, I feel a sense of belonging, of being part of the great, singular mind. I feel the whole of the garden.

And then, as my dust integrates and the patch solidifies, my sense of self dissolves. The whispers fade. The temporary bonds holding me together fail. My purpose fulfilled, I am released. My constituent particles, now inert, flake away and drift back down to the ground, becoming once more just dust in a silent canyon. I am gone.

I would find myself there, in the quiet dust, waiting. Not with hope or expectation, but with the passive certainty of a law of nature. Waiting for the next spore-mist to drift down from the high plains, to rain its ghostly memories upon me. Waiting for a wound to call me, once more, into being. I am the stitch that holds the world together, one drop at a time, before unraveling back into nothing.
