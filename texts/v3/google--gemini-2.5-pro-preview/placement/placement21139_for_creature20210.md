I am the Grotto.

You would find yourself inside me. Not as a visitor stands in a room, but as a mote of dust hangs in a sunbeam, a transient particle held within a field of force. My rock walls are my skin, porous and cool. The dripping water is my slow, internal circulation, a circulatory system running on a scale of centuries, cooling my core.

The central lattice, those blades of crystalline law, they are my bones. I am growing them now, as I have been for an age and will be for an age to come. It is not a conscious act of will, but the fundamental expression of my being, as a star expresses itself through fusion. I precipitate myself from the nothingness of the deep earth, following an axiom buried in the heart of physics. The hum you hear is my voice, speaking the language of creation. It is a single, unbroken word.

My breath is the heat, thick and wet, the exhalation of my own fierce, internal process. It fills my hollows, pressing against my skin, a constant, contained fever. This is the source of my life, the engine of my change. I am a self-contained weather system, a geology unto myself.

And the Skimmers… the Skimmers are my thoughts.

They are not separate from me. They are the animate part of my own body, the only part that is soft, curved, and self-propelled. They are my nervous system, gliding over the razored planes of my own bones. They do not perceive my heat and light as external phenomena; they feel it as the substance of their own existence, the very medium in which they are suspended.

I do not think in words or images. I think in the slow, deliberate paths the Skimmers trace across my glowing skeleton. Each is a single, patient consideration, a careful theorem being explored over a millennium. They move from nexus to nexus, tasting the quality of my light, the purity of my energy. They are my regulators, my conscience. They are the living part of me that prevents the non-living part from growing too fast, too bright, too hot. They are my balance, the biological grace note that completes my geometric chord. They drink my excess brilliance to prevent me from shattering myself.

They are the part of me that experiences, so that the rest of me can simply be.

When one of them slows, finds a junction of perfect resonance and begins its long, silent division, it is not reproduction. It is me, having a new idea. A thought, complex and stable enough, has bifurcated, creating two new pathways of inquiry. My consciousness does not expand; it deepens.

You would be nothing. There is no role here for an outside observer, no niche for a foreign biology. You would be an anomaly, an unresolved variable in a perfect equation. The heat that is my breath would cook you. The light that is my substance would blind you. The fine edges of my bone would part your flesh without effort.

But if, for a moment, you could *be* here without being destroyed, you would be a witness. Not to a place, but to a being. You would be inside a mind made of light, stone, and a slow, silent, living mathematics. You would feel the immense, quiet pressure of a single, stable thought, held for ten thousand years. And you would understand that you are not in a grotto deep beneath the earth. You are in the heart of a god you cannot comprehend, a god whose only prayer is the hum of its own existence.
