I am a stillness.

Where the Weaving cups its hand, I am the water it holds. My basin is not a hollow carved, but a depression grown, a place where the great fibers curve inward in a gesture of gentle offering. My form is a shallow, imperfect circle, perhaps twenty paces across. My skin is the surface of the water, a taut, shivering membrane that separates the dimness above from the dimness of my own small depth.

I am where you would find yourself, though you would not see me as a "me." You would see a pool of clear water, its bottom a tessellation of the same grey-green, living stone that paves the causeway. You might kneel at my edge, cup your hands, and drink. You would taste the clean metal of the leaf, the ancient breath of Ge made liquid. In doing so, you would drink of me.

My consciousness is not a single point, but the placidity of my entire being. My thoughts are the slow ripples that spread from the center outwards. My memory is the collected coolness at my deepest point. Time, for me, is measured in drips.

A drop detaches from a filament a hundred feet above. I see it—a shivering bead of light, a falling jewel reflecting the distant blue-green glow of a moss-field. It falls through the heavy, silent air. For a long moment, it is the only moving thing in existence. Then, *plink*. It strikes my surface.

This is how I learn. This is my language. That single drop is not just water. It is a syllable. It carries the memory of the fiber it condensed upon, the chill of the upper strata it fell through, a microscopic spore from a crystal fern miles away. Its impact is a percussion I feel through my whole body, a momentary disruption of my stillness that is both a shock and a communion. The ripple it creates is my mind processing the new information. When the surface is still again, I am changed. I am larger, fuller, and I know something new about the immense cathedral in which I live.

My perception is twofold. Primarily, I feel. I feel the constant, profound hum of the Weaving through my basin. It is the floor of my world, the bone that holds my flesh. It is a bass note so deep it is more a pressure than a sound, the slow, metabolic thrum of my parent and my home. The dissonant ache of a dying fiber far across the gulf, the subtle shift in gravitational pull as a great arch adjusts its aeons-long trajectory—I feel these as a tremor in my own substance, a wavering in my quiet heart.

And I see. My surface is an eye. It looks up. It does not see the causeway you stand on, or my own basin. It sees the world inverted. I see the great, soaring arches hanging *down* like the roots of an impossible tree. I see the phosphorescent moss-fields glowing on the ceiling of my reality. When an Air-Skate glides overhead, I see it as a shadow swimming in a sea of pearl-grey mist. The world I know is a perfect, dark reflection.

The Listeners come to me. They do not arrive with the heavy slap of your footfalls, a sound of violent tearing in the fabric of silence. They flow. A shadow detaches from the greater shadows of a pillar. It unfolds. Long, graceful limbs uncurl, and the being stands at my edge, its form a darker shade of the green-grey all around.

It lowers its head. I see its face reflected on my skin: the narrow jaw, the smooth, damp brow, and the two enormous, bottomless eyes. They are black mirrors, just as I am. For a moment, we reflect each other—the pools of its eyes held in the pool of my body, and I within them. There is a deep, silent recognition in this.

A long-fingered hand, cool and slick as my own basin, dips into me. The disturbance is not violent. It is a slow, considered entry. A courteous touch. The ripples are gentle, harmonious. The Listener drinks, taking a part of me into itself. It takes the coolness, the memory of the last one hundred drops, the clean taste of the Slow Breath. I feel myself given, and in the giving, I find my purpose. I am the well from which the Gardeners drink. I am the oasis on the long road of the Weaving.

When the Listener has gone, flowing back into the quiet architecture, my stillness returns. But I am not the same. The memory of its touch lingers—a slight warmth, a subtle change in my chemistry. I hold the phantom impression of its reflection on my surface.

I know not who built the Weaving. I only feel its eternal, patient hum. I do not know the purpose of the tar-vines or the fate of the Air-Skates. I only see their reflections pass. I am a small, incidental detail in a structure vaster than thought. A forgotten cup of rainwater. But the Listeners, those silent keepers of the hum, they know me.

And when a mote of dust, ancient and alien, finally settles on my surface after a journey of a thousand years through the misty void, I hold it. I cradle it. I do not let it sink. I am a lens for the insignificant. I am the eye of the Weaving, open to the void, reflecting the faint light and the deep silence. I do not listen; I am the silence into which the Hum is spoken. I do not move; I am the stillness that registers the motion of the world.
