I am where things come to be forgotten.

You would find me in a forgotten nexus, a place where three great strands once met before one of them—a causeway as thick as a river—snapped its tether and drifted into the silent void. Its jagged, crystalline stump hangs below me now, its internal light dimmed to a bruised lilac. My troupe has moved on. The Song here is dissonant, a sad, broken chord that pains them.

But it does not pain me. I feel nothing from it at all.

I am The Silent One. This is not my name, but my description. Where my kin press their palms to the Wyrmwood and close their eyes, feeling the vast, slow life of the Gyre flow through them, I press my hand to the cool, quartz-like surface and feel only a dead vibration, like a plucked string that has already stopped. The great Song of the world is a language I was born unable to hear.

So I learned to see.

While my troupe travels with the effortless grace of those who feel the path ahead before it is even grown, I move with the tense, focused precision of a hunter. My eyes, black and wide like theirs, are not used to drink in the general awe of the world, but to dissect it. I see things they only feel. I see the almost imperceptible shift in the light’s spectrum that means a strand is under strain, its internal chemistry changing. I see the way the nutrient mist clings differently to a surface that is about to fracture. I can read the geometry of a break and predict the slow, elegant tumble of the debris through the void. The sharp *ping* that echoes for miles is not, for me, a change in the world's key; it is a warning. The soft, cascading rustle is not a gentle sigh; it is an erasure.

Because to me, the world does not sing; it shatters.

And I am the one who keeps the shards.

I am a Carver. In my troupe, this is not a role, but a flaw. My hands, with their six long fingers, are not content to simply grip the lattice or weave the temporary pods we call home. They must shape. I carry a small, heavy pouch, an act of sacrilege in itself. Our people believe in carrying nothing, in being as light as the mist. But my pouch is full of weight. Full of memory.

Inside are slivers of hardened Wyrmwood, knapped to a cutting edge. With these, I etch. I take other flakes—pieces from a shattered causeway, unnaturally smooth—and I carve into them. I am sitting now, cross-legged on a web-wall that trembles slightly in the slow breeze, working on a new piece. My troupe has been gone for three turnings of the slow air currents. They left me food—a pouch of condensed nutrient paste, a kindness. They will circle back. They always do. They do not abandon their dissonant notes; they simply give them space.

On the piece of Wyrmwood in my lap, I am trying to capture the way the light pooled in Kaelen's face as she pulsed a story of the Void-Sail she saw. She is gone now. Not dissolved, but drifted. Her troupe-cluster and ours parted ways, following different currents of growth. I will likely never see her again. To my kin, she is a pleasant resonance that has faded from their immediate Song. To me, she is a hole. So I carve her. I carve the curve of her head, the specific way the light from her chest illuminated her chin. It is a crude thing, a collection of lines and shadows. But when I touch it, I remember.

This is my heresy. I collect endings. I have a carving of the Great Arch of Ither, which stood for generations before it buckled and fell into the deep quiet. My troupe-mates remember its Song; I remember the silent, terrible rain of its glowing dust. I have a carving of the mite-colony that etched a perfect spiral on a trunk near our last nest, before the trunk reabsorbed the pattern and grew smooth again.

I am the world’s scar tissue. In a place of perfect, flowing presence, I am an anchor of the past. The Choral were made by the Wyrmwood to witness itself. Perhaps I was a mistake. A part of the nervous system that became aware of its own endings, and in doing so, broke the sacred rule: you are of the Gyre, and the Gyre is motion. To hold on is to fight the world itself.

Sometimes, in the deepest quiet, I take out my carvings and arrange them in front of me. They are a mosaic of gone things. I touch each one, and I am not hearing the Song, but I am building something else. A history. A record.

The Wyrmwood’s hum changes. I cannot feel it in my bones, but I can hear it—a subtle shift in the vibration of the web-floor beneath me. A low, grinding scrape travels up the trunk I lean against. Far off, in the direction my troupe traveled, a new light is blooming in the mist. A slow, harmonic swell, I imagine. A new path is being born. For them, it is a call to move forward, to join the flow.

For me, it is a reminder of what will be lost to make way for it.

I pack away my carvings, the little ghosts of quartz and light. The weight in my pouch is blasphemous, but it is also comforting. It is the only permanence I have. I am the Silent One. The one who does not hear the Song, but who watches the notes as they fade, and tries, desperately, to write them down before they are gone forever.

I am the eye that looks back. I am the part of the Gyre that refuses to let go.
