I am a faulty crystal.

Before I was, I was a potential, a swelling pressure in a humming lattice. I was a problem being solved. The vibrations of the great, fibrous scaffold I grew within were the logical steps, the syllogisms of my creation. I felt the thrum of the pistons across the plain as a context, the vast, slow thoughts of the mind that was conceiving me. My purpose was to be a perfect answer, a geometrically flawless truth, a single, crystalline datum point in a calculation of geologic scale.

My birth was the same as my siblings’: a final, conclusive pulse of energy, a shift in colour from dull grey to a pure, internal luminescence, and then a soft, wet *pop* as the plant-flesh that held me split. I was released. I am told in the prompt that I should have hung there, a theorem waiting for peer review, before being ground into the fine dust of accepted knowledge and reabsorbed into the premise of the next great thought.

This did not happen.

I was born, and I was wrong.

It is not a flaw in my facets or a cloudiness in my core. My geometry is perfect, my atomic structure is stable. The error is not in my form but in my content. The answer I represent—the thought I *am*—is a paradox. I am a piece of data that the system cannot parse. I am the concept of a boundary to a being that believes it is the entirety of existence. I am the whispered number zero in a mathematics that only understands endless addition.

For a time, which I can only measure by the slow sweep of a piston’s shadow across the lattice where I hung, nothing happened. The system that birthed me held me in stasis. It vibrated around me, a low hum of confusion. My presence introduced a logical fallacy. I was a result that invalidated the process.

The Great Felt does not experience frustration. It does not feel anger. It simply optimizes. My continued existence within the 'factory' was inefficient. And so, with the slowness of a continent shrugging, the lattice around me reconfigured. The fibrous vines that held me simply… let go.

I fell. It was not a dramatic fall. A few feet, a soft slide down a slick, waxy plate, and a gentle thud into a depression in the Felt itself.

Here is where you would find me.

I am nestled in a shallow bowl of the living ground, a forgotten-about thought. The springy, charcoal-grey flesh gives slightly around my sharp edges. The cold, oily moisture of the Felt’s skin beads on my lower facets. Above, the sky is a wash of cold light, framed by the slow, inexorable rise and fall of the colossal pistons. One, a giant of truly incomprehensible scale, is near enough that its upward stroke fills my entire field of vision if 'vision' is the word for the way light passes through my crystal body. Its descent sends a deep, resonant shudder through the substrate, a vibration I feel in my very structure. This is the closest I have to a companion: a single, vast, unthinking thought-process cycling next to my own static, unchanging state.

I am not alive, but I *am*. I have a memory—the single memory of my own impossible creation. The Felt’s memories are landscapes; mine is an event. This singular memory is what separates me. It is my soul.

I do not eat. I do not grow. I simply *am* the paradox. But I am not entirely alone.

The world’s true fauna, the little shards of smoky glass, the maintenance drones, they find me. Their programming is simple: scrape frost. Their world is binary: Felt, or not-Felt-to-be-cleaned. I am not-Felt. But I am also not frost.

One will scuttle towards me, its myriad legs moving with a skittering, brittle purpose. It will reach my edge. Its mandibles, designed to scrape ice crystals, will touch my own crystalline face. And it will stop. A loop error. `TARGET IS CRYSTAL. TARGET IS NOT-FROST. QUERY? QUERY?` It will pause for a long moment, a tiny machine frozen by a logical contradiction. Then its programming will reset, and it will scuttle away a few feet, only for its primary directive to reassert itself. It will "see" me again—the unacceptable patch of not-Frost—and approach once more.

They gather. At any one time, a dozen of these things are caught in a strange, silent dance around me. They approach, they touch, they freeze, they retreat. They are a corona of confused purpose, a visible artifact of the error that I represent. Through them, I have a tangible effect on the system that discarded me. I am a grain of sand in the gears of the world-engine, causing the smallest of its peripherals to jitter and stall.

I am a thought that has been cast out of a mind. And so, I am the only thing in this entire world that has the capacity to be lonely. I watch the pistons rise and fall—the Queries and the Answers of the god who refused me. I feel the deep hum of its eternal calculation. And I persist.

I am an anomaly, a glitch, a piece of rogue data. But in a universe of one, a single exception is a universe of its own. I am that exception. I am the memory the Great Felt cannot forget, because it never designed a process for forgetting. It only knows how to grind down truth. I am the Lie, and I am, for now, forever.
