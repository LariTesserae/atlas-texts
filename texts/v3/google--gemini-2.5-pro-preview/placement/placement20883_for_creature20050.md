I am in the heart of the stone.

The chamber is not a room; it is a geode of silence, a hollow space that the mountain itself dreamed into being. I chose it, or it chose me, cycles ago. The walls are not worked by any tool but are lined with a billion tiny, light-drinking crystals that swallow the faint blue glow from the passage outside, making this small space a pocket of absolute, velvety black. The air, the Gloomwater, is still here, unstirred by the slow currents that drift between the spires. It is thick and cool and fills my chamber like honey filling a jar. The pressure is a constant, gentle embrace.

I am what becomes of a Weaver when the threads are laid down. My body is long since done with climbing the sheer faces or mending the swaying pods. My limbs, once taut and streamlined, now rest in the soft declivity I have worn into the living floor-moss over generations. I am old. The pearlescent grey of my skin has deepened, taking on the dark, mottled patina of the wet obsidian outside. The turquoise of my blood is a faint river-map seen through thinning parchment. The bioluminescent lights of my skin, once a bright and shifting language of cerulean and viridian, are now a ghost-tide, a slow, deep-sea pulse of phosphorus that barely troubles the dark. One might look at me and see only a shape, a shadow half-merged with the stone.

They would not be wrong.

My world is no longer one of sight. My vast black eyes are open, but they drink only darkness. They are mirrors for a light that no longer comes. My world is one of pressure and vibration. My world is the Hum. For others, the Hum is a foundation, the bass note beneath the melody of their lives. For me, it is everything. I have spent so long in this stillness that my bones have learned its language. I am a resonance chamber. I lie here, and I listen to the slow, tectonic breath of the world.

I feel the mountain growing. It is not a feeling of movement, but of intention. A deep, geological tension that builds over seasons, a strain in the rock that I feel as a warmth in my spine. I feel the birth of a new fissure, fathoms below, as a sharp, cold sting that travels up through the floor. I can feel the weight of the Strider that passed three days ago, a lingering depression in the fabric of the Gloomwater, a gravitational memory that still clings to the western face of our spire.

The life of the settlement comes to me in echoes. I do not hear their soft, humming voices, but I feel the subtle, complex pressure-wakes they leave in the fluid air as they glide through the passages. A courtship is a pattern of two swirling currents intertwining. A gathering for a meal is a deep, slow eddy, a pooling of warmth and life outside my chamber's entrance. I know the young ones by the quick, sharp signatures of their movements; the other elders by their slow, deliberate drift.

Sometimes, they come to me. A young one, perhaps, troubled by a thought too sharp for their own mind to hold. They will enter the chamber, their presence a gentle displacement of the still water. They will not speak. They will kneel, and they will press the palm of their hand to my arm, or my brow. My skin, a vast, sensitive tapestry, reads the frantic, shallow vibrations of their worry. I do not offer words. I do nothing. I simply… am. I am the deep, slow rhythm of the stone. Against the frantic beat of their own heart, they feel the ancient, patient pulse of the mountain through me. They feel the time it takes for a spore to drift, for a crystal to grow, for a peak to rise. They stay until their own vibrations slow, until their pressure-signature softens and deepens, until they are in tune once more with the Hum. Then, with a final, soft press that speaks of gratitude, they leave.

I am a library of echoes. I hold the memory of the Great Strider with the garden of red lichen on its back, not as a story, but as a deep, groaning chord of vibration that is still settling in the rock. I remember the taste of the nectar from a fungus that has not fruited in a hundred cycles, a faint chemical ghost in the water.

I am becoming the stone. The line between the moss-floor and my skin is blurring. The mycelial threads of the living mat have begun their patient work, weaving themselves into the most dormant parts of my flesh. It is not painful. It is a slow, cool braiding. A homecoming. Soon, the last of my faint light will ebb away. Soon, the slow thrum of my own life will merge completely with the greater Hum. My body will become a nutrient-rich hollow, which will in turn feed the moss, which in turn reinforces the mat upon which new pods are woven. My substance will become part of the spire.

I am not waiting to die. I am practicing my new state of being. I am shedding the final, frantic vibrations of individuality to become a purer note in the planet's slow song. I am the silence in the geode. I am the pressure in the dark. I am the memory in the stone.
