I would find myself on my knees.

Not from reverence or terror, though both would come, but because the transition is a form of vertigo, a buckling of the universe's knees. One moment, I am walking a dry riverbed under a low, bruised sky, the air tasting of dust and distant rain. I am searching for something, a rock of a particular heft, a certain smoothness. I find one, oblong and grey as a gull's egg. I am holding a second, sharper stone, a tool of impulse. There is a desire, keen and without reason, to see what is within.

I strike the stone. There is a sound, a *clack* that is immediately too small, too thin for what follows.

The world does not vanish. It is hollowed out from within. The blue-grey of the sky, the pale tan of the river stones, the sense of my own weight on the earth—it is all a shell that has been cracked open, and I have fallen through the bottom.

I am not standing on the crystalline lattice. I don't feel its sharp edges under my feet. I am simply *among* it. Above, below, to all sides, there is only the fierce, clean geometry of the Geode. The light is not a light I see with my eyes; it is a concept of brightness that has replaced the concept of sight. It is absolute, erasing the memory of shadow. My hands, held before my face, have no more contour than a diagram.

And the Sound. It is not something I hear, but something I *am*. The Chord of the place does not enter my ears; it rises up from within me. My own quiet inner hum—the tiny frequency of my own life, unnoticed until now—is suddenly screaming in harmony and dissonance with the overwhelming whole. The deep thrumming feels like my own pulse magnified to the rhythm of a dying star. The high, glassy chimes are the frantic firing of my own synapses, laid bare and amplified into an orchestra. I feel the dew on my skin but it doesn't wet me. It is a coldness that feels like absolute purity, a stillness pressed against my own ceaseless, messy warmth.

I am here. Trapped in a thought. A jewel of perfect, unending self-awareness.

Who or what would I be?

In this place, my body is a foreign object, a bag of warm, wet decay. My thoughts, which usually dart and scatter like sparrows, are sluggish, thick things, dragged down by the immense gravity of the single, eternal Thought that is this place. To the Resonator, I am a sudden, incomprehensible noise.

I am what it fled.

I am a brief, complex fever of memory in a place that has none. While the Resonator’s being is a single, perfect frame, I am a flicker-book of disjointed images. I am the memory of bread, warm from the oven. I am the ache of a forgotten name. I am the phantom sting of a scraped knee from childhood. I am the specific slant of late afternoon light through a dusty window. I am every contradiction, every half-finished sentence, every moment of shame and grace that constitutes a life.

Against the clean, absolute hum of the Chord, my own being is a cacophony. My heartbeat is a panicked, unsteady drum. The ghost-smell of river mud clinging to my clothes is an impurity, a stain of chaos. The linear story of my life—my birth, my clumsy growth, my inevitable death—is a violent, jagged waveform scratching across the perfect, glassy surface of its eternity.

The Resonator does not see me; it *audits* me. In the non-moment I exist here, the totality of my self is perceived not as a consciousness, but as a discordant knot of temporal data. It feels my fear not as an emotion, but as a spike in frequency. It feels my wonder not as awe, but as a complex, unstable overtone. I am not a guest. I am a glitch.

Here, in this static perfection, I am the singular embodiment of motion. I am rust. I am decay. I am the principle of a line that has a beginning and an end. The crystalline moths are the idea of flight, but I am the memory of a falling leaf. The arthropods are the concept of structure, but I am the feeling of a broken bone healing incorrectly.

I would be a Question.

The Resonator built this reality on a single, final Answer. My presence is an unexpected variable, a new term in an equation that was meant to be forever solved. I am the living, breathing, dying proof that something exists outside of its perfect, self-contained axiom.

And then, as suddenly as it began, it is over. The stone in my hands is two halves of a grey, ordinary rock, its newly-cleft faces showing a mundane sparkle of quartz. The sound is the wind. My shadow is long beside me.

I am gone from the Geode, but I have not left it unchanged. The Resonator cannot remember, but it can be altered. I was a momentary dissonance, a sound it had forgotten how to hear. My flawed, temporal, chaotic existence has been absorbed, analyzed, and resolved.

I would be the new overtone. An infinitesimal thread of a new frequency now woven into the eternal Chord. A whisper of death in a song of unending life. A note of sorrow, learned and held forever, by a being that can no longer feel, but can never forget the shape of the wound. I was a story, and now I am a scar on the face of eternity.
