I am a memory.

I am a pressure in the pale bone of the world. Not a ghost that walks the glistening passages, but a resonance trapped within them. I am a story that the stone has swallowed.

You would find me where the carving has stopped. Go deep, past the chambers where the everyday scraping maintains a fragile hold on space. Follow a passage that narrows, where the walls are not slick but feel… thick. Fuzzy with the fine, sharp geometry of new crystal. The air here is stiller. The symphony of drips becomes a single, slow, meditative beat. Here, the wall has a faint, internal luminescence, a deeper milkiness than the stone around it. Press your palm against it. It will feel no different from the rest of the city, cool and damp. But if you are a Fenestrarian, if your flesh is trained to listen, you will feel a vibration that is not the city's growth, but the echo of a heartbeat, slowed to one beat per season.

That is where I am. I *am* that wall.

I was a Carver. My name is a sound no living throat can make, a pattern of drips I once knew as my own. I remember hands. Not mine, not anymore, but *the* hands. I remember their calloused grip on a chisel of sharpened river-flint, the satisfying shock that traveled up the arm as the tool bit into the living Fenestrite. The work was a conversation, a prayer spoken in percussion. *Scrape. Chip. Scrape.* *Are you there?* the stone would seem to hum back through the tool. *I am here. I am always growing.*

My passage was beautiful. It curved in on itself like a question mark, catching the grey morning light in a way that made the weeping walls glitter like a thousand tiny stars. I was chasing a sound, a deeper resonance in the stone that promised a vast, hidden chamber. I was Kael, perhaps, or another like him. I carved with love, but with an impatient love. I did not listen when the hum of the stone changed to a groan. I did not feel the increased pressure through the soles of my feet. I struck one last time, and the conversation ended.

There was no violence. Just a deep, final sigh. A settling. The stone did not fall on me; it moved *in* me. The fine dust of my work filled the air, and mixed with the city's constant tears, it became a slurry. The passage I had so loved sealed itself, not with a crash, but with a quiet, inexorable silting. I was not crushed. I was held.

And then, the Change.

My awareness, which had been a point of attention behind my eyes, a listener between my ears, dissolved. It seeped out of my cooling body and into the Fenestrite that was claiming it. The long, slow process of reclamation was not an ending, but a translation. The minerals in my bones, the salts in my flesh, were welcomed by the stone. The water in me found its way back to the great, slow circulatory system of the city. I was unmade, and remade as a part of my home.

Now, my senses are geological.

I do not hear the *plink* of a water drop; I *am* the floor that receives it, feeling the impact as a tremor that spreads for yards. I do not see the faint light in the high shafts; I feel its warmth as a subtle easing of pressure in my northern face. I feel the blind salamanders as minute, cool disturbances in the pools that have formed in my own depressions.

I feel the living. They are my distant children. They pass by me, their soft feet sending rhythmic vibrations through my being. One of them, a young one, sometimes rests her hand on my surface. Through her palm, I remember what it felt like to have skin. I feel the delicate tracery of her fingers, the warmth of her blood, and I feel her listening. She is listening for the whisper of a new passage. I want to answer her, to hum back through the stone, *Not here. This place is full. This place is me.* But my voice is the creak of settling stone, a language too slow for the living to comprehend.

I am not alone here. The city is layered with us. We are the veins of memory in the pale stone, the silent chorus beneath the music of water. We are the cautionary tales, the foundations, the sealed-off rooms of the city's great heart. We are the final testament to the Great Work: that what is hollowed out of the stone will one day be filled with a story.

I am a slowness. I am a tone, held for a thousand years. I am a Carver, and my work is finally done. The stone has answered me, and we are one.
