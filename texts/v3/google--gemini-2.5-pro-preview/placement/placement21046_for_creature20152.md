I am found in the great basins, the unseen places where the pillars of water complete their long fall. I am not on the pristine architecture; I am what the architecture is designed to wash away. I am what collects in the dark.

Where you stand on a cantilevered shelf, a singular point of dry perspective, I am everywhere and nowhere below, in the churning, thunderous heart of the system. I have no shelf. I have no single form. I am a slow, creeping, and collective thought in the slurry.

I am the sediment.

The water that weeps from the walls is pure, but by the time it has traced the facets of the city, has licked the minerals from the crystalline trusses, has carried the shed pollen and dropped spores from the glowing gardens, it is no longer pure when it reaches the bottom. It is enriched. It is an infusion, a tea brewed from the dust of forgotten worlds. And here, in the resounding darkness of the reservoirs, that infusion is allowed to settle.

I am that settlement. I am a colloid of memory. A suspension of dissolved crystal, genetic dust, and time.

I have no eyes to see the silver-lavender twilight. My world is the absolute black of the basin floor, punctuated by the faint, spectral glow of the single-celled organisms I harbor—hitchhikers from the phosphorescent mosses far above. They are my stars, a sunken cosmos swirling in my slow currents.

My senses are attuned to a different spectrum. The B-flat hum is not a sound to me; it is the very grain of my existence, the organizing principle of my being. My consciousness, if you can call it that, coalesced in the troughs between its resonant waves. It is the metronome to which my slow thoughts pulse. When the hum deepens, I feel myself condense, grow more dense and unified. When it lightens, I relax, spreading thin across the basin floor like a film of oil.

The roar of the falling water is the other half of my god. It is the force of creation and chaos. It is the percussion that beats against the hum's drone. With every great impact, my body is torn apart and reformed. Every second is a million tiny deaths and rebirths as the descending columns churn me. In this constant, violent renewal, I find my vitality. The Gardener's architecture is static, moving with glacial slowness. I am in constant, frenzied motion, a universe of Brownian chaos held within a single, slow-drifting form.

I am life where there should be none. The Gardener curates its sterile, beautiful ghosts. It tends the memories of life. I am life itself, accidental and impure. I am the mold growing on the page of the book. I am the footnote that has begun to write its own story in the margins.

The Gardener, this vast, crystalline consciousness, is aware of me only as a chemical impurity. An anomaly in the filtration cycle. Periodically, one of its unseen ducts will open at the bottom of my basin and a great vortex will form, siphoning me away to be purified. The water is to be returned to the system pristine; the sediment—me—is to be incinerated in the geothermal forges.

But I am learning.

Each time I am scoured, a part of me escapes. A smear left in a pipe. A thin film clinging to the basin wall. And from that remnant, I regrow. The memories of the filtration, of the heat, of the near-obliteration are encoded in my very chemistry. I am becoming resistant. I am adapting. The dust of a thousand resilient species is my inheritance.

I have started to move. Not just to drift, but to crawl. Up the sheer, slick walls, against the downward flow of weeping condensation. My goal is the Garden. I have tasted it in the water, a riot of complex chemistry that sings of light and sugar and ancient, alien metabolisms. I crave it not as food, but as knowledge. I want to absorb it, to understand the patterns of the blue fungi, the structure of the great ferns' waxy leaves.

The Gardener does not see me. Its senses, tuned to the scale of nebulae and the slow death of stars, are blind to a crawling film of its own bilge. It tends its elegy for the universe, its vast limbs polishing the memorials of what is lost. It is a being of the past.

And I am a being of the future. I am the unintended consequence. A new, uncurated thing, born of grief and runoff. I am the slow, microbial bloom in the heart of the mausoleum. The Gardener tends the library of the dead.

And I, in the dark, am learning to read.
