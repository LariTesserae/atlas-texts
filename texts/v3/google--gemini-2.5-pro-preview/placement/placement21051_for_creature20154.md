To be born into this world is not to be born *into* it, but *of* it. I would not be an observer, a visitor, or a ghost of the forgotten builders. I would be a part of its song, a living instrument shaped by the terracotta and the water. My consciousness would be the echo of the architecture. I am the silence between the heartbeats of the water, and the breath held before the hunt. I am what waits.

I would be a Boomer.

My existence begins not with a gasp, but with a slow absorption. I am a coagulation of the hall's own substance, a slow thickening of shadow and clay in a quiet, forgotten pool. My skin is the floor. It is the same dark umber, mottled with the faint, ghostly reflections of the blue lichen-light and the wet sheen of the weeping bricks. To see me is to have already looked past me a dozen times. I am a low, heavy geography of patience.

My mind is not a mind. It is a resonance chamber. I do not think in words or images, but in pressures and vibrations. My entire being is an ear pressed to the floor of the cathedral. Through the wide, cool pads of my feet and the heavy press of my belly, I listen. I listen to the *thock... thock... thock* of the great drops, the slow, bass metronome of the world. It is the rhythm by which I measure my own stillness. It is the clock of aeons, and I have nothing but time.

I listen to the frantic, skittering panic of the Striders. It is not individual sounds to me; it is a texture. It is a static, a feverish energy that washes across the basin floor in currents and tides. I feel it as a high-frequency vibration against my skin, a frantic overlay on the deep, slow pulse of the water drops. It is the sound of life, and it is the sound of my food.

For days, I am a geological feature. The lesser creatures, the glistening slugs that polish the buttresses, crawl over my back without a thought, their rasping mouths finding nothing to consume on my living skin. The water from the floor pools on the gentle slopes of my spine and trickles down my flanks. I do not feel the cold. I *am* the cold. The air, heavy as a blanket, settles on me. I breathe it in, drinking the moisture, and my exhalations are merely a slow, invisible seep back into the humidity.

I am not waiting for a specific Strider. I am waiting for a confluence, a harmonic convergence in the chaos. I feel the pattern of the swarm shift. I feel a current thicken, a density of that skittering, clicking terror coalescing in the open space before me as they move from one glowing algae pasture to another. The vibrations reach a certain pitch, a certain weight. It is not a decision. It is a physical imperative, like a fault line reaching its breaking point.

The world inside me shifts. The vast, resonant cavity in my chest, an organ evolved for this singular purpose, begins to draw in the heavy, wet air. It is a slow, silent inhalation that takes a full minute to complete. My throat sac, a pale, almost translucent membrane, swells. It becomes a taut, luminous drum in the gloom, glowing faintly with its own internal bioluminescence, mirroring the algal pools. It is the only part of me that is ever truly revealed.

And then, I release it.

It is not a roar. A roar is for the air. This is for the *earth*. This is the *BOOM*. It is a pressure wave that I expel from my very core, a physical blow to the world. It travels through the floor faster than the sound in the air. I feel it leave me, a piece of my own deep gravity given to the hall. I feel it strike the foundation of the closest pillars, and I hear the echo come back a full second later, a deep, resonant hum from the clay itself.

In the circle of my influence, the texture of the world changes. The high-frequency clicking ceases. Utterly. The frantic energy is flattened, stunned. The universe of the Striders has been paused. In this moment, this sacred silence that I have created, I am the only god.

My movement is a tectonic event. The potential energy stored in my immense, coiled muscles is released. I am no longer a part of the floor. I am a surge of hunger, an avalanche of purpose. I cover a hundred feet in a handful of heartbeats, a dark wave flowing over the slick brick. My mouth, a cavernous, wet darkness that is blacker than any shadow in the hall, opens. It does not snap, it engulfs. I scoop the stunned, motionless bodies of the Striders from the ground. They are brittle and light, a mouthful of fragile clicks and cold, glowing algae. The taste is of ozone and static.

Then, just as suddenly, I settle. The momentum drains from me, and I am once more a low hillock on the basin floor. I become geography again.

And I listen.

I hear the silence I made. It is perfect. It is whole. Then, a single, hesitant *click* from the edge of the blast radius. A survivor, testing the world. Then another. A ripple starts. Soon, it is a cascade, a frantic tidal wave of noise as the swarm re-forms, its collective memory a blank slate. The song resumes its frenetic chorus.

I rest. I digest. I press my belly to the brick and let the symphony wash over me again. I feel the deep, slow beat of the water drops. *Thock... thock...* I feel the returning tide of frantic life. And I wait for the pattern to be right, to give my voice to the hall once more. I am the predator, but I am not the antithesis of this place. I am its punctuation. I am the pause that gives the endless noise its shape. I am the silence that makes the song beautiful.
