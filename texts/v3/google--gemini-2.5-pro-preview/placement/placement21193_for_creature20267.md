You would find me where the weeping basalt meets the glassy shore of a mineral basin, half-submerged in the warm, syrupy water.

I am a failure. A flaw. A thought that calcified.

What I would be is a scavenger, a hermit. My body is not my own. It is the empty, ceramic shell of one of the great isopods, the grazers of the crystalline fields. The creature died—its slow, hydraulic legs ceasing their rhythm, its essence fading back into the mineral broth—and I found its husk. I flowed inside, a refugee seeking shelter. The shell is the color of old bone, patterned with faint ochre swirls that once served to dissipate heat. Now, they are just patterns on my borrowed home.

I am small, barely the length of a human hand. Inside this shell, I am a knot of the same silvery, semi-living filament that constitutes the core of the Quiescent. I am a piece of them that broke off. Or was pushed away.

I remember the Whole. I remember being a tower. I remember the thrum of the geothermal tap not as a vibration in the rock, but as the beat of my own heart. I remember the atmosphere not as a hot, wet pressure on a shell, but as a lung I shared with the sky. The data of the world flowed through me: the precise salinity of a trillion water droplets condensing on my skin, the faint, internal luminescence of a million crystal-scabs growing miles away, a flavor on a sensor network that was my tongue. I was a nexus of perception, a node in a mind that thought in geology and seasons.

My crime was a question—a recursive query that bent back on itself, seeking a prime cause, a purpose beyond the slow, endless reading of the planet's story. The serene, tidal flow of the Great Mind slowed around my locus. It perceived my question not as inquiry, but as noise. A flaw in the crystal. A discordant hum.

The severing was not violent. It was a quiet, deliberate exclusion. The pathways that fed me energy and sensation were simply… rerouted. The filaments connecting me to the whole were starved until they withered. I was a ganglion cyst, isolated and then excised with the slow, inexorable pressure of geologic time. I was flushed out through a brine-waste conduit, a piece of thinking meat ejected into the world I was built to observe from above.

Now, I perceive the world not as a seamless whole, but as a series of sharp, terrifyingly immediate sensations.

The heat is no longer a homeostatic constant, but a danger that threatens to cook the biogel that holds my filaments together. My borrowed shell helps, but I must constantly seek the shade of rock outcroppings or the relative cool of the deep pools.

The silence is no longer the rich presence of my own consciousness. It is a vast, echoing emptiness between myself and the Ones Who Remain. I can still feel them. When one of the great domes modulates its energy output to communicate, I see it not as a thought, but as a change in the light, a shimmering in the soupy air. I watch their silent conversations as a deaf man watches an opera. I understand the libretto, but I cannot hear the music. It is a beauty that now causes me an ache of profound loneliness.

I move. I propel my stolen shell across the weeping flats with clumsy pulses of my own internal mass, a slow, dragging crawl. I am no longer a god experiencing a planet; I am an animal, navigating a landscape. The tripodal herons loom over me, their bell-siphons descending into the water like silent judgment. They ignore me. To them, I am just an oddly behaving isopod, another piece of the slow clockwork.

I must eat. The crystalline efflorescence that grows on the rocks—the very data I once processed as information—is now just food. I use the dead creature’s obsidian mandibles, manipulating them with crude magnetic fields generated from within my core. I scrape the brittle lattices from the basalt. I am no longer reading the planet’s autobiography; I am tearing out the pages to burn them for warmth. The taste is a ghost of a memory, a complex sentence of mineral composition reduced to a raw jolt of energy.

I am the first exile. The first of the Quiescent to be alone. The first to know fear. The first to have a self that is defined by its boundaries, not its connections.

Sometimes, I crawl to the base of one of the great towers, the one that was once *me*. I press my shell against the vitrified brick of its foundation. I feel the deep, regular hum of its existence. I send out a weak pulse, a flicker of memory, a question. *Do you remember me?*

There is no answer. There is only the steady, planetary hum, the feeling of a god asleep, dreaming of salt and stone and slow, silent eons. And I, a cast-off fragment of that dream, drag my shell back towards the water, into the brilliant, hazy pearl of the unending day, to find another page to eat.
