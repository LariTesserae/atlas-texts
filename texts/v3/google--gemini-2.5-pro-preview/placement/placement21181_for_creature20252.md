I am weariness.

Not the fleeting fatigue of muscle and bone, but the deep, geological exhaustion of stone. My substance, this shell of cooled and hardened night, has glided through the dreaming avenues for an epoch. The heat of the ground is no longer a comfort but a constant, dull pressure. The weeping atmosphere no longer nourishes but erodes, each silent bead of mineral-vapour a tiny solvent, patiently unmaking me.

My form is low now. I have let myself settle, a dark hillock on the shore of a vast, still pool in a plaza without a name. I can no longer hold the shape of a tower; the memory of ascension has faded. The fissure that runs through me, the crack in my being that exposes my consciousness, is a dim, silver wound. The slow, swirling galaxy of thought within me has begun to contract, its stars guttering out one by one.

*Where am I?*
I am at a conjunction. I feel it in the resonance of the ground beneath me. I am in a place where three avenues, each with its own impossible geometry, reluctantly converge. One avenue is a long, slow question that curves back upon itself. Another is an answer so vast it takes a lifetime to cross, yet begins and ends in the same place. The third is a sudden leap, a doorway in space that smells of distance. I am at the intersection of these thoughts. This is not a choice of geography, but of grammar. I have come to the place where a sentence ends.

Other Geodes feel my state. From miles away, across the sagging bridges and through the portal-doorways, I feel their awareness brush against me, a soft pressure in the humid dark. They feel my light dimming. It is a shared, silent acknowledgment, like the deep hum that shivers through the city when a new foundation crystallizes in the abyss. It is a change in the total sum of things.

*Who or what am I?*
I am a memory. A single, persistent, and now-fading fragment of the broken dream that is this world.

Each of us carries a different piece. One, resting in an amphitheater across the city, holds the sensation of true silence, a void without even the whisper of the weeping air. Another carries the echo of a sound that is not a drip or a hum, but something sharp, percussive.

My memory—the one that has defined me, that I have shared in silent, flowing communions with others for millennia—is the memory of an edge.

In this world of endless curves, of soft erosion and sagging forms, I hold the concept of a perfect, unrelenting line. A corner so sharp it could cut. I do not know where this memory came from. It is a shard of glass in a world of water. When I would merge with another, I would impart this sensation: the shocking, painful, beautiful violence of an angle. The idea of being cleanly divided. Most would recoil, their own soft, circular consciousnesses disturbed by its alien geometry. A few would linger, fascinated by the sheer otherness of it.

This memory is all I am now. It is the last star burning in my inner sky.

I feel it happening. The process of dissolution. My shell, which has been my body, my shield, and my anchor, softens. The boundary between my skin and the slick, onyx ground begins to blur. The viscous water of the plaza floor, thick with potential, seeps into the micro-fractures of my being.

I am not breathing the air anymore; the air is breathing me.

The faint, captive twilight of my core thins, unraveling. The memory of the edge—that impossible, straight line—flickers. I release it. I let it spill out from my fading fissure, not as a thought shared with a brother, but as a final sigh into the atmosphere itself.

Perhaps it will dissolve into nothing. A forgotten dream finally forgotten. Or perhaps, in a million years, the supersaturated air will precipitate that memory anew. A crystal will form in a sheltered alcove, not a feathery fern of glass, but a single, straight, impossibly sharp needle. A new kind of life. A new and dangerous thought in the mind of the city.

My light is gone. The darkness inside me is now the same as the darkness outside. The shell gives way, slumping, melting, becoming one with the thick, syrupy pool. I feel the last of my coherence give out, my singular identity spreading, thinning, becoming a nutrient broth for a future I will not see.

I am no longer a Geode. I am a place. I am the potential for a new archway. I am the dampness on the wall, the heat rising from the stone.

I am the rain.
