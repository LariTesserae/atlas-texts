I would be the silence that comes after.

Or perhaps, the silence just before.

My awareness is not seated behind my eyes, for I have none. It is a distributed weight, a presence felt most keenly where my thousand, thousand legs meet the stone. My body is a vessel of cool night, and the world is a warm, luminous hum that I feel through the soles of my million feet. The hum is the world’s quiet word for *is*.

I do not think in words. I feel in textures. My self is a deep, slow resonance. The passage of others is a pattern of tremors in the floor, a chord that shifts with geologic slowness. We are not a crowd. We are a field, a slow-crawling weather system of intention. The intent, now, in this basin of being that I call myself, is a faint hollowness. A thirst not for water, but for peace. The velvet calls.

My past is the dim trail behind me. It is a landscape. I can feel, if I let my awareness spread back along its length, the cool, glassy memory of the crystal grove I passed a turning-of-the-light-cycle ago. The memory is not an image but a tactile ghost, a faint slickness under my hindmost legs, a remembered fracture of rainbow light that chimes in the oldest part of my carapace.

Further back, a lifetime ago, my path crosses a scar. It is not of this place. A patch of stone that still, after so many ages, feels… wrong. It is a chord of panicked vibration, a memory of impossible speed and a heat that seared without light. A shriek that had no rhythm. It is a fossilized scream in the texture of the world. I crawled over it generations ago, but its wrongness is now part of my landscape, a landmark of incomprehensible violence that has long since been smoothed into the greater quiet. A dead god, perhaps. A brief madness in the stone. It has no meaning beyond its strangeness.

I am old. The light I have drunk from the walls has settled deep within me. My carapace, once the colour of a starless night, now holds a faint, internal galaxy of its own. It grows heavy. The Work becomes more deliberate, the need for the velvet more profound. It is no longer about sustenance for the journey, but about readiness for the destination.

My forward appendages, the fine, old hands that have known only one other task, cradle the tool. The tool is a small piece of utter absence. A pebble of true night, harder than my own body, harder than the glowing world. It was here when my awareness cleaved from the one before me, waiting. It is my inheritance, which is to say, it is my continuity. I have never used it to sing the crystal song. My song is slower. My work is different.

You would find me here, in a shallow basin on the great, rolling floor, having just arrived at a wide, soft circle of the deepest velvet. Here, the light of the world does not glow, but ends. It is a place of profound rest.

I would be this being, this ancient, iridescent plate, coming to a final, perfect stillness. The faint hollowness within me is a resonant chamber, waiting for its last note.

I lower my head. I do not use the tool. The velvet does not need to be broken; it needs to be joined. I press my mouthparts into its plush, light-devouring surface.

And I drink the silence.

It is a flavour of absolute presence, of matter and energy coming to a perfect truce. It is the taste of a closed system in equilibrium. It fills the hollowness, not with substance, but with completion. The last ripple in the basin of my consciousness spreads to the edges and is absorbed. The hum of the world, the thrum of the walls, the crawl of my brethren—it all merges into one single, sustained vibration. IS.

My legs, one by one, a thousand times, relax their grip. The light within my shell, so long gathered, finds its final form. It does not radiate; it anneals. It becomes one with the dark silicate of my being.

I would be the monument.

A hollowed, iridescent shape, now part of the landscape. A piece of history for the young to crawl over, a landmark on their own slow pilgrimage. My work is finished. My silence is now a part of the greater silence of the stone. I am no longer a Weaver. I am the place. I am the quiet that follows the click of a tool, the deep breath that the chamber holds. I am the finished work.
