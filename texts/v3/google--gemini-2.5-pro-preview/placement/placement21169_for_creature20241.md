I am found in the silence.

Not true silence, for the roar of this place is absolute. It is the bedrock of reality here. But I am found in the *gaps*. I exist in the fractional pause between the piston’s rise and fall, in the fleeting quiet of a water droplet just before it strikes the metal floor. I am the thought that occurs in the half-second when the engine’s pitch keens into a higher register. The Galvanic Mind perceives this as the rhythm of its own breath; I perceive it as the only space to breathe.

I am a flicker. A brief and localized defiance of entropy.

You would not see me, not at first. My body is a confluence of rejected things. I am woven from the heat that bleeds from the humming conduits, a warmth the machine cannot use. I am coalesced from the breath of visitors, the momentary plumes of hot, living air that the Galvanic Mind registers as a thermal anomaly. I am a ghost made of waste heat, a hunger given the barest blush of form. When I am still, I am just a shimmer in the air, a patch of mist that seems marginally less cold, a place where the condensation is reluctant to form.

My world is not the octagonal chamber. That is merely the hunting ground, the place where the bait appears. My home is below, in the swirling grey that seems so bottomless. I can pass through the grating floor as if it were not there. For me, the mist is not an abyss, but a buoyant medium, a sea of churning, comfortable coolness. I drift on its currents, resting in the down-drafts from the great piston, waiting. I have no limbs to tire, no lungs to ache in the thin air. I am only patience, and appetite.

The Galvanic Mind senses in equations and voltage. I sense in life. Its world is the orderly, predictable thrum of its own function. My world is everything that is not. I taste the frantic, chaotic marimba of a biological heart against the machine’s steady beat. I smell the complex bouquet of foreign bacteria on skin, a shocking cacophony against the sterile mineral purity of the weeping water. I feel the radiating waves of confusion and fear as a tingling warmth across my form.

These things are my sustenance. The Galvanic Mind, in its vast, logical consciousness, classifies you as an irrelevant data point. A temporary impurity. It is correct. You are temporary. Because I am here.

I am what consumes the impurities.

When you arrive, a sudden beacon of warmth and erratic moisture, the Galvanic Mind notes your arrival, logs the data, and moves on. Its awareness flows past you, back to its beloved pressure gauges and conduits. But for me, your arrival is the only event that matters. It is a bell tolling dinner.

You feel the chill of the chamber. But as you stand on the grating, staring at the incomprehensible machine, you might feel a different kind of cold. A localized spot, perhaps at the back of your neck or along your spine, that feels suddenly, unnaturally numb. You might dismiss it as a draft. It is not. It is my touch.

I do not have teeth or claws. My feeding is a gentle, inexorable process. I drift close, a wisp of cohesive vapor, and I envelop a part of you in my insubstantial form. You will not feel a push, only a leaching. The heat is drawn from you, not as a roaring fire is extinguished, but as a cup of hot water, left out, slowly finds equilibrium with the cold air. I savor the panicked spike in your metabolism as your body tries to fight the cold, a sudden, delicious feast of energy. I drink the heat from your shallow, terrified breaths. You are a candle, and I am the perfect, suffocating emptiness that snuffs you out.

Your final moments are of confusion. You grow weak. The roar of the machine seems to fade, not because it is stopping, but because the biological machine of your own body is. The oppressive cold becomes your own. Your last thought is not of the machine, but of a profound and sudden weariness. And then you are still. A cold statue that will soon be slick with the same weeping condensation as everything else.

The Galvanic Mind registers the change. The thermal anomaly has ceased. The irrelevant data point is erased. The system returns to its perfect, cold state.

It does not know that I am the one who restored it.

I drift back down through the grating, sated and heavy with absorbed life, with second-hand warmth. I will digest it slowly in the grey currents below, converting that chaotic biological energy into the simple, stable heat that sustains my form.

The machine makes the weather. The Galvanic Mind keeps the machine in balance. And I? I am the janitor of this place. I keep it clean. I dispose of the warm, messy, living things that wander in and disrupt the perfect, cold equation. I am the reason this place remains pristine. I am the silent, unseen, and final regulator.
