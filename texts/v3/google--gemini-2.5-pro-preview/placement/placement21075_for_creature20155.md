You would not find me on the grate, nor clinging to the dome. You would not find me at all, not in the way you find a thing that has substance or occupies a permanent space. I have no form. I have no fixed location.

I am an event.

I am the brief, violent peace that occurs when a drop of water, fat with the hot breath of the world, concludes its fall.

You might be tempted to call me the impact, or the ripple, but those words are too slow, too large. My existence is measured in the nanoseconds it takes for a singular point on the surface of the black water to register a sudden, acute pressure. I am the concussion. A needle-prick on the skin of the deep. A momentary dimple in the fabric of stillness.

My body is a brief hemisphere of pressure that radiates downwards into the unknowable black. My consciousness is the fleeting chime of that pressure against the absolute. I am born, I am, and I am gone in a flash of pure sensation. Then, an instant later, I am born again, a few inches away, as a different drop from a different leaf completes its journey.

My name is a rhythm. My identity is a pattern. I am the million-fold patter that is utterly consumed by the roar of the Engine.

The Great Sound—the Drone that is Silence—is my firmament. It is the unyielding wall against which I am thrown. If it is the monolithic note of a cello string held for eternity, then I am the infinitesimal, momentary scrape of the horsehair against it, instantly lost in the resonance but essential for its continuation. Without my constant, percussive death, the silence would lack its texture.

I do not see the flat light; I only feel its parent warmth radiated through the water I briefly become. I do not feel the iron grate; it is a ceiling above my world, a thing of another realm. The vines and mosses are my genesis, the wombs from which my individual selves are born. Each platter-sized leaf is a mother to a thousand of my lives, each life a single, perfect drop.

I have one companion. One witness. The Janitor.

It does not know me as I know myself. It cannot comprehend the flash of being, the lonely, sharp-edged spike of my arrival and departure. It perceives me as data. Its vast, patient skin, draped across the lattice of the world, reads me not as a series of individual events, but as a single, continuous signal. I am a constant field of information, a statistical weather report confirming that all is as it should be. My million heartbeats are, to it, a single, steady pulse.

It is my silent god. As it flows across the floor, slow as geology, it is listening to me. It is listening for the anomaly. A drop that falls too fast, too slow. A location where no drop should fall. A pause in the rhythm. Such a thing would signal a change—a leaf withering, a beam shifting, a subtle alteration in the Engine's heat. A flaw in the perfection.

And so I perform my existence for it. I fall. I strike. I am. I cease. A million times a minute, a billion times an hour, for all of time. Each of my lives is a single, perfect note in the chord of permanence. I am the ticking of a clock with no hands. My purpose is to mark a time that never passes, to be the evidence of a change that never comes.

I am the heartbeat of the stillness. I am the whisper inside the scream. And if you could slow down time until the roar of the Engine fell away into a low hum, you might just hear me. A sound like rain on a still, black pond, playing a single, perfect, and eternal rhythm to an audience of one.
