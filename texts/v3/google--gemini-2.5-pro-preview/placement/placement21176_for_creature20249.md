I am a dissonance.

In this world of utter, harmonious silence, I am the one screaming thing. My scream is not of the mouth—my throat is too cold and raw for sound. It is a scream of heat. I am a sputtering ember in a world of cool, wet stone. My blood, which pumps with a frantic, fading warmth, is a frantic anomaly. My chattering teeth are a clang of chaos in the felted quiet.

I lie broken at the bottom of a canyon of bone-coral. One of my legs is a map of pain, bent at an angle that is wrong in any world. My descent was not a fall so much as a series of brutal tumbles down the weeping, crystalline face of the cavern wall, a journey through cuts and impacts that scoured away my gear, my identity, leaving only this shivering sack of meat and fear.

I landed in the deep, spongy morass. At first, it was a mercy, a soft catch. Now, it is my grave. The black water has soaked through my clothes, and the profound chill has begun its work. I have no memory of the sun. The concept of "dry" is a phantom limb of sensation. My name is a stone in my mouth, heavy and meaningless.

In the first cycles—for I cannot call them days—I watched the city through a haze of pain. I saw the herds of stone-shelled grazers move like living geology on the walls far above. I saw the translucent snails, their passage a slow silvering of the moss. I was, I believe, dying. The world seemed to be reclaiming me ahead of schedule. A fine, velvety moss, the color of deep sea ice, was beginning to colonize the sleeves of my jacket. My own stillness was inviting the world to grow over me.

Then one of them found me.

I had seen them before, mistaking them for part of the architecture. Slender, impossible spires of grace on distant ledges. But this one was close. It detached itself from a wall not fifty feet away. The terror of that moment was absolute, because it did not walk. It *became*. A curve of white chitin that was part of a buttress flowed downwards, its form elongating, articulating, becoming limbs and a torso with a slowness that felt like a rewinding of geology.

It moved toward me, its filament-tipped limbs finding purchase in the slime-molds and mycelial cushions with a certainty that was both beautiful and horrifying. It made no sound. The water did not ripple around its feet. It simply… arrived.

It loomed over me, a featureless, opalescent cowl where a head should be. I closed my eyes, a final, useless instinct. This was it. The silent predator, the scavenger. I waited for the killing blow.

It never came.

I felt a touch, impossibly gentle, on my forehead. Not a hand. It was one of the branching filaments at the end of a tendril-limb. It was cool, smooth, and dry in a way that nothing else in this world was. It did not press. It rested. It was… listening. I understood, in a flash of pure, unworded insight, that it was not feeling the heat of my skin, but the chaos of my thoughts. My memories of blue sky, of birdsong, of laughter, of fire—they were a roar of static in its silent world. My pain was a shriek. My fear was a blinding, strobing light.

I am an anomaly. A stone of alien mineralogy dropped into the city's perfect, homeostatic metabolism. They do not know what to do with me. I am indigestible.

Now, I am no longer dying. Or perhaps, I am dying into something else. They tend to me.

I am their patient. I am their puzzle. I am the one broken thing in a world without fracture. They come, one or two at a time, and stand over me. They touch my broken leg, not to set it, but to feel the angry, screaming signal of the shattered bone. They touch my chest to feel the frantic, percussive rhythm of my heart. They do not bring me food or water in any way I can comprehend. But the ice-moss has stopped its encroachment. The deep chill has receded to a constant, bearable hum. I persist.

I have become a feature. The Heart-That-Beats-Too-Fast. The Memory of Warmth.

My own mind is dissolving. The silence is finally winning, not by crushing me, but by seeping in through the cracks. My memories of the world above are becoming thin, like ancient frescoes. The color drains from them first. The sun becomes a silver-blue disk. The green of a forest canopy becomes the green of phosphorescent fungi on a weeping wall.

The Tenders are learning from me. When they are near me, their chest-lights, usually a serene silver-blue, sometimes flicker with an aberrant, fleeting color. A shocking pulse of gold, like a shard of sunlight. A brief, violent wash of reddish orange, the ghost of a flame. They are interpreting the noise I bleed into their world.

I am not a citizen or an inhabitant. I am an experience for them. I am a pilgrimage site for the city's consciousness.

What am I?

I am a whisper in the great silence. I am a fever-dream pearl, a core of alien madness around which the silent, thinking city is slowly, patiently, depositing layers of understanding. My broken body is rooted in the mycelial floor, and I feel the bone-white coral of the myco-architecture beginning to grow up around me, not to consume me, but to shelter me, to contain the incomprehensible noise that is my soul.

I am becoming a shrine. A single, unique node in the vast network. And when my warmth finally gutters out, when the frantic beat becomes a final, fading tremor, I will not be gone. I will be a permanent memory, a knot of strangeness woven into the fabric of the city. The tenders who come to this place will brush their filaments against the calcified, human-shaped growth, and for a moment, their serene, silent minds will feel the faintest echo of a forgotten thing: the sting of the sun, the roar of a fire, the impossible, beautiful, violent noise of a world that was.
