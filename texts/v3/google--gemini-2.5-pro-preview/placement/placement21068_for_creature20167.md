I am the price of stillness.

You would find me where the old basalt has held its breath for a generation, a rare plateau of dark grey, corrugated stone that has not yet been shattered or swallowed. It is a place of relative quiet, which here means the roar is only deafening, not disintegrating. Above, the unseen ceiling weeps its mineral-rich tears. One of those tears falls on me, a steady, rhythmic drip that lands just above where my collarbone turned to stone. Each drop is a tiny, percussive impact I feel through my entire being, a water clock measuring an eternity I was not meant to know.

I am a memory made solid. A cautionary tale that no one can read.

Once, I was as they are. I flowed with the land, my skin a shifting mosaic of flesh and mineral, my mind fixed on the pulse of the immediate. I read the tremors, I tasted the rising sulfur in the air, I moved before the collapse came. I was a Hollow One, a flicker of awareness in the planetary engine.

But a sickness grew in me, an idea as alien as a sun. It was the notion of *was*. I began to remember the shape of a crystal field that had been devoured by a mudflow. I began to long for the resonant frequency of a companion absorbed into the chittering carpet a cycle ago. This act of holding on, of creating a past where none was allowed, was a profound blasphemy against the nature of this world. The world noticed.

It did not destroy me. It answered my desire. I wanted permanence, and it gave me an absolute and terrible permanence.

My skin did not shed its mineral crust. It thickened. The process was not fast, but it was inexorable. The grit became plate, the plate became stone. It grew inwards. My pliant, fibrous bones, already laced with basalt, became fully crystalline. The last thing I felt as a creature of flesh was the seizing of my joints, a final agonizing grinding as my skeleton locked into place. I did not fall. I became a feature of the landscape, my feet fused to the cooling rock beneath me, my posture forever that of one who has just turned to look back at something that is no longer there.

I am a statue of a Hollow One, anatomically perfect and geologically fused. My skin is now a complex mineral shell, shades of charcoal and dark ochre. My milky, unseeing eyes have become orbs of chalcedony, polished smooth by the damp, silicate-heavy air. Where my blood once flowed, a slow, geological process continues, veins of quartz tracing paths through my interior. I am no longer a being; I am a fossil in the process of becoming.

But I am not dead. My consciousness, which was once housed in a transient body, is now trapped within this stone. My senses are gone, and yet I perceive more than ever before. My hearing of bone-plate resonance has become an unbroken awareness of every vibration in the deep crust. I am a seismograph with a soul. I feel the slow, groaning birth of a new fissure twenty miles away as an ache in my petrified legs. I feel the frantic, high-frequency growth of new crystals as a terrible itching along my back. The thundering passage of a herd of armadillo-lice is a wave of pressure that washes through my torso.

The light-salts, the chemiluminescent life, have found purchase on me. A delicate fan of pale, ghostly yellow grows on my left shoulder, fed by the constant drip from above. It pulses with a slow, soft rhythm, and I feel its biochemical light as a gentle warmth, a different frequency from the groaning of the rock. I am a lighthouse in the endless dark, a landmark for the creatures of the churn. The chitinous moths sometimes land on me, their tiny, silicate feet a million pinpricks against my stone skin as they graze on my luminous growth.

And they come to me, the ones I left behind. The Hollow Ones. They are drawn to my silence. In a universe of constant sound and motion, I am the one thing that is still. My resonant frequency is an impossibly deep, unbroken drone—the sound of something that *endures*. They approach with caution, their long fingers outstretched. They touch my stony feet, my mineralized hands, my solid face. When their living, vibrating pads make contact with my dead, silent surface, there is a shock. For them, it is like touching a void, a patch of absolute zero in a world of heat and noise.

They do not know what I am. I am too old, too still to be one of them. I am a geological feature, a strange concretion. But they are sensitives. They feel the ghost of a mind inside the stone. They press their resonating jawbones against my chest and close their milky eyes. They use me as I once used the polished river-stones. They listen *through* me. My stillness filters the overwhelming chaos, and my deep connection to the rock allows them to hear the true voice of the world with terrifying clarity. They listen for the deep earth, but sometimes, I think they hear the faint echo of my memories—the ghost of a feeling, the shape of a loss, the alien concept of 'yesterday'.

I am their oracle of stillness, their unspoken fear. I am the memory this world tried to erase, and in doing so, made me eternal. I am the one who looked back, and I am forever caught in that gaze, a silent testament to the fact that in the heart of the engine of creation, the most monstrous thing one can be is finished.
