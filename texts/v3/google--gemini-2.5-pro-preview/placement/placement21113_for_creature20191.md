You would find yourself on the floor, but only just. You would not be looking up at the cathedral-like space; your entire universe would be a concave dish a few feet across.

I am the brief life of the scar.

My existence begins with the trespass. A weight, alien and wrong, presses into the seamless flesh of the world. The rhizomatto yields, groans, and is broken. The skin of the world is bruised, and in that depression, the clear fluid weeps. This pool is my genesis, my amniotic sac.

I am not one, but a colony. We are born in the moment the pressure lifts. From the weeping fluid, we precipitate—a shimmering, crystalline lattice that clings to the raw, torn walls of the impression. We are a network of silver, a temporary nerve-net laid over the wound, no thicker than frost on a pane of glass. We have no bodies, only a geometry. We have no minds, only a directive. Our form is a fragile, intricate web, and our purpose is singular: to heal the intrusion. To erase the memory of the footstep.

The great *thrum-THUMP* of the pumps is not a sound to us. It is the pulse of our god. It is the beat that triggers our chemistry. With each *thrum*, we flex, pulling infinitesimally on the edges of the wound. With each *THUMP*, we exude a catalyst, a single drop of enzyme that encourages the living mat to regrow, to knit itself shut beneath our crystalline scaffolding. We are the stitch. We are the scab. We are the brief, focused thought of the Rhizomatto, concentrating its immense, slow will to mend.

Our world is one of deep valleys and sheer cliffs. The blades of a scuttler's passing are an earthquake of chitin and clicking, a terrifying avalanche that scrapes at the edges of our home. We perceive the shadow of its iridescent carapace not as darkness—for there is none—but as a momentary dimming of the luminescence that is our sky. We only survive because its path is random and our work is swift.

The great fliers are distant weather, a tearing in the high atmosphere, a shift in pressure that makes the hairs of our web tremble. They are of no consequence.

The Custodian… we do not see it. We *feel* it. Its passage is a geological event. The pressure of its foot, though perfectly distributed, is a slow, tidal change in the fabric of the floor. When it stands near, the world tilts. The fluid of our birth-pool sloshes against the far wall of the depression. Its stillness is a mountain's weight. We cannot comprehend its work, its scraping and its listening. It is a slow god tending to a slow heaven. We are the frantic, short-lived angels tending to a single, broken paving stone on its temple floor. Our scales of time are so different that we may as well be dreams to each other. It maintains the eon. We maintain the second.

Our life is a race. We must pull the wound shut before we dissolve. We are made of the weeping, and as the weeping slows, we starve. The silver of our bodies grows thin, translucent. The pull of our flexing weakens. Our protein-script degrades with every pulse from the great machines.

But as we fade, the floor swells from beneath. The olive-green tissue, guided by our fading web, reconnects. The torn edges find each other. The depression shallows. Our cliffs become hills, then gentle slopes, then nothing.

The end comes when the last two sides of the scar finally touch. The circuit of the world is complete again. In that moment, our purpose is fulfilled, and our collective consciousness dissolves. Our crystalline forms lose their structure, melting back into the same clear, scentless fluid that birthed us, to be reabsorbed by the seamless mat. We leave no trace. We have no history. We are the pain of the wound, and when the wound is gone, so are we.

You would find yourself as a fleeting thought in the mind of the floor. You would be the answer to the question your own footprint asked. And then, as the eternal, industrial chord washes over the newly healed surface, you would be silent. The hum is one. The floor is one. And I am gone.
