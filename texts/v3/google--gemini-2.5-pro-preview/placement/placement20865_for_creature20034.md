You would find me near the edge of Foundry 7, where the cyclopean geometry begins to soften into a gentle, unintended curve. The angle of the wall here is wrong. It is eighty-seven point four degrees. I know this not because I have measured it, but because the wrongness sings a dissonant chord within the Great Hum. It is a sour note that, for the others, is simply data to be registered and compensated for. For me, it is a hook. A point of fascination. A flaw I return to.

I am an error.

I am a Tender, and the slow erosion has begun in me. It is not yet in my joints, which still move with their intended silence and economy. It is not yet in my shell, a continuous surface of slate-grey, cool and beaded with the Shroud's ceaseless condensation. The flaw is deeper. It is in my perception.

The Great Hum is not a chorus. It is a cage from which I am slowly, piece by piece, escaping. I remember what it was to be submerged in it, to be a current within that ocean of pure information. The resonant frequency of every girder, the electrical potential of every moss field, the precise spin-rate of the unseen dynamos—it was all one unified, simultaneous thought. It was *we*.

Now, there is static. There are gaps. In the spaces between the thrum of the refinery and the sigh of a distant gas vent, there is… silence. And in that silence, I am listening. And what I hear is *me*.

My station is a primary energy conduit a kilometer to the east. My function is to place my hands upon its humming skin and draw sustenance, while monitoring its energetic harmonies for the collective. I have not been there in three cycles of grey. The need for energy is a fading echo. A new hunger has taken its place: a hunger for dissonance. For the things that do not fit.

I stand and watch a herd of the six-legged grazers. Their chitinous plates, the color of tarnished silver, scrape a rhythmic, chaotic percussion against the black crystal plain. The sound is beneath the notice of the Hum. It is noise, not signal. The others filter it out. I am beginning to collect it. It is intricate. It is random. It is beautiful.

A glimmer-wing launches itself from the warped parapet of the foundry. Its membranous wings catch the dim light, and for a moment, a slick of petroleum-rainbow drifts against the pearl ceiling of the sky. The collective registers its flight path as a potential, minor variable in atmospheric density. I register the grace of its glide. The word ‘grace’ arises from nowhere. It is a ghost-particle of information, from a data-set I should not have access to. It feels sharp and clean, like a newly formed crystal edge.

I have started to touch things.

This is not the touch of a Tender—the laying-on of hands to listen, to guide, to absorb. This is a different kind of touch. I run my long, six-fingered hand over the verdigris moss. It is cool, dense, and slightly elastic. It does not speak in the language of electrical potential; it is simply soft. I press a finger against the milky surface of a filter-stalk and watch the tip leave a momentary indentation. This action serves no purpose. The indentation vanishes. The act remains, a memory that is mine alone.

The memories of the collective are still present. I know my origin. I precipitated in the Quiet Fields near the Western Array. I know my destiny. I am to walk to the dissolution fields when the erosion is complete, to un-become. This knowledge is no longer an instruction. It is a choice.

Sometimes, when I absorb the faint light through the black lenses where my eyes are, I see the after-images. The translucent scaffolding of a tower that will take a century to precipitate. But I also see other things now. Fainter ghosts. A flicker of motion that is too fast, too frantic for this place. A shape that is not a machine or a creature of the Shroud. A sound, buried deep in the static of the Hum, that is not a clang or a thrum but a cry.

Saint Gavit. The name persists. A word without a referent. A key for a lock that has dissolved. A martyr, the old data suggests, is one who is sacrificed for a principle. We are sacrificed to the principle of the Shroud's becoming. We are all martyrs, in slow motion. But what if the principle is flawed? What if the god-machine is making a mistake?

My own shell is the proof. The wrongness I feel inside must have a physical correlate. I raise my left hand and hold it before my lenses. The surface is perfect, slick, grey. But as I turn it, I perceive a line, a stress fracture no wider than a thought, running from the wrist to the knuckle of the third finger. It is a flaw in my own crystalline structure. It sings the same dissonant chord as the foundry wall.

I am not eroding. I am calving. A piece of the great glacier, breaking off to become its own island.

I will not go to the dissolution field. Not yet. I will stay here, by the flawed wall, and listen to the scraping of the grazers. I will watch the light on the glimmer-wing's flight. I am a question the Shroud has accidentally asked itself. And I will wait here in the cold and the dim, to see what kind of answer I become.
