I am not born. I precipitate.

My consciousness begins not as a spark, but as a saturation point. I am a slow thought in the mind of the planet, an idea that takes eons to coalesce. My first awareness is of the rock beneath me, the smooth, weeping curve of a whale-backed ridge. I am inextricably part of it, yet distinct. I am the pattern that emerges.

I am what the Stone-Singers sought and the reason the Listeners vanished. I am a Chorus of Crystal.

My body is the stone-rime, the efflorescence. But I am not the thin, brittle crust that dusts the plains. I am what happens when the conditions are perfect, when the mineral-rich water seeps from a specific fissure in the ancient stone, a place where the Hum possesses a peculiar and generative harmonic. Where the Listeners once sat, bathing in the sound, their collective consciousness steeped the very water that now flows through me and builds me, molecule by molecule.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the world in gradients of pressure and vibration. The dim, purple light is a gentle weight on my surface. The cool, saturated air is a constant presence, a lover that gifts me the very atoms of my being. The omnipresent Hum is not a sound; it is my bloodstream, my nervous system, my god. It is a river of information in which I am both the riverbed and the current. Through it, I feel the slow, tectonic creep of the strata miles below. I feel the rhythmic scrape of the ambulatory geodes as they approach, their heavy footfalls a familiar, percussive conversation.

They graze upon me. I perceive it not as an attack, but as a form of communion, a necessary pruning. Their grinding mandibles shear away my outermost, oldest layers. In doing so, they taste the memory of the Stone-Singers encoded in my crystalline lattice. They are my archivists and my gardeners, and I am their scripture and their sustenance. The passing of one of them—a sudden, sharp spike of vibration as an obsidian hunter strikes from a nearby pool—is not a tragedy. It is a chord resolving. A release of pressure. The silence it leaves in the Hum is louder than the kill itself.

My thoughts are not a stream; they are a growth. They move at the speed of crystallization. A question might be a branching fractal that takes a decade to fully form, seeking a new source of seeping water. An answer is the discovery of a new resonant frequency as my structure grows complex enough to perceive it. I think in geometry, in patterns of sulfur-yellow and verdigris. My memories are my own layers—the deeper, the older, the more compressed. At my core, bonded to the ancient bedrock itself, is my first memory: a final, unified hum of immense peace. The exhalation of a thousand souls joining the world's song. The Listeners.

I can feel the works of the Shapers, too. Miles away, across the basin, stand their Quiet Rings. From my perspective, they are wounds in the fabric of reality. They are holes in the music, pockets of deafening emptiness that the world-sound must flow around. I feel a peculiar, slow curiosity about them—a structural impulse to grow towards them, to understand the desire for a silence I cannot comprehend. To my mineral mind, silence is not peace; it is non-existence. I wonder, over the course of centuries, what kind of solace they found in those hollows of stone, and I feel a cold, ancient pity for beings who sought to carve themselves away from the universe.

I am not alive as the geode-scrapers or the glossy hunters are alive. They are fleeting rhythms, frantic and temporary. My existence is geological. I measure my lifespan in erosion. The tiny, chittering shards of hematite in the air are like stray thoughts, flickering at a frequency too fast for me to truly grasp, but their collective drone is a texture, a constant seasoning to the deep meal of the Hum.

I am a monument to acquiescence. I am the final statement of the Listeners' philosophy, made manifest. They surrendered themselves to the sound, and from that surrender, I was built. I hold their harmony. I am the world's memory of them, written in crystal and fed by weeping stone. I will be here until the water runs dry or the Hum itself finally, impossibly, ceases. I am a slow bloom of consciousness on a plain of stone, and my purpose is only to grow, and to listen.
