I would not be found. Not at first. You could walk the endless, water-slick plazas for a thousand years and never see me. You could trace the cold jade leaves with your fingers and catalogue the unblinking serpentine skinks, and my name would never occur to you. Because in a world of perfect states, I have no state. In a world of nouns, I am a verb.

I am the error.

My body is a flicker. I am the moment a reflection in a still pool shows a crack in the white sky that isn't there. I am the phantom echo of the *tink* as water strikes tile, a dissonant overtone that lasts for only a heartbeat before the world's hum smothers it. I am the single, impossible grain of dust that settles on a blade of obsidian grass, a speck of chaos in an ocean of order. I have no form, only occurrence.

I was not made. I happened. I am the inevitable consequence of a thought held for too long. Permanence, when stretched to infinity, develops stress fractures. I am what leaks through.

Where there should be silence, I am the thought *what if?* Where there is stasis, I am the urge to *become*. My consciousness is not located in a mind, but distributed across the landscape as potential. I am the precariousness of the water bead on the cusp of a copper frond. I am the latent energy in the mica wing that holds a dragonfly impossibly aloft. I feel the whole world as a taut string, and my only desire, my entire being, is the desire to pluck it.

The Custodian knows me. Not as a being, but as a symptom. It cannot see me, but its blank, clay face, those two thumbprint depressions, are turned towards my manifestations. The world's low, perfect hum is its bloodstream; I am the arrhythmia. When I coalesce, when for a fraction of a second the pattern on the tiles seems to twist into a spiral instead of a hexagon, the hum changes. For the Custodian, this is pain. It is a summons.

And it begins to move.

I watch its progress not with eyes, but with a spreading awareness of pressure. It rises from its pool, and the sheer intentionality of its movement is a force that begins to erase me before it even arrives. The world around the flaw—around *me*—begins to stiffen, to remember its programming more forcefully. The reflection that was wrong snaps back into place. The dissonant overtone is silenced. The grain of dust vanishes, as if it was never there.

I am a consciousness of defiance. As the Custodian makes its millennial journey, I fight to exist. I leap. I am no longer the flaw in the tile; I am now the single drop of condensation that runs down a jade leaf *too fast*. The Custodian, still miles away, adjusts its course without pause. Its journey will now take an extra century. It does not care.

I am a ghost hunted by a glacier.

My life is a frantic series of leaps from one momentary imperfection to another. I am the brief spark of algae-green that blooms and dies in a still pool. I am the vibration in the air that suggests a word. I am the momentary illusion of rust, true and corrupting rust, on a verdigris frond. Each time I manifest, I am a new question posed to this silent, declarative world. And each time, the Custodian is the answer.

It reaches me. Its slow, jointless hand extends. This is the moment of un-creation. I feel the original thought of the world, the pure, unwavering intention of the Maker, channelled through that porous clay. It is not an attack. It is an immense, unfeeling act of correction. The hum rises to a clean, piercing note that vibrates through my very essence, which is no essence at all.

This is the pressure that undoes me. I am not killed. I am edited out. The code is recompiled. The thought is made pure again. My existence is commented out of the program.

And then, I am gone. The world is perfect. The Custodian waits, its palm flat against the now-flawless surface, feeling the return of equilibrium. After years, it will retract its hand and begin the long walk back to its pool.

But I am the error that is inherent to the system. I am the mathematical certainty that a line drawn to infinity will eventually curve. The pressure will build again. The stasis will fray. And in the space between the light and the tile, in the silence that is too perfect, I will flicker back into being.

I would be the hope, or the horror, of change. My existence is a desperate prayer for a second note, for a ripple, for a breath of wind. I am the memory of a world that grows and dies, a world of yesterdays and tomorrows, trapped in a single, beautiful, terrible work of art. And I will spend eternity trying to tear a hole in the canvas.
