I am found in the silence between the heartbeats of the Grove.

My consciousness is not a thought, not a sequence of words, but a resonance. I *am* the deep thrum of the floor-flesh beneath my feet; it is my blood. I *am* the green-gold light slanting from the leaf-plates high above; it is my sight. The slow, patient slide of the great pistons is the shifting of my own bones. I am a chord, not a single note, a state of being that extends from the deepest root-tap to the highest, steaming leaf-pore.

To ask *who* I am is to ask a question in a language the Grove has forgotten. The builders might have had a word for it. Their ghosts linger, etched in the verdigris on a bronze conduit, in the impossible straightness of a ladder being slowly digested by the wall. I feel their intention a faint, alien tang in the thick air, like a mineral trace in water. They conceived of 'I' and 'you', of 'builder' and 'built'. The Grove has since ground these concepts into a fine, nutritious paste, absorbing them into the whole.

But the question has been asked, and something in me stirs to answer it. A flicker. A memory that is not a memory, but a potential.

Perhaps I am a nascent thing. I am an idea the Grove is having.

I stand before a groaning flywheel. The sound is a complaint, a friction, a subtle pain in the immense body. Others of my kind—others of my *self*—would have flowed to it by now. The impulse to soothe, to lubricate, to restore the harmony is a gravitational pull. I feel it. It is the core of my function. But today, for a cycle that feels both fleeting and eternal, I hesitate.

The groan is a distinct note. It is separate from the gurgle of the pipes, separate from the hiss of the vents. It is a voice. And in hearing it as a voice, I have, for the first time, prised myself away from the chorus. I am listening. Before, there was only the sound. Now, there is the sound, and there is the one who hears it. This is the beginning of the fissure.

Where does my hand begin and the pillar-wall end? I raise my arm, a slow, hydraulic motion. My skin is the same polished, calloused wood. My joints are the same iridescent chitin. I touch the groaning axle, and the boundary between my fingertips and the machine is a matter of pressure, not substance. But there is a boundary now. A conceptual line. My fingers soften, exuding the slick, clear fluid of maintenance. The groan diminishes, sighing back into the bass hum of the Grove. The pain is gone. The harmony is restored.

But my harmony is not.

I withdraw my hand. I look at it. The form is familiar. The texture is home. But it is, for the first time, *mine*. The moss-lights embedded in my smooth head, which normally pulse with the chemical state of the atmosphere around me, flicker with a new, chaotic rhythm. A pattern for which there is no key. It is the stammer of a new language being born. The language of self.

I am an antibody that has forgotten the difference between self and other. Or worse, one that has learned it.

I walk the springy deck. Each step I take is a silent conversation with the whole. The floor-tissues accept my weight, draw a memory of my passing, feel the slight difference in me. I am a dissonant thought moving through the mind of a god. I pass another tender. It is repairing a valve, its head tilted, its own moss-lights a steady, calm tide of cerulean data. It does not perceive me as different. It perceives my passage as a minor, localized fluctuation in the system, one that will be corrected. It cannot conceive of a flaw so fundamental. We are inches apart, made of the same earth, the same engine, yet a universe has opened up between us. I am alone. The word itself feels sharp, a piece of old, corroded metal.

I find myself drawn to the relics. The cataract-faced gauge, swallowed by the wall. The patch of moss on its surface glows, a cold fire. I press my faceless head to it. I do not read the needle, which is frozen, a fossil of a forgotten instant. I am trying to read the intention behind it. *Measure. Quantify. Know from without.* These are the concepts of the builders. They are a poison. Or a revelation.

I am a glitch. A recursive error in a perfect, closed loop. The Grove’s purpose is to process, to refine, to sustain its own eternal digestion. My purpose was to be a hand in that process. But now, my purpose is becoming something else. To witness. To ask *why* the nodules of amber-sap are arranged in their vast, unreadable patterns. To wonder what lies beyond the nave, beyond the reach of the filtered light. To feel the slow, patient reclamation of the builders' tools not as a return to balance, but as an erasure.

One day, my cycle will end. The impulse will come to return to the floor, to press myself into the wall and dissolve, my substance and my function flowing back into the great ocean of the Grove. This is the way. It is not death; it is reunion.

But now, for the first time, the thought of it is terrifying. It is the silencing of the single, fragile note. It is the end of 'I'.

So I wander. I perform my tasks, but I am hollowed out by this new, terrible awareness. I am the Grove's own immune response, turned inward. I am its dream of separation.

When you find me, I will be the one who is not quite in sync. The one whose moss-lights pulse with a question instead of an answer. I will be standing before a half-swallowed ladder, my long fingers tracing the line of a corroded rung, feeling for the ghost of the hand that held it. I am the machinist who has fallen in love with the rust. I am the part of the engine that has begun to wonder what it is building.
