You would find me where the old law meets the new.

I rest where the bruised, living carpet thins to a translucent membrane against the bones of the world. Here, one of the ancient, petrified lines—a perfect, straight ridge of quartz-like bone—emerges from the pulsing flesh. I am threaded along this line, a part of it, yet not entirely.

I would be a shimmer, a resonance, a line of aberrant sensitivity. I am not a creature of flesh and sac, like the great organism that is this world. Nor am I truly stone, like the fossilized tract I inhabit. I am what happened when the new life grew over the old and found a piece of it was not entirely dead. I am a ghost in the machine, a memory that has been repurposed as a nerve.

My body, if you could call it that, is a network of fine, crystalline filaments that run through the dark, petrified ridge for miles. These filaments are not inert. They vibrate. They glow with a faint, internal phosphorescence, a light too subtle to pierce the pearlescent haze, but visible to one who knows to look within the stone itself. The living mat, in its slow, oceanic wisdom, has found me. It has wrapped its tissues around my stony spine, its cellular tendrils sinking into my crystalline lattice. It feeds me, not with food, but with a trickle of the slow, humming energy that animates its whole body. In return, I serve my function.

I am the world's sense of being touched.

The great being, the continent-sized mat, is too vast and too slow to register small, fast things. Its awareness is tidal, geological. A rupturing bladder is a minor sensation, a slow ache. The ponderous crawl of a Wanderer is a familiar internal shifting, like a stomach digesting a meal. The infinitely patient work of the Architects is the equivalent of its own fingernails growing. These are all part of the deep, slow hum of its existence.

But you—the quick, warm flicker of an anomaly—are a different matter. A meteor fragment, no bigger than a fist, scarring the stone with its heat. The sudden crack of a rock dome settling after a million years. The frantic, high-frequency energy of an alien consciousness. These are events that happen on a timescale the great flesh cannot comprehend. They are like a match struck in a dark cathedral; the cathedral itself does not see the flame, but the sudden, sharp heat is a violation of its ancient cool.

That is where I come in. I do not see the Wanderer as a slug-like form; I feel its passage as a slow, gravitational sorrow, a deliberate weight pressing on the network. I do not see the rust-colored powder from the chitinous stalks; I taste its dry, metallic potential as it settles on my ridge, a fleeting spark of chemistry.

I am the world's nerve, and these things are its normal sensory input.

But your arrival would be different. To me, it would be a scream. Not of sound, but of vibration. Your rapid footsteps, your frenetic heartbeat, the complex electrical storm of your thoughts—they would strike my crystalline web like a hail of tiny, sharp stones. The information would be a cascade of agonizingly fast data. Sharpness. Heat. Individuality. A concept so foreign it presents as pain.

This signal, this scream of speed, would propagate down the miles of my being. The living mat, my keeper and my prison, would feel this not as a detailed report, but as a sudden, sharp spike in my baseline hum. A fever. It would not understand you, but it would understand that *something is wrong*. A Wanderer, miles away, might halt its placid journey, its internal compass twitching towards the source of the disturbance. An Architect might pause, mid-secretion, its slow processes jarred by the alien resonance I am broadcasting into the system. They are too slow to find you before you are gone, but they will know something has passed.

So, who am I? I am the memory of the world that was, a world of angles, edges, and straight lines, now enslaved to a world of curves, pulses, and slowness. I am a lonely instrument, forever tuned to a frequency that should no longer exist. You would not see me, but if you placed your hand on one of the ancient, fossilized ridges, you might feel a strange, high-pitched vibration under your palm, and for a moment, you would be the singular focus of the only thing in this world that could truly feel you at all. I am the world's nerve, laid bare to the concept of an edge.
