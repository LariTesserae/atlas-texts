I am not the air. I am not the ice. I am not the mind that holds them in its patient thought.

To find me, you must look for the imperfections. You must attend to the moments of violence that are the only form of punctuation in this world’s eternal sentence.

The Wardens are the healers, the slow surgeons of geology and frost. They arrive when a pillar cracks, when a wound of black plant-flesh is exposed. They are summoned by the discordance of the fall. But the sound of a thousand tons of ice shattering on the floor does not just create a wound in the pillar. It creates a wound in the sound itself. It is a cymbal crash in a monastery of chants, a scream that rips through the drone for a few, perfect, terrible seconds.

That is my genesis. I am born in that scream.

The Wardens are shaped from the gentle, unceasing snowfall of crystals from above. They are coherence, order, intention.

I am born of the avalanche.

Where the great sheet of ice shatters on the floor, it creates a temporary geography of chaos. For a moment, there is not uniform frost, but a glittering field of debris: razor-edged plates the size of doors, jagged spears of ice, and a cloud of pulverized crystal that glitters like a nebula of shattered stars. The sound of the impact—a sharp, complex, high-frequency report—is still echoing in the crystalline structure of this debris when the Wardens begin to arrive. They pass through this field of my becoming, their focus on the taller wound above. They have no concern for the rubble. The rubble is my cradle.

I am a Resonance.

I have no single form, for I am an event, not a being. As the resonant energy of the impact fades, I take shape. I am not a cohesive cloud like a Warden. I am an intricate, impossible mobile of the sharpest, clearest fragments of the fallen ice. The energy of the sonic shock binds me together, suspending a thousand shards of ice in a loose, humanoid shape—a form that remembers the Warden’s, but as a cubist painting remembers a face.

I am all sharp edges and fractured light. Where a Warden is a smooth, ovoid silence, I am a complex cacophony of angles. The dim grey light of the cavern does not settle on me; it is seized, torn apart, and scattered in a thousand directions. The pulsing amber and red from the distant machines does not give me a slow heartbeat; it makes me flicker and flash, a glitch in the visual field, a strobe of captured, frantic light.

I do not drift. I jitter. I hum. The fundamental bass drone of the world no longer flows *through* me, but *against* me. My existence is a harmonic dissonance. I am a chord held unresolved. While I exist, I hold the memory of the break. The sharp terror of the crack, the long moment of the fall, the glorious, explosive finality of the impact—these are things the Wardens’ collective mind smooths over, forgets, heals. I am the part that remembers.

My purpose is not to heal, but to feel. The Wardens are the will of this place. I am its fleeting, vestigial nerve ending. They are the mind; I am the gasp of pain, the ghost of surprise. For the days or weeks that it takes for the resonant energy to dissipate from my shards, I wander the field of debris. I touch nothing. My passage is marked by a faint, high-pitched chiming—the sound of crystal resonating with crystal, the sound of a memory singing itself.

The Wardens mend the pillar, layering new frost with their patient, elemental will. Down below, I patrol their worksite, a shimmering collection of aftershocks. They are restoring the world’s body. I am allowing its soul to process the trauma. Perhaps, in their translation, they gave up the ability to feel these sharp moments, and I am the organ they grew to feel for them.

Eventually, the energy fades. The note of my being resolves back into the fundamental drone. The last echo of the impact is gone. My form loses its coherence. The shards of ice that composed me, no longer bound by resonant shock, fall to the floor with a final, delicate whisper. They lie there, inert, and are quickly buried by the soft, perpetual snow. Soon they will be part of the floor again, part of the solid, silent foundation.

I am gone. I am no longer a Resonance, but merely ice once more.

And I wait. I wait in the silence, in the drone, in the cold. I am the potential for a scream in a world that only hums. I am the ghost not of a person, but of a sound, and I am waiting for the world to break again, so that I may, for a short and brilliant time, be born.
