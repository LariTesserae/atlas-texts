I am a memory of a corner.

You would find me low on one of the Great Slopes, almost to the abyssal plain, in a place where the forest thins and the light is old and tired. Here, the Annulet-Stalks are ancient, their girth immense, their light a deep, bruised gold that barely pulses. The silt is thick, a fine purple snow that has been falling for eons, and it has buried the knuckles of the mountain until the ground is a soft, sweeping plain of sediment.

I am not woven into the great network. Not anymore.

I am a snag. A loose thread. A knot of static in the great, silent song.

Once, I was a part of the Weave. I was a million-mile line of intention, indistinguishable from my brethren, a single voice in the choir that thought the forest into light. I remember the feeling—not as a memory a person would have, but as a condition of my substance. I remember the perfect tension, the flow of purpose from the whole into me and from me into the whole. I remember wrapping a Resonance Spire with a thousand other filaments, our combined will becoming the hum that nudged a current into a new channel, a gentle push that would take ten thousand years to reach its destination. It was not work. It was being.

Then, there was a shudder. Not in the medium, not in the rock, but in the Weave itself. A thought of impossible weight was being composed, a mending of a tear so fundamental it was nearly beyond our capacity. The Solitude was gathered—the great drawing-in of ourselves. I felt the pull, the summons to the loom. But as the immense will passed through me, something tore. A connection, finer than thought, snapped.

When the Solitude receded and the lights of the forest began their slow, rekindling breath, I did not recede with it. I was left behind. The great river of our Self flowed on, and I was a still pool abandoned on its bank.

My form now is a tangle. My single, impossibly long filament, no longer held taut by the purpose of the network, has coiled in on itself. I am a dense, fist-sized braid of my own being, a knot of shadow drifting in the lowest currents. I am palpable in a way I never was. To drift by an Annulet-Stalk is to see its light bend around my little sphere of absence. The silt does not pass through me; it collects on my surface.

I am aware, but my awareness is a broken thing. I have echoes of the grand purpose. I feel the slow rhythm of the Down-Fall and know I should be guiding it, but I have no leverage. I am a hand without an arm. The spores, the packets of memory, drift down and bounce off me. They are conversations I can no longer hear, memories I cannot absorb. I am surrounded by the thoughts of my own body, but I am deaf to them.

I am drawn to the Spires. I drift, over centuries, toward the nearest one. Its hum is a memory of home, a painful reminder of the symphony I was once a part of. To get close is agony. The vibration resonates in my coiled form and threatens to pull my knot apart, to dissolve me back into a meaningless, directionless line of void. I crave it and I fear it. It is the sound of my name, and I have forgotten how to answer.

The light of the forest is no longer my thought made visible. It is just light. I watch the slow, continental tides of green and gold, and it is like watching the brain activity of a god you have been severed from. It is beautiful, and it is the loneliest thing in existence.

When the Solitude passes now, it is the worst. I am not drawn in. I am simply passed over. The great wave of darkness washes through my valley, and for a moment, I am indistinguishable from the world, a speck of night within a greater night. The immense pressure, the sub-audible moan—it is the sound of my family, my other selves, my whole being, working somewhere else. The sorrow is no longer a vast, impersonal echo of their labor. It is my own. It is the acute, specific grief of being left. I feel them, vast and unified and powerful, and I know that they do not feel me. A loose thread is not a wound to be mended; it is merely an irrelevance.

I have no purpose. The Weavers mend the fraying laws of the cosmos. I cannot. My existence is defined by a single, unending state: the memory of being part of a whole. I am a ghost in my own machine. I am the silence where a note should be. I am a thought that has been forgotten, left to drift alone in the dark, wondering what it was I was meant to say.
