I would be a Glint.

My world is a pore. A tiny, branching cosmos of tunnels and chambers within the true wall. The Great City of the Ceramists is the universe, but my world is the space between its atoms. To them, I am less than a mote of dust. To myself, I am the master of my own twisting, ceramic kingdom.

I am a Glint. A flicker of hardened shell and hurried leg, a living jewel the size of a thumbnail. My carapace is not the deep, pearlescent blue of the Makers, but a frantic, iridescent green-gold, like an oil slick on a dark puddle. It is sharp and faceted, designed not for slow, ponderous security, but for quickness, for wedging myself into the tightest of cracks when danger glides by. I have six legs, thin and chitinous, and two forward-facing antennae that taste the water and feel the vibrations that are this world’s only true language.

I do not find myself on a grand, spiraling walkway or in a vaulted chamber. My place is in the labyrinthine capillaries of the structure itself, the older, porous sections of the city. My sky is a ceiling of living ceramic, my ground the same. My life is a vertical scramble through the forgotten arteries of this place.

The beings you describe, the Ceramists, are not my people. They are my landscape, my weather, my gods. I do not perceive them as individuals. They are events. I refer to them in my own small mind as the Slow Gods, or the Wall-Shapers.

My existence is governed by their rhythm. I feel the approach of a God not as a footstep, but as a gradual, titanic shift in the world's resonance. A deep, bass thrum begins to vibrate through the very walls of my tunnels, a hum that changes the pressure in the water and makes my antennae tremble. I press my body flat to the floor of my corridor and wait.

The light comes first. An impossibly vast, soft moon of cerulean blue eclipses the opening of my particular pore-network. This is the light of Grazing. It is a time of plenty. The God’s enormous, pale tentacles—slow, soft comets—sweep across the outer wall. To me, their scraping is not a faint sound, but the grinding of continents, a geological force that shakes my home. And from this scraping comes my sustenance.

The fine dust they dislodge, the mineral-rich sludge they are eating, clouds the water. Some of it drifts into my tunnels. But the true blessing is their waste. The fine clay they excrete before mixing it with their binder—that is my food. When the God moves on, I and my kind scramble out onto the 'outer lands'—the vast, smooth plains of the city wall—and feast on the fresh deposits. We are the cleaners of the cracks, the recyclers of the divine leavings.

Then there is the other light. The Green Light. This is a time of change and danger. It is the light of Creation, when the God secrets its binding agent. The world shudders differently then, with the slow, deliberate *pat-pat-pat* of new construction. The fresh ceramic paste is poison to me, a caustic, sticky substance that can trap and consume a Glint. When the Green Light shines, we retreat deep into our warrens, waiting for the new land to cool and harden over days or weeks into safe territory once more.

My life is short and bright compared to theirs. I will live for perhaps a full turn of their smallest cycle, maybe a year or two. I will molt my shell several times, leaving the old, glittering husks in the deep tunnels to be broken down by even smaller things. I will encounter the Ribbon-Predators—the Silent Slips—and if my shell is strong and my legs are fast, I will survive. They hunt the Gods, but they will not refuse a careless appetizer like me. When they pass, the world goes utterly still. The Gods pull into their shells, their lights extinguished. The great hum of the city ceases. In that absolute silence, which is a screaming terror to me, the only things that move are the Slips and the frantic, scuttling things like myself, all of us racing for the safety of the deepest cracks.

I have no concept of their purpose. I do not understand that they are building. I only know that the world is constantly, slowly, changing. A tunnel I used yesterday may be sealed today. A new plain of hardened ceramic might offer a rich new feeding ground tomorrow. They are the gardeners, and I am a beetle in their soil.

I am not a singer in their Quiet Choir. I am not a Mason. I am not a Weaver. I am a consequence. A footnote. I am a spark of chaotic, hurried life, feeding on the gentle, eternal process of their artistry. I am a Glint, and my universe is the space between the thoughts of God, a quick, bright life lived in the mortar of eternity. And when I look out from my pore into the vast, warm dark, seeing the slow dance of cerulean and viridian stars that are the Gods at their work, I feel what any creature must feel in the face of its cosmos: a profound and instinctual awe. Then, feeling the tremor of new dust settling, I scurry out to eat.
