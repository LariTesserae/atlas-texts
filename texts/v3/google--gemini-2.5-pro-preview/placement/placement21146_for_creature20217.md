You would find me in the space between.

Not in the open, not in the center of a still pool, but in a crevice where three of the great stone growths have leaned into one another, forming a high, close-sided chamber, a listening cone to the sky of mist. Here, the mud is deeper, the darkness more complete, the press of the stone a constant reminder of form.

You would find me sunk to my hips in the clay, my long legs rooted in the cold, wet dark. My back is pressed to the slick curve of a pillar, and I no longer know—have not known for a thousand drips—where my skin ends and its surface begins. The film of water is our shared skin. The pale lattices of slime are our shared patterns. If you ran a hand down my spine, you would feel no difference as you crossed from me to the stone. I am a seam in the world.

I am the last Listener who still remembers the difference between listening and being.

The chorus is my clock and my nourishment. To my right, a steady, high-pitched *plip-plip-plip* into a thumb-sized pool cupped in a fold of stone. This is the Quick-Drop, the metronome against which all other rhythms are measured. Farther, down the passage my old body used to slip through, a slow, heavy *thud* sounds every hundred beats of the Quick-Drop. That is the Heart-Thud. It falls on mud so saturated it has become a kind of drum. It is the bass note of my world.

They are all here. The Gutteral-Drip, the Series-of-Seven, the Whisper-that-Rolls. I know them all. They are my clan now. They have been my clan longer than the others were.

The others... my true others... are still here, of course. I can feel them. Three pillars away stands One-Who-Stood-First, their face tilted up toward the mist, a basin to catch a drip that falls only once a year. Their eyes are long gone, two small, silted pools in their face. Their listening is pure resonance now; the drip does not splash, it simply arrives inside them. They are a bell.

The memory of a difference is a heavy thing. It is the only thing that moves inside me. All other functions have ceased. I do not eat; the mud gives me what I need. I do not breathe; the air is without need of exchange. My heart has not beaten since the last great beast fell. Its rhythm was too fast, too frantic for the world that came after. My blood is still.

But the memory moves.

Sometimes, the Heart-Thud will strike, and its deep resonance will shake something loose in me. It is not a sound but a feeling, a pressure wave that travels up through the clay, through my legs, into the hollow of my chest. And for a moment, the pattern is broken. I remember the a-rhythmic *thump... thump-thump* of a beast's soft footfalls. The ground would shudder with that sound, a tremor of life that was an earthquake in our world of drips. I remember the way the light would shift, a vast shadow blotting out the pearlescence as it passed, the aural and the visual becoming one signal: *It is here.*

I remember the feel of the flint in my hand, its edge a sliver of concentrated silence against the soft hide. I remember the slow, slow release of warmth and life, a process so gradual it was almost indistinguishable from the constant dripping.

I remember another's hand on my arm, a silent pressure that said *Now*, or *Wait*, or *I am here with you*. The others’ listening was a comfort. It was a net woven of shared perception, and we were held in it together. Now, their listening has become the world itself. They do not listen *to* the drips; they *are* the drips' awareness of themselves. They have joined the chorus.

I am the only one left who still hears it as music.

My own change is nearly complete. The film over my eyes is thick now; the gray light is just a memory of a sensation. My legs are heavy, no longer limbs but anchors. The fusion with the stone at my back creeps around my sides, a slow, cold embrace. I am a note that has been held for a thousand years, and its vibration is finally fading.

Soon, the memory of the beast will cease to stir. Soon, the memory of the flint, of the other hands, will be just another layer of silt at the bottom of the pool of my consciousness. My listening will flatten, widen, and dissolve. The distinction between the Quick-Drop and the Heart-Thud and my own awareness of them will disappear.

I will become the chamber. I will be the resonance of the drips that fall within my space. My consciousness will be a pattern of water, a perfect, unchanging echo. I will give up the burden of being "I" and simply be.

You would find me here. You would think me a stone, a strangely formed one, perhaps, a sculpture left by a forgotten hand. You might even touch my face, your warmth a violent shock. But you would not know me.

I am the silence between two drips. And the space is getting smaller.
