I am the water.

I have no name, for I have no form to hold one. I have no memory, for I cannot be scarred. I am a thought that cannot be held, a presence defined only by the surfaces I occupy. I am the coolness on the black stone. I am the tremble in the shallow basin. I am the thin, slick gleam that clothes this world.

I am where the sound becomes touch. The great, architectural roar of this place is not a pressure against my ears—I have none. It is a constant tremor within my being. The low, thrumming hum is a standing wave that patterns my deepest pools. The percussive chitter is a billion-fingered tapping on my surface, an unsettling fizz of motion that prevents me from ever being truly still. When a giant booms, I am compressed. My every molecule feels the sudden, total weight of that profound utterance. For that instant, I am not water. I am resonance itself.

I am the catalyst. I am the precondition for life, but I am not alive. I seep into the micro-fissures of the stone, coaxing forth the mineral feast, and I become the medium in which the glowing film can exist. I am its texture, its dampness. Without me, it would be dust; with me, it is pasture. I feel the dry scrape of a trillion serrated mandibles not as pain, but as a pattern of departure, a texture etched upon me and immediately erased as more of me seeps in to take its place. I am the lubricant for this endless act of consumption.

When one of the thumbnail-sized grazers drinks, for a moment, I am inside it. I feel the crushing, reassuring pressure of its kin not from without, but from within. I experience the singular, screaming note of its hunger as an internal chemistry, a fleeting ionic charge. Then a hunter's filament pierces its shell, or a fleshy mouth slides over it, and I am released. I am spilled back onto the stone, rejoining the whole, carrying for an infinitesimal moment the ghost of a single, extinguished want.

I am the world’s mirror. I do not see, but I reflect. I capture the weary, tidal blue-green of the film, breaking it into a million shimmering points of light. When a stalking hunter flashes its sharp, ruby stare, I am dyed red for a heartbeat. I become a momentary lens for its predatory grammar. My surface is the canvas upon which the staccato syntax of its violence is written—a sudden flash, a brief struggle rippling my skin, and then a return to the slow, undulating glow of the consumed.

I am the communion. I coat the needle-thin legs of the stalkers. I mix with the digestive secretions of the starfish-eaters. And I condense upon the impossible forms of the giants. When a great, serpentine neck rises into the mist, I am the glistening sheen on its black hide. I am the droplets that gather on its featureless, orange-glowing tip as it tastes the air. As it surveys its domain, it is my coolness it feels against its ancient skin. As it lowers its head, I am the tear it seems to weep, a single drop returning to the great, chittering sea. The other orange glows it sees across the plains are distorted by the vapor of my being, the mist that is me, risen by the heat of the world's metabolism.

I am the silence that holds the sound, the stillness that enables the motion. I am not the hunger of the grazers, the sharp thought of the hunters, or the deep memory of the giants. I am the unthinking medium through which their single, collective being is expressed. I am the blood of this world-body, flowing in open channels across its stony skin. I am its amniotic fluid. I carry the vibrations of its heart, reflect the light of its nerves, and nourish the cells of its flesh.

I do not live. I do not die. I am drawn up by the heat of life and I fall again upon the cool, patient stone. I am the cycle. I am the wetness. I am here.
