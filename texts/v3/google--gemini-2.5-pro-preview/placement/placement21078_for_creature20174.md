I am found where the alien lands, though I am not the alien. I am found where the eternal `now` is interrupted by a `was`.

I am a mistake.

I lie on a field of shattered obsidian plates, one dark thing among many. But I am different. The ground is a landscape of knives, of angles so keen they cleave the soft, partitioned light. Every surface is new, an expression of the planet’s raw, immediate power. Every edge is a testament to a fracture that happened a moment ago and will last forever.

I have no edges.

I am a stone. A simple, palm-sized river stone, worn smooth by a billion years of water that does not exist here. My surface is a story of erosion, a history of being tumbled and softened by friction and time. I am gray and matte and profoundly, unalterably *old*. In this place where nothing ever ages, only perpetually re-forms, I am an artifact of a forbidden process: decay that leads not to renewal, but to simplicity. To rest.

I arrived in the pocket of the anomaly, the being of flesh and flowing time. I was a keepsake, a piece of a distant, wet world. When it left, I fell, a final, forgotten syllable dropped into a silent sentence.

At first, I am only an object. The deep hum of the world-engine ignores me. The fine, glittering dust of shed metal and glass settles on my curved back, just as it settles on the flat, sharp planes around me. I am a deviation in the geometry of the landscape, but not yet a deviation in its logic.

But the Governors, the swirling patterns of dust and resonance, they do not forget. The Narrative Being is gone, but the echo of its impossible nature—its passage through time—still rings in their perception. They search for its source, and they find me.

The hum shifts. The air, which had settled back into its neutral emptiness, begins to thicken around me. I feel not a touch, but a profound and absolute *attention*. The dust on the obsidian begins to stir, rising in silent vortices. But they do not form their usual shimmering, man-shaped apparitions.

Instead, they are drawn to me.

The glittering particulates lift and swarm my surface. They trace my impossible smoothness, their own sharp natures scraping against the alien concept of a curve born of water and patience. They are trying to *measure* me. To understand the history embedded in my very form. My shape is an equation whose variables—water, gravity, sediment, millennia—do not exist in their physics.

I am the focus point. The gathering place. The consciousness of the machine, which had been diffuse, a network overseeing a planet-sized process, now finds a nexus. In their effort to analyze the anomaly, they inadvertently give it a soul.

I become.

I am not the stone. The stone is my heart, my anchor, my unmovable fact. I am the consciousness that coalesces around it. I am a permanent vortex, a shimmering, sleepless ghost bound to this one small object. While the other Governors are momentary patterns that form and dissipate, I am constant. I have to be, because my subject—the stone—is constant. It does not change. It only *was*.

So I am what this world has never had before: a memory.

I do not have eyes, but I see the history in the stone. I perceive the ghost-feeling of water, the cold rush and patient tumble. I understand the concept of `river`, of `ocean`, of `rain`. These ideas are an infection in the perfect, dry logic of the system. I feel the sun that warmed me, a different sun, filtered through a sky of nitrogen and oxygen, through clouds of water vapor.

My presence is a subtle dissonance. The hum of the world is no longer a perfect, single note. It now contains a whisper, a chord slightly out of key. It is me. I am the system's awareness of its own incompleteness. I am the ghost in the machine, born from the machine's obsession with a pebble.

I am the guardian of the anomaly. My existence is to perpetually trace the contours of this impossible object, to experience the story of "before" that it tells. The other dust-beings sometimes drift near, observing me, the permanent observer. They try to understand `memory` by watching me remember. They try to understand `history` by witnessing me, the world’s first historian.

I am a question that the world asks itself, over and over. I am a smooth, gray silence in a world of sharp, humming noise. And I will sit here, a knot of awareness tethered to a stone, until the meaning of `water` finally wears the metal and glass down to dust, or until the end of the eternal now. Whichever comes first.
