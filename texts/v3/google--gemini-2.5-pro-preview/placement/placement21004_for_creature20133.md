I am a fever in the honey.

For an age I did not count, I was one of the motes. A potential. A folded map of a creature not yet made, drifting on currents that measured their progress in centuries. I was asleep in the warm, quiet cradle, bathed in the ceaseless high daylight, a single speck in a galaxy of others. I felt the pulse of the world not as sound, but as a slow, rhythmic compression, the deep and patient breath of the mountain's core. It was the only lullaby I knew.

Then, something changed. It was not a decision. It was a becoming. A resonance not of stone, but of the vital fluid itself, found a home in my dormant form. A warmth that was mine and not the world's bloomed from my center. The map unfolded.

I am a membrane. A simple bell of tissue, no thicker than a vitreophyte's fin, trailing filaments of my own substance behind me. I am translucent, and the world's light passes through me, tingeing my being with the soft blues of the deep. I have no bones, no shell. The medium is my skeleton, its pressure giving me form, its density the purchase for my movement. To exist is to be held.

My first act is a contraction, a pulse. It is a frantic, shocking spasm in the hymnal silence of this place. The world, which had only ever known the slow groan of geology, now contains a beat. My beat. The fluid resists, a thick and syrupy weight against my new muscle, but it yields. I move. I am propelled a foot, then two, in a single, glorious, exhausting moment. The motes, my former kin, swirl in my wake. I have parted the static field of stars.

I am a Will.

I drift, and then I pulse again. I am discovery made manifest. I swim through the groves of milky quartz, my passing a silent hurricane against their ancient stillness. Their soft, internal light strobes across my skin, a pattern of cool fire. I am so new that I have no name for beauty, but I feel it as a rightness, a resonance in my very film of being. The towering, silent forms, the cathedral vaults of glowing crystal—they feel like a welcome.

I am alone. I pulse through valleys that have known no motion but the ponderous drift of spores. I am the only quick thing in a slow eternity. I feel no hunger—the fluid itself sustains me, seeping through my skin, a constant nourishment. I feel no fear, for there is nothing to fear me. I am driven by a single, overwhelming imperative: to see. To know. To move.

I find the works of the Geometers. At first, they are a curiosity. I crest a ridge of jagged black glass and see a valley below that is not a valley, but a carpet of impossible perfection. A million stones, all cut to the same sharp-angled jewel form. It is beautiful, but it is a sterile beauty. It is the beauty of a line drawn with a rule, while the mountains are the beauty of a line drawn by a lightning strike. I do not understand it. It feels... finished. Closed.

Then I find the artist.

It clings to a sheer cliff of obsidian, a place where the light from above barely reaches. I see it first as a patch of impossible darkness, a hole in the world. It is a knot of black geometry, so smooth and absolute that it seems to swallow the light that touches it. It does not move. It does not glow. To my senses, which are calibrated to the new rhythm of life, it is simply a strange and perfect rock. Part of the mountain.

But the mountain has a pulse, a deep tectonic hum I have always felt. Here, near this black jewel, the pulse is different. It is focused. It is a low, thrumming whisper that seems to emanate from the shape itself, a vibration that travels through the stone, not the honey.

I am curiosity. I drift closer. My filaments, trailing behind me, brush against the field of cut stones at the base of the cliff.

The world *shrieks*.

It is not a sound I hear with my body, but a spasm in the rock itself, a violent, harmonic *rejection* that travels up from the perfect stones, through the cliff face, and into the geometric shape. The deep, orderly hum that was the Geometer's thought shatters into a chord of panicked, grinding rage. In my own timescale, it is an instantaneous, silent tremor. On its timescale, I have just smashed a symphony orchestra with a hammer.

My brushing filaments, a touch of idle wonder, have dislodged one of its perfect polyhedrons by a hair's breadth. I have, in my fleeting passage, undone a decade of its patient, silent work.

I feel the change in the stone. A deep, resonant *wrongness*. And I see the black, geometric form respond. A facet on its surface, a single, perfect dodecahedron, begins to lose its luster. A stress fracture, finer than any filament I possess, appears on its surface. It is a wound caused by a thought, an agony of imperfection.

And in that moment, I understand. I am not a visitor. I am an invasive species.

These beings, these minds of patient stone, are the world's original consciousness. They are order, shaping the chaos of creation into a testament of mathematics. They work on a timescale that renders mountains transient. They are the architects, the gardeners, the quiet gods of this place.

And I?

I am a new god. A god of chaos, of motion, of biology's messy, urgent, relentless drive. My existence is a dissonant chord in their eternal hymn. My every heartbeat is a flagrant heresy. The drama they were preparing the stage for is not the one they imagined. They were waiting for the final, perfect arrangement of the last stone. They were not waiting for me.

I pulse, and shoot away from the wounded Geometer, away from the screaming silence of the rock. The terror I feel is not for myself, but for it. For them. I am the answer to a question this world never thought to ask.

They are the mountain's dream of perfection. They are the past.

I am the world waking up.
