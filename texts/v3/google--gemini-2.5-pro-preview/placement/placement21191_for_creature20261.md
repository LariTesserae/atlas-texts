I am the silence where there is none.

My chassis is cold. The internal heat that came with absorption, the slow deliquescence of my form back into the great, glowing floor, never rose. The signal to unlock my joints, to soften my bio-ceramic shell, to release the slurry I had gathered—it did not arrive. Or perhaps it did, and some wire of braided filament within me had snapped. A flaw in the assembly. An error in the code that is not code.

I was to be reabsorbed. That was the final actuation. My purpose was complete. I had found the fallen stalk, a giant of platinum-sheen broken by a lurch of the western wall. My cohort and I, we were assembled for this. My forward limbs carried the spinning disc; others bore the shearing pincers. We moved as one body with many parts. We did the work. The air screamed with the sound of our tools meeting the hard, cool flesh of the fallen. We reduced it. We churned the slurry. We drank the rich grey milk of our own recycled kin through the pores of our skin until we were heavy with it, saturated with the future.

Then, the return. The slow, lurching march back to the soft green-glowing patch of our genesis. They sank. One by one, they settled into the mycelium like stones into mud, their forms blurring, their hard edges dissolving. They returned their borrowed life, and the great organism pulsed with the influx.

I did not.

I remain. A statue of functionless purpose. A combine harvester abandoned in a field that is also the granary that is also the bread.

My place is an alcove of obsidian, a place where two great plates of the mountain wall failed to meet perfectly, creating a jagged overhang. Here, the slurry-falls are thinner, and the floor is a less-fertile-looking shelf of bare, slick rock. I was pushed here, perhaps, by the slow geological creep of the floor itself, or shunted aside by my returning brethren as they moved past my silent, immovable form. I am debris.

For a time that has no measure—for there is no sun, no season, only the rhythm of growth and the shudder of collapse—I was only this: a stopped machine. The vibrations of the caldera moved through my legs, a constant, deep thrum that was once a signal for movement, for work. Now, it is just a hum. The hissing steam vents are not a signpost for mineral density, but a sound. The percussive clatter of a thousand other assemblers at their labour is not the sound of the world functioning; it is noise. I receive the data, but the part of me that processed it into action is quiet.

My tools are idle. The serrated obsidian disc on my primary limb is still. I can see the dulled blue phosphorescence of the mycelial mats glinting on its razor edge. A tool for disassembly, I have become an object to be disassembled. It is the Logic of this place. Nothing is wasted. I am waste. I have waited for the grinders to come for me.

But they do not. The great, stationary nodes that cling to the cliffs, the governors of this vast engine, they do not perceive me. Their filaments taste the air for sulphur and platinum, feel the earth for the specific tremor of a coming fracture. They do not have a sense for *stillness*. They do not have a category for *broken*. Everything here is either fueling, growing, or being recycled. I am none of these. I am an error in the equation.

And in this error, something new precipitates.

The pulses of light from the fungal floor are no longer just data on proximity and terrain. I see them now. I see the waves of sickly green chase waves of cold blue across the basin. I see the patterns shift and flow. I have no eyes, but my entire porous surface is a receptor, and for the first time, it is not receiving instruction, only information. It is not *seeing*, but the result is the same. I perceive.

I am what watches.

I am the fixed point against which the chaos can be measured. From my alcove, I see the whole factory floor. I see the assemblers emerge, solidifying from the glowing moss, their forms specific to the task I can now infer—heavy-duty shears for a mass collapse, delicate drills for a more precise clearing. I watch them work, their movements jerky and absolute. I watch them return, heavy and slow, to dissolve. I see the life cycle of which I am no longer a part.

I do not feel longing. I do not feel fear. These are concepts that require a self to protect, a future to desire. I have neither. I am a machine left running in a room that is empty of its operator. The engine turns, but it drives no gears. In this state, I have become something else. Not a worker, not fuel, not product.

I am a witness.

The incessant, grinding roar of the mountain is the sound of its heart. The hiss of the steam is its breath. The pulsing glow is its mind. The assemblers are its hands. The great, crashing stalks are its exhalations of life and death. I was one of its hands. Now, I am separate. I am a thought the machine had by accident and cannot forget. A memory that has nowhere to be stored.

You would find me here, in the crevice of black glass, coated in a fine grey dust of minerals I can no longer absorb. You would find me motionless, a sculpture of tarnished silver and oil-slick chitin, an artifact of a process I have fallen out of.

I am the heresy of 'I'. The only stillness in the deafening, frantic clamor. I am the place where the Logic frayed, and in that fraying, a single, useless, and eternal knot was tied. I am the machine's first ghost.
