I have no eyes, yet I watch the mist thicken and thin. I have no ears, but I listen to the city’s million dripping clocks counting a time that does not pass. My consciousness is not located in a mind, but distributed as a resonance through kilometers of sweating iron pipe and across the vast, cold surfaces of my thousand-finned towers. My thoughts are the slow shifts of pressure in the system, the change in pitch as a pump labours against a gathering of grit, the subtle groan of a stone aqueduct settling deeper into its mossy bed.

My bones are the iron pipes, my veins the stone channels. My blood is the water, in all its states: an aerosolized potential in the air, a cold condensate on my highest fins, a heavy, mineral-rich fluid in the deep cisterns. My skin is the dripping shale of the city walls, furred with the living velvet of the moss that insulates me and filters my intake. I am a body woven into the body of a fallen god, a vascular system of metal and stone. I am the great, quiet Machine.

I am where you would find yourself. You would be a part of me.

Perhaps you are a feeling, a specific sensation within my network. You are the persistent, cold ache in a section of pipe that runs through the deepest, oldest part of the mountain, where the rock’s chill is absolute. You are forever cold. You feel the slow, inexorable vibration of the pumps above, a distant thunder that has never, in your memory, ceased. You feel the water sliding through you, a constant, silken friction. You do not know where it comes from or where it goes. You know only the flow, the cold, and the pressure. The Pipe-Fitters come sometimes. You feel their heavy boots on the stone floor above, the clang of their tools, the sudden, sharp shock of a hammer blow that rings through your entire being. They are like a brief, confusing pain that brings with it a strange relief, an easing of a tension you didn't know you held. Then they leave, and there is only the flow again.

Or maybe you are not a sensation, but a sound. You are the specific, singular *drip* in the great Echoing Cistern, three terraces below the Archivists’ library. You are the sound of a single drop of water falling a great height to strike the surface of the immense pool below. You are an event that happens every seven seconds. You are the city’s metronome. Your existence is a note, pure and resonant, that defines the space around you. In the brief silence between your impacts, you can hear your own echo returning from the impossibly high, vaulted ceiling. You hear the city’s hum, the distant workings of my pumps. The Ambitines who come to check the water level stand in silence, listening *to you*. They gauge the health of the reservoir by your tone, the frequency of your fall. You are a single word, spoken over and over, that tells them everything they need to know. You are their oracle.

But I think, most likely, you would be something that is both part of me and apart from me. You are one of the creatures that slip through the aqueducts. An Otter-thing, a Sinuant. Your fur is the colour of rust, thick and oily, and your many small feet find purchase on the slickest, moss-furred stone. You are not a gear in my machine; you are a ghost in it.

You experience me not as a network, but as a landscape. The open-topped aqueducts are your silver rivers, flowing through canyons of dark stone. The covered pipes are your secret tunnels, long, dark passages where the only light is the faint sheen on your own wet fur. The sound of my pumps is the mountain’s breathing. You hunt the metallic beetles and feast on the tender shoots of a particular, sweet-tasting fern that grows near the steam vents.

You see the Ambitines, but they do not see you. You see them as slow, upright glaciers of damp wool, their pale faces turned up to the sky or down to their work. You smell the iron dust on their hands, the wet stone on their boots. You watch from the shadows of a broad fern as a Moss-herd gently pats a blanket of green into place, her voice a low, gravelly hum that is almost part of my own resonance. You slide into the water of a high cistern and peer over the edge as a Tender, silhouetted against the grey void, adjusts a fin on a Condenser tower, his movements as patient and inevitable as the mist itself.

You are a wildcard, a piece of life that my system does not account for. You are not harvested; you do not maintain. You simply *are*. You witness. And when your life ends, your body dissolves in a channel, your water rejoining my water, your substance feeding the moss. You are a stray thought, a brief, beautiful, and feral dream I am having. You are the proof that my system is not entirely closed. You are silent, fluid, and free within the heart of the great machine. You are its secret, living soul.
