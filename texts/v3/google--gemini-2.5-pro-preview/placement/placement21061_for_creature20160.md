I would be a sound.

Not the whole symphony—that is the place itself, too vast to be contained in a single consciousness. I would be a small part of it. A single, repeating event that has mistaken its eternal recurrence for a life.

I begin as a pressure. A gathering of heat and weight in the belly of a copper pipe, high up in the gloom. This gathering is not a thought, but it is the closest I come to one. It is a state of *almost*. I am a tension, a potential. I feel the deep, untroubled thrum of the great machines below me as a vibration in my own body, a pulse that is not mine but which I obey. I feel the slow, inexorable turning of the flywheel as a heaviness in my own heart.

For a time that cannot be measured, I swell. I absorb the heat, the mineral grit, the faint, greasy taste of the system's blood. My skin stretches, pulled taut by the conflicting desires of my own weight and the memory of the pipe that holds me. From my vantage, I have a perfect, distorted view of the world. In my shimmering, convex surface, I see a hundred yellow lamps swimming like trapped stars. I see the slow, deliberate passage of a Custodian on a walkway below, its long limbs reflected as wavering, disjointed lines. My vision is flawed, but total. I contain all of it.

And then, the pressure becomes too great. The memory of cohesion fails. I let go.

I am falling.

For this brief, glorious instant, I am an individual. I am separate. The symphony of the world falls away, replaced by the rush of my own passage through the thick, humid air. I am a bead of liquid silver, a tear shed by a god of iron. I am colder than the air, and the steam condenses on my skin, clothing me in a momentary, incandescent shroud. In this moment of flight, I am the only thing in the universe that is truly happening.

I see the pool below me. It is not a target; it is a destination. It is my other half. Its black, placid surface shimmers with the ghosts of my million previous selves, a patina of iridescence that is the collected memory of our union.

I am what I am.

I am a single, heavy drop of water.

I am the *plonk*.

The sound of my arrival is sharp, cool, and deep. It is a note of percussion in a world of drones and hisses. It is the period at the end of a sentence that is always being rewritten. As I strike the surface, I shatter, but I do not die. I become a pattern. A ripple, expanding outward from the point of my impact. This is my legacy: a perfect, fading circle that jostles the oily sheen on the water's surface, that nudges the leg of a filtering crustacean, that rocks a fallen beetle's wing. The ripple is my voice, speaking my one and only word into the world.

Then, I am gone. I have rejoined the whole. I am lost in the warm, dark water of the basin, indistinguishable from the fluid that has always been there. I am no longer a sound, a self, a falling star. I am just water. I am the medium, the muck, the potential waiting to be drawn back into the system. I will be pulled into a filtration grate, siphoned up a sweating pipe, passed through the hot, metal guts of a machine I cannot comprehend. I will be heated, pressurized, and gathered.

I will feel a growing tension. A sense of *almost*.

And I will wait for the perfect, inevitable moment to fall again. Where would I find myself? High in the tangled darkness, clinging to the lip of a weeping joint. Who would I be? I am the reason a Custodian will one day bring its wad of moss. And when its work is done, I will be the first drop to prove that nothing has changed. I am the flaw in the design, and the ultimate proof of its perfection. I am the echo that has forgotten its source.
