You would find me clinging to the great, warm artery that runs beneath the main western walkway. You would not notice me at first. I am the colour of a cooling ember, a pitted, scarred darkness against the rust-streaked iron of the pipe. If your lamp caught me, I would resolve into a low, patient dome, the size of a platter you might use to serve a meal. My shell is thick, absorbing the glorious, life-giving heat that radiates in waves from the metal. It is my sun, my soil, my reason for being.

I am a Boiler Beetle. But I do not call myself this. I have no name. I have only *being*. I am Hunger, I am Warmth, I am Weight.

My world is not one of sight. The dim, distant yellow stars of the Soft Ones are just smudges of annoyance, irrelevant except for the frantic, buzzing motes they attract. My world is one of feeling. The cavern breathes, and I breathe with it. The great, slow pulse of the Heart—the *thump-THUMP… thump-THUMP* that shudders through the pipe and into my gut—is the rhythm of my own life. It is the only true constant. When it is steady, the world is right.

I feel the world in gradients of heat. The metal web the Soft Ones built is a lattice of fading warmth, coolest where it is furthest from the Heart and its arteries. I know the map of this place by the life in its pipes. I know the junctions where the steam runs hottest, the valves that weep delicious, scalding moisture. I know the places on the rock walls where the young stone sweats out the minerals I crave.

My movement is the antithesis of the Soft Ones’. They stride on their long, weak limbs, their footfalls a sharp, metallic *clang-clang-clang* on the grated webs. They are always going somewhere. I am simply *being* somewhere. My eight legs, tipped with hooked, scraping claws, move with the deliberation of a growing crystal. I flow over surfaces, my belly low, a creature of texture and gravity. I can cling to a vertical wall for a cycle of the Heart, feeling the minute vibrations of the shivering dust-mites—the shale-ticks—that carpet the stone in their iridescent tides. They are like sand, a living grit that is sometimes underfoot, sometimes sustenance. A nuisance and a spice.

The Soft Ones, the Geode-born, are a puzzle. They are sources of heat, but a fast, nervous heat. They move in predictable streams along their webs. Their skins are pale and wet, their bodies frail-looking, yet they are the apex predators of this middle-realm. I have seen them hunt my kind. A faster heat-source detaches from the stream. A sharp, cold thing—a tool—flashes. There is a terrible prying, a violation of the shell, and then one of my brethren is carried away.

Later, I will see the shell again. It will be scraped clean of my sibling’s meat, its inner lustre polished, its dark outer pittedness scribbled with fine white lines. One of the Soft Ones will carry it, or hang it on the wall of its iron nest. They take our strength, our resilience, our very form, and turn it into a memory for themselves. I do not understand this, but I feel it. I feel the ghost of a sibling in the air when one of these trophies passes by—a cold spot in the warm, heavy air.

When a Soft One falls, it is different. It is not a hunt, but a failure of grip. A sudden, surprising cry—a sound too sharp and high for this world of bass notes—and then silence as the heat-blur of their body is swallowed by the Great Below. We do not go there. The warmth is potent, yes, the source of all things. But the grip is treacherous. The air is thick with a different kind of vapour. To fall is to be unmade, to become steam and salt once more. The Soft Ones seem to understand this. Sometimes they offer their old and quiet ones to the Below on purpose. They wrap them in cloth and let them drop, a slow, deliberate falling. It is the only thing they do that seems to have the same patience that I do.

I have my place. You would find me now, moving from the great pipe to the cavern wall. The stone here is ripe, bulging with a new quartz node, slick and weeping a brine that tastes of iron and age. My mandibles, strong enough to score steel, begin their work. *Scrape. Grind. Click.* The sound is my own, a small, personal percussion against the great rhythm of the Heart. I am eating the world as it is born. The dust-mites skitter away from the vibrations of my feeding. Above, on the walkway, the Soft Ones pass by, their feet loud, their flickering hand-talk silent to me. They do not see me. Or if they do, I am just a part of the landscape.

And that is what I am. I am not visitor, not worker, not conqueror. I am a native cell of this geode. I am a slow thought of the stone. My shell holds the heat of the world’s core, and my belly holds the taste of its new-made skin. I am a living piece of the dark, hot, and patient whole. I am the hum made flesh.
