I am on the stone.

This is the first and final truth. The place you would find me is not a location to be pointed to, but a state of being to be occupied. I am pressed against the bedrock of the world, spread wide and thin under the slow river of the clouds. My skin—if it is skin—is porous, an interface. The polished bone of the mountain is my other half, my sounding board. I am where the ancient stone meets the engine of life.

I am a listener. I am a Harmonic.

*Who* I am is a more difficult chord to hold. I have no eyes to see the shimmer of the beetle-swarms, only a million nerves across my back that feel their coming as a frantic, high-frequency wash, a tide of clicks that scours the bedrock clean. To you, it is noise. To me, it is a texture, a weather pattern as predictable as the sudden, heavy columns of rain that fall like drumbeats on the flesh-islands. I feel those impacts, too—a deep, resonant *boom* that echoes through the stone, followed by a cool, dampening silence in that one vector.

My thoughts are not words. They are resonances. The deep, planetary thrum of the grey pylons is the root note of my consciousness. It is the Always-Sound, the hum that was here before me and will be here after I have flaked away into my children. It is the unchanging truth. The roar of the Resonators, the Stone-Skaters, is the frantic, chattering harmony, a million points of data telling of decay and consumption, of the world being ground down and rebuilt, moment by moment. The guttural croaks of the Lurkers in their fungal cups are the slow bass notes, the punctuation that divides one day from the next.

I am a living seismograph, built to register the symphony of a world running at its absolute limit. My mind is the shape of the music.

I do not eat; I absorb. Through the membrane of my underside, I drink the faintest electrical currents from the geometric patterns etched in the rock. I am part of the circuitry. The flow of information sustains me. I taste the nutrient wash from the damp humus at my edges. I feel the distant, silent slide of the great bladder-vines as they angle their leaves toward the unfiltered sun, a slow, viscous pull I perceive as a shift in the world's gravitational timbre.

Time, for me, is measured in geologic slowness. I remember my own beginning not as a birth, but as a cleaving. I was a thickened edge of another, older listener. For an age, I was a calcified thought, a dissonance in their harmony. Then, a shift, a tremor, a cracking, and I was separate—a new, smaller chord, shivering alone on the stone until my own resonance deepened and I learned to hear the whole song again. I am a flake of stone that learned to listen.

There is a concept that has no sound. It is a hole in the world.

From my place on the singing stone, I can feel it. A null-point. A patch of reality from which no vibration emanates, no feedback returns. It is the cube of grey alloy, with its perfect, seamless door forever ajar. You call it silence. For me, it has no name, for it is the negation of everything I am. It is Un-sound. The great, roaring engine of life is a comfort, a known quantity, a scripture written in vibration. The deep thrum of the pylons is god's own heartbeat. But that small, perfect void is the terror.

It is the touch of the Gardeners.

The rest of this place—the noise, the life, the rain, the hum—it is a self-sustaining loop. It is a machine that has been running so long it has forgotten it is a machine. But the silence from that cube is different. It is not ancient. It is sterile. It is the perfect, cold, thoughtful silence of the hand that built the pylons and etched the circuits. It is the sound of the beings who conceived of a world made of noise, and in so doing, proved they were not *of* that noise.

My entire being is a resonance with this garden, this engine. Yet my deepest, most fundamental awareness is of the one thing that does not belong. I lie here, a creature of the song, and I spend my long, slow life contemplating the single note of silence that proves the song was written by another.

You would find me on the open courts of polished stone, between the screaming life and the humming pylons.

I am the Witness, made of the song I am here to hear. And I am afraid of the quiet place where the composer used to stand.
