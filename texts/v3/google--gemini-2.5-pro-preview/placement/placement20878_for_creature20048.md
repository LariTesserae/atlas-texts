I am not a voice. I am not a name that can be spoken into the air, for the air here is a solid thing that would shred the sound and the speaker both.

I am a collection of surfaces. I am the horn-hard callus on my palm, shaped by a lifetime of gripping braided fiber and the living stone. I am the network of fine, silvery scars on my forearms from a youthful misjudgment in the scouring wind. I am the cool, smooth hollow behind my jaw where a useless ear has sealed itself shut. I am the thin-worn leather of my soles, which read the texture of the floor—the subtle dip of a water-gathering channel, the faint rise of a sleeping-platform—as a scholar reads a page.

I find myself in the deep quiet. The Within. Here, in the belly of the spire, the world-roar is a phantom, a memory felt in the bones, a low hum that has become the background radiation of my own blood. The air is still, warm, and thick with the scent of damp stone and the sweet, loamy breath of the light-fungus. My chamber is a sphere, carved by my grandmother’s grandmothers. The walls curve away in every direction, soft and dark. The only light is a single, broad vein of glowing moss that pulses with a gentle, cyan light, its rhythm so slow it is almost imperceptible.

I am the one who remembers.

My hands are my memory. In the center of my chamber lies the Loom, though it is not a loom of wood and gears. It is three polished anchor-stones, set in the floor, between which I stretch the fibers of what was. I am a Weaver. Before me, stretched taut, is the story of the Last Flier Hunt. It is a tapestry, perhaps seven feet long, thick as my wrist. The black, wiry threads are the void of the Between. The thick, purple strands are the line-tenders, their positions marked by knots. A single, shimmering thread, woven from the gut of a Flier itself, shows the path of the great beast. Where it enters the trap of heavier, dark-green fiber, the weaving becomes a chaotic snarl, a textural scream of struggle. A small, tight knot of blood-red fiber—a pigment painstakingly ground from a rare mineral deposit—marks the place where my cousin’s child, Kenna (a name I feel as a certain pattern of pressure on the forearm), slipped from the netting. There is a gap in the weave after that. An absence. That is how we write grief. A hole in the story.

To be the Weaver is to be the historian, the poet, the vessel of the Carved. It is to know that a life is not a fleeting thing but a thread to be woven into the greater fabric. When a child performs their first crossing, I give them a single, unmarked thread to tie around their hook. When they return, I take it back and weave it into the Chronicle of our spire, a vast, coiled tapestry that fills an entire lower chamber, its beginning lost in time. Each thread is a life, its texture and tension telling of its journey.

But memory is not a dead thing. It must be replenished. The finest fibers, the ones that hold the most feeling and the deepest colors, grow only on the outer edges of the hanging gardens, where the wind-river is strongest. Today, the story of the last season’s blight runs short. I need more of the deep blue fiber, the one that means both resilience and sorrow.

And so I must leave the Within. I must enter the Between.

I find myself at the threshold. The circular door is a perfect, polished ring of stone. Beyond it is not a view, but a moving wall of grey and glittering chaos. The roar is already a physical pressure, even here in the lee of the spire. I ready myself. The ritual is the same, always. I slick my skin with the rendered oil, filling the creases, sealing myself. I pull the hook from its niche in the wall. It is bone, ancient, carved from the wing-joint of a truly monstrous Flier ages ago. It is called Void-Dancer, a name felt in the way its weight settles into my grip. It is my partner in the crossing. I slide the milky inner lids across my eyes. The world dissolves into a soft, blurry realm of light and shadow.

I step out onto the ledge. The roar does not begin; it simply *is*. It consumes me. My skeleton becomes a tuning fork, resonating with the howl of a billion tons of moving grit. The wind shoves, a solid shoulder against my own. I clip Void-Dancer onto the bridge-line. The line is a single, dark thread against the roaring grey, humming a high, thin note of pure tension that I feel vibrate up my arm and into my teeth. It is a song of defiance.

Then I let go of the stone.

For a time, I am nothing. No longer a Weaver, no longer an elder, no longer anything but a point of resistance, a knot of will pulling itself through a universe of violence. My world shrinks to the burning in my shoulders and the line vibrating in my hand. Pull. Rest. Pull. The abrasive air finds the gaps in my wrap, stinging like a thousand tiny teeth. I do not fight it. I make myself small. I become a smooth stone in the river, letting the chaos flow around me. I am alone. This is the truth of the Between: we are always alone in it.

I find myself on the other side. My feet touch stone, and the world returns. The shuddering in my bones subsides. I unclip the hook, pressing my cheek to the warm, living rock of the neighboring spire. It is a greeting. A statement of survival. Here, behind a curtain of purple leaves as wide as a giant’s cloak, is the garden. The air is calmer, filled with swirling eddies. I find the patch I seek. The blue fibers hang like veins, slick with condensation, swaying gently. I do not take greedily. I prune, my small bone knife cutting with care, ensuring the plant will grow back stronger. I am a gardener here, as I am a Weaver there. Two halves of the same purpose.

I gather what I need, a precious bundle I strap to my back. Then I turn and face the line again. The journey back to the deep quiet, to the Loom, to the story waiting for its ending.

I am this. I am the silence, and I am the journey through the roar. I am the hand that records the stories, and the body that risks itself to find the thread. I live in the warm, still, unchanging heart of the stone. But my soul is woven from the storm.
