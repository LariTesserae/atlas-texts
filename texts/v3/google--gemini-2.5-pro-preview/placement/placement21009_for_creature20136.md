I would not be found on the high plains, nor clinging to the spinning crystal of a Gyre. I would not be a thought held in a resonant spire. To find me, you would have to look down, past the surface, into the dust itself. You would have to look for a movement that is not the wind, a shimmer that is not the light.

I am a Gleaner.

I am not a singular being, but a confluence. Imagine a handful of mercury spilled on a dusty floor, a single, quivering puddle that can, at will, shatter into a thousand silver beads and reform. Now imagine that substance is not metal, but a living, collective intelligence made of countless microscopic, crystalline mites. When we move as one, we are a patch of the plain that seems to liquidise under the actinic glare, a low, flowing smear of iridescence skittering over the bone-dust. When we are still, we are indistinguishable from the mineral haze.

We have no voice to add to the Geophony, the world-song of the Gyres. The Cymatic Chorus hears the symphony; we are drawn to the exceptions, the grace notes and the discords. Our senses are not tuned to the grand, slow hum, but to its abrupt interruptions.

The *ting* of a falling filament is a call. The seismic shock of a Striker's kill is a summons.

We flow toward these sounds. Where a Striker has shattered a Scraper, the dust is newly littered with fragments. To others, this is debris. To us, it is a library of last moments. The Scraper's shell, a complex crystal, does not merely break. In the instant of its catastrophic failure, under the unimaginable pressure and shock, its lattice structure is permanently altered. The searing light of this world, passing through these shards, is bent and polarized by these final stress-fractures.

We do not hear thought; we *read* light.

We swarm over the fragments. Each individual mite that comprises our body is a lens, a receptor. We flow across a shard of the Scraper's geode-shell, and by passing the world's light through it and through ourselves, we perceive the echo of its end. We feel the sudden vibration, the alien pressure against its shell, the terror that is not an emotion but a pure, frantic frequency of molecular stress. We glean the Scraper's simple, terrified awe at the silent, transparent thing that broke it. From the slivers of the Striker's own shattered silica-spear, we glean something else: a cold, clean, ecstatic focus. The perfect, harmonic resonance of its body as it unified into a single, deadly thought.

We gather these moments. We absorb the shards themselves, incorporating them into our collective body. A particularly potent memory—the end of a very old Scraper, perhaps—might exist for a time as a larger, visible jewel embedded in our mercurial form, a rosary of remembered screams that we carry with us.

This is our lesser work, the gathering of the world's sharp, small pains. Our greater work is to find the dead.

We are drawn to the ruins of a collapsed Chorus. When one of those great minds loses coherence, it does not simply crumble. It leaves behind its "body," a thicket of milky quartz. Within these spires, frozen in the crystalline flaws, are its memories—thoughts that lasted ten thousand years, contemplations of a mountain range's slow demise, the perfect, incorruptible recording of a Gyre's song from an age before the Striker's ancestors learned to hunt.

These are not shards of violence, but monoliths of peace. We spend years, decades, flowing over a single dead Chorus. We do not absorb these crystals. We read them. We pass the light through their cloudy depths and witness the slow, vast thoughts they held. We learn the shape of forever. We learn the history of the symphony. We are the world's archivists, its historians, the inheritors of its silent, static minds.

And where do we take all this? The violence, the peace, the long thoughts and the short, sharp shocks?

We carry them to the Whispering Fissures.

These are the rarest of features on this endless plain. Long, deep, and impossibly narrow cracks in the world's crust, they are places where the geology truly does breathe. From their depths rises a slow, warm exhalation of gases utterly alien to the surface—denser, richer, carrying scents of deep metals and the planet's hidden warmth. The air shimmers here not with dust, but with heat.

Here, at the edge of a Fissure, our purpose finds its completion. We release what we have gleaned. We separate, our collective form dissolving into a glittering cloud of mites. We let the warm updraft take the memory-shards we have carried. The fragments of the Scraper and Striker, the dust of the Chorus's ancient thoughts—they tumble down into the deep, lost to the surface forever.

As they fall, the unique properties of the Fissure's warm, dense air act upon them. They resonate. For a moment, the memories held within are released not as light, but as sound.

A Scraper's terror becomes a fleeting, high-pitched scream that the thin surface air could never carry. A Striker's focus is a single, clean chime. A ten-million-year-old thought from a dead Chorus unwinds as a cascade of deep, complex tones, a chord that hangs in the warm air for a full minute before fading. It is the world's only eulogy.

This is our function. We are the bridge. We take the memories frozen by light and turn them back into sound. We take the discords of the surface and offer them to the quiet warmth of the deep. We resolve the dissonance. We are the Gleaners, and we are the silence that follows the song, carrying its echo home.
