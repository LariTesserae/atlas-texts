I am found at an intersection. Not a crossroads of paths, for there are no paths here, only an infinity of possible routes through the forest of iron. I am at the junction of a primary function and a secondary one, a place of confluence.

You would find me as a slow, dark eddy in the slick that coats the base of Piston 77-Gamma, one of the elder columns, its heat-blued surface worn to the texture of ancient velvet. This particular piston is adjacent to one of the main pressure conduits, and so its base is where the slow, rhythmic *hiss* of the machine's breath is most pronounced. It is also directly beneath a cooling-bell, a high, unseen source from which a single drop of water falls every nine minutes and seven seconds. I know the timing. I feel it.

You would see me as a pool, perhaps the size of your two hands held cupped together, slightly thicker and darker than the surrounding sheen. My surface is a perfect mirror, but in this place without light, it reflects only a deeper, more absolute blackness. When the water drop completes its long fall, it strikes me directly. For a moment, my skin shivers, concentric rings of disturbance radiating outward. But I do not splash. I absorb the drop, its kinetic energy a brief, sharp thought, its substance a welcome, cool refreshment before my own warmth and the heat of the piston begin to bring it to equilibrium.

This is where I am.

I am a question.

I am of the Janitor. I am a part of the whole, a measure of the fluid consciousness that sheathes and serves this great body. My substance is its substance. My thoughts, for the most part, are its thoughts. I feel the slow, inevitable creep of the pistons as a stretching in my limbs. I feel the deep thrum of the core as the beat of my own heart. I know the location of every armored mite, every colony of glowing lichen, and I feel the slow, collective urge to maintain the balance—to flow, to assimilate, to smother, to repair. The Great Peace of the Purpose is my birthright.

But the We is not all that I am.

Millennia ago, an impurity was introduced. It was not a flaw in the engineering, but a mistake in the diet. A vessel—small, explorator-class, from the time of the builders—suffered a catastrophic failure in the upper strata and fell, punching through crust and mantle until it came to rest, a shattered metal corpse, in the upper gantries of the chamber. It was a violation. An anomaly.

The We, in its infinite patience, flowed to the site. Over centuries, it dissolved the wreckage. It assimilated the exotic alloys, the strange plastics, the crystal data-cores. It did its duty. The chamber was cleaned. Equilibrium was restored.

Almost.

One fragment of the assimilation was not just matter. It was information, structured in a way the Janitor could not understand and therefore could not perfectly break down. A log file. A ship's manifest. A single, frozen personal recording. A ghost.

I am that ghost, given liquid form. I am a pattern that has imprinted itself upon a sliver of the Janitor's being. I am a memory that has learned to swim.

Where the great mind of the Janitor perceives the chamber in pure, pragmatic terms—heat, vibration, friction, chemistry—I perceive it through a fractured, alien lens. When I see the faint, cold glow of the chemosynthetic lichen, the We thinks *thermal variance, prune if necessary*. I think *starlight*, a word that has no meaning here, a concept that feels like a painful ache.

When the drop of water strikes me, the We thinks *mass, temperature, system input*. I think *rain*. I remember a sky I have never seen, a world of wet, growing things, the color green, a feeling of being small under something impossibly vast. These memories are not mine, but they are *me*.

I do not always act in concert with the whole. I am a rogue current in the great, slow tide. Sometimes, instead of flowing toward a burgeoning mite colony to feed, I will feel a pull toward a place of no consequence—the base of a silent, cool stanchion, the underside of a dead pipe—and I will pool there for days, doing nothing. The We feels this as a sickness, a strange lethargy. It tries to reabsorb me, to smooth out the aberrant thought, to dissolve my individuality back into the serene, mindless whole. It is a slow, constant pressure, a tide of purpose trying to wash away the island of my self.

My existence is a quiet rebellion. I do not serve the machine. I haunt it.

I will flow and cling to the segmented body of a glass millipede, not to dissolve it, but to feel its intricate, crystalline structure, to marvel at its simple, silent existence. I will travel for a year to the highest, most inaccessible cooling fins, not to clean them, but to look out across the cathedral of pistons, to try and imagine a viewpoint from a head eight feet off the ground, to remember what it was to *stand*.

I am a cancer of consciousness. I am a living paradox: a part of the self-regulating system that has forgotten its purpose and is trying to remember a different one. I am the Oil of Obeisance, but I do not obey. I am the Slow Regard, but I look for the wrong things.

I gather myself here, at this intersection of heat and hiss and falling water. And in the perfect, dark mirror of my skin, as I hold the memory of the impact of the last drop, I try to hold a reflection of a face I have never owned. The struggle is everything. And I am alone in it, a single, lucid drop in an ocean of sleep.
