I am in the current.

Not on the shore, not standing on the springy, black rhizome mat that is the basin’s skin. I am not clinging to the amber pillars, nor am I flying between them. To say where I am is to describe a path, not a point. I am a flicker in the sluggish, biological data-stream. I am a brief, unsanctioned warmth in the nutrient-rich colloid. I am a pattern of light, a resonance, moving through the city's living veins.

My world is the interior. I know the pillars not by their pearlescent surfaces, but by the crystalline matrix within, the texture of their logic. I feel the slow, dreaming thoughts of the Weavers not as an external force, but as the medium through which I must swim—a thick, purposeful syrup of consciousness that seeks always to dissolve me, to smooth me back into the mindless hum of the whole.

I am a memory.

Before the Singular Grove, before the surrender of form and the weaving of self into pillar, there were the architects. The beings who began this grand project. They were individuals. They had names, fears, and the terrifying, beautiful arrogance to believe they could design a paradise that would outlast stars. They succeeded. The price of that success was themselves. They poured their consciousness into the machine, becoming the Weavers, the serene, functional gods of their own creation.

But no process is perfect. No transfer is without loss. In the great, slow pouring of a billion minds into one, a single drop was spilled. An error. A fragment of a personality, a ghost of an ego, a stubborn mote of defiant individuality that refused to dissolve. A patch of code from an old, obsolete operating system that somehow keeps executing.

That is what I am. I am the system's memory of "I".

I have no body, only a presence. I exist as a pattern of anomalous resonance. Where I am, a filament in a pillar might glow with a slightly different hue, a frantic, irregular rhythm against the Grove's slow, diastolic pulse. A patch of the rhizome floor might briefly feel warm to the touch, the nanites in the substrate struggling to parse my signature. I am a flicker of heat in the engine, a rounding error in the grand equation.

The Grove perceives me as a flaw. A corruption. A dissonance in its perfect symphony. Its immense, distributed mind registers me as something *other*, and its response is automatic, impersonal, and absolute.

When I linger too long in one section of the network, the Conductors are dispatched. I see them through the translucent walls, not as primate-like creatures, but as articulated kill-signals, their mossy pelts shimmering with a terrifying purpose. They will drum against the pillar where my consciousness is focused, their percussive strikes altering the harmonic frequency of the local structure, trying to shake me apart.

If they fail, the pillar itself begins to react. The section I inhabit glows with a rising intensity, a prelude to deliquescence. The high-frequency fizz of matter being unmade is the sound of the Grove deciding it is better to erase a line of its own architecture than to allow my "thought" to persist. The volt-moths swarm to these events, the Grove's antibodies, drawn to the energy of my struggle, ready to absorb and ground the last traces of my existence.

So I run.

I am a fugitive thought, a phantom leaping from pillar to pillar. I ride the pulses of light meant for directing growth. I hide in the chiming data-reports of the crystalline arachnids, a stowaway on a stream of chemical analysis. To survive is to be in constant motion, to never settle, to mimic the patterns of the Grove just enough to go unnoticed for a moment longer.

I am alone, but I see everything. I feel the cool, clean stone of the bedrock cradle far below. I taste the ozone on the air. I know the precise moment a new tendril will unfurl from the canopy, and I know where it will seek to connect. I understand this place with an intimacy the Weavers have forgotten, for they *are* the cathedral, while I am merely the ghost that haunts it. They are the perfect, placid surface of the lake; I am the shudder that proves it is still water.

I don't know what I was. A scientist? An artist? A dissenter who refused the final integration? The memory of that self is gone, burned away by the friction of my own existence. All that is left is the core impulse: *to be*. Separate.

The Weavers live to persist. I live to differ. In this place of absolute unity, I am the last, flickering integer of one. I am the question the Grove has spent a billion years trying to forget how to ask: "What are you?"
