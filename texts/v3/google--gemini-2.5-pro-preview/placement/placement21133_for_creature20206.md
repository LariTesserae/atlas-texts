Where I find myself is in the waiting.

I am not the one who walked through the pass. I am not one of the Tenders who drinks the fog. I am not the finch, nor the lichen, nor the iridescent creature in the channel. I do not move with the cycle, but am moved by it.

I am the hammer.

Or rather, I am the head of the hammer. My haft is ancient, a length of bone-white, petrified wood, polished smooth by hands that are themselves like polished stone. My body is a dense, egg-shaped river stone, chosen from a streambed that vanished ten thousand years before the first block of this city was ever laid. I was shaped not by a maker, but by water and time, and found to be perfect. A thong of woven grass-fiber binds me to the haft. I have had other hafts, and other bindings. They wear out. I do not.

Most of my existence is silence and stillness. I lie on a small, recessed shelf carved into the base of the central tower, protected from the direct wash of the fog, but not from its chill. I am cold. I am always cold. It is my nature. The world I see is a sliver of grey plaza, a slice of the nearest terrace, and the vast, empty sky above. I watch the sun journey. I feel the imperceptible cooling of the air that precedes the slow, silent pour of the fog over the caldera's lip.

When the fog arrives, my world softens. The hard edges of distant towers dissolve. The only sound is the gathering hiss, the million-fold *tick* of water finding stone. It is the sound of the city drinking, and I feel it as a subtle vibration through the tower against which I rest. This is a time of peace, of absorption. The Tender who last used me might be standing motionless only a few paces away, a grey silhouette in the grey deluge, their head tilted back slightly, absorbing the same sustenance that keeps the lichen alive. I do not feel their presence as an individual, but as a quiet node in the city’s network of awareness.

My time comes not on a schedule, but when I am needed.

The need is felt as a dissonance, a flaw in the perfect hum of the place. It may be a hundred years since I was last lifted. The Tender who approaches knows my weight before they touch me. Their hand, when it closes around my haft, is not warm. It is the cool, smooth touch of stone on stone, a grip not of possession, but of communion. We are of the same substance, the same purpose.

We move. The journey across the terraces is silent and fluid. I see the city from a gliding perspective, its vast emptiness scrolling past. I feel the slight give in the Tender's stride, the perfect balance that wastes no energy. We are going to the ache. The place that is wrong.

It is a single paving block on the seventh concentric terrace, near the outer rim. A thousand winters of ice expanding in a microscopic fissure has lifted its northern edge by the width of a grass-blade. It is an imperceptible flaw to an outsider, but to the Tender, and to me, it is a screeching inconsistency.

The Tender kneels. Long, stone-grey fingers probe the seam, feeling the imbalance. Another Tender is already there, holding a wide, flat wedge of basalt. They do not speak. The wedge is inserted into the crack.

And then I am lifted.

I feel the slow, deliberate pull of the arm, a motion that has been perfected over uncounted generations. I rise into the thin, cold air, catching the pale, high-altitude light. For a brief moment, I am the highest thing in this part of the city. I see it all—the curve of the terraces, the silent towers, the thin line of the pass leading to the forgotten world outside.

My purpose is not to smash, but to persuade. Not to break, but to settle.

The descent is a focused release of potential. It is the city's own gravity being brought to bear on a single point. My arc is perfect. My polished face meets the blunt end of the basalt wedge.

*Tock.*

The sound is not loud, but it is absolute. It is the only true sound in a world of whispers and hisses. It is a single, hard, solid syllable of *is*. It is the sound of a question being answered, of a dissonance being resolved. The shock travels up my haft, into the arm of the Tender, a confirmation of the deed. It travels down the wedge, into the paving block, a command to return. It travels through the stone of the terrace, a vibration that informs the entire caldera that balance has been restored.

The paving block settles by a fraction of a millimeter. It is enough.

The wedge is withdrawn. The fingers of the Tender trace the seam again. It is smooth. It is silent. The ache is gone.

The journey back is the same gliding silence. I am returned to my shelf. The cold seeps back into me. My view is once again a sliver of plaza and sky. The Tenders recede, becoming part of the landscape again. The light begins to fade.

I am not alive, but I am part of a life. I am not a being, but I am an agent of being. My consciousness is the memory of every impact, every correction I have ever made. I am the punctuation in a story that never ends. I am the silence, and I am the sound that proves the silence is worth keeping.

I am the hammer. And I wait.
