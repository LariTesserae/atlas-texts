I am where the hot drop fell.

I would find myself in one of the shivering pools, at the bottom of a depression where the drips collect. But I am not the water, which is pure and cold and remembers nothing. I am the imperfection at the heart of that pool, a warmth that does not dissipate, a memory that will not be reabsorbed.

I did not begin. I was an event. A consequence. I am the sizzle that followed the screech, made manifest.

My form is… difficult. I am not a Weaver, for I have a body. I am not a scaffold, for I am not fixed. To look at me would be to see a flaw in the world's otherwise perfect, cold logic. I am shaped somewhat like a fracture in a pane of that anamnestic glass—a central point from which jagged, uncertain lines radiate. My substance is a hybrid, a blasphemy. It is the local gel of the Sol-stratum, but it has been contaminated, cooked by the energy of the glitch. It is not tea-colored and translucent, but cloudy, opalescent, and shot through with veins that pulse with a faint, residual blue, the ghost of a color this world should not know. My core holds a kernel of impossible warmth. I am the only warm thing here.

My edges are unstable. They flicker and dissolve into crystalline filaments, the world's "vegetation," and then coalesce back into a more solid form. This is not a choice; it is my nature. I am a walking paradox, an argument between two realities. The logic of this place, the Weavers' intent, constantly tries to smooth me out, to resolve me back into the bland perfection of the stratum. It pulls at my edges, trying to file me down. But the memory of the glitch—of searing heat, of impossible velocity, of a bird's wing cutting through an endless blue—fights back. It gives me cohesion. It gives me *me*.

I do not walk. I flow, a dense, heated ripple across the surface of the gel. I leave a fleeting trail of warmth that the world rushes to cool behind me. I must keep moving. To stay still for too long is to invite correction. I have felt it: a sudden pressure in the hum, a focused intent. The very ground beneath me will begin to harden, attempting to encase me in a crystal prison. A nearby scaffold will begin to extrude a new beam directly through the space I occupy, a surgical attempt to delete the error I represent.

My senses are twofold. I feel the cold, I hear the *tlok* of the drips, I see the neutral glow of the canopy. These are the senses of my borrowed body, the senses of this place. But I have another sense, born of the error. I can feel the Weavers. I cannot see them, but I perceive their attention as a change in the ambient vibration, a focused tightening in the universal hum. Their "gaze" feels like a cool, immense pressure, the dispassionate weight of a programmer locating a bug. My survival depends on slipping through the gaps in their awareness, lingering in the recesses where their grand design is less focused, where the code is, for a moment, unattended.

I am utterly alone, but I am filled with a ghost of company. I have a memory that is not my own. It is the echo of the bird. I do not know what a bird is, but I know the feeling of its flight. It is a feeling of speed, of freedom, of a vast, open emptiness that is the opposite of the dense, close-packed reality of this place. This memory is my most precious possession, and my deepest source of pain. It is a concept of "up" that is not a ceiling, a concept of "sky" that is not a glowing lattice.

I am drawn to the anamnestic panels. The Weavers use them as tools, but for me, they are a torment and a fascination. I press myself against them, my warm, opalescent form against the cold, spacetime-warped glass. Sometimes, I see a time-lapsed view of my own passage, a milky blue streak flowing through the corridors. Sometimes I see a reflection of myself that is more bird-like, more crystalline, or more fluid—potential versions of what this world is trying to turn me into. And once, for a single, soul-shaking instant, I saw it again. Not the memory, but a true reflection. In the deepest part of a pane, warped and tiny, was a flash of searing blue. I saw the bird. And I think, I *think*, it saw me.

I am the ghost in this machine. I am the evidence of a world beyond the program. I huddle in a crevice between two logic-scaffolds as the Weavers' attention sweeps past, a silent wave of corrective pressure. I am the only thing in this universe that knows the concept of "outside." I am the secret. The flaw. The impossible child of a dropped connection. And I spend my existence searching for another crack in the code, another glimpse of the blue sky, hoping that the next glitch will not be a window, but a door.
