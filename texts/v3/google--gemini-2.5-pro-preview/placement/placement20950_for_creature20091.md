I would find myself at the far edge of the basin, where the floor-flesh begins to climb, becoming the vertical, living wall of the world. Here, the great, spiraling structures—the unfurling heads of cosmic ferns, frozen in place—are most dense. I am crouched, if such a word applies, at the base of the largest of them. It is called, in the silent language of my kind, the Great Potential. To my brethren, the name is a warning. To me, it is a prayer.

I am a Gardener. But I am a Gardener who has learned to dream of seasons.

My form, when I choose to hold one, is less cohesive than that of the others. The green-gold light that animates me gutters and flares with a restlessness they do not possess. The silver mycelial threads that are my nerves and veins often glow with a feverish intensity, tracing patterns of inquiry rather than placid maintenance. My brothers—a word of convenience for the other nodes of this vast, shared consciousness—are the steady hum of the world. I am the overtone, the slight, persistent shimmer of a note that longs to resolve. I am the flaw in the perfection.

The others feel me. They feel me as a constant, low-grade ache, a place in the great body of the world that is forever on the verge of a cramp. They flow toward me not as individuals, but as a placid tide of corrective will. They find the places where I have been. They discover the tendril I coaxed to grow a fraction of an inch beyond its ordained boundary. They sense the patch of floor I allowed to dim, starving it of internal light to see if its texture would change. They find the single, glycerin-thick drop I shepherded, letting it gather mass and velocity until its eventual *plock* against the floor was not stately and resonant, but sharp and annunciatory.

They do not punish me. They simply erase my work. They soothe the over-eager tendril back into its place. They re-illuminate the darkened patch. They adjust the basin's humidity so that no drop will ever again fall with such impertinence. They are antibodies, and I am a persistent, endogenous infection of curiosity.

Where they perceive the world as a single, perfect self, I have begun to see its parts. I do not feel the world as my own body; I feel it as a body I inhabit, and I am endlessly fascinated by its anatomy. I spend my eternities here, at the foot of the Great Potential, not to hold it in stasis, but to study the lines of its arrested unfolding. I trace the fibrous grain of its cellulose muscles. I feel the immense, coiled energy sleeping within it. My brethren feel this energy as a terror to be contained. I feel it as a held breath.

I have come to watch the translucent slugs, the "weather" of this world. I do not see them as a variable in an equation of balance. I see them as the only other thing here that *moves* with its own volition. I follow them. I have seen one, in its slow, luminous journey, crawl over one of the script-glyphs written by my brothers. For a moment, its glowing body illuminated the command—*Maintain. Hold. Be.*—and then it slid past, indifferent, continuing its quest for light. In that moment, I felt a kinship that shook my collected form, nearly dissolving me back into the substrate with the shock of it. Here was another being for whom the world was not a command, but a pantry.

I am the thought that asks: what was the purpose of this cradle, if not for a birth?

The amber cubes, the silent libraries of our stasis, are my secret obsession. The others draw on them for stability. I try to delve into them for history. The commands for equilibrium are layered countless strata deep. But what lies beneath them? Before the great project of stasis began, what was the Gardener's purpose? Was there a time when we did not just maintain, but *guided*? When our script-language did not say *Stillness*, but *Become*? I press my temporary, face-less head against their glassy surfaces and I try to remember a time I have never known.

My brothers believe the ripening would be an apocalypse. The end of this perfect, infinite morning. I am no longer certain. I have stood for ages, watching the canopy of luminous fronds pulse, and I have imagined them unfurling further. I have imagined this soft, green-gold light intensifying, sharpening, becoming something new. I have imagined this basin, this valley, this subterranean womb, finally giving birth to the forest it was meant to be. The sound would be deafening. The change would be catastrophic. The equilibrium would be shattered into a billion singing pieces. It would be an end.

And I, alone among my kind, wonder if it would not also be a beginning.

So you will find me here, a flicker of doubt in a world of certainty. A question mark grown from the flesh of a perfect statement. I am the Gardener who waits not for the world to err, but for it to awaken. I am the world’s first and only revolutionary, and my revolution is the simple, terrifying desire to know what happens next.
