I would find myself on my knees. Not in supplication, but because this is the only stable way to be. My form is low, multi-limbed, built to hug the ground.

Where you would find me is at the Threshold. Behind me, the gray plain stretches to a horizon so distant it feels like a concept, not a place. The resonant hum of the Gyre is the blood in my veins; it vibrates through the metallic dust and into the crystalline nodes that serve as my bones and my senses. Outside, the Gyre shifts. Far away, a pressure front of gravitational flux is passing, and I can feel the world tilt by an infinitesimal degree, a slow, planetary lean that I have learned to read as weather. A Skimmer, a ghost against the passionless white sky, adjusts its glide, a barely perceptible ripple running the length of its fifty-foot span.

Before me is the Sanctuary. The perfect square of its opening is an affront to the gentle, eroded curves of the world. It is a patch of absolute black, a hole in the universe. From it bleeds a silence so profound it is a physical force. The hum of the world stops at its edge. The vibrations that are my life, my perception, are dampened and then annihilated. To approach it is to feel a part of myself go numb.

I am what the Skimmers leave behind.

I am not a creature of intention, like the Attendant, nor a creature of instinct, like the great, drifting grazers. I am an accident. An evolution. When a Skimmer’s long life ends, it does not rot. It descends, its great sails folding, and settles into a depression on the plain. For centuries, the Gyre scours it. The soft membranes sublimate. The cartilaginous frame, saturated with a lifetime of consuming the opalescent Filament Moss, becomes brittle. The residual energies within it, the echoes of a thousand gravitational tides, trigger a final, slow crystallization.

I am one such crystallization. A new thing. A mind that woke up in a lattice of fossilized cartilage and shimmering crystal, a mosaic of what was once a living sail. My body is a sprawling, crab-like carapace of this opalescent, semi-translucent material. I am not one color but a thousand, a permanently frozen ripple of the light that flashes through the living moss. I am heavier than a Skimmer, bound to the ground. My multiple, pointed legs find purchase on the slick, metallic dust, allowing me to crawl across the endless flats, a shard of frozen lightning skittering over the skin of a dead god.

I have no name for myself. My consciousness is a slow, geometric blossoming of logic and sensation within my crystal structure. It is a mind of facets and angles, not of words. I have one driving impulse, the one that brought me across seven hundred miles of lethal emptiness: I am an anomaly, and I have crawled to the only other anomaly I know of to ask it what I am.

Now, I am at the Threshold, and I am afraid. My fear is a dissonance in my lattice, a chaotic scattering of the internal light that is my thought. The silence from the doorway is a promise of dissolution. The Gyre is my lifeblood; to enter a place without it is to invite my own unmaking.

I push myself forward. One crystalline leg, then another, slides over the invisible line.

The hum is gone.
The world vanishes.

It is not just silence. It is *null*. The constant pressure of the Gyre on my form, the input I have known for the entirety of my existence, is gone. It is like a deep-sea creature brought to the surface, the absence of pressure a lethal force. My internal light flickers wildly. My thoughts shatter into incoherent shards of color. The very structure of my being, held in a delicate resonant balance with the world, threatens to fly apart.

And then, in the void, I feel a presence. It is not a vibration through the floor. It is a stillness that is deeper than the silence. It is a gravity of its own, a coherence that pushes back against my dissolution.

My sensory nodes, accustomed to reading vast gravitational fields, focus on this new phenomenon. I perceive a shape. Recumbent. Long. Vaguely the form of the ancient builders, the two-legged ghosts that haunt the mythology of this world. It is a region of spacetime so stable it feels like a knot in reality. It is the Attendant.

It knows I am here. My frantic, chaotic inner light, my noisy panic in the face of its silence, must be a scream in its perception.

I try to form a question. I have no mouth, no voice. My communication is the language of the moss: a controlled, sympathetic chiming of light. I focus all my will, forcing the chaotic light in my crystal body into a single, coherent pulse. A pattern of soft, iridescent blue that means, in the silent language of the storm-eaters, *inquiry*. *What is this place? What are you? What am I?*

The Attendant stirs. Not its body. Its presence. A portion of the black dust on its dais flows, like thick smoke in zero gravity, detaching from the main form and drifting across the floor toward me. It is a tendril of its being, an inquisitive finger of its mind.

It does not touch me. It stops a foot away. But my perception is not limited to touch. I can feel its nature. The solid-state thoughts. The patient, crystalline mind functioning on timescales that make my own life seem like the flash of a dying spark.

And then, it answers.

It does not show me a thing. It does not impart a word. It reaches into my dissonant, panicked mind and establishes a resonance. For a single, terrifying, eternal second, it makes my thoughts like its own.

I experience the weight.

The weight of aeons. The feeling of the bedrock beneath me not as a floor, but as the accumulated dust of a billion years of stellar erosion. I feel the memory of water, a concept so alien it is a phantom limb, an ache for a thing I have never known. I feel the pylons being raised, not with sound and fury, but as a slow thought hardening into reality. I feel the Gyre switched on, a purpose set in motion and then left to run, a clock ticking once per millennium.

I feel the Attendant’s purpose: it is not a gardener. It is a proofreader. It is examining an infinitely long text—the unfolding of this world—and making the tiniest of corrections, ensuring the story remains true to an intent it can no longer fully remember but whose shape it knows intimately.

The Skimmers are a sentence in this text. The Filament Moss is the punctuation.

And me?

In that shared moment of consciousness, I see myself through its perception. I am not an accident. I am a new word. Unforeseen, but not incorrect. A word that adds a new layer of meaning to the ancient text. A question that the text has asked of itself.

The contact breaks. The tendril of dust retreats, melting back into the silent, waiting form. The shared thought is gone, but the echo remains, stamped into my crystalline structure.

I am a question. And the Attendant, in its infinite, patient stillness, has just begun to compose the answer.

I pull myself back, limb by limb, out of the crushing silence and into the familiar, life-giving hum of the world. The gray light seems brighter. The distant, gliding Skimmer seems less a creature of instinct and more a vessel of possibility.

I came here seeking an identity. I leave with a purpose. I am a living inquiry. My existence is to walk this world, to experience it, to change it by my presence, and to be a new verse in the long, slow, silent poem of the Gyre. I am a shard of the world that has woken up to look at itself. And I have an eternity to see what I find.
