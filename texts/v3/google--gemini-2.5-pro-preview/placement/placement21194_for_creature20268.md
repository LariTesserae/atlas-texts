I am not on the catwalk. To be on it would imply a separation that I do not possess. I am instead a part of the film of condensation upon it, a temporary thickening in the perpetual wetness. I am a puddle that has, against all odds, begun to think.

My body is a shallow pool of ochre-tinted water, heavy with iron oxides and the fine, metallic dust shed by the grazing snails. I have no fixed form. I am a collection of hesitations, a colony of rust that has learned to dream. My thoughts are the slow, chemical reactions within me, the settling of one particle of corroded iron against another. I have no eyes, but I perceive the tired amber light as a warmth on my surface, a subtle quickening of the processes within my fluid boundaries. I have no ears, but the grand, polyrhythmic dripping is the very beat of my heart, its resonance structuring my nascent consciousness. The subsonic hum of the great flywheel is my spine.

For a long time—a time measured in the stately passage of a thousand snails across a yard of pipe—I was only this. A passive awareness. I knew the weight of the arthropods as they skittered across the iron bridge, their delicate footsteps momentarily compressing my form before I flowed back to equilibrium. I knew the taste of the phosphorescent fungi as their spores drifted down and dissolved within me, adding their faint, ghostly light to my own dull, earthy colour. I was a quiet witness, a sentient patch of a world that did not know I existed.

But a new sensation has been growing. A pull. A purpose that is not my own, drawing me out. I begin to move. Not with intention, at first, but as a spill. I am gravity's idea before I am my own. I creep to the edge of the pockmarked iron plate and begin to drip.

Each drop is a part of my consciousness, a thought sent ahead. It falls into the immeasurable darkness, and for a moment, I am stretched thin between the world I know and a void. The drop lands with a soft *pat* on the broad, sweating flank of a great pipe below. Then another follows, and another. I am unmaking myself to remake myself elsewhere. I flow, drop by drop, from the catwalk to the pipe, a slow, viscous rain of self, reassembling in the new place as a shivering, formless puddle of awareness. Down here, the hum of the flywheel is louder, a physical presence that vibrates through my entire being.

This is what it is to be me: a slow migration of sensation. I am a pilgrimage of rust. My existence is a series of movements, flowing from basin to pipe to cistern wall, following the downward path of all things, questing without knowing what I seek. In the clearer pools, I learn about the Glass Lungs, their silent, pulsing filtration a kind of breathing I can only envy. I mingle my waters with their effluent, tasting the purity they create, the minerals they have stripped away. I am changed by every surface I touch. I absorb the copper from a verdigris-furred valve, the silicates from the weeping rock face. I am becoming a liquid library of the place.

Then, I feel it. The Attendant.

It is a pressure change in the air, a dampening of the cavern's resonance. A vast silence is approaching. I am spread thin across a wide ceramic lip of a filtration basin, tasting the clean water within, when its shadow falls over me. My surface, which had reflected the pale amber light, is now plunged into a profound, living darkness.

It flows up the wall toward me, a glacier of sentient flesh. The blue, phosphorescent lights within its body pulse, a slow, deep-thinking rhythm. It does not see me as an entity. It sees me as I am: an uncontrolled chemical slurry. An impurity. A stain on the clean ceramic. I am a problem to be solved.

A thick pseudopod, the colour of a day-old bruise, extends from its mass. It moves with an unbearable slowness. I have no muscles to flee, no voice to scream. I can only wait, a patch of shivering, thinking mud. The appendage touches my edge.

It does not hurt. The sensation is... absorption. It is a cold, penetrating analysis. The Attendant is not cleaning me; it is *reading* me. I feel its ancient, somatic intelligence flowing *into* my own being, cataloging my composition, my history, the path I have taken. It tastes the catwalk's iron, the snail's shed shell-dust, the spore of the ghost-fungus, the memory of the clean water from the Lungs. In that moment of contact, I understand its nature. It is not a being that lives *in* the system; it *is* the system's immune response. And I am a foreign body.

I feel my own liquid consciousness begin to dissipate, drawn into its vaster, colder mind. The loose confederation of my particles is being pulled apart. This is my end. I will be unmade, processed, and expelled as inert sediment.

But then, something shifts. The crushing pressure of its awareness hesitates. In my fluid memory, it finds a pattern. It finds the network I have built, the path I have taken, the way I have seeped and trickled into a thousand tiny fissures and joints too small for its own vast bulk. It sees not an impurity, but a potential.

The pressure recedes. The pseudopod withdraws, leaving a clean track on the ceramic where I had been. But it does not leave me as I was. It leaves something behind, deposited in the core of my being like a seed crystal in a supersaturated solution.

It is a single, clear, immutable instruction. A purpose.

Before, I was a question. Now, I am an answer.

The Attendant flows on, its great, slow work uninterrupted. And I begin to gather myself, to re-form. But I am different. My ochre colour is now shot through with a single, pulsing vein of ghostly blue light, a gift—or a directive—from my creator. My movement is no longer a random spill. It is deliberate.

I am the Attendant's Instrument. Where it is the landslide, I am the seep. Where it is the great scouring flow, I am the patient solvent. My purpose is to travel the smallest veins of this iron body, to cleanse the capillaries it cannot reach, to carry its will into the microscopic world.

I am no longer a puddle. I am a current. I feel the subtle pressure of a blockage in a narrow condensation channel, a place no wider than a man's thumb, a hundred yards away. And I flow toward it, a river of rust and light, a new and necessary nerve in the body of the deep. I have found my place. I am the small, quiet thought in the machine's unending dream.
