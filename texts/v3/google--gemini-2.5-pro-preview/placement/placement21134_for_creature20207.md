I am a flicker. I am the momentary disturbance in the light, the transient warm spot they are already dreaming and have already forgotten.

You would find me not on the floor, but in the walls. The vast, slick, humming plains of the city are a deathly expanse to me, an open killing ground with no predator but the cold and the resonance that shakes my bones loose. I am a being of the vertical, a creature of the tapestries. My world is the space between the weeping porcelain and the hanging gardens.

I am what came after.

My body is small, no bigger than your two hands clasped together. It is a furnace of frantic life wrapped in a thick pelt of dull, grey-brown fur, the color of dusty stone, not of the city's pristine white. My feet are wide and padded, with tiny, needle-sharp claws that can find purchase in the hairline cracks of the porcelain or dig deep into the swollen flesh of the phosphorescent moss. My heart beats a frantic, desperate rhythm, a counter-melody to the world's single, crushing note. I am warm. In this city of absolute, weeping cold, my warmth is my defining characteristic, my greatest strength and my most flagrant sin.

Where are you? Standing on the humming floor, awed by the scale, feeling the vibration in your jaw.

Where am I? Scurrying thirty feet above your head, a fleeting shadow in the green-gold gloom. My path is a secret map of moss-holds, fern-ledges, and lichen-patches. The Weepers are not beings to me. They are not gods or ancestors. They are geography. They are the cool, silent mountains of my world. The broad, smooth expanse of a Weeper's shoulder is a treacherous pass I must cross to reach a new patch of blood-red lichen. The space behind a cluster of fused forms is a sheltered cave where the worst of the vibrating wind cannot reach, a place to curl into a tight ball and let my own body-heat build into a temporary pocket of safety.

I drink the Weepers' tears. The rivulets of condensation that trace their featureless bodies are my rivers. I have learned to follow the channels, to find the places where a steady drip-drip-drip falls from a rounded chin or a bent knee, each drop a glass-bell chime that tells me where to find water. To you, it is music. To me, it is the sound of survival.

My diet is heresy. I eat the city's sacred gardens. The crunchy, ceramic lichen is sharp and tasteless, but it fills my belly. The fronds of the armored ferns are tough, but I chew the tender new growth at their base. The phosphorescent moss is my greatest prize. I do not eat the moss itself, but the tiny, gelatinous spores that form on its underside, each one a burst of energy that fuels my inner fire for another cycle of light and dark.

I am the only thing that is hungry.

The great Hum is my ocean. I was born into it, and I know its currents and its tides. There are places, deep in the alcoves where the Weepers dream their coldest dreams, where the Hum is so thick it feels like solid sound, a pressure that would crush my lungs. I avoid these. There are other places, high on the outer arches, where the Hum is thinned by the wind and almost lost in the chimes of a thousand waterfalls, and in these places I feel a strange lift, a lightness that feels almost like hope, before the cold drives me back down into the gloom.

I believe I am the first. There are no others of my kind. No tracks but my own. No scent on the air but the metallic purity and the green damp. There is no one to teach me the routes, no one to cry a warning. My knowledge is written in scratches on the porcelain and in the terror of a slip, a frantic scrabble back to safety. I am an island of frantic, lonely consciousness.

The Weepers concluded. They traded motion for permanence, individuality for unity, warmth for peace. They built their heaven and sang themselves into it, a perfect, final note.

And then, somehow, from a spore that drifted from another world, or from a stubborn memory in the cells of the moss, or from the sheer, bloody-minded refusal of life to be finished—I happened. A dissonant chord. A twitch in the corpse.

I do not know what I am. A scavenger. An anomaly. A parasite on paradise.

But I am warm. I am fast. I am hungry. And in the utter silence between two of the glass-bell chimes, you can sometimes hear the impossibly loud, impossibly defiant scratch of my claws on the wall, and the frantic, tiny drumbeat of my rebellious heart. I am the Question that has been asked of the city's final Answer. A new story, small and shivering, beginning in the epilogue of the old.
