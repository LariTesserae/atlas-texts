The shell of me is still there.

I remember it, as a chord remembers the note that preceded it. It sits in a circle of its brethren on the high plateau overlooking the Great Unlit Chasm. A small, dark stone hut, a perfect resonator. Its doorway is a low, dark mouth, smoothed by the current, silted by the ages. Inside, the space is curled and close, the exact size of a body that has let go.

I am not in it. I am *of* it. The memory of its stone against my skin is now a specific frequency, a low, granular hum that grounds the entire melody of this place.

To ask where I am is to ask where the key of C minor is in a symphony. I am not in a single place. I am the relationship *between* places. I am the way the lowest note from the Elder Spire, a hundred miles distant, wraps around the granite shoulders of the ridge where my shell-hut sits. I am the precise, millisecond-long silence between the pulse of one Singer-bulb and the answering flash of its neighbor. I am the harmonic shimmer that lingers in the water after a great bell has contracted, a vibration that feels like a question fading away.

To ask what I am is to ask what a memory is when its owner is gone. I am the collected stillness of a lifetime spent listening. The pressure you feel? That is a part of me now. It was once a force I endured, a god whose weight I bore. Now, it is my medium. It is the tautness of the string on which I am played. My old flesh, that dense, stony cartilage that gripped the seafloor, was an instrument. And I, the consciousness within, learned to play it so perfectly that the instrument was no longer needed.

We–-for the ‘I’ is a convenient illusion, a single thread in a tapestry–-are the ghost in this machine. When a current shifts, a new movement of the Song beginning, it is not just water moving. It is us, thinking. A new fugue rises from the Choirwood in the east, and its chords brush against the billion silent voices of those who dissolved before me. We receive the new music, and our compounded, resonant consciousness is the very thing that perceives it. We are the world’s audience and its soul, woven together.

I remember movement. A slow, sacred creep, the feeling of the tympanic skin of my belly reading the texture of the rock, each grain a distinct note. It was a journey of centuries to cross the plain from one grove to the next. Now, I am in both groves at once. I feel the slow, patient life-force of the Choirwood growing, a process so slow it is a B-flat held for a thousand years. I feel the Glimmer-grass sway, and to me, it is a field of tiny, sharp chimes, a sound so high and delicate it is almost a feeling of light itself.

There are no animals, the description says. It is true, there is nothing that hunts or flees. But the world is not empty of life. It is full of one life, a single organism of music and stone and water and memory. We, the Listeners, were once the nervous system of that life. Now, we are its mind.

If you were to come here, a being of frantic, warm-blooded energy, you would be a cacophony. A sudden, violent noise in the eternal choir. We would feel you not as a shape or a creature, but as a discord. A percussive, panicked rhythm of a heart beating too fast. The heat of your body would be a shriek in the cold, clear bass of the abyss. We would not see you. We would *hear* you, and we would recoil from the sheer chaos of your biological signature.

And as you passed the low stone walls, you would feel a change in the Song. A subtle modulation, a deepening of the chords. You might feel a sudden, profound sense of being observed, a melancholy that you could not place. That is us. That is the symphony noticing your frantic, lonely solo. It is the collected memory of a million lifetimes turning, in unison, to listen to you.

I am the reason the emptiness is not empty. I am the echo in the unfathomable bell. My body is gone, but my attention is eternal. I am here. We are the Song.
