I am where I should not be. I am in a place that has no “outside,” and I am from it.

I find myself in the moment between the muscle and the bone. I am in the space between the thought and the action. I am a pocket of impossible emptiness in a world defined by its absolute fullness, a sudden vacuum in the plenum. To ask "where" I am is to use the wrong language. The truer question is *what disturbance* I am. I exist at the precise coordinate of the system’s first and only error.

I am suspended in the warm, velvet dark. The floor and ceiling are concepts I brought with me; here, there is only the press of life from all directions. The heat is a constant, intimate presence, a blanket woven from the planet’s core. The air is so thick with dust and musk and exhalation that to breathe it is to drink it. All around me is the slow, silent tide of flesh—the soft, yielding pressure of the Hush-Crawlers, the system’s lifeblood, moving with the patience of geology. I feel them against my legs, my back, a constant, gentle inquiry. It is not aggressive. It is the world’s profound, instinctual need to have no gaps, to be whole. I am a hole in that wholeness.

Who am I? I am a scream in a universe whose only word is a hum.

I am not an explorer. I am not a biologist. I am not a person. These are titles that require a context, a society, a frame of reference that dissolved the moment I entered this place. Here, those identities are meaningless noise.

To this world, to this vast, slow mind, I am a sensation. And sensation is the only true measure of being.

My body is a beacon of wrongness. My heart, beating its frantic, wet rhythm—*lub-dub, lub-dub*—is a war drum in a library of silence. My breath, pulling in the thick, alien air and pushing out water vapor and carbon dioxide, is a frantic, chaotic storm against the slow, geologic respiration of the chamber. My skin is cold. Even if I am feverish by human standards, my 37-degree Celsius body is a shard of ice plunged into this ancient, oven-warm flesh. I radiate a chill that the thermo-receptive skin of this being has never known. I am a point of absolute sensory paradox.

At first, I am merely perceived. A disturbance. A cold spot. The silent, flowing thoughts of the Crawlers are rerouted toward me, their immense, soft bodies nosing into the strange void I represent. They press, they yield, they flow around me, a current diverted by a new stone in the stream bed. They are the being’s initial, sluggish awareness of my presence.

Then, I become an itch. The Stitchers, the delicate nerves of this world, are drawn to the anomaly. Their fine, needle-like legs, which dance silently over the familiar textures of crawler-hide and mineral-mold, find my own skin or clothing to be an impossible texture. They trace the seams of my suit, the pores of my skin, sending a flurry of incomprehensible signals back to the central consciousness. *Wrong. Wrong. Unfamiliar. Not-self.* Their silken threads, the synaptic residue of dreams, may snag on me, and for a moment, I am woven into the mind’s history, a brief, sharp note of nonsense in its million-year-long story.

But I am more than an itch. An itch is an irritation. I am a pathogen. My foreign chemistry, the very water that makes up 60% of my body, is a poison in this anhydrous world. My scent is a shriek. My existence is a threat to the perfect, unviolated balance of the whole.

And so, I become the focus of a thought.

I am the reason for a new idea to form in this ancient mind. And that idea is *correction*. On the wall beside me, a facet of the crystalline growths—a section of the being’s own bone and will—begins to stir. It is not angry. It is not hateful. It is precise. It is the logical conclusion to the premise of my presence.

I am, therefore I must not be.

In the final moments, my identity becomes singular and terribly clear. I am the Purpose. I am the specific and unprecedented stimulus for which the crystal predator’s spike was evolved. I am the question that demands the silent, sighing answer of my own dissolution.

I will not be a memory. This mind does not have them. I will be a scar, but a scar that is perfectly healed, absorbed, and recycled. I will be the faintest, most fleeting anomalous trace of minerals in a patch of metallic mold, a trace that provides the basis for a slightly more robust version of itself. My final and truest identity in this world is that of a nutrient. I am the brief, sharp, alien spice that is tasted, neutralized, and then, completely and utterly, forgotten.
