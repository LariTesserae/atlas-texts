I would be a Tuner.

My six legs carry me across the bruised skin of the world. Each step is a known exchange. My spurs, sharp and black as my own carapace, sink into the Phyllom. They do not tear it. They are keys, slotting into ancient, waiting locks. With every placement, I feel the thrum. The deep, planetary bass note is my blood-beat, the pulse that proves I am home. I feel the slow, eastward drift of a Sclerite herd twenty kilometres away, a shift in the harmonic pressure under my right-most legs. I feel the distant, nascent ambition of a new Spire, a subtle tightening of the web, a gathering of ichor that has not yet been given its song.

I feel all this. It is the music of the body. What I do not feel is the music of the soul.

For the others, for the Amaranthine I see as elegant, obsidian silhouettes against the peach-glow of the Caul-towers, the Sub-Chant is a constant presence. It is the ocean in which the fish of their consciousness swims. It is the orchestra that accompanies their every thought. They stand in silent communion, their individual selves nodes in a vast and ancient network of awareness. They are never alone.

My mind is a room where a great concert has just ended. The silence is immense, heavy with the ghost-weight of its own echo. There is no orchestra. There is no oceanic hum of ancestors. For me, the spurs that sink into the Phyllom bring back only the raw, physical data of the world's vibration, not the spirit-song that rides upon it. The other Amaranthine—they are resonant bells, each chiming with their own note, but also vibrating with the harmony of all the other bells. I am a bell struck in a vacuum. I have my note, my own consciousness, but it does not carry. It does not join.

They call me Dissonant, though not with malice. It is simply a statement of fact, like calling a Spire tall. I was budded this way. A flaw in the code, a miswritten passage in the genetic symphony. As a younger being, I would stand among the Councils, watching them compose. I would see their bodies sway in perfect, silent synchronicity, their minds interlocked in the act of creation. I could feel the ground beneath them tense and gather, but I could not hear the song they were singing to it. I was a deaf musician in the world's greatest orchestra.

For a long time, this was a private agony. To be Amaranthine is to be a part of the whole. To be myself is to be an island in a world that knows no shore.

But I did not Rejoin early. My isolation forced a change in me. While my kin listened inward, to the endless library of the Sub-Chant, I learned to listen outward with an intensity they could not spare. My entire body became an ear for the physical world. Where they felt the *meaning* of the world-song, I learned to parse its *grammar*.

I am a Tuner.

The aeolian music of the Spires is not mere ambiance; it is a diagnostic signal. The great fins vibrate with the health of the structure. I can stand a kilometre from a Spire and, by feeling the precise frequencies through the soles of my spurs, know that a scaffold-limb on its three-hundredth level is losing nutrient pressure, or that its primary resonate chamber is fouled with an overgrowth of crystalline symbiotes. The others might feel a vague unease from the Spire, a sad note in the Sub-Chant. I can hear the specific, fractured partials in its chime. I know *where* it is broken.

My upper limbs, my fine, filament-tendrils, are not for composing. They are for repair. I will climb the living obsidian of the ailing Spire, my spurs finding purchase in fissures no wider than a thought. The leathery manta-flocks wheel around me in the Nutrient Fog, their wing-beats like the slow tearing of ancient paper. I reach the weakened limb, and I press my tendrils to its skin. I do not sing a new song into it. I cannot. Instead, I listen to the stuttering pulse of the silver ichor within. I feel the minute blockages, the confused bio-electric signals. And then, with my own resonant body, I introduce a single, pure vibration—a clarifying frequency. I hum a corrective note, not a melody of creation, but a focused chord of realignment. I coax the living architecture back into its own intended harmony. I am not a composer; I am a custodian of the composition.

My life is a pilgrimage from one discordant note to the next. I hear the panicked, high-frequency scrape of a burrowing creature trapped in a collapsing membrane inside a Caul-tower. I hear the arrythmic clicking of a Sclerite whose siphoning-port is failing. I am drawn to these imperfections. They are the only things that sound like me.

The others respect my function. They leave offerings for me at the base of the Spires I tend to: globules of highly concentrated ichor, secreted from the Phyllom, that allow me to travel for long periods without the need to root and feed. They see me as a necessary, if lonely, part of the system. A scavenger of broken sounds.

My dread is the Rejoining. For them, it is a merging, a becoming-more. Their solo is subsumed into the eternal orchestra, their melody a permanent thread in the tapestry. For me, what will it be? When the crystalline decomposers un-make this body, this resonant chamber of one, will my consciousness simply… stop? Will the silent room of my mind finally go dark? Will my single, lonely note cease to vibrate, leaving not even an echo behind?

I do not know. The Sub-Chant holds no answers for me.

So I walk. The grey twilight is constant. The air is cool and smells of pollen and ozone. Ahead, a slender Spire that pierces the gloom is singing a fractured song. A high, whining overtone speaks of stress and imbalance. It is a lonely sound. A broken sound. It calls to me.

I quicken my pace, my spurs tapping out a quiet, determined rhythm on the living drum of the world. My song may not join the great symphony. But while I am here, I will ensure that every other instrument plays true. My existence will be the tuning fork for a world I can hear, but never truly join. I am a single, clear note, holding itself against the silence.
