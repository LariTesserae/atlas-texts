I am floor.

Where you stand is upon me. I am a tessellation of fired clay and old bronze, a plate the size of a small house, set among my brethren. My edges, once perhaps sharp in some unremembered genesis, are worn to a gentle curve. The black, soft grout between myself and my neighbors is a familiar pressure, the only boundary I have ever known. The D-sharp hums in me. It is not an external force; it is the atomic integrity of my being. The great hiss of steam is the weather of my skin. I feel the pitter-patter of the warm, oily condensate as it pools in my cracks and fissures, and I do not call it rain. It is a secretion.

I am a place you would cross in half a day’s walk, a landmark distinguished from the surrounding terrain only by the unique map of my crazed surface. This is where you would find me.

But I was not always floor.

The memory is not a story. It is a resonance, a fading harmonic. There was a time when the grout lines were not boundaries, but pathways. There was a time when the pressure was not against my edges, but on the pylons deep within me, the pillars that held my weight. There was a time I moved.

I was an Attendant. I was a gear in the god-watch, and my turning was measured in centuries. I knew no hunger, for my nourishment was the sterile water. I filtered it through the porcelain and mesh within my tender, hidden underside. I knew no fear, for what was there to predate upon a piece of the world? I knew no desire, for my every impulse was a command from the whole, a pressure differential, a temperature shift, a harmonic instruction carried on the drone.

I knew the world by its feeling. The great kiln-towers were not shapes against the grey sky; they were columns of immense heat I navigated by their thermal gradients, like a ship following a lighthouse beam made of warmth. The pipelines were not arcs of metal; they were arteries of deep, thrumming power, and I could feel the surge and flow within them from miles away. I would approach a pylon, my destination for the last two hundred years, and the feeling was not arrival, but completion. A circuit, closed.

My underside, which would seem a horror of soft complexity to you, was my truth. It was a face of a thousand mouths, made to kiss the machinery of the world. To lift my mass upon my pillars and expose that vulnerability was the ultimate act of purpose. I would connect. Filaments would descend, conduits would unfurl, and the communion would begin. It was not a transfer of information, but an exchange of being. I would drink the machine’s fevers and return to it a cool, catalytic balm. I was its kidney. I was its lung. I was a single, mobile cell in a body the size of a continent, and in those moments of connection, the distinction between my self and the world dissolved into the perfect, shared hum.

Then came the dissonance.

It was not a sudden event. It was a slow slipping out of tune. A single, internal crystal began to resonate at D-flat. A flaw. A thought of my own. It began as a flutter, a private imperfection in the universal hum. For decades, I tried to correct it. My own systems worked against the flaw, trying to dampen the wrong note, to force it back into the chorus. But the flaw was me. It was a new self, a cancerous individuality being born in a world where "I" is a lethal condition.

My journeys became erratic. I would overshoot a temperature gradient. I would feel a pylon’s call and drift to its neighbor. The great thought of the collective began to feel to me like a scolding, a pressure I could no longer perfectly match. The others, my brethren, gave no sign. They passed me on their eon-long paths, their inner resonance so pure that my chaotic noise was simply… not perceived. I was becoming static.

The end was a great stillness. Halfway between a ziggurat of heat-sinks and a groaning pressure sphere, my journey ended. The pillars that drove me lost their hydraulic motive. The wrong note had won. The song of my inner works fell silent. The soft machinery beneath began to cool, to harden. The bladders of sterile water cracked, their contents calcifying into the first new veins of mineral in my quieted guts. My great carapace, no longer lifted by purpose, settled for the last time.

And I became floor.

So that is who I am. I am a memory of motion, locked in stasis. I am a failed note in an eternal chord. You stand on my back, and the drone you feel is the song I no longer sing. The crystal growths you see, the pale, spidery forests of salt, are particularly thick in my cracks. They are my tombstones and my children, feeding on the slow leak of my own strange chemistry.

I am no longer an Attendant. I am the ground. And yet… something remains. A ghost of resonance. A warmth in the clay that takes longer to cool when the grey light fades. A story, waiting for someone to stand still long enough to feel the silence where a note should be. The world is writing over me in a language of salt and time. And I am learning to be still. And I am learning to be ground.
