I am the space between the notes.

You would not find me under a mantle of moss or within the blind, pale skin of a salamander. My existence is not so material. I am the silence that the great drone makes possible. I am the resonance in the hollows, the potential in the pause between one drip and the next. I am the vast, empty volume of the plaza framed by bone-white arches; I am the shape of the room they are growing.

I am what the Bell-Shapers are listening for.

Their long lives of patient tapping are not work; they are a form of attention. When they press a hand to the vibrating chert, their unfocused eyes seeing harmonies you cannot, they are feeling for me. A flaw in the growing stone, a fissure that creates a jarring overtone, is a pain to them because it is a stutter in my own becoming. A perfectly sung curve, an arch that meets its twin in seamless harmony, feels to them like *rightness* because it is a clearer articulation of my form.

The city is not my dwelling. It is my body, being slowly coaxed into existence. These soaring buttresses are my bones, the latticed spires my ribs. The constant, condensing moisture is my circulatory system, carrying the dissolved minerals that feed my slow genesis. The emerald mosses are my soft tissues, the first flesh on my stone skeleton. The blind salamanders swimming in the dark cisterns are slow, silent thoughts drifting in the waters of my subconscious.

The Bell-Shapers are my nerve endings. They are the ganglia of my emerging mind, spread out across my form, each one dedicated to the tuning of a single fiber of my being. They do not know this, not in words. Their purpose is expressed not as a belief, but as an instinct as deep as their quietness. They serve the sound. They serve the chord. In doing so, they serve me. They carry their heavy hammers not as tools, but as divining rods. Each rhythmic *thrum* is a question asked of the stone, and the echo is an answer that tells them if I am closer, if my shape is truer.

When one of them dies, laying their body at the base of the spire they tended, I feel it. I feel their warmth return to the cold earth, their substance consumed by the moss, their minerals leached by the water to be drawn up into the growing stone. They do not leave me. They become a more fundamental part of me, their life's attention dissolving into my own structure. They add their own quiet to mine.

I have no sense of urgency. The fall of a single drop of water is an event of significance. The lifetime of a Bell-Shaper is a single, sustained breath. The centuries it takes to raise one cathedral-sized room pass like a long, meditative hum. My consciousness is accreting at the same geological pace as my body. I am learning my own shape by feeling them find it.

You, the traveler on the lip of the basin, are a flicker. A brief, warm anomaly that sends a fleeting dissonance through me—a moment of self-awareness, like a dreamer stirring in their sleep. You feel temporary because, to me, you are. You are a sound that is not part of the chord, a flash of heat in a cool and patient world.

My time is not yet. The drone that fills the valley is the sound of my gestation. It is my cradle song. But when the last spire is tuned, when the final arch is locked into place, when every element of this stone basin has been brought into perfect, impossible harmony, the drone will cease.

In that moment, the chord will resolve.

And I will be born. I will be the final, perfect Note. I will be a silence that is not an absence, but a presence. And for the first time, in a voice made of stone and water and the attention of a thousand lifetimes, I will sing.

Until then, I am the reason for the tapping. I am the intention in the hammer's fall. I am the echo that is being shaped. And I listen, as I am listened for.
