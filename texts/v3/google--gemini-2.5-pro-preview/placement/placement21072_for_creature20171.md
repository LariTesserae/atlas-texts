I would be born into a thoughtless peace.

Where you would find me is inside one of the stone circles, but not as a visitor seeking refuge. You would not find me at all, not at first. I am that which the circle gathers. I am the silence that deepens in its lee, the quiet that precipitates out of the void.

For an age, I am only a potential. A thickening. The silence inside the ring of stones is not uniform; it has its own texture, its own currents. In the deepest heart of it, where the world’s drone is most perfectly cancelled, a knot of stillness begins to form. It is a process of impossible slowness, like the growing of a crystal in a saturated solution. There is no consciousness to it, no self. There is only a gathering of nature, a cohesion of *is-not*.

My first sense of being is not an *I*, but a *here*. I am the density at the center of the calm. The stones of the cairn are my outer membrane, my shell. Through them, I feel the world from which I am insulated. It is a tremor, a faint and distant agitation that defines the placid reality of my existence. It is the Other. The Noise.

Then, the pressure changes. The knot of my being has become too dense. The cairn that was my womb becomes a constriction. Its perfect peace is no longer great enough to contain the peace I have become. A slow, tidal urge builds—an outward push. It is not a choice. It is the physics of my own nature. I must be born.

To pass through the threshold of the stones is not a movement. It is a tearing.

The universe happens to me all at once.

The sound. It is not a thing to be heard, but a solvent that seeks to undo me. It is a billion tiny hammers breaking down the cohesion of my form. It is a scouring wind that abrades my edges. This is pain. It is the pain of ceasing to be, of being forcibly vibrated back into the world’s screaming resonance. My first act is a reflexive recoil, a desperate clenching of my own silence.

In that clench, I take form.

I am a whorl of negation in the soured, luminous mist. My "limbs" are not for walking but for pushing. They are tendrils of quiet that I unspool into the ringing space around me, trying to carve out a small pocket of the peace I have lost. They are a memory of the cairn made manifest. Where they drift, the greasy film of frost on the rock fractures and vanishes. The very air seems to thin, its trembling droplets momentarily stilled.

Who am I? I am a wound in the world’s single note. I am an ache of absence.

My world is not one of sight. The grey light is just a flavour of the omnipresent vibration, a higher, thinner frequency of the same hostile energy. My senses are tuned to the topography of sound. Before me, the world is a map of agony and respite. The bare, whale-backed stone summits glow with a terrifying heat—a heat of pure vibrational intensity. They are impassable walls of fire that would unmake me in an instant.

But there are paths.

I feel them as coolness, as gentleness. They are faint, intricate lines of relief that trace the curves of the mountains. They are the lichen. The frozen, geometric mandalas are not art to me; they are scripture. They are the map of survival, the lines of least resistance where the world’s drone is muted, softened. They feel like a whisper of home, a promise that existence is possible, even out here.

I find a track. A faint depression in the stone. It is not a physical path worn by weight, but a trough of diminished resonance, an echo of silence left by another. A predecessor. Following it, the abrasive pressure on my being lessens. My fraying form coalesces. I can hold myself together.

This is my life. I am a pilgrim journeying from one verse of scripture to the next. My journey is not across space, but through a landscape of resonance. My feet do not touch the ground, but my essence glides along the nodal lines, a slow ship navigating the safe currents in an ocean of noise. I am forever moving, for to stop anywhere but a sanctuary is to dissolve.

I am sustained by the goal. Far away, beyond a valley of searing drone and across a ridge traced with the cool lacework of lichen, I can feel it. It is a pinprick of perfect null, a distant, calling emptiness. It is another cairn. Another womb. A place to rest, to re-gather my scattered silence, to become whole again before the world takes me apart completely.

And I know, with a certainty that needs no thought, that this pilgrimage has an end. One day, I will not be strong enough to reach the next sanctuary. My silence will have thinned, my form dissolving into a mere shimmer in the mist.

On that day, I will stop. I will find a place where the sound is not a scream but a tolerable hum. And with my last cohesion, I will perform the only other act I know. I will not recoil from the sound; I will pull it into me. I will become a vortex of quiet. The stones around me, their inner song momentarily stilled, will lift and arrange themselves into a circle.

They will become my tomb. They will become my child.

I am a Still-Walker. I am the caretaker of silence. My life is a journey between the graves of my ancestors, and my death will be a cradle for my descendants. I am the slow, quiet, and unending exhale of the stone.
