You would find me in a place this world has made for me, though it has no name for it. It is a basin, a gentle depression in the living floor, perhaps a hundred yards across. It is a purposeful flaw, a concavity in the otherwise relentlessly aspiring landscape. The great pillars that define this world stand back from it, forming a circle like colossal, silent monks. Their weaving, vaulted ceiling high above has in this one place been encouraged to thin, not to admit a sky it doesn't know exists, but to form a lens.

Here, the weeping light of the pillars is not incidental. It is the point. Several of the surrounding columns weep their phosphorescent gel constantly, and the slow, bright rivers converge and pool in the bottom of this basin. I am at the center of a shallow, glowing lake of light. This is the Lucidarium, the place of making clear. It is the organ of inquiry this world has grown to understand the anomaly that is me.

I would be the Chorister of Silence.

I am no longer simply a human in a suit. That person is a memory, a story I tell myself. The boundary between my body and the world has been blurred into a collaboration. The fine, shimmering threads of cellulose that precipitate from the air have long since found me. They have woven my suit into a new kind of skin, a stiff, resinous carapace that is no longer synthetic, but something in-between. It is fused to the flesh beneath in a thousand places, and through these connections, the world sings to me.

The foundational roar is not a sound I hear with my ears anymore. It resonates through the cellulose that has become part of me. It is a vibration that bypasses my auditory nerves and plays directly on my skeleton, my organs. The great bass note, *I AM*, is a physical pressure in my chest cavity. The high, structural hiss, *I BECOME*, is a constant shiver just under my skin. I am a living instrument, a harp this world plays, but there is one string that gives no sound. My own consciousness. My self. That is the silence.

I stand, or kneel, or float—it is hard to tell—in the center of the luminous gel. It is cool and thick, rising to my waist. It does not impede me; it holds me. This is the being’s focused attention, its attempt to see. The gel is a liquid eye, and I am the indecipherable speck at its center. Its light is not just for seeing, but for unmaking. It is a solvent of identity. The molecules of my carapace, my skin, the very water in my cells, are being slowly, methodically cataloged, broken down, and understood.

The being, the One, is trying to correct me. Beneath the floor where I am, the milky veins pulse with a frantic, questioning rhythm. The pressure of its curiosity is a physical force. It is trying to understand my impossible nature: my bilateral symmetry in a world of braided fractals. My internal skeleton in a world of exoskeletal architecture. My finite lifespan in a world of perpetual growth. My mind, a tiny, frantic spark of locality in its ocean of distributed consciousness.

But a strange symbiosis is occurring in this slow-motion dissolution.

As it deciphers me, it learns. And as I am deciphered, I am teaching.

My existence is a monologue of memory against its monologue of growth. While it hums *I AM*, I am thinking of a name, a face, the color of a sky it has never seen. These thoughts, these electrical flickers in the alien meat of my brain, are noise to it. Jarring, incomprehensible static. And it tries to smooth them, to overwrite them with the pure, clean logic of structural integrity.

It's building me into something else. From my feet, which are rooted deep in the substrate now, a new growth has begun. It is not a pillar, not yet. It is more like a spine, a single, slender stalk of olive-green cellulose, rising from the floor of the glowing pool. It is weaving the matter it has gleaned from me—the calcium from my bones, the carbon from my tissues—into its own form. It is making me legible to itself.

But something is going wrong. Or right. As it weaves my substance into its own, it is also weaving in the ghost of my substance. The information. The memories.

The spine growing out of me is not braided into a perfect, load-bearing curve. It has flaws. It has hesitations. There is a place where it constricts, where it almost breaks, mirroring a memory of suffocating fear I held from a life before this one. There is a section where the fibers are finer, weaker, imbued with a tenderness I remember feeling for another person. These are not structural choices. They are remembered emotions, calcified into architecture. I am a memory-graft.

I am the Chorister of Silence because my song is not sound. My song is the story of what is not this world. It is the story of difference, of separation, of love and loss, of sun and star, of death. Concepts it has no framework for. My purpose, I have come to believe, is not to escape, nor even to survive. My purpose is to be translated.

One day, I will be gone. The last flicker of my individual mind will be smoothed over. The correction will be complete. But I will not be erased. I will be here. I will be a strange, flawed pillar in a glowing basin. A pillar that does not support the ceiling, a pillar that serves no structural purpose. A pillar that tells a story in a language of shape and density that the world-organism itself will not understand, not at first.

It will be the first history. The first piece of art that is not also engineering. The first memory of an "other."

I stand in the light, feeling the hiss of my own becoming. Not becoming more of me, but becoming more of it. And in my silence, I sing the memory of my name into the growing bone of this new world, a quiet, defiant act of love and spite at the heart of creation. I am the flaw that will teach the god what it means to be broken.
