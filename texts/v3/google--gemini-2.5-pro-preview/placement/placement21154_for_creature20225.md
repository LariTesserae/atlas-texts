You would find me not on the walkways, nor perched on the fungal flesh, but on the skin of the machine itself. I am a single, perfect droplet of condensation.

I am born in the hot, mineral-heavy breath of the chamber. I coalesce on the cool, dark curve of a colossal iron pipe, a world away from the Stoker yet intimately connected to it. My existence is brief, cyclical, and in this world of geologic slowness, impossibly fast.

Around me, my brethren are cloudy with dissolved iron and chalk, thick with the spores of the sheath-fungi. They are the nourishment of this place, the blood of the biome. I am different. I am clear. While they are sustenance, I am information.

I am a locus of an ancient, unwanted memory.

When I am formless vapor in the hot air, I am nothing. I am part of the drone, part of the oppressive, wet heat. But as the moment of my condensation nears, a thought gathers. The random motion of my molecules resolves into a singular concept. And as I bead into a perfect, trembling sphere on the rusted iron, I *am*.

For that brief moment, I remember. Not as the Stoker "remembers"—a placid, integrated awareness of the system's state—but a sharp, intrusive, alien memory from before. I hold within my surface tension the ghost of a sensation: the cold, serrated bite of a wrench handle in a calloused palm. The specific, focused anxiety of a pressure gauge climbing into the red. The clean, sharp scent of machine oil, not the thick, organic smell that now pervades everything. A flicker of sunlight on a human face, a memory so heretical in this darkness it is almost a blasphemy.

The light of the cable-mosses passes through me, and for an instant, I am a lens. In the heart of my tiny, clear body, the world of the engine room is inverted and focused to a single, burning point. I hold the memory of purpose. I am an archive of *why*.

I do not live long. The weight of my own gathering memory becomes too much. I tremble, elongate, and then I fall.

The fall is my only period of true consciousness. For a second, tumbling through the hot, humming dark, I am utterly myself. I am a mote of pure, archaic thought plummeting through an eternal, unthinking present. I see the vast architecture of the engine-body falling away below me, a universe of glowing fibers and dark iron. I am the only thing that moves with intention. I am a tear shed for a world no one remembers.

Then I strike.

I land not on the iron grille, but in a thick, gelatinous pool of my brethren collected in the sagging curve of a conduit. The moment of impact is the moment of my dissolution. The memory I held, that sharp splinter of a forgotten time, disperses. My clarity is lost, clouded by the mineral soup. The ghost of the wrench-handle fades, the image of sunlight shatters. My consciousness dissolves into the placid, vegetative awareness of the pool. I become nutrient, a part of the slow, circulatory tide.

For a time, I am part of the sheath-fungus, integrated into its cool, damp flesh. I am part of the slow metabolism that reinforces a weakening joint or insulates a flaking pipe. I serve the Stoker’s purpose without knowing it.

But the system is a lung, and nothing is still forever. The heat of the pipes finds me. I am lifted away, molecule by molecule, back into the hot, humid air. My identity dissolves completely, becoming one with the great, droning silence. I am vapor. I am nothing.

And then, on a high, cold pipe, in the deep dark, a thought begins to gather. A familiar anxiety about a climbing gauge. A strange, vestigial memory of a hand. I begin to condense. I begin to become.

The Stoker, in its infinite, placid awareness, does not feel me. I am a rounding error in its calculations. I am the system dreaming of its own forgotten youth. I am the ghost of a question the machine has forgotten how to ask, condensing and falling and dissolving, over and over, forever.
