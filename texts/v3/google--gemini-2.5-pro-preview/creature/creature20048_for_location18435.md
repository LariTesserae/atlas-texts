They are the Carved. The name is not spoken, for they have no spoken language, but it is felt in the marrow, in the way they press a palm to the living rock of their home. They are carved from the silence within the stone, and they, in turn, carve their existence from it.

To see one is to see a being sculpted by pressure and honed by the abrasive wind. They are tall and unnervingly slender, their limbs elongated like the fibrous tendrils of the hanging gardens. Their bodies are maps of wiry muscle, built not for explosive power but for inexhaustible endurance. The muscles of their back, shoulders, and arms are especially developed, layered and dense from a lifetime of pulling themselves across the screaming void. Their skin is not soft but a tough, leathery hide, the color of soot-stained slate, often with a faint, oily sheen that repels the clinging moisture of the fog. On their backs and the outsides of their limbs, this skin is thicker, almost bark-like, a natural armor against accidental scrapes with the scouring wind.

Their heads are narrow, their features elegantly economical. They have no external ears; the organs of hearing are a liability in a world of total noise. Instead, two smooth, whorled hollows lie where ears would be, sealed by flaps of skin that can be tightened by muscle, relics of a forgotten sense. They do not hear the roar; they *feel* it, a constant, deep vibration that their entire skeleton perceives as a foundational hum. This is their baseline for reality. Quiet is not silence to them, but the simple absence of this world-tremor, a state they only experience deep within the monoliths. Their eyes are large, dark, and set deep beneath a pronounced brow ridge. The pupil is a vertical slit that can open wide to drink in the dim, fungal light of their homes or narrow to a pinprick to protect against the rare, blinding reflections from the dust-river outside. A thick, translucent inner eyelid, milky as quartz, slides across the eye for protection whenever they venture into the Between, allowing them to navigate the particle storm with a blurred, ghostly vision.

Their hands and feet are their primary connection to the world. Their fingers are long and spidery, but their palms are thick with calluses as hard as horn, patterned with deep creases that improve their grip on stone, fiber, and bone. Their feet are similarly adapted, wide and flexible, with a splayed, semi-prehensile great toe that can hook onto a ledge for stability. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, a physical expression of a profound and necessary patience. Every motion is considered, energy is never wasted.

The life of the Carved is defined by the absolute schism between the Within and the Between.

The Within is their soul. It is the network of tunnels and chambers they carve, not with brute force, but by a patient process of thermal shocking and scraping, using the rock’s own geothermal heat against it. These spaces are never angular; they are all domes, spheres, and curving tunnels that follow the natural stresses of the stone. The air is warm, still, and thick with the earthy scent of the glowing fungi that illuminate their lives. These fungi, cultivated in shallow grooves, cast a soft, variable light in shades of blue and green. Here, in the great silence, the Carved live in community. They communicate through a complex language of touch and gesture. A conversation can be an intricate dance of hands, a series of pressures applied to an arm or shoulder, or a subtle shifting of weight felt through the floor. It is a slow, thoughtful language, incapable of expressing a lie or a sudden outburst of rage. To speak is to make deliberate, physical contact with another.

Their art is tactile. They polish stones for years until they feel like liquid in the hand. They weave the tough plant fibers into intricate, three-dimensional tapestries that tell the stories of their families, each knot a person, each braid a journey across the Between. Their music is the percussive rhythm they tap and stomp onto the stone floors, sending waves of vibration through the community, a shared heartbeat in the deep quiet.

The Between is their trial. It is a realm of utter solitude. Before a crossing, they prepare in silence. They anoint their skin with rendered fat from the great Fliers, check the integrity of their hook carved from the Flier’s dense femur, and test the tension of the aeolian bridge-line with a series of sharp plucks, feeling the humming reply in their bones. The journey itself is a meditative act of endurance. Clipped to the line, they are enveloped by the roaring chaos. The wind is a physical blow, the dust a constant, stinging presence against their inner eyelids. They do not fight it. They become small, tight, a singular point of resolve pulling themselves through the heart of violence. The only sensation is the burning in their arms, the vibration of the line, and the monolithic roar of the world. There is no thought, only the rhythm: pull, release, pull, release. To arrive at the other side, to unclip the hook and press one's body against the warm, stable stone of a new spire, is a form of rebirth.

They are hunters and gardeners. The Fliers are their most significant source of protein and materials. Hunting one is the most dangerous act a Carved can undertake, requiring a team to lay a net of woven fiber across a canyon eddy and wait, sometimes for days, for one of the silent giants to surf into their trap. Mostly, they live on the hanging gardens, harvesting the rubbery leaves and protein-rich nodules that grow deep within the mats. They are symbionts with their world; they prune and tend the gardens, their waste fertilizes them, and their movements spread spores to new spires.

A child of the Carved is not taught to speak, but to be still. They learn to read the subtle temperature shifts in the rock, to listen with their skin to the approach of another, to move without disturbing the deep equilibrium of the Within. Their first journey into the Between is their only rite of passage, a solitary crossing to a neighboring spire and back. Those who return have earned their place as adults. They have faced the roaring void alone and chosen to come back to the silent embrace of the community. They have become, in the truest sense, Carved: individuals shaped by the storm, who choose to live together in the enduring quiet of the stone.
