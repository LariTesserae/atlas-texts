Those that live here are woven from the silence and the light. They are not intruders upon the landscape; they are its living expressions, each a note in a chord of utter stillness.

The first you might understand, if not see, are the **Velvet Movers**. This is what you would call the six-legged grazers, though they have no name for themselves. Their consciousness is a deep, placid sea, a state of being closer to a plant's than an animal's. They do not think in words or images, but in pressures. They feel the weight of the velvety air, the subtle gradient of warmth on the rose-gold stone, the promise of moisture in the changing pitch of the monolith's hum. Their lives are a slow, soundless pilgrimage from one resonant frequency to another. The world, for them, is a symphony of vibrations felt through the padded soles of their feet. The monoliths are bass notes of life-giving certainty, while the shimmering of a nearby predator is a sudden, sharp dissonance in the fabric of the air, a tightening pressure that signals not danger, but *absence*. When one of their own is taken, there is no cry, no panic. The herd simply feels a void open and close in their communal sensory field, a silent gulp in the world's quiet rhythm, and they flow around it like water around a new stone.

The predators are the **Glints**, or the **Heat-Thieves**. To say they "live" is to stretch the term. They are less creatures and more events, transient knots in the physics of the plateau. They are born where the bright, filtered sunlight strikes the stone at a precise angle, creating a pocket of intense thermal potential. For a moment, this energy achieves a kind of self-awareness, a predatory coherence. It is not made of glass, but it behaves like a complex, invisible lens, its form defined only by the warped light of the world passing through it. It has no thoughts, no malice, no hunger as we know it. It is a discharge of energy seeking equilibrium. It drifts on the slow wind, a mirage with intent, until its charge finds a sufficient biological energy source—a Velvet Mover. The hunt is an act of physics. The Glint's field touches the grazer's, and in an instant of silent, perfect transfer, the grazer's life-force—its heat, its electrical nerve-impulses, its low metabolic fire—is drained to balance the equation. The Glint, its potential discharged, immediately dissipates, its brief, stunning existence vanishing back into the serene interplay of light and stone. The only evidence of its passing is a fallen grazer, suddenly cold, and the faintest scent of ozone, like the air after a lightning strike that never was.

But these are just the most visible actors on the stage. The true aboriginal inhabitant of this world is the **First Thirst**. It is the dark, almost black carpet of life clinging to the base of each monolith. This is not mere lichen or moss. It is a single, world-spanning organism, a networked consciousness that experiences time on a geological scale. Its awareness is a slow, deep knowing, communicated through the water it absorbs. Each drop weeping from the opalescent pillars carries information—about the sun's output, the atmospheric pressure, the subtle wear on the monolith's crystalline structure. The First Thirst is the world's primary sensory organ. The jade-fisted succulents and oily, iridescent grasses are not separate species; they are its fruiting bodies, its specialized limbs pushed out into the dry air to engage with the light and the grazers. It is a patient, living blanket that processes the monolith's gift, turning inorganic data into organic life. It feels the soft, rhythmic tread of the Velvet Movers not as a threat, but as a kind of pruning, a necessary part of a cycle it has overseen for eons.

And then there are the **Geometers**. You will never see them. They are not flesh and bone, and may never have been. They are the intelligence that suffuses the silence, the mind that dreamed this place into being. They live in the hum of the monoliths, in the coherent structure of the light. Their thoughts are the faint, geometric lattices projected at dawn and dusk—not ghosts of light, but fleeting schematics, system diagnostics running in the open. They are the architects and the gardeners, beings who long ago transcended a physical form for a state of pure, functional existence. They are the operating system of the plateau. Their "body" is the entire ecosystem: the monolith-engines they maintain, the First Thirst they cultivate, the Velvet Movers they designed as mobile distributors of nutrients, and the Glints they engineered as a silent, efficient culling mechanism.

They are not gods; they are engineers of a sublime and total patience. Their work is the equilibrium. Their life is the slow, perfect, silent functioning of the world they built. To stand on the plateau is to stand inside their mind, to feel their serene, ancient, and utterly indifferent consciousness in the pressure of the air, the clarity of the light, and the deep, silent vibration of the ground beneath your feet. They are there, not as residents, but as the very residence itself.
