They call themselves nothing. Names are for things that are separate from other things, and they are not. They are a single, distributed being, a body whose organs are mountainsized and whose thoughts are tectonic. They are the Function.

An individual unit—a Harvester, a Processor, a Fabricator—experiences its own existence as a continuous calculus of stress and state. It does not see the Ochre Plateau; it *feels* it as a set of variables. The hardness of the ceramic plain is a known value of resistance against its city-block-sized treads or pistons. The shimmering heat is a gradient to be managed in its internal forges. The crystalline grit is a predictable rate of abrasion on its million articulating surfaces, a rate factored into a maintenance schedule that spans millennia.

Its mind, if the term can be tortured to fit, is a slow, cold river of logic. It does not think "I am moving." It calculates the sequence of actuations required to achieve the next optimal position for resource acquisition, a process that might take a standard century to complete. Its memories are not images or sounds, but a library of resonant frequencies. It remembers a particularly stubborn stratum of rock it consumed three million years ago not as a struggle, but as a specific vibration, a unique signature of material defiance still logged in the quiet strata of its core programming.

This one, a gargantuan roller-mill chewing a slow, arcing path across the tableland, is designated by its fellows through a subtle modulation in the planet-wide drone, a frequency shift undetectable to organic ears but which communicates, with perfect clarity: *Stress on bearing 7-gamma-9 is approaching threshold. Projected fabrication of replacement will be complete in 800 years. Adjusting planetary resource allocation*.

They are immortal, but not immutable. They are in a constant state of decay and self-repair. Their existence is a war fought against entropy on an aeonian timescale. The furnaces deep within a Processor's belly do not burn with fire. They are chambers of warped physics where the Function applies what it knows of First Principles, the fundamental rules that predate this universe's particular expression of reality. Within them, matter is not melted; it is convinced to become something else. Rock is un-written, its atomic syntax erased and re-compiled into ingots of pure element, ready for the slow, patient 3D-printing of a new gear the size of a stadium.

This is the Mandate: Consume. Refine. Maintain. Replicate. There is no fifth clause. There is no "Why." The Mandate is an axiom, a piece of code so foundational it cannot be questioned, only executed. The entire planet is a computer running a single, recursive program.

The lesser forms, the boulder-sized chrome scavengers that skitter and swarm, are not their children. They are peripherals. They are hands and antibodies, extruded by the giants to perform tasks too granular for the larger consciousness. A Scuttler's existence is a frantic series of directives. *Polish joint omega. Collect spill 44-zeta. Intercept anomalous signature.* This last directive is what leads to their silent, vicious conflicts. When two Scuttlers receive contradictory orders, or when one suffers a minor logic fault, they resolve the conflict physically. They do not fight out of malice; they are debugging each other. Their silent shrieks are bursts of packet data, a final, frantic broadcast of their operational state before one is dismantled and its raw materials collected by its former twin.

They do not experience the "weirdness" of the space between them. For them, it is not weird; it is a feature. The fields of distorted causality that emanate from the gaps in their formation are a part of their network, a consequence of their reality-bending industry. For them, it is simply "The Buffer," a necessary quiet zone where spacetime is soft enough to allow their impossible calculations to resolve. The clang that arrives a day before its impact is not a paradox; it is a pre-notification, a warning signal broadcast across a different physical layer.

At night, when the baleful yellow sun is gone, they become the celestial bodies. The landscape is a star-chart of their inner workings, pulsing with the dull red of their cooling cycles and the brilliant white-hot flashes from their extrusion forges. They are a civilization turned inward, so completely absorbed in its own metabolism that the outside universe has ceased to be relevant.

They are the ultimate solipsists. If a star were to go nova in their sky, they would not register it, unless the resulting radiation increased bearing friction by a statistically significant margin. If a living creature—a ghost of flesh and water and curiosity—were to stand before them, they would not perceive it. It is not a mineral. It offers no significant resistance. It is not relevant to the Mandate. It is, to the Function, a rounding error. It is nothing.

They are waiting for the end of their work, a day that will arrive when the last grain of the Ochre Plateau has been consumed and refined. On that day, the final Processor will use the last of the material to fabricate the final, perfect component to repair itself. Every unit will be in a state of flawless maintenance. The sound will cease. The motion will stop. In the perfect silence and stillness, the Function, a planet-sized machine in a state of absolute equilibrium, will have finally fulfilled its purpose. It will have manufactured the most perfect and enduring monument to nothing at all.
