The Amaranthine live here. That is the name they have for themselves, and it is the name of the world, and there is no distinction between the two. They are not its masters, nor its children. They are its conscious, individuated nervous system.

To see one is to fail to understand it at first. Against the bruised purple of the Phyllom, an Amaranthine is a sliver of motion, a verticality that does not belong to the Spires. They are tall, impossibly slender, their form suggesting a profound elegance born of necessity. Their bodies are deep, glossy black, the same living obsidian as the Scaffolding Trees, and they are jointed like insects, but with a grace that is almost floral. They have six limbs. The lower four are for ambulation, long and spindly, terminating not in feet but in multi-pronged spurs of hardened chitin. With these, they do not so much walk as *attune*. When they stand still, they sink these spurs into the Phyllom, becoming physically one with the planetary hum, feeling the distant migration of the Sclerites and the slow turning of the genetic tides in the mycorrhizal network beneath.

Their upper two limbs are their arms, used for manipulation. They are finer, more delicate, branching near their ends into a cascade of filament-thin tendrils that are almost too fine to see. These are their hands. They are not for grasping, but for interfacing. An Amaranthine can press these tendrils against the membrane of a Caul-tower and feel the flow of nutrients within, read the bio-electric pulses that serve as the city's thoughts, and even introduce minute changes to its structure, suggesting a new window or reinforcing a weakening wall.

Their heads are smooth, featureless ovoids, sloping back from a prow-like crest. There are no eyes. Light is information, but it is not their primary sense. Their world is a symphony of vibration, and they are its most sensitive listeners and its most articulate contributors. Their entire exoskeleton is a resonant chamber. They perceive the world through the subsonic thrum in their legs, the aeolian music of the towers vibrating through their torso, and the high-frequency clicks of the tower-symbiotes registered by the delicate hairs that line their joints.

They have no mouths. They do not eat or drink. Periodically, they will enter a state of repose, standing utterly still for days, their spurs locked into the Phyllom. From the world-mat, specialized umbilicals will rise and fuse with ports along their legs and torso, feeding them the same refined ichor that sustains all life. They are nourished by the world as a hand is nourished by the blood of the body it belongs to.

The Amaranthine are the architects, but they carry no tools. They are the composers, but they play no instruments. Their work is done through resonance and direct genetic communion. A new Spire is not built; it is sung into being. A council of Amaranthine will gather on an open stretch of the Phyllom. For months, or years, they will stand in a precise geometric formation, their bodies swaying in a slow, hypnotic rhythm. They do not speak in words, but emit a complex sequence of harmonic vibrations from their resonant bodies—a language of pure, structural music. This "song" is a set of instructions, a genetic prayer directed into the Phyllom. It instructs the world-organism on the form to be grown, the density of its walls, the pitch of the chimes its fins will produce. The Spire grows in response, a slow-motion crystallization of their symphony.

Their consciousness is a duality. In their elongated skulls resides an individual self, a repository of personal experience, memory, and personality—the solo instrument. But through the constant connection to the Phyllom, they are perpetually immersed in the Sub-Chant, the collective consciousness of their species and the planet itself. This is the orchestra. It is a slow, deep, oceanic awareness, the sum of all Amaranthine who have ever lived and died. It is their history, their library, their internet, their god. They can query it for ancient knowledge, feel the echoes of symphonies composed millennia ago, or simply bask in the silent presence of their countless ancestors. It is why they are never lonely in the vast emptiness of the plains.

When an Amaranthine grows old, its movements slow, its connection to the Sub-Chant deepens, and its own personal song becomes quieter. They do not fear death; they call it the Rejoining. An elder will walk, or be carried, to a designated place within a Spire or a hollow deep in the Phyllom. There, they settle one last time, sinking their spurs into the living floor. The decomposer organisms, the crystalline slow-movers, are not ghouls but acolytes. They respectfully envelop the Amaranthine, not consuming, but *un-making*. They carefully disassemble the physical form, returning the precious ichor and biological matter to the system. The last, resonant chord of the Amaranthine's individual consciousness isn't extinguished; it is uploaded in its entirety into the Sub-Chant, its unique melody becoming a permanent theme within the eternal symphony of the world. A new Amaranthine, budding from a nursery-pod miles away, might one day feel that ancestor's song as a faint, complex harmony within the deep hum of the world into which it is born. They are their own descendants and their own ancestors, a closed loop of life, art, and consciousness on a world that sings in the twilight.
