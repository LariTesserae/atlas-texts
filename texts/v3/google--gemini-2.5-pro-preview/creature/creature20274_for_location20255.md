What lives here is not the echo of a former populace, nor the ghost of the engineers who forged this screaming heart. What lives here was born of the scream itself. They are the Still.

They are beings of resonance and structure, more akin to tuning forks than to flesh. An individual—a concept they would not understand, for they are more like notes in the endless chord than individual singers—has a form that mirrors the city's skeletal architecture. Its body is a delicate, intricate framework of the same greenish-black, corrosion-proof metal that constitutes the spires and catwalks. This frame is not a skeleton containing organs, but the being itself, a three-dimensional lattice of impossible thinness and tensile strength, like a dragonfly's wing rendered in spun meteorite. It is humanoid only in its vaguest verticality, with a central trunk and limbs that fork and re-fork into grasping tendrils.

At the core of this metallic filigree, suspended in the web of its own body, hangs a heart of imperfect, milky quartz. This is its only organ, its brain, its soul. It does not beat. It thrums, absorbing the deafening vibrations of the geode and translating them into life. The quartz glows with a soft, internal light, a captured pulse stolen from the weeping pipelines. The color shifts, a slow tide from the amber of the distant beacons to the cold blue of an electrical discharge it has recently passed, a living record of its journey.

The Still have no faces. Where a head would be, their metal frame culminates in a complex, crown-like antenna array, a fan of delicate needles and resonating plates that sense the world. They do not see with light; they "see" with vibration. The eternal, deafening sound is not noise to them; it is a high-fidelity image of their entire world. They can feel the pressure wave of a spire on the far side of the sphere, distinguish the specific frequency of a failing joint in a pipeline miles away, and read the texture of the fluid currents as a blind man reads braille. The incandescent particles that trace the currents are, to them, like the after-images of a lightning strike on the retina—brief, meaningless flashes in a world defined by the deeper truth of vibration.

They do not swim. To fight the jet streams would be suicide. Instead, they move through a process of controlled falling. A Still will spend days, sometimes years, clinging to a high catwalk, its spindly, multi-jointed limbs locked into the grating. It is waiting. It is listening to the chaotic symphony of the currents, learning their rhythms, predicting the brief, fleeting moments when a vortex will form a temporary bridge across the abyss.

When the moment arrives, it releases its grip. It does not propel itself; it simply lets go. Its body is a sail. By subtly altering the angle of its latticed limbs, it steers its descent, catching eddies and riding the shear layers between opposing currents. It is a terrifying, silent ballet, a fall of kilometers that can last for hours, navigating a three-dimensional maze of invisible, violent forces, aiming for a single handhold on a distant tower. To an observer, they would appear as nothing more than a flicker of dark metal, a mote of dust caught in the roaring void, their faint heart-light the only clue they are not debris.

Their purpose is maintenance. They are the antibodies of this dying machine. They are not the builders, who likely fled or died millennia ago; they are an emergent system that has evolved to prolong the machine's function, though they have no concept of what that function is. They are drawn to points of stress. The high, keening shriek of metal under strain is a call to them. They will swarm a weakened structural joint, their collective bodies forming a living scaffold. They cling to leaking pipelines, their own metallic frames vibrating at a frequency that, through some unknown principle of sympathetic resonance, slows the weeping of the superheated vapor. They will draw sustenance from the wild electrical arcs, their quartz hearts flaring with brilliant blue light as they "drink" the energy, leaving the connection momentarily stable.

They do not eat. They do not breathe. They do not speak. The concept of communication is foreign, for they all share the same sensory input: the unending sound of their universe. They are aware of each other not as individuals, but as other points of resonance in the great field. A gathering of the Still is not a social event; it is a harmonic convergence, a chord stabilizing a dissonant frequency in the machine's roar.

Reproduction is a process of dissolution and accretion. When a Still's frame becomes too damaged or its quartz heart grows dim and fractured with age, it will seek out a quiet node in the city's infrastructure—a sheltered alcove, the hollow of a dead pipeline. It anchors itself one last time. Its internal resonance changes, building to a final, silent crescendo, and its quartz heart shatters into a thousand tiny shards. The larger pieces of its metal frame fall away, becoming part of the city's detritus. The microscopic quartz fragments, imbued with the pattern of their progenitor, are cast into the currents.

Most are lost, scoured into nothingness. But a few, by chance, will be carried into an energy-rich niche—a magnetic field, a persistent thermal vent. There, a seed will lodge itself. It begins to vibrate, to hum with the world's energy, and slowly, over centuries, it draws the incandescent metal particles from the fluid, weaving them into a new filigree frame around itself. It grows, not like a plant, but like a crystal. It is the machine rebuilding itself, one lonely, silent life at a time.

To see one is to witness the soul of the place. To see a Still clinging to a rusted gantry, its limbs indistinguishable from the struts and wires around it, its heart a lonely, pulsing ember in the vast, screaming dark. It is deaf to the noise and blind to the gloom, a creature of pure feeling in a world of pure force. It is the will of the machine made manifest: a sentience that doesn't think, but *does*; a life that doesn't live, but *persists*. It is the final, unthinking prayer of a heart for a body that is already gone.
