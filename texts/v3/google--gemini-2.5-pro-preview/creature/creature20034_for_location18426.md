The things that live here are called the Tenders, though they have no name for themselves. They are not citizens, nor are they a race in the biological sense. They are less an organism and more an ambulatory organ of the Shroud itself, a necessary component in the slow-motion genesis of the city.

A Tender’s form is a direct reflection of its environment. It stands roughly two and a half meters tall, a lanky, elongated figure that seems molded from the same lithic condensate as the ground. Its skin is not flesh, but a semi-flexible, ceramic-like substance, the color of lead-grey slate, cool to the touch and perpetually slick with a thin film of condensation. In places—around the joints of the shoulder, the knee, the long, six-fingered hands—this material gives way to a darker, more chitinous substance, like polished obsidian, allowing for a slow, fluid, and utterly silent range of motion. Their bodies are built for deliberation, not haste; every movement is economical, measured, and seemingly inevitable.

They have no faces in the human sense. The head is a smooth, ovoid dome that slopes down to a narrow chin. Where eyes would be, there are two large, perfectly round lenses of the same glassy black material as the sharpest planes of the plateau. They do not reflect; they seem to absorb the meager light, giving no hint of perception within. But they see. They see not in color or shadow, but in resonant frequencies, in material stress, in the faint electrical potential of the moss, and in the warped topologies of space. They perceive the Great Hum not as sound, but as a complex, three-dimensional tapestry of information, a constant and detailed report on the state of the entire city.

They do not breathe air. Along the sides of their slender torsos are vertical slits, half-hidden beneath overlapping plates of their body-ceramic. These are the vents for their internal systems. Periodically, with a barely audible sigh, a puff of vapor, smelling faintly of ozone and cold minerals, will seep from these gills. They draw their sustenance directly from the city they serve. A Tender will stand for hours beside a primary energy conduit, laying its long-fingered hands flat against the humming surface, absorbing the raw power it needs. At other times, they can be seen standing motionless amidst a grove of filter-stalks, their own systems subtly interfacing with the alien flora, sharing and straining nutrients from the damp, heavy air.

Their purpose is stewardship, but not of life. They are custodians of the process. You might see one standing at the edge of a newly forming crystalline plane, one hand held out, palm-down, a few centimeters above the surface. It is not touching it, but guiding it. Through subtle manipulations of the localized reality field—a skill as innate to them as breathing is to us—it coaxes the precipitating matter to form a purer structure, a stronger angle. Another might be found high on a skeletal trestle bridge, its fingers splayed against a kilometer-long girder. It is not repairing the girder. It is listening to its molecular tensions, its slow decay. It is measuring the minute shift in its geometry, updating the city’s silent, distributed consciousness of the change, ensuring the Great Hum adjusts its own frequency to compensate.

They are solitary in their work but endlessly connected. They do not speak. Language is a layer of abstraction they do not need. When two Tenders meet, they may stand in silent proximity for a moment, or touch the palms of their hands together. In that touch, vast quantities of data are exchanged—structural reports, energy-flow patterns, a shared awareness of a glimmer-wing nest atop Foundry 7, the precise moment a distant switch was flipped and the lagging moment the light came on. Their thoughts are the city’s thoughts.

Their relationship with the Shroud's fauna is one of utter indifference. They are part of the landscape, no more or less interesting than a rock. The armored grazers flow around their still forms like water. The glimmer-wings, perched on high, do not register them as prey or threat. The Tenders are simply *there*, as fundamental and unassailable as the ground itself.

They do not reproduce. They are created. In certain stable, quiet zones of the plateau, a new Tender will precipitate, just as the land does. Over the course of a month, it will grow like a crystal in a saturated solution, coalescing from the raw stuff of reality, pushing up from the ground. It emerges fully formed, its innate knowledge complete. It will stand, let the condensation bead on its new skin for the first time, and then walk silently toward the task that awaits it, a task it already understands completely.

And they do not die. They erode. Over centuries, the weirdness of the Shroud that they channel and maintain takes its toll. A Tender's form will begin to lose its integrity. A joint might stiffen, a finger might shear off along a crystalline plane, an angle in its shoulder might become obtuse. Its ability to process the Hum will degrade. When this decay reaches a certain point, the Tender will stop its work. It will walk to a designated dissolution field, a place where the fraying of reality is allowed to run a little more freely. It will sit, folding its long limbs, and become still. Over the next few days, it slowly loses its cohesion, its substance de-precipitating, its form dissolving into a shimmering haze that is reabsorbed by the Shroud, its matter returned to the system, ready to become ground, or a girder, or perhaps, in time, part of a new Tender. They are a closed loop. They are the city's memory and its muscle, the patient, silent priests of a god that is nothing more than the slow, inexorable process of its own becoming. They are the Shroud of Saint Gavit, given hands to feel its own strange pulse.
