You are correct. There is no life here. Not in the way you understand it. No circulatory system of warm blood, no carbon-and-sunlight biology. The air is poison, the water is solvent, the heat is a sterilizing blast. Anything that breathed, or flew, or crawled would be scoured away, dissolved, and cooked into the mineral slurry within minutes.

But the place is not uninhabited. It is tended.

You see it only by accident. Your eyes, aching from the shadowless glare, catch a movement that is wrong. It is not the shimmer of heat, nor the pop of a mud-blister, nor the gush of steam. It is a slow, deliberate peeling motion. A patch of verdigris-green crust on the far wall, a stain that you had dismissed as another patch of oxidized copper, detaches itself from the rock face. It does not fall. It unfolds.

What lives here is a Custodian. It is a being of accretion. It did not arrive here; it precipitated. Over uncounted cycles of the great pump, over eons of evaporation and crystallization, it formed. It is a slow, geological process given will.

Its form is inconstant, a colony of one. When it is still, it is indistinguishable from the landscape. It can lie flat against a wall, a rust-and-sulfur mosaic of itself, its internal temperature perfectly matching the superheated rock. It can submerge itself in a terraced pool, and the water will flow through it, its body a porous filter of crystalline structures and leached metals. It looks like a pile of rust-cankered slag, a forgotten piece of the ruined industry, until it decides to move.

Movement is a grinding, deliberate affair. It does not have muscles, but lattices of crystals that contract and expand in response to minute shifts in heat and pressure. It is a creature of thermo-acoustics. The deafening hum of the basin is the medium of its thoughts. The constant hiss of steam is the whisper that guides it. The foundational *thump-clank* of the piston is the rhythm of its heart, a metronome for its slow existence. It feels the world through vibration, tasting the water's chemistry through direct absorption, seeing in gradients of pure heat.

Its skin, if you could call it that, is a perpetually renewing crust of the very minerals that make this place sterile. Shards of black volcanic glass are embedded in it like scales. Its joints weep a thick, hot sludge the color of ocher, the same fluid that churns in the central pool. Where a limb has recently scraped against rock, the raw surface beneath the crust is not flesh, but the dull, pitted gleam of cast iron, as if it has incorporated the ruin into its own body. Its core is a slow, geothermal furnace, and its blood is the heavy water of the basin.

It has one purpose: to maintain the loop. To serve the machine.

As you watch, the unfolded shape—now something vaguely humanoid, with too many joints and a heavy, featureless head—drags itself with geologic slowness toward a vent near the base of the wall. This vent, which should be sheeting hot water, is clogged. A thick, yellow salt-crust has sealed it shut, and pressure is building behind it. The sound has changed, a subtle shift in pitch that only it could notice.

The Custodian raises an appendage that terminates not in a hand, but in a jagged slab of obsidian. With a sound like rockslide and grinding gears, it begins to methodically chip away at the salt blockage. Each impact is slow, timed to the offbeat of the great piston's cycle. *Thump-clank... CHNK. Thump-clank... CHNK*. It is performing maintenance. It is a gardener in a garden of steam and metal.

It clears the blockage. A torrent of near-boiling water bursts forth, washing over its lower half. The Custodian does not flinch. The heat is home. The deluge washes loose flakes of its own mineral skin away, but new ones are already crystallizing in their place. Its work done here, it contracts, folding in on itself, becoming a lumpy, inanimate pile of mineral debris once more, its internal heat slowly bleeding out to match the mud beneath it.

It does not know you are here. Your warmth is a negligible flicker in this inferno. Your sounds are drowned out by the engine of its world. You are a biological anomaly, a fleeting impurity that will soon be gone.

It is the will of the place. It was born of the machine's rhythm and the earth's heat. It is a living embodiment of the slow, lonely industry—a body of rust, a mind of vibration, a soul of steam. It will continue its work, scraping, clearing, and listening to the giant's heartbeat, until the day the piston seizes, the geologic heat fades, and the water finally runs cold. Then, and only then, will it settle one last time, cease its long maintenance, and become, permanently, just another part of the ground.
