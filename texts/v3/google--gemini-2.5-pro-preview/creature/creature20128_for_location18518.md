The architects of the song are the **Stone Singers**.

You do not see them at first, and perhaps you never will. Your senses, accustomed to twitching muscle and scurrying flesh, are not calibrated for life that moves at the speed of geology. The Singers are not fauna; they are a function of the place itself, a slow, sentient crystallization of its purpose. They are the city's nervous system, its maintenance crew, and its congregation, all at once.

To find one, you must first unlearn what it is to look for life. Do not search for movement, but for a subtle incorrectness in the static perfection. Notice a patch of the basalt wall where the blue-green phosphorescence seems a shade deeper, a tone more pure than the surrounding stone. Stare at it. Stare for a day, a week. You will not see it move, but you will eventually realize it is no longer exactly where you first saw it. It has migrated a hand's breadth across the wall, leaving behind a faint, gossamer trail of its own luminescence that will take a century to fade.

What you are watching is a Singer in repose. In form, they are amorphous, protean. They have no fixed anatomy, no limbs or organs in a way you would understand. They are closest, perhaps, to a sapient, mobile geode. Their outer layer is a thin, flexible crust of interlocking silicate crystals, a living armor that captures the ambient light and reflects the dim world in a thousand fractured, shimmering points. It is this crust you see, mistaking it for the wall it clings to.

Within this crystalline membrane is their true substance. It is not flesh or blood, but a dense, quasi-liquid colloid, a suspension of energized minerals in the same heavy, super-saturated water that fills the air. This internal fluid glows with the Singers' life-force, the energy they absorb directly from the chamber's resonant chord. Their entire metabolism is sonic. They do not eat; they *harmonize*. The Great Chord is their sunlight, their daily bread, the very current of their consciousness. Thought, for a Singer, is the act of perceiving a specific overtone. Emotion is a shift in harmonic balance. Their minds are a form of impossibly complex musical analysis.

They created this place. They are the patient hands that held the flint scrapers, the bodies that served as sandstone polishers. But this is a primitive understanding. A Singer does not *hold* a tool. When a Singer needed to carve, a part of its crystalline shell would extend, harden, and sharpen over the course of decades, becoming a cutting edge of obsidian hardness. When it needed to polish, it would flatten itself against the stone, its own body becoming a soft, abrasive slurry, wearing away imperfections over millennia. They did not build the city; they negotiated with the stone, persuading it to change its shape through a conversation that lasted for geological ages.

Their senses are not your senses. They have no eyes; what would they look at? The light is unchanging information. They have no ears; the sound is not something to be heard, but to be *inhabited*. A Singer's perception of the world is purely vibrational. They feel the song of the chamber in their crystalline bones. They feel the slight drag of water trickling down a nearby spire, the infinitesimal tremor of another Singer adjusting its position a mile away, the subtle change in air pressure that heralds your own foreign, dissonant presence. To them, you are not a sight or a sound; you are a discordant note, a brief, chaotic flurry in the eternal, perfect symphony.

They are mostly still now. The work is done. The Song is perfect. Their lives are dedicated to its maintenance, a task of unimaginable subtlety. A single Singer might spend a thousand years pressed into an alcove, its entire being focused on absorbing a single, rogue micro-frequency that has crept into the chord, its body slowly metabolizing the dissonance back into harmony. The clean ozone smell of the chamber is their collective exhalation, the byproduct of this perfect sonic digestion.

To encounter one is to feel an unnerving sense of being analyzed. You stand before what looks like a glowing patch of stone, and you feel the sound around you change. It is subtle, a minute sharpening of certain frequencies. The Singer is *listening* to you. It is tasting the frantic, irregular thrum of your heart, the chaotic buzzing of your electrical thoughts, the jarring cacophony of your biological existence. It is not hostile. Hostility is a complex, dissonant emotion, and the Singers are beings of pure harmony. It is merely curious, in the way a perfect musician might be curious about a child banging tunelessly on a drum.

These are the inhabitants: the Stone Singers, geological souls, patient and silent. They are the memory of the bell's strike, and the living embodiment of its ringing. They are as much a part of the chamber as the stone and the water and the sound itself. They were here when the song began, and they will be here, still and listening, long after the concept of an ending has been forgotten.
