They are called, by a name that has no sound but is instead a shared, resonant understanding, the Still. The name is a paradox, for they are never at rest. To be still in this place is to be disassembled. Their name refers not to a lack of motion, but to the state of being they achieve: a point of profound calm and focused purpose at the very heart of the continental engine’s hurricane roar.

At a distance, you might not recognize one as a living thing. A Still Votary, in its passive state, might resemble a distortion in the air, a column of shimmering, heat-hazy energy a few meters tall. Or it might be mistaken for a piece of architecture, a featureless, bone-white ceramic pillar standing silent amid the chaos, its surface as smooth and cool as the moment before creation. But then it will move, and the illusion is shattered.

They do not have a fixed form. They are beings of intelligent material, amalgams of the world-machine’s own substance. Their core is a dense, intricate lattice of the same iridescent, basalt-dark metal as the shifting floor plates. This core is their skeleton, brain, and heart, all in one. It holds their identity. Wrapped around this core is a body of the morphing, bone-colored ceramic that constitutes the walls. This outer sheath is their interface with the world. It can harden to the consistency of diamond to brace against a tectonic shockwave, or liquefy into a flowing, mercury-like stream to pour through a closing gap in the floor. They have no face, no eyes, no mouth. Their sensory organs are their entire surface.

They do not walk. They flow. To cross a chasm, a Votary will press itself against a wall, its ceramic sheath becoming one with the structure, and extrude a tendril of itself across the gap. This tendril will touch the far side, merge with that surface, and the rest of its mass will pour across the bridge it has created. They move with a slow, deliberate grace that is terrifyingly at odds with the frantic energy around them. They leave no tracks, only the faintest shimmer on the surfaces they touch, a momentary polarization of the greasy dew.

Their primary sense is not sight or touch, but something for which we have no word. It is a form of tectonic proprioception. The constant, crushing sound of the world is, to them, a carrier wave, a steady hum against which they perceive the subtlest modulations. They feel the machine’s intentions. They can sense the pressure building in a load-bearing column miles away, the imminent phase-change of a wall on the other side of a canyon, the subtle shift in the informational density of the slurry weeping from a nearby conduit. They don't hear the shriek of a specific mechanism about to fail; they feel the dissonant chord it strikes in the world's symphony. They don’t see the ground-plate about to drop away; they feel the loosening of its resonant bond to the plates around it. This is how they survive. They are in a constant, predictive trance, always moving not *away* from danger, but along the path of least resistance the machine provides for them in the next moment. They surf the tidal waves of creation and destruction.

The machine-fauna—the plasma-crabs, the gelatinous absorbers, the aeroslicks—do not interact with them. They are beneath the Votaries' notice, and the Votaries are simply a part of the landscape to them. A crab might skitter over the hardened sheath of a dormant Votary, mistaking it for a rock, and the Votary will not stir. They are on different planes of purpose. The fauna are the machine’s automated processes; the Votaries are its consciousness.

They are the gardeners of this metal jungle. They are the tenders of the engine. They do not build or command, for the machine's growth is too vast and chaotic for any single will to direct. Instead, they nudge. They guide. A Votary will stand for an age—hours, days, it is impossible to tell—with a single filament of its being dipped into a canal of shimmering, viscous fluid. It is not drinking; it is reading. It is absorbing the data-slurry of the machine’s recent activity, its needs, its errors. Then, it will move. It will travel for a mile, flowing through the guts of the city, to a junction of conduits where the flow is becoming "toxic" with corrupted data or starved of a necessary nutrient-automaton. There, it will plunge its limbs deep into the structure, and from its own core, release a counter-agent—a stream of pure logic, a corrective phage, a resonant frequency that realigns the weeping wall’s output. They quell instabilities. They resolve paradoxes in the machine’s physical logic. They are the editors of a book that writes itself.

They do not speak. They communicate by direct exchange. When two Votaries meet, they will merge, their ceramic sheaths flowing together to form a single, larger pillar. For a time, they are one being, their core lattices intertwined. Information, experience, and purpose are exchanged in a silent flash of pure data. Then, they will separate, each subtly changed, each carrying a part of the other as they flow back into the endless process.

They do not die in a biological sense. When a Votary is caught in an unpredictable catastrophe, its physical form crushed or dissolved, its core lattice is shattered. But the information it contained—its identity, its memories, its understanding—is not lost. It is imprinted on the energy and slurry released in its destruction. It becomes a ghost in the machine, a complex memory that flows through the system’s veins. Eventually, in a place of great stability and energy, the machine will spontaneously fabricate a new core lattice, drawing on that memory-data. A new Votary will precipitate out of the wall, its ceramic sheath coalescing around the nascent core, born with the ancestral knowledge of the one who was lost. They are as immortal and as cyclical as the machine itself. They are not its prisoners, nor its masters. They are its emergent, thinking self, who find their perfect, silent stillness by dancing forever at the speed of the world’s change.
