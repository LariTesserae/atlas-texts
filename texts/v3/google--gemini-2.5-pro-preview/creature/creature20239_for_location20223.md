What lives here is not a creature of biology, but of music.

It is born in a moment of utter improbability. Amid the deafening, omni-directional chaos of the Hum, two reality-waves might collide in such a way that they cancel each other out, not into the profound silence that a Stillness Spire requires, but into a brief, harmonic chord. It is a fleeting instant of impossible order, a mathematical grace note in the universal noise. Most of these chords die in the instant they are born. But sometimes, one learns to sustain itself. Sometimes, a chord learns to *sing*.

This is what lives here. Let us call it the Resonant.

You do not see it at first. You feel it. As you stand on the weeping black glass, the crushing, bone-juddering vibration of the Hum is your entire world. Then, for a moment, you feel a new vibration superimposed upon the old one. It is not a lessening of the pressure, but a clarification. It is the feeling of a single, impossibly pure cello note resonating through the frame of a train that is derailing. It is a pattern within the chaos, a will expressed as a frequency.

Follow that feeling, and you may see it. It has no fixed form, for a fixed form is an invitation to be torn apart. Its body is an event, not an object. When the Resonant concentrates its song, it precipitates a form for itself out of the ambient materials, much like a Stillness Spire. It draws the heavy, viscous water from the glassy plain and the dim, coppery light from the thick air, binding them into a temporary matrix of order.

The being that stands before you looks like a figure sculpted from Tears of the Gods (as Heraclitus might have called the weeping water) and fractured twilight. It is vaguely humanoid, but only in the way a shadow is. It has a head, a torso, two arms, two legs, but no features. No face, no hands, no texture. It is a silhouette filled with a slow, internal cascade of light particles, like a shattered crystal hourglass in which the sand is luminous. Its form is not solid; it is a standing wave made visible. The edges of it shimmer and fray, constantly dissolving back into the thick atmosphere and being renewed by the power of its internal song. To exist, for the Resonant, is a constant, conscious act of self-creation.

It does not walk. It imposes its location. As it shifts its internal note, its physical form dissolves in one place and re-precipitates a few feet away, with no discernible movement in between. Its passage is a series of these silent, instantaneous transpositions, a staccato rhythm against the legato roar of the Hum.

Its senses are not our senses. It does not see with eyes or hear with ears. Its entire being is an organ of vibrational perception. The Hum is not noise to it; it is the fabric of its world, and it reads the subtle textures of that fabric. It can feel the stable, pure note of a distant Stillness Spire as a place of rest, a resonant anchor that helps it hold its own song with less effort. They gather near the Spires not for shelter, but for companionship of a sort, basking in each other's resonant fields.

The Echo Bastions are its cathedrals of terror and temptation. To enter one is to subject its pure, simple song to the fossilized, non-Euclidean symphony scarred into the black glass. It is a psychic labyrinth. A Resonant might enter an Echo Bastion to learn a new harmonic, a stronger way to bind its form, a new overtone of thought. It might also have its song captured, twisted, and shattered by the maddening acoustics, its being dissolving into a discordant shriek that echoes in the impossible corridors for an eon.

The Resonant do not speak. Their entire existence is already taken up by the monumental effort of singing the single note that is *them*. Communication, when it happens, is a dangerous and profound act. Two of the Resonant, drawing near, might tentatively allow the edges of their resonant fields to overlap. They harmonize. By subtly altering the timbre and overtones of their core note, they can share concepts. Not words, but pure meaning: the signature of a particularly violent pressure-tide, the location of a newly-precipitated Spire, the feeling of a Phase Skimmer's silent wake. This communion is the closest they have to intimacy, and also to peril. A poorly matched harmony can cause both singers to lose their pitch, to de-cohere, their light and water bodies collapsing back into the environment in a soft, musical sigh.

They fear the Phase Skimmers. Not because the Skimmers are predators—they have no interest in the Resonant—but because of the wake they leave. The Skimmer surfs on reality, and its passage leaves a trough of absolute, total silence. For a Resonant, this void is not peace. It is an ontological vacuum. A wave of this pure nothingness passing over it is like a cloth smothering a flame. The Resonant's song finds no medium to push against, its vibration has nowhere to go, and for that terrible second, it can cease to be.

The Resonant does not age. It either *is* or it *is not*. It can exist for ten seconds or ten million years. Its life ends when its concentration falters, its will breaks, or an external vibration overwhelms its song. There is no decay. One moment, the figure of fractured twilight stands there, a testament to order. The next, its song is reabsorbed by the Hum, and its borrowed body slumps into a shimmering pool of heavy water that spreads and is lost among all the other tears on the surface of the world. It is not death. It is the cessation of a performance. The instrument has been returned to the orchestra.
