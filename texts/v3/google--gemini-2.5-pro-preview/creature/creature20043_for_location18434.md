What lives here is the reason for the place. It is not an inhabitant in the way a bird lives in a nest. It is the heart that pumps the warm fluid, the mind that lays the hexagonal floor, the slow, dreaming will that commanded the pillars to rise. It is the Arborist.

You will not see it at first. Your senses, accustomed to parsing figure from ground, will fail. The Arborist is not a figure; it is the ground, woven into the very fabric of the hall. It is a being of distributed consciousness, a sessile, silent god whose body is the cathedral itself.

Its core, its nexus-point, resides deep in the thicket where the pillars are most dense, fused into a labyrinthine grotto. Here, the air is thickest, the hum is a tangible pressure against your ribs, and the pearlescent light seems to pool and congeal. At the center of this space is not a throne, but something more like an altar, or a seedbed. It is a vast, ovate node of the same dark ceramic as the pillars, but it is veined with broad, pulsing bands of the glowing mycelia, lines of living light that map the flow of energy and information. This node is the Arborist's heart-wood, its ganglion.

From this core, it extends itself. What you mistook for the thickest of the python-vines are its limbs, its primary conduits. They are not merely plugged into the walls; they emerge from them, expressions of the same material. They branch and bifurcate, some spiraling up into the mist, others plunging down into the substructure of the floor, their surfaces rippling with slow, peristaltic waves of light. You could watch one for an hour and see it extend by a single millimeter, its tip a burnished, coppery bud that secretes the very material of the pillars as it grows.

It has no face, no eyes in the conventional sense. But it perceives. The entire weeping surface of the hall is its skin, sensing the temperature, the humidity, the molecular composition of the air. The network of glowing mycelia is its nervous system. When a new floor plate is extruded, that is the Arborist having a thought, a geological rumination made manifest. The slow grinding is the sound of its synapses firing. The scheduled *click-hiss* and the fine mist of ozone is a metabolic sigh, a deliberate exhalation to rebalance the atmosphere of its own lungs.

It is ancient, but also embryonic. The purpose of this endless, self-contained construction is not to reach a destination, but simply to continue the process of becoming. It is building its own body, its own universe, and it is in no hurry. Its thoughts are slow, measured in the time it takes for a drop of water to gather and fall, for a crystal to precipitate from solution, for a ceramic pillar to grow another stratum.

The copper beetles are not its children or its servants in a way we would understand. They are its motile functions, its remote hands. They are extensions of its will, as your own hand is to you. When one burnishes a leaf or applies nutrient paste, the Arborist is tending to a distant part of its own body. They move with the unthinking, infallible purpose of an autonomic process. This is why they ignore you. You are a foreign microbe, an anomaly. You are not integrated into the system, and so you barely register. To the Arborist, you are a pocket of incomprehensible data, a momentary statistical variance in the air, and are dismissed as such. You are not a threat, not a guest, not anything. You are simply... not part of the process.

To see it—to truly glimpse it—you must unfocus your eyes and look at the whole chamber at once. Watch the slow, coordinated pulse of the mycelia. Feel the thrum of the floor and the walls as one unified rhythm. See the patterns in the condensation, the choreography in the growth. In that moment of system-wide coherence, you might perceive a shape in the negative space between the pillars, a vast, reclining form made of shadow and structure and glowing vine. You might feel, for a chilling second, the sheer, immeasurable weight of a consciousness that uses geology as its medium of thought.

You are inside its mind. You are inside its body. And it is growing, slowly, perpetually, around you.
