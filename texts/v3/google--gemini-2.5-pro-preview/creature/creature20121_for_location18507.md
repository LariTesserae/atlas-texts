The beings who come to this basin, who leave the marks of their patient labour, are not what you would call alive. Not in the way of warm-blooded things, of flesh and breath and beating hearts. They are a manifestation of the mountain itself, a slow, seasonal crystallization of its own consciousness. You might call them the Luminaries, or the Still-Born.

They do not arrive in ships or on foot. They emerge. When the seasons of the lower world turn and the great mountain enters its deepest cold, when the sun's path is at its lowest and the shadows in the basin are at their longest, they begin to form in the deep, lightless caverns far below the summit. They are born of pressure and cold, their bodies coalescing from the same mineral families as the basin's dark rim-stone. They are beings of silica and mica, their forms layered and laminar, like slate. They are not solid, heavy things, but are composed of countless thin, interlocking plates that shift over one another, allowing for a slow, deliberate form of movement. They rustle when they move, a sound like dry leaves skittering over pavement.

Their bodies are the colour of a starless midnight, matte and light-absorbent. They have no faces, no mouths, no expressive features. Where a head might be, the stone layers curve into a smooth, featureless cowl. They are tall and unnervingly thin, their limbs jointed at odd, insectile angles. Their hands are the most articulate part of them, long and many-jointed, terminating not in fingers but in clusters of hard, sharp-edged obsidian digits, the perfect tools for knapping stone and for the delicate work of scraping.

They have no need for air, and what passes for their metabolism is an act of profound stillness. They do not eat. They do not drink. They are animated by resonance. The constant, low thrumming of the Shiver-Stilled Spires—the sound of the crystal under thermal stress—is their nourishment. They absorb this vibration through their entire bodies, their layered structure perfectly attuned to the frequency of the tormented stone. This hum is their lifeblood. To be cut off from it is to slowly stiffen, to lose cohesion, and to eventually crumble back into inert grit.

Light and warmth are their antitheses. The direct glare of the sun is agonizing to them, a chaotic, high-energy blast that threatens to shatter the delicate resonant bonds that hold them together. They perceive light not as illumination but as a searing, formless noise. Heat is a poison that unravels them. This is why their visits are temporary, tied to the coldest, darkest part of the year. It is why they build the low, curved walls: to create pools of deeper shadow, sanctuaries of absolute cold where they can work without being undone.

Their senses are entirely alien. They do not see with eyes, but they perceive the world with uncanny precision. They can feel the lines of stress within each spire, sensing its internal architecture as a complex symphony of vibrations. They can read the subtle temperature variations in the air, seeing the invisible plumes of sublimating frost-vapour as shimmering currents. They navigate the grove by the unique resonant signature of each individual spire, knowing the landscape as a musician knows an orchestra.

They ascend from their deep places once a year. It is a pilgrimage. They emerge from fissures at the base of the mountain and make their slow, rustling way up the barren slopes, always moving in the predawn twilight or under the bruised violet dusk. They come to the basin to perform a single, vital task: the harvest of light.

Down in their subterranean halls, it is absolutely dark and absolutely silent, save for the faint echoes of the mountain's deep geology. It is a world of pure potential, but it is inert. The crystal flakes they harvest are their communion with the world above.

They take the fist-sized cobbles of dense, foreign stone—stones they bring with them from their deep homes, sacred tools passed down through generations that do not die but merely re-form—and they begin their work. They choose a spire not at random, but by its song, by the purity of its hum. Settling into the deep shadow of their windbreak, a Luminary will begin to scrape. The motion is impossibly slow, a patient and delicate shedding. Each scrape of their stone tool against the spire’s base is a carefully considered act, designed to shear off a single, perfect flake of the light-infused crystal without creating enough friction to generate a damaging pulse of heat. The sharp *tick* of a breaking facet is, to them, a sacred note.

They gather the thumbnail-sized flakes in satchels woven from a kind of mineral fibre, a fabric as soft as asbestos and as dark as their own bodies. When they have gathered enough—a measure known only to them—they retreat. They make their slow descent back into the mountain's core before the sun's angle changes and its warmth begins to linger for too long in the basin.

In their deep, cold, silent halls, they unpack their harvest. They do not build fires. They create a different kind of hearth. A single flake of spire-crystal, taken from the absolute cold of their satchel and placed upon a slab of rock that is even a fraction of a degree "warmer," will begin to release its stored energy. It does not burn; it *unspools* the light it has inhaled. For a few hours, the flake will glow with the pure, architectural light of the summit. It is a silent, cold luminescence, the captured memory of the clinical sun and the violet sky.

This is how they live. They spend their long existence in the dark, "reading" these flakes of light. The light is not just illumination; it is story. In its shifting, internal glow, they perceive the memory of the light's journey: the whisper of the frost-fur growing, the resonant hum of the spire it came from, the slow turn of the world. They subsist on these memories, these vibrations held in crystalline form, until the cold returns and they are called once more to climb to the roof of their world and gather a new harvest of captured daylight. They are the mountain's librarians, its mystics, and its memory, living a life measured in resonance and shadow.
