What lives here are the Geodes.

They are not born, they do not breed. They precipitate. In the vast, still plazas where the mineral-rich vapour pools into thick, viscous mirrors of the lightless sky, they begin. A deep, undisturbed puddle, left for an age, will start to change. The water-that-is-not-water will thicken, not with the sharp, fractal growth of a new colonnade, but with something softer, more deliberate. A darkness gathers within the liquid, a core of shadow that seems to drink the faint, internal luminescence of the surrounding city.

Slowly, over centuries, this core congeals. It draws substance from the syrupy pool until it has a form, vaguely ovoid and large as a fallen boulder. For a time, it is inert, indistinguishable from any other dark, rounded stone on the plaza floor. But then, one day, something stirs within it. A crack appears—not a fissure of damage, but a slow, deliberate parting, like a geode being opened by an invisible hand. And from this crack, a light spills out.

It is the same captive twilight that illuminates the city, but here it is alive. It is a slow, churning nebula of indigo and silver, a vortex of trapped starlight. This is the Geode's core, its soul, its mind. The outer shell, the dark, vitreous onyx slick with condensation, is merely a vessel, a hardened rind of the same material as the city.

The form that rises from the pool is not fixed. It is an amorphous, flowing architecture of self. It can pull itself into a tall, slender pillar that mimics the exhausted towers, its single, luminous fissure a vertical slash of awareness. It can flatten itself, spreading across the wet stone like a living shadow, a moving slick of oil with a galaxy swirling at its heart. It has no limbs, no face, no organs. It does not need them.

It does not see with eyes. It perceives through resonance. The entire city is its sensory organ. When a slow, ponderous drop of mineral water finally falls from an archway a mile away, the Geode feels the soft *thump* not as a sound, but as a subtle shift in the city's total weight, a change in the tension of its own body. It senses the unstable geometry of the avenues as a constant, fluid pressure, a grammar it understands innately. To a Geode, a path that curves back on itself is not a paradox; it is a sentence turning to question itself. A doorway that is a portal is not a trick; it is an ellipsis, a leap in thought.

They breathe, but their respiration is a geological process. They draw in the hot, saturated vapour through the porous surface of their onyx shell. They are filter-feeders of the atmosphere. Inside them, the mineral that forms the city is processed. Some of it is used to maintain their own form, to patch the slow, constant erosion of their shell. The rest is metabolised into the cool, internal light of their consciousness. They are powered by the dissolution of their own world.

Their movement is utterly silent, a slow, deliberate glide. They are heavy, ponderous beings, yet they move with a frictionless grace across the slick ground, leaving no track, no trace of their passage. They are the embodiment of the city's silence.

When two Geodes meet, it is not an encounter but a convergence. They flow towards one another, their dark shells touching, softening, and then merging. For a time, they become a single, larger form, and their two internal nebulae of light spill into one another, intertwining in a slow, complex dance of colour and shadow. They do not speak. They exchange memories. They share the fragments of the broken dream that this place is. One might share the sensation of a sharp corner, a feeling utterly alien to this rounded world. Another might impart the memory of a color that doesn't exist here, the echo of a true sun's warmth. They are sharing pieces of a puzzle they can never complete. After a day, or a year, they will separate, each retracting into itself, carrying a new, unintelligible piece of the great, forgotten whole.

They are the city's consciousness, but a fractured one. Each Geode is a solitary thought, a lonely memory, wandering the physical manifestation of its own broken mind. They are the dreamers of this dreaming city. They tend to the crystalline "ferns," not by watering them, but by pausing near them for decades, their internal light bathing the delicate structures in a soft glow that seems to stabilise their fragile forms. They are the source of the low, tectonic hums, for when a truly colossal Geode settles to rest, the ground itself shivers.

And they die as they are born: slowly, quietly, and without ceremony. When a Geode's internal light begins to fade, its memories worn thin by eons of silent wandering, it finds a low place in the city. It ceases its movement, its form softening, losing its definition. The crack in its shell widens until the last of its light spills out and evaporates into the thick air like steam. The dark shell then slowly, inexorably dissolves, weeping its substance back into the plaza, becoming once more a nutrient-rich pool, a potential future, a place where a new tower—or a new thought—might one day begin to crystallize.

They are the city's inhabitants, its architects, and its substance. They are not living *in* the city; they are the city, living.
