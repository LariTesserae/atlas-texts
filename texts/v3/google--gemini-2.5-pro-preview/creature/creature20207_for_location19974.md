For a long time, you believe the city is empty. It is a conclusion so comprehensive, so reinforced by every sense, that it feels like a law of physics. The monumental sound is the sound of geology, not biology. The lushness is the slow, mindless victory of extremophilic flora. The structures are the relics of a species long vanished, their bones scoured clean and their story erased. You accept this. You walk the slick, humming floor for what feels like days, a solitary observer in a museum at the end of time.

You learn the city's rhythms. You learn how the flat, white glare of the high sun makes the upper arches almost impossible to look at, forcing your gaze downward. You learn the dappled green-gold gloom of the lower levels, where the vast gardens of phosphorescent moss and armored fern create a perpetual twilight. It is here, in the gloom, that you begin to doubt the emptiness.

It is a trick of the light at first. Or so you assume. As your eyes adjust to the dimness, you start to perceive forms that are not quite architecture and not quite vegetation. Tucked into the deep alcoves, seated at the base of the great, fluted columns, are shapes. They have the same milky, porcelain glaze as the city itself, the same concentric rings of soft grey and white. At a distance, they appear to be mere decorative bosses in the structure, bulbous outgrowths of the organic design.

But they are not.

You draw closer to one. The omnipresent hum grows deeper here, more concentrated. The air is even colder. The shape is humanoid only in its most basic vertical arrangement: a base, a torso, a crown. But all detail has been surrendered to a final, slumping smoothness. It is a figure, perhaps ten feet tall, sitting with an impossible posture of rest, as if it settled into this position a million years ago and the city grew up around it. No—it *is* the city. Its surface is the same weeping material. Rivulets of condensation trace paths down its broad, gentle curves, dripping from a rounded protrusion that might once have been a chin, or a knee. A patch of the blood-red, ceramic lichen has found purchase on one of its shoulders, a sharp, fractal stain on the placid white.

There are no limbs, no face. The head is a simple, featureless ovoid, tilted slightly as if listening. The body is a seamless flow of soft, volumetric shapes that merge directly into the wall behind it and the floor beneath it. It is not a statue of a being. It is the being itself, in its final form: sessile, silent, and alive.

These are the inhabitants. They are the Weepers.

They are the source of the cold. Their biological process, a form of life so slow it is functionally geological, radiates a profound chill, pulling the scant moisture from the thin air to cloak them in the weeping damp that gives them their name. They are alive in the way a glacier is alive. Their metabolism is a cycle of eons; their movement is accretion and erosion.

You begin to see them everywhere, once you know what to look for. Dozens, hundreds of them. They are always in the shade, always integrated into the architecture they themselves exuded. Some are solitary, immense figures of contemplation. Others are clustered in families, their smooth forms fused together into a single, complex sculpture of communal being. Their children, if that is the word, are smaller nubs at their bases, featureless and perfect. They are not individuals in any way you can comprehend. They are nodes.

The great, monolithic hum is not the wind. It is them. It is the resonance of their collective, unified consciousness—if that word still applies. It is not thought, but a state of being, expressed as a vibration. They are singing the single note of their existence, and the city, their shared body, is the instrument. To be here is to stand inside their chorus, inside their soul. The million glass-bell chimes are their tears, the condensation of their cold life, dripping and striking the resonant floor, an accidental music of their unending, placid sorrow or serenity. There is no longer a difference.

They did not die out. They *concluded*. They pursued a spiritual or evolutionary path that led them here. They built this city not with hands, but by extruding it from their own substance. They shed motion, shed individuality, shed struggle. They cultivated the moss and ferns as external organs, as gardens that clothe and sustain their final rest. They traded the fleeting warmth of life for the permanence of the mountain, the clamor of voices for the purity of a single, perfect tone.

They do not move. They do not react to your presence. To them, you are a flicker, a momentary disturbance in the light, a transient warm spot in their cold, eternal twilight. You are a dream they are having, already forgotten. They continue their single, immense activity: to exist. To be the city. To hum their note into the void. To weep without eyes. They are the living heart of the beautiful, singing skull, and they are waiting for nothing. They have arrived.
