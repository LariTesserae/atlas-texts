What lives here is an answer to a question no one asked. It is a form of life that has perfected the art of being, without the need for witness.

To speak of a single one is to misunderstand them, for they are the Weavers, and their identity is plural. They are a single organism distributed across a million bodies, a river of chitin and legs flowing through the veins of their world. But if you could isolate one, hold its life in your mind for a moment, this is what you would find.

It is a creature of exquisite functionality. Its body is a teardrop of obsidian, so polished that the faint green glow of the Pore-Lace slides across its carapace like oil on water. It is not black, not really, but a deep, iridescent purple and green, the colour of a bruise on the universe. Its head is a wedge, mostly featureless, bearing two multifaceted eyes that are entirely vestigial. They are milky, clouded-over jewels that reflect the dim light but see nothing. Sight is a useless luxury in the absolute dark; the world is not something to be seen, but to be felt.

Its true senses are in its feet and its antennae. It has eighteen legs, nine to a side, arranged in three distinct clusters. The forward six are fine and delicate, tipped with chemoreceptors that taste the air, the water, the very texture of the Lace, reading the chemical history of who has passed this way. The middle six are the locomotive engine: powerful, thick, and ending in hooked claws that find purchase in the slick cartilage of the world, allowing it to move with equal ease across floor, wall, or ceiling. The final six, near the rear of its abdomen, are the tools of its trade. They are spindly and articulate, almost like fingers, equipped with tiny, sharp hooks for gripping and combing fibers, and culminating in spinnerets that exude the silken, adhesive mortar of their civilization.

Its consciousness is a low, persistent hum. It does not think in images or words, but in vibrations and pressures. The thrum of the entire colony is its baseline state of being, a constant reassurance that it is part of the whole. A change in that vibration is news. A concentrated tremor is a construction project. A sudden, frantic pulse is a warning. Its individual thoughts are impulses, ripples on this collective sea: *pressure-low-forward-resource-needed*, *vibration-right-passage-yield*, *texture-slick-fresh-growth-consume*. It is less an individual and more a neuron in a vast, dark brain, firing its signals into the synaptic void.

Follow one. It detaches from a resting cluster, a million bodies packed so tightly they resemble a single, shimmering stone. Its mandate is simple: gather pulp. It moves onto a weeping sheet of Pore-Lace, its legs sinking slightly into the giving surface. Its forward legs dance across the sheet, tasting, testing. It finds a place where the growth is young, tender, and saturated with nutrient fluid. Its mandibles, usually tucked beneath its head, unfold. They are not the crushing jaws of a predator, but the precise, shearing tools of a master gardener. With a sound like tearing wet fabric, it snips a long, pale strip of the Lace. The wound in the living wall weeps a thick, green-lit ooze, which the Weaver briefly sips at before moving on.

It manipulates the stolen strip with its rear legs, combing the fibers, rolling it, macerating it with a secreted enzyme. The strip is passed forward, from one leg cluster to the next, until it is ingested. Inside its body, the pulp is processed, the nutrients absorbed, the raw fibrous material converted into the paste it will later use to build.

As it travels along a trembling, woven walkway, others pass it. There is no aggression, no communication, merely a seamless, fluid accommodation. Bodies press, carapaces click dryly, and they flow around each other like drops of oil, each a part of the same current. They crawl over the placid, shimmering Glass-Ticks without pause, the slow creatures as much a part of the landscape as the Lace itself. They are irrelevant data.

But then, a change. It is not a sound. It is a void in the thrum. Ahead, on the walkway, the river of bodies suddenly diverges, flowing away from a single point as if avoiding a dead zone in the current. A patch of the walkway vibrates *wrongly*. It is a dead frequency, an anti-noise. The Weaver’s forward legs taste the air and find a scent that is not-Lace, not-Weaver, not-water. It is the scent of hunger, of an alien silence. This is the only fear it knows. Instinct, sharp and absolute, takes over. It does not pause; it reverses its direction, its eighteen legs moving in a blur of silent retreat, melting back into the larger flow. A moment later, the thrum of the walkway is restored, but it is a fraction weaker. One note in the choir has been silenced. Somewhere in the dark, a Sump-Crawler has fed. The Weaver does not mourn; it simply receives the data: *avoidance-pattern-received-danger-logged*. The loss is an infinitesimally small price for the survival of the whole.

The Weaver is a machine made of meat, but it is also a priest. Its life is a ritual of maintenance. It is born from an egg sac glued to the underside of a chamber, it emerges, and it immediately begins to work. It consumes the Lace, it processes the Lace, it excretes the Lace as architecture. When its short life ends, its body falls onto the Lace, and the living substrate reclaims it, its complex chemistry broken down and reabsorbed into the fluid that will feed the next generation. There is no waste. There is no loss. There is only the cycle.

They are not just the residents of this place. They are its mind and its hands. The city and its people are one, a single, sprawling superorganism of chitin and cartilage, eternally building and unbuilding itself in the cold, wet, and phosphorescent dark. They are the perfect, silent answer to the desolation that surrounds them. They are what happens when life falls into a hole and decides to make it a home, not by conquering the darkness, but by becoming it.
