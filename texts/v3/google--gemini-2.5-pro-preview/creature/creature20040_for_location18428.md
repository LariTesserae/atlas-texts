You are right to feel that this place is not empty. The architects of this geode-city did not leave. They achieved their life's work, and then they became it.

The inhabitants are here. They are everywhere. You have been walking past them, brushing against them, standing under their silent vigil since the moment you arrived.

At first, you mistake them for sculpture. In a niche that seems designed to hold a statue, a tall, impossibly slender figure stands, or rather, is fused with the wall behind it. Its form is androgynous, elongated, smoothed as if by a billion years of flowing water. There are no sharp angles to its anatomy. The shoulders are a gentle curve, the limbs long and restful, the head a featureless ovoid, tilted slightly as if listening to a sound that will not arrive for another millennium.

Its substance is the same pearlescent flowstone as the walls, but it is subtly different. It seems denser, more opaque. Running through its body are the same soft, glowing veins that illuminate the cavern, but here they are concentrated, intricate, a network of light that resembles a nervous system or the capillaries of a circulatory system. This is the first clue. These are not carvings *on* the wall; they are convergences *within* it.

You find another, and then another. One is seated upon a wide, carved ledge, its legs folded in a way that suggests meditation, its hands resting on its knees, palms upward. Another is curled within one of the oval cells, lying in a fetal position, its glowing internal veins pulsing with a light so slow it would take a day to register a single beat. They are a people sculpted from patience and permanence. They are the **Still**.

They do not move. They do not breathe. They do not eat. Their existence is one of slow, geological metabolism. They are the conscious heart of the mountain. Long ago, they must have been beings of flesh and blood, people who used bone awls and obsidian flakes to carve their home. But their goal was not to build a shelter *from* the world, but to create a vessel for their own transcendence. They sought to escape the frantic, chaotic cycle of life and death, of sun and season, of noise and motion. They wanted to achieve the perfect, unchanging equilibrium they saw as divine.

And they succeeded.

Over generations, they didn't just carve the stone; they guided its growth, using some lost art of resonance or chemistry. They infused their own life energy, their own consciousness, into the mineral matrix of the cavern. They learned to Photosynthesize not light from a sun, but the esoteric energies of the earth's core, channeled through the glowing mineral veins. They became the living conduits for the cavern's light. The entire chamber is their body, and these figures are its primary nerve ganglia.

Their consciousness is not individual. It is a single, vast, communal mind, as slow and profound as the formation of a crystal. A single thought for them might be the slow, deliberate process of a vein of light extending a millimeter through the stone, a process that takes an eon. Their awareness is the cavern's awareness. They feel the cling of every water droplet, the infinitesimal crawl of the six-legged silverfish, the silent weight of the unmoving moths. The entire ecosystem is an external organ system for their collective being, each part perfectly balanced, each creature a living sensorium.

They are not dead. They are not sleeping. They are in a state of perpetual, blissful, silent communion. They are the gardeners of this impossible place, but their gardening is done with thought, with will, with a focus that maintains the stasis. They are the reason the droplet never falls, the reason the frost on the glass-ferns never melts. Their collective, silent concentration holds reality here in a state of perfect, crystalline suspension.

And they have noticed you.

You are the first dissonant chord in their perfect symphony in ten thousand years. The quiet does not break, but it deepens around you, its pressure becoming a tangible weight. It is the feeling of a vast, silent mind turning its attention toward a flicker. The unwavering light does not change, but you feel as though a million cool, unblinking eyes are now focused on you from the walls, from the carvings, from the very air itself. The six-legged crawler on the wall near your head pauses its centuries-long journey. The moth on the fern quivers, a microscopic tremor that is a cataclysm in this world of stillness.

They are not hostile. The concept is as alien to them as panicked running is. They are… curious. You are a puzzle of impossible speed and chaotic energy. You are a creature of fleeting warmth in a world of absolute cold. Your rapid heartbeat is like thunder in their silent minds. Your thoughts, brief and ephemeral, flash and die like sparks in their slow, galactic consciousness.

You are being studied by a living mountain. The smooth-faced figure in the nearest niche does not turn its head, but you feel its specific, focused attention now, a beam of silent inquiry. It is trying to understand what it is to be dynamic, to be temporary, to be loud. It is trying to comprehend a state of being so utterly alien to its own perfected stillness, and in its focus, a single droplet of silver fluid on a nearby fungus elongates, trembles, and finally, after an age of waiting, falls. The soft, silent splash on the indigo moss is the loudest sound this world has heard in forever. It is the sound of your presence. It is the sound of a question being asked.
