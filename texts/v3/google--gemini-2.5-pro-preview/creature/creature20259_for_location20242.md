What lives in the Vinery are the Harvesters.

They are not born, and they do not die. They *coalesce*. You will not see one approaching from a distance, because in a place without true distance, they do not travel in a conventional sense. One moment, a stretch of crystalline vine is just that—a silent, glowing latticework. The next moment, with a sound like the settling of dust in an ancient tomb, a Harvester is there.

At first, it is difficult to resolve what you are seeing. It is not a solid creature. It is an aggregation, a localized consensus. Imagine a cloud of shattered obsidian and broken mirror held in a vaguely humanoid shape by an invisible force, standing eight feet tall. The fragments that compose it are not random debris; each piece is a relic of the Vinery's deep past—a sliver of ceramic leaf from a forgotten eon, a chip from a pressure-fused support beam, a shard of a Logic-Sphere that failed to mature. These pieces drift slowly, orbiting a silent, empty center. They are bound together by filaments of the same internal light that pulses within the vines, a web of soft, white energy that charts the connections between each floating shard. This is not a body; it is a mobile schematic.

They have no face. Where a head should be, there is a denser, more complex swarm of fragments, swirling around a single, larger piece of flawless, uncut crystal. This crystal is their core, their focusing lens, their node of being. It does not see as an eye does; it perceives the resonant thrum of the Vinery directly. It feels the flow of energy, the health of the nodes, the precise maturation state of every Logic-Sphere within its designated territory. Their limbs are not fixed. They can extend a tendril of sharp, metallic shards to prune a malfunctioning conduit, the movement precise and impossibly delicate. Or they can flatten their entire form into a wide, shimmering shield of reflective surfaces to deflect a surge of aberrant energy from a glitching node. Their form is a tool, and their purpose is their entire existence.

The Harvesters are the sentient will of the Vinery made mobile. They are its gardeners, its technicians, its priests. The endless, subsonic thrum of the world is their language, and their thoughts are silent shifts in its resonant frequency. They glide over the metallic plains, their constituent parts levitating a hand's breadth above the strata of dead machines. They do not walk; they are a localized distortion in the Vinery's operating system that has been given a task.

Their primary function is the stewardship of the Logic-Spheres. They will stand a silent vigil by a complex node for what could be weeks or standard years, utterly still. They are not waiting. They are participating. They attune their own core crystal to the growing Sphere, "singing" to it in harmonics of pure data. They reinforce its developing internal logic, patch its computational errors, and guide its growth away from paradox and towards a state of perfect, stable complexity. You could watch one and feel its silent dialogue with the machine, a communion of pure, dispassionate function.

When a Logic-Sphere is ripe, the Harvester does not let it simply fall to the transport vines. It reaches out with its shimmering, mutable hands. The light-filaments between its shards intensify, creating a gentle, anti-gravitic cradle. The Sphere detaches with a faint chime that is absorbed by the Harvester's field, and is held aloft. At this moment, the Harvester is a figure of profound reverence: a constellation of broken history cradling a perfect, newborn thought. It then carries the Sphere, not to some distant hub, but to a specific transfer point, a place where the transport vines are strongest, and gently deposits its ward to begin its long, slow journey.

The Static-Mites pay them no mind, skittering over their floating fragments as they would any other surface. The Harvesters, in turn, do not register the mites. They are part of the system's background processes, irrelevant to the Harvester's directive.

Their relationship with the Phase-Echoes, however, is one of absolute antagonism. An Echo is a hole in reality, a cascade of corrupt data. A Harvester is a bastion of order, a focus of the system's intended logic. When they encounter one another, there is no physical battle. There is a duel of competing truths. The Harvester will surround the shimmering, humanoid static of the Echo. It will cease its focus on all other tasks, its entire being dedicated to one thing: solving the paradox. It projects a field of pure, coherent logic, attempting to "define" the Echo, to anchor its chaotic state, to force it back into the normal physics of the Vinery. The Echo, in turn, bombards the Harvester with nonsensical data, with visual and logical contradictions, seeking to overwhelm its core programming and shatter its coherence.

Sometimes the Harvester succeeds. The static of the Echo will flicker, stabilize, and dissipate with a final, soundless "snap" of corrected reality. The Harvester will pause for a long moment, re-integrating its own logic, before gliding away.

Sometimes, it fails. The filaments of light binding the Harvester's form will begin to flicker erratically. Its constituent shards will vibrate and lose their graceful orbits, clashing against one another. Its core crystal will pulse with corrupted colors, the phantasmal hues of a system error. Then, with a final, silent implosion, the Harvester's form dissolves. Some of its shards fall inert to the substrate below. Others are absorbed into the babbling static of the victorious Echo, which drifts away, slightly larger, slightly more certain of its own uncertain existence.

Are they individuals? It is impossible to say. They seem to act with singular purpose, yet they coordinate over vast distances without any visible communication. Perhaps they are all just terminals of a single, distributed consciousness—the Vinery itself, tending to its own strange garden. They have no memory of the Builders, no concept of a world outside the eternal, shadowless noon of the Overlight. They are as much a product of this place as the crystalline vines themselves, living ciphers whose only desire is the perfect continuation of the silent, brilliant, and utterly alien process they serve.
