The question of who lives here is based on a flawed, surface-world assumption: the distinction between inhabitant and environment. In this basin, that line has been erased. The city and its denizens are a single, sprawling organism, and to ask who lives in a body is to receive the answer: the cells, the organs, the thoughts, the self. All are true at once.

The closest thing to a singular being, the architect and the architecture, is the city itself. It has a name, though no one speaks it. The concept vibrates in the water—a slow, foundational hum. It is the **Koru-Mar**, the Great Root. The city is the calcified body of its own ancient consciousness. The spires of cartilage and bone are its memories, physical records of eons of slow, deliberate growth. Its mind is not in one place, but diffused throughout its entire structure. A thought, for the Koru-Mar, is not a flash of electrical activity but the decision to grow a new archway over the span of a century, or the slow, programmed decay of an old district, a conscious choice to forget. The rhythmic pulsing of light across its membranes is not a mere display; it is its resting heartbeat, its nervous system made visible, a city-wide thought-pattern of unimaginable slowness and complexity. It does not rule its inhabitants; it *is* them, in the same way a reef is its polyps.

Floating in the grand plazas, like motes of dust in the shafts of a drowned cathedral, are the **Luminarchs**. These are the immense, gelatinous beings, the city’s chandeliers. They are not mere decorations; they are the city’s primary metabolic organs. Drifting slowly over the geothermal vents, they are the first line of consumption, their vast, translucent bells acting as living chemical reactors. They absorb the raw, superheated, mineral-rich soup from the vents and, through a slow alchemical fire within their bodies, transmute it. The soft golden light they emit is a byproduct of this process, waste energy shed as photons. The purified, nutrient-rich water they exhale in gentle currents is the city’s true bread. Their tentacles, trailing for dozens of meters, are not weapons but gardens, each filament and stinging cell a unique micro-habitat for symbiotic algae and bacteria, a hanging farm that feeds the smaller creatures that dare to live so close to these serene, god-like chemists. They have no will of their own, only a deep, instinctual tropism that guides them toward heat and sustenance. They are the city's lungs and its stomach.

The city's arteries and veins are a fluid consciousness known as the **Argent Swarm**. These are the schools of sleek, silver-sided swimmers. They are blind, but their entire bodies are pressure-sense organs of exquisite sensitivity. They do not experience the world as individuals. A school of a thousand is a single entity, its thoughts the intricate pressure-whorls left by its own passage, its decisions the instantaneous, cascading reactions to the movement of its neighbors. They are the city’s nervous system, carrying information at a speed the Koru-Mar cannot fathom. A shift in the current, a spike in chemical concentration in one district, the vibration of a predator’s attack—all are transmitted across the city in moments as the Swarm adjusts its fluid, shimmering course. They are also the city’s pollinators, carrying genetic material and spores from one side of the metropolis to the other, caught on their slick skin, ensuring genetic diversity and seeding new growth on the bones of the old. Their collective, silent purpose is to connect, to carry, to know the city as a whole.

And then there are the workers, the countless smaller lives that are the city's cells. In the peristaltic tubes, the trillions of tiny, glowing creatures are the **Cilia-Kin**. They are not passengers in the "subway"; they *are* the subway. Their collective, rhythmic beating drives the currents through the transport network. They are janitors and mechanics, consuming biofilm from the tube walls, their bioluminescence a functional signal of flow-rate and purity. They live and die in their millions every second, a constant, necessary sacrifice to keep the city's circulatory system flowing.

In the foundations, in the dark under-city, dwell the **Osteoclasts**, or the Deep-Gnawers. These are the heavily-armored scavengers with grinding mandibles. They are not pests; they are the agents of the Koru-Mar’s renewal. Guided by chemical gradients that signal structural weakness or decay, they slowly, methodically chew away at the ancient, failing foundations of the city. Their passage is a low, grating vibration, the sound of the city digesting itself. They do not consume for hunger alone; they break down the old bone and chitin into a fine, nutrient-rich paste, laying the groundwork upon which new structures can be secreted. They are the demolition crew and the cement mixers, the agents of urban renewal given biological form.

Finally, there are the hunters, known only as the **Absences**. Sleek, swift, and utterly black, they are the only things in the city that do not produce their own light. They are visible only as perfect predator-shaped holes in the surrounding glow. They are the city’s immune system. Their purpose is not chaotic predation, but the enforcement of rhythm and order. They hunt the sick, the malformed, the arythmic—any creature whose light-pulse is out of sync, whose movements are erratic, whose chemical signature is "wrong." Their hunts are not acts of malice but of editing. They are the silent, swift correction that maintains the system’s perfect, instinct-driven equilibrium. To be seen by an Absence is to have already been judged by the city as dissonant. Their passage is marked by a brief snuffing of light, a silent implosion, and then the tapestry of the city is whole and perfect once more.

Every being here, from the god-like Luminarchs to the microbial Cilia-Kin, is a component. There are no individuals, no egos, no "people." There is only function, purpose, and place within a single, vast metabolism. The inhabitant of this city is the city itself, a living, breathing, dreaming entity of bone and light, existing in the warm, endless, and absolute dark.
