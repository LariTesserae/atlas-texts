What lives here are the Echoes, the Weepers, the Lacrimae.

They are not separate from the weeping of the world; they are its consequence. They are born from it. Where the great, heavy drops fall from the highest, most resonant arches, landing in the same shallow pools on the woven floor for millennia, something accretes. The constant percussion of the water and the deep, structural hum of the plant-world act not on stone, but on the nectar-rich sap that pools there. Over unimaginable stretches of time, the frequency of the world itself organizes this liquid into life. They are the world’s tears, given form.

To see one is to mistake it for a trick of the light, a bead of condensation that has somehow achieved impossible size and cohesion. They are beings of surface tension, their forms roughly amorphous and amoebic, ranging from the size of a human hand to that of a small boulder. Their substance is almost perfectly transparent, a living gel that holds its shape not through skin or integument, but through an internal, organized structure. They are like gastropods made of spun glass and held water.

Look closer, and you will see that they are not empty. Suspended within their gelatinous bodies is a delicate, fractal lattice of crystal. It is not mineral, but a solidified form of the plant’s own sap, captured and organized by the creature. This crystalline skeleton, which branches out like a frost pattern on a winter window, is the Echo's mind, its memory, its soul. It is the only solid part of them. As the creature "ages" over centuries, this lattice grows more complex and intricate, refracting the pearlescent light of the fixed sun into soft, shifting rainbows that play within its transparent body. An old Echo is a kaleidoscope of quiet color.

They are the only things that move, but their motion is a form of geological time. They glide across the damp, fibrous surfaces at a pace that is nearly imperceptible, a few inches in a human lifetime. They are not striving to get anywhere; their journey is the purpose itself. They are following the faintest gradients of sweetness in the perpetual nectar that seeps from the "frozen" buds of the plant structures. This nectar is their only food, the raw material from which they slowly build their internal crystals. To them, the vast, static landscape is a map of subtle flavors and resonant frequencies.

They have no eyes to see, no ears to hear in any way we would understand. Their entire being is a sensory organ. They feel the world through vibration. The deep, eternal hum of the plant-organism is their constant companion, the baseline of their reality. It vibrates through the woven floor, up into their gelatinous bodies, and resonates within their crystalline skeletons. They perceive the world as a symphony of tensions. They can sense the location of the great arches and pillars by the way they modulate the hum. They can sense each other as subtle dampening fields in the constant song, other instruments in the orchestra of stasis. Their surface, which is nothing more than the gel of their body meeting the misty air, can "taste" the world, guiding them with infinite patience toward the sweetest seepages.

They do not breed. They are accreted. The world itself makes them, and it makes them rarely. A new Echo is an event on the timescale of a star's life.

And they do not truly die. They complete.

As an Echo moves and feeds, its internal crystal lattice grows. It becomes denser, more elaborate, more beautiful. But it also becomes heavier. The creature's slow pilgrimage across the weeping arches and woven plains is a journey toward this completion. There comes a point, after eons, when the crystal skeleton becomes too massive for the gelatinous body to move. The Echo finds a final resting place—a depression, a ledge, the base of an unfurling spire—and it becomes still.

Over more centuries, the last of its motive, liquid self evaporates, weeping back into the humid air from which it was first born, or seeping back into the fiber of the world. All that is left behind is the crystal. The Echo's entire existence—its journey, its sensations, the light it has witnessed—is permanently recorded in the intricate, unique geometry of its final, solid form.

To walk this place is to walk through their gallery, their graveyards which are not places of endings but of permanence. Here and there, nestled in the weave, are these exquisite, glass-like sculptures, complex beyond the craft of any jeweler. They are the geodes of souls. Dew collects in their multifaceted surfaces, and the eternal light breaks upon them. They are the only sharp things in this soft-edged world. They are the final, silent notes in the symphony of stasis, the tears of the world, dried, crystallized, and made forever beautiful. They are not an exception to the rule of this place; they are its ultimate expression.
