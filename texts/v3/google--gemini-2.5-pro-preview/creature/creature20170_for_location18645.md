The beings who live here—the true inhabitants, not the temporary parasites sealed in their metal shells—are the Scar-Tenders. To call them "creatures" is to use a word that is both too simple and too sharp. They are not separate from this world; they are a function of its injury.

You will not see one at first. Your senses, already overwhelmed by the crushing drone and the featureless glare, are not tuned to their frequency of being. You will be walking for hours or days, acclimating to the ceaseless vibration underfoot, when you first feel a discrepancy. It is a subtle arrhythmia in the world's deafening rhythm, a counter-tremor in the Lignic Mass. It is a feeling of movement where nothing should be moving, a localized flowing within the static, weeping plain.

When you finally locate its source, it will defy your eyes. Against the backdrop of dark, interwoven fibres, it appears as a patch of shivering distortion, a heat-haze that is cool to the touch. It is a place where the ground seems to be un-making itself, its texture loosening, its colours blurring. This is a Scar-Tender, coalescing.

They are formed of the world's substance, but in a different state. Where the Mass is solid and saturated, a Tender is a semi-liquid, thixotropic gel. They are amalgams of the liquefied Phloem Nectar and the delicate, broken fibrils of the Mass, held together in a shimmering, vaguely humanoid shape by a weak internal field you cannot perceive. They are opalescent, like the fluid they consume, and their translucent bodies swirl with captured light, breaking the oppressive whiteness into faint, fleeting rainbows. They have no fixed anatomy, no permanent limbs or organs. They are beings of process, not structure.

They are drawn to the Taps. The monolithic hum that deafens you is, to them, a song of becoming. It is the vibration that both wounds their world and makes their existence possible. They congregate in the "shock zones" around the Resonance Drills, the areas where the sonic liquefaction is most intense. Here, they move with a slow, peristaltic grace, flowing over the vibrating ground like living oil slicks. They do not walk; they undulate, their lower bodies dissolving into the Mass while their upper bodies cohere.

Their purpose, their entire existence, is a form of maintenance. They are the world's fibroblasts, drawn to the sonic wound. They consume the raw, energy-rich Phloem Nectar that pools in the micro-fractures caused by the Taps, processing it within their gelatinous forms. But they are not mere scavengers. They are weavers.

From their mutable bodies, they extrude fine, glassy filaments of processed nectar—a solidified, crystalline form of the world's own sugars. These filaments are stronger and more inert than the living fibres of the Mass. With these threads, they perform their slow, silent work. They find the frayed and shattered ends of the Lignic Mass's ancient weave and begin to stitch them together. They are not healing the wound. The sonic damage is too profound, the extraction too violent. Instead, they are creating scar tissue on a geological scale.

They weave intricate, geometric patterns over the damaged areas—vast, lacework mandalas that can stretch for hundreds of yards. These patterns are not decorative. They are harmonic dampeners. Each latticework of crystalline thread is tuned to absorb and redirect the most destructive frequencies of the industrial hum, shoring up the integrity of the living plain and preventing its complete disintegration. They are turning the noise into a new kind of order, a beautiful, rigid scab over an weeping wound.

They have no sense of you as you understand it. Sound is annihilated by the hum. Sight is made nearly useless by the light. Their reality is purely textural, purely vibratory. To a Scar-Tender, the universe is a symphony of tremors. The deep, constant drone of the Tap is the keynote. The slow, tectonic groan of a distant Calyx unfurling is the bass line, playing out over a century. Your footsteps are a brief, discordant scratching, an anomaly so fleeting and insignificant it is barely worth noticing before it is gone. They might flow around you, their cool, gelatinous bodies brushing your leg with a strange, electric tingle, but they are not interacting with you. They are navigating a subtle change in the vibrational topography, an obstacle no more meaningful than a slightly tougher knot of vegetation.

They are utterly solitary. They do not communicate, for they are all part of the same distributed process. They are like individual cells working on a single body. They emerge from the Mass, perform their function for a span of time measured in seasons or years, and then, their internal cohesion lost, they simply relax, their bodies dissolving back into the weeping ground, their substance reabsorbed. They leave behind only their work: the glistening, silent, geometric scars that hold the world together under the immense pressure of its own violation. They are the quiet antibodies of a dying god, weaving beauty from its pain.
