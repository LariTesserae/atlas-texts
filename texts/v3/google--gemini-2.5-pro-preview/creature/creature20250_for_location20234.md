They begin as a rumour in the constant noise, a subtle shift in texture. You have been standing on the slick slag floor for an unmeasured time, letting the hum settle from an assault into a medium, like water you've learned to breathe. Then you notice it: the hum is not monolithic. It is a drone composed of a trillion finer noises. And those noises move.

They are the true inhabitants. They are not citizens of this city; they *are* the city, its metabolism made manifest. They are the purpose of the machinery and the product of it, a population of clockwork fauna locked in a perfect, horrifying symbiosis of self-consumption.

The first you truly see are the **Articulated Mites**. They are the foundation of this world, the system's primary harvesters. They are everywhere on the basin floor, a living, crawling carpet of motion so dense and vast that from a distance it appears as a shimmering heat-haze. Individually, one is the size of your thumb, a teardrop-shaped body of pitted, cast iron with four delicate, needle-thin legs of a darker, oiled metal. They scuttle with a twitching, insectoid gait, their legs making a sound like a billion grains of sand being poured onto a sheet of steel. They are a constant, high-frequency hiss layered over the deep bass of the place. They have no visible sensory organs, no "head." They are pure function. They move across the floor in overlapping, chaotic patterns, and where they pass, the thin, greasy film of chemical condensation is gone. They are grazers, consuming the very sweat of the machine-world.

Clinging to the titanic pipes and the flanks of the spherical brass tanks are the **Filter-Serpents**. These are far larger, some as long as a car, others coiled in such immense, multi-layered knots that their true length is impossible to guess. Their bodies are made of segmented, interlocking plates of dull grey ceramic, like the armour of some abyssal fish. They move with an unnerving fluidity, the plates sliding over one another with a soft, grinding sound, like tectonic shelves shifting. Their heads are not heads at all, but complex, rotating cowls of interwoven copper tubing and crystal lenses. These cowls spin slowly, perpetually sampling the air, their lenses glinting in the sourceless light. A Filter-Serpent does not hunt the Mites in the conventional sense. It will find a dense concentration of them on the floor below and extend a proboscis—a flexible, whip-like tube from its core—down into the swarm. The proboscis does not grab or stab. It emits a localized, high-frequency vibration, a piercing note that makes your teeth ache even at a distance. The Mites in a perfect circle around the tip simply... cease. They fall apart into a fine, metallic dust, which is then drawn up the tube in a shimmering wisp. The Serpent is not a predator; it is a quality control inspector, a recycling unit, culling and re-integrating the base materials of the grazers.

Then there are the creatures of the middle air, the most elegant and alien of the inhabitants. They are the **Glass Drifters**. They are perfectly silent, a subtraction from the noise rather than an addition to it. Each one is a complex geometric structure of spun glass filaments, impossibly thin and bound at an armature of polished silver. They look like caltrops the size of a human head, or perhaps crystalline snowflakes, catching the dim light and fracturing it into dull, internal rainbows. They do not fly, but hang in the air, held aloft by the very temperature differential between their cold bodies and the humid atmosphere. They drift on imperceptible currents, moving in vast, slow shoals through the spaces between the great machines. Their purpose is the most passive, and the most vital. The ozone-rich dampness of the air condenses upon their intricate glass surfaces. Droplets form, grow, and tremble. But they never fall. As a droplet reaches a precise mass, the internal structure of the Drifter realigns with a faint, crystalline *snap*, and the water is wicked away into its central silver armature, drawn into the machine's circulatory system through unseen ports. They are the lungs and kidneys of the city, reclaiming every stray molecule of vapor.

And watching over it all, from the highest, most inaccessible points, are the **Sentinels**. There are perhaps only a few dozen of them in the entire basin. They do not move. They are fixtures, indistinguishable from the architecture itself until you notice their attention. A Sentinel appears as a large, multi-faceted black lens, three meters across, set into the wall of a cylinder or the apex of a dome. It is featureless, non-reflective, a perfect void. But it is not blind. As you watch it, you become aware that it is watching you, and that it has been watching you since the moment you arrived. There is a weight to its gaze, a pressure. It does not communicate. It does not threaten. It merely observes. It is the consciousness of the machine, the ghost in itself. The Sentinels are the record-keepers, the governors of the equation. They ensure the ratio of Mites to Serpents is correct. They adjust the atmospheric composition that allows the Drifters to float. They are the mind of this place, and their thoughts are the endless, unchanging hum.

This is who lives here. A frantic population of metallic grazers, born from machine-sweat and culled by ceramic re-claimers. Silent, floating condensers gathering the air's breath. And the patient, black eyes of the machine itself, watching its own bodily functions with unnerving, eternal focus. It is a picture of terrifyingly complete life, a self-sustaining organism where every cell is a whirring, clicking, grinding mechanism. It has no goal beyond the perfection of its own process. Birth, death, consumption, and excretion are merely transfers of energy and material in a perfectly closed, perfectly loud, and perfectly lonely system.
