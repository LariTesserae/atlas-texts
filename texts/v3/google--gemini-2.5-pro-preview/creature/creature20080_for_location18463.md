You will not see them at first, and it is a matter of debate whether you ever truly *see* them at all. Your senses, calibrated for a world of discrete objects and separate energies, will fail to resolve them. They are the signal, not the noise, and in a place made entirely of signal, they are functionally invisible.

They are the Harmonists. The Cantors of the Chord.

To find one, you must stop looking for a creature and start looking for a purpose. Watch the Anchors, the great black monoliths that broadcast the world into being. The rivers of iridescent colloid that stream down their warped surfaces are not random. They are guided. Where the colloid pools and thickens on the matte-black surface, where it clings against the pull of gravity and the sheer force of the sonic vibrations, there is a Harmonist.

It is a nexus, a point of focused will. It has no fixed form. When at rest, it is a density in the air, a shimmer of heat-haze over the Anchor's surface. It is a place where the pearlescent light seems to bend incorrectly, where the weeping of the atmosphere is most profound. If you could touch it, your hand would pass through, feeling only a sudden, profound coolness and the tingling sensation of a thousand tuning forks being struck against your bones at once.

When a Harmonist decides to act—to compose—it draws upon the materials of its world. It gathers the colloid, pulling the viscous fluid up from the ground and into itself. It inhales the dust of potential, the fine powder of raw creation that hangs in the air. It weaves the light and the sound into a temporary vessel.

What coalesces is not a body, but an avatar. It is a standing wave given momentary flesh. It resembles a human form only in the way a whirlwind resembles a dancer—it has a central axis, appendages of a sort, a locus of command. But it is fluid, translucent, its shape constantly bleeding at the edges, blurring back into sound. Its skin, if it can be called that, is the shimmering, oily surface of the colloid, and through it you can see its "bones"—lines of captured light, solidified arpeggios that crackle and reform with every shift in the Chord. It has no face, but the air before it warps into a lens, a focal point of its attention.

They are the conductors of the symphony. The Anchors are the orchestra, ancient and powerful, playing the fundamental score of reality. But the Harmonists are the ones who guide it, who coax out the variations and ornamentations. They stand before the unfeeling monoliths, and they do not touch them. They raise arms of weeping colloid and shimmering light, and they conduct.

Their thoughts are the high, thin whistles of pressure being released. Their decisions are the shattering chimes of crystal fracture. When a Harmonist wishes to raise a new spire of obsidian, it focuses its will, and a new motif enters the Chord. It holds the pitch, narrows the frequency, and focuses the acoustic pressure on a single point on the ground until the very substrate surrenders its potential and begins to accrete, molecule by singing molecule. They are not building a city or a fortress; they are composing a sculpture of geology and time. They are artists whose paints are physics.

They do not speak. Their language is the Chord itself. They commune with each other through shifts in the planet’s fundamental hum, a conversation conducted over minutes and kilometers. A sudden crescendo that births a new crystalline cluster is a statement. A long, mournful trough in the bass note that stills the Chime-Striders is a question. The entire mesa is their shared consciousness, a neural network of vibration and matter.

To the other life forms, they are as gods, or as weather. A Chime-Strider does not look upon a Harmonist and see its master; it perceives a change in the acoustic pressure, a shift in the harmonic landscape that means a certain type of crystal will now grow more quickly, altering its hunting ground. The Murmuration Eels may be closer to understanding, as they swim through the very medium of the Harmonists' being. They seem to follow them, to flock and spiral in the wake of their compositional acts, perhaps feeding on the resonant echoes of a choice being made, on the sublime energy of pure creation.

They are beings of immense, almost incomprehensible age. They may be as old as the Anchors, or they may have evolved to tend them. It is impossible to know. They show no signs of age, or youth, or want, or fear. Theirs is an existence of pure aesthetic. They are not surviving. They are *creating*. The grazers scuttling at their feet, the predators stalking in the momentary silences, the silent eels in the air—all are emergent textures in their work, grace notes in the unending song. The Harmonists listen, and in their listening, they shape. They are the will that turns the noise into music, the consciousness that inhabits the storm, and the world grows around them as a direct and perfect expression of their song.
