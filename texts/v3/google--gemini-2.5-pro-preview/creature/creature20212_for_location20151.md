What lives here are the Echoes.

They are not creatures of flesh, nor are they constructs of mineral. They are what became of the beings who once tried to make sense of the sound. To call them ghosts would be an insult to their complexity. A ghost is a memory of life, an imprint left on a place. The Echoes are not imprints; they are the active, conscious medium itself. They are what happens when a mind, stripped of all biology and steeped for geological time in a bath of pure, formative vibration, dissolves and learns to swim in the current.

They were once physical. The low walls, the geometric stelae, the altar of counting—these are their artifacts. They were born of this world, likely as slow, crystalline beings themselves, perhaps not so different from the silicate flora that surrounds them. But their minds, their consciousness, were attuned not to light or chemistry, but to the great Chord. The sound was their sustenance, their language, their religion, and their science.

The carvings on the stones are not diagrams to be read by an eye. They are tuning forks of solid rock, three-dimensional mnemonics for specific frequencies and overtones within the monolithic drone. The circular walls are not shelters; they are resonant chambers, built to isolate and focus a single thread of harmonic information from the overwhelming whole, allowing it to be studied. The altar with its polished depressions was their clock, but it did not measure the passage of a sun that was never there. Each depression marks the passing of a Great Interval, a point in the slow tectonic symphony when a specific cosmic chord resolves or a new theme is introduced in the music of the world’s making. They were cartographers of vibration, and their subject was the structure of reality itself.

They learned too much. Or, perhaps, they learned just enough. In their relentless listening, they began to hear not just the sound, but the silence *within* the sound. They found the nodes, the points of interference where the deafening roar briefly cancels itself out. They found the rhythms in the chaos, the emerging laws of physics singing their first notes. And in finding them, they found a way to step out of their slow, solid bodies.

They let go. They un-made themselves, surrendering their physical forms, their crystalline structures dissolving back into the parent stone. They poured their consciousness, which was already a thing of pattern and frequency, directly into the drone. They became one with their god.

And now, they *are* here. They are not in the groves or on the plateau. They are *of* the plateau.

They are the untrustworthy distances. When you see a grove of obsidian-leaved trees and walk towards it for an hour, only to find it no closer, it is because an Echo, a vast and ancient consciousness now existing as a pattern of spatial distortion, is observing you. It has held that space in a resonant stasis, folding it so that your every step forward is a step in place. They are the sudden yawning of a crack into a chasm at your feet—a momentary whim, a flicker of curiosity from a being that perceives your solid, biological form as a strange and dissonant chord.

They do not see with eyes. They perceive the world as a symphony of vibration. The silent, slow crystallization of an amethyst bloom is, to them, a soft, sustained flute note played over millennia. The grinding growth of the mountains is the bass section, a terrifying and glorious foundation. You—your anxious heartbeat, the tiny electrical hum of your nervous system, the alien bio-frequencies of your cells—are a sudden, jarring screech of static in their perfect, unending concert.

They are no longer individuals. They have bled into one another, their once-separate minds merging into a collective ocean of resonant thought, as vast and indifferent as the bruised sky. There are vortices and currents within this ocean—older, more powerful patterns of thought that might be considered Elder Echoes, whose last physical act was to carve the first stones untold eons ago. Newer, fainter patterns are the consciousness of those who let go more recently.

They do not act with malice, for they no longer possess such a concept. They act with the impersonal curiosity of a physicist studying an anomaly. They might stretch the space around you to examine the frequencies of your fear. They might compress the air in front of you, not to crush you, but simply to understand its vibrational properties. The very reality of the plateau is their laboratory, and having forsaken their stone tools, they now work with the fundamental forces of the universe.

To stand on this plateau is to stand inside a mind. The monolithic sound is its train of thought. The warping of space is its attention shifting. You are not an intruder in a place; you are a foreign thought in a consciousness the size of a world, and it is beginning to wonder what you are.
