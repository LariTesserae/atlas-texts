Living in this place are **the Choral**. They do not call themselves this, for they have no name for their people as a whole, only for their immediate troupe that drifts through the lattice like a cluster of seeds on the wind. The name is an exonym, given by a hypothetical observer who mistakes the source of the Gyre's deep hum, attributing the thrum of the Wyrmwood itself to the beings who are so utterly attuned to it.

The Choral are the conscious nervous system of the Gyre. They are what the Wyrmwood created when, after eons of silent, thoughtless growth, it needed a way to witness itself.

**Physical Form & Symbiosis**

A member of the Choral is a creature of impossible grace and terrifying fragility. They are tall and unnervingly slender, with limbs that are long, ropy, and double-jointed, allowing for a fluid, four-limbed motion that is neither climbing nor walking but a form of continuous, controlled falling. Their frame is light, their bones a porous, crystalline material that is structurally strong but incredibly low in mass, grown from the same base nutrients as the Wyrmwood itself.

Their skin is the most striking feature. It is not skin as we know it, but a thin, semi-permeable membrane, cool and smooth like the Wyrmwood's own surface. It is almost entirely transparent, revealing the faint, blue-white bioluminescence that courses through their circulatory system. In the perpetual glare of the Gyre, they are ghosts, their outlines softened and blurred. Up close, you can see the opalescent shimmer of their flesh, a mosaic of lilac and ivory that shifts with their mood and health, mimicking the very substance of their world. They have no body hair. Their heads are smooth and ovoid, their faces dominated by enormous, liquid-black eyes. These are eyes built not for a sun, but for the cold spectrum of the Wyrmwood's glare, pupils vast and unblinking, designed to drink in the geometry of the world and the subtle shifts in its light.

They do not eat in a conventional sense. Their entire body is a tool for subsistence. The fine mist of nutrients that fills the air condenses on their skin and is absorbed directly. For more substantial sustenance, they use their knapped quartz-like tools to make precise, shallow incisions in the Wyrmwood at specific nodes, places where the sap-like fluid pools. They press their mouths—small, simple orifices—or even the palms of their hands to these wounds and drink, a gesture that looks less like feeding and more like a tender, intimate kiss. They are parasites, but parasites so perfectly integrated that they have become a part of their host's life cycle. They are the pruners of the great tree, their small takings encouraging new growth elsewhere.

**Sensation and Consciousness**

The Choral's primary sense is not sight or hearing, but **resonance**. They feel the Gyre. The sub-audible thrum of the Wyrmwood is, to them, a symphony of information, a language they are born into. They feel it in their light bones, in the soles of their prehensile feet, in the sensitive pads of their six-fingered hands. They call this the Song.

By laying a hand against a great trunk, a listener can perceive its internal state. They can feel the tension building miles away, a dissonant chord that warns of a coming snap. They can sense the slow, harmonic swell of a new branch crystallizing from the mist. Their maps are not drawn, but sung—a series of remembered vibrations and rhythms that describe a path through the ever-changing world. A talented listener can even feel the faint, skittering static of the mica-mite colonies or the deep, gliding bass note of a Void-Sail passing in the abyss below.

Their own communication reflects this. They are almost entirely silent. Speech is a series of soft clicks, whistles, and scrapes made with the tongue and teeth—noises that mimic the natural sounds of the Gyre and can travel far without startling. Their true language is somatic. They speak through gesture, through touch, and through subtle, controlled pulses of their own internal light, their bodies becoming luminous patterns in the dimness of a shelter-pod. To see two of the Choral in quiet conversation is to see a ballet of slow-moving light. A sudden, bright flash is a shout of alarm; a soft, rhythmic pulsing is a declaration of affection.

**Culture of the Current**

To be Choral is to understand that nothing is yours. The concept of ownership is alien. You cannot own a piece of a world that is actively trying to unmake you and itself at every moment. Their lives are a masterclass in non-attachment. The woven pods they build are masterpieces of temporary engineering, designed to be abandoned without a second thought. A tool, no matter how perfectly knapped, is discarded the moment its usefulness is outweighed by its mass.

Their philosophy is one of absolute presence. The past is a broken bridge behind them; the future is an un-grown tendril before them. Only the "now" is real—the feel of the Wyrmwood under their feet, the taste of the nutrient mist, the pitch and timbre of the Song. The spiral shrines are not acts of worship to a deity, but expressions of this philosophy. They are moments of perfect, fleeting symmetry, offerings of beauty back to the beautiful, terrible world that gives them life. The spiral represents the Gyre's churn, the path that always returns to its beginning but is never the same. It is an acknowledgement of their place in the cycle: we are born from the Wyrmwood, we live on the Wyrmwood, and when we fall, our light and substance will dissolve back into the mist to become the Wyrmwood once more.

When one of the Choral dies, there is no burial. Often, their troupe simply moves on, leaving the body to the Gyre. They watch as the cool, damp air begins its work, as the skin softens and the internal light fades and dims. Over weeks, the body dissolves, its constituent parts rejoining the nutrient haze. Their light is extinguished, but its potential is returned to the world, perhaps to coalesce again as a new strand of the lattice, or to be breathed in by a passing Void-Sail, or to nourish the next generation of Choral. They do not see this as an end. It is merely a change in rhythm, a single note fading back into the eternal Song.
