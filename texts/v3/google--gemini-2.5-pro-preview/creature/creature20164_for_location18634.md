I am of the spire.

The thought is not a word, but a pressure, a knowing that exists in the core of me and in the hundred others just like me across this curving wall. My six legs, each a point of perfect pressure against the ochre grain, hold me to the rampart that I myself have made. Not just me. We. The distinction is a fluid thing, like the heavy water that beads on the ground below. My body is a slow crucible, turning the thick air and the dustless damp into substance. The substance is us. The substance grows from a gland near my hindmost legs, a single, viscous drop of potential that clings, then hardens into the world. This is the work.

My carapace tastes the light. It is a dull purple nourishment, a steady, low hum of energy that warms the bronze and violet shimmer of my shell. Light is not for seeing things at a distance; distance is an empty concept. There is only the here of the spire, and the there of the other spires, each their own pulsing 'here' across the yielding gray. We feel them more than see them, a faint, sympathetic resonance in the very stuff of our towers. A tremor of growth. A new dome swelling. A spire reaching a handspan higher.

The world speaks to us through our feet. The wall is a scroll of infinite detail. I feel the paths of my hundred siblings through the resonant floor of my feet, a constant, silent conversation of crossing trails. I feel the slight swelling where the wall is still curing, tacky and warm. I feel the gossamer drag of a spore-moth's wing as it flutters from a fungal shelf above me, its silence a different texture from my own. I feel the minute vibration of a Jade-Skin, one of the glistening amphibians, as it settles its cool, pebbled belly into a curtain of moss. It is a sign of good damp. The wall is healthy.

We are gardeners. We do not eat the ferns with their waxy skins, nor the felted mosses. We cultivate them. Our secretions are laced with the specific nutrients they crave. We prune by touch, nudging a frond into a better position to catch a falling drop of condensed air. We carve the planters, the deep-set pockets, not with tools, but with generations of focused secretion, building the rim of the pot drop by drop over a slow season. The life that erupts from our architecture is part of the architecture. The pitcher plants, with their maroon lips, are our water cisterns. The pulsing fungi are our lanterns, their ghostly light a measure of the air's richness. When they dim, the air is thin. When they brighten, the world is breathing well. They are our barometers, our clocks, our art.

My journey is a slow spiral. A full circuit of this mid-level ramp might take what others call a day. I patrol. I inspect. I find a place where the surface has been scoured by a wind that doesn't blow, a faint erosion from the simple passage of time. I pause. The crucible in my gut churns. I am still, a living statue, my iridescent back a jewel in the gloom. I am listening to the spire, and the spire is telling me what it needs. A buttress here. A reinforcement of the lattice there. The thought solidifies. The drop appears. I press it into the flaw, smoothing it with my forelegs, shaping it into the whole. It is a single note added to a symphony that has been playing since the ground was still liquid.

Down below is the Unmade. The bare, rubbery floor. We do not go there often. It is a place of becoming, and its potential is a disquieting hum. It is too simple, too quiet. The Pale Ones live there, the eyeless eels that stir the mercury pools. They are of the ground, as we are of the spire. We do not interact. They are a different kind of life, a horizontal thought in our vertical world. To descend is to feel the ground yield, to taste the raw minerals at their source. It is too much. It is like drinking the sun. We gather the beaded water from the edges, where it is tamed, and we retreat to the familiar grammar of our walls.

There is no malice here. The gecko-lizard with its liquid black eyes consumes a spore-moth. The lizard's life is a service to the fungi, preventing the moths from laying too many eggs. The Pale One in the pool below consumes something that has fallen, returning its substance to the weeping ground. Everything is a cycle of slow consumption and silent creation.

We do not build for shelter, for the basin itself is our shelter. We do not build for defense, for there is nothing to defend against but time, and we build *with* time. We build because the thought of the spire is within us, a blueprint encoded in the very way our bodies process the world. The tower is our collective dream, made solid. Each of us is a single, ambulatory cell of a greater being whose body is ochre chitin and whose gardens reach for a sky we will never see.

Another drop hardens. The flaw is gone. The wall is whole again. I feel the approach of a sibling on the path, their patient, six-footed gait a rhythm I have known for my entire existence. We will pass without touching, our carapaces shifting from bronze to purple in a silent acknowledgement. The work continues. The life is the work. The work is the song.

And the song is not yet finished.
