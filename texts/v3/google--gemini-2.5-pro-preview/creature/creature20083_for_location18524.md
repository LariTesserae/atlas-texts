They are what the water births from the stone.

They do not come into the world screaming. There is no shock of first breath, no violent separation. A new life begins as a thought held in common by the community, a focused point of contemplative will. This will is directed toward a prepared place: a smooth, womb-like hollow carved deep within the growing stone, fed by the purest, most mineral-rich seepages. Here, in the dark and the damp, over a period of many rotations of the pearlescent sky, a form begins to coalesce. It is not grown from a seed or an egg, but precipitated out of the water itself, drawing minerals from the stone and vitality from the silent, focused attention of its people. The being that eventually emerges, slipping silently from its stone font into a waiting pool, is not a child but a miniature, fully-formed echo of its creators, possessing an ancestral stillness from its first moment.

To see one is to see the landscape embodied. They are tall and impossibly slender, their forms suggesting an elongation born of constant, gentle gravity and the need to move through water with minimal disturbance. Their skin is smooth, without hair or scale, and has the colour and sheen of a wet river stone—greys, dark greens, and muted blacks, often with a faint, milky iridescence that mirrors the sky. Their skin is completely permeable, constantly drinking the moisture from the air, so that they appear perpetually slick and dewy, as if they, too, are weeping the world’s water.

Their limbs are long and delicate. Their feet are wide and splayed, with tough, leathery pads on the soles and fully webbed toes, allowing them to walk across the vast, shallow pools with a preternatural grace. They distribute their weight perfectly, their senses reading the texture of the submerged stone so they never place a foot on an edge sharp enough to cut. Their movements are slow, deliberate, and fluid, more like a dance than a walk. When they must move through the deeper cisterns of the older city, they do not thrash or swim, but simply sink and glide, their bodies propelled by slight, near-imperceptible undulations.

Their hands are their most expressive feature, with long, six-fingered digits that are also webbed, though the webbing is finer and more flexible. These hands are their primary tools and their primary voice. With them, they can feel the subtle tensions and growth-lines in the living stone, knowing exactly where to apply the pressure of a bone chisel to guide a wall's growth over the next century. With them, they weave the tough fern-fibres and arrange the mosses in their rooftop gardens.

And with these hands, they speak. Their language is a slow, intricate ballet of gesture, a silent semaphore that can convey immense complexity of thought and emotion. A slight curl of the fingers might be a treatise on the mineral content of a specific water source; a slow turn of the wrist might express a feeling of deep, contented absorption. Vocalization is rare. When it occurs, it is not speech as we know it, but a series of hushed, resonant, tonal hums, produced deep in the chest. These sounds are not for conveying information but for sharing a state of being—a resonance of tranquility, a chord of shared focus, a low note of communal sorrow when one of them dissolves back into the world. In the damp, heavy air, these sounds travel no more than a few feet before the silence swallows them whole.

Their faces are narrow and serene, with no discernible nose but rather a series of small, slitted apertures along the cheekbones that open and close to sample the air's chemistry. Their mouths are simple, lipless lines. But their eyes are profound. They are large, dark, and liquid, like pools of oil that perfectly reflect the silver-white sky. They have no visible pupils or irises, just a uniform, deep blackness that seems to drink the gentle light. In these eyes lies the stillness of their world, the depth of a consciousness that does not race but seeps, like water through stone.

They embody the world’s core paradox of hardness and softness. While their skin is pliable and their movements are fluid, their bones are incredibly dense, almost stone-like. Along their spine, knuckles, and the sharp line of their elbows, the bone sits just beneath the skin, forming hard, smooth ridges. These are the points of contact with their world, the parts of them that rest against the stone when they recline or that add weight and precision to the guiding of a tool.

They do not know ambition or urgency. They do not think in terms of goals to be achieved, but of harmonies to be maintained. A rock-wright does not labour to "finish" a wall; they commune with it, tending its growth as a gardener might tend a plant, their life's work being but a single sentence in a conversation that spans millennia. There is no hierarchy, only function, which shifts as fluidly as water. An individual might spend decades tending the succulent groves, then a century guiding the growth of a new terrace, then an age in pure contemplation, their body perfectly still in a shallow pool for years at a time, simply being.

Their thoughts are like their world: deep, still, and layered. They do not see time as a line, but as depth. The past is not behind them, but beneath them—in the smoother, older stones of the city. The future is not ahead, but potential—in the uncarved, weeping rock. To them, existence is not a journey, but a saturation.

And when their time comes, there is no struggle. The inner vitality simply quiets, the focused will dissolves. The being will find a secluded pool, often the very font of its emergence, and submerge itself. Over days and weeks, its form softens, loses its definition, and unravels. Its physical substance—the minerals borrowed from the stone, the water that formed its being—dissolves without rot or decay, becoming once more part of the great, silent, mineral-rich soup of the world. It is a quiet return, leaving no ghost, no memory, only a slight enrichment of the water from which a new form will, one day, precipitate. They are the stone's way of knowing itself. They are the consciousness of the great, quiet stillness.
