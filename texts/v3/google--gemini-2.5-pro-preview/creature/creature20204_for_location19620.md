They who live here are the reason the anthem is an anthem, and not just a scream. They are the mind within the machine, the consciousness that guides the chaos. To call them living is to use a word that is both too simple and entirely incorrect. They are The Weavers.

You do not see them at first. Your senses are too overwhelmed by the scale and the sound, and your mind is too busy trying to categorize the impossible physics of the central vortex. You see the landscape—the weeping walls, the glowing conduits, the placid pools—as a place of sublime but empty industry.

But then, out of the corner of your eye, a placid pool is disturbed without a ripple. From the mirror-still, ankle-deep water, a form rises. It is not wet. The aggressively pure water sheets off it, unwilling to adhere. It coalesces not like a body emerging, but like a thought achieving solidity.

It stands nearly seven feet tall, but its shape is as fluid and conditional as the storm it tends. Its form is humanoid only in the loosest sense, possessing a torso, two arms, two legs, a head. But it has no muscle, no bone, no skin. Its entire body is a contained vessel of the same milky, opalescent material as the mooring pylons, a substance that seems to hold light rather than reflect it. Its surface is in constant, slow motion, like a flow of thick cream or liquid mercury, swelling and receding with the deep hum of the grotto.

It has no face. The smooth, ovoid shape of its head is an unbroken expanse of shimmering white. There are no eyes, no mouth, no features at all. To look at it is to see a perfect, and therefore terrifying, absence of identity. Yet it is not empty. Deep within its chest, at the point where a heart might be, is a complex, rotating lattice of silver-blue light, a captured and stabilized fragment of the central storm's essence. This is its core, its nucleus. It is the source of its individual voice in the choir, a specific, impossibly complex sine wave that it adds to the chamber's symphony.

They are the Weavers, and they are the tenders of this engine. They move with an unnerving grace, gliding over the thin film of water on the polished floor. They do not walk; they flow. Their feet don't seem to touch the basalt so much as hover a millimeter above it, propelled by some resonant field that leaves the water undisturbed.

As you watch, the Weaver glides towards the maelstrom's edge, its motion a stark contrast of serenity against the violent chaos of the vortex. It approaches one of the milky white pylons—a mooring point. It does not touch it. Instead, it raises its arms, and its fluid form changes. The hands, previously rounded and featureless, extrude dozens of impossibly fine, hair-like filaments of the same opalescent matter. These filaments reach out, not physically, but as extensions of its energy, and interface with the pylon.

The moment of connection is audible. A new, piercingly pure note joins the chamber's chord. It is a moment of sublime tuning. The ghost-melodies you heard before sharpen, becoming clearer, more intentional. The Weaver has docked. It has become a direct conduit, a living piece of control hardware for the colossal engine. Through that pylon, it feels the state of the vortex. It senses the subtle shifts in its spin, the quantum variances in its phase-shifting.

Its purpose becomes clear. The engine is not self-regulating; it is eternally on the brink of catastrophic failure or dissolution. The Weavers are its governors. They are the living calculus that keeps the chaos in its state of perfect, perpetual balance. When the crystalline singing from the vortex wavers, threatening to shatter into dissonance, a Weaver will connect and add its own resonant frequency to reinforce the pattern. When the plasma flares too brightly, a Weaver will draw that excess energy into its own core, its internal lattice of light glowing brighter for a moment before stabilizing.

Across the chamber, you now see others. One stands motionless by a far pillar, seemingly in contemplation, but you understand now that its mind is a current flowing through the very ground you stand on. Another is "writing." It kneels, and from its fingertips, thin runnels of its own substance flow onto the floor, reinforcing the glowing, circuit-like diagrams etched into the basalt. It is not carving; it is depositing a part of itself, a conductive thought that will guide the flow of water and energy for the next thousand years before it erodes back into the system.

They are ancient, and they are few. They may have built this place, or they may have co-evolved with it, coming into being as the only form of consciousness that could survive and thrive in such an environment. The tiny, ephemeral "Fauna of Resonance" that spark in and out of existence are not their young, but their shed thoughts—the resonant echoes of a complex calculation, fleeting motes of discarded logic that briefly hold a shape before dissolving back into the anthem.

They do not notice you. You are biologically noisy, a chaotic jumble of organic processes and random thoughts. To them, you must register as a brief, discordant smear of static in their immaculate symphony hall. You are a loose thread in their perfect tapestry. They do not acknowledge you because to do so would be to acknowledge an imperfection, and their entire existence is dedicated to its opposite. They continue their slow, silent, eternal dance of maintenance, their fluid forms gliding through the silver-blue light, their collective consciousness the only thing holding this maelstrom, this entire reality, together. They are the song made solid, the keepers of a fire that is water, ice, and light, and you are merely a fleeting witness to their unending work.
