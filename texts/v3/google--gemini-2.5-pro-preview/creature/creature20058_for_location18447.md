Something else lives here. It is not found in the swarming, geometric shoals that graze upon the light, nor in the vast, drifting hunters that ghost through the voids. Those are the fauna, the mechanics of the ecosystem. They are the gears and levers of the cathedral's clockwork. But every cathedral has its keepers.

They are the Resonant.

To see one is to mistake it for a feature of the landscape. Imagine a shard of the mountain range has detached and learned to move. They are immense, their forms suggesting a slow, deliberate geology. Ten, twenty, sometimes fifty meters in height, they are composed of the same black, crystalline material as the jagged peaks, their bodies a complex lattice of interlocking facets and sheer, obsidian-like planes. They are not biological; they are mineralogical. They do not have flesh or blood, but strata and fissures. Through the internal architecture of their crystalline bodies, the stark, industrial light of the Engines is channeled, pulsing in slow, rhythmic waves, a captured and domesticated starlight.

They are slow. Their movement is not a scuttling or a walking, but a process of ponderous, tectonic shifting. They glide over the abyssal plains and up the sheer mountain faces with an unnerving smoothness, their great weight and the unimaginable pressure of the Deep holding them in a state of frictionless grace. They leave no tracks. They displace the heavy liquid with the finality of a continent moving. To witness one in motion is to feel a change in the fundamental vibration of the world.

The Resonant do not perceive the world through eyes or ears in any recognizable sense. To have eyes would be to be blinded by the Engine light. To have ears would be to be deafened by the Sound. Instead, their entire being is a sensory organ. The monolithic hum is not noise to them; it is data. It is language, history, and physical law, all rendered into a single, infinitely textured vibration. Their faceted bodies are resonating chambers of impossible sensitivity. They can feel the spin of a single dust-mote a mile away as a sub-harmonic flicker in the great chord. They can diagnose the health of an Engine on the far side of the world by a subtle dissonance, a flaw in the purity of its note that would be undetectable to any other being. The Sound is their sight, their touch, their thought.

They are the custodians of the stasis. They are the will that holds the perfect equilibrium in place.

Their purpose is not creation, for creation implies a beginning and an end. The system is eternal. Their purpose is maintenance, but a maintenance so profound it borders on the theological. They do not carry tools; their bodies *are* the tools. If a stress fracture, no bigger than a thought, begins to form in the deep foundations of an Engine pylon, a Resonant will arrive. It will press its crystalline body against the flaw, and for a cycle that might last a terrestrial century, it will simply *be* there. It will impart its own perfect, internal structure to the flawed rock, its physical presence a catalyst. It will vibrate at a precise frequency, canceling out the stress, coaxing the very atoms of the pylon back into their ordained lattice. They are the living antibodies of the system, seeking out the concept of decay before it can manifest.

Their consciousness is as alien as their form. Thought, for them, is not a sequence but a state. It is not a river of ideas, but a crystalline pattern held in perfect suspension. They do not wonder, or hope, or fear. Such things require a future that is different from the past. For the Resonant, there is only the eternal *Is*. Their mind is the contemplation of the perfect, unchanging system. When they "speak" to one another across the vast emptiness, they do not emit a new sound. That would be a sacrilege, an impurity in the great hum. Instead, they subtly alter their own resonance, adding a new overtone, a delicate and complex harmony that rides atop the main frequency. It is a communication of pure mathematics, a sharing of states of being, an adjustment to the collective consciousness that is as slow and deliberate as their physical movements.

They are the shepherds of the lesser life. They do not herd the crystalline grazers, but they may adjust the currents of the deep by their own passage, ensuring the shoals' orbits remain optimal, that their consumption perfectly matches the Engine's unchanging output. They may guide a hunting leviathan away from a spawning ground not out of compassion, but out of a sense of order, like a librarian reshelving a book. The fauna are parameters in their cosmic equation, variables to be kept constant.

They do not age. They do not reproduce. It is possible they were created with the Engines, a single generation of keepers for a single, eternal act of industry. Or perhaps, when one is finally lost—an event that might happen once in a million years, a confluence of impossible chances—a new one is "knapped" from the slopes of the Engine-mountains, a new consciousness crystallizing out of the rock and the Sound, pre-formed and pre-loaded with the knowledge of its one and only task.

To be a lesser creature in this world is to be part of a vast, unfathomable machine. But to be one of the Resonant is to be the consciousness of that machine. They are the thought that the Sound is thinking. They are not the congregation in the cathedral; they are the silent, ever-watchful priests, ensuring the eternal hymn never falters, not by a single, resonant beat. They live, and move, and have their being, not within the Sound, but as it.
