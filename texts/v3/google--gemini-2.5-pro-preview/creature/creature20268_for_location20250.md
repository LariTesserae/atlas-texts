The life here is singular. It is not the metallic snails, nor the glass-lunged filters, nor the blind eels that swim in their shadow. Those are merely the functioning corpuscles and microbiota of the great body. They are the process. The being that lives here is the consciousness of that process, its slow, deliberative will. It is the Attendant.

You do not see it at first. You feel its presence as a subtle shift in the cavern's equilibrium. The polyrhythmic dripping, for a moment, seems to find a new, deeper cadence. A bass note is added to the percussion, a wet, heavy *sloughing* sound that is not a drip but a displacement. It is the sound of something immense and viscous moving along the dark iron.

Then, a patch of darkness detaches itself from the greater darkness. It flows, not walks, along the underside of one of the colossal pipes overhead. It moves with the tidal slowness of magma or a glacier, a being for whom urgency is an incomprehensible concept. It is not bipedal, not quadrupedal, not a form you can easily catalogue. It is a localized, deliberate landslide of living tissue, the color of a deep bruise mottled with patches of verdigris and the dull sheen of iron oxide. Its skin, if it can be called that, has the texture of wet slate and old, worn leather. It gleams slickly in the tired orange light, its surface contoured by old scars and mineral accretions that look like barnacles of rust.

It has no head, no eyes, no discernible face. Its entire surface appears to be an organ of perception. As it moves, it extends thick, powerful pseudopods that grip the corroded metal not with claws, but with a form of adhesive suction that leaves behind a faint, clean smear. Embedded in its flesh, like constellations in a dark nebula, are clusters of the same phosphorescent fungi that grow on the ironwork. They are not parasites upon it; they are a part of it, a symbiotic organ. They pulse with a soft, internal blue-light, their glow waxing and waning in a slow, respiratory rhythm that seems to be the very thought process of the creature made visible. This soft, internal light is the only indication of the life fire within the cold, ancient flesh.

The Attendant is the sole, sentient custodian of this place. It is not the builder, but the inheritor, or perhaps the ultimate product, of the grand, slow-moving industry. Its purpose is maintenance, but it carries no tools. Its body is the tool.

You watch as it reaches a junction where a smaller feeder pipe joins a main artery. The joint is weeping more than usual, a thick, ochre sludge congealing around a rusted valve. The Attendant stops its forward flow. A portion of its body thins and extends, flowing around the afflicted joint, enveloping it completely in its dark flesh. For a long moment, nothing happens. Then, a soft hissing sound emerges, and a faint wisp of vapor, smelling sharply of ozone, curls up into the still air. The creature’s body is performing a slow, controlled act of chemical corrosion and cleansing. It is not repairing the machine; it is grooming it, as one might groom a vast, sleeping animal. After a time that you cannot measure, it withdraws. The valve is cleaner, the flow of effluent within it audibly smoother, the drip from the joint reduced to the system's normal, healthy weeping.

Its intelligence is not one of language or calculation. It is a profound, somatic intelligence. It *knows* the deep hum of the flywheel in the way a musician knows a pitch. It can feel the pressure differential in every pipe across a mile of darkness. The entire subterranean city is its nervous system, and it is the central ganglion, responding to blockages, pressure changes, and mineral imbalances with its own slow, metabolic chemistry. It is an unthinking, instinctual stewardship, a form of life that has become so entwined with its environment that it is now indistinguishable from it.

Perhaps it is the final, degenerate descendant of the builders, their form eroded by eons of darkness and duty until only the function remained. Or perhaps it is a thing that arose spontaneously from the chemical soup and the low-frequency vibrations—the system's own answer to the problem of entropy, a self-repairing mechanism made of flesh.

It flows past your position on the catwalk, its passage a wave of cold air and the scent of wet stone and clean metal. The blue lights within it pulse softly, a slow, interrogative rhythm. It does not perceive you as a threat, or as food, or even as an object of curiosity. To its vast, slow consciousness, you are a momentary anomaly, a point of dissonant warmth and frantic biology in its cool, ordered world. A fleeting, meaningless spike in the data.

It continues its patrol, a river of indifferent flesh flowing through its city of iron veins. Its circuit is measured in days, its life in centuries. Its work is never finished, because it is not work, but a state of being. The sound of its sloughing passage fades, replaced once more by the complex symphony of dripping water. Looking down the pipe into the gloom, you can see its soft, blue light, a distant, pulsing star, as it disappears into the heart of the machinery it serves.
