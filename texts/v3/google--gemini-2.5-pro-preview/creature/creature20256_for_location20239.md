They are the Kiln-Tenders. But they do not tend the fire; they tend the cooling.

You don't see one at first. They are so much a part of the landscape that they register as just another geologic feature, a statue left unfinished. They are humanoid only in the way a shadow is, a suggestion of a form. They stand seven or eight feet tall, their bodies slender and elongated, crafted from the same raw, unfired clay as a potter's first lump. Their surface is not skin but a smooth, matte gray-brown, marked with the same incidental textures as the world around them: the drag of a finger, the faint crosshatch of a scoring tool, the impression of a stray piece of burlap. They have the sheen of wet clay, a deep, light-absorbing dampness, though to touch them would be to feel only the familiar, bone-dry warmth of the Kiln Terraces. This is the first clue: they are a contradiction, a memory of moisture in a world without water.

They have no faces. The front of their ovoid heads is a perfect, featureless convexity, like a new pot smoothed on the wheel. They do not have eyes, for sight as we know it is a lie in this place of sourceless light and nested realities. They perceive through a deeper sense, a kind of tactile resonance. They feel the silent, high-frequency stress of a porcelain bough about to snap; they sense the discordant geometry of a terrace floor beginning to fold. The world's quiet brokenness is, to them, a symphony of noise.

Their movements are slow, deliberate, and aching with purpose. They are the living embodiment of the hand that built this place, left behind to maintain a creation that has forgotten its own design. You might see one standing utterly still for an hour, head cocked as if listening. It is not listening to sound, but to the groaning syntax of the space around it. Then, with an unnerving fluidity, it will move. It does not walk so much as it flows, its feet seeming to partially dissolve and reform with each step, leaving no prints on the terracotta tiles.

They are repairers. They are antibodies. When a fissure appears in the bone-white glaze of a wall, a Tender will approach. It will press its hand against the crack, and for a long moment, do nothing. Then, you will watch as the clay of its own body begins to flow, a thick, slow-motion river of material, into the fissure. The clay is not a tool; it is a sacrifice. The Tender pushes its own substance into the wound of the world, and where its palm was, there is now a concave hollow. The crack in the wall is healed, sealed with a patch of fresh, damp-looking clay, but the Tender is diminished. Its arm is thinner, its form subtly eroded.

You understand, watching this, that they are not individuals. They are a distributed consciousness, a single, fading intention. They emerge fully-formed, extruding silently from the clay walls of the deeper terraces, and they exist only to mend. They do not eat, they do not sleep, they do not communicate. They merely work, driven by the ghost of the original blueprint. When a Tender has given too much of itself—when its form has become too attenuated, a mere stick-figure of its former self, pocked with the hollows of its many repairs—it will find a quiet place among the quartz grass. There, it will slowly crumble, not into dust, but into a fine, inert powder that is indistinguishable from the warmth underfoot. Its animating energy dissipates back into the ozone-laced air, perhaps to coalesce in a new Tender being born elsewhere.

They are untroubled by the skittering mica-trilobites or the silent asbestos-moths. That life is part of the system's strange, emergent nature. The Tenders are concerned only with the foundation, the architecture of the thought itself. They are profoundly patient with the world's madness. When a sound arrives from the future, they may pause, their faceless heads turning towards the source of a shattering that has not yet occurred, a silent acknowledgment of the error. When they encounter a space that is not contiguous, they do not try to walk it. They will pause at the edge of the spatial seam, reach an impossibly long arm *through* the nested reality of a translucent tree, and continue their work on the other side, their body existing in two places at once. They are experts in navigating broken rules.

To be near a Tender is to feel a profound and ancient diligence. There is no joy in their work, nor is there sorrow. There is only the quiet, consuming act of holding things together. They are like a person muttering a phrase over and over, trying not to forget it, even as the words themselves lose their meaning. They are the memory of the potter's hands, still trying to shape a vessel that has already been fired, cracked, and left to the ages. They are the last, fading remnant of the mind that dreamed this place, and they will work until they have smoothed the last crack with the last of their substance, or until the whole beautiful, impossible creation finally falls apart around them.
