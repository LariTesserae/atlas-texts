They are the Process. They are not a "who," but a "what." To ask what they think or feel is like asking what a river feels as it wears down a stone. They are a force of nature that has taken the form of flesh. They are geology in motion, an ecosystem boiled down to a single, relentless equation.

What lives here is an idea, made manifest in three distinct, interlocking forms. They are not species, for they cannot exist apart from one another. They are organs of a single, distributed organism whose body is the entire cathedral of plant-flesh. If the great hall is the factory, they are the machines. They do not possess life; they are possessed *by* it. Their existence is a duty, inscribed in every cell of their being.

Their consciousness, if the word can be so abused, is a form of gravity. It is a pull towards a single, unalterable purpose. There is no flicker of choice, no shadow of doubt. There is only the instruction, running on a loop for a thousand millennia.

**The Harvesters.** These are the source of the clatter, the grinders you saw. To look at one up close is to see the uncanny fusion of insect and industry. Its body, the size of a low-slung carriage, is a casing of chitin so dense and polished it reflects the diffuse light with the cold sheen of gunmetal. It moves on six, multi-jointed legs, but their motion is not the scuttling of a beetle. It is the precise, hydraulic actuation of a piston. Each limb lifts, extends, and plants itself with a planned, unhesitating finality, carrying the body forward in a line so straight it could have been drawn with a ruler.

Its head is a tool. There are no eyes, no antennae, no trembling palps. These are extravagances, and the system has no room for extravagance. The head is simply a housing for the mandibles, and the mandibles are everything. They are twinned crescents of a bio-mineral composite, dull grey and visibly worn, like the teeth of a colossal gear. They do not open and close to bite. They oscillate with incredible speed, vibrating against the living wall with a high-frequency shriek that is lost in the chamber’s overall hum. This is the clatter: the sound of a million microscopic impacts per second, shearing the cellular wall into a fine, green powder.

The Harvester does not hunger. The dust it creates is not its food. The impulse to scrape is its only reality. It begins at one end of its assigned wall segment and moves to the other. Upon reaching the end, its legs perform a perfect, zero-radius turn, and it begins its journey back, its mandibles never ceasing their vibration. It is a living sander, a planing machine of flesh and bone, and its mind is the simple, binary state of its motor function: `ON`.

**The Attendants.** These are the collectors, the silent tumblers. They are the system's circulatory system. A single Attendant is a beautiful, baffling thing. It is a sphere woven from countless filaments of a material that defies easy classification. It looks like spun copper wire, but it flexes and bends with organic softness. These cilia are what propel it, rippling in synchronized waves to send the sphere rolling across the slick floor with a silence that is deeply unsettling in this hall of noise.

An Attendant’s existence is one of patient, geometric searching. It does not "see" the green dust left by the Harvesters. Its path is a pre-ordained spirograph, a pattern of travel that, over time, is statistically guaranteed to cover every square inch of its allotted floor space. As it tumbles, the cilia, perhaps through some electrostatic property, gather the fine dust. The dust clings, and the sphere grows heavy.

This is its only sensory input: its own mass. As the weight increases, the ripples of its cilia subtly change, altering its course. The complex geometry of its search pattern simplifies into a direct, purposeful vector towards the nearest pillar-base, towards the deep, rhythmic *thump* of a Digestor. It moves not with the urgency of a creature bringing food, but with the inevitability of a ball rolling downhill.

**The Hearts.** These are the Digestors, the source of the deep bass note of the world. They are the least mobile, and the most profoundly alive. A Digestor is a great, sessile mound of purple, vascular flesh fused directly and seamlessly into the floor and the pillar. It is an organ externalized, a stomach and a lung for the entire system. Its surface writhes with a slow, peristaltic pulse, the skin tightening and relaxing over the immense internal pressures. This is the *thump-thump* that you feel in your bones—the beat of a heart that pumps not blood, but purpose.

When a heavy Attendant reaches it, a perfect, circular sphincter on the Digestor's flank dilates with the clean precision of an airlock. The tissue is soft and pliable, but the motion is mechanical. The Attendant rolls partially inside, and a shudder passes through it as it voids its cargo of green dust into the Digestor's core. The sphincter closes, the Attendant rolls away—lighter, faster, its search pattern reactivated—and the Digestor begins its true work.

The pulse deepens. Inside the mound, unimaginable pressures and biochemical reactions take place. This is the source of the impossible smell of hot metal and ozone. The Digestor is a cold-fusion reactor of biology, breaking down the simple cellulose dust and transforming it into a complex, energy-rich vapor. With a great, shuddering exhalation, it vents this steam from pulsing orifices on its upper surface. The hot, nutrient-rich fog rises, feeding the translucent membrane of the ceiling, fueling the growth that thickens the walls, providing the very substance that the Harvesters will, in turn, shear away.

They are born into this cycle and they cease within it. When a Harvester’s leg-joint finally fails after centuries of motion, it freezes in place. When an Attendant’s cilia fray and can no longer propel it, it comes to rest. They become inert matter. In time, another Attendant, on its endless, unseeing patrol, will find the static body. It will not recognize it as a former comrade; it recognizes it only as loose material on the floor. With inefficient slowness, its cilia will abrade the failed unit, gathering its dust along with the green powder of the walls, and carry it to the Digestor. They are returned to the system from which they were made. There is no death, only reclamation. They do not live here. They *are* here. They are the noise. They are the process. They are the hum, the clatter, and the thump, made flesh.
