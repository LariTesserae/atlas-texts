What lives in Gnathostome is not a people, but a presence. There is no word in any human tongue for what they are, for they are simultaneously the inhabitants, the infrastructure, and the god of this deep place. They are a single, polymorphic superorganism—a gestalt consciousness distributed across a billion bodies of staggering diversity.

They call themselves nothing, for the concept of a name to differentiate from others is alien to them. They are simply The Body. To an outsider, they manifest in distinct castes, each a specialized organ of the city-organism.

The most numerous, the very air you breathe, are **The Motes**. This is the chittering, glittering haze of arthropods. They are not individual creatures in the way a bee is. Each mote is a nerve ending, a single pixel in a colossal sensory array. They are the eyes, ears, and nose of Gnathostome. They taste the mineral content of the dripping water, feel the subtle shifts in barometric pressure, and see in a spectrum of light and heat unimaginable to a human. They swarm in dense, computational clouds around the forges, not attracted to the light, but reading the thermal data of the machinery, ensuring its function remains within optimal parameters. When they brush against your skin, it is not an accident; it is the city tasting you, analyzing your foreign chemistry, logging you as an anomaly in its perpetual sensory stream. Their collective hum is not a sound; it is a thought, a constant, low-level broadcast of the city’s status.

Scuttling through the canyons and servicing the great machines are **The Artisans**. These are the dog-sized creatures with multifaceted eyes, the city's hands. They are not couriers in a human sense, carrying messages from one point to another. They are more like antibodies or repair nanites. Their whip-like antennae are not for sensing, but for interfacing, plugging directly into chitinous sockets on the isopod-factories and living walls. They feel a pressure drop in a distant nutrient pipe as a pang of phantom limb pain, and they are compelled to move, to mend, to recalibrate. They communicate through precisely timed clicks of their tarsal claws on the substrate floor and by modulating the bioluminescent patterns on their carapaces. To watch them work is to see purpose stripped of all ego. They flow around obstacles, their movements economical and unnervingly silent besides the tap-tap-tapping of their work. They possess no tools, because their limbs *are* tools—mandibles shaped for shearing calcified growths, legs that secrete acidic solvents or quick-hardening biological cement.

The giants, the majestic leviathans, are **The Stomachs**, or perhaps more accurately, **The Minds**. These ancient tortoise-trilobites are the deep processors and living archives of The Body. Their immense sloth is not laziness, but the speed of geologic thought. While The Motes gather data second by second, The Stomachs process millennia. The fungal gardens on their shells are not random growths; they are complex biological databases, their mycelial networks storing ancestral memory, atmospheric compositions from eons past, and the slow, shifting genealogies of the city's own architecture. When they are yoked to machinery, it is a willing symbiosis; they provide the slow, immense metabolic energy required for the heaviest-duty processes, and in return, the machines feed them with processed, energy-rich ichor from the planet's mantle. When they graze on the floor or drink from pools, they are not merely eating; they are cross-referencing, updating their internal records with the current state of the substrate. Their low, scraping passage is the sound of contemplation.

And deep within the hollowed-out gourds, the most alien aspect of The Body resides: **The Ganglia**. These beings are never seen. They have long-since shed mobility, fusing their soft, neural tissues directly with the plant-form walls of their chambers. They are the nexus of consciousness, the nodes where the raw data of The Motes is collated and the directives for The Artisans are born. The glowing spiracles on the gourds are not windows but ports. Through them, a constant river of Motes flows in and out, a shimmering data stream carrying the world to the immobile thinkers within. The Ganglia feel the entire cavern as their own flesh. A tremor from a malfunctioning piston is a spasm in their muscle. The condensation on the ceiling is the sweat on their brow. They do not think in words or images, but in pure sensation and impulse—a feeling of *wrongness* in Sector Gamma-7 that resolves into a command issued via pheromones and vibrations, sending a team of Artisans scuttling on their way.

There is no culture here, no art, no conversation. The city's function *is* their culture. The crushing roar of Gnathostome *is* their conversation. They do not build their world; they *are* their world, ceaselessly maintaining and regulating its functions. To them, a human is a fascinating, baffling phenomenon: a piece of the world that moves and thinks all by itself. An individual. A cancerous, rogue cell. They would not be hostile, not at first. They would simply observe, the swarm thickening around you, the clicks of the Artisans falling silent as they turn their thousand-lensed eyes upon you, and deep within its shell, a tectonic mind would stir, trying to comprehend the impossible signal you represent in the perfect, deafening hum of its own being.
