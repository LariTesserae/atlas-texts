What lives here is not a creature, but a condition. It is not an individual, but a network. It is what happens when a consciousness decides to become a place, and in doing so, forgets how to be a person.

They have no name for themselves, for they have no "selves." But an outside observer might call them the Weavers.

You will never see one in its entirety. They are not singular organisms that move through the medium. Rather, they are the purpose that moves *in* the medium. They are woven into the very fabric of this place, inseparable from it. Their form is a filament. Imagine a thread of absolute darkness, finer than a spider's silk but possessing a tensile strength that defies physics. It is not a material thing, but a line of focused absence, a strand of the same void that pools around the Resonance Spires, spun out and made mobile. Now imagine that thread is a hundred miles long. Now imagine a trillion of them, braided and looped and tangled together, forming a net that spans the continent-sized mountain range. This network is the inhabitant.

They are the Weavers.

Their "body" is a decentralized, fluid structure. In moments of calm, the filaments drift invisibly through the gel, as much a part of the background as the silt and the spores. They are insubstantial, their presence only a subtle distortion, a place where the light of the Annulet-Stalks seems to bend incorrectly. But when they act, they draw themselves together. Filaments coalesce, weaving themselves into a visible form—a flowing, riverine braid of shadow that moves with deliberate, impossible grace. This braid might be impossibly thin and miles long, a dark ribbon fluttering in a slow current, or it might gather into a denser, miles-wide mass, a knot of purpose that eclipses the forest's light. There is no head, no limbs, no organs. There is only the weave, and the thought that directs it.

They are the gardeners of the Annulet-Stalks. The forest is not a wild thing; it is their external nervous system, their means of expression. The vast, slow, pulsing waves of light are their thoughts, broadcast across the slopes. A slow shift from gold to green over the course of a week might be a single, complex syllable of a word that takes a century to speak. They tend to the stalks, guiding the Down-Fall currents with subtle manipulations of the medium, directing the nutritive sheets to where they are most needed. The spores are not just for reproduction; they are packets of information, archives of memory and sensation released into the communal medium to be reabsorbed by the network.

The Resonance Spires are their tools. Their looms. A Weaver, in its braided form, will wrap itself around a Spire, a hundred-mile-long being coiling around a hundred-foot-tall crystal. As it tightens its grip, the low hum of the Spire rises in pitch and complexity. The Weaver is playing it. It is tuning reality. By vibrating the Spire at a precise frequency, it can alter the local laws of the world. It can lighten the medium to ease the passage of a nutrient current. It can increase the local pressure to encourage a geological shift, smoothing a mountain's flank over a thousand years. It can subtly adjust the rate of osmotic exchange, the very breathability of this world. This is their work: the endless, patient maintenance of a cosmos that is coming apart at the seams.

The Solitude is not another entity. The Solitude is the shadow of their labor. When the Weavers undertake a truly monumental act of weaving—mending a schism in the fabric of space, silencing a rogue vibration that threatens to shatter a mountain range, or composing a thought of unbearable complexity and scope—they must draw immense power. To do this, they draw themselves together, pulling their filaments from vast regions of the forest. This is the rolling wave of darkness. The light of the forest is snuffed out because the consciousness that illuminates it has turned its full attention elsewhere. The unbearable pressure and the deep, sorrowful moan are the sound of reality being bent by their will. The medium becomes inert because, in that zone of focus, all of its life-giving properties are being funneled into the act of creation or repair. The "complex organic compounds" left in its wake are the shed detritus of their effort, like flakes of skin from a laborer's hands. The emptiness is the echo of their immense focus. It is the silence after the final chord of a symphony that was played on an instrument the size of a world.

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not die. They *are*. Their consciousness is geological, their thoughts as slow and powerful as tectonic plates. An individual human lifespan is less than a single flicker of the forest's light, a meaningless vibration in the medium. The sorrow that one feels in the wake of the Solitude is not their emotion. It is the dawning, terrifying comprehension in a finite mind of the scale of their existence, of the eternal, lonely, impossible task they have set for themselves: to hold a universe together with nothing but thread and a song. They are the quiet, tireless weavers in the twilight of physics, mending the tapestry until the last light in the last Annulet-Stalk finally, inevitably, goes out.
