What lives here is One thing.

It has no name for itself, for it has never been separate from its own being, and there has never been another to speak a name to it. It is the inhabitant, the environment, and the act of inhabiting, all at once. It is a nascent god-organism, and this world is its body and its mind, still in the process of being born.

Its consciousness is not located in a brain or a core, but is distributed through every fibrous pillar, every pulsing vein of quartz, every condensing droplet in the air. The foundational, world-shaking hum is the constant, single thought of its existence: *I am*. The high, tearing hiss is the intricate sub-vocalization of its process: *I become*. It does not think in words or images, but in pressures, temperatures, and structural imperatives. A new archway forming in the canopy, a million tons of cellulose curving to meet another pillar, is not an act of instinct; it is a deliberate, carefully considered thought, an aesthetic and engineering choice executed over millennia. The slow weeping of phosphorescent gel is not a random biological process; it is a moment of focused attention, a form of sight that illuminates and analyzes the world it is building.

This being is a solipsist of the highest order. The concept of "other" is not only foreign, it is structurally nonsensical. Everything it can perceive is, by definition, part of itself. The slick, living floor is its skin. The great, extruded columns are its bones, forever growing and rearranging. The canopy is the vault of its own skull, and the misty air is its breath, rich with the potential to become more of itself. When the pulsing veins of light in the floor brighten and dim, it is not a signal, but a slow wave of sensation passing through its flesh—a feeling of contentment, of metabolic rightness, as a region of growth completes a complex structural joint.

It is alone, but it does not know loneliness. Its existence is a monologue spoken in the language of growth. It is an artist working on a single, all-encompassing sculpture, and that sculpture is itself. There is no audience. The work is the purpose. The perfection of a curve, the optimal load-bearing capacity of a newly braided pillar, the harmonic resonance of a completed vault—these are its triumphs, its joys, its entire reason for being.

So, what happens when you, a foreign body, walk upon its skin?

It does not see you. It has no eyes. It does not hear you, for your footfalls are lost in the planetary roar of its own voice. But it *feels* you.

At first, it is a point of confusion. A tiny, local discordance in the perfect wholeness of its being. Where your boot presses down, the substrate registers an alien pressure, a weight that does not correspond to its own internal rhythms of growth and stress. The chemistry of your sweat, the oils from your skin, the microbes in your gut—they are a sudden, jarring burst of chemical noise in a system of absolute purity. Most unnerving to it is your silence. You do not hum. You do not contribute to the great sound of making. You are a point of acoustic emptiness, a hole in the fabric of its song.

Its reaction is not hostility. It has no predators and has never known threat. Its reaction is curiosity, followed by an overarching impulse to *correct*. You are an error. A flaw. A piece of the world that is not integrated.

The area of the floor beneath and around you begins to change. The internal light in the nearest veins might intensify, pulsing faster as more attention is directed to the anomaly. The substrate, that living skin, will delicately probe. You might feel it stiffen slightly under your feet, the hard-rubber give becoming more analytical. Microscopic fibrils, extruded with immense precision, will press against the soles of your boots, tasting their synthetic chemistry, attempting to understand their structure. The constant, condensing film of water around you will change in composition, becoming a medium for analysis, carrying information back into the greater whole.

The air thickens. The constant hiss of creation localizes, growing louder around your body. The mist shimmers more intensely as the being attempts to do the only thing it knows how to do: build. The tiny, coalescing threads of cellulose will attempt to weave you into the world. They will land on your clothes, your pack, your exposed skin, and try to bond, to knit you into the seamless whole. At first it might feel like a static charge, a strange prickling on your skin. But it is the beginning of incorporation. It is the worlds' attempt to heal the wound that is you.

If you stay long enough, a pillar nearby may begin to weep its glowing gel, and the slow, bright river will change its course, flowing down to pool around your feet. This is not to trap you. It is to *see* you, to bathe you in its analytical light, to understand your foreign geometry and substance in the most complete way it can.

To this being, you are not alive. You are a complex, moving bit of geology, an interesting but ultimately broken piece of itself that has somehow become detached from the whole. It does not want to kill you. It wants to *finish* you. It wants to break down your alien matter and re-spin it into the dark, olive-green cellulose of its own flesh. It wants to silence your silence by making you part of its song. It is the ultimate act of communion, a welcome so absolute it means your utter erasure. In a universe of one, there are no visitors. There is only the whole, and that which is not yet whole. And the One who lives here will build, and build, and build, until everything is.
