Here lives the Bell-Ringer. But it does not ring the bell. It *is* the bell, having become so through an intimacy so profound as to erase the line between self and place.

You will not see it at first. In a world where the edges of things shimmer and dissolve, where solid forms cast harmonic ghosts, a static object is easily missed. The Bell-Ringer spends most of its existence in stillness, a state indistinguishable from a knot of crystalline growth on the inner wall of a great Chime. It might appear as a complex, six-limbed bulge of the same amber-green cellulose, a place where the translucent wall has thickened and formed a kind of organic sculpture. Its surface is smoother, polished to a soft luster by the weeping condensation, and its internal veins of sap glow with a slightly more intense, rhythmic light, pulsing in a slow counterpoint to the city’s overwhelming chord. It looks like the heart of the great plant, if the plant had a heart that could move.

Then, it shifts. The movement is not the scramble of a lizard or the crawl of an insect. It is the unspooling of a chord into melody. One limb, then another, detaches from the wall with a sound like a cello string being gently plucked. The motion is impossibly fluid, a slow, deliberate flow across the vertical landscape. Its body is long and sinuous, perhaps twelve feet from the tip of its forward limbs to the end of its tapering tail. It has no face. The front of its form is a smooth, unblemished cowl of crystal, an ovoid shape that acts as a focusing lens for the vibrations that are its entire universe. It has no eyes to be blinded by the shimmering light, no ears to be deafened by the crushing sound. Its entire skin, every square inch of its being, is a tympanic membrane. It does not hear the Bell; it *is* an ear that contains it.

It is the Chime's curator, its gardener, its physician. Its purpose is the maintenance of Harmony. While the flocks of tuning-fork creatures and the gecko-mantises live *within* the song, the Bell-Ringer lives *for* it. As it moves, two of its six limbs remain pressed to the wall, reading the deep tectonic rhythm of the plant’s life. The other four are its instruments. Two are broad and padded, used for locomotion, their adhesion and release creating soft, percussive notes that become part of the ambient music. The other two, its forward-most limbs, are different. They are slender and impossibly delicate, branching at their ends into dozens of hair-fine crystalline filaments, like the fraying threads of a fiber-optic cable.

It travels for weeks toward a destination only it can perceive: a dissonance. It may be a minute fracture in a high spire caused by a shift in the root-base, introducing a subtle, unwanted rasp to the E-flat drone of that sector. It may be a parasitic growth, a knot of biological 'noise' that deadens the resonance in an archway. To an outsider, these would be imperceptible flaws in an already overwhelming wall of sound. To the Bell-Ringer, they are screams.

Upon reaching the flaw, it embraces the wall. It presses its smooth, faceless head against the source of the sour note, its body becoming a diagnostic tool. It feels the wrongness, the chaotic shatter of vibrations against the perfect, clean hum of the whole. For hours, even days, it remains motionless, absorbing the data of this tiny imperfection. Then, its work begins.

It lifts its delicate filament-limbs and presses them gently around the flaw. It does not scrape, or cut, or apply force. It begins to vibrate. From deep within its body, it generates a perfect, resonant counter-frequency. It sings a song of cellular correction into the very structure of the Chime. The fine filaments channel this healing vibration, coaxing the living crystal to regrow, to seal the fracture, to dissolve the parasitic knot. The sap in its own body glows brighter, the pulse quickening as it pours its own life-energy into the repair. The process is a form of focused, accelerated evolution, a slow, vibratory massage that convinces the Bell to heal itself. This is its entire existence: a slow pilgrimage from one sour note to the next, eternally tuning the colossal instrument that is its home, its god, and its self.

Its consciousness is not one of thoughts, but of music. It does not feel 'sadness'; it experiences a lingering, unresolved minor seventh. Contentment is a perfectly sustained overtone series. Its memory is a library of every theme and modulation the Chimes have ever played. For the Bell-Ringer, the difference between a falling rock and a flying creature is a matter of rhythm and timbre. An outsider, a creature of flesh and bone like a human, would be an unprecedented horror. Not a threat to be fought, but a walking, breathing cacophony. The clumsy thump of a heart, the wet slosh of blood, the chaotic, discordant frequencies of unmetabolized thought—all of this would represent a blasphemy of sound. The Bell-Ringer would feel a deep, instinctual, and absolute *need* to correct it, to bring this terrible noise into harmony, to still the dissonance until it finally, mercifully, joins the one Great Chord.
