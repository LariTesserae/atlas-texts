### **Traveler's Advisory: The Reservoir**
**Field Protocol for Sub-Pylon Traverse**

**Foreword: Mindset is Your First Line of Defense**

Before we discuss equipment, you must prepare your mind. You are not going for a dive. You are installing yourself, a foreign and fragile biological anomaly, into the circulatory system of a planetary-scale engine. The greatest danger in the Reservoir is not the pressure or the fauna, but a psychological condition known as **Structural Agoraphobia** or **Scale Collapse**.

When you are suspended in the cyan void, miles from the nearest solid object, with the ceiling and floor lost to a uniform haze, your mammalian brain will struggle to find purchase. There are no horizons, no sun, no familiar sense of up or down beyond your suit's internal gyros. The sheer, non-fractal, inhuman scale of the pylons and the spaces between them can induce a paralytic sense of insignificance.

**Your mantra must be: The Grid is Real.** The pylons, the floor-plates, the distant trusses—this is your reality. Cling to it mentally before you ever need to cling to it physically. Do not allow your mind to drift into the blue. The blue is an illusion of infinity; the grid is the truth.

### **Section 1: The Kit - Your Personal Environment**

Standard multiverse travel gear must be significantly augmented. Assume your base kit includes a pressure-safe suit and basic life support; the following are non-negotiable additions for this specific environment.

**1.1. Suit & Life Support:**
*   **Pressure Rating:** Your suit must be rated for immense, sustained hydrostatic pressure. We are talking depths that would crush a military submarine. Seek a solid-state articulated suit over a flexible one; the joints will be more reliable under the constant strain.
*   **Closed-Loop Rebreather & Water Filtration:** While the water is "clean," it is not sterile. It is a coolant and a medium for an engineered ecosystem. Your suit's life support must be entirely self-contained. Do not plan on filtering ambient water for drinking. The onboard system should run for a minimum of 72 standard hours without resupply.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The ambient temperature is cool and stable, but you will encounter **Thermal Plumes**. These columns of super-heated water, rising from heat exchangers, can flash-boil unprotected surfaces and will overwhelm a basic suit cooler. Your system must be able to rapidly dump excess heat. Conversely, extended periods of inactivity will require active heating to combat the relentless heat-sink of the surrounding water.

**1.2. Propulsion & Anchoring:**
*   **Variable-Vector Thruster Pack:** You will need a whisper-quiet, electrically powered thruster system. Combustion is impossible. Prioritize fine control over raw speed. You will be navigating tight spaces and making minute adjustments to your position.
*   **Grapnel & Winch System (x2):** This is more important than your thrusters. You will need to anchor yourself to surfaces to rest, observe, or ride out a **Circulation Jet**. A magnetic grapple is useless against cerametal; you need a kinetic-fire, piton-style anchor. Have two, for redundancy and for "spidering" your way across surfaces without thruster use. Your winch should be powerful enough to haul you against a moderate current.
*   **Drogue/Sea Anchor:** An essential piece of safety equipment. In the event of thruster failure in open water, deploying a drogue will stabilize your drift and make you easier to find, assuming you have a recovery plan. It can also slow you down if you are caught in a major current.

**1.3. Navigation & Sensory Suite:**
*   **Grid Positioning System (GPS):** Not *Global* Positioning, but *Grid* Positioning. This device triangulates your position based on the known locations of pylons. It pings the pylons with active sonar and calculates your coordinates within the grid. Pylon #000-000 is the designated origin point for most charts. Do not leave your entry point without a calibrated GPS lock.
*   **Active Sonar & Acoustic Imaging:** Your eyes will deceive you. The uniform light and vast distances render conventional sight unreliable. Your primary sense must be hearing, augmented by technology. An advanced hydrophone array linked to a holographic display will allow you to "see" the soundscape. You will see the hum of the machinery, the roar of the thermal vents, the tell-tale scrape of a Hull-Grazer, and—most importantly—the silent passage of a Stalker moving through the acoustic shadows.
*   **High-Lumen, Variable-Focus Lamps:** Your suit should be equipped with powerful, directional lights. These are not for general illumination but for punching through the gloom to inspect surfaces or identify targets. Critically, you must also be able to extinguish them instantly. Light makes you a target. Your default state should be darkness. Use light like a scalpel, not a lantern.

### **Section 2: Environmental Dynamics - The Weather Machine**

To move through the Reservoir is to navigate its "weather," a constant, predictable, but immensely powerful set of man-made phenomena.

*   **The Hum:** This is your compass and your clock. The multi-layered drone of the machinery is not uniform. Different sectors have unique resonant frequencies based on the machinery active within them. A skilled traveler can identify their rough location by the specific flavor of the Hum. Learn its rhythm or be crushed by it.
*   **Circulation Jets:** These are underwater rivers, blasted from apertures in the pylons. Charts exist that map the major jets. They are the Reservoir's highways. If you can safely enter one, it can carry you between pylons in a fraction of the time it would take to use thrusters. Be warned: they are turbulent at the edges and utterly unforgiving. Miss your "exit," and you could be carried for a hundred miles before the current dissipates.
*   **Thermal Plumes:** Visible as shimmering distortions, these are vertical elevators. Ride one, and you can ascend thousands of feet in minutes. They are often rich in the nutrients that feed the biofilm, so expect increased biological activity. The turbulence and heat can be extreme.
*   **Light & Shadow:** The cones of white light from the pylon lamps are safe zones from a certain perspective, offering clear visibility. However, they also make you painfully obvious to anything watching. The shadows they cast are absolute voids of black. They offer perfect concealment but can also hide a waiting Stalker. Never cross from a light cone into a shadow, or vice-versa, without first doing a full 360-degree acoustic sweep.

### **Section 3: Ecological Encounters - Know Your Neighbors**

The ecosystem is as engineered as the architecture. It is simple, but the scales are inhuman.

*   **The Biofilm:** This iridescent, algae-like layer is the foundation of the food chain. It is harmless, but its presence indicates a surface is "permanent" and not a moving part. The clean, dark trails cut through it show the recent passage of a Hull-Grazer.
*   **Hull-Grazers:** These creatures are geography as much as biology. They are slow, predictable, and utterly indifferent to you. Their carbide grinding plates produce a high-frequency *shhhhhh-krrrrrr* that is audible from miles away through a hydrophone. Do not get underneath one. While not malicious, their sheer mass can crush you without notice. Their presence is a good sign for one reason: they are the primary prey of Stalkers. Where you find grazers, you will find predators.
*   **Stalkers:** This is your apex threat. They are biomechanical hunters, perfectly adapted to this environment.
    *   **Hunting Method:** They do not hunt by sight. They hunt by sound. They filter the ambient Hum, listening for anomalies: the scrape of a grazer, the whine of a malfunctioning thruster, the clang of a dropped tool, the panicked pulse of a traveler's heartbeat transmitted through their suit.
    *   **Avoidance:**
        1.  **Go Silent:** When you suspect a Stalker is near, your best defense is to kill your thrusters, anchor yourself to a pylon, and shut down everything but passive life support. Go completely still and silent. Try to match the rhythm of the Hum.
        2.  **Avoid Prey Sounds:** Do not make high-frequency scraping noises against surfaces. This mimics a grazer and is a dinner bell.
        3.  **Use Acoustic Shadows:** The immense machinery casts sound-shadows just as it casts light-shadows. Hiding behind a massive pump housing can make you acoustically invisible.
        4.  **Acoustic Decoys:** A wise traveler carries several small, disposable noisemakers that can be launched to mimic grazer activity, drawing a Stalker away from your position.

### **Section 4: The Unseen Purpose - A Traveler's Etiquette**

You are a ghost in this machine. You are not part of its function. The purpose of this colossal water-cooled computer, or power plant, or filtration system, is unknown. Your goal is not to understand it, but to pass through it.

Do not attempt to interface with any systems.
Do not damage any structures.
Do not try to communicate with the life forms.

You are a microbe on a microchip. Observe, record, survive. The logic of this place is cold, vast, and utterly uninterested in you. Grant it the same courtesy. Your survival depends on your ability to be insignificant.
