**Document Class:** Traveler-to-Traveler Advisory, Unofficial
**Author:** An unnamed traveler who has been inside.

### **Phase I: The Approach & The Breach**

Before you can even consider the interior, you must prepare for the moment of its birth into your world. The breach is the single most violent and dangerous moment of the entire expedition.

**1. Identification:**
The geodes are masters of camouflage. They look like any other large boulder or rock formation. Travelers have reported them ranging from the size of a skull to that of a small cottage. There are no external clues to their nature—no faint hum, no strange thermal signature. They are perfectly insulated. Identification is often a matter of local legend ("the singing stone," "the rock that rang when it fell"), geological survey data showing an impossible density anomaly, or sheer, dumb luck. Do not waste time striking random rocks; you are more likely to break your tools than find a Choir.

**2. The Ethical Consideration:**
I must state this again. The moment you crack the shell, the geode's timeless, internal equilibrium is shattered. The sound and light will bleed out until its internal energy dissipates—a process that could take minutes, years, or millennia. You are killing a thing you cannot possibly comprehend. Be at peace with that decision before you lift your hammer. There is no going back.

**3. Breach Equipment - Sensory Protection is NOT Optional:**

This is the most critical gear list you will ever follow. Failure here will result in permanent disability or death before you even glimpse the interior.

*   **Auditory Protection:** The sound is not merely loud; it is a physical force.
    *   **Primary:** You will need a full-head, hermetically sealed helmet with an active noise cancellation system rated for extreme industrial environments. Standard earplugs or gun-range earmuffs will be destroyed, and your eardrums with them. The system should not just block sound, but actively broadcast counter-frequencies to protect your inner ear.
    *   **Secondary (Somatic Dampening):** The sound will vibrate through the ground and air, directly into your body. Wear a gel-infused or vibro-absorbent full-body suit beneath your outer layers. This is akin to what high-G pilots wear, but designed to dampen vibration, not pressure. Without it, the resonance will literally shake your skeleton apart, cause micro-fractures in your bones, and turn your organs to jelly. You will feel it in your teeth first. That is your final warning.

*   **Visual Protection:** The light is absolute, shadowless, and brilliant white.
    *   **Primary:** Your helmet's visor must have adjustable, full-spectrum polarization and tinting that can dial up to 99.99% opacity. Imagine staring into a snowfield lit by a noon sun, but the light is coming from the snow itself. It erases contours and depth.
    *   **Backup:** Carry several pairs of glacier-grade sunglasses with full side-shielding in a shielded pocket. If your helmet's visor fails, you will be rendered instantly and permanently snow-blind.

*   **The Breach Tool:** Choose your instrument of destruction.
    *   **For smaller geodes (head-to-torso sized):** A high-density, non-resonant sledgehammer (tungsten carbide is recommended; do not use steel, which may ring sympathetically and shatter).
    *   **For larger geodes (car-to-house sized):** A targeted seismic charge or a diamond-tipped hydraulic press. Hitting it with a hammer will do nothing. The goal is a single, clean fracture, not pulverization.

**Procedure for The Breach:**
1.  Suit up completely. Perform full systems checks on audio and visual dampeners.
2.  Clear the area. The sonic shockwave can travel for miles. Warn any local populations.
3.  Position your breach tool.
4.  Activate all sensory protection to maximum.
5.  Brace yourself against the ground. Strike the blow or trigger the device.
6.  You will *feel* the pressure change before anything else. It will feel like the world has suddenly been pressurized. Then the sound will hit your suit, and the light will flood your visor. Do not rush in. Let your systems and your body acclimate for a full minute. Your suit will be straining. Your bones will hum. This is your new reality.

### **Phase II: Gearing for the Interior**

You have broken the world. Now you must learn to navigate its remains.

**1. Environmental Suit & Life Support:**
The interior is cool, dry, and has no breathable atmosphere. It is a vacuum packed with crystal.

*   **Thermal Regulation:** The "marble in a cellar" coolness is deceptive. It is constant and pervasive. It will leach your body heat relentlessly. Your suit needs an active heating system, not just passive insulation. Set it to maintain a steady core temperature.
*   **Respiration:** A closed-loop rebreather is mandatory. There is no air inside. Do not assume the air that rushes in will penetrate deep into the labyrinthine structure. It won't. Bring more oxygen/scrubber cartridges than you think you'll need. Calculate your time and double it.

**2. Movement & Navigation:**
There is no "floor." You are not walking; you are climbing through a three-dimensional sculpture.

*   **Climbing Gear:** Standard caving and rock-climbing gear is your starting point. Harness, carabiners, ascenders, descenders. However, the surfaces are preternaturally smooth.
    *   **Anchors:** Bring specialized anchors. Friction-based camming devices might work in some of the tighter cracks. More reliable are micro-spikes or pitons made of a material harder than the crystal itself, which can be hammered into place. This is destructive, yes, but so was your entry. Also consider adhesive anchors, but test their efficacy in a cool, dry, high-vibration environment first.
    *   **Rope:** Bring plenty. Your entry point is your only exit. Anchor a rope securely at the breach and spool it out as you go. This is your lifeline, your Ariadne's thread. Do not lose it.

*   **Navigational Aids:** Your senses are useless for judging direction or distance.
    *   **3D Mapper:** A handheld device that uses sonar or laser scanning to build a real-time, three-dimensional map of your immediate surroundings is essential. It will help you see the "shape" of the space your eyes cannot interpret. Mark your path on the map constantly.
    *   **Laser Rangefinder:** Crucial for determining the distance to the next handhold or anchor point. What looks a foot away might be ten.
    *   **Chronometer:** The environment is timeless. Wear a rugged, reliable timepiece on your wrist and on your helmet's HUD. Set strict alarms for rest, sustenance, and—most importantly—your turnaround time. When the alarm to go back rings, you go back. No exceptions.

### **Phase III: The Experience & The Hazards**

This is why you came, and this is what will try to break you.

**1. The Chord of Being:**
Even with dampeners, you will not escape the sound. It will bypass your ears and enter you through your suit, your boots, your gloves. It is a constant, full-body hum. It will feel like a cat purring on your chest, in your skull, in your marrow. Some find it meditative. Others are driven mad by the inability to escape it. Do not try to fight it. Hum with it. Let it be the rhythm of your breathing and movement.

**2. The White-Out:**
The shadowless light makes the world flat. Your brain will struggle to build a model of the space. Rely on your hands and your mapping tools, not your eyes. Close your eyes frequently to give your optic nerves a rest, even behind the visor.

**3. The Filigree Sprouts:**
These dark, silent, fractal growths are the only landmarks you have. They are the "places" in this non-place.
*   **Treat them as sanctuaries.** They are the only visual and sonic resting points. The area around them seems to have a slightly dampened resonance. If you need to rest, do so near a Sprout.
*   **DO NOT TOUCH THEM.** They are described as "exquisitely delicate." Their structure is likely integral to the localized resonant patterns of the geode. Breaking one could have unknown, and potentially catastrophic, consequences. Think of them as the sacred groves of this crystalline god. Observe, photograph, map, but do not touch.

**4. Psychological Strain:**
*   **Claustrophobia & Agoraphobia:** You will be in tight, winding passages (claustrophobia) that are part of an infinitely repeating, incomprehensibly vast internal structure (agoraphobia). You will feel both crushed and lost at the same time. Focus on your immediate task: the next handhold, the next anchor, the next breath.
*   **Temporal Disorientation:** Without change, your mind will lose its grip on the passage of time. Trust your chronometer. Stick to your schedule. A partner is highly recommended to keep each other grounded in consensual reality.

### **Phase IV: The Exit & The Aftermath**

**Leaving:**
Follow your rope. Follow your 3D map's trail. Do not deviate. The world outside the breach will seem shockingly chaotic, dim, and blessedly, blessedly silent. The silence will be as deafening as the chord was. Re-acclimatize slowly. Do not remove your helmet until you are well away from the breach, as your ears will be hypersensitive.

**The Scar:**
You have left a wound in the world. The geode will now sing its endless note and shine its endless light into the atmosphere. This will change the local ecosystem. It might attract things—other travelers, interdimensional scavengers, or beings that feed on raw resonance. You have a responsibility. At the very least, you must report the location to a registry that tracks such phenomena, so that it can be monitored. Do not simply walk away. You broke it; you own the consequences.

**Personal Aftermath:**
You will not be the same. You may experience phantom vibrations for months. A profound tinnitus that sounds faintly like bells. You will find a new appreciation for shadows, for the quiet rustle of leaves, for the imperfect, messy, changing world. You have stood inside a piece of eternity, and in doing so, you have sentenced it to death.

May you find the silence you have lost.
