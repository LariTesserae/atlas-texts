**Traveler Advisory & Preparation Guide: The Vitreous Heart (Provisional Designation LV-404-G)**

**1. PRELIMINARY PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT & MENTAL FORTIFICATION**

Before we discuss equipment, you must understand the primary challenge of the Vitreous Heart: sensory and psychological endurance. You are not entering a space; you are inserting yourself into the guts of a running engine the size of a mountain.

*   **Claustrophilia & Agoraphobia:** The chamber is both a single, confined room and impossibly vast. You will feel simultaneously crushed by the lack of open sky and dwarfed by the scale of the geology and its inhabitants. Meditate on this paradox. If you have a psychological reliance on horizons, abort now.
*   **Sensory Overload:** The noise is not merely loud; it is a physical presence. It is a multi-layered assault on the senses, from the bone-shaking bass of the Golems to the nerve-shredding shriek of the Lice. The heat is a constant pressure. The light is a confusing, monochromatic flicker. You will be in a state of constant, low-grade sensory shock. Your mental training must include techniques to compartmentalize these inputs to avoid overload and panic. Practice focusing on a single task while subjected to extreme white noise and vibrational stimuli.
*   **Aparanoia (The Absence of Intentional Malice):** Nothing here wants to kill you. This is crucial. There is no predator-prey dynamic as you understand it. The dangers are incidental. You are simply in the way of a process. A Clatter-Golem will crush you with the same dispassionate finality it applies to a faulty rock column. This lack of malice is, for some, more terrifying than any monster. You are not a threat to be neutralized; you are an irrelevance to be erased.

**2. ENTRY & ENVIRONMENTAL SUIT PROTOCOL**

Breaching the geode is a delicate affair. It is under immense internal pressure. A brute-force entry will cause catastrophic, explosive decompression, destroying the target environment and you along with it. A resonant-frequency drill or a carefully calibrated micro-portal is the only viable method. Seal the entry point behind you; you do not want to vent the Heart's atmosphere and heat into your reality.

Your life depends entirely on your environmental suit. A standard EVA or hazard suit is a death sentence. You require a **Forge-Walker Hardshell** or equivalent. Key specifications:

*   **Thermal Management:** The ambient heat will overwhelm passive insulation. Your suit must have a closed-loop, high-capacity **active heat-pump system**. The external surfaces should be composed of ablative ceramics capable of withstanding sustained contact with cherry-red rock. The system will be a massive power drain; budget for at least three times the energy cells you think you'll need.
*   **Atmospheric Filtration:** The air is a cocktail of superheated dust, metallic particulates, and exotic gases. Your filtration system needs a multi-stage approach:
    1.  A magnetic cyclonic separator for metallic dust.
    2.  A dense physical filter for silicates and other particulates.
    3.  A catalytic chemical scrubber to neutralize the unknown gaseous by-products. They are likely poisonous, corrosive, or both. Do not, under any circumstances, expose your bare skin or respiratory system to the local atmosphere.
*   **Structural Integrity:** The suit needs to be heavily reinforced. Not for combat, but to withstand accidental impacts, falling debris, and the immense vibration. The feet are especially critical, with thick, insulated soles and carbide cleats for traction on both crystalline surfaces and the loose scree of metal pellets.
*   **Hydration:** There is no water. None. The dry heat will try to bake the moisture out of your very cells through your suit's inefficiencies. Your suit's recycling system must be 100% efficient, and you should carry a reserve of sterile water injected directly into your nutrient feed. Dehydration is a swift and insidious killer here.

**3. SENSORY & NAVIGATION EQUIPMENT**

Your own eyes and ears are liabilities.

*   **Vision:** The constant shimmering of the air distorts light, and the overwhelming infrared radiation will blind standard thermal imaging. You require a composite holographic display inside your helmet that integrates three feeds:
    1.  **High-Frequency Sonar/Lidar:** This will be your primary means of navigation. It will build a 3D wireframe of the physical space, allowing you to "see" the terrain despite the visual chaos.
    2.  **Filtered Thermal Imaging:** Tuned to specific, narrow bands, this will allow you to identify the most dangerous heat sources: fresh obsidian extrusions, the vents of the Spire-Jacks, and the glowing cores of the Furnace-Lice. Use it for threat identification, not navigation.
    3.  **Polarized Visible Light:** For when you need to see the actual color and texture of a surface. This will be the least reliable feed.
*   **Audio:** Complete noise cancellation is suicide. You need to hear the Golems. Your suit must have an **intelligent audio dampening system**. It should filter out the constant high-frequency shriek of the Lice and the mid-range ticking of the Jacks, compressing them into a manageable background hum. However, it must be programmed to let through and even amplify the low-frequency, percussive sounds of the Clatter-Golems. The thrum in your feet and the slam of their steps is your only early warning system.
*   **Navigation:** There are no landmarks. The geology is constantly, albeit slowly, changing. Your only fixed point is your entry portal. Deploy a non-heat-based, psycho-kinetically resonant beacon at your entry point. Standard electronic beacons will be cooked or deafened by the ambient noise. Your suit's navigation system must be constantly mapping your route in 3D space relative to this beacon. Do not trust your memory.

**4. INTERACTING WITH THE ECO-MECHANICAL SYSTEM**

Assume every surface is either dangerously hot, unstable, or occupied.

*   **Terrain:** The floor is your first enemy. The dark, older basalt is cooler and more stable. The glowing red and orange-white sections are new, searing hot, and under immense strain. Avoid them. The metallic scree left by the Furnace-Lice is treacherous; it's like walking on hot, perfectly round ball bearings. Move slowly and deliberately. The metallic webs of the Spire-Jacks can serve as ladders and bridges, but test each one before putting your full weight on it. Some are newly extruded and will be soft and melt-hot.
*   **Furnace-Lice:** Do not disturb the shimmering masses. They operate on a simple stimulus-response. They are drawn to heat and raw minerals. Your suit's heat signature will attract them if you stand still for too long, especially on hot ground. Keep moving. If they swarm your legs, do not panic. Scrape them off on a rock; their carapaces are tough, but they are small. Their primary danger is piling on and overwhelming your suit's cooling system.
*   **Spire-Jacks:** These are the architects. They are deliberate and oblivious. Their danger lies in their tools. They have focused thermal vents on their undersides that can melt metal; they can slice through your suit in an instant. The filaments they extrude are incredibly hot and sharp. Never cross directly under a Spire-Jack or get between it and its work. They follow predictable paths between scree piles and their current construction. Observe their routes and give them a wide berth.
*   **Clatter-Golems:** These are the primary threat to your existence. They are the system's janitors, its immune response.
    *   **Detection:** Listen. Feel the ground. The deep, rhythmic GRIND-SLAM-GRIND is your cue. Check your sonar. Identify their location and direction of travel. They move in ponderous, generally straight lines until they detect an "error."
    *   **Their Mandate:** They consume failure. A collapsed rock formation, a tangled Spire-Jack web, a malfunctioning machine. You, a foreign object made of strange materials, are a walking, breathing definition of failure in this place.
    *   **Survival Strategy:** Avoidance is paramount. Use the terrain to your advantage. Get to high ground, onto the Spire-Jack scaffolding they cannot climb. Press yourself into an alcove. Freeze. Minimize your heat signature if your suit allows it. Do not leave *anything* behind. A dropped tool, a spent energy cell—these are alien intrusions, failures in the landscape that will draw their attention. They will grind it to dust, and if you are standing next to it, they will grind you to dust as well. Their cold, white optical sensors are not looking *for* you, but they can lock *onto* you. If you see that white light pointing your way, you have been categorized. Your only hope is to break line of sight and get as far away as possible.

**5. MISSION & EXIT**

Your time here is limited by your power supply and your sanity. Get in, accomplish your objective (be it sample collection, observation, or data recording), and get out. The pellets from the Furnace-Lice are a tempting resource, but scooping them up is a noisy, conspicuous activity that involves lingering on the most treacherous terrain.

Decontamination on exit is not optional. The dust of this place is fine, abrasive, and likely contains exotic, heavy-metal isotopes. It will contaminate your ship, your equipment, and your biology. A full magnetic, chemical, and radiological scrub-down of your hardshell is mandatory before you even consider bringing it back inside your vessel.

Remember, you are a ghost in this machine. The goal is to leave without ever proving you were there. The Vitreous Heart is a song of creation, and it has no room for your verse. Listen, watch, and do not interrupt the performance. The silence when you finally leave will be the loudest thing you have ever heard.
