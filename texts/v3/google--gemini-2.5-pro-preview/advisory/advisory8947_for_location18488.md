### **Traveler’s Advisory: Subterranean Biome Designation CVN-7 ("The Gland")**

**CLASSIFICATION:** Level 4 Bio-Integrated Environment. Non-Hostile but Unconditionally Lethal to Unprepared Biorganisms.

**PRIMARY DIRECTIVE:** Observation and Non-Interference.

### **I. Foreword: Understanding the Environment**

Before you pack a single item, you must adjust your thinking. You are not entering a cave. You are entering an organism. Imagine yourself as a microbe preparing to enter a human bloodstream. The environment is not actively trying to kill you, but its fundamental processes—its immune system, its metabolic functions, its very temperature and pressure—are incompatible with your own existence. Every step you take is an intrusion. Every breath you might take of the local air is a poison.

This place, which we'll call the Gland, is not dead rock. It is a living, breathing, metabolizing entity. Its rules are biological, not geological. Your survival depends on respecting this fact implicitly. Hubris is a death sentence here. Conquest is impossible. The only viable approach is one of absolute humility.

### **II. Pre-Departure Preparations (Minimum 3 Weeks Prior)**

Your body and mind must be primed before you even approach the entry fissure.

**A. MEDICAL & PHYSIOLOGICAL:**

1.  **Full-Spectrum Immunological Work-up:** You need a complete baseline of your own microbiome and immune status. The Gland is a sealed terrarium of untold age. Its virology and bacteriology are entirely alien. Your immune system will have no reference for them.
2.  **Mycology Prophylaxis:** Given the prevalence of massive fungal bodies, airborne and contact-based spore contamination is the single greatest biological risk. Begin a course of powerful, broad-spectrum antifungal medication 7 days prior to entry. This is non-negotiable. A breach in your suit could lead to a systemic fungal infection that no off-world medicine can treat.
3.  **Heat Acclimatization:** The environment is described as having "feverish heat." You will be in a sealed suit, but your cooling system can fail. You must be prepared for extreme heat and humidity. Spend at least two weeks undergoing daily heat stress training: saunas, steam rooms, and strenuous exercise in a climate-controlled hot room. This will pre-adapt your cardiovascular system and reduce the shock in case of an emergency.
4.  **Allergy Suppression:** Take a high-dose, non-drowsy antihistamine starting 24 hours before entry. The air is thick with complex organic compounds. A breach could trigger a massive, and likely fatal, anaphylactic response.

**B. MENTAL & PSYCHOLOGICAL:**

1.  **Sensory Deprivation Training:** The Gland swallows sound. The silence is profound and can be deeply unnerving. Spend time in an anechoic chamber or sensory deprivation tank to become accustomed to the absence of external auditory stimuli. Learn to be comfortable with the sounds of your own body: your breathing, your heartbeat. In the Gland, that will be your only soundtrack.
2.  **Sensory Overload Mitigation:** Paradoxically, you must also prepare for sensory overload. The air is thick and tactile, the light is dim and shifting, and every surface is alive and damp. This can be disorienting. Practice mindfulness and focus exercises. Be able to ground yourself mentally when your senses are providing confusing or overwhelming data.
3.  **Study the Rhythms of Silence:** Read accounts of deep-sea explorers, spelunkers, and monks who have undertaken long vows of silence. Understand that silence is not an absence, but a presence. The Gland's silence is the sound of life humming at a frequency you can't hear. Prepare to feel, rather than hear, the world around you.

### **III. Equipment & Gear Loadout**

Your gear is your life support. Redundancy is not a luxury; it is the bare minimum.

**A. THE SUIT: Your Personal World**

*   **Type:** Class-IV Environmental Hazard Suit. Must be fully sealed with positive air pressure (meaning if a small tear occurs, air blows *out*, not *in*).
*   **Cooling System:** An integrated liquid-cooling garment is essential. The ambient heat will cook you alive otherwise. Carry a backup coolant pack and an emergency heat-sink chemical patch.
*   **Helmet/Visor:** Must have a full-spectrum Heads-Up Display (HUD).
    *   **Filters:** Polarizing filters to manage the pulsing biological light. Infrared and Ultraviolet modes are critical; much of the life here may communicate or display patterns outside the human visible spectrum.
    *   **Anti-Fog:** A multi-layered, heated visor to combat the extreme temperature differential between the inside and outside of your helmet. Condensation will blind you.
    *   **Audio:** External microphones with adjustable gain. You want to be able to amplify the faint *plink* of water droplets or the scuttling of isopods, but filter out the white noise of your own suit's systems. You will also need a high-quality internal comms system. Do not rely on voice commands; use a manual or neural interface.

**B. ATMOSPHERIC & BREATHING:**

*   **System:** A closed-loop rebreather, not standard oxygen tanks. You need to be able to operate for at least 24 hours. A rebreather scrubs your CO2 and recycles your oxygen, making it far more efficient.
*   **Emergency Backup:** A small, 15-minute "bailout" bottle of compressed air. This is for a catastrophic failure of your primary system, giving you just enough time to get back to the entrance.

**C. NAVIGATION & UTILITY:**

*   **Footwear:** Standard boots will fail. The humus floor is spongy and sound-absorbent. You need boots with wide, snowshoe-like soles to distribute your weight and prevent you from sinking. The soles must also have deep, soft-rubber cleats for gripping the slick, wet flowstone terraces.
*   **Navigation:** GPS is useless. You need an Inertial Navigation System (INS) calibrated at the entrance. Deploy a string of small, low-lumen marker beacons every 20 meters. These will serve as your breadcrumbs back. Your HUD should map your path in 3D.
*   **Lighting:** Do NOT use a high-powered floodlight. This would be like firing a flashbang in a cathedral during midnight mass. It will disrupt the ecosystem and potentially trigger unknown defensive reactions.
    *   Your primary light should be helmet-mounted, low-lumen, and adjustable across different color temperatures. Start with deep red, which is least likely to disturb the local fauna.
    *   Carry one handheld, narrow-beam analytic light for examining specific features.

**D. SCIENTIFIC & INTERACTION:**

*   **Tools:** A multi-function walking staff is your best friend. Use it to probe the ground ahead, to steady yourself on slopes, and to gently push aside hanging flora without touching it with your suit. The tip should have integrated sensors (temperature, pressure, basic chemical analysis).
*   **Sampling:** All sampling must be non-invasive. Bring sterile air-sampling canisters, acoustic sensors for the ritual stones, and high-resolution imaging equipment. Do not plan on taking physical samples of flora or fauna. The risk of contamination is too high.
*   **The Stones:** To interface with the spiral stones, you will need a specialized vibrometer and a broad-spectrum electromagnetic field sensor. If you must touch one, your suit's gloves should have a layer of disposable, sterile biosilicone over them.

### **IV. On-Site Protocol: The Rules of Entry**

1.  **The Descent:** As you descend, move slowly. Let your suit's systems equalize. The feeling of pushing through the heavy air is real. This is your first test. Do not fight it.
2.  **The First Step:** Upon entering the Gland proper, stop. Stand still for five full minutes. Observe. Listen with your microphones. Let your eyes adjust. Let your HUD map the immediate area. Deploy your primary anchor beacon at the threshold. This is your only way home.
3.  **Movement: The Humus Tread:** Walk slowly, deliberately. Place your weight with care. Do not drag your feet. Every step is a compression of centuries of life. Treat it with reverence. Use your staff to test every footfall. Sound is swallowed here, so you will not hear if the ground is unstable.
4.  **Interaction:** Touch nothing. The velvety fuzz on the vines, the cool skin of the fronds—assume they are coated in digestive enzymes, neurotoxins, or allergens. Admire the salamanders from a distance; their stillness is a hunter's patience. Do not disturb the isopods; they are the janitors of this world, and their work is sacred.
5.  **Water:** The pools are hot and crystal clear. Do not be tempted. The water is full of minerals and unknown microbes. It may be scaldingly hot in places. Do not step in it. Use the handholds worn into the walls to navigate around the terraced pools. They are a path left for you by something that understood this place. Follow their wisdom.
6.  **The Spiral Stones:** This is the heart of the Gland. Approach it as you would an altar in a holy place.
    *   Walk the spiral from the outside in. Follow the pattern. This may be part of the ritual.
    *   Use your sensors first. Measure the vibration. Analyze its frequency and complexity. This is the "song" of the cavern.
    *   If you choose to make contact, do so with a single, gloved palm on one of the outer stones. Do not place your full weight on it. Close your eyes. Do not try to interpret the vibration as sound or data. *Feel* it. It may feel like a resonance in your bones, a pattern in your mind. Be prepared for a sudden, overwhelming sense of connection, of being known by the entire cavern. Have a partner ready to pull you back if you become disoriented. Limit contact to 30 seconds on your first visit.

### **V. Departure & Decontamination**

Leaving is as critical as entering.

1.  **The Retreat:** Follow your marker beacons precisely. Do not rush. Make sure you have all your equipment. Leave nothing behind but your footprints, which will soon be gone.
2.  **Full Decontamination:** Before re-entering your primary vehicle or passing through a portal, you must undergo a full, multi-phase decontamination protocol in a portable airlock.
    *   **Phase 1:** Chemical shower to neutralize surface biologicals on the suit.
    *   **Phase 2:** High-energy UV/radiation bath to sterilize the suit's exterior.
    *   **Phase 3:** Careful removal of the suit. All filters, disposable glove-linings, and waste products must be incinerated.
3.  **Personal Quarantine:** You should remain in personal quarantine for at least 72 hours post-visit, monitoring for any unusual medical symptoms.

This is not a journey for the thrill-seeker or the tourist. Visiting the Gland is a pilgrimage to a world that created its own light, its own water, and its own meaning, entirely separate from the sun-drenched universe we know. Go as a student, a worshipper, or a ghost. Go quietly. And you might just be allowed to witness its silent, slow, and magnificent truth.
