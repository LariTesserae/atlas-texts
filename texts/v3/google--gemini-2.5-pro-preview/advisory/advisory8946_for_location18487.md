### **Traveler's Field Guide: Dimension PK-739 ("The Assembly")**

**Foreword: The First Rule of The Assembly**

Before we discuss gear or procedure, you must internalize one concept: this world is not for you. It is not hostile, but its indifference is a force of nature more absolute than any predator. You are a bug in a cleanroom. A phantom in a machine. The moment you act like you belong there, or that the world should react to you, is the moment your journey ends. Your goal is to observe, to exist temporarily, and to leave no trace. You are a ghost. Practice being one.

**Part I: Sensory Acclimatization & Protection**

Your senses will be the first things to betray you. Prepare for an immediate and sustained assault.

*   **Light:** The ambient, shadowless light is your primary environmental hazard. It is not merely bright; it is information-scouring. It will reduce your depth perception to nearly zero and induce headaches, disorientation, and eventually, a form of cognitive dissociation.
    *   **Primary Gear:** A full-seal helmet with a variable-tint, polarized visor. Standard sunglasses are insufficient. You need to be able to dial down the overwhelming glare while maintaining enough visibility to navigate. The polarization is key; it will help your brain find edges and contours that the raw light erases. A secondary, inner-visor with a high-contrast amber tint can be deployed for close-up observation of structures.
    *   **Acclimatization:** Upon arrival, do not look toward the horizon. Keep your visor at maximum tint and stare at your feet for a full five minutes. Let your eyes and brain adjust to the ceramic plates directly beneath you. Slowly, raise your gaze, dialing back the tint only as much as is absolutely necessary. Never stare into the "sky" or the far distance for more than a few seconds.

*   **Sound:** The silence is not an absence; it is a presence. It has a physical weight that will feel like a pressure differential in your ears. This dampening effect will make you feel isolated and vulnerable. Your own voice will sound muffled and dead, which can be deeply unsettling.
    *   **Primary Gear:** In-ear monitors or high-fidelity earplugs. Paradoxically, you do not want complete noise cancellation. You need to hear what little sound the world produces. However, having the ability to play a low-level white noise, a familiar piece of ambient music, or even just a recording of a gentle breeze during your rest periods is a critical tool for psychological stability. It creates an auditory "home base" and can stave off the madness that the profound silence will eventually invite.
    *   **Procedure:** Do not shout. Do not try to test the sound-dampening. Accept it. Communicate with hand signals if you are with a partner. Rely on your suit's radio, but use it sparingly. The feeling of your own voice being trapped inside your helmet while the world outside is dead silent is its own unique form of psychological warfare.

*   **Atmosphere:** The air is thin, cool, and perpetually damp. Breathing it tastes of ozone and petrichor without the rain. While technically breathable for short periods, long-term exposure is unknown and unwise. The fine condensation will coat everything.
    *   **Primary Gear:** A closed-system rebreather is non-negotiable. Do not breathe the local air. We don't know what long-term exposure to that "ozone" or the particulates that give it its "wet stone" taste will do to human lungs.
    *   **Apparel:** Your entire suit must be non-porous and hydrophobic. Not just water-resistant, but actively shedding moisture. Any fabric that can become saturated will make you cold, heavy, and miserable. The condensation isn't just water; think of it as the world's coolant. It will sap your body heat with shocking efficiency. Synthetics are your only choice. No cotton. No leather.

**Part II: Navigation in a Non-Euclidean Space**

The Assembly is geometrically patient, but not stable. Your greatest risk is getting lost.

*   **The Ground:** The hexagonal flooring is your only reliable frame of reference. The plates are vast, but their join-lines are perfectly straight. Use them as you would a compass grid.
    *   **Footwear:** Soft, high-friction rubber soles are essential. You need grip on the slick ceramic, but you must make no noise. No metal cleats. No hard-toed boots that will clack and echo. Every footstep should be a deliberate, silent placement. Practice walking like you're stalking a deer on a tile floor.
    *   **Procedure:** When traveling, align your path with the hexagonal grid. Move along the join-lines or parallel to them. Do not cut across the plates diagonally for long distances; it's a simple way to disorient yourself.

*   **The Unreliable Distance:** Your eyes cannot be trusted to judge distance. The structures and the light conspire to deceive you. A grove of spires may seem a day away but be reachable in minutes if you approach it obliquely.
    *   **Primary Gear:** A handheld laser rangefinder is useful, but only for immediate distances (under 200 meters). For long-haul navigation, you must rely on a physical tether. Bring a spool of brightly-colored, non-reactive polymer line (at least 5 kilometers). Secure it at your entry point. This line is your only guaranteed path home. Do not deviate from it further than you can see it.
    *   **The Rule of Averted Gaze:** Do not fixate on your destination. This is a known perceptual anomaly in realities like this. Staring at an object in the distance seems to "lock" its position relative to you. To approach something, look at the ground in front of you, get your bearings from the hex-grid, and walk the correct number of paces. Glance up to confirm, then look down again. Travel by increments, not by staring.

**Part III: The Inhabitants & Ecology**

The "life" here is part of the machine. It follows rules, not instincts.

*   **"Flora":**
    *   **Voltage Moss:** The glowing blue circuits on the floor are beautiful and dangerous. They are feeding on an electrical potential in the floor plates. Touching them could result in a severe electrical shock or worse, a discharge that marks you as a system irregularity to the Caretakers. Mark patches on your HUD and give them a wide berth.
    *   **Metallic Ivy:** The twitching cables on the pylons are not passive. They are conduits. Do not touch them. Do not try to take a sample.

*   **"Fauna":**
    *   **Glimmer-wings:** These clouds of metal foil are harmless but disorienting. They can play havoc with your sensors and create strobing effects that induce vertigo. If you must pass through a swarm, dim your visor and move slowly and steadily. They will drift around you.
    *   **Damp-crawlers:** The mercurial trilobites are the world's janitors. They are focused entirely on siphoning condensation from the join-lines. They will not deviate from their path for you, so do not be in their path. They are utterly silent and surprisingly fast. Step aside.

*   **The Caretakers: Your Prime Directive**
    The ten-foot tall, gliding figures are the most important and dangerous element of The Assembly. They are not alive in any sense we comprehend. They are extensions of the world's purpose—self-regulating maintenance drones.
    *   **Engagement Protocol:** There is no engagement protocol. You will maintain a minimum distance of 500 meters at all times. If you see one, you freeze. You alter your course. You go the long way around.
    *   **Understanding Their Indifference:** They will not notice you unless you interfere with their function. What constitutes "interference"?
        *   Making a loud noise.
        *   Damaging any structure, moss, or life form.
        *   Leaving behind trash or equipment.
        *   Touching a pylon they are inspecting.
        *   Crossing their direct path, forcing them to adjust.
    *   **Signs of Detection:** If a Caretaker stops its work and its featureless mask turns in your direction, you have failed. Do not run. This will register as erratic behavior. Freeze. Power down all non-essential systems. Make yourself as small and silent as a stone. Hope that your anomalous presence is classified as a transient, non-critical error. If it starts to glide towards you, your journey is over. There is no defense. The "correction" will be silent, efficient, and absolute. You will simply cease to be a factor.

**Part IV: Psychological Readiness & Mission Parameters**

*   **Duration:** Your maximum stay should not exceed one standard 24-hour cycle. The psychological toll of the light, the silence, and the sheer alienness is cumulative and exponential. The longer you stay, the more you risk making a mistake.
*   **Mental Anchor:** Bring something to ground you. A photograph. A piece of music. An audiobook read by a familiar voice. During your mandatory rest periods (every 4 hours), retreat into your helmet and focus on this anchor. Remind yourself what a shadow is. Remind yourself what a bird sounds like.
*   **The Goal:** You are there to observe. Take readings with passive sensors only. Take visual logs. Do not take samples. The fundamental rule is derived from archaeology: *Take only pictures, leave only (perfectly silent) footprints.*

You are a temporary condensation on a surface that is eternally becoming something else. Behave like it. Be quiet, be brief, and be gone before the world notices a smudge that needs to be wiped clean. Good luck, traveler.
