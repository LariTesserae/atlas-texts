### **Field Manual & Traveler's Advisory: World-Engine 734 ("The Foundry")**

**FOR THE ATTENTION OF THE MULTIVERSE TRAVELER. READ IN FULL BEFORE ATTEMPTING TRANSIT. FAILURE TO COMPLY WILL RESULT IN CATASTROPHIC EXISTENCE FAILURE.**

#### **Section 1: First Principles - The Nature of the Threat**

Before we discuss gear, you must understand the philosophical nature of the danger. The Foundry is not hostile. Hostility implies intent, and this world has none. It does not want to kill you. It does not care if you live. You are a fleck of dust that has landed on a colossal, malfunctioning engine. If your gear fails, if your foot slips, if you are vaporized by a plasma vent, it is not an attack. It is simply the engine continuing its function. Your survival depends entirely on your ability to predict and avoid its processes, not on fighting or hiding from a predator. This is a battle against physics, chemistry, and overwhelming industrial scale, not against a will.

#### **Section 2: Essential Gear & Bodily Preparation**

This is not a list of recommendations; it is a list of prerequisites. Arriving without any one of these items is a non-survivable event.

**2.1: Auditory Insulation & Dampening:**
This is your primary defense against sensory and psychological collapse. The sound is not merely loud; it is a physical force and a psychic poison.
*   **Primary System:** A full-helmet system with military-grade active noise cancellation is the bare minimum.
*   **Secondary System:** In-ear passive dampeners (custom-molded silicone/foam) to be worn *under* the helmet. If your helmet’s system fails, this will give you the few precious seconds needed to initiate emergency extraction before the raw sound causes vertigo, nausea, and irreversible cochlear damage.
*   **Tertiary System:** A subvocal microphone and bone-conduction audio system. You will not be able to speak or hear normally. All communication will be through your suit. Your own voice will seem like a distant whisper.

**2.2: Environmental Suit & Life Support:**
The atmosphere is a cold, corrosive chemical soup. The ground is a biological contaminant.
*   **Suit:** A Class-IV sealed environmental hazard suit, pressure-rated and reinforced with a micro-weave of non-reactive ceramic fiber. It must be completely proof against chemical solvents, extreme temperatures (both cryogenic and incandescent), and minor kinetic impacts (falling debris). The outer layer should be non-porous and slick to prevent adhesion by local lifeforms.
*   **Rebreather:** A closed-loop system is essential. The air contains trace elements that can bypass standard filters and accumulate with toxic effect over time. Your oxygen supply must be self-contained. The smell of ozone, metal, and unripe fruit is a sign that your filters are failing.
*   **Thermoregulation:** Your suit's climate control will be in constant flux. You will be walking through frigid, damp air one moment and skirting a radiator fin shedding thousands of degrees of heat the next. A high-capacity thermal battery and distribution network is critical.

**2.3: Footwear & Locomotion:**
The "ground" or "Substrate," as we will call it, is your constant companion and a constant threat.
*   **Boots:** Integrated into your suit, with deep, multi-directional treads made from a material that can grip a slick, semi-organic, yielding surface. Most importantly, they need wide, articulated soles to distribute your weight. This prevents you from sinking into softer patches of the Substrate.
*   **Ankle Support:** The yielding nature of the ground makes ankle-twists and falls a serious danger. High, rigid support is necessary. A fall here is not just an inconvenience; it can compromise your suit.

**2.4: Vision & Navigation:**
Your naked eyes are liars here.
*   **Visor:** A multi-layered, polarized, and thermally shielded visor is required. It must have selectable spectrum modes: Thermal (to see heat gradients and imminent vents), EM (to detect energy buildup), and Low-Light/Contrast enhancement (for general navigation).
*   **Reality Stabilizer Overlay:** The shimmering effect of the air is not just heat haze. It is a minor spatial distortion. Your HUD must incorporate a gyroscopic and inertial navigation system that plots your path in a 3D overlay, correcting for the visual jitter. Do not trust what you *see* a Reactor's position to be; trust what your inertial tracker *calculates* its position to be. GPS is nonexistent.

#### **Section 3: On Arrival - Surviving the First 60 Seconds**

1.  **Brace for Sound:** The moment of arrival is a physical blow. Your helmet's dampeners will engage violently. You will feel the sound in your skeleton, a deep, bone-shaking vibration that never stops. Do not panic. This is the new normal.
2.  **Ground Check:** Do not move. Test your footing. Gently shift your weight from one foot to the other. Feel how the Substrate gives. This is not solid ground. Learn its rhythm.
3.  **System Diagnostics:** Stand still and let your suit’s sensors acclimate. Monitor your rebreather, your temperature, and your suit integrity. Watch your navigation system lock onto your starting coordinates.
4.  **Establish a Baseline:** Look at the nearest Reactor. Note its state. Is it humming steadily (mature), glowing with new fissures (growing), or shuddering and venting (failing)? This is your first lesson in reading the landscape.

#### **Section 4: Navigating the Foundry - Rules of Movement**

*   **The 3-Kilometer Rule:** Never approach within 3km of a "Prime" Reactor (a mature, mountain-sized one). This is the minimum safe distance to survive a catastrophic detonation. For smaller, "Sprout" Reactors, a 500-meter berth is advised.
*   **Read the Signs of Failure:** A Reactor about to fail gives warnings. Listen for a change in its resonant frequency (your suit's audio analyzer will flag this). Watch for a sudden intensification in the glow of its fissures. Look for "weeping" of superheated fluids from cracks in the pipework. If you observe any of these, turn 90 degrees to the Reactor and move away at a steady, deliberate pace. Do not run. Running on the Substrate is how you fall.
*   **Shatterfall Zones:** When crystalline pipes shatter, the shards are razor-sharp but dissolve before hitting the Substrate. The danger is not the impact, but the aerosolized particles they release, which can overwhelm your suit's filters. If you see a shatterfall, check your suit’s atmospheric analyzer and move perpendicular to the prevailing vapor drift.
*   **Walk the Ochre Veins:** The dull, oxidized ochre veins in the Substrate are typically firmer and less slick than the blue-grey flesh. Use them as natural pathways where possible.

#### **Section 5: The Inhabitants - A Bestiary of Indifference**

These are not creatures you can reason with or frighten. They are functions of the ecosystem.

*   **The Janitors (Armored Beetle-Crawlers):** These are programmatic. They follow set paths to scrape waste from Reactor casings. They will not notice you unless you are directly in their path, at which point they will walk over you without slowing. Their mandibles can shear through your suit like paper. Give them a wide berth. Their clattering work is a useful audio cue for their location.
*   **The Soot-Gliders (Leathery Manta-Rays):** Essentially harmless. They are part of the weather system. However, passing through the chemical trail they leave behind can be corrosive. Your suit will alert you to this. Treat them as you would a toxic cloud.
*   **The Reclaimers (Amorphous Mercury Pools):** **THIS IS YOUR GREATEST BIOLOGICAL THREAT.** They are drawn to complex, non-native materials, especially metals and polymers, which they break down with powerful solvents. Your suit is a walking feast.
    *   They are found almost exclusively around Rector wreckage. **DO NOT SCAVENGE.** No piece of alien technology is worth attracting their attention.
    *   They are slow. If one begins to move towards you, simply walking away is sufficient.
    *   Do not stand still for extended periods (more than five minutes) on the open Substrate. Your suit's static energy field and minor emissions can eventually register as an inert "object" for reclamation. Stay mobile.

#### **Section 6: Psychological Warfare - Maintaining Your Sanity**

The Foundry's greatest weapon is existential dread.
*   **The Ghost of Syntax:** Your mind will try to find patterns in the noise. You will start to "hear" whispers, commands, fragments of music. This is an auditory pareidolia and the first step toward madness. Use your helmet's audio system to play a simple, repetitive sound (a metronome tick, a low hum) as a mental anchor. Focus on it. It will drown out the "ghosts."
*   **Abolish Purpose:** Do not ask "why." Do not wonder what the Reactors are making or what the purpose of this world is. There is no purpose you can comprehend. Attributing one to it is a psychological trap. It is a system. That is all. You are an observer of a process.
*   **Have a Mission:** Go to The Foundry with a clear, simple, achievable objective. "Collect a sample of the Substrate." "Record 10 minutes of a Reactor's thermal signature." "Triangulate the position of three Primes." Once your objective is complete, leave. This is not a tourist destination. Lingering invites disaster. The crushing sense of futility and insignificance can erode the will to survive faster than any chemical leak.

Your transit window is your lifeline. Your suit is your universe. Your mission is your sanity. Trust your gear, distrust your senses, and remember that in a world of pure, chaotic function, you are the only thing that has a purpose.

Now, check your gear one more time. Good luck, traveler. You will need it.
