### **Part I: Pre-Departure Preparations & Equipment Mandates**

This is not a world you can adapt to. This is a world you must insulate yourself from entirely. Your survival is measured in the integrity of your personal environmental suit and your psychological fortitude.

**1. The Environmental Suit: The "茧" (Cocoon) System**

You are not visiting Geode. You are bringing a tiny, sealed bubble of your home reality *into* Geode. Your suit is your universe.

*   **Atmosphere & Sealing:** The suit must be a fully autonomous, closed-loop life support system. The air on Geode is not merely unbreathable; it is a component of the machine. It is saturated with hyper-chilled moisture, metallic particulates, and ozone. A single lungful would flash-freeze your alveoli while simultaneously causing severe chemical burns and heavy metal poisoning. Your suit must be rated for hard vacuum and extreme temperatures, with redundant seals at every joint.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** This is your primary concern. The cold of Geode is an *active* process. The entire world is a heat sink. It will pull warmth from your suit with shocking, industrial efficiency.
    *   **Passive Insulation:** Standard aerogel/myomer layering is insufficient. It provides a buffer, but will eventually lose the thermal war.
    *   **Active Heating:** Your suit requires an integrated active heating system, circulating warm fluid through a capillary network. This system will be under constant, heavy load. Prioritize a robust and field-serviceable model.
    *   **Conduction Shielding:** The ground you walk on is the single greatest threat to your warmth. Your boots must have a multi-layered sole incorporating a vacuum gap and a thermal conduction breaker plate. We recommend boots with short, deployable, insulated pylons for any stationary observation lasting longer than five minutes, minimizing contact with the surface.
*   **Mobility & Traction:** The world is slick with freezing coolant. The ground itself is unstable.
    *   **Footwear:** Magnetic-soled boots are mandatory. They must be capable of generating a powerful enough electromagnetic field to anchor you to the basalt-obsidian plates, which have a high ferrous content. This is your only defense against a plate unexpectedly tilting or rotating. The system should be adjustable, allowing for a "walk" setting and a "lockdown" setting.
    *   **Tethering & Anchoring:** A standard kinetic-cord tether system with at least three self-drilling carbide anchors is required. Should you find a rare moment of stability, anchor yourself. The ground *will* move.
*   **Power:** Your suit's functions are power-intensive. A single power source is a death sentence. You must have a primary source (miniature fusion, quantum battery) and at least two independent backup sources. It is wise to keep one backup source completely powered down and isolated to protect it from the energy spikes caused by the feeding Shards.

**2. Sensory Management: Preserving Your Sanity**

Geode will attempt to unmake your mind through sensory overload long before it freezes your body.

*   **Audio System:** This is arguably the most critical piece of non-life-support gear.
    *   **Exterior Muting:** Your helmet must provide total audio isolation from the outside world. To hear the unfiltered sound of Geode is to invite a form of neurological trauma. The deep bass notes do not just enter the ear; they resonate with the skull and spine, and prolonged exposure can lead to synaptic shearing and permanent cognitive damage.
    *   **Filtered Input:** You will need to "hear" your environment to survive. Your suit should have a suite of exterior microphones that feed into a sophisticated audio processing unit. This unit must filter out the bone-shaking bass and the high-frequency friction whine, translating the critical sounds—the approach of a Dredger, the shift of a plate—into a manageable, symbolic soundscape inside your helmet. Do not, under any circumstances, listen to the raw feed.
*   **Visual System:** The dim, perpetual twilight is punctuated by flashes of tearing spacetime.
    *   **Low-Light & Thermal:** Your visor needs to integrate low-light amplification, thermal imaging, and standard visual input. You will need to switch between these modes constantly to interpret your surroundings. The "fauna" register poorly on thermal, but are clear in low-light. The heat radiating from the Fan Fins can blind your thermal view.
    *   **Flash Suppression:** An auto-darkening visor with a reaction time in the nanoseconds is mandatory. The shriek of a Shard feeding is a visual event as much as an auditory one. Looking directly at it without protection can cause permanent retinal damage and induce seizures.

**3. Sustenance & Supplies**

You will find nothing to eat or drink here.

*   **Nutrition:** All food must be brought with you in the form of high-energy, nutrient-dense paste or bars. The constant stress and cold will burn calories at an alarming rate. Plan for at least 1.5 times your standard caloric need.
*   **Hydration:** Your suit's water recycling system is your only source of drinkable water. Do not, for any reason, attempt to collect or purify the water on Geode. It is not H₂O in the simple sense; it is a complex, mineral-rich hydraulic fluid. Ingesting it would be like drinking a cocktail of liquid mercury, antifreeze, and sand.

### **Part II: On-World Protocol & Hazard Mitigation**

Your primary goal is to be an uninteresting ghost. You are an error, a smudge of warm, soft biology in a world of cold, hard function. Act like it.

**1. The Prime Directive: Irrelevance**

The Skitterers, Dredgers, and other mobile components are not "wildlife." They are parts of a system. They operate on a logic of function and process. As long as you do not interfere with that process, you are an anomaly they are not programmed to address.

*   **Avoid the Swarms:** Do not impede the path of the Skitterers. They will likely flow around you as if you were a rock. However, do not let them "clean" you. Their purpose is to scour and lubricate. Their abrasive bodies and slurry-spraying appendages will strip your suit's outer layers and compromise its integrity.
*   **Respect the Dredgers:** Give Dredgers the widest possible berth. Think of them as glaciers, not bulldozers. Their path is set, their movement inexorable. Do not assume you can dodge them. They are so large and their movement so fundamental to the geology that the plate you are standing on may be the very thing they are about to consume. If a Dredger reconfigures into its defensive mode, you are in the middle of a conflict. Your only course of action is to lock your boots down, minimize your energy signature, and pray the kinetic blasts are not aimed in your general direction.
*   **The Shards are the True Predators:** These are the only entities that are not part of the system's core function. They are parasites, and their behavior is consequently less predictable. They hunt energy potential. While their primary targets are the Processing Basins, a traveller in a high-power suit is a novel, bright spark in the dim landscape.
    *   When you detect a Shard (a flicker of silent, impossible movement), immediately cut all non-essential power to your suit. Go into "cold mode." Hunker down and appear as inert as possible.
    *   They seem to be drawn to the act of *transmutation*. Do not use personal teleporters, energy weapons, or any reality-bending technology on the surface. That is like ringing a dinner bell.

**2. Navigation & Movement**

The world is a moving map. Constancy is an illusion.

*   ** Landmarks:** The Fan Fins and the Processing Basins are your only reliable landmarks. The Fins are relatively stable, radiating heat. The Basins are where the world's purpose is enacted.
*   **The Ground:** Walk on the center of the great plates. Their edges are the most active zones, constantly being ground down, re-formed, or tilted to release coolant. Watch for the quartz conduits just beneath the surface; their internal glow can signal an imminent surge of hydraulic pressure that can cause micro-fractures in the plate above.
*   **NO-GO ZONES: The Processing Basins:** It cannot be stressed enough: **DO NOT APPROACH THE PROCESSING BASINS.** The laws of physics are suggestions there, not rules. The pillars of solidified sound and vortices of time-dilated space are not mere curiosities. Exposure can result in ontological contamination—where your own physical and temporal integrity begins to fray. We have records of travelers returning from brief exposure who experienced memories of events that never happened, or whose bodies began to phase between different stages of their own lifespan. The basins are where the world digests reality. You are not on the menu.

**3. The Exit Strategy**

Your visit should be brief. You are a battery running down in an infinitely cold room.

*   **Set a Hard Time Limit:** Before you even set foot on Geode, determine your maximum mission duration based on your power consumption and psychological tolerance. Stick to it. Every extra minute increases your risk exponentially.
*   **Emergency Extraction:** Have a clear, pre-planned extraction point and a protocol for immediate retrieval. Your on-world actions should always maintain a clear path back to this point.
*   **Decontamination:** Upon your return, you and your suit must undergo a rigorous Class-5 decontamination protocol. This is not just for metallic dust. It is for potential info-hazards, temporal echoes, and microscopic fragments of the self-replicating machinery. We do not know what a single Skitterer “cell” could do if introduced to a biosphere. We cannot afford to find out.

To visit Geode is to stand inside the workings of a god's watch, a watch that has been ticking for longer than your species has had words for "time" or "fear." You are not important here. The machine will continue its grinding, self-absorbed purpose long after your footsteps have been erased by the next sweep of skittering cleaners. Be humble. Be quiet. Be invisible. And get out before the world notices you are there.
