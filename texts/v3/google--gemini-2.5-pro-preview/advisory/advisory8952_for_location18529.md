### **Pre-Departure Briefing: Site Designation "WOMB-01" (The Geode)**

**Traveler Advisory Level:** **EXTREME (Existential Risk)**

**Location Profile:** Class-V Metaphysical Industrial Site. Closed, self-contained, and operating under non-standard physical and causal laws.

**Primary Hazards:** Absolute Desiccation, Extreme Hyperthermia, EM Saturation, Ontological Instability, Sensory Deprivation/Overload Paradox, Cognitive Hazard.

**Mission Parameters:** Observation only. Interaction is forbidden. Survival is not guaranteed.

### **1.0 Pre-Translocation Mental & Physical Conditioning**

This is perhaps the most critical phase of your preparation. You will not be traveling *to* The Geode; you will simply *be* there. There is no journey, no approach, no time to acclimate. One moment you will be in the transfer chamber, the next you will be standing on the floor of a cosmic engine.

**1.1 Mental Anchoring:**
Two weeks prior to your scheduled translocation, you must begin a rigorous regimen of mental anchoring. This involves focusing on a core, unchangeable memory—one rich with sensory detail (smell of rain, taste of salt, feeling of cool fabric). Practice recalling this memory until you can invoke it in its entirety within a single second. This will be your "reality-tether" should you become disoriented. The Geode will attempt to overwrite your senses with its own stark reality; you must have a competing reality ready to hand. Do not choose a traumatic or overly complex memory; simplicity and profound personal meaning are key.

**1.2 Physical Desiccation Training:**
You will need to understand what true dryness feels like to avoid panicking when you experience it. Spend increasing amounts of time in a low-humidity, high-temperature sauna. While this is a pale imitation, it is crucial to train your "water-panic" reflex down. Learn to ignore the sensation of your skin tightening and your throat feeling like sandpaper. Your suit will handle the physical reality, but your mind must be prepared for the phantom sensation.

**1.3 Hydration Loading:**
For 72 hours prior to departure, you must engage in hyper-hydration protocols. Your body will need to be a fortress of moisture, as your suit's recycling system will be under immediate and immense strain. Consume electrolyte-rich fluids and cease all diuretic intake (caffeine, alcohol).

### **2.0 Essential Gear: The Environmental Survival Ensemble (ESE)**

Standard exploration gear is not only inadequate; it is a suicide vector. You will require a specialized ESE tailored to WOMB-01's unique threats.

**2.1 The Body Suit & Closed-Loop Life Support:**
*   **Atmosphere:** You will be on a fully closed-loop rebreather system. The air in The Geode is not merely hot and dry; it is aggressively hygroscopic. It will actively pull moisture from any surface. A standard rebreather would fail as its chemical scrubbers overheat and desiccate. Your system must feature an integrated meta-material condenser that can reclaim 100% of moisture from your exhalations, sweat, and urine. There is no margin for error. **A single drop of lost water is a catastrophic failure.**
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The suit cannot use evaporative cooling. It must feature a network of micro-capillaries filled with a phase-change fluid. This fluid will absorb the ambient heat, circulate to a solid-state thermoelectric radiator on your back, and vent this energy as tightly-focused infrared radiation *upwards*, towards the glowing ceiling. Be mindful of where you aim your heat vent.
*   **Outer Layer:** The suit's outer layer should be a polished, hyper-reflective silver or white to reflect the maximum amount of radiant heat and light. It must be non-conductive to insulate you from the immense electrical potential of the floor.

**2.2 Visor & Sensory Interface:**
The light in The Geode is your second-greatest enemy after the heat. It is a psychological weapon.
*   **Auto-Shading is Useless:** Standard auto-darkening lenses will be permanently black. Your visor must use a different technology: **active optical mitigation.** It will need to sample the ambient light and project a "negative" image onto the inside of the visor, effectively creating a world of normal brightness and contrast for you.
*   **The Shadow Projector:** The lack of shadows will quickly lead to vertigo, nausea, and a complete loss of depth perception. Your helmet must be equipped with a low-power laser system that projects a faint, artificial shadow of yourself and nearby objects onto your visual field. This is not for illumination; it is a neurological hack to keep you sane and upright.
*   **Causality Filter:** The areas near the mica-ferns will distort spacetime. Your visor must have an inertial sensor grid that can detect these micro-distortions. When a significant warp is detected, the affected portion of your vision will be highlighted in red. This is your signal to **STOP AND RE-ROUTE. DO NOT WALK THROUGH A DISTORTION FIELD.** See section 3.2.

**2.3 Footwear:**
The floor is a slow, powerful piston. You will need boots with integrated gyroscopic stabilizers and multi-layered vibration-dampening soles. The outer sole should be of a high-friction, non-conductive polymer to prevent you from slipping during a "heave" event and to insulate you from the energy flowing beneath your feet. Do not think of it as a floor; think of it as the skin of a sleeping giant. Walk accordingly.

**2.4 Tools & Provisions:**
*   **No Liquids:** Do not bring any liquid provisions outside of your closed-loop system. A ruptured water bottle would flash-boil, then sublimate, in an instant.
*   **No Standard Electronics:** The electromagnetic field from the floor and capacitors will fry any unshielded electronics. Essential tools (chronometer, data-logger, audio-recorder) must be of hardened, military-grade quality or, preferably, mechanical. A spring-wound watch is more reliable here than a smartwatch.
*   **The Stick:** Bring a simple, non-conductive rod, perhaps made of fiberglass or treated wood, about five feet long. Use it to probe the ground ahead of you. Not for traps, but for feeling the subtle vibrations and temperatures that indicate proximity to a capacitor bulb or the edge of a floor plate. It will be your third leg and your primary sensory organ.

### **3.0 On-Site Protocols & Hazard Mitigation**

**3.1 Movement and Navigation:**
Walk slowly. Place your feet deliberately. The floor's hum is your baseline; listen for any change in its pitch or intensity. Navigate by triangulation between the great obsidian bulbs and the more distant walls. Remember that distances are deceptive due to the sheer scale and the atmospheric shimmer. Trust your suit's rangefinder, not your eyes.

**3.2 The Flora: A Hierarchy of Avoidance**
Treat every piece of "vegetation" as an active industrial machine with no safety guards.

*   **The Capacitor-Bulbs (Obsidian Teardrops):** These are your primary landmarks. They radiate intense heat and a high-frequency whine. The air around them shimmers violently. Your suit will register a sharp temperature spike if you get too close. **Minimum safe distance is 50 meters.** Do not cross this threshold. Do not touch the braided copper cables.
*   **The Mica-Ferns (Heat Sinks):** This is the **single greatest threat** in The Geode. The spatial distortions are not optical illusions; they are localized breakdowns in causality. Walking through one could result in temporal displacement, dimensional shearing, or instantaneous ontological erasure. If your visor's causality filter flags a distortion field, find another route. If you are surrounded, stand perfectly still and wait for the fern's impossibly slow "breathing" cycle to shift the field. Patience is survival. **NEVER look directly at the world *through* a fern's fronds.** The perspective shift can induce irreversible psychosis.
*   **The Vines & Their Threads:** The vines are the assembly lines. Their movement is too slow to pose a physical threat. Their product, however, is a different matter. The black threads are filaments of stable vacuum. Do not theorize about what touching one would do. The consensus among those who have studied the data is simple: catastrophic, localized annihilation. The thread would not break; your matter would simply cease to be where it made contact. Give the hanging threads the widest possible berth. Do not walk under them.

**3.3 Sensory Management:**
The soundscape is a constant, three-part drone. It will invade your mind. Your helmet's audio system should be set to a counter-frequency to dampen the worst of it, but do not block it out entirely. It is a vital source of information about the environment's state.

Do not attempt to understand the *purpose* of the factory. To contemplate the "why" of manufacturing threads of nothingness is a cognitive hazard. It is a question that has consumed and broken other travelers. You are here to observe the *how*. Document the process, record the readings, and leave the philosophy for a safer dimension.

### **4.0 Exfiltration**

You will not choose the moment of your departure. Translocation is centrally controlled and timed to the nanosecond. Your only job is to be in a "safe" position when the recall signal is triggered. A safe position is defined as:
*   At least 100 meters from any Mica-Fern.
*   At least 50 meters from any Capacitor-Bulb.
*   Not directly underneath a hanging thread.

Ten seconds before exfiltration, a blue indicator will light up on your HUD. Cease all movement. Invoke your mental anchor. You will feel a sensation not of travel, but of the world being pulled away from you, leaving you behind.

Then, you will be back. The debriefing and mandatory psychological evaluation will begin immediately. Do not be alarmed if you feel cold for weeks afterwards, or if you instinctively look for shadows that aren't there. This is normal. You have been to a place that makes gods of heat and light, and you have returned. That is more than most can say.
