This is a Traveler's Primer for what our cartographers have designated "The Great Mechanism," or, more colloquially, "Sub-Strate." If you have received a ticket to this destination, understand that you are not going as a tourist. You are going as a witness, an observer, and a potential contaminant. Your preparation will determine whether you are the former or the latter.

Read this document three times. Once now, once before you pack, and one last time before you step through the gate.

### **Part 1: Conditioning the Self - Psychological and Sensory Preparation**

The first and most critical hurdle is not physical; it is perceptual. Sub-Strate will attempt to unmake your sanity through sensory overload. You must prepare your mind before you prepare your gear.

**On The Sound:** You have read the description, but you do not understand. No recording can prepare you. The sound is not something you *hear*; it is a condition of the environment you *inhabit*. It will be inside you, a constant, physical vibration in your bones, your teeth, your organs.

*   **Actionable Prep:** For two weeks prior to your departure, you must acclimate. Find a high-quality sound system and a recording of deep, industrial ambient noise layered with sub-bass drones and high-frequency mechanical shrieks. Play it constantly. Start with it in the background, then slowly increase the volume day by day. Sleep with it on. Eat with it on. Your goal is not to learn to ignore it, but to learn to *think through it*. You must find the quiet center of the hurricane in your own mind. It is a form of intensive meditation.
*   **Crucial Warning:** Under no circumstances should you pack active noise-canceling technology. The deafening baseline sound is your shield. It is the specific, localized noises—a high-pitched tensile whine before a cable snaps, the guttural grinding of a plate about to shift, the chittering of a maintenance drone—that will be your only warnings. Noise-cancellation is suicide. You need to learn to parse the symphony of destruction, not mute it.

**On The Sight and Air:** You will be in a state of perpetual, humid twilight. Your eyes will adjust, but your sense of space will be perpetually challenged by the lack of a horizon and the inverted geometry of the sky-ceiling.

*   **Actionable Prep:** Spend time in large, enclosed industrial spaces if you can: power plants, cavernous warehouses, the under-levels of major transit hubs. Get used to the scale and the feeling of being enclosed. Practice spatial awareness drills in environments with poor lighting and confusing layouts.
*   **The Smell and Taste:** The air is an aerosolized cocktail of ozone, super-heated lubricant, and vaporized bio-mechanical particulate. It will coat your tongue and the back of your throat. It will feel like you're breathing in soup. Do not get used to it.

### **Part 2: Essential Gear - The Traveler's Shell**

Your body is a soft, fragile, water-based inconvenience in Sub-Strate. Your gear is your exoskeleton. Do not skimp.

**1. Respiration and Filtration:**
*   **Primary:** A full-face, sealed rebreather system. Not a simple mask. It must have its own oxygen supply and a multi-stage chemical filter capable of scrubbing complex volatile organic compounds, metallic particulates, and microbiological agents. The "protein-like" element in the air is likely composed of nano-constructs or other informational matter; you do not want this in your lungs. The rebreather also serves to keep the greasy dew off your face.
*   **Secondary:** At least four spare filters and two spare oxygen canisters. Assume a 50% higher consumption rate due to stress.

**2. The Bodysuit and Outer Layers:**
*   **Base Layer:** A moisture-wicking cooling suit, the kind worn by race car drivers. You will be sweating constantly in the hot, humid air. Managing your body temperature and preventing skin breakdown is paramount.
*   **Outer Shell:** A non-reactive, slick-surface jumpsuit. Think advanced polymer or Teflon-coated fiber. The environment is "greasy." The suit's primary job is to prevent the ubiquitous dew from adhering to you. The dew is not just water; it is a medium for transport within the machine. Letting it soak into your clothing would be like marinating yourself. All seams must be factory-sealed. Integrated gloves and boots are recommended. Color should be a bright, unnatural hue (e.g., emergency orange, cyan) to distinguish you from the environment.

**3. Footwear:**
*   **The Sole is Key:** This is the most important piece of gear. You need boots with thick, but highly sensitive, flexible soles. You will not be walking; you will be "reading" the ground with your feet. The soles should be wide to distribute your weight. Cleats or deep treads are a liability; they can get caught in the seams of the hexagonal plates. You want something more like a hybrid of a rock-climbing shoe and a high-tech deck shoe.
*   **Proprioceptive Feedback:** Some advanced models come with haptic feedback systems that vibrate in concert with the ground, amplifying the tectonic shifting and helping you find the "rhythm" of the plates faster. If you can afford it, get it.

**4. Sensory and Utility:**
*   **Aural Modulators:** Built into your helmet or as separate earpieces worn under your rebreather hood. These are NOT noise-cancelers. They are advanced audio filters that dampen the overwhelming baseline drone to a manageable level while isolating and amplifying anomalous frequencies—the specific shrieks, clicks, and groans that signal immediate, localized events. Think of it as turning down the bass on a cosmic stereo to hear the treble more clearly.
*   **Harmonic Probe:** A simple-looking metallic rod, about a meter long. It is, in essence, a sophisticated tuning fork. Before leaning on a wall, touching a surface, or stepping onto a new platform, touch it with the probe. The feedback—the resonance, the vibration, the immediate damping—will tell you more about its solidity and imminent state change than your eyes ever could. A "dead" surface is stable for now. A surface that "sings" or rings is in a state of flux. Do not trust it.
*   **Lighting:** Your helmet should have a powerful, variable-spectrum headlamp. You will need to switch between visible light, ultraviolet, and infrared to analyze the nature of the "waters" and identify energy deposits the local fauna feed on.
*   **Sustenance:** All food and water must be brought with you in sealed, durable containers. Nutrient pastes and hydration packs are ideal. There is zero consumable biomass or potable water in Sub-Strate. Attempting to drink the "water" will lead to rapid dissolution and integration into the system. Leave no trace; pack out everything you pack in. Leaving behind a foreign biological sample (even waste) could trigger a catastrophic "immune response" from the system.

### **Part 3: On-Site Protocol - The Rules of Motion**

Survival in Sub-Strate is a dance. Learn the steps or be crushed.

1.  **The Sub-Strate Shuffle:** Never stand still. Never lock your knees. Maintain a constant, swaying motion, shifting your weight from foot to foot. Keep your feet wide apart. This constant movement allows you to feel the pre-tremors of the ground plates and adjust your footing before a shift throws you. You are a sailor on a tectonic sea.
2.  **Respect the Walls:** A wall is not a static object. It is a temporary state of matter. Watch for the iridescent stress-fractures. If you see them shimmering or shifting, the wall is preparing to flow. Do not be near it. The harmonic probe is your best friend here.
3.  **Water is Not Water:** The fluids of this world are its blood, its lymph, its data streams. Do not touch them. The clear, weeping drips might be coolant. The viscous slurry in the canals might be nutrient for the machine. The steaming pools are for heat exchange. Falling in is not drowning; it is becoming raw material.
4.  **The Fauna are Process, Not Life:** The metallic crabs, the gelatinous blobs, the soaring aeroslicks—they are not animals. They are autonomous maintenance units. They are not interested in you. They have jobs. Do not get in the way of their work. A plasma-torch scuttler will carve a line through a plate to access a conduit, and it will not care if your leg is on that line. An absorptive blob will flow over a surface to clean it, and it will not care if you are standing there. Give them a wide berth. They are predictable in their unpredictability; their paths are dictated by the needs of the machine, not by malice or hunger. Observe their function, predict their path, and stay out of it.
5.  **Embrace the Rhythm:** This is the most abstract but most important rule. Stop trying to impose your own will on the world. Instead, feel its pulse. Feel the deep, slow rhythm of the ground plates, the faster cycle of the arms reconfiguring structures, the frenetic scuttling of the "fauna." Your survival depends on your ability to subconsciously sync with this rhythm, to move with the chaos instead of against it. You will know you are succeeding when you start to react to events a half-second before they happen. This is not precognition; it is deep listening.

You are entering not a place, but a system. An organism. Your goal is to be an unnoticed, harmless bacterium passing through its gut. Do not try to understand *why* it builds. Do not seek its purpose or its creator. Your purpose is singular: to observe, to endure, and to leave, taking nothing but impressions and leaving nothing but footprints that the shifting ground will erase in moments.

Good luck, Traveler. Try to find the beauty in the terrible, magnificent beat.
