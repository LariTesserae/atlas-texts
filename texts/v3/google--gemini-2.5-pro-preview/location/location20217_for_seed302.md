This is a landscape of pure state. There is no becoming, only being.

The first and most total sensation is not of sight, but of sound and pressure. The sound is an indivisible, monolithic chord, a deafening E-flat that has no attack and no decay. It is simply present, a vibrational force that pushes against the bones of the skull and makes the fluid in the inner ear feel thick and heavy. It's a sound so absolute and unchanging that after a time the brain attempts to subtract it, failing, and leaving you in a state of ringing disorientation, as if you've just been struck. The air itself is a resonant body, and the chord is its natural frequency, perpetually excited.

When the eyes adjust to the second assault—the light—they find no relief. The brightness is total, a wall of sourceless, shadowless white. It is not the pinpoint glare of a sun, but a luminous, saturated fog. The air, cool and heavy with moisture, is itself the light source. Every droplet, every wisp of vapor, is a microscopic lantern, scattering a brilliant, featureless glare in all directions. There is no sky to be seen, no sun, no point of reference; there is only the immediate, blinding sphere of your own perception.

Through this sensory overload, the world gradually resolves. You are standing on a high, sprawling plateau that extends into the white distance. The ground is not soil or rock in a familiar sense. It is a dense, seemingly infinite forest of vertical crystalline structures. They are not sharp or faceted like quartz, but smooth, milky, and rounded, like immense, elongated river stones stood on end. They are packed together with barely an arm’s-span between them, ranging in height from a few feet to thirty or forty. Their surfaces are slick, constantly weeping a clear, cold fluid that is not quite water. It is thicker, with a faint metallic scent, and it is this fluid condensing from the luminous air that coats everything in a perpetual clammy wetness.

The ground between these pillars is a saturated mire of crumbled crystal and the same viscous fluid, forming still, silent pools that perfectly mirror the glaring whiteness above. There are no ripples. The fluid drips from the pillars with an
impossibly slow, rhythmic regularity, yet the drips make no sound, swallowed instantly by the colossal drone that fills the world. Each drip lands with a soft, silent bloom of concentric rings that fade back into stillness, the only motion in a landscape held in perfect equilibrium.

This is a world without biology, yet it feels intensely occupied. The density of the pillars gives the impression of a crowd, of a space so full there is no room for anything else. They are the world. Their composition is some form of silicate, but one that is piezo-luminescent and piezo-sonic. It is the subtle, immense pressure of the plateau's own weight, a tectonic equilibrium achieved over eons, that causes them to resonate. They are not ringing because something struck them; they are ringing because their very existence is a form of tension and release, held in a perfect, unending moment. The light is the friction of their being. The sound is their eternal voice.

Life here is almost geological. There are no plants, no fungi. But rarely, on the slick surfaces of the wider pillars or gliding with immense slowness across the mirror-like pools, one might see a fauna. It is a creature of silica and fluid, a semi-transparent disc several feet in diameter. It has no discernible organs, no eyes, no mouth. It moves by a fractional, peristaltic ripple of its own body, leaving no wake on the water's surface. These beings, these 'Glass Skaters', seem to feed on the resonance itself, or perhaps on some mineral precipitate that the constant vibration and condensation leaches from the pillars. They are utterly silent, eyeless navigators in a world of blinding light and deafening sound, their existence a quiet harmony within the greater, overwhelming one. They seem as ancient and as unchanging as the singing pillars they inhabit.

To travel here is not to walk, but to weave. You must turn sideways to slip through the dense-packed, weeping columns. The constant chord vibrates up through your feet from the ground, a different texture of the same sound that batters your eardrums. Looking up is pointless; the pillars vanish into the luminous white. Looking out, you see only more of the same, a valley floor and its opposing slopes packed solid with the same vertical forms, their collective drone layering into an oceanic presence that transcends sound and becomes a simple fact of reality, like gravity. It is a world that has found its single, perfect note and has been singing it, unchanging, for a time beyond measure. There is no weather, no wind, no day or night. There is only the cool, wet, bright, and singing stasis.
