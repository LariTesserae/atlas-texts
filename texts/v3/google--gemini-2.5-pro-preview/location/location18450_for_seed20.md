The sound is not a sound. There is no ear to hear it, no air to carry it. It is a condition of existence, a grinding absolute that permeates every particle of being. It is the first and last thing. You feel it as a vibration in the marrow of your bones, a pressure behind your eyes that threatens to turn your skull inside out. It is a roar so complete and so total that it achieves a kind of silence, the way a universe of pure white light would be indistinguishable from blindness. All other senses are secondary, distorted interpretations of this one crushing fact: the Sound.

You are submerged, but not in water. The medium is thicker, warmer, like pressurized oil or amniotic fluid at a slow boil. It flows, not with the gentle push of a current, but in vast, turbulent shears of force that move with the speed and violence of a continental plate subducting. To be caught in one of these shears is not to be swept away, but to be dimensionally stretched, unwritten. The medium is the engine of the Sound, and the Sound is the frantic, screaming texture of the medium.

This place is a peak, a high lonely place thrust up into the violent sky-sea. It is a single spire of black, vitreous rock, so new its edges are still fractures, sharp enough to slice the thick medium that scours it. The scale is that of a cathedral, but a cathedral that is only a steeple. Below is a chasm of unimaginable depth, a well of greater pressure and louder Sound. Above, the storm-fluid fades into a bruised, violet dimness, where the light is weakest.

Light is not shed from a sun. It is born from stress. When the titanic currents of the medium grind against each other, or tear across the face of this rock spire, they spark with faint, cold piezo-luminescence. Veins of ghostly blue and lilac light flicker and die within the fluid, illuminating nothing for more than a moment. The world is a perpetual lightning storm with no thunder, because the thunder is already everywhere, always.

There is no soil. The ground underfoot is a treacherous mosaic of newly-cooled basalt and shattered obsidian plates that chime and shift with every tremor. What passes for flora are the Resonance Corals. They are not alive. They are a physical phenomenon, a form of crystallization driven by the sonic violence. In sparse, scattered clusters, delicate, glass-like structures grow from the rock. They are spires and fans and fractal blossoms of frozen sound, each one vibrating at a specific harmonic of the universal roar. They glow faintly with a captured, constant light, humming a pure, impossibly high tone that is almost lost in the deafening chaos. Some are small, like handfuls of spun glass. Others rise ten feet high, intricate latticeworks that seem to sing their own private, perfect note into the maelstrom. These are the scattered, faint marks of building—not by hands, but by physics itself.

The rules here are simple. Solidity is temporary. The Sound is a form of erosion that works from the inside out. It seeks resonant frequencies in the rock, and when it finds one, a boulder or a ledge will not break or crumble, but simply dissolve into a cloud of vibrating dust, its structure unravelled by the overwhelming vibration. The mountain is literally screaming itself apart. This is a young peak, but it will not grow old.

Change is constant and violent. Without warning, the pitch of the Sound will shift, a cataclysmic drop of an octave that sends a physical shockwave through the medium. The Resonance Corals will visibly shudder, some shattering into dust, others flashing brightly as their internal structure is retuned. A current can reverse, a pocket of vacuum can open and implode. Reality here is a frayed edge. Time itself seems to ripple, to stutter. A glance away and back again might reveal a new glass-coral spire where there was none, or a familiar ledge simply gone, its absence a quieter note in the unending symphony of destruction.

And in the flickering, pressure-cooked chaos, something moves. It is vanishingly rare, a flicker of motion so contrary to the churning violence that the mind struggles to accept it. It is not swimming, but gliding. A creature, perhaps, or a fold in the medium that has achieved a semblance of life. Long and ribbon-like, it has no discernible features, just a body of deeper, more absolute darkness against the dimness. It rides the shear-lines, the most violent parts of the flow, drawing energy from the chaos. It moves with an impossible grace, a sliver of intention in a world of pure reaction. It does not fight the sound; it is part of it. It dances in the apocalypse. To see it is a terrifying privilege, a glimpse of a being for whom this hell is a placid sea. It is the only fauna, the ghost in the machine-scream, and then it is gone, folded back into the noise and the dark.
