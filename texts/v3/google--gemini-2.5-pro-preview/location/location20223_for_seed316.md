This place has a single, fundamental law: the Hum.

It is not a sound in the way a rockfall or a scream is a sound. It is the condition of the air itself, a standing wave of such impossible energy and density that the atmosphere is a physical weight. It is a deafening, sub-audible, and screamingly ultrasonic roar that occupies every possible frequency at once. The pressure varies, not like wind, but like a tide. You feel it in your bones, a vibration that threatens to un-knit the molecules of you. It is the sound of a universe being continuously, violently born and un-born in the same space. This is the source of the turbulence.

The ground underfoot is, paradoxically, the most stable thing in existence. It is a vast, near-perfectly flat plain of black, vitreous material, erosion's final masterpiece. Over uncounted eons, every mountain has been ground to dust, every ocean has boiled away and been reabsorbed, every tectonic plate has locked into a final, quiescent configuration. The land is dead. It is a planetary sheet of obsidian-like glass, polished by ages of reality-scouring winds. And it is wet.

The water does not fall as rain or flow in rivers. The sheer pressure and energy of the Hum forces it out of the air and the ground. The atmosphere is saturated past any earthly limit, a warm, thick soup that clings to everything. The black plain weeps, constantly extruding beads of viscous, heavy water that crawl across its surface in shimmering, amoebic patterns before being re-atomized by another pressure wave. Looking down, you see your reflection not in a puddle, but in a million sweating, shifting lenses on the surface of the world. The light is a dim, coppery twilight, filtered through this dense, humming medium, the sun or stars a forgotten rumor.

Vegetation is a misnomer. Nothing *grows* here. Instead, things *occur*. Where a pocket of reality momentarily stabilizes, where the chaotic frequencies of the Hum briefly cancel each other out into a pocket of profound silence, a structure precipitates. These are the Stillness Spires. They are sparse, scattered across the vastness like lonely sentinels. They look like splinters of smoky quartz, some small as a finger, others the size of a redwood, all shot through with internal fractures that catch the dim light. They are not alive. They are axioms of physics that have momentarily achieved physical form. They hum with a pure, single note in defiance of the cacophony, and it is this resonance that holds them together. When the surrounding turbulence becomes too great, they do not fall; they dissolve back into the noise, their single note reabsorbed by the all-consuming roar.

The entire landscape is in a state of constant, violent flux. Not of matter, but of principle. Space itself shimmers, buckles, and folds. You might see a distant Spire duplicate, its twin shimmering beside it for a moment before one or both evaporate. The horizon will suddenly flatten into a hard line, as if perspective itself has been temporarily abolished, then rush forward in a nauseating wave of parallax. This is the visual component of the Hum. You are inside the engine of creation, and it is sputtering. Colors smear on the periphery of your vision. Solidity is a suggestion, not a rule. Gravity will pulse, making you feel twice as heavy, then light as a phantom.

In this chaos, there are scattered artifacts that defy the ephemeral nature of the Spires. They are not built, but *scarred* into existence. Where an unusually coherent wave of reality—a flicker of causality, a brief, sustained thought of physical law—has swept across the plain, it leaves behind a structure, like lightning vitrifying sand. Locals, if there were any, might call them Echo Bastions. These are low, melted-looking buildings of the same black glass as the plain, but fused into impossible, non-Euclidean shapes that hurt the mind to look at. They have corridors that turn back on themselves and chambers that are larger on the inside than the outside. They are not shelters. Stepping inside one is to step inside a fossilized law of physics. The Hum is different in here—not gone, but distorted, its chaotic roar channeled into a complex, maddening geometry of sound.

Fauna is almost non-existent. The only thing that can survive here is something that does not resist the chaos, but is made of it. Rarely, a flicker of movement can be seen skimming the weeping surface of the plain. It is a Phase Skimmer, a creature less of biology and more of coherent energy. It looks like a manta ray woven from liquid mercury and television static, perhaps thirty feet across. It has no discernible features, no organs, no front or back. It surfs the waves of reality, feeding on the differential between one state of being and the next. It banks on a curve in spacetime, accelerates through a pocket of lapsed inertia, and dives into a momentary fissure in the world. It is the only hunter and the only prey. Its passage leaves a temporary wake of true silence, a shocking and total absence of the Hum that is more terrifying than the noise itself.

To be here is to exist as a temporary anomaly. The rules are simple: there are no rules. The world is an ancient, polished drumhead, and the Hum is a storm of cosmic fists beating upon it, and you are just a speck of dust, waiting to see which vibration will be the one to shake you apart.
