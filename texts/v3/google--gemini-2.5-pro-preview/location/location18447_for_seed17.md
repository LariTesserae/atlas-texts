This world is the Sound.

Before sight, before touch, before the cold seeps into you, there is the Sound. It is not merely a noise that occupies the medium; it is the medium. It’s a deafening, all-encompassing, monolithic hum that has the texture of grinding continents and the resonant purity of a bell the size of a moon. It is a physical pressure on the body, a constant and unwavering vibration that trembles in the bones and blurs the edges of thought. To be here is to be an instrument played by the world, a chamber for its single, infinitely complex chord. The Sound is the first and last law.

Everything is submerged. Not in water as it is commonly known, but in a liquid far denser, colder, and under unimaginable pressure. At this elevation, in the high, thin atmosphere of a gas giant, this ocean is a suspended sphere, a planet within a planet, held together by its own gravity and the crushing forces of the gases outside it. To call it an "ocean" is to miss the point; it is the entirety of the world, a globe of near-frozen, heavy liquid. The pressure makes the fluid feel thick, like moving through cold oil. There is no surface, no shore, no sky—only the Deep.

Through the thick, clear medium, immense structures are visible, but only as faint presences in a vast emptiness. This is a place defined by its scale and its vacancy. Between points of interest lie voids of staggering size, abyssal plains of nothing but the Sound and the cold. Then, a mountain range will rise from the unseen floor. These are not the worn, rounded mountains of old worlds. They are young, sharp, and cruel. Their peaks are jagged spires of black, crystalline rock, like shards of obsidian freshly knapped from a larger whole. They rise for miles, their faces sheer and unworn, without a trace of silt or the rounding touch of time. This is a world of perpetual, sharp-featured youth.

Scattered across these high, black peaks are the sources of the light and the Sound: the Engines. They are vast, industrial constructions, defying any simple architectural description. Part-foundry, part-cathedral, they are titanic assemblies of pylons, conduits, and humming, glowing cores, anchored deep into the rock of the mountaintops. They are not numerous; one might be visible every hundred miles, a lonely star in the crushing dark. From their hearts, they pour forth a steady, unwavering, normal-spectrum light, casting hard-edged shadows that stretch for miles across the abyssal plains. This is an industrial light, stark and unblinking, without dawn or dusk.

The Engines are also the source of the Sound. This deafening roar is the noise of their operation, a process of planetary scale and perfect, unchanging stasis. They seem to be processing the very stuff of the world, drawing in the dense liquid and the mineral-rich rock, and in a process of perfect, waste-free alchemy, perpetuating their own existence. This is not a dynamic system; it is a frozen equilibrium. The Engines have been running in this exact state since their creation, their output never wavering, their machinery never degrading. They are a single act of industrial creation, held for eternity. The "young" mountains are not eroding because the Engines' process might also be one of creation, laying down new crystalline rock as quickly as any force could wear it away. It's a system of perpetual genesis without decay.

There is no plant life. No photosynthesis is possible in this abyssal light. The rock is bare, sharp, and sterile. But the world is not dead; it is teeming with fauna. Life here has evolved in response to the Engines. The food chain begins not with sunlight, but with the Engines' effluent—a fine, shimmering particulate, an energetic chaff that precipitates into the water in their immediate vicinity.

Vast shoals of creatures, geometric and armored, swarm near the light of the Engines. They are not fish, but something else. Many are crystalline, their bodies faceted like the mountains, refracting the industrial light into dazzling patterns. They move in silent, precise formations, their orbits dictated by the currents of the Sound. They graze on the energy-rich dust. Further out in the dark voids between the mountain ranges drift larger forms. Gelatinous leviathans, translucent save for the constellations of pulsing bioluminescent organs within them, hunt the smaller crystalline grazers. Their bodies are built to withstand the pressure and register the subtlest frequency shifts in the global drone, using the Sound as a form of sonar that spans continents. There are things that cling to the rock faces, filter-feeders with mouths like gramophone horns, angled perfectly to catch the drift of particulate. Their bodies are little more than resonating chambers, utterly passive and integrated into the monolithic hum.

This ecosystem is as stable as the Engines that power it. The populations do not boom or bust. The predators' hunger is perfectly matched to the grazers' numbers. The grazers' appetites are perfectly met by the Engines' unchanging output. Every niche is filled, and has been, forever. It is an ecology in a state of suspended animation, furiously active yet never, ever changing.

To be here is to float in a cold, loud, empty cathedral built for a machine god. The light is harsh and pure. The architecture is sharp and permanent. The choir is a single, deafening, eternal note. And the congregation of armored, crystalline, and gelatinous life drifts through the nave in silent, perfect, unending worship.
