This is a world born of mud and noise.

There is no sky, only a ceiling of perpetual, low-hanging vapor, so thick and warm it feels less like air and more like an unbreathable broth. The light that penetrates this miasma is a uniform, pearlescent grey, robbing the landscape of shadows and depth, making distance impossible to judge. The temperature is constant, a damp, oppressive heat that clings to any surface and beads as condensation on every exposure. The air smells of hot stone, wet clay, and ozone, with a sharp, ammonia-like undertone that catches in the back of the throat.

The sound is the first and last thing you perceive. It is not a collection of noises but a single, deafening, physical presence. It’s a wall of sound with the texture of a grinding stone and the pitch of a billion clicking, chittering things, underpinned by a deep, resonant thrumming that vibrates up from the soles of your feet. The sound is so absolute that it erases silence, making the concept of quiet an unimaginable fantasy. Conversation is impossible. Thought is a struggle against the overwhelming sensory input.

You stand on the edge of the Concretion, a city-basin that stretches to a hazy, featureless horizon. The ground underfoot is not rock or soil, but a semi-plastic, mineral-rich clay. It is firm in most places, but yields like packed silt in others. This entire lowland is a vast geological seep, a place where the planet’s mantle, under immense pressure, sweats a slurry of minerals and hot water to the surface. This is the raw material of everything.

There is no vegetation. Not a single leaf, no moss, no lichen. The world is shades of ochre, grey, brown, and terracotta. And yet, it is furiously, abundantly alive.

The "city" is not built in the human sense; it is excreted. It is a single, contiguous structure grown by its inhabitants. Trillions upon trillions of creatures, the Concretors, swarm in a slow, constant, river-like motion. They are exoskeletal, multi-limbed beings, ranging from the size of a thumb to the size of a cart. Their forms are functional, a fusion of crustacean and insect, with broad, spade-like forelimbs for scooping and patting, and complex mandibles for shaping and smoothing.

They work the geothermal slurry. They dredge the warm, wet clay, mix it with secreted bonding agents from their own bodies, and pat it into place. The result is a dense, labyrinthine architecture of unparalleled scale. It’s a metropolis of interconnected ramps, tunnels, arches, and buttresses, all formed from the same homogenous, damp material. The walls are thick and porous, weeping mineral-rich water that trickles down in steady, glistening streams. There are no windows, no doors, no streets—only a near-solid mass riddled with passages just large enough for the city's builders to pass through. The density is staggering; there is almost no empty space, only the raw material of the city and the bodies of its architects, layered one upon the other, near-touching in the dim, wet air.

This landscape is both embryonic and eternal. The "emerging" nature of the geology and the "never changing" dynamic are locked in a perfect, horrifying equilibrium. The geothermal updraft constantly delivers new slurry to the basin's floor. The Concretors work this new material, building their fused structures ever upwards. But as they build, the sheer weight of the Concretion presses the lowest, oldest layers back down into the plastic substrate, where they are heated, softened, and recycled.

The entire city is a treadmill. It is perpetually under construction at its base and perpetually sinking back into itself. From a fixed point, the architecture seems to flow upwards at an imperceptible rate, a single, slow-motion ballet of creation and dissolution. A specific archway you touch today will be a meter higher in a month, its form identical but its substance entirely new, while the material you touched will have sunk back into the crushing, formless depths. The sound never changes. The temperature never wavers. The population of the Concretors remains stable, their birth and death rates perfectly matching the city’s metabolic cycle.

Other creatures exist in the interstices. Pale, blind predators, sleek and worm-like, hunt the smaller Concretors in the fluid darkness of the inner tunnels. Micro-fauna cloud the weeping pools, filtering minerals. Nothing here is a primary producer in the photosynthetic sense. Life begins with chemosynthesis in the hot slurry. The entire food web is built on eating rock, and then eating the things that eat rock.

To be here is to be a foreign object in a perfectly closed system. The world is raw, unfinished, and yet utterly stable. It is a city roaring with the sound of its own making, a place of immense, mindless, deafening purpose, forever building itself from its own grave.
