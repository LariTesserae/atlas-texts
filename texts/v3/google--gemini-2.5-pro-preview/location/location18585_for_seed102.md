The first thing is the sound, and it is the only thing.

It is not a sound you hear with your ears alone, but one you feel in the marrow of your bones, a constant, complex, and deafeningly loud chime. It thrums up through the soles of your feet, vibrates in your teeth, and pushes against your skin like a physical presence. This is not the roar of a waterfall or the drone of machinery, but a chord of impossible richness, layered with the ringing of a thousand crystal bells, the deep resonance of a subterranean organ, and the high, shimmering hum of charged air. The sound is the atmosphere. It is the solid, immovable pillar upon which this entire place is built.

You are in a room. It is the only room. It is a geode, a pocket universe sealed within the deep earth, a hollow no larger than a great hall. The walls are not worn smooth by time and water; they are jagged, sharp planes of black, crystalline rock, fractured into existence and left untouched. They are a canvas of obsidian and dull, non-reflective basalt, broken by angular fault lines. There is no dust, for there is no wind to stir it. There is no moisture, for there is no water. The air is thick, warm, and utterly still, vibrating with the eternal chord. It carries no scent you can name, only a clean, mineral sharpness, the smell of hot stone and ozone.

From the floor and hanging from the ceiling grow the "plants," the source of the place's lushness and its dim light. They are not vegetable. They are vitreous, geological. Great, sprawling structures of fibrous crystal rise from the dark floor, each one an intricate, chaotic lattice of glowing filaments. They are like vast, frozen bursts of selenite or tourmaline, but they pulse with an inner luminescence. This is a cold light, a spectrum of deep blues, violets, and magentas, that waxes and wanes not with any rhythm of time, but in direct response to the harmonics of the ever-present sound. When a deep bass note swells in the chamber's chord, the thicker, trunk-like structures glow a resonant purple. When a high, piercing chime rings out, the delicate, hair-like fibers at their tips scintillate with a brilliant, nervous blue. Though there might be twenty feet of empty, black space between the bases of these crystalline growths, their "canopies" of light-soaked fiber interlock high above, creating a dense, glowing ceiling that feels both vast and enclosing.

This is a world without water. Life here is not based on aqueous chemistry. Nothing drinks, nothing flows. The vitreous flora does not grow in the way a plant does; it exists in a state of perfected, solid-state accretion. It is a conductor, a resonator, a living instrument built of stone and light.

And it is not uninhabited. The place is teeming with fauna, though the word feels wrong. Skittering across the glowing crystal branches and navigating the dark, sharp-angled floor are creatures of chitin and obsidian. They are abundant. Some are the size of your hand, multifaceted beetle-like things whose carapaces are iridescent, catching the dim violet light and fracturing it into a thousand tiny rainbows. They move with a brittle, clicking gait, their legs tapping a syncopated, arrhythmic beat against the sonic backdrop. Larger forms, like multi-limbed spiders woven from strands of mica and graphite, cling to the darker spaces on the walls, their dozens of tiny, unblinking eyes like scattered diamond dust.

They do not hunt or eat in a way that can be understood. Their existence is one of maintenance. You see one of the beetle-creatures climb a glowing violet stalk and use a specialized forelimb, a tiny, hardened hammer, to tap a specific crystal fiber. It produces a sharp, clear *tink* that is instantly absorbed into the great, overwhelming chime of the cavern. They are not consuming the flora; they are tuning it. They are the chorus, the percussion section, the groundskeepers of this sonic garden, ensuring the great chord never wavers, never changes. Their clicks and taps and skittering are not noise against the silence, but a necessary texture within the all-encompassing sound.

Here and there, there are faint marks of another intelligence. Not the creatures. Something else. On the floor, arranged in a pattern that seems to defy the natural chaos, are small, chipped stone basins. They hold nothing, but they are polished on the inside, as if by countless hands. A delicate lattice woven from the shed carapaces of the smaller creatures is strung between two low-slung crystal growths, looking for all the world like a primitive listening horn or a receiver, aimed at the heart of the chamber. These are not dwellings or forges. They are tools of observation, crude and ancient attempts to parse, to focus, or perhaps to endure the overwhelming sensory input of this place. They are the only things that hint at a past, a time before the perfect, unending present.

Because this place never changes. It is a system in absolute, unshakable equilibrium. The temperature is always warm. The light is always dim and pulsing in harmony with the sound. The sound is always loud, its chord the same today as it will be in a thousand years. The creatures do not breed or die; they simply *are*, performing their function. The crystals do not grow or wither. There are no seasons, no days, no nights. Time, gradient, and change are alien concepts. To be here is to be inside a single, sustained note, held for an eternity. It is a world without breath, a lung that is forever full. It is strange, beautiful, and utterly, profoundly static.
