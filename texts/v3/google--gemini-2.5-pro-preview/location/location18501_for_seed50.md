The sound is the first thing, the last thing, the only thing. It is not merely a sound that enters the ears; it is a physical pressure that permeates the body, a vibration that thrums in the bones and makes the teeth ache. It is the sound of a billion waterfalls at once, the grinding of a world being born, a deafening, ceaseless, harmonic roar that has no peak and no trough, just an absolute and total saturation of noise. All other senses are secondary, filtered through this wall of sound.

You are inside a pocket of this noise, a hidden chamber in a world that is nothing but water. But it is not the placid water of a sea. It is a medium in constant, violent motion, a turbulent fluid that is simultaneously the atmosphere, the ocean, and the weather. This pocket, this intimate sphere perhaps no wider than a grand hall, is called a Bell, though it has no clapper but the universe itself. The water within is cool, the kind of deep-body chill that numbs the skin, but it is super-saturated with light. There is no sun, no surface far above. The light is intrinsic to the water itself, a brilliant, strobing luminescence generated by the friction and the chaos. It is a physical manifestation of the sound, a cold, silent fire that illuminates everything in a frantic, flickering brilliance.

At the center of the Bell is the reason for the sound. It is a spire, a pillar of raw geology, thrusting up from an unseen, immeasurable depth and terminating somewhere in the flashing tumult above. This is the 'high ground', the only solid thing in a liquid cosmos. But it is not old stone. It is being created, right now, before your eyes. The water, oversaturated with dissolved mineral and potential, precipitates matter onto the spire. Needle-like crystals, milky white and razor-sharp, fuzz out from existing surfaces. Sheets of pearlescent material, thin as mica, plate themselves onto the pillar, cracking under their own instantaneous tension. This process of accretion is not gentle. It is a constant, chaotic construction. The deafening roar is the sound of this crystallization, the scream of matter being torn from a dissolved state into a solid one. The water around the spire boils without heat, a chaos of pressure waves and shearing currents as new land is born from liquid.

Because the geology is embryonic, nothing is stable. A handhold of freshly-sprouted crystal might hold for a moment, then snap off and dissolve back into the fluid. A seemingly solid ledge could be buried in a minute by a new flurry of accretion. The spire is a living, growing thing, but it has no biology, only a violent, raw physics.

Clinging to the less-violent faces of the spire is the only vegetation. It is sparse, found only in rare patches that have achieved a temporary equilibrium. These are not plants of flesh and cellulose, but lattices of resonating crystal. They look like tufts of spun glass, silvery and translucent, humming silently in a frequency just beneath the overwhelming roar. They feed directly on the ambient energy of the Bell, drawing sustenance from the vibration and the saturated light, their delicate structures a testament to life's impossible persistence.

There are signs of others. Not a civilization, but a struggle. Carved into some of the older, momentarily stable facets of the spire are simple, profound marks. Loops, bored through the stone, designed to have a rope or a tether threaded through. These anchor points are worn smooth on their inner surfaces, testament to something or someone holding on for dear life against the turbulent currents. Here and there, tattered remnants of a net, woven from the glassy filaments of the crystal-weeds, are snagged on spurs of new rock. They were not for catching fish, but for creating a baffle, a small zone of relatively calmer water to hide within. These are the primitive tools of survival in a world of pure chaos.

Life, true fauna, is an event. Entire cycles of watching might pass with nothing but the growing stone and the flickering light. Then, a shadow will detach itself from the wider chaos. A Current-Skate, perhaps. It is a creature of shell and cartilage, shaped like a flattened diamond, its surface a mosaic of iridescent plates that ripple with the shifting light. It does not swim but surfs, angling its body to ride the intricate and violent pressure waves. It is blind, its head a smooth armored prow, for eyes are useless in this strobing maelstrom. It navigates by feeling, its entire body an organ for sensing the minute shifts in the currents and the resonant frequencies of the growing spire. It comes to scrape algae-like mineral growths from the new stone with a mouth like a hardened rasp, its passing a rare and fleeting moment of deliberate motion in a world of chaos.

To be here is to be unmade. The rules are simple. There is no 'up' or 'down', only 'toward the spire' and 'away from the spire'. There is no silence, ever. There is no stillness. The world is a screaming, light-filled womb, perpetually in the act of giving birth to itself, and everything within it is either being created, holding on, or being swept away into the roaring, luminous nothingness.
