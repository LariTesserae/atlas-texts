The air is the first thing you notice. It is warm and thick, a solid presence in the lungs that tastes of wet minerals and something like the sweet-rot of blooming fungi. You cannot call it fresh. It’s too heavy, too full of the world it occupies. To breathe it is to drink the place.

This is a basin, a hollow, a cupped hand in the palm of a greater world you cannot see. The scale is intimate, a room with no ceiling. The light is a dim, bruised purple, as if filtering through a perpetual thunderhead or a layer of deep water far, far above. There are no shadows, only degrees of gloom.

The ground is wrong. It is not soil or stone, not clay or mud. It is primordial stuff, a geology still deciding what it will become. It yields slightly underfoot, a firm-set, rubbery surface like unfired clay or cold, dense wax. The color is a spectrum of grays and liver-purples, swirled and marbled as if the pigments of the world were stirred here and left to set. The land is still warm from its own making. There is no dust. Here and there, the ground weeps, exuding a clear, heavy water that beads like mercury and pools in shallow, perfectly still depressions. This is the only standing water, these placid mirrors that reflect the dim, colorless light above. This world is damp, not wet. The moisture is in the air, on the surfaces of things—a constant, condensing dew.

From this pliable floor rise the structures. It is a city, but a city grown, not built. Without tools or intention beyond that of biology, something has created a settlement. These are towers, dozens of them, scattered with a surprising amount of space between. They do not crowd; each stands in its own sovereign territory. They are not uniform. Some are slender, tapering spires fifty feet high; others are squat, bulbous domes like massive termite mounds. They are secreted structures, made of a matte, ochre material somewhere between chitin and unglazed ceramic. Their surfaces are a metropolis of texture: spiraling ramps, weeping pores, latticed vents, and deep-set pockets that serve as planters. The entire process is silent.

And from this sparse, echoing architecture, a rampant lushness erupts. The towers themselves are the substrate for a vertical jungle. Heavy, dark green ferns with waxy leaves, so glossy they look wet even when dry, unfurl from ledges. Veils of emerald moss, thick as felt, drink the condensing air and hang in soft curtains over openings. Fungi grow in tiered shelves, their gills a delicate, ghostly white in the low light, some pulsing with a slow, faint bioluminescence. In the pockets of the towers, carnivorous pitcher plants, striped in maroon and cream, hold their own little pools of water, waiting. The vegetation does not grow from the ground, only from the towers. The space between the structures is bare, nascent geology. The effect is of a sparse cityscape overgrown by a dense, vertical swamp.

Life is everywhere, but it is quiet. The abundance of fauna is a thing of rustles and flickers, not calls or roars. The architects of this city are six-legged beings, the size of a human hand, with iridescent carapaces that shift from oil-slick purple to bronze. They move with a slow, methodical grace, their legs making an infinitesimal rasping on the ceramic-like walls as they patrol and repair their towers, adding new material one tiny secreted drop at a time. Their work is the dominant dynamic here, a process of silent, ceaseless construction.

In the damp mosses live amphibians the color of jade, their skin pebbled and glistening. Long, multi-jointed arthropods, like blind, white centipedes, flow through the internal tunnels of the spires. Tiny, gecko-like lizards with wide, black eyes cling to the undersides of fungal shelves, hunting the silent, fluttering moths that feed on the fungal spores. If you watch one of the weeping pools on the ground, you might see a pale, eyeless eel-thing stir the still water for a moment before vanishing back into a fissure in the soft ground. Predator and prey act out their dramas in utter silence, a dance of ambush and escape conducted in a hushed world.

The only sounds are those of the place itself. The slow, rhythmic patter of a water droplet falling from a fern frond onto the rubbery ground below. A soft, wet *schloop* as a pocket of gas escapes the still-forming earth. The almost inaudible scrape and rustle of the tower-builders against their own creation. The silence is not an absence, but a presence. It is a thick, muffling blanket woven of humidity and soft surfaces.

There are rules here. Sound does not travel far. The ground is still becoming, and to scar it is to change its geological future. Light is a gift, never a guarantee. The towers are both home and garden, a complete ecosystem unto themselves, born from the bare floor. This place is not a ruin, nor is it complete. It is a city in the act of being born, in a world that is itself still in the womb. To be here is to witness the slow, silent, and profoundly active process of creation.
