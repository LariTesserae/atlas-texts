The sound is the first thing, and the last. It’s not a sound that travels to you through the air, but one that feels as if it’s being generated inside your own skull, a deep and complexly-layered choral hum. It has texture. It has architecture. It is a constant, voluminous pressure, like being at the bottom of an ocean of noise. The sound has a pitch, but it also has harmonics that feel geometric, as if you’re hearing the shape of a cathedral instead of its echo. This is the baseline of existence here.

You stand at the edge of a great, shallow basin, a valley so wide and its slopes so gentle that the horizon feels both immensely distant and deceptively close. The scale is one of open plains, but the space is filled to bursting. This is a low, cool, wet world, perpetually caught in the moment after a week of rain. The air is dim, not from a simple lack of sun, but as if the light itself is tired, its photons struggling to push through an atmosphere thick with more than just moisture. It’s an iron-grey twilight that never deepens to black nor brightens to day.

Underfoot, the ground is a saturated sponge of black soil, ancient peat, and dense, cushiony mosses that have been growing, compressing, and re-growing for millennia. There are no sharp edges here. The long work of erosion and deposition has softened every contour, leaving a landscape of rolling, indistinct swells and shallow, waterlogged hollows. Small, dark rocks, worn smooth as river stones, poke through the vegetation, but they are not dry. They weep. A constant, oily slick of water beads on their surfaces and crawls in rivulets, sometimes sideways, sometimes even *upwards*, before dripping into the moss. The water doesn’t obey. It hangs in the air in perfect, trembling spheres of mist that drift on currents that have nothing to do with wind. In the pools that collect in the hollows, the surface is not flat but subtly warped, and a stone tossed in might cause ripples that slow down, halt, and then reverse their direction, converging back to their point of origin with a silent *pop*.

This is a place of profound density. The vegetation is not just lush; it is hysterically, impossibly so. But it doesn't strive for the sky. Great, swollen-looking ferns uncoil in spirals that seem to have too many dimensions, their fronds overlapping in ways that defy simple geometry. Carpets of phosphorescent moss cover everything, pulsing with a faint, sickly green light in time with a sub-audible frequency of the omnipresent hum. Fungi grow in tiered, crystalline shelves from the damp soil, looking more like mineral formations than anything biological. They are cool to the touch and resonate with the sound, vibrating like tuning forks. Every cubic inch of air, every clod of earth, every drop of water is saturated with presence, with the sheer weight of *stuff*.

The omnipresent hum is the engine of this place. It is not technological in the sense of a machine; there are no artifacts, no ruins, no sign that anything was ever *built* here. The technology is the operating system of the environment itself. It is a resonant, informational field that permeates spacetime, a legacy of something that either failed catastrophically or succeeded all too well. Reality is a local variable here, and the field is constantly tuning it. The weird physics of the water, the impossible biology of the plants, the very quality of the dim light—these are not exceptions to the rules. They *are* the rules. The world is being actively, perpetually written by a language you can only hear as a deafening, monotonous hum.

Fauna is nearly non-existent. The sonic and informational pressure is too great for complex nervous systems to cohere. To be an animal here would be like trying to form a thought in the heart of a supernova. But there is something. Drifting through the thick air, usually near the ground, are the Drifters. They are not quite creatures. They are shifting, shimmering curtains of iridescence, like oil slicks given a third dimension and a slow, deliberate will. They are perhaps two meters long, a meter high, and only a centimeter thick. They move without propulsion, ignore gravity, and pass through the distorted ferns and weeping rocks without interaction. They are echoes, or data ghosts, or perhaps antibodies formed by the landscape’s strange immune system. They are the only other moving things, and they make no sound, which in this place, is the loudest statement of all.

The ecosystem is active, but it’s the activity of a process, not a food web. Plants grow, absorb the distorted information from the field, and express it as physical form. The field causes the water to move, to pool, to climb, redistributing the fundamental ingredient of this place. The Drifters patrol their meaningless territories. Everything is in a state of dynamic, stable flux. It is a settled system, weathered into its own alien equilibrium. It has been this way for an unthinkably long time, humming its terrible, silent song to itself in the Grey. To be here is to feel like a glitch, a foreign piece of data in a system that has no protocol for you, a system whose very existence is a violation of your own. You can’t stay, you can’t think, you can only listen to the hum as it tries to rewrite you.
