This place is a contradiction held in permanent, vibrating tension.

The first and last thing you perceive is the sound. It is not merely loud; it is a physical component of the environment, a pressure on the skin and a vibration in the bones. It is a deafening, foundational drone, a chord struck on an instrument the size of a mountain range. Within the drone, a million glass-shard chimes ring at frequencies that feel just beyond the edge of hearing, creating a sense of auditory vertigo. The sound is the bedrock. All other senses are layered on top of it. Conversation is impossible. Thought is difficult. The world is a bell, and you are inside it.

You are standing in a valley, or what was once a valley. The floor is a grid of polished black material, slick with a constant, cool moisture. The scale is immense; the grid stretches for miles, crisscrossed by hairline fractures that pulse with a faint, internal, cyan light. There is no soil. But this is not an empty plaza.

From the valley floor, and hanging in the vast space above, is the city. It is not a city of buildings, but of filaments. Immense, knife-edged arches and impossibly thin bridges of the same resonant black material rise and interlace, creating a three-dimensional scaffold that fills the volume of the valley. It is an urban structure, ordered and complex, but it is almost entirely hollow. The density is near zero. Looking up, you can see through a dozen layers of superstructure to the perpetual, dim grey ceiling of the sky—a sky filtered through even more, higher layers of this same lattice. It’s like being inside the skeleton of a skyscraper a hundred miles long.

The air is warm and heavy, saturated with a humidity that doesn't come from rain, but from the structure itself. The drone’s vibration causes a constant condensation to form on the cool, black surfaces. This atmospheric moisture coalesces and drips, running in clean rivulets down the filaments, gathering in shallow, perfectly geometric pools on the wider platforms of the grid. This is the world’s water: an endless, technologically-driven dew.

And on this scaffold, life thrives with a desperate, tenacious lushness. Biology has conquered the architecture. Thick, velvety mosses, the color of bruised plums and deep teals, carpet the undersides of arches. Vines as thick as pythons, studded with geometric, crystalline leaves, hang like gargantuan drapes, swaying almost imperceptibly with the structure’s vibration. Where a bridge widens, it supports a hanging garden of enormous, cup-shaped flora that collect the dripping water. These plants are bioluminescent, casting a dim, shifting light into the spaces between the filaments. The effect is of a vast and cavernous cathedral, decorated with living stained glass, but one where all the windows have been smashed out, leaving only the leaden frames and a few clinging shards of color.

This vertical jungle is teeming with fauna. Since sound is useless for communication or hunting, life here is a ballet of silence and sight. Herds of six-legged, deer-sized creatures, their bodies covered in iridescent scales that shift from blue to copper, move with impossible grace along paper-thin struts, their wide, padded feet giving them purchase. They browse on the hanging vines, their movements slow and deliberate. Hunting them are things that are more glide than flight, creatures with vast, membranous wings that stretch between four limbs. They hang motionless in the updrafts between structures, indistinguishable from the shadows, before detaching to fall upon their prey in utter silence. In the water pools on the grid floor, metallic, jewel-toned arthropods scuttle, their chirps absorbed by the overwhelming drone.

This world is not static. The drone, the fundamental sound, is not a simple loop. It is a symphony of creation. Its harmonics shift, subtly but with immense consequence. As the chord changes, the structure itself responds. A new filament might precipitate from the humid air, sharp and perfect, extending from one arch to another over the course of an hour. A whole section of latticework might lose its resonant integrity, dissolving back into vapor and agitated air, its constituent mosses and vines falling into the void below. To exist here is to understand that the ground beneath your feet is not a guarantee. The landscape is being actively, perpetually generated and erased. The sharp, uneroded features of the city are not a sign of geological youth, but of constant, instantaneous renewal.

This is a place of profound emptiness and overwhelming presence. It is a city built for no one, a machine humming its deafening song of self-construction into a hollow space. You can see for miles through its skeletal frame, yet the vibration of its becoming fills every particle of your being. It is a warm, dim, damp, and deafeningly loud garden, growing on the bones of a song.
