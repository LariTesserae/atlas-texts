The place is contained, a single, vast chamber shaped like a shallow, multi-tiered bowl. You might call it the Kiln Terraces. Everything smells of warm dust, of baked clay and the faint, sharp scent of ozone that speaks not of storms, but of immense, contained energy. The air is still and carries a dry, uniform heat, like the inside of a server room or the air over a sun-baked rock. There is no water. Not a drop, not a wisp of vapor. The concept feels alien here.

The ground underfoot, the walls, the tiered levels climbing towards the center—it is all a single, continuous material. It is a work of planetary-scale pottery. The floor is a pavement of warm, unglazed terracotta tiles, each one subtly unique, bearing the faint impression of a thumb-swirl or a fabric press. The walls of each terrace, rising a few feet or a few dozen, are smoother, finished in a matte, bone-white glaze. It's all hand-built, or seems to be. The scale of the craft is impossible, yet the evidence of the hand is everywhere. It is a primitive place built with a god's tools. The edges are sharp, the surfaces uneroded. It seems to have been fired, cooled, and populated only yesterday.

This is a jungle, but not of flesh and cellulose. Filling every basin, crowding every terrace, is a riot of lush, mineral vegetation. It grows, but not through biology. It extrudes. From hairline cracks in the terracotta, forests of crystalline grass push silently upward, each blade a sliver of smoky quartz. Great, curling fronds of what looks like verdigris-coated copper unspool in slow-motion, their surfaces covered in a fine, powdery patina. Where there would be trees, there are towering, branching structures of raw, unglazed porcelain, their limbs delicate and impossibly balanced. Their "leaves" are sheets of thin, crazed celadon glaze, so fine they tremble and chime when disturbed, a sound that is quickly swallowed by the profound quiet. Flowers bloom here, but they are blossoms of spun glass, their petals capturing the ambient light and fracturing it into soft, internal rainbows.

There is no sun. The light is sourceless and even, a diffuse golden-white glow that seems to emanate from the very air, or perhaps from the high, unseen ceiling of the bowl. It casts no distinct shadows, only a soft ambiguity at the base of every object, a slight darkening that gives things presence without defining their relationship to a light source.

The place is teeming with life, but it adheres to the local chemistry of silicate and stone. Skittering among the porcelain trunks are creatures like trilobites, their carapaces made of laminated flakes of mica. They move with an unsettling, stop-motion gait. Larger things, like armored pangolins made of overlapping ceramic tiles, methodically crawl up the glazed terrace walls, their claws making a faint, dry *scritch-scratch* sound. They don’t eat the crystal plants; they abrade them. They use hardened facial protrusions to grind away microscopic dust from the mineral leaves and stalks, which they seem to absorb as sustenance. Small, moth-like beings made of what looks like asbestos fiber flutter on silent wings, their purpose unclear, their flight paths jerky and unpredictable. They will land on your shoulder, weightless and inert as a stray piece of lint, before jittering back into the air.

This is a world breaking down at the seams. Its own internal logic is a tapestry woven by a mad or dreaming weaver. The weirdness is not flashy; it is fundamental and quiet. You might walk along the lip of a terrace for ten minutes, only to look back and see your footprints begin just a few yards away. The space you traversed never existed. A porcelain tree branch seen from one angle might appear solid and white, but shift your head a few inches to the left, and it becomes translucent, revealing another, identical terrace nested within it like a phantom. These are not illusions; they are competing realities occupying the same coordinates.

Sounds are untethered from their origins. The delicate, high-frequency *tink* of a glass blossom shattering might reach your ears a full minute before you see the event occur, or from a direction where nothing is happening. Sometimes, you hear the sound of your own footsteps arriving from somewhere in front of you.

The rules are patient, but not stable. The slow, gradual extrusion of the mineral flora is the landscape's primary dynamic. But sometimes, a section of the terrace will decide to reinterpret its own geometry. A flat expanse of tile might, over the course of a day, silently buckle and fold itself into a complex origami of a hillock. A perfectly circular basin might, over a week, elongate into an ellipse, stretching the porcelain trees within it into thin, screaming forms before they snap and regrow in the new configuration.

To be here is to be inside a thought. A slow, warm, dense, and deeply unstable thought, crafted by hand and then abandoned to its own devices. It is mostly quiet, but it is the quiet of a thing listening to itself, trying to remember the rules of its own existence and failing, slowly, beautifully, piece by crystalline piece.
