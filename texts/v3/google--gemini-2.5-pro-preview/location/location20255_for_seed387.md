You are inside a heart. Not a biological one of flesh and blood, but a geologic, mechanical one. You are contained. The world is the interior of a hollow sphere no more than a kilometer in diameter, a geode a god might have cracked open and found disappointing. The walls are a mosaic of dull, wine-dark crystal facets, so ancient and worn that they have the soft, non-reflective texture of sea-worn glass.

This world is submerged. It is entirely filled with a liquid, not water, but something denser, warmer. It feels like heated oil or amniotic fluid, pressing in on all sides with a tangible, uniform warmth that seeps into any surface. The fluid is in a state of perpetual, violent turmoil. Vast, slow-moving currents, like atmospheric jet streams, circle the equator of this spherical space, while smaller, more chaotic vortices peel off to churn the poles. You can see these currents not because the liquid is murky—it is unnervingly clear—but because of the incandescent particles they carry, tiny flecks of what might be rust or superheated metal that tracer-fire through the dimness.

The source of this energy, and of the single most defining characteristic of this place, is the sound. It is deafening. It is absolute. It is a physical presence that vibrates through the liquid, through the crystal shell, through the very marrow of your bones. It is a chord, composed of a bass-note thrum so low it is felt more than heard, the oceanic roar of the churning liquid, and a high, keening shriek of metal under unimaginable stress. The sound never changes in character, only in its chaotic, surging volume. It is the sound of a vast machine operating far beyond its intended capacity, forever on the verge of catastrophic failure. Silence is not a concept here; it is an impossibility more profound than a vacuum.

Clinging to the inner curve of the crystal walls is a city. It is not built upon the floor, but hangs from the walls and ceiling, a network of spires, platforms, and bridges that jut inwards towards the chaotic center. The architecture is skeletal, industrial. The primary materials are a dark, greenish-black metal, immune to corrosion, and sections of fused quartz, like cataracts in the metal skeleton. The structures are old. Every edge has been rounded, every surface scoured smooth by millennia of the roaring fluid. Catwalks made of punched-steel grating connect towers that serve no discernible purpose. Great pipelines, broader than a person, snake along the walls, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic light, their joints weeping threads of superheated vapor that dissolve instantly into the surrounding fluid.

The city is urban in its density of infrastructure, but it is paradoxically empty. The vast, churning void at the center of the geode dominates the volume. The city is a mere crust, a fringe settlement on the edge of a roaring emptiness. Distances are deceptive. You can see a spire on the opposite side of the sphere, its dim warning lights blinking across the central abyss, but the journey to it would be a terrifying navigation of cross-currents and eddies. There are no signs of life, no crowds, no voices to be lost in the din. The built environment implies a population, but the space itself tells you it is hollow.

Light is a rarity, a tool. It is not for comfort or illumination. It is the dull cherry-red glow from thermal vents along the pipelines, the cold blue-white of occasional electrical arcs between substations, and the steady, lonely blink of amber beacons atop the tallest spires. The rest is a profound dimness, a twilight of deep water and dark crystal.

Ecology is mineral and mechanical. There are no plants. Life, what little of it exists, has adapted to this high-pressure, high-energy environment. Rarely, like a myth made real, a Scale-Crawler may be seen. It is a creature of stone and metal, resembling a colossal, flattened trilobite, perhaps ten meters long. Its carapace is made of the same dark crystal as the walls, allowing it to blend in perfectly when still. It moves with impossible slowness, its multiple, spindly legs gripping the metal spires as it scrapes the surfaces for, perhaps, mineral deposits or some form of electrical residue. It is deaf, blind, and ancient, a scavenger in a world with little to scavenge. It seems less an animal and more a piece of self-replicating machinery, another component of this roaring engine-world.

The rules of this place are simple. The current is law. The sound is the only constant. Gravity is a gentle, persistent pull towards the outer wall, no matter your orientation. The purpose of this city, this machine inside a geode, is the fundamental and unanswerable question. Is it a power generator for something on the outside? A prison? A failed terraforming experiment? Or is it simply a heart, mindlessly pumping a hot, dead fluid for a body that has long since crumbled to dust, its deafening roar the only proof it ever existed at all? To be here is to be a ghost in a machine that has forgotten its function, a lonely presence in a hidden, screaming void.
