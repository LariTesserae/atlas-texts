The air is thin and carries no scent of rain, soil, or decay. It is perfectly neutral, a dry and empty medium, warm against the skin like the inside of a room that has held the sun all day. You stand on a high, windless plateau. The ground underfoot is not rock weathered by ages, but something newer, sharper. It is a landscape of raw tectonics, of basaltic extrusions and obsidian fields fractured into razor-edged plates that jostle against each other in a frozen jumble. There is no sand, no dust worn from mountains, only this coarse, nascent geology, all angles and unsoftened planes.

The scale is immense. This plateau is a tableland stretching to a horizon of yet-higher, equally raw peaks. But what commands the eye is the forest. It is a forest of impossible lushness in a world without water.

Across the vast, dark plain, separated by miles of barren ground, stand colossal structures. From a distance, they have the silhouettes of ancient, broad-crowned trees, their forms suggesting a wild and organic proliferation. But as you draw nearer to one, the illusion of biology shatters. What appeared to be a trunk is a hollow, latticed pillar of spun metal, dull as lead but woven with the intricacy of a wicker basket. It rises for hundreds of feet, a complex, self-supporting braid of metallic strands, some as thick as a torso, others as fine as wire. There is no bark, only the cool, unchanging surface of the metal.

From the top of this pillar erupts the canopy. It is not a spray of leaves, but an explosive profusion of glass. Billions of gossamer filaments, spun impossibly thin, branch and weave and tangle into a vast, cloud-like dome. They capture and refract the normal, clear light of the sun, breaking it into a thousand soft, partitioned radiances that dapple the obsidian floor below. The canopy is so dense, so intricate, that it appears lush and verdant, a vibrant crown on its metallic stem. Underneath it, the world is a mosaic of sharp shadow and diffused, gentle light. The colors within the glass are subtle—faint shades of copper-green, manganese violet, and iron-oxide red, giving the entire structure the pale, washed-out palette of a sea creature.

This is a world without animals, without insects, without any skittering life. The only sound is not the rustle of leaves or the call of a bird, but a deep, barely audible hum. It is the sound of the world’s true engine.

At the base of each metallic tree, the ground is pierced by vents. From these circular fissures breathes a slow, steady exhalation of superheated, mineral-saturated vapor. This is the world’s secret process, its anhydrous lifeblood. The vapor, invisible in the warm air, carries elemental metals and silicates up from the planet's molten core. As it rises and cools, it doesn't condense into liquid; it deposits itself directly onto the crystalline lattice of the "tree." This is the industrial heart of the landscape. It is a factory without builders. The process is one of constant, microscopic accretion. Metal atoms bond to the trunk, glass molecules adhere to the filaments of the canopy. The tree is perpetually *growing*, but it is not alive.

Yet, nothing ever changes. The system is in a state of perfect, frozen equilibrium. For every new particle that crystallizes onto the structure, another is shed. The immense heat and internal resonance of the structure cause a constant, imperceptible shedding of micro-filaments and metallic dust. This dust, glittering and abrasive, is the only soil this world knows. It coats the obsidian plains in a fine, sparkling layer. The rate of growth and the rate of decay are precisely, mathematically equal. The tree is a standing wave, a physical process locked in a moment of frantic activity that results in absolute stasis. It is forever emerging, forever under construction, but it will never be finished.

The landscape is therefore both empty and full. The space between the colossal glass-and-metal trees is vast and vacant, a sterile desert of sharp rock. But to stand beneath one is to be enveloped in its intricate, overwhelming presence. You are inside a machine that mimics a forest, a geological engine running in perfect, silent, pointless equilibrium. Its existence is a fact, not a story. It has no past and no future. It is simply, and eternally, *now*.
