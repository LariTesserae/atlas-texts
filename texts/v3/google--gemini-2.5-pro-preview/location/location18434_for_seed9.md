The air is thick, warm, and heavy with the scent of hot rock and wet moss. You stand on a floor that is not quite solid. It is a mosaic of hexagonal plates of a dark, glassy material, like cooling obsidian or industrial slag. Many are cool and slick with condensation, but here and there, a plate is new, still glowing a sullen cherry-red at its seams, radiating a palpable heat that warms the humid air around it. The ground is under construction, not by workers, but by some intrinsic process, as if the world here is being 3D-printed from the bottom up. A low, grinding hum is the only announcement as a new hexagon is slowly extruded from a circular orifice in the floor, steaming as its 1000-degree surface meets the cool, wet atmosphere.

This place is a chamber, a hall, a nave. Its scale is that of a grand cathedral, yet it feels claustrophobically dense. It is built, entirely built, with no natural substrate visible anywhere. Towering pillars, ribbed and fluted like colossal steel bones, rise into the high mist, disappearing before their tops can be seen. They don't stand in orderly rows but grow in a tangled, organic thicket, so close together that in places they fuse into solid walls of grooved, dark material. This material isn't stone or metal, but a ceramic composite, warm to the touch and perpetually weeping.

Water doesn't fall here; it seeps. It bleeds from hairline fractures in the pillars and from the mortar-like seams between immense, arching wall panels. It beads on every surface, gathering into heavy drops that tremble and fall with a soft, resonant *plink* onto the floor plates or into the cupped leaves of the rampant vegetation. The atmosphere is so saturated that the air itself seems to condense, leaving a fine, glistening sheen on your skin and clothes. The light is a uniform, pearlescent grey, the bright, edgeless glare of being inside a cloud at high elevation.

Despite the sterility of the architecture, life is riotously lush. It does not grow *on* the structure; it is integrated *into* it. Thick, python-like vines, with bark that mimics the texture of the ceramic pillars, spiral upwards, plugging into humming, electrified ports every few meters. Their leaves are enormous, heart-shaped, and the colour of polished jade, their waxy surfaces channeling condensation down their stems and back into the system. Carpets of moss, impossibly vibrant and shot through with fibrous, glowing mycelia, cling to every horizontal surface, thriving in the warmth and constant damp. This is not an ecosystem that has colonized a ruin; it is a designed, engineered wilderness.

The quiet is profound, but it is not silence. It is a layered quiet, composed of the constant, gentle hiss of condensing water, the slow, thick drip from leaf-tips, and the almost subsonic hum that vibrates through the very floor beneath your feet. This is the sound of the place's metabolism. It's the hum of vast, unseen pumps circulating nutrient-rich water, of electro-thermal generators embedded deep within the walls, of the slow, tectonic creep of the structure's own growth. Periodically, a sharper sound punctuates the calm: a precise, mechanical *click-hiss* from high above, followed by a fine mist of vapor that drifts down, carrying the sharp scent of ozone and minerals. This is the weather, a scheduled micro-climate event.

Fauna is almost nonexistent. There are no birds calling from the unseen ceiling, no lizards skittering on the warm plates. The only movement, other than the sway of leaves and the drip of water, comes from small, beetle-like constructs, the size of a human hand. They are six-legged, with burnished copper carapaces, and they move with unnerving, purposeful slowness. They are the gardeners and janitors of this place. One might be seen methodically scraping an overgrowth of algae from a conduit with a tiny, whirring brush; another might be carefully tending to a newly-sprouted vine, its manipulator arms extending a filament of nutrient paste to the growing tip. They are silent, and they ignore you completely.

This world is active. It is a system in constant, churning, but deliberate motion. You can watch a translucent patch on a wall slowly opacify as its internal crystalline structure precipitates and locks into place. You can feel the subtle shift in temperature as a new heating element cycles on. The place is a machine, an organism, an ecosystem, and a geology all at once. Its one, unwritten rule seems to be that everything is part of the process. The water is the machine's coolant and the plants' lifeblood. The plants are the machine's lungs and decorative finish. The structure is the ground, the sky, and the engine. It is a sealed terrarium on a mountaintop, a self-building vivarium in the clouds, perpetually embryonic and endlessly complex.
