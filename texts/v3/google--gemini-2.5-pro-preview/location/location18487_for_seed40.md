The light has no source, yet it is everywhere. It is a brilliant, clinical white that scours detail from a distance, rendering the horizon in stark, overexposed monochrome. There are no shadows here, only gradients of occlusion where the world layers over itself. The air is cool and thin, carrying a dampness that doesn't feel like humidity but like the fundamental state of the atmosphere. Every surface is slick with a perpetual, fine condensation. Breathing tastes of ozone and wet stone.

The ground is not earth or rock, but a seamless, continent-spanning floor of interlocking hexagonal plates. They are made of a material like dark, unpolished ceramic, cool and utterly solid underfoot. The plates are immense—each one the size of a town square—and their join-lines are so fine as to be almost invisible. But it is along these faint cracks that the world’s moisture gathers, seeping downward into some unseen, labyrinthine plumbing. The silence here is profound, a pressure in the ears. It is not the absence of sound, but the active dampening of it. A shout is swallowed within a few feet. The scrape of a boot on the ceramic floor produces a dull, unsatisfying thud that dies instantly.

This vast, flat expanse is punctuated by density. Growing from the plate-floor are structures, thickets of them, clustered into groves and ranges like a kind of architecture that thinks it is a forest. They are not buildings in any recognizable sense. They are spires, pylons, and gantries of impossible complexity, woven from materials that defy categorization. Some are the color of verdigris-eaten brass, latticed like bone. Others are smooth, white porcelain that seems to issue a low, subliminal hum felt only in the teeth. Still others are tangles of what look like glass optic fibers as thick as a human arm, carrying pulses of soft, internal light.

This is a young landscape. The edges of the ceramic plates are knife-sharp. The metallic spires show no corrosion, only the fine, geometric precision of their recent formation. Nothing is weathered or worn. The world is not eroding; it is being extruded. The process is too slow to witness directly, but its evidence is everywhere. You might notice a pylon that seems taller than you remember, or that a new, crystalline spar, sharp as obsidian, has pierced the air from the side of a brass lattice. The world is a factory whose only product is more of itself, operating on a geological timescale but with industrial precision.

Despite the sterility, there is life, or what passes for it. What might be mistaken for vegetation is sparse. Patches of "voltage moss," a shimmering, circuit-board-like growth, colonize certain floor plates, feeding on a faint electrical potential that runs through the substrate. They glow with a faint, pulsing blue. Thicker, wire-like growths, like metallic ivy, climb the pylons, their segmented bodies occasionally twitching in slow, rhythmic waves.

The fauna is abundant and deeply strange. Swarms of "glimmer-wings" drift through the air. They are not insects. They are tiny, articulated contraptions of metal foil and wire, catching the ambient light like a cloud of broken mirrors. They don't fly so much as hang in the air, shifting and rotating with an unnerving, silent coordination. On the ground, larger things move. "Damp-crawlers," resembling multi-legged trilobites made of mercury and chrome, scuttle along the plate-joins, siphoning the condensation with proboscises that look like soldering irons. They are the size of dinner plates and move with a liquid, silent grace.

And then there are the Caretakers. They are vaguely humanoid, ten feet tall, their bodies seemingly woven from the same cable-like growths that ascend the towers. They move with an immense, unhurried deliberation, their faces blank, featureless masks of polished ceramic. They do not walk so much as glide a hair's breadth above the floor. They perform inscrutable acts of maintenance: tracing a finger along a pylon, causing a cascade of soft chimes to ripple up its length; pausing to rearrange a cluster of glimmer-wings into a new, resonant pattern; or simply standing motionless for days, their heads tilted as if listening to the world’s silent machinery. They take no notice of anything else.

The weirdness, the breakdown of our reality, is subtle but absolute. The open spaces between the structural groves feel vast, but distances are unreliable. A cluster of spires that seems a day’s walk away can be reached in a hundred steps if you don't look at it directly. A bridge of solid light might flicker into existence between two pylons, hold for an hour, and then dissolve into motes of dust. The geometry is patient but not fixed. The world is not hostile, merely indifferent, operating on a logic that is complex, purposeful, and utterly alien. You can exist here, for a time. But the rules are not for you. You are a temporary condensation on a surface that is slowly, quietly, and eternally becoming something else.
