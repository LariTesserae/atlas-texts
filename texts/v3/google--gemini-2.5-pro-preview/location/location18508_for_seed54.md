The sound comes first, a bass-heavy, choral drone that is not so much heard as felt, vibrating up through the soles of the feet and into the hollows of the chest. It is the aggregate hum of a trillion tiny engines, a resonance that has saturated the very stone of the world. This is the baseline, the sound of silence in the Umbral Sprawl.

The ground is a scabland of nascent structure. There is no soil, no sand, no recognizable rock. Instead, the floor of this high-altitude basin is a lattice of glossy, black basalt-like material, extruded in hexagonal pillars that are still warm to the touch, despite the profound, biting cold. These pillars are imperfect, emerging. Some are mere bumps, just beginning to break the surface like geological teeth. Others stand a few feet tall, their sides weeping a supercooled, viscous fluid that is not quite water. This fluid slicks every surface, catching the world's meager light in oily, shifting rainbows. It does not freeze solid, but thickens into a gel in the coldest hollows, trapping bubbles of gas that pulse with faint, internal light. The air is thick with the condensation of this fluid, a perpetual, freezing mist that clings to everything.

This is a city, but one that is being born, not built. The Sprawl is urban in its density and organization, but geological in its process. Towering, skeletal structures of the same black material curve into the oppressive darkness above, not as finished buildings, but as the ribs of some colossal, still-forming creature. They are cantilevers of impossible grace, bone-white filaments that stretch between them like spider-silk scaffolding, and vast, sheer faces that glitter with crystalline mineral deposits. The entire landscape is under construction by a force that is patient, inexorable, and utterly alien. The low `dynamic` means this growth is imperceptible in the moment, but its evidence is everywhere. You can place a hand on a wall and feel the slow, deep thrum of its molecular accretion, a process of an engineering far beyond human ken. The city does not rise; it accretes. Its tectonics are its architecture.

There are no plants. The energy for this world comes from somewhere else. The ecology is overwhelming, a direct consequence of the city's self-construction. The `fauna` are not visitors or pests; they are a functioning layer of the metropolis. The source of the deafening hum is a living, moving carpet of arthropodal life that covers nearly every surface not currently undergoing active extrusion. They are armored creatures, ranging from the size of a thumbnail to the size of a human hand, with multifaceted, sapphire-blue eyes that cast no reflection. Their bodies are a deep, rust-red chitin, and they move with a slow, deliberate crawl, a million-bodied tide flowing over and around the emergent architecture. They are the Chittering Chorus, the city's janitors and its lifeblood. They do not eat each other, nor do they consume anything an Earth-based biologist would recognize as food. They graze. With complex, grinding mandibles, they scrape the mineral dust from the black pillars and consume the phosphorescent residue left behind by the weeping fluid. They are lithovores, chemovores, deriving their energy from the raw geology and the waste products of the city's formation.

The scale is vast and open, yet feels claustrophobic. From a high vantage, one can see the Sprawl stretching for miles across the basin, a dense, dark forest of slowly growing spires disappearing into the thick, low-hanging mist. The horizon is not a clean line, but a jagged, saw-toothed silhouette of half-finished megastructures. But down within the canyons of the city, the density is absolute. The near-touching walls, the crawling carpet of fauna, the thick, wet air—it all presses in. The darkness is near-total, punctured only by thin, Cherenkov-blue seams of light that appear where the basalt pillars are actively growing, and by the collective, dim bioluminescence of the fauna's digestive tracts, which gives the ground a sickly, undulating, reddish glow.

Sound is everywhere. Over the foundational drone of the fauna is the constant trickle and viscous drip of the supercooled fluid, a sound like a world endlessly weeping. There are groans and deep resonant cracks, not of destruction, but of creation—the sound of a crystal lattice locking into place on a geological scale. The movement of the Chittering Chorus is not a skittering, but a slow, continuous grinding, the sound of a billion sets of legs on rough stone, a relentless, abrasive whisper that underlies everything.

The rules of this world are simple and strange.
1.  Life does not photosynthesize; it consumes the planet's own genesis.
2.  The distinction between geology and technology is a false one. The world is building itself according to a complex, embedded code.
3.  Motion is constant, but speed is almost nonexistent. The overwhelming dynamism of the place is one of mass and presence, not velocity. Change is measured in millimeters per hour, cycles per century.
4.  There is no light, only the faint glow of ongoing creation and consumption. To navigate here is to read the landscape by the light of its own metabolism.
5.  There is no silence. The sound is the audible state of existence, a drone you must learn to forget in order to think. It is the song of a city eating itself into being.
