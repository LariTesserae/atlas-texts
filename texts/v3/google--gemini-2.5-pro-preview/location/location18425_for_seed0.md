This is a world of noise and darkness.

The darkness is not an absence, but a presence. It is the thick, felted black of a deep cave, a medium as substantial as water. There is no sun, no moon, no star. The sky, when it is visible through the thick canopy and perpetual mist, is a uniform, lightless void. All illumination is generated from within the landscape itself.

The noise is the first and last thing you perceive. It is a physical pressure on the ears, a constant, multi-layered roar that vibrates through the soles of the feet and into the bones. The bass note is the world’s own engine: a deep, grinding groan of rock shifting under immense pressure, punctuated by the sharp, percussive crack of cooling crystalline formations and the high-pressure hiss of steam venting from a million unseen fissures. Layered over this geological foundation is the sound of life. It is the sound of a trillion insect-analogues, a deafening, unified sizzle of stridulating legs and vibrating membranes that fills every pocket of air. And cutting through that ceaseless sizzle are the calls of larger things: wet, bellowing roars, piercing whistles, and guttural clicks that echo across the low basin. The sound is so dense it feels like a solid thing, a wall you must push through. Conversation is impossible. Thought is difficult.

The air is warm, thick with humidity, and heavy with competing scents: the sharp, metallic tang of ozone near a steam vent, the rich sulfur of geothermal activity, the sweet, cloying perfume of decay, and an alien, unplaceable scent like bruised mint and hot stone. Moisture is constant. It does not rain in storms, but the air is so saturated that water weeps from the flora, beads on every surface, and runs in trickles down trunks and stalks, pooling in freshly-opened cracks in the ground. The world is perpetually damp, slick, and dripping.

You stand in a low-lying basin, a vast caldera whose distant, jagged rim is only occasionally glimpsed as a silhouette against a field of terrestrial light. The ground is a treacherous, chaotic knife-yard. This is a young landscape, one still in the violent throes of creation. Sharp-edged plates of black, glassy basalt jut from the earth, their surfaces still warm to the touch. Between them, forests of crystalline structures grow, pushing up from below in geological time measured in hours. These are not quartz; they are something else, lattices of brittle, resonant minerals that catch and amplify the bioluminescent light. They range from finger-sized shards to monolithic spires that tower into the canopy, their facets impossibly clean and sharp. The ground is unstable; tremors are constant, and new formations can split the earth open without warning, while old ones can shatter under internal stress, collapsing in a percussive rain of shattered crystal.

And on this violently active floor, life is not just present, it is riotously, oppressively lush. There is no photosynthesis here. The "vegetation" is a form of massive, chemosynthetic and radiotrophic life that thrives on the heat, minerals, and radiation from the planet's core. Great, fungus-like stalks, thick as ancient trees, rise into the dark. Their surfaces are slick with moisture and pulse with a soft, internal, sickly green or blue light. Some are capped with vast, undulating membranes that ripple and shimmer, casting moving patterns of light and shadow on the ground below. From their bases, and covering every inch of ground not made of fresh basalt, is a dense undergrowth: glowing mosses that trace the patterns of cracks in the rock, bulbous sacs that swell and contract with a wet, rhythmic sigh, and vine-like tendrils that glow with a fierce amber light as they snake through the crystal formations, their tips visibly drawing energy from the charged stone. The density is staggering; life is layered upon life, new growth strangling the old, everything near-touching, interwoven.

This forest of light is swarming with fauna. The air is a blizzard of winged creatures, most no larger than a thumb, their carapaces iridescent, their wings a transparent blur that contributes to the overwhelming background hum. They are the base of a food chain that is visible everywhere. Skittering across the glowing mosses are multi-limbed grazers that resemble crustaceans, their mandibles clicking as they scrape nutrients from the rock. Larger forms move in the middle-gloom between the giant stalks. You glimpse a herd of six-legged, armored beasts, their hides like obsidian, their heads low to the ground as they use broad, spade-like horns to clear rubble from a hissing steam vent, lapping at the mineral-rich condensation.

And there is intelligence here, of a sort. The "tech" of this world belongs to the animals. One of the larger predator species, a creature like a praying mantis the size of a bear, has been observed using tools. It will select a long, sharp shard of a fallen crystal spire and use it to pry open the carapaces of the armored grazers, its movements slow and deliberate. It does not craft the tool, merely selects it, but its use is undeniable. These tools are discarded after a single use, as the abundance of sharp-edged rock makes them entirely disposable. They are not building a civilization; they are simply surviving in a world that demands brutal efficiency.

To stand on a rise—a newly-formed pressure ridge, perhaps—is to resolve the paradox of this place. At your feet, the world is dense, claustrophobic, a tangled, glowing, screaming chaos. But your eyes can see for miles across the basin. It is an open vista of pure turbulence. An endless, rolling sea of pulsing, shifting, shimmering lights, a city of bioluminescence stretching to the dark horizon. You watch as a section of the forest a mile away flares into brilliant white light, then abruptly goes dark as a fissure opens beneath it, swallowing the glowing life and venting a colossal pillar of black steam. Moments later, a new network of faint, blue lights begins to trace the edges of the new crater as pioneer organisms take hold. Nothing is permanent. The landscape is a canvas being furiously painted and scraped clean, over and over, every minute of every day. The single, unifying rule of this world is violent, deafening, and constant creation.
