# The Unchanging Bell

You are inside something's last breath, held forever.

The water here is warm—blood-warm, the warmth of a body that stopped cooling and simply stayed. It fills everything. You float within it, suspended in a space no larger than a cathedral's apse, though *cathedral* is the wrong word. This is a bell. A single enormous bell, overturned and half-buried in silt that hasn't shifted in longer than counting matters, now full of water that entered once and never left.

The bell is bronze gone green-black, its interior surface covered in raised characters from a language that predates the concept of language—not writing but something earlier, the attempt to make marks mean. Primitive hammer-work, beautiful in its uncertainty. Someone made this with hands and heat and hope, and then the ocean took it, and then something else happened.

What happened is this: time stopped moving through this space while continuing everywhere else.

---

The water does not circulate. There is no current, no convection despite the warmth, no microscopic drift of particles. When you move—if you can move, if you're something that moves—the water parts around you and closes behind you and returns *instantly* to the exact configuration it held before. Not flowing back. Simply *being* back, as if your passage was a thought the water decided not to remember.

There is almost nothing here. The bell. The water. The silt below, which is not truly silt but powdered glass from volcanic origins so ancient the volcano has been forgotten by geology itself. Three stones rest on this glass-silt: one whiteite, one red, one the color of old teeth. They are arranged in a triangle. They have always been arranged in this triangle. When the bell was cast, they were already here, waiting in this configuration, in water that had not yet arrived to hold them.

And there is the sound.

---

The sound is a single sustained tone—the note the bell would ring if struck, except the bell has never been struck and will never be struck and *is not being struck now*. The tone simply exists, has always existed, fills the water with pressure-that-isn't-pressure, a vibration with no source and no decay. It is loud the way your own heartbeat is loud when you hold your breath too long. It does not come from anywhere. It does not go anywhere. It is the sound of a bell that learned to ring without beginning and so cannot end.

The tone should have harmonics. It doesn't. It should create standing waves in the water. It doesn't. It should make the silt dance, should blur your vision, should *do* something to the space it fills so completely. But the sound and the stillness have reached an agreement. The sound can be loud—extraordinarily loud, loud enough to feel in your teeth if you have teeth—and in exchange, it will change nothing, move nothing, disturb nothing.

This is not a truce. This is what both of them always were.

---

Light enters through a crack in the bell's crown where the hanging-loop has corroded through. The crack is perhaps three fingers wide. Through it comes ordinary sunlight, filtered through however much ocean lies above—not much ocean, you think, the light is too clean, too golden-warm—and this light illuminates the interior with the soft brightness of late afternoon. The late afternoon has lasted since before the bell sank. It will continue.

Sometimes—not often, but sometimes—something alive passes by the crack. A shadow-flicker, a suggestion of fin or tentacle or something else. Once, a single eye appeared in the gap: large, golden, curious, ancient. It looked in. It saw you, or saw the space where you float, or saw the three stones, or saw something you cannot perceive. Then it moved on, and the light returned to its uninterrupted state, and you cannot remember if the eye was there for a heartbeat or a century because duration has no meaning here.

The eye will return. It always returns. It never returns for the first time.

---

On the interior surface of the bell, among the proto-writing, someone has scratched newer marks with a primitive tool—bone or stone or sharpened shell. The scratches form a tally. Four hundred and seventy-three marks, grouped in fives, the work of someone who was counting something before they stopped.

The scratches are not old and not new. They exist in the same frozen present as everything else. Whoever made them is not here now, but *not here now* means nothing in a place where now has swallowed all other times.

Perhaps they escaped. Perhaps they dissolved. Perhaps they are you, and you've forgotten, and the counting was days or breaths or attempts to move one of the three stones, all of which failed, all of which changed nothing.

The water is warm. The tone continues. The light holds steady. The stones wait in their triangle.

Nothing here is wrong, exactly. Everything here has simply *agreed to be this way forever*, and the agreement holds, and will hold, and there is nothing in this small drowned bell that remembers how to want anything different.
