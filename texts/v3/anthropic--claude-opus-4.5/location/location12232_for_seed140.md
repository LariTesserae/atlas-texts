# The Breathing Hollow

Darkness here is not absence but presence — a warmth that presses against the skin like velvet, like the inside of a sleeping creature. You stand in a basin perhaps forty meters across, walled by slopes so anciently eroded they feel more grown than carved, their contours softened by epochs into the geometry of dream. The stone beneath your feet — you know it is stone — has been worn by time into something almost organic, undulating gently, holding shallow pools of blood-warm water that you feel before you see, your boots finding the edges of them by sound: the small *chup* of water displaced, the whisper of it settling back.

The air itself is wet, not falling but hovering, condensing against anything cooler than the ambient warmth — which is almost nothing. Water beads on your eyelashes. It pearls on the growth around you.

And there is growth, invisible growth, a lushness you know by the smell of chlorophyll and the softer smell beneath it, the fungal musk of things decomposing into other things. When you reach out, your hand finds fronds — ferns, perhaps, or something that has been ferns for so long it has forgotten any other shape. They are chest-high in places, their surfaces slightly sticky with the moisture they cannot release, their stems thick as your thumb and older than any tree you have known.

The walls of the hollow climb perhaps twenty meters before vanishing into darkness that your eyes cannot pierce. But the walls are not silent. They speak.

*Click.*

*Click-click.*

*Whirrrrrr.*

Something moves up there, many somethings, their sounds braiding together into a continuous architecture of noise: the scrape of chitinous bodies against wet stone, the fluting calls of creatures echolocating in frequencies that touch the edge of hearing, the sudden *rush* of wings that sound larger than they should be. The fauna of this place has inherited the darkness, has evolved into it or through it or alongside it so completely that light would be a violence to them. They are abundant in the way that stars are abundant — countless, but each separated by gulfs of warm black air, their presences registered by sound alone.

When one passes near, you feel the displacement of air against your face, the faint thermal signature of a body warmer than the warmth around it. Something with a wingspan your arms could not match. Something that clicks twice, directly beside your ear, and then is gone.

The water in the hollow does not flow. It waits. It has been waiting so long that waiting has become its nature. The pools are connected by the shallowest of channels, worn into the stone by millennia of the same slow exchange: water rising through capillary cracks in the basin floor, evaporating into the saturated air, condensing on the walls, trickling down to join the pools again. The cycle is so gradual that watching it would be like watching mountains breathe.

At the center of the hollow, barely distinguishable from the stone around it even by touch, stands a structure. You find it by accident — your hand brushing something too smooth, too regular, too *intentional*. A pillar, perhaps a meter thick, its surface marked with channels and whorls that feel almost like writing, almost like circuitry, almost like the growth-rings of something that counted its age in ways time cannot measure. When you press your palm flat against it, the stone — if it is stone — pulses faintly. Not light. Not sound. Something that moves through your bones like a question asked in a language older than questions.

The technology here, whatever it was, has settled into the landscape so completely that the landscape no longer remembers being interrupted by it. Other fragments wait in the ferns: a curved panel the size of your torso, worn into the perfect shape for water to collect in; a lattice of something metallic that the vegetation has grown through and around, incorporating it into a trellis; the suggestions of foundations beneath the pools, too regular to be natural, too submerged to examine. Whoever built here built for the long collapse. They knew the hollow would take their works back, and they designed them to be taken gracefully.

The warmth is persistent, sourceless, as if the hollow itself generates heat through some vast metabolic patience. The elevation reveals itself not through cold but through the quality of the air — thinner than it should be, sweeter, carrying scents from heights you cannot see: the iron tang of exposed rock, the green-black smell of mosses clinging to the upper slopes, and something else, something that might be ozone or might be the electrical signature of the creatures moving in their endless dark rounds above you.

Sound fills the space without crowding it. The clicking and calling of the aerial fauna layers over the drip and seep of water layers over your own breath layers over something deeper, something that might be the hollow itself settling another fraction of a millimeter into its final form. This is a place that has almost finished becoming. The erosion is nearly complete; the stone has nearly found its resting shape; the ecosystem has nearly achieved its equilibrium. Another million years and this basin will be a few meters shallower, the ferns a few centimeters taller, the creatures' calls pitched perhaps slightly higher.

But not yet. For now, it waits, warm and wet and alive in its darkness, patient as geology, breathing its slow breath while things with wings you will never see describe geometries in the air above your head and the ancient engines of someone else's wanting pulse their faint questions into your bones.

You could stay here a long time. The hollow would not mind. It has learned to hold things gently in its warmth, to let them become part of its long slow dream of finishing. When you finally leave — if you leave — it will remember you the way it remembers the builders: as a brief warmth that passed through, that added a single drop to one of its pools, that stirred the ferns once, that made the creatures above go quiet for a single held breath before resuming their patient, clicking song.
