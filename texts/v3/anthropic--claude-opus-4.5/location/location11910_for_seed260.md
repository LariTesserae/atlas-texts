# The Screaming Spires of the Unfinished

The mountains are still being born, and they are *loud*.

Not with the thunder of eruption or the grinding of tectonic plates—those would be sounds with cause and effect, sounds that belong to a world that has finished deciding what it is. These peaks scream because they are making themselves out of nothing, and the nothing *objects*.

---

You stand on a ridge of something that might become granite, if granite ever learns how to hold still here. The stone beneath your feet is cool, almost cold, and it sweats a fine crystalline dust that catches what little light exists. There is no sun. There is no sky, exactly—there is a dim luminescence that seems to emanate from the idea of "above," pewter-gray and sourceless, casting shadows that don't quite align with anything that might be casting them.

The ridge is one of dozens, perhaps hundreds, rising in a vast amphitheater of peaks that stretch toward that non-sky. They are impossibly tall—not merely high, but *excessive*, spires that thin to needle-points and then continue thinning past the point where structural integrity should mean anything. Some of them twist. Some of them fork like frozen lightning. One, far to what your mind insists is the east, grows in a perfect spiral, and at its apex, it is still visibly *growing*, extruding new rock from its tip like a candle being formed in reverse.

And everywhere, the sound.

It is not a single tone but a chorus—a vast, dissonant harmony of frequencies that press against your skull from the inside. Some of it is deep enough to feel rather than hear, a subsonic rumble that vibrates in your chest cavity and makes your teeth ache. Some of it is piercing, needle-thin whistles that seem to come from the growing edges of things. And beneath it all, there is something that might be voices, or might be the stone trying to learn what voices are. Words that almost resolve into meaning and then shatter into pure vibration.

The locals—if you can call them that—left their marks here. Primitive stone shelters huddle in the valleys between spires, built from rocks that predate this place's ongoing argument with existence. The shelters are half-buried now, not by snow or soil but by the ambient *becoming*—new mineral formations growing over them like frost, incorporating them into the landscape's endless draft of itself. Inside one, you find tools: chisels of greenite, mallets with handles of something like hardite bone (though there are no animals here, have never been animals here—the bones must come from somewhere else, traded or carried or dreamed into being). The tools are for carving, but the carvings they've made are strange: not images or symbols but *dampening patterns*, geometric spirals etched into flat stones and propped against the walls.

When you touch one, the screaming softens—not much, but enough to think.

---

The vegetation is the strangest part. It shouldn't exist. Nothing should grow here without water, without proper light, without the stability that life requires. But the valley floors between the spires are carpeted in something lush and impossible: forests of glass-leaved ferns that ring like bells when the sound-pressure shifts, meadows of fiber-crystal that photosynthesizes the ambient noise itself, vast spreading organisms that look like moss and act like moss but are made of something closer to frozen sound given tangible form. They grow dense where the screaming peaks, sparse where the dampening stones have been placed.

The plants are cool to the touch—cooler than the air, which itself hovers somewhere between autumn chill and the edge of frost. They do not move when you brush against them, but they *respond*: their crystalline structures shifting slightly, refracting the dim light into brief prismatic flares, absorbing some frequency of the endless noise and converting it into... what? Growth. Patience. The slow business of being alive in a place that hasn't decided if it will allow such things.

You walk among them for what might be hours. Time moves strangely here—not broken, but *distracted*, like a river that keeps pausing to consider alternate courses. The light doesn't change. The sound doesn't diminish. But the landscape shifts at the edges of your perception: a spire that was there now isn't, another taking its place, the transition so smooth you can't be certain anything happened at all.

---

There are pathways worn between the shelters, and you follow them because the alternative is navigating the raw unfinished, where the ground might decide to become something else beneath your feet. The paths are marked with small cairns of the dampening stones, their carved spirals creating corridors of relative quiet—the sound here merely painful rather than annihilating. Someone, somewhen, learned to live in this impossible place. They built their shelters. They carved their silence. They cultivated the sound-gardens, perhaps, tending the crystal-ferns and the noise-moss.

But they are gone now, or were never here the way you are here, or exist in some parallel tense that this place allows. Their tools remain. Their shelters remain. Their paths remain.

And the mountains keep screaming, keep growing, keep being born.

---

At the center of the valley, where the paths converge, there is a structure that might be a temple or might be a machine or might be both. It is built from the dampening stones, hundreds of them, carved and stacked in a pattern that creates a pocket of near-silence at its heart. Inside, the quiet is so sudden and complete that it feels like a wound—your ears ring with the absence, your body still vibrating with phantom frequencies.

On the floor, scratched into the stone in that same primitive hand, a single image: a mountain, complete, no longer screaming.

Below it, a word in a language that doesn't exist yet, in letters that keep rearranging themselves until you stop looking directly at them.

It might say *"waiting."*

It might say *"soon."*

It might say *"help us finish."*

---

Outside, the spires continue their eternal emergence. The crystal-ferns chime. The nothing screams back at the something trying to become. And slowly, so slowly you could watch for lifetimes and barely see it, the mountains rise.
