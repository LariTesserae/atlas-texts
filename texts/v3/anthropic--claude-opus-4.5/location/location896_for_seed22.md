# The Congregation of Empty Bells

The city stretches across the basin like a thought that forgot to finish itself—towers and arcades and spiraling ramps that rise from the wet lowland jungle, built from something that resembles milk-glass and holds light the way a bruise holds memory. The structures are vast. They dwarf the canopy. But they are hollow, all of them, and the wind that moves through this place finds every opening, every shaft, every chambered void, and *speaks*.

The sound is constant and immense. Not chaotic—*orchestral*. The city was designed for this, you understand after a while. The buildings are instruments. Their walls thin where they need to resonate, thick where they need to anchor. Openings face the prevailing winds at calculated angles. The result is a chord that has been sustaining itself for longer than anyone can say—a drone that lives in your chest, in your teeth, that makes the moisture on leaves tremble in patterns too regular to be random.

You feel it before you truly hear it, because hearing implies you could stop.

---

The jungle below and between the structures is excessive, compensatory, as if the vegetation wanted to fill what the builders left empty. Ferns the color of verdigris climb the milk-glass walls. Vines thick as your arm loop between towers, forming aerial highways that sway in the wind-current. The canopy floor is damp—not wet, but never quite dry. Condensation beads on every surface at dawn and dusk. Small streams appear after the mists roll through, running for an hour or two before sinking back into soil that seems to drink reluctantly, already sated.

The warmth is vegetal, close. It smells of chlorophyll and the faint mineral tang of the building-material, which sweats something alkaline when the humidity peaks.

---

The fauna have adapted to the sound in ways that suggest deep time—or something faster than evolution should allow.

There are the echo-moths, for instance: palm-sized creatures with wings that vibrate at frequencies meant to cancel out specific harmonics. They cluster on the towers in patches of relative silence, and other creatures cluster around them. You see this ecosystem repeated across the city—nodes of quiet amid the overwhelming chord, each one a micro-habitat. Predators have learned to hunt the boundaries, where prey move between sound and its absence and lose their bearings for just a moment.

Other creatures have gone the opposite direction. The bell-singers are something like birds, something like bats, and they *harmonize*. They add their voices to the city's chord, finding niches in the frequency spectrum the way other animals find niches in space. Some of them are barely visible—dark shapes wheeling through the dim amber light that filters through the canopy and refracts through the milk-glass. Others roost openly on the high spirals, throats bulging with sustained tones that complement the wind-song.

Below, in the wet understory, long-bodied creatures move through the fern-shadows. They have no ears that you can see. They navigate by vibration, by the way the great chord bounces off obstacles, and they are so perfectly attuned to the city's voice that they freeze in the rare moments of silence—confused, vulnerable, waiting for the world to start again.

---

The technology that built this place is not visible in the way you might expect. No machines. No obvious infrastructure. But the buildings *do things* that glass and stone should not. The walls are warm to the touch regardless of air temperature. The ramps and walkways repair themselves—you can find places where the jungle has cracked the material, and beside the cracks, new growth, smooth and seamless, slowly closing the wound. The resonance chambers adjust. You notice this over hours: a tower that was producing a low thrum at midday has shifted to something higher by evening, matching changes in the wind.

The city is listening to itself and tuning.

There is no one here to listen with it. The emptiness is profound. You can walk for hours through arcade after arcade, through plazas where mist pools and ferns grow through the joins in the paving, and find no evidence of occupation—recent or ancient. The bell-singers roost where inhabitants might have lived. The long-bodied understory creatures drink from basins that might have been fountains.

If there were builders, they left nothing behind but the instrument of their city, playing for an audience of wings and leaves and the things that have learned to live inside an unending sound.

---

And yet: the city is not still. The processes are ongoing. The repairs continue. The tuning adjusts. The chord evolves—slowly, over days, over seasons, in response to variables you cannot track. The jungle pushes in and the city pushes back, not violently but persistently. The condensation cycles drive the vegetation which drives the fauna which affects the acoustics which the city corrects for.

You begin to wonder if the emptiness is the point. If the city was always meant to play only for itself. If the builders left, or dissolved, or became something that doesn't need to occupy space anymore—something that only needs to *hear*.

The light dims further as clouds roll across the basin's edge. The chord shifts down by a half-step, finding a new equilibrium.

The bell-singers adjust their harmonies, one by one, until the sound is whole again.
