# The Rookery of the Breathing Cliffs

The ledge is perhaps thirty feet across at its widest point, a shelf of young basalt jutting from a cliff face that drops away into darkness below and rises into darkness above. The rock is sharp-edged, recently cooled, still holding the memory of its emergence from somewhere deeper—you can see where gas bubbles froze mid-escape, leaving the surface pocked and grabbing. Your hands, if you touched it, would come away scraped.

This is a small place. A room carved by nothing but the mountain's own becoming. And it is full.

---

They roost here in the hundreds. *Thousands*, perhaps, though counting is impossible in this darkness. The birds—if birds they are—press against each other on every available surface, their bodies dense as scales on a fish. They line the back wall where the ledge meets the cliff. They cluster on the spurs of rock that jut upward like broken fingers. They hang from crevices overhead, gripping with feet that bend in too many places. Where the ledge narrows to nothing at either end, they simply stack, the lower ones flattened by the weight of those above, patient as stones.

They are pale. Not white—pale, like something that has never needed color. Their feathers, if feathers they are, seem less grown than *secreted*, lying flat against bodies that carry no fat, no softness. Their eyes are enormous, taking up most of their skulls, and they do not blink. Each eye contains no iris you can discern, only a darkness that drinks what little light exists here, and something behind that darkness that *calculates*.

They do not move. Or—they move so slowly that it registers only as a gradual pressure shift in your peripheral vision. You look away for a moment, look back, and three more of them are facing you. Their heads have rotated perhaps forty degrees. You did not see it happen.

---

The sound is not what you expect. No harsh cries, no flutter of wings. Instead, there is breathing.

Thousands of small lungs, expanding and contracting in near-unison. A wet, rhythmic susurration, like something enormous inhaling through a membrane. The synchronization is imperfect—there are ripples in the pattern, waves of slight delay that move through the colony like shivers. Sometimes a section falls out of phase entirely, their breathing stuttering into something individual, almost panicked, before being pulled back into the larger rhythm.

Beneath the breathing, you become aware of another sound: the faint, continuous *tick* of claws adjusting on stone. Micro-movements. Thousands of tiny corrections, happening too slowly to see but fast enough to hear when multiplied by numbers. And beneath that, lower still, something that might be the mountain itself settling, or might be the birds making sounds in registers you weren't meant to parse.

---

The air is cool and holds moisture that doesn't fall. It clings. Your skin dampens within minutes of arrival, though you cannot identify the source—no springs visible, no ice overhead. The water seems to condense from the breath itself, from the exhalations of all these pale bodies, accumulating on the rock until it runs in thin streams toward the ledge's edge and disappears into the void below.

Where the water pools in depressions, a thin crust has formed. Biological, though you couldn't say what organism. It glows faintly—not enough to illuminate, only enough to prove that illumination is possible, that light was once a thing that existed. The crust is the only reason you can see the birds at all. The only reason you know how many there are.

---

Sparse vegetation clings to cracks in the basalt: lichens of a type that grows only in the presence of this particular colony, feeding on what they excrete, taking on a faintly iridescent quality that serves no purpose you can name. A single shoot—some hardy alpine thing that wandered up from below—has pushed through a gap near your feet. It's been growing for perhaps a century. It has achieved six inches of height. The birds have not touched it, but they have grown *around* it, leaving a small pocket of emptiness in their packed ranks, a respect or a wariness you don't understand.

---

The rules of this place:

Move slowly, or they will not see you as one thing. Move quickly, and they will see you as many things. It is unclear which is worse.

The breathing will sync with yours if you stay long enough. You will feel your lungs expand and contract with theirs, and you will not be able to say whether you are breathing with them or they with you.

Nothing here changes quickly. The ledge crumbles at the pace of decades. The colony shifts its mass at the pace of seasons. But they are *watching*—always watching—in a way that suggests they are waiting for something that will happen very fast indeed. A single moment they have been anticipating for geologic time.

The darkness is not complete. It is *withheld*. Something in this place could provide light, if it chose to. It does not choose to.

---

You are not the first creature to find this ledge. Small bones—not from the birds, from something else—line the back crevices, gnawed clean by nothing visible, arranged in patterns that might be meaningful or might be the random accumulation of bodies falling the same way over and over. Some of the bones are human-shaped. Some are not. None are recent enough to concern you.

None are old enough to comfort you either.
