# The Respirating Foundries

The plateau heaves. Not violently—slowly, rhythmically, like something vast drawing breath across geological time. You stand on what appears to be compacted plant matter, centuries of growth pressed into a surface dense as coal but warm, almost feverish, radiating heat upward through your boots. The darkness is near-total, broken only by the sullen orange glow of vents that open and close in the biomass beneath you, exhaling gusts of heated air that smell of chlorophyll and copper.

This is the Fundament—a high tableland that extends for leagues in every direction, its edges lost in the perpetual dark. Once, perhaps, there were mountains here. The erosion of ages has ground them to nothing, and in their absence the plants came, and the plants *built*.

---

Everything here is vegetation. Everything here is also machinery.

The distinction ceased to matter long ago. Stalks rise in bundled columns, woody and segmented, functioning as smoke-stacks and exhaust pipes for processes happening far below in the root-warrens. Leaves the size of doors hang in layered banks, and they *move*—not with wind, for there is none, but with purpose, opening and closing in sequences that regulate the flow of heat through the living architecture. Tendrils thick as bridge cables span the spaces between growth-towers, and along them travel pods of compressed organic matter, nutrients or signals or something else entirely, ferried from node to node in an economy you cannot parse.

The plants drink no water. Whatever chemistry drives them, it pulls from the air itself—from the volcanic gases that seep up through fissures in the ancient bedrock, from minerals dissolved in the constant exhalations of the Fundament. Their leaves are waxy, almost metallic, and when you touch one it leaves a residue on your fingers like machine oil.

---

The sound is sparse but present. A background hum that rises and falls—the collective respiration of the plateau, millions of stomata opening and closing in near-synchrony. Occasionally: a deep thunk from somewhere below, like a piston engaging. The creak of a growth-tower shifting its weight. The wet sliding sound of a tendril retracting.

But mostly silence. The silence of a factory floor at night, when the workers have gone but the machines continue their slow cycles.

---

You walk between structures that defy easy naming. Here: a bulb the size of a granary, its surface ribbed like industrial piping, pulsing with faint bioluminescence that doesn't reach far enough to qualify as light. There: a lattice of interwoven stems forming something like scaffolding, or perhaps a cooling apparatus, heat shimmering off its surfaces. The density is overwhelming—everywhere you look, more growth, more structure, packed so tightly that navigation means finding the narrow maintenance-corridors that the plants have left between their workings.

These corridors exist. That's the strange part. The Fundament *accommodates* movement through it, as if the whole system anticipates the need for something to walk its pathways, to witness its operations. The paths are floored with that same compressed plant-matter, worn smooth in places by passage. By what? Nothing animal lives here. Nothing with legs or eyes or hunger.

Yet the paths are worn.

---

The turbulence is constant but subtle. Growth-towers collapse and regrow elsewhere within hours—you can see the evidence of recent falls, massive vegetative structures toppled and already being reabsorbed into the substrate, their material reclaimed for new construction. New stalks punch upward through the compacted floor, uncoiling with visible speed, and you learn quickly to check where you stand. The Fundament is not hostile, exactly, but it is *busy*, and it will build through you if you stand still too long.

The heat comes in waves, tied to the breathing rhythm of the plateau. Hotter when the vents exhale, cooler—merely sweltering—when they draw in. Your skin prickles constantly. Sweat evaporates before it can bead.

No water anywhere. Not in pools, not in the plants themselves when you break a stem—only that oily residue, that non-aqueous chemistry. The Fundament runs on something else. Fire and mineral. Volcanic patience. The slow-burning metabolism of a world that forgot what thirst means.

---

In the distance—far enough that perspective flattens, near enough that you can see its outline against the orange-lit darkness—a structure rises higher than the others. A central processing node, perhaps. A cathedral of wound stems and layered leaf-banks, venting heat from its crown in a perpetual thermal plume. The Fundament's heart, or one of them. The paths seem to lead toward it, or away from it—the grammar of the place is unclear, whether pilgrimage or evacuation.

You could walk there. The paths would take you. The plants would let you pass, as they let all things pass that do not interfere with the work.

What is the work? You don't know. The Fundament doesn't explain itself. It only builds, and breathes, and tends its ancient, waterless fire.
