# The Woven Flats

Everything here was made. That is the first thing you understand, and the understanding comes slowly, because the making is so old and so complete that it has forgotten itself.

The ground beneath your feet is braided. Thousands of years ago—or perhaps thousands of years from now, the direction is unclear—someone wove this plain into existence from fibers that no longer exist anywhere else. The weave is tight, waterproof in some places, deliberately porous in others. Where it has grown porous, water rises through it in slow permanent seeps, and the puddles that form are rectangular, following the underlying grid of the weave. The water is blood-warm and tastes of reeds, though no reeds grow here. The color of the ground shifts as you walk: ochre, rust, the faded orange of old terra cotta, occasional stripes of deep indigo where the original dyes still hold.

The sky is impossibly bright, sourceless white light that casts no shadows. Nothing here casts a shadow. You can look directly upward without pain, but you see nothing—no sun, no clouds, only luminous white that extends forever or stops just above your head, you cannot tell which. The heat radiates from everywhere equally. It soaks into your skin, into the braided ground, into the standing water. Nothing evaporates. The warmth simply *is*, as fundamental as the weave itself.

---

Where vegetation grows, it grows from seams. Someone sewed the vegetation here. You can see the stitches if you look closely—great loops of some fibrous material, now indistinguishable from the stems that push through them. The plants are thick-leaved, waxy, deep green shading to almost black at their centers. They cluster in deliberate arrangements: circles of twenty, lines that run for miles, single specimens placed at precise intervals across the empty expanses. Each plant is lush, overfull, bursting with more leaves than any stem should support. But between these arranged growths, the braided ground stretches bare and hot and still for long distances. You could walk for an hour and pass only three clusters of vegetation, each one dense as a fever dream, each one surrounded by emptiness.

The plants do not grow. They do not die. They were placed here in this fullness, and they remain.

---

The animals move across the weave with careful purpose.

There are the Carriers: long-bodied creatures like soft-furred salamanders the size of dogs, their bellies perpetually damp from the seeps. They travel in loose groups of four or five, never closer than thirty feet to one another, and they carry things in their mouths—small woven pouches, spheres of compacted plant fiber, objects you cannot identify. They are going somewhere. They have always been going somewhere. You could follow them for days and never see them arrive.

There are the Weavers: birds with needles instead of beaks, or beaks that have become needles through slow transformation, or needles that have grown feathers and learned to fly. They perch along the seam-lines of the vegetation, their heads cocked, listening to something below the threshold of your hearing. Occasionally one will stitch—a quick darting motion, needle passing through the braided ground, pulling through a thread you cannot see. Then it goes still again. The stitches they make are repairs to something, or additions to something, or simply the thing they have always done, their purpose worn smooth by repetition until only the motion remains.

There are the Sleepers: mounded shapes in the distance, too far away to see clearly in the bright directionless light. They might be large animals curled in rest. They might be structures. They might be the same thing. They do not move. You have a sense that they have not moved since the weaving began, that they are part of the original design, ballast to hold the plain in place against something.

And there are smaller things: insects with geometric bodies, their segments arranged in patterns that repeat at every scale; translucent creatures that swim through the rectangular puddles, their bodies long and flat like living ribbons; things that burrow through the weave itself, leaving trails of tighter threading behind them.

The animals are abundant, but they are *scattered*—each occupying its own portion of the vastness, each separated from the others by stretches of hot, bright, silent ground.

---

The silence is the hardest thing.

Not true silence—there are sounds. The soft drip of water rising through porous weave. The distant *tick tick tick* of a Weaver making repairs. The brush of wind across your clothes, though you cannot feel wind on your skin. But these sounds exist in isolation. They do not layer. They do not combine. Each sound emerges into the quiet, exists for its duration, and then the quiet closes over it again as if it had never been.

Your own breathing sounds like a violation. Your footsteps on the braided ground—a subtle creak, a faint compression—seem to occur in a different world than the sounds that belong here.

---

You walk, and the landscape extends.

Here is a place where the weave buckles upward in a low ridge, the ground folded like fabric gathered on a drawstring. The ridge is no taller than your waist, but it runs in a straight line to the horizon in both directions. On one side, the seeps are denser; the vegetation clusters are larger, more frequent. On the other side, the weave is drier, the plants sparser, the Carriers fewer. Someone made this distinction. Someone built a border from gathered thread.

Here is a place where a circular depression has been woven into the ground, a shallow bowl perhaps a quarter mile across. The bottom is a single puddle, warm and still, its surface perfectly reflecting the white sky above. Ribbon-creatures slide through it in slow circuits, and at the exact center, a single plant grows from a raised seam-mound—a specimen twice the size of the others, its leaves so dark they appear to absorb light rather than reflect it. Something important happened here, or will happen here, or is always happening here in a tense that does not map to linear time.

Here is a place where the weave has come undone. Not eroded—the ground is too young for erosion, too recently made. But *unwoven*, deliberately, by hands or by beaks or by something else. The loose fibers hang in the bright air, not falling, not moving, frozen in the moment of their unraveling. Through the gap you can see *underneath* the woven plain: a darkness that the sourceless light does not reach, a suggestion of depth, a hint of the substrate upon which the plain was built. Or of the absence where a substrate should be. The Weavers cluster thickly at the edges of this wound, stitching, stitching, but they do not close it. They stitch the edges into shapes instead—spirals, nested squares, patterns that might be writing. They are not repairing. They are *annotating*.

---

This is the strange thing, the thing that makes your thoughts slip sideways: nothing here is changing, but everything here was *once* changed. The weave did not grow. It was woven. The plants did not seed. They were placed. The animals did not evolve. They were made, or trained, or dreamed into being by minds that no longer exist here.

And yet.

The Carriers carry. The Weavers weave. The water seeps. The light shines. The system continues, stable as mathematics, frozen in an eternal maintenance of itself. Whoever made this place—however they made it, with whatever primitive hands and simple tools and incomprehensible vision—they made it to *last*. They made it to persist without them. They wove their absence into the design.

You can walk for days. The horizon never comes. The light never changes. The heat never breaks. You will find more ridges, more depressions, more places where the weave thins or thickens or changes color. You will find more Sleepers in the distance, more Weavers at their endless stitching, more Carriers bearing their burdens toward destinations that recede as you approach.

And sometimes—if you stand very still, and listen to the silence between sounds, and let your eyes go soft in the bright directionless light—you will feel it. The sense that the whole plain is *breathing*. Not moving, not changing, but breathing: a vast slow respiration that takes centuries for each inhale, each exhale. The breath of a made thing that does not know it was made. The breath of a world that has forgotten it has seams.

You cannot stay here. But you cannot leave either, not easily. The weave extends in all directions, and the Carriers are watching now, four or five of them scattered across the middle distance, their soft bodies low to the warm ground, their mouths full of purposes you do not understand.

You begin to walk. The braided ground creaks beneath your feet. The silence closes over the sound.
