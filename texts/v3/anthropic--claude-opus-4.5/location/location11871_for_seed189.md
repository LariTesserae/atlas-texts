# The Hollow That Grows Itself

Beneath the meadow, beneath the root-mat, beneath the clay and the forgotten water table, there is a cavity in the earth that should not exist. It is the size of a small chapel, oblong and irregular, and it is entirely alive.

The walls are plant.

Not plants growing on walls—the walls themselves are plant. Pale rootflesh pressed so dense it has become structural, a weave of living tissue that formed this pocket in the dark through some process that geology does not recognize. The roots are thick as rope, thin as thread, packed in overlapping layers that breathe. You can see them moving if you watch long enough. The whole chamber expands and contracts in a rhythm too slow to be respiration, too fast to be growth. Something between the two. Something that has no name yet.

The air is warm and wet. Condensation beads on every surface and runs in thin rivulets down the curved walls, collecting in shallow depressions in the root-floor before being reabsorbed. The chamber drinks its own sweat. The humidity carries a smell that is green and mineral and faintly sweet, like sap mixed with cave-water, like breathing into cupped hands full of moss.

---

There is sound here. Too much sound.

The roots creak against each other as the walls shift—a wet wooden groaning that rises and falls in overlapping waves. Somewhere in the mass of tissue, fluid moves through channels: a rushing, gurgling music that suggests the chamber has circulation, has something like a pulse distributed through its structure. Drips fall from the ceiling in irregular patterns, striking pools with sounds too resonant for their size, as though the chamber's acoustics follow rules that have nothing to do with physics.

And beneath it all, a hum. Not mechanical. Not quite organic. A vibration that seems to originate from everywhere at once, rising from the root-floor and falling from the root-ceiling, meeting itself in the warm wet air. It shifts in pitch without pattern. Sometimes it sounds almost like speech slowed beyond recognition. Sometimes it sounds like the earth itself is trying to remember a song.

---

The space is empty. Devastatingly empty.

For something so alive, so actively being, the chamber contains almost nothing *in* it. No furniture, no debris, no accumulated sediment. The root-floor is bare and clean, slightly yielding underfoot, warm to the touch. The air holds moisture but no particulate. There should be insects, fungi, the small busy lives that fill any organic space, but there is only the chamber and its own processes.

This emptiness feels wrong. It feels intentional. As though the space is *waiting* for something to fill it, or as though it has already consumed whatever once tried.

---

Light comes from within.

The root-walls emit a soft phosphorescence, a warm gold-green that pulses faintly with the chamber's breathing rhythm. It is bright enough to see by, bright enough to cast no shadows because the light comes from every direction at once. In certain places, the glow intensifies—nodes where the root-tissue is denser, or where some internal process runs hotter. These bright patches move through the walls like slow thought, like signals propagating through a nervous system too alien to recognize.

Sometimes the light flickers. Sometimes whole sections of wall go dark for a heartbeat before returning. When this happens, the hum changes pitch, and the condensation seems to fall faster, and there is a moment where the chamber feels less like a place and more like the inside of something *deciding*.

---

The rules here are wrong.

A dropped stone does not fall straight down. It curves, gently, following contours in the air that have no visible source. Sound echoes too long, or not at all, or arrives before its source. The warm wet air seems thicker in some places, resisting movement, while in others it pulls you forward as though the chamber wants you closer to its walls.

The roots grow. You can see it happening if you focus on one spot—slow enough to be imperceptible, fast enough that you know you're not imagining it. The chamber is remaking itself in real time, fibers weaving and unweaving, the shape of the space subtly different moment to moment. It is a pocket of becoming. It is a place still deciding what it is.

---

And there is something that lives here.

You glimpse it rarely—a movement in the root-wall, a shape pressing outward from inside the tissue before withdrawing. It might be a creature that has learned to swim through the dense plant-matter. It might be a part of the chamber itself, a mobile organ performing some function you cannot guess. It moves too fast for the eye to track, leaving only impressions: pale flesh, something like limbs, a sense of curiosity that you feel rather than see.

The chamber watches you. The chamber has always been watching you. You are the first thing to enter it that did not grow here, and it is very, very interested in what you are.
