# The Nursery of Wrong Angles

Beneath a thin shelf of limestone—no more than arm's length down—there is a pocket of space that should not exist. You find it by following the condensation: droplets that bead on the underside of rocks and roll *upward*, gathering in defiance of gravity until they slip through a crack barely wide enough for a child's fist. But the crack opens into more than it should contain.

The chamber is small—perhaps the size of a modest room, if rooms could be described as *unfinished*. The walls are still deciding what they are. In some places, the stone has the wet-clay softness of something not yet set, dimpling under the pressure of nothing at all. In others, crystalline structures are mid-emergence, their facets incomplete, their angles not quite matching any geometry you remember learning. The light comes from everywhere and nowhere—bright as midday, shadowless, sourceless—as if illumination itself is one of the things being born here and hasn't yet learned to behave.

Scattered across the floor are the remnants of someone's attempt to understand this place: a surveyor's tripod with two legs (there was never a third, the brass fittings simply *stop* where the third should begin), a leather notebook swollen with moisture whose pages are blank except for the imprints of words that were never written, a single boot standing upright with no footprints leading to or from it. These objects feel ancient and brand new simultaneously—artifacts of an investigation that is always about to begin.

The sparse vegetation grows in clusters that relocate when you're not watching. Pale rootless mosses. A single fern that unfurls its fronds in the wrong direction, tip-to-stem. Something that might become lichen, or might become something else entirely—it hasn't committed yet, and neither has the stone it clings to.

But the *fauna*—

They are everywhere, these creatures of the threshold state. Translucent beetles with shells that haven't finished hardening flow across the clay-stone in rivers of soft clicking, their legs leaving impressions that fill themselves in moments later. Pale salamanders with too many developmental stages visible at once—gill and lung, tail-fin and digit—slip through pockets of water that hover at odd heights throughout the chamber. Moths with wings still crumpled from emergence circle the sourceless light, though they emerged from no cocoon anyone could find; they simply *began*, mid-flight, from folded paper into struggling life.

Something larger breathes in a corner where the angles refuse to resolve. It might be a mammal. It might be several mammals, or the idea of one, or what comes before the idea. It watches with eyes that are still learning to see, and its watching has a weight, a pressure, a *becoming*.

The quiet here is the quiet of growth: cells dividing, crystals accreting, the wet sound of potential condensing into form. Occasionally, a droplet completes its upward journey and *plinks* against something overhead that isn't quite there yet. Occasionally, one of the salamanders makes a sound that isn't quite a call—more like a question posed in a language still inventing its own grammar.

The walls creep inward by fractions you can't measure, not shrinking the space but *specifying* it, narrowing down what kind of chamber this will be. The process has been happening for longer than the chamber has existed. It will continue after you leave, after you forget, after the survey equipment finishes never having been brought here.

You cannot stay long. Not because it's dangerous—though it might be—but because you are too *finished*, too *decided*, and your presence creates a kind of friction against all this unresolved becoming. The salamanders begin to avoid you. The moths spiral wider. The breathing thing in the corner closes its unready eyes.

The crack will let you out. The condensation will continue its wrong-way journey. And below you, in a space the size of a small room that contains more than it should, the nursery will go on assembling itself from raw possibility, one unfinished creature at a time, bright and damp and full of quiet almost-life waiting to learn what it will be.
