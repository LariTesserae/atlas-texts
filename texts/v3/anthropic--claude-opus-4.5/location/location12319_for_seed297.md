# The Stillgarden of Unwater

There is no soil here, and yet everything grows.

The basin extends for kilometers in every direction, a shallow depression in what might once have been seafloor—or might have been generated three seconds ago and given the *memory* of having been seafloor. The ground is crystallized salt, bone-white and faintly luminous, cracked into hexagonal tiles that fit together with geometric precision no erosion has yet disturbed. Each tile is perhaps two meters across. Each tile is exactly two meters across. You notice this, and the noticing makes it more true.

From the salt rise the plants—except they are not plants, not in any way that involves water or chlorophyll or cellular respiration. They are *growth-forms*, structures that occupy the ecological niche of vegetation through means that would make a botanist weep and then forget how to weep.

The dominant species—if species is the word—resembles bamboo reimagined by something that understood the concept of "tall" and "segmented" but had never encountered matter. The stalks are translucent, filled with slowly churning interference patterns in violet and gold. They grow in dense groves, so close together that the light fractures between them into permanent rainbows that hang motionless in the warm air. The stalks do not photosynthesize. They do not metabolize. They simply *are*, drawing their persistence from some substrate beneath physics, some layer of reality where "alive" and "dead" are not yet differentiated.

Between the stalks, the understory riots.

Fern-analogues with fronds of frozen sound—visible as rippling distortions, tangible as the faint pressure of a note you cannot quite hear. Moss-things that are less organisms than they are *agreements*—patches of salt crystal that have consented to be soft, to be green, to participate in the aesthetic of moss without committing to its biology. Flowers that are holes, precise circular absences in the visual field through which you can see—not darkness, not light, but the *concept* of elsewhere, formless and patient.

The warmth here has no source. The sky is white, not clouded but simply *white*, as if someone painted a ceiling and forgot to add depth. The light comes from everywhere equally; nothing casts shadows. Nothing has ever cast shadows here. The hexagonal salt tiles show no wear, no weathering, no gradient of age from center to edge. The growth-forms do not compete, do not die back, do not spread. Every plant is where it has always been, where it will always be, growing to precisely the height it is meant to grow and then—not stopping, because stopping implies a process that continues—simply *being finished*.

You walk between the groves, and your footsteps make sounds: the crunch of salt, the whisper of your clothing, your own breathing. These are the only sounds. The stillgarden does not acknowledge you with noise. A beetle-shaped thing sits on a fern-frond of frozen sound, motionless, its carapace shifting through mother-of-pearl iridescence at a rate too slow to perceive directly. You watch it for what feels like ten minutes. It does not move. It is not dead. It is simply not *in motion*, a creature that experiences time as a single held note rather than a melody.

Deeper in, the groves grow denser, the translucent stalks pressing so close that walking between them requires turning sideways. The interference patterns inside them grow more complex, suggesting depth that cannot exist in objects so narrow—suggesting chambers, architectures, entire *rooms* visible through the plant-flesh like blueprints drawn in light. You understand, with a certainty that arrives from outside your own thoughts, that these patterns are not stored in the stalks. The stalks are windows. You are seeing structures that exist *somewhere else*, vast and intricate and utterly still, frozen mid-construction or perhaps mid-collapse or perhaps in that instant between intention and action where a thing has been decided but not yet done.

The technology here is the place itself. Reality has been engineered at a level beneath atoms, beneath quarks, beneath the fields from which particles condense. Someone—something—reached down into the source code and wrote *garden*, wrote *growth*, wrote *warm* and *green* and *forever*, and the universe compiled their poetry into crystal and light and impossible vegetation. There are no machines because the entire basin *is* a machine. There are no buildings because to build here would be redundant; the stillgarden is already architecture, already designed, already *made* with more precision than any structure you have ever seen.

No water. The plants do not need water because they do not *need*—need implies lack, implies time, implies a future in which sustenance must be sought. Here there is only presence, only the eternal momentary now that stretches across the kilometers of white salt and prismatic growth.

At the center of the basin—you have been walking toward it without deciding to, following a path that exists as surely as the tiles beneath your feet—the groves open into a clearing. The salt here is not cracked into hexagons but perfectly smooth, mirror-flat, reflecting the white sky and the surrounding vegetation with absolute fidelity.

In the center of the clearing, something that is not a flower and not a tree and not a sculpture rises perhaps fifty meters into the windless air.

It is the shape of growth itself, the Platonic form from which all the stillgarden's vegetation derives—a spiral of translucent material that branches and rebranches in patterns you cannot count, cannot map, cannot hold in memory for more than an instant before your mind elects to simplify it into *beautiful* and *complex* and *still*. Inside it, the interference patterns do not shimmer; they *speak*, in a language of light and geometry that your visual cortex almost recognizes, almost translates, almost—

The beetle-thing you passed earlier is here too. Or another beetle-thing, identical, sitting on a low growth-form at the clearing's edge. It does not watch you because watching implies perception, implies change. It is simply present.

You are simply present.

The warmth holds you. The light illuminates you equally from every angle. The sound of your own heartbeat is the loudest thing for kilometers in any direction.

Nothing will change here. Nothing can. The stillgarden was made to be finished, to be *complete*, to be the final statement in a language of engineered existence that requires no revision, no development, no time.

You could stay forever.

You might already have.
