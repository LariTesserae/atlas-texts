# The Singing Cistern

Beneath the old city—beneath even the Roman pipes and the Ottoman channels—there is a cistern that was not carved by hands. The entrance is a crack in the foundation of a parking garage, widened over decades by municipal workers who learned not to speak of what they'd found, only to leave offerings of copper wire and watch batteries at the threshold.

You descend through wet limestone for twenty meters before the space opens.

---

The chamber is perhaps forty meters across, roughly oval, its ceiling a dome of dripping rock that rises fifteen meters at its apex. The walls are slick and pale, veined with minerals that catch the light—and there is light here, fierce and sourceless, emanating from the water itself.

The floor is flooded to ankle depth. The water is warm, blood-temperature, and it glows with a shifting luminescence that throws moving shadows against every surface. Not bioluminescence—nothing lives in this water that you could name. The light pulses in complex rhythms, brightening and dimming in ways that suggest communication or calculation or something between the two.

And the water *moves*.

---

This is what the municipal workers learned to accept: the water here does not obey the chamber's geometry. It flows in streams and currents that ignore the slight slope of the floor, that spiral around invisible axes, that sometimes climb the walls in defiance of gravity before cascading back down in luminous sheets. Small standing waves appear and disappear across the surface—not random interference patterns but deliberate formations, shapes that hold for seconds before dissolving into new configurations.

The sound is constant and layered. A deep thrumming resonance from somewhere far below, felt in the chest more than heard. Higher frequencies that emerge from the water's motion—tones that shift and modulate as the currents change course. Occasionally, a clear note will ring out, pure as a struck bell, and echo off the wet walls until it fades into the background chorus. The acoustics of the chamber seem designed for this, though designed is perhaps not the right word. *Evolved*. The cistern has been singing to itself for a very long time.

---

The sparse vegetation clings to the walls above the waterline. Pale root-systems spread across the limestone in fractal patterns, drawing moisture from the perpetually weeping rock. The plants themselves are minimal—small clusters of fleshy leaves, white and pink, that fold closed when the light pulses too bright and open again in the relative dimness. They are the only visible life, though the water occasionally produces bubbles that rise and burst with the scent of ozone and something older, something mineral and electric.

No fish. No insects. The warmth and moisture should breed abundant life, but the water refuses it. Workers who've tried to introduce organisms—goldfish from the pet store, mosquito larvae, algae cultures—report that they simply dissolve within hours, absorbed into whatever process the cistern is running.

---

Near the chamber's center, rising from the luminous water, stands the only structure: a pillar of fused metal and ceramic, perhaps three meters tall, clearly artificial but in a language of engineering that doesn't match any known tradition. Its surface is covered in patterns that might be circuitry or might be writing—the distinction seems less meaningful here. The pillar is warm to the touch, warmer than the water, and it vibrates faintly at a frequency just below the threshold of hearing.

The water is most active around this pillar. Currents spiral around its base. Light pulses travel toward it from all corners of the cistern, converging and then radiating outward again in new configurations. Sometimes the surface of the water near the pillar rises into complex shapes—temporary sculptures that last for seconds before collapsing back into turbulence.

The municipal workers call it the antenna, though they don't know what it's receiving or transmitting. They've learned not to touch it during the most active periods, when the singing grows loud enough to harmonize with the bones of the skull.

---

The chamber has been here, in this state, for longer than the city above. Core samples from the walls suggest the limestone formed around the space rather than the space being carved from the stone—as if the cistern created a bubble in the bedrock and the rock simply accommodated it over geological time. The pillar shows no signs of weathering despite constant immersion. The water never rises or falls. The light never fails.

Something is happening here. Something has been happening for a very long time. The cistern processes and sings and waits, and the water dances through patterns too complex for human mathematics to yet describe, and the pale plants fold and unfold in the pulsing light, and beneath everything that steady thrumming continues—a heartbeat or an engine or a prayer in a language made of pressure waves and heat.

The municipal workers leave their offerings and return to the surface. The cistern keeps singing. The water keeps glowing and dancing with its impossible currents, solving equations or remembering something or simply being what it has always been: a warm wet space beneath the world where the rules are different, where something brilliant and patient and strange continues its long work in the dark that is not dark, in the silence that is not silence, in the stillness that is anything but still.
