# The Throatworld

You descend through a wound in the earth that breathes.

The cavity opens gradually at first—a slump in dry terrain, a depression where red dust gives way to pale rootmass, then steepens into a passage that corkscrews down through living tissue. There is no stone here. The walls are plant: fibrous, warm, faintly pulsing with the circulation of something that is not sap and not blood but serves both purposes. The descent takes hours. You lose the sky early.

What opens below is a space that should not exist—a cathedral-hollow kilometers across, roofed by interlocking root systems so dense they form a continuous ceiling, lit from within by bioluminescent organs the size of houses that bloom and die and bloom again in slow irregular rhythms. The light shifts amber, shifts green, shifts to colors that have no surface-world names. It is warm here, the warmth of metabolic process, the warmth of a body's interior.

There is no water. This is important, this is foundational, this is the rule around which everything else arranges itself. The plants of the Throatworld have forgotten water or perhaps never knew it. They subsist on mineral vapor, on the slow exhalations of deep geology, on chemical gradients that rise from depths no one has measured. They have built themselves into a single interconnected mass that fills the hollow from floor to ceiling—and they are still building.

---

**The Floor That Isn't**

You cannot see ground. You cannot *reach* ground. What you stand on is rootwork, a dense woven mat of vegetative matter that extends down through unknown depths, always growing, always dying, always replacing itself. It gives slightly underfoot like living muscle. In some places it has thickened into platforms, into ridges, into organic terracing that creates topology without geology. In other places it is thin and treacherous, and the sounds that rise from below suggest the spaces beneath are occupied.

The plants grow in layers. Near the ceiling, thin filaments drift and tangle like aerial kelp, catching whatever nutrients float in the heavy air. Below them, bulbous structures cluster—storage organs, reproductive bodies, things that serve functions you cannot guess. Lower still, the growth becomes architectural: trunk-analogues as wide as towers, horizontal structures that span gaps like bridges, shelf-growths that create floors within floors within floors. The space is not open so much as *porous*, a three-dimensional maze of organic structure that extends in every direction.

It is all still forming. You can see it happening. Root-tips extend visibly, pushing through air, through older growth, through anything in their path. A trunk-structure nearby is actively swelling, adding girth hour by hour, its surface glistening with exuded resin that hardens into bark-analogue as you watch. Something that might be a fruiting body is emerging from a wall of foliage to your left—a slow eruption, a geological event at biological timescales. The Throatworld is building itself, continuously, chaotically, without plan.

And in every space the plants leave empty, something else has moved in.

---

**The Fauna Condition**

They pour through the vegetative labyrinth in numbers that overwhelm counting. They swarm. They carpet surfaces and fill passages and hang from every structure in clusters and chains and writhing masses. The Throatworld is not inhabited; the Throatworld is *infested*, saturated, overflowing with animal life in such density that the distinction between organism and environment begins to collapse.

Near you—very near, too near—a river of centipedal creatures flows along a root-highway, each individual the length of your forearm, their collective body hundreds of meters long and still extending back into the amber dimness. They move with a sound like heavy rain on leaves. They are eating the root surface as they travel, stripping away the outer layer, and behind them fresh growth is already beginning, paler and more tender than what they consumed.

Above, something with too many wings moves between the aerial filaments. Not one creature but a *colony*-creature, a distributed organism connected by strands of silk or sinew, its components working in unison to harvest the floating threads. It makes a sound as it moves—a thrumming, a vibration at the edge of hearing that is also somehow in your teeth, your bones, the fluid of your eyes.

The shelf-growth to your right supports an entire ecosystem in miniature: crawling things tending sessile filter-feeders, flying things that never land, burrowing things visible only as movement beneath the surface-skin of the living floor. Everything is eating. Everything is breeding. Everything is dying and being consumed and returning to the cycle without pause.

There are larger things. In the middle distance, where the light shifts green, a shape moves between the trunk-structures with slow deliberation—something with mass, with bulk, with a locomotive rhythm that shakes the growth you're standing on. You cannot see it clearly. You see its effect: the parting of smaller creatures, the swaying of vegetation, the shadow it casts across multiple layers of the space. There are many such shadows moving in the depths of the hollow.

---

**The Sound**

You cannot hear yourself think.

The noise is not any single thing. It is the aggregate of millions of creatures engaged in millions of activities—the clicking and shrieking and humming and rustling and wet organic sounds of consumption and reproduction and territorial dispute and communication in frequencies you can and cannot hear. It is the sound of the plants themselves: the creak of growth, the pop of expanding tissues, the subsonic groan of structures adjusting to new weight. It is the sound of the light-organs cycling, a faint electrical crackle as they ignite and a soft hiss as they cool and dim.

Occasionally something cuts through. A vocalization from one of the large shadow-shapes, a bellow or roar that silences everything in its range for exactly two seconds before the din resumes. The sudden collapse of a growth-structure somewhere in the hollow, an avalanche sound, a thunder of organic matter falling through layers toward the unknowable floor. The coordinated screaming of a swarm passing overhead, so synchronized it becomes almost musical, a chord of distress or triumph or something else entirely.

The echoes complicate everything. The hollow is not simple in shape; it folds and branches and divides and reconnects, and sound travels through it in paths you cannot predict. A noise from your left returns from your right. Something ahead of you is also behind you. The acoustic space is as tangled as the physical one.

You cannot hear yourself think. This is, in some ways, a mercy.

---

**The Built Things**

There are structures that are not plants.

Scattered through the hollow—hanging from growth, perched on shelf-formations, wedged into gaps between trunk-structures—you find constructions of woven fiber and hardened resin, of bone and chitin salvaged from the endless death that feeds this place. They are small. They are crude. They show evidence of hand-work, of intention, of minds that needed shelter from the chaos they inhabited.

A platform here, anchored to three different plants, swaying slightly with their uncoordinated growth but holding. A partition there, dividing a small pocket of space from the larger hollow, creating something that approaches privacy. Containers of various sizes, stoppered with wax or fiber, some empty, some holding substances you cannot identify. Tools: simple cutting edges, leverage-devices, things for gripping and prying and scraping.

You do not find whoever made them. You find evidence that they were here recently—disturbed surfaces, shed materials, the remains of meals. You find evidence that they travel, that this is one of many such stopping-places throughout the hollow, a network of improvised refuges in the overwhelming sea of life. You find evidence that they are adapted to this place: the structures are designed to sway, to accommodate the constant motion of their foundations. The tools are designed for the specific substances available. The containers hold things that could only matter here.

They live with the noise. They live with the density. They live in a world that is always under construction, always being eaten, always dying and being reborn. They have found some way to exist in the spaces between.

You wonder if they are watching you now, from somewhere in the layered darkness, waiting to see what you are.

---

**The Deep Processes**

The Throatworld is not stable. It cannot be stable. It is a system in constant disequilibrium, inputs and outputs never quite matching, feedback loops amplifying rather than dampening, the whole arrangement hovering always at the edge of collapse.

The plants grow faster than they should, faster than makes sense, drawing on mineral flows from below that surge and diminish unpredictably. When the flow is strong, growth explodes—you can see it happening now in a region to your west, a bloom of new tissue expanding visibly, structures forming in hours that should take months. When the flow weakens, die-off follows, vast sections of growth going brown and soft and falling, consumed immediately by the waiting fauna.

The fauna populations boom and crash with the vegetation, but also independently, following dynamics you cannot trace. A species will suddenly proliferate beyond all reason, filling every available space, and then just as suddenly collapse, leaving only remnants that will slowly rebuild over time. You are witnessing one such collapse now: somewhere in the upper reaches, something is dying in great numbers, and the bodies are falling through the layers like strange rain, and everything below is feeding.

The hollow itself is still forming. The walls—where you can see them through the growth—show evidence of ongoing expansion, of root-action cracking and pushing and slowly widening the space. Somewhere in the depths, new galleries are opening. Somewhere above, old galleries are being filled by the inexorable growth of the vegetative mass. The Throatworld's geography is provisional, temporary, written in materials that rewrite themselves.

Nothing here is meant to last. Everything here is meant to continue.

---

**The Color of the Light Now**

The great bioluminescent organs have shifted while you were not watching. The amber has given way to something cooler, something with blue in it, and the character of the space has changed. Shadows fall differently. The fauna have responded—some emerging, some retreating, some simply changing their behavior in ways you cannot categorize. A frequency of sound has dropped out of the din, replaced by another. The smell has shifted.

This happens. This will keep happening. The Throatworld moves through states, cycles through conditions, follows rhythms that have nothing to do with day or night because there is no day or night here, there is only the pulse of the hollow, the metabolism of the living space, the slow heartbeat of something too large and too strange to be a single organism but also not quite an ecosystem, not quite a biome, something in between, something new.

You could climb back up through the throat that brought you here. The passage is still there—for now—still open—for now—still connected to the surface world—for now. You could leave.

Or you could go deeper.

The sounds that rise from below are different from the sounds around you. Lower. Slower. More rhythmic. Almost like breathing.

Almost like speech.
