# The Plateau of Standing Echoes

The sound reaches you before understanding does—a continuous roar without source, without direction, filling the grey air like the memory of an ocean that was never here. It presses against your chest. It lives in your teeth. And yet nothing moves. The cold mist that beads on every surface hangs suspended, neither falling nor rising, and has hung this way for longer than hanging should be possible.

You stand on a basalt plateau so high that the world below exists only as theory, a suggestion of depth glimpsed through occasional tears in the permanent fog. The rock beneath your feet is old—not weathered smooth but weathered *resolved*, every fracture and seam having long ago found its final position. Lichen the color of dried blood marks the stones in patterns that repeat too precisely, tessellations that continue past the edge of vision into the grey. The lichen does not grow. It completed its growth in some unrecorded past and now simply persists, each thallus identical to its neighbors, frozen in a perfection that living things should not achieve.

The plateau extends in all directions—not infinitely, but far enough that distance loses meaning. Perhaps a mile. Perhaps seven. The mist makes liars of all measurements. Scattered across this expanse, at irregular intervals that nonetheless feel *placed*, rise formations of darker stone: basalt columns that have fused and twisted into shapes like frozen screams, like prayers abandoned mid-utterance. Some reach thirty feet. Some barely clear your head. All of them are producing the sound.

Not producing. *Being* the sound.

---

The roar has no variation. This is the wrongness you cannot escape, the thing that makes your inner ear ache with confused signals—sound this loud should shift, should have texture, should come and go with wind or source or distance. But the wind does not blow here. Has not blown here since before the concept of *since*. The air simply exists in its position, cold and damp and permanent, and the columns emit their single sustained chord, a frequency that is somehow every frequency, a noise that contains within it the ghost of every sound that will never be made in this place.

You realize, after a time that cannot be measured because time here has lost its familiar weight, that the sound is not the columns vibrating. The columns are solid, unmoved, unmoving. The sound is something the stone *is*—a property like color or density. The basalt here is loud. It has always been loud. It will always be loud. To be this stone is to be this sound, and the sound to be this stone, and both have achieved a state of being that requires nothing so crude as change.

---

Water collects in shallow depressions—not pooling, because pooling implies motion, but simply *present*, occupying the low places as it has always occupied them. The water is very cold but does not freeze. Ice would be a change, and change does not happen here. Instead the water maintains itself at some temperature just above freezing through what must be an impossibility of thermodynamics, or else through a local agreement that physics need not apply in its usual sequence. When you pass near these depressions, you see that the water has depth—more depth than the shallow stone bowls should contain. The surfaces are mirrors that reflect the grey sky, but beneath them, darkness extends downward past seeing. If you look too long, you begin to suspect the plateau is thin, a skin over void, and the water-pools are wounds through which the void regards you.

Things live in the water. You glimpse them peripherally—movements that never quite resolve into forms, shapes that exist in the corner of vision and vanish when addressed directly. They are pale. They may have been fish, once, before this place became its permanent self. Now they are something that drifts without swimming, that persists without breathing, that occupies the waters the way the sound occupies the air. When they surface—and they do surface, briefly, their pale backs breaking the tension of the water for the span of a held breath—the sound does not change. Nothing changes. They sink back down and the water-mirrors seal over them as if they had never been.

---

Scattered across the plateau, you find the ruins.

*Ruin* is perhaps the wrong word. These low stone walls, these arrangements of flat rocks that might have been foundations, these occasional pillars of stacked slate—they are not decaying. Nothing decays here. But neither are they maintained, and something about their dimensions suggests they were not built for human proportion. The doorways, such as they are, would require you to crawl. The interior spaces are too low to stand in, too narrow to lie down. Whatever used them fit differently than you do, or else curled itself in ways you do not curl, or else did not have a body in the sense that you understand bodies.

In one such ruin—the largest, though still barely the footprint of a small room—you find marks on the stone floor. They might be writing. They might be erosion. They repeat the same three symbols, over and over, cut into the basalt with something that must have been sharper than any tool the builders could have possessed, because the cuts are precise and deep and there are no tools here, no technology, nothing but stone and cold water and the wet air and the endless endless sound.

The symbols move when you are not watching.

Not much. Not in ways you can prove. But each time you look away and look back, they have shifted subtly—the angles different, the spacing changed, the depth of the cuts somehow both deeper and shallower. The plateau does not change, but the symbols change within the plateau's changelessness, and this contradiction does not resolve itself. This is simply how it is. This is how it has always been.

---

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere. There is no sun. The grey above you is not cloud—it has no texture, no movement, no suggestion of atmospheric phenomenon. It is simply *not-dark*, a dimness that extends upward past the point where upward means anything. The mist that wets your face originates from this not-sky, condensing out of the grey itself rather than falling from any source. It does not rain here, but surfaces are always damp. It has not rained here in epochs, but nothing has ever dried.

You find bones occasionally. Not skeletons—never anything so organized. Just single bones, resting on the basalt in orientations that suggest they were placed rather than dropped. A femur here, dense and grey and cold. A jaw there, with teeth that are too numerous and too uniform. These are not fresh. They are not old. They are simply present, and the lichen grows around them but never upon them, and the pale creatures in the water avoid the pools nearest to where the bones rest.

The roar does not pause. The roar has never paused.

---

If you stand very still—which is easy here, because stillness is the natural state, and moving requires a continuous assertion of intent—you begin to hear something beneath the roar. Not quieter. Not softer. Simply *underneath* it, in a register that the main sound does not occupy. It might be voices. It might be the echo of voices that were spoken when speaking was still possible. It might be the plateau itself, making its single observation about its own existence, a thought that takes geological time to complete and has been running since the rock first cooled and will run until the rock melts again, which will be never, because melting would require change.

The observation might be contentment. The observation might be horror.

From this high, in this permanent present, there is no meaningful difference.
