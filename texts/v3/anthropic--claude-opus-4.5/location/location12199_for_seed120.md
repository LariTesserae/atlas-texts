# The Kiln-Womb of Ascending Geometries

The chamber exists at the apex of something that is still deciding whether to become a mountain. You enter through a gap that shouldn't admit your body—a slot between two surfaces of fired clay that flex apart like breathing ribs, exhaling wet heat that tastes of iron and fermenting flowers.

Inside: a space no larger than a closet, every surface packed with construction.

The walls are not walls. They are *accumulation*—hand-pressed layers of clay and crusite and some mineral that weeps orange moisture, built up in fingerprint whorls that spiral toward a ceiling you cannot see. The ceiling keeps happening. It ascends into dim orange murk, and the spiral patterns go with it, and you understand with a vertigo that lives in your knees rather than your head that you are standing at the bottom of a shaft that rises impossibly far for something so narrow. The mountain is being built from the inside, one pressed handful at a time, and you are in its forming throat.

Everything is construction. Nothing here precedes the making.

The floor—if you can call it floor—is a compressed mosaic of thumb-sized tiles, each one irregular, each one bearing the impression of a different texture: bark, scale, woven fiber, something that might be frozen sound. They fit together with no gaps. You can feel them settling under your weight, clicking against each other, rearranging themselves into configurations that support the exact geography of your feet. When you shift, they shift, a rattling percussion that echoes up the endless shaft.

The sound—

The sound is enormous, far too large for the space. From somewhere above comes a rhythmic pounding like a heart the size of a house, and harmonizing with it, a wet squelching breath-sound, and beneath both of these a constant crackle-hiss of hardening clay. The acoustics are wrong. The narrow space should muffle, should deaden, but instead it amplifies, focuses, makes a concert hall of a crevice. Your own breathing joins the chorus, impossibly loud, and you realize the walls are memorizing it—incorporating the pressure-waves of your presence into their still-soft surfaces.

It is hot. Kiln-hot. Firing-hot. The clay walls radiate heat from some process occurring in their depths, and condensation forms instantly on every surface including your skin, runs down in rivulets that the walls absorb and use. You are sweating and the mountain is drinking your sweat to build itself higher.

In the cracks between tiles, in the spiral grooves of the walls, vegetation has already begun: thick-leafed succulents the color of bruises, their flesh translucent and veined, packed so tightly into their crevices that they've begun growing into each other, merging, forming communal organisms that pulse faintly with their own heat. Some have begun to fruit—dense clusters of something between berry and blister that weep the same orange moisture as the mineral seams. The smell is overwhelming. Sweet rot. New growth. Bread rising.

Now the strangeness:

The spiral stops making sense about halfway up. The patterns begin to contradict themselves. You see a whorl turning both clockwise and counter-clockwise simultaneously—not alternating, *simultaneously*—and your eyes refuse to resolve it so they skip past, only to catch on a section where the wall appears to be *behind itself*, layered in an order that admits no sequence. The clay fingerprints up there belong to hands with the wrong number of fingers. Four, seven, eleven. The pressed patterns include impressions of objects that cannot exist: holes with thickness, edges with no surfaces, corners that open rather than close.

A tile near your left foot shows the impression of your own face. It was made before you arrived. It is still wet.

The builders are present but unwitnessed. You hear them—that pounding heartbeat is their work, that wet breathing is their labor—but the space admits no bodies besides your own. When you look directly at any section of wall, it is still. When you look away, you hear the soft *pat* of a fresh handful being pressed into place, just at the edge of your vision. The mountain is being constructed by attention's negative space.

Occasionally, something moves in the vegetation. Pale, elongated, segmented—gone before you can see it whole. The succulents shiver in its wake. It leaves no impression on the tiles.

The chamber wants to complete itself. You can feel the intention pressing against you from all directions, the urgency of unfinished architecture demanding witness. The shaft continues upward toward whatever summit is being formed, and the heat rises with it, and the sound intensifies, and the geometries grow stranger with every meter of ascent.

You understand, in the way you understand dreams, that if you stay long enough, the walls will incorporate you. Your shape will join the pressed patterns. Your heat will feed the firing.

The mountain will remember you in clay.
