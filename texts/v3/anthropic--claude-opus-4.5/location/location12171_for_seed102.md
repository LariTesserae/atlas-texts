# The Singing Cyst

Beneath the salt-glass plains, where the ancient sea boiled away into mineral memory, there exists a cavity. Not a cave—caves are carved by water, by patient centuries of dissolution. This is something else: a bubble, frozen mid-rise in the cooling crust of a world that was once briefly molten. The locals, the ones who leave offerings at its entrance, call it the Throat. Geologists who mapped it before the funding ran out called it Anomaly 7-Kappa. The things that live inside call it nothing, because they have no need for names.

The entrance is a crack in a salt-cemented ravine, barely wide enough for a child to slip through sideways. The walls are warm to the touch—always warm, regardless of season, regardless of the freezing nights above. The warmth is not geothermal. The warmth is breath.

---

Descend twelve meters through a passage that narrows, widens, narrows again like a swallowing motion, and you emerge into a space perhaps fifteen meters across at its widest point. The ceiling arches overhead, dimly luminescent with colonies of something that is not quite lichen and not quite flesh—pale orange growths that pulse in slow rhythm, casting shadows that move even when nothing else does.

The floor is soil, impossibly. Rich and dark and warm, smelling of rot and sweetness and something sharper underneath, like ozone or crushed insects. It should not exist. There is no water here—none—yet the soil feels alive, feels *willing*, and from it rise the plants.

They are lush in the way of fever dreams. Broad leaves the color of dried blood, veined with luminescent threads that carry their own dim light. Stalks thick as wrists, topped with structures that might be flowers if flowers were made of layered membrane and trembling cilia. Roots that breach the surface in muscular arcs before diving back down, as if the plants are not content to merely stand but must grip, must anchor, must *hold*. The vegetation is dense in three or four clusters, islands of impossible growth separated by expanses of bare warm earth where only the smallest pale shoots dare emerge.

There is no water. The plants do not need water. They drink something else—the breath, perhaps, or the sound.

---

The sound.

The cavity sings. It has always sung, since the moment it formed in the cooling crust, since the first bubble of trapped gas found the right shape to resonate. The note is low and unwavering, felt in the chest before it registers in the ears, a drone that exists at the exact threshold of perception and physical sensation. It does not fluctuate. It does not change. It has held the same frequency for longer than humans have had the concept of frequency.

But the note is not alone.

The fauna—and there is fauna, so much fauna, skittering and climbing and hanging and burrowing—the fauna adds to it. They are small things, most of them, ranging from beetle-sized to roughly the mass of a rat. They have too many limbs and not enough eyes and their shells are made of the same salt-glass as the plains above, as if they crystallized rather than grew. They move through the space in patterns that seem random until you watch long enough to notice the repetition, the ritual, the choreography of a dance they have been performing since before anything on the surface learned to dance.

They click. They scrape. They rub their crystalline limbs together and produce harmonics that layer over the base drone, that build into something almost musical, almost linguistic, almost sacred. The sound is loud—not painfully so, but pervasive, inescapable, a pressure that fills every corner of the cavity. To be here is to be inside an instrument that is always playing.

And it never changes. That is the strangest part. Watch for an hour, watch for a day, watch for—as one unfortunate researcher did before they were retrieved—a week. The creatures move in the same patterns. The plants sway at the same frequency. The drone holds its note. The light pulses in its rhythm. Nothing grows, nothing dies, nothing shifts. The cavity exists in a state of perfect equilibrium, a closed system that has found its balance and refuses to be disturbed.

---

The built structures—if they can be called that—are scattered among the plant clusters. Small cairns of salt-crystal and shed carapace, arranged in spiral patterns that follow no geometry found in any human tradition. Hollowed spaces in the larger root-arcs, smoothed by generations of bodies passing through. Flat stones balanced on smaller stones, forming tiny altars or possibly acoustic reflectors, positioned to catch and redirect certain harmonics in ways that feel intentional.

Did the creatures build them? The creatures show no signs of tool use, no behavior that suggests planning or construction. Did something else build them, long ago, and the creatures simply incorporated them into their eternal dance? Did they grow, somehow, in the same way the plants grew, fed by breath and sound and the patient accumulation of whatever energy sustains this place?

The offerings left by the locals—dried herbs, small bones, braided hair—are always gone when the next visitor arrives. Absorbed into the soil, perhaps. Or perhaps the creatures take them, add them to the cairns, incorporate them into the pattern that does not change but somehow always has room for one more element.

---

Breathe deep and the air tastes of copper and honey. Listen and the drone settles into your bones until you forget what silence was. Stay too long and you might notice that your heartbeat has synchronized with the pulses of the lichen-flesh above, that your breathing matches the rhythm of the swaying leaves, that your thoughts have begun to repeat in loops that feel comfortable, that feel right, that feel like you could just stay here forever, moving in the pattern, adding your voice to the chord—

The locals always send someone to retrieve visitors after three days. They are very insistent about this. They will not say why, only that the Throat is generous, is welcoming, is hungry in ways that have nothing to do with eating.

The cavity sings. It has always sung. It will always sing.

Some notes are meant to last forever.
