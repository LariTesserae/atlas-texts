# The Lattice of Still Meridians

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere—a brightness without source, without shadow, without the directional mercy of any sun. The sky, if it can be called that, is a membrane of luminous milk stretched taut across a geometry that refuses comfortable comprehension. This is a built world, a made thing, a construction so ancient and so total that the very concept of "natural" has become a category error.

You stand on a surface that extends beyond the curve of any horizon you have ever known. The Lattice sprawls continent-vast, planet-scaled, a manufactured terrain of interlocking crystalline tiles—each one perhaps a kilometer across, perhaps ten, the distances deceiving in this sourceless radiance. The tiles are not flat but subtly domed, their edges meeting in seams so precise they seem less like joints than theoretical boundaries. The material is neither stone nor metal nor ceramic but something that drinks the omnipresent light and returns it as a deep, warm amber glow, as if the floor itself were an infinite field of ancient honey frozen at the moment of pouring.

Here and there—and the "here and there" spans distances that would take days to walk—structures rise from the tiles. Spires. Columns. Things that might be mechanisms and might be monuments and might be both or neither. They are dense, these constructions, layered and overlapping, their forms interlocking like the fingers of countless praying hands. Some twist upward in helical progressions that seem to rotate imperceptibly even as you watch. Others branch fractally, their surfaces covered in script or circuitry or decoration—the distinction is meaningless here, where everything is infrastructure and everything is ornament.

The warmth is constant, a pervasive ambient temperature that neither rises nor falls, neither comforts nor threatens. It is the warmth of a system in equilibrium, of entropy long since settled into its most stable configuration. Nothing sweats here. Nothing shivers. The air—and there is air, thin and sweet and faintly metallic—simply exists at the temperature it has always existed, the temperature it was designed to hold forever.

---

No rivers run across the Lattice. No lakes pool in its depressions. No moisture beads on the warm crystalline surfaces. The world is absolutely, perfectly, terminally dry—not desiccated, not parched, but fundamentally non-aqueous, as if water were a concept from some other physics entirely. And yet: in the seams between tiles, in the spaces where the great structures meet the floor, there are faint accumulations of something. A powder. A residue. Particles so fine they seem less like matter than like the memory of matter, settled over eons into the lowest points of the terrain. Mineral dust the color of old bone, of bleached coral, of salt that has never known the sea.

Vegetation—if the word applies—emerges from this dust in sparse, patient clusters. Crystalline filaments, some no larger than grass blades, others rising to the height of small trees. They grow, if "grow" is the right word, in spiraling lattices that echo the architecture of the larger world. Their surfaces are faceted, prismatic, breaking the sourceless light into faint spectral halos. They do not photosynthesize. They do not respire. They accrete. Slowly, over timescales that would make mountains seem impetuous, they organize the ambient particulate matter into more of themselves. Some have been building their structures for longer than your species has existed. They are neither alive nor dead but something else entirely—processes rather than organisms, algorithms of matter executing in geological time.

---

And the Lattice moves.

Not visibly. Not in any way your eyes can track. But the great tiles shift against each other in patterns so slow they register only as a kind of subsonic pressure, a feeling in the bones that something vast is happening just beneath the threshold of perception. The interlocking structures flex and rotate through cycles that take centuries to complete. Somewhere far away—a thousand kilometers, ten thousand—a column that has been rising for a million years finally reaches its apex and begins, imperceptibly, to descend. Somewhere else, two tiles that have been separating since before your world had oxygen finally touch, their edges meeting with a contact so gentle it produces no sound at all.

This is the activity of the Lattice: maintenance. Optimization. The endless, patient work of a system adjusting itself toward configurations that were calculated before the first star in your sky ignited. The technology that built this place—technology that operates at scales and through principles that would read as magic or miracle to any physics you know—has not ceased functioning. It has simply slowed to a pace commensurate with eternity. The Lattice is not ruined. It is not abandoned. It is operating exactly as intended, doing work whose purpose was set so long ago that the purpose itself has become a kind of natural law.

---

Fauna exists here, though "rare" understates the case. You might walk for weeks before encountering another moving thing. But they are present: maintenance organisms, or perhaps maintenance machines, or perhaps something that predates the distinction. You glimpse one in the middle distance, perched on a structure half a day's journey away—a thing like a geometric sketch of a beetle, all angles and surfaces, moving with the careful deliberation of a creature that has never needed to flee from anything. It is cleaning. Or repairing. Or simply existing in whatever way existence means for a being engineered before your galaxy achieved its current shape.

Others drift through the luminous air in patterns too slow and too regular to be called flight—more like scheduled transit, like the movement of satellites through their ordained trajectories. They are going somewhere. They are always going somewhere. The Lattice is vast beyond imagining, and its caretakers have distances to cover that would take lifetimes even at their steady, patient pace.

---

The silence is not total but nearly so. Sound exists here as a kind of rumor, a suggestion. The crystalline vegetation chimes faintly when the air currents—themselves generated by the minute thermal differentials between tile surfaces—brush past their faceted branches. The distant movement of the Lattice's larger mechanisms produces a bass note so low it is felt rather than heard, a vibration that seems to come from the floor and the air and the structures and the sky simultaneously. Your own footsteps return to you muffled and distant, as if the surface were absorbing them, cataloging them, filing them away with all the other incidental sounds this place has collected over its eons of operation.

And occasionally—rarely—there is something else. A tone. A frequency that rises from somewhere deep within the Lattice's crystalline bones, sustains for a moment, and fades. Communication, perhaps, between components of a system too vast to coordinate through any faster medium. Or something older. A sound the Lattice has been making since before there was anyone to hear it, a sound it will continue making until the last star goes dark and the universe itself achieves its own final, settled state.

---

To walk here is to understand that you are temporary. That everything you have ever known is temporary. The Lattice does not care about your presence—not out of malice, but out of a kind of mathematical indifference. It was built to outlast you. It was built to outlast everything. And it has succeeded, and it continues to succeed, and it will go on succeeding in its bright eternal warmth while epochs fall like leaves around it.

But there is peace in this, somehow. The war is over. The struggle is done. Whatever the Lattice was built to accomplish, it has accomplished. Whatever questions it was designed to answer, it has answered. It exists now in a state of permanent resolution, of ancient equilibrium, of work completed and purpose fulfilled. The light falls evenly on every surface. The warmth holds steady in every corner. The great tiles shift in their eternal dance of optimization.

The Lattice abides.
