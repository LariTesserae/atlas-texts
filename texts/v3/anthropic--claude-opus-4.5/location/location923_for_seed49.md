# The Lattice Flats

The city has no edge. That's the first thing you understand, standing anywhere within it — there is no horizon where buildings end and something else begins. The Lattice Flats extend in every direction, a continental sprawl of interlocking structures that long ago consumed whatever geography preceded them. Somewhere beneath this is bedrock, sediment, the bones of an older world, but no one alive has seen them. The city is the ground now.

It builds itself. That's the second thing.

---

The structures rise low and dense, rarely more than six or seven stories, but they crowd so tightly that the spaces between them are measured in arm-spans. Bridges, catwalks, tension cables, and membrane-tunnels connect everything at every level, creating a three-dimensional mesh that fills the air as thoroughly as the foundations fill the earth. You cannot walk in a straight line for more than thirty meters. You cannot see farther than a city block in any direction. The density is total — not claustrophobic exactly, because there is always *somewhere* to go, always a passage, a gap, a route threading through — but absolute. Every cubic meter of space either contains structure or is shaped by structure: light wells, pressure channels, acoustic funnels, thermal chimneys. The emptiness itself has been engineered.

The materials shimmer with a wet iridescence. Everything here sweats. The warm air — blood-temperature, constant, pressing against skin like breath — carries so much moisture that condensation forms continuously on every surface. Water beads on the ceramic-polymer walls, runs in thin sheets down the curved facades, collects in the countless gutters and channels that vein every structure. The city drinks this moisture somehow, pulls it into itself through capillary networks too fine to see. You can watch a wall slowly darken as humidity migrates toward it, then lighten again as the absorbed water moves deeper inside. The buildings are thirsty. Or perhaps processing. It's unclear whether this serves a function or is simply metabolism.

No plants grow here. No moss, no lichen, no algae in the wet channels. The surfaces are too active, too continuously renewed. The city replaces its own skin faster than any sessile organism could establish itself. What look like organic patterns — the branching channels, the cell-like clustering of windows, the way structures seem to *grow* around each other — emerge from purely mechanical processes, though the mechanics themselves remain obscure. Nanoscale construction, some have theorized. Templated crystallization. Self-organizing architecture that follows rules no one wrote down. The Lattice Flats predate any records of their building. They simply are, and they continue.

---

The sound is annihilating.

Imagine standing inside a engine the size of a small continent. The city *thrums* — a fundamental vibration so low you feel it in your chest cavity, in your joints, in the fluid of your eyes. Layered above this: the shriek and grind of construction, constant somewhere, carried through the structure-lattice in waves. The hiss of steam venting from thermal processes. The clatter and crash of the inhabitants — millions of them, tens of millions, packed into the endless warrens — going about their lives at whatever volume such density requires. Conversations become shouts. Commerce happens at the edge of screaming. Vendors use amplification systems that layer their voices into towers of sound, each competing for auditory territory in the acoustic bazaar.

And threaded through all of this: the fauna.

They are not insects, though they fill the role insects might. Crystalline things, mostly — silicate-shelled, lattice-bodied creatures that scuttle along the wet walls and swarm in the thermal updrafts. They grow from seed-structures scattered by the city's self-building processes, assembling themselves from minerals dissolved in the condensation. Their bodies click and chime as they move, thousands of them producing a constant high-frequency chatter that rises above the industrial roar like static above a signal. Some grow larger — hand-sized, cat-sized, rarely the size of small dogs — and these produce deeper tones, almost musical, resonating through the passages they claim as territory. They eat nothing recognizable. They seem to process the air itself, the moisture, trace chemicals the city generates. When they die, their shells dissolve back into the water-film within hours.

The inhabitants have names for the most common forms. *Clickers*. *Chime-crawlers*. *Wet glass*. *The singing nothing*. The taxonomy is folk knowledge, unreliable, contested between districts. No one is certain whether these creatures are alive in any biological sense, or whether they are simply another expression of whatever process builds the city itself — ambulatory architecture, structures that have learned to walk.

---

The turbulence here is not weather, exactly, but it functions like weather.

Waves of construction move through the Lattice Flats — you can hear them coming, the drilling and hammering and the shriek of material being reshaped, a wall of sound that precedes the actual change by minutes, sometimes hours. When a construction wave arrives, the local architecture transforms. Walls shift. New passages open where solid barriers stood. Familiar routes seal themselves and become building-mass again. The inhabitants have learned to read the signs: certain harmonics in the city's bass-note, changes in the moisture patterns on walls, the way the crystalline fauna migrate ahead of major restructuring. Districts sometimes vanish overnight, not destroyed but *reorganized*, their residents emerging into a transformed neighborhood they must relearn.

Other waves move through too. Pressure differentials create winds that howl through the passage-maze, hot and wet, carrying the smell of whatever the city's internal processes produce — ozone, hot metal, something almost organic, like bread baking or blood. These winds can rise without warning, strong enough to knock a person off a catwalk, then die just as suddenly when the pressure equalizes somewhere else in the system. The inhabitants have engineered their clothing for this: weighted hems, grip-surfaces on shoes, hoods that seal against the blast.

And there are *social* waves — crowds that form and disperse, market-swarms that materialize in a particular junction and then migrate as the acoustic conditions shift, running disputes between districts that move through the population like weather fronts. The density means that everything human propagates quickly: news, panic, celebration, violence. A disturbance in one sector reaches neighboring sectors within minutes, attenuated but recognizable, like ripples in an infinite pond made of people.

---

The light is strange only in its consistency. It comes from everywhere — the city emits it, walls glowing at a frequency tuned to human visual comfort, bright as an overcast day, constant, unchanging. There is no night in the Lattice Flats, no dimming, no natural rhythm of illumination. The inhabitants impose their own schedules through habit and social coordination, but the city itself does not sleep and does not acknowledge the passage of time. Clocks exist, carefully maintained, because without them there would be no way to know when anything happened relative to anything else. The glow varies slightly between districts — some areas warmer, amber-toned; others cooler, almost clinical — but these variations seem arbitrary, tied to nothing the inhabitants can control or predict.

Water collects in the low places. Not puddles exactly — the drainage is too efficient for standing water — but areas where the moisture concentrations run higher, where the air itself feels thick enough to swim through. In these wet-zones, the crystalline fauna grow larger and stranger, their lattice-bodies incorporating minerals the drier areas lack. Inhabitants of the wet-zones tend toward particular occupations: maintenance of the humidity-processing infrastructure, cultivation of the fauna for whatever uses they can be put to, services that benefit from the slight cooling the evaporation provides. The culture of the wet-zones differs from the drier districts — slower speech, longer silences between words, a patience that seems to have been learned from the water itself.

---

No one knows what the city *is*. Whether it was built by ancestors and achieved self-modification, or whether it was always this way, growing from some seed-structure planted in unrecorded history, or whether it is a single vast organism that happens to provide habitat for the millions who live within it. The inhabitants do not speculate much. The city provides: shelter, light, moisture, the conditions for life. The city demands: adaptation, flexibility, the willingness to learn new routes when old ones close. This seems fair to most who live here. A relationship, if not a partnership. They raise children in it, conduct business in it, grow old in it. They do not ask what it wants because it has never said, and they do not ask where it ends because they have never found an edge.

Somewhere, presumably, the Lattice Flats must have a boundary. Perhaps the construction-waves grow weaker at some impossible distance, the structures thinning until something else begins — ocean, wasteland, some geography the city has not yet consumed. But no traveler has ever returned with news of such a place. The expeditions that set out to find the edge simply... continue. Sometimes they send messages back, descriptions of districts that match no known maps, accounts of fauna never cataloged, reports of acoustic conditions unlike anything the home districts produce. Eventually the messages stop. Not abruptly — they attenuate, grow less frequent, become harder to interpret through whatever relay-systems carry them, until finally they fade below the noise floor of the city's constant roar.

Perhaps the travelers are still out there, still walking. Perhaps the city is that large. Perhaps there is no edge to find.

The sound never stops. The construction continues. The water runs down the walls, and the singing nothing chimes in the thermal currents, and somewhere in the endless maze a market is forming, a passage is closing, a new district is being born from the reshaped flesh of the one that stood there yesterday.

The Lattice Flats breathe in. The Lattice Flats breathe out.

And in the low wet places, where the glow filters through a hundred layers of bridge and walkway and crystalline swarm, something that might be patience settles over the inhabitants like weather they have learned to live inside.
