# The Cellar Gardens of Veth-Morai

You descend through a gap no wider than your shoulders, where root-fingers have pried apart the flagstones of some forgotten plaza. The cold hits you first—a wet, vegetal cold that settles in your lungs like breathing mist. Then darkness, total except for the faintest suggestion of form, the way you sense a wall before you touch it.

This is a cellar-space, though cellar seems too small a word. Once it was something else—a cistern perhaps, or the undercroft of buildings that no longer exist above. The ceiling presses low in places, opens into small vaults in others, all of it colonized so thoroughly by root-mass and hanging vegetation that the original stonework exists only as suggestion, as the bones beneath living flesh.

Your eyes never quite adjust. Instead you learn to feel the space: the brush of hanging tendrils against your face, the give of moss-carpet beneath your feet, the way sound changes when you enter a larger chamber versus a narrow passage. The plants here have never known sun—they are pale things, white and gray and the faintest lavender, their leaves reduced to sensing filaments, their stems thick with stored starches stolen from partner-fungi that web through everything.

And the animals. *Gods*, the animals.

They move in the darkness with an intimacy that makes you the intruder. At first you hear only your own breathing, the drip of water through root-channels. Then you begin to notice: the soft pad of many feet just behind you. The dry whisper of scales sliding over stone. A breathing that isn't yours, coming from very close. Something small runs across your hand and you don't flinch—can't flinch—because flinching might be noticed.

The cellar gardens are sparse but not empty. Between the root-columns and the hanging curtains of pale growth, there is space—room to move, voids of stillness, chambers where the air hangs cold and wet and *waiting*. It's in these spaces that the fauna gathers, drawn by the condensation that beads on every surface, by the soft fungal fruits, by each other.

---

You find signs of habitation—human habitation—but never fresh. Shallow alcoves scraped into root-mass, lined with dried moss that has since grown damp again. Stone tools, beautifully worked, their edges still sharp: blades of flaked obsidian, awls of bone, scrapers whose handles have been wound with plant-fiber cord. Small clay vessels, unfired, slowly dissolving back into mud. Whoever lived here shaped their tools with patience and skill, but they built nothing that would last. Perhaps they knew better than to try.

The sounds they must have heard: the constant soft dripping, musical in its irregularity. The settling of root-mass, creaking like old ships. And beneath it, always, the movement of animals—footfalls, breath, the occasional high chirp of echolocation from things that navigate by sound. A quiet place, yes, but a *populated* quiet. A quiet full of presence.

You round a root-pillar thick as your torso and freeze.

Three creatures crouch at a seep-pool, drinking. They are the size of large dogs but shaped wrong—too many joints in their legs, bodies low and broad, heads that seem all jaw and nostril. Their skin gleams wet and pale, hairless, stippled with darker spots that might be sensory organs. They know you're there. They've known since you entered this chamber. But water is precious and you are only one, so they drink, and their many eyes—you can just make out the clusters, like dark pearls—track you with patient attention.

Something else hangs from the ceiling above them. You see it only as shape: sinuous, coiled, waiting. It doesn't move. Neither do you.

After a long moment, the drinking creatures finish. They pad away into the darkness on their too-many legs, unhurried. The thing on the ceiling shifts—you hear the soft rasp of it—and then it too is gone, following.

---

The ecology here operates on rules you slowly intuit. The water that seeps through the root-mass carries nutrients; the pale plants convert them to growth; the fungi web through everything, trading and stealing; and the animals—*so many animals*, a staggering density of life packed into this lightless space—cycle through in patterns you can only glimpse. Predators and prey, yes, but also grazers, scavengers, parasites, symbionts, things that seem to serve no purpose except to exist.

They press against the space. Not physically—the chambers remain sparse, the passages passable—but *presentially*. You are never alone. You are always being watched, listened to, tasted on the air. The darkness is full of them. You walk between bodies that shift aside for you, that circle back once you've passed. The cold wet air carries their scent: musk and mineral and something fungal-sweet.

Some are bold. A creature like a long-bodied weasel, its fur reduced to pale fuzz, follows you for what feels like hours. It climbs the root-walls beside you, hangs from tendrils overhead, circles your feet. Testing. Curious. Others keep their distance but don't hide: you hear them breathing, sense their warmth in the cold air, occasionally catch the gleam of eyes—though what light they're reflecting, you cannot say.

In one small vault-chamber, you find a congregation. Dozens of animals, species you cannot begin to classify, gathered at a junction of root-channels where water drips in a steady rhythm. They are not drinking—there is water everywhere. They simply *are there*, together, in the darkness. Some groom each other. Some sleep in piles. Others sit alert, their attention fixed on nothing you can perceive. A truce-space, perhaps. Or something stranger—a ritual, a parliament, a phenomenon that has no name in languages spoken under sun.

You wait at the threshold. Something signals—you don't know what—and the gathering dissolves, creatures dispersing into the root-maze without urgency or alarm. When they're gone, the chamber feels vast. Empty. Impossibly quiet.

Then the sounds return: the drip, the creak, the soft pad of footsteps resuming somewhere behind you.

---

The way out, when you find it, is no wider than the way in. You emerge into gray light that feels blinding, air that feels thin and dry, a world of hard edges and visible distances. Your clothes are soaked through, muddy, spore-dusted. You carry the smell of that place with you—will carry it for days.

Behind you, the gap in the flagstones exhales cold breath. Pale tendrils curl at its edges, reaching upward, recoiling from the light. Something moves in the darkness below—you hear it, sense it, know it for certain—but you cannot see it.

You never could.
