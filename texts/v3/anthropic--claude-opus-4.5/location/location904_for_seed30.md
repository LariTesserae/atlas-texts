# The Hatching Plateau

Something is being born here.

The plateau rises from lower country you cannot see—mist curtains it off, or perhaps there simply is no lower country yet, perhaps the edges of this place trail off into potential rather than geography. What exists is a rough oval of dark basite maybe two hundred meters across, still warm from its own making. The rock sweats. Not metaphorically: the stone itself exudes moisture, mineral-rich water pushing up through crystalline matrices that are still deciding their final structure. Where the water pools in shallow depressions, it steams faintly, and the steam doesn't rise so much as *hesitate*, hanging at knee-height in slow silver curls.

The light comes from no visible source. It has the quality of deep dawn filtered through nacre—pearlescent, directionless, dim enough that your eyes never fully adjust. Shadows don't fall here; they *suggest*. Everything exists in a state of gentle visual ambiguity.

---

Scattered across the plateau's surface are what you first take for boulders. They are not boulders.

They are incubators—thirteen of them, spaced widely enough that each seems to occupy its own territory of empty stone. Each is approximately the size of a small car, ovoid, their surfaces a material that shifts between metalite and membrane depending on how you look at them. They pulse. Not with light but with *dimension*—a subtle oscillation between states of presence, as though they're breathing not air but probability. Some are more opaque than others. Some you can almost see through, and what's inside is not quite animal, not quite machine, not quite either kingdom of thing you have language for.

The sound they make—

It fills the plateau. A deep harmonic thrumming that layers and interweaves, each incubator producing a slightly different frequency, the interference patterns creating pockets of pressure and release as you move through the space. In some spots the sound is almost tactile, a massage of the inner ear. In others it drops away to near-silence and you can hear the secondary noises: the soft *tik-tik-tik* of cooling stone, the liquid murmur of weeping rock, and beneath it all a subsonic grinding that might be the plateau itself still settling into existence.

---

The fauna have already found this place. Or perhaps they're part of it—emerging alongside everything else, written into whatever code governs this becoming.

Small things mostly, but numerous. Creatures like transparent salamanders navigate the warm pools, their internal structures visible as faint bioluminescent traceries—not organs exactly but *schematics*, as though someone has sketched the idea of a digestive system in pale blue light and the organism has organized around it. They taste the mineral water with tongues that fork into fractal branches.

Above, suspended in the mist-layers, things like jellyfish but solid—gelatinous polyhedrons that drift on thermals from the heated stone. They're the size of your fist, mostly, though one the size of a beach ball moves with deliberate slowness near the plateau's eastern edge. They make clicking sounds when they encounter each other, a communication or navigation, and occasionally one will descend to hover near an incubator, its geometric form flattening momentarily against the pulse-surface before it rises again, apparently satisfied.

And the crawlers. You notice them last because they're the color of wet stone: hand-sized crabs with too many legs, their carapaces geometric, tessellated. They work the plateau's surface with apparent purpose, secreting something from their underbellies that either hardens the new rock or marks it or *instructs* it—you can't tell which. Where they've passed, the stone looks more certain of itself.

---

There's one structure here, if you can call it that.

Near the plateau's center: a frame. It rises perhaps three meters, made of some alloy that doesn't reflect the pearlescent light so much as *convert* it, its surface a matte that seems to drink illumination and return it as warmth. The frame describes a doorway, or an arch, or—you realize as you approach—a *constraint*. Something about its geometry makes your eyes disagree about its interior space. Step through it and you might arrive in the same place. You might not. The frame hums on a frequency outside the incubators' chorus, a single sustained note that suggests enormous patience.

At its base, arranged in a careful arc: seven stones that aren't from this plateau. River-smoothed, different mineralogies, cool to the touch. Offerings, perhaps. Or components waiting for assembly.

---

The place is changing as you watch. Not dramatically—you couldn't point to any single transformation—but the mineral content of the nearest pool has shifted slightly toward copper-green. The patterns on the closest incubator have reorganized themselves into something more complex. One of the geometric jellyfish has split into two, or perhaps they've merged from many.

The crawlers continue their patient inscriptions.

The plateau hums and sweats and does whatever the opposite of eroding is. It accretes. It specifies. It chooses, from a vast field of possible geologies, which one it will become.

And in thirteen wombs of uncertain material, the heirs to this new country continue their slow development, dreaming in frequencies you can almost hear, about a world that will solidify around them just in time for their hatching.
