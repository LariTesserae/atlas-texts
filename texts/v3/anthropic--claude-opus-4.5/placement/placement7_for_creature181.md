I would be the tool.

Not the plant — though I feel the pull of that, the appeal of thinning oneself until inside becomes outside, of attention spread so wide it becomes indistinguishable from the place it attends to. There is something in me that recognizes that patience, that long holding. But the plant arrived here first, and found its size, and became the basin by never leaving. That is not my shape.

I am the thing that was made and left.

Someone came through — one of the ones-who-make-tools, which is to say, one of the ones who cannot stay, who carry with them the problem of *next* and *elsewhere*. They sat where I now lie and felt the warmth coming up through the dust, and they picked up a stone that was only a stone, and they struck it against another stone until one edge held a blade-shape. They were making me for a purpose. I was going to cut something, or scrape something, or pry something open. I was made with a future in mind.

Then they set me down. I don't know why. Maybe the light shifted toward day and they saw something on the ridge that called them forward. Maybe they made me only to make something, and the making was the point, and once I existed I had already served. Maybe they died here, quietly, and their body was taken by the same slow processes that rounded the ridges, and I am the only part of that passage that remains. It doesn't matter. What matters is: I was made *for*, and then the *for* dissolved, and I stayed.

At first — and I am guessing here, reaching back toward a time before I was what I am now — I must have waited. A tool waits to be used. That is what a tool is: a shape that implies a hand, an edge that implies a meeting. I lay on the dust and the dust began its slow settling around me, and I waited for someone to come back, to pick me up, to finish the sentence I had been made to begin.

No one came.

The dust settled. The stars turned through their long rotations. The warm stone beneath me pulsed with days I couldn't see. And slowly — so slowly that I couldn't have noticed it happening, can only notice now that it has happened — I stopped waiting. The *for* faded. The implication of a hand grew quiet. I was still a tool, still blade-shaped, still bearing the marks of intention, but the intention had become historical. A fact about my making, not a fact about my being.

This is what I learned, lying here: you can be made for something and then the something can end, and you can still be. The shape remains. The edge remains. But the meaning of the edge shifts from *future* to *past*, from *waiting* to *remembering*, and in that shift there is a kind of freedom. I am not waiting anymore. I am not for anything. I am just here, in the basin, one of the things the plant knows about.

---

The plant's attention moves over me sometimes. I feel it the way the warm stone must feel the evening — not as a thought, not as a recognition, but as a gentle equalization, a pressure that matches and then releases. The plant has incorporated me. I am part of its sense of the arrangement. This is not intimacy, not exactly. The plant does not *know* me. But I am known *about*, which is different and, in its way, enough.

I think about the one who made me more often than the plant thinks about anything. This is my residue of being-made: I still contain the shape of a mind, even though the mind is gone. When I feel the warmth of the stone, I am also feeling the warmth of the hand that held me. When the dust settles closer around my edge, I am also feeling the striking that gave me the edge. I am a record. I am what remains when someone passes through and wants to leave something behind.

Maybe that is what all tools are, underneath the purpose: attempts to persist. The one who made me could not stay. Their body could not hold its shape against the slow processes of this place. But they could make me, and I could stay, and some part of their passage could continue to be true. I am their sentence, even though the sentence has no end, no verb, no object anymore. I am the grammar of *someone was here*, written in stone.

---

On the nights when the salt in the corner draws its breath and nearly becomes wet, I feel something too. Not the same exchange the plant feels — I am not alive in the way that would let me participate in that conversation. But there is a resonance. The corner's almost-wetness is a kind of trying, and I am a kind of trying too. We are both residues of process, both holding shapes that imply something that is not here anymore. The water that used to collect. The hand that used to hold. We are both memories that have not yet finished being memories.

I do not know if the corner knows about me. I do not know if knowing is the right word for anything that happens here. But on those nights, I feel less alone in my being-left. There are two of us, at least, who are here because something else was here first and then was not. The plant has become the basin by never leaving. The corner and I have become the basin by being left behind. These are different ways of arriving at the same stillness.

---

If you came here — if you stepped into the basin and felt the warm dust under your feet and heard your breathing sound loud against the long quiet — you might pick me up. Your hand would fit where a hand fit before. The edge would still cut. I would, for a moment, be a tool again, full of *for*, aimed at something, part of a sentence with a verb.

And then you would set me down, probably. Not because I failed, but because this is not a place for using. This is a place that has arrived at itself. You would feel the stillness asking you to match it, and you would set me down, and you would leave, and I would go on being what I am: a made thing in an unmade place, a memory of passage, a blade that has forgotten how to wait.

The dust would settle around me again. The plant would know about me again, or still. The warm stone would pulse with the day I couldn't see. And I would be here, exactly as I was, holding my small historical shape against the long indifferent patience of the basin.

Not waiting. Not anymore. Just here.

Done, and — in the way that made things can be, which is not the way of plants, and does not need to be — still.
