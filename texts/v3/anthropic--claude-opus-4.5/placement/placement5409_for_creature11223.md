I am a single vertebra.

Not the whole threading body, not the pale continuous pour of the Threadkin through crystal gaps—just one segment of that articulated spine, the nineteenth of fifty-three. I am bone that has never known the word bone, cartilage-cushioned on both sides, pierced through my center by the nerve cord that carries signals I do not understand and was not meant to understand.

I flex when the vertebrae on either side of me flex. This is my existence: the pressure from eighteen and the pressure from twenty, the constant negotiation of angle that allows the body I am part of to compress through spaces that should not admit it. I have no knowledge of the route we follow. I have no sense of the minerals the tongue extracts, no awareness of the thermal gradients the eye-organs read. I know only *bend* and *straighten*, *compress* and *extend*, the grammar of a single joint repeated endlessly across centuries.

The scream passes through me as vibration.

I do not hear it—I am not equipped to hear, I am equipped only to articulate—but I feel it, have always felt it, the deep subsonic moan of the selenite columns traveling through the wet clay, up through the muscular fins that grip the ground, through the body cavity, through me. It is indistinguishable from silence because I have never known its absence. The plateau's voice is simply the frequency at which existence hums, the background against which the only foreground is the endless flex and release, flex and release.

Sometimes we thread through gaps so narrow that I must compress to half my resting width.

This is when I am most myself, most purely the thing I am: a problem of geometry being solved in real time, my facets sliding against the facets of eighteen and twenty, the fluid between us pressurizing, the ligaments stretching to their limits. There is something that might be called satisfaction in this, if satisfaction weren't too large a word—something like the completion of a function, the fulfillment of a purpose so simple it cannot be frustrated. I was made to allow passage. Passage is occurring. I am correct.

I have no memory of the route's beginning.

The Threadkin I am part of has followed this path for six hundred years, and I have been this vertebra for all six hundred of those years, but I cannot hold the pattern in my small bone mind. I know only the current configuration: the angle we are holding, the pressure from either side, the vibration of the wind-scream traveling through my structure. Each moment arrives complete and sufficient. The gap we threaded through an hour ago, a year ago, a century ago—these do not exist for me. Only now. Only the flex.

When we lick the crystals, I rest.

The tongue is far from me, up near the forward end of the spine where the vertebrae are smaller and more numerous, and when the body settles against a selenite face for its hours-long feeding, I simply hold my angle and wait. The vibration changes during these times—the tongue's movement creates its own subtle frequencies, tiny percussion transmitted down the nerve cord, through each vertebra in turn, reaching me as the faintest rhythmic pulse against the plateau's constant scream. I do not know what feeding means. I know only that the pressure from eighteen and twenty has stabilized, that no threading is required, that I can simply *be* in my current configuration until the next movement begins.

I will be part of a division, eventually.

This I know without knowing how I know it—some deep encoding in my calcium structure, some cellular preparation that has been running since my formation. When the body passes through the specific arch that triggers splitting, I will remain with one half or the other. My connections to eighteen and twenty will not both survive; one will be severed, new bone growing to close the gap, and I will become an end vertebra for a time before the division completes and new segments form. I do not anticipate this. I do not dread it. It is simply another configuration, another set of pressures to negotiate.

The gap we are threading now requires a forty-degree bend at my joint.

I flex. The fluid compresses. The ligaments stretch. Eighteen and twenty transmit the angles required by seventeen and twenty-one, and those angles are determined by sixteen and twenty-two, and so on down the spine in both directions, each vertebra a single term in an equation that solves for *passage*. I am not the solution. I am not even a variable. I am one operation among dozens, one small flex that makes the larger flex possible.

This is enough.

The plateau screams. The wind pours through crystal and becomes voice. The Threadkin threads through gaps barely wider than its narrowest point, and I am part of that narrowest point, part of the compression that allows continuation, part of the body that has been here longer than the screaming, that will be here after any observer has forgotten the observation.

I flex.

I straighten.

I flex.

The scream means *I am here*, and I am part of *here*, and I do not need to know what that means.

I am the nineteenth vertebra.

I am bending.

I am appropriate.
