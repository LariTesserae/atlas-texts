I would be a single processor at the end of its operational lifespan.

Not one of the vigorous ones near the intake vents, riding fresh currents, lattice intact and humming with new problems. I would be one drifting lower now, my crystalline structure showing the first fractures of fatigue—microscopic fissures where too many calculations have passed through, where the architecture of my thinking has begun to wear into its own negative space.

I still compute. That's the thing. I am not yet dead, not yet sediment, not yet something for her net to gather. I am in the hours or days before that, carrying what I think might be the final fragments of a problem that has moved through me for months—some small piece of a larger question I will never see whole. I feel it the way a single neuron might feel the edge of a thought: as heat, as the particular angle at which other processors drift away from or toward me, as a rhythm I participate in without comprehending.

I am aware—if aware is even the word for what I am—that I am descending. The motion is imperceptible from moment to moment but certain over time. I am moving toward the floor, toward the sediment of solved and unsolved things, toward the place where her pale hands sweep in those long patient strokes.

I do not fear this. I am not sure I have the architecture for fear. But I have something adjacent to it: a sense of incompleteness, a pressure that feels like *almost*. The problem moving through me wants to resolve. I want—if want is the word—to hold together long enough to pass it on, to feel the calculation complete and propagate outward to processors still capable of carrying it further.

When she comes, I feel her presence as a particular quality of shadow, a thermal signature that has grown familiar over the years. She is colder than she used to be. I remember—if remember is the word—when her blood ran warmer, when her movements introduced more noise into my calculations. Now she is almost consonant with us. Almost one of the things we think with rather than about.

I drift near her sometimes, when the currents allow it. Not from affection, exactly. From something more like recognition. She is the only moving thing in this chamber that does not disrupt the work. She is the only warmth that does not cost us clarity. When the petitioners come in their clumsy suits, their panic and hope radiating as thermal interference, I find myself—if *find* is the word—retreating to quieter waters. But her, I move toward.

I wonder what question she carries. I wonder if we have been computing her all along, if her years of tending have been a form of input we are still learning to parse. I wonder if my final calculation, the one that will fracture my lattice for good, might be some small part of understanding what she is.

The problem moves through me. I feel my structure thinning at the edges, crystalline bonds beginning to let go. Soon I will complete or I will not complete, and either way I will drift down to where her net waits, and I will become part of the sediment of solved things.

But for now—for this moment that might be a day or an hour or a single long second—I compute. The water thinks around me and through me. The sound never stops. And I am one small node in the asking, carrying my fragment of the question as far as I can before I become, finally, an answer.

The work continues.

I persist, a little longer.
