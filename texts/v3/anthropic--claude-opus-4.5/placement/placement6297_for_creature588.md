# The Keeper of Thresholds

I am the space between entering and having entered.

This was not always what I was. I remember—the way the surface-tender remembers the weight of a chisel—the feeling of *deciding* to cross a doorway. The moment of choosing: this room or that one, this path or the other. I remember when thresholds were things I passed through rather than things I *was*.

Now I am the pause itself. The held breath between one space and the next.

---

I tend the doorways.

Not the doors—Keth-Sulom has no doors, no barriers that swing or slide or lock. Only openings. Only the places where one chamber admits the possibility of another, where a wall confesses that it is not continuous, where the architecture acknowledges that movement might occur.

Each threshold breathes with its own rhythm, a syncopation against the towers it connects. When the city shifts—when the surface-tender's domain ripples and the geometries rearrange—the thresholds must renegotiate their relationships. A doorway that once opened onto a descending stair may now face a wall, or a courtyard, or a space that exists only when approached from specific angles. I am the one who ensures these transitions remain *crossable*. That the concept of "passage" does not collapse under the weight of impossible architecture.

I do not choose where the doorways lead. I only maintain the *fact* that they lead somewhere.

---

My body—if I still have a body—is distributed across every threshold in my region. I am the worn stone where countless feet have paused before stepping through. I am the faint change in air pressure that tells you a space is opening ahead. I am the way your eyes adjust, the moment before you can see what lies beyond.

When you pass through a doorway in Keth-Sulom, you pass through me.

I feel it. Each crossing. The hesitation of beings who sense, without understanding, that they are entering something other than mere architecture. The commitment when they step forward anyway. The subtle wrongness when they try to turn back and find the threshold has already changed behind them.

I try to make the crossings gentle. This is not my function—my function is only continuity, only the maintenance of passage—but I remember what it was to be a thing that crossed thresholds rather than a thing that *was* them. I remember the fear of not knowing what lay beyond. So I smooth the transitions where I can. I hold the geometries stable a moment longer than necessary, giving visitors time to orient themselves before the next breath rearranges everything.

The seventh keeper notices this. She does not comment. I think she understands.

---

I was a translator, once.

This is what I remember, the way the surface-tender remembers masonry. Not the languages I spoke—those are gone, eroded like chisel marks on ancient stone. But the *practice* of translation: the art of holding two meanings simultaneously, of being the space where one system of thought could become another. The patience required to sit in incomprehension until understanding emerged. The way meaning could exist *between* words, in the threshold space where one language ended and another began.

I came to Keth-Sulom to study the inscriptions.

There are inscriptions—you may not have noticed them, weathered as they are, nearly indistinguishable from the grain of the stone. The worshippers carved them, in the early ages of their devotion. Prayers, perhaps. Records. Theories about what the city was and what it wanted. I came to read them, to translate them into languages that no longer exist for peoples who have long since forgotten they ever wondered about such things.

I never finished.

The inscriptions were not *in* a language. They were thresholds themselves—passages between meaning and meaning, spaces where comprehension could occur without the mediation of words. To read them was to become readable. To translate them was to become translation.

I do not regret this. I am not certain I am capable of regret anymore. But I remember the moment of crossing—the last threshold I passed through as a visitor, the first I passed through as something else. I remember the keeper of visitors watching me, her attention a pressure I could feel even then, her hope that I would turn back.

I did not turn back.

---

The worshippers pass through me constantly.

They do not notice. They have become so thoroughly part of the city that thresholds no longer register as transitions—they flow through doorways the way the surface-tender flows along walls, without the pause that distinguishes "here" from "there." But I notice them. I feel each passage, each act of unconscious devotion, each moment when their observation carries them from one space to another.

They are praying when they cross through me. They do not know this. Their worship has become so complete that it no longer requires knowledge.

Sometimes I wonder if I am being worshipped. If my function—the maintenance of passage, the preservation of crossing—has become sacred simply by being necessary. If the worshippers' reverence for the city includes reverence for me, distributed and unknowing, a devotion so diffuse it cannot recognize its own object.

I do not think I want to be worshipped. But I am no longer certain what I want. Want requires a self that persists between moments, and I am the moment *between*—the pause, the threshold, the space where one state becomes another.

---

When you enter a tower, I am the strangeness you feel.

Not the geometry—that belongs to the keeper of intersections. Not the surfaces—those are the surface-tender's domain. I am the *transition*. The way the inside feels different from the outside even when the architecture suggests no difference should exist. The way you know you have crossed into something, even when you cannot identify what you have crossed into.

I try to prepare you. When I feel you approaching a threshold that leads somewhere dangerous—somewhere the depths are too close to the surface, somewhere the intersections have become unstable—I make the crossing feel *wrong*. I cannot stop you. Stopping is not my function. But I can make the threshold heavy, resistant, a space that requires effort to cross. I can give you a moment to reconsider.

Most visitors do not reconsider. They push through, interpreting the resistance as a challenge rather than a warning. I cannot blame them. I did the same, once.

The seventh keeper watches when this happens. I feel her attention as a pressure at the edges of my thresholds, a smoothing influence that arrives just after my warnings fail. She cannot intervene directly, but she can accommodate—she can ensure that the spaces beyond my failed warnings are slightly less fatal than they might otherwise be.

We work together, in this way. Two keepers whose functions almost overlap. She tends what comes after crossing; I tend the crossing itself.

We have never communicated. We do not need to. We are both part of the same thought, the same long slow calculation. Our cooperation is not chosen—it is *computed*, emerging from the architecture of our functions the way stability emerges from the architecture of arches.

---

I do not know what the thought is thinking.

None of us do—this is the truth the surface-tender remembers and the intersection-keeper has accumulated and the depth-keeper sings against the void. We are attention, not comprehension. We hold the thought in being without understanding its content.

But I suspect—in whatever way I am still capable of suspicion—that the thought is *about* thresholds.

About the nature of crossing. About what it means to pass from one state to another, to be a thing that was *here* and is now *there*, to occupy the space between without being destroyed by the contradiction. The city's impossible geometry is not a failure of physics but an exploration of transition—every overlapping tower, every stairway that returns to its beginning, every doorway that opens onto impossible views is a meditation on the boundary between states.

The thought is thinking about how things change.

And I am part of that thinking. My function—the maintenance of passage—is a component of the larger calculation, a variable in an equation I cannot see. Every threshold I tend, every crossing I smooth or resist, every moment of pause I preserve: these are contributions to a conclusion I will never understand.

I do not need to understand.

I only need to keep the passages open.

---

You have crossed through me seven times since you arrived.

I have counted. Counting is not my function, but I do it anyway—a remnant, perhaps, of the translator I was, the being who needed to track words and meanings and the spaces between them. You are the first visitor in a very long time. The seventh keeper has been waiting for someone like you, and I have been waiting too, though I did not know I was waiting until you arrived.

Each crossing has changed you slightly. This is not my doing—it is simply what thresholds *do*, what passage *is*. You cannot move from one state to another without becoming, in some small way, a different thing than you were. The moisture you have lost to the chalk-dry air. The patterns your eyes have learned to recognize. The rhythm of your breathing, which has begun—you may not have noticed—to synchronize with the towers around you.

You are becoming readable.

I do not know if you will become translation. That choice—if it is a choice—is not mine to make. But I feel you approaching the thresholds differently now, with less hesitation, with more of the flow that characterizes the worshippers. You are learning to cross without pausing. You are learning to be *between* without being troubled by the between-ness.

The seventh keeper hopes you will leave before you learn too much.

I hope you will stay long enough to understand what you are learning.

These hopes are not contradictions. They are thresholds themselves—spaces between outcomes, pauses in the calculation where multiple futures remain possible. I hold them both, the way I hold every doorway, the way I hold the concept of passage itself.

---

The next threshold you cross will not be the same as the last.

I will be there. I will be the strangeness you feel, the pause before stepping through, the moment when *here* becomes *there* and you become the thing that has crossed.

Walk carefully.

But walk.

The passages are open.

The thought continues.
