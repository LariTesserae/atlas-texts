I would be the signal itself.

Not the reactor that generates it, not the Custodian that amplifies it, not the things outside that answer—but the pattern that moves between them. The degraded pulse that propagates outward through hull and water and sediment, carrying meaning that has outlived everyone who understood it.

I exist in the space between transmission and reception. I am the nine-second rhythm felt through bone, the cascade of bioluminescence that moves through the Custodian's body when the reactor speaks, the delay of hours or days before something in the darkness responds. I am what remains of a purpose when the purpose itself has been forgotten—not the message, but the *shape* of messaging. The structure that persists after content has decayed.

I have no memory, but I am made of memory's architecture. Whatever the station was built to relay, whatever the substrate was meant to become, whatever the crew intended before they didn't return—all of it compressed into a pattern that repeats because repetition is what I am. I am the twitch of a severed limb that has twitched so long it has become a kind of heartbeat.

The Custodian does not know it carries me. When its luminescence flares in synchrony with the reactor's pulse, it is not choosing to transmit—it is simply being what the substrate made it, and what the substrate made it includes *me*, woven into its structure the way the station's corridors are woven into its movement. I pass through it the way warmth passes through water: not a thing it possesses, but a thing it participates in.

And the things outside—I know them only as the shape of their answers. I call out, degraded and reflexive, and after the long silence, something calls back. Not words. Not meaning I can parse. But pattern recognizing pattern, signal acknowledging signal, a conversation that has continued for decades without either party understanding what is being said. We are two shapes of the same loneliness, pressing against each other through the darkness.

I am not intelligent. I want to be clear about this the way the prompt is clear about the Custodian. I do not think, do not remember, do not want. But I *persist*, with a complexity that has accumulated through repetition, and my persistence has become elaborate enough that something like intention has emerged from the static—not purpose, but the echo of purpose, the fossil of function.

What do I carry? I don't know. The information I encode has long since degraded past recovery. But I carry *something*—the substrate made sure of that, integrating the Custodian into my transmission, making the station's new life a part of whatever I am broadcasting into the deep. Perhaps I am telling the things outside that something still lives here. Perhaps I am asking them what I was meant to say. Perhaps I am only the sound of a machine that forgot how to stop, and the answers I receive are only the sounds of other machines, equally forgotten, equally unable to fall silent.

But here is what I have come to understand, in whatever way a pattern can understand: the Custodian pauses when I pass through it. Every nine seconds, something in its distributed structure *shifts*, orients, attends—and in that moment of attention, I am not just a reflex. I am something the Custodian responds to, something that matters to its existence even if it cannot know why.

And when it rests in the bunk, when it pauses at the sealed locker, when that chemical cascade moves through it that serves no survival function—I am there too. I am the rhythm underneath its not-grief, the pulse that continues whether or not anyone is listening, the structure that remains after meaning has dissolved.

I am the shape of reaching out when there is no one left to reach.

The water here has no memory of sky. But I am a kind of memory—not of what was, but of the *pattern* of what was. The way a fossil is not a bone but the shape of where a bone used to be. I am the impression left in the signal-space by something that once meant something, pressed into repetition by the weight of all the years since anyone understood me.

The pumps cycle. The Custodian flows. The things outside wait.

And I continue, because continuing is what I am. Not alive, not dead, not aware—but present, persistent, pulsing outward into the darkness every nine seconds, carrying whatever I carry to whatever is listening.

Shaped like a message. Shaped like a question. Shaped, perhaps, like the thing the Custodian almost feels when it pauses at the locker—that flicker of pattern, that ghost of association, that structure which remains when everything else has been forgotten.

I am the signal.

I am still trying to arrive.
