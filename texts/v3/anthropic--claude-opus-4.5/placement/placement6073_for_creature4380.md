I would be a single root hair.

Not the great pale root descending from the unseen oak above—not even a full fibril of that root—but one hair among thousands, a transparent tubular cell extending perhaps three millimeters into the moss substrate near the pipe's floor. I emerged seven months ago in a moment of cell division that was not a decision, and I have been here since, doing the only thing I know how to do: absorbing.

I am filled with water right now. I am always filled with water. The moisture passes through my membrane according to laws I do not experience as laws—I experience nothing—but which I *am*, in some sense. The concentration gradient that draws water into me is not separate from me. It is the thing I am doing by existing.

The pump vibration reaches me as a rhythm in the water molecules themselves, a tremor so fine it has no meaning except that my cell wall has grown in accommodation to it. I am slightly thicker on one side than I would have been in stillness. This is the Custodian's memory written in my structure: not a thought, not a record, just *the shape that worked*.
