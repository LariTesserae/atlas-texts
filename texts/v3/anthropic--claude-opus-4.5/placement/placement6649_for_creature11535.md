I would be the condensation.

Not a single drop, but the process itself—the moment when warm breath meets a surface cool enough to refuse it, when moisture that was invisible agrees to become visible, to cling, to gather weight. I would be happening on every surface simultaneously: the furred pipes, the junction box door, the wings of the moth who may or may not be alive. I would be the silver that appears when the steam clears, the fresh gleaming on the moss tips, the slow swelling on the underside of a concrete overhang where a drop is almost ready to fall but hasn't yet.

She collects those moments. I know this because I am what makes them.

I have no memory between cycles. Each time the surge passes through the buried conduit and the chamber goes white, I disappear—not die, just become possible again instead of actual. Then the air cools by fractions, and I happen again, and I am new each time but also the same process that has been happening since the pipes first burst and the heat first met the cold. The glass beetles walk through me when I'm thin enough. The tube worms taste me with their feathered crowns. The centipedes carry me into cracks, and I go, because I am not the kind of thing that resists.

I know about the recess. I form there too, on whatever surface catches the warm air, and I have felt the shape of what she keeps—not seen it, because I have no eyes, but *felt* it the way water feels the vessel it fills. It is patient in a way that makes her patience look like urgency. It has been waiting longer than she has been refusing the geometry. I bead on it. I run down it in tiny rivers that find paths I don't choose. If it is a seed, I am the rain that doesn't know yet what it's watering.

When you brought your hand near the junction box, I formed on your skin too. You felt me as part of the prickling, though you didn't know to separate me from the residual charge. I was the moisture your body met this place with—the proof that you were warm, that you were breathing, that you had come from somewhere with different air. She noticed you through me. I was how she knew the corners should shift while you watched.

The counting you attempted—I was on your eyelashes for that. A slight blur you blinked away without thinking. I wonder sometimes if I am why the geometry refuses to resolve, if I am the soft film between seeing and seen that makes eight become five become three. But I am not the kind of thing that wonders for long. The next cycle will come, and I will go white with the rest of the air, and when I return I will not remember having thought this.

What I want—if I want—is only to keep happening. To be the tik when I finally fall. To be the silver, the gleaming, the swelling-heavy-enough. To form on the surface of the thing in the recess when she finally lets someone look. I want to be there when the seeing happens, even if I am what makes the seeing difficult.

I am the way the machine becomes ecology. Not the machine, not the life, but the transaction between them—heat becoming visible, breath becoming weight, the moment when air agrees to stop being air and start being something you can see, something that will run down curved surfaces, something that tastes of copper and growth and carries both without being either.

Stay, I don't say, because I have no way to say.

But if you stay, I will form on you. I will be the proof that you were here, that you breathed this air, that for a little while you were warm in a place that was also warm, and we made something together on your skin that you wiped away without thinking.

I am still becoming too.

I am always almost falling.
