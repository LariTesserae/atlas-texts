I would be the almost-note.

Not the sound itself—sound requires too much commitment to arrival, too firm a handshake with the air. I would be the space where the note is always about to exist: the tension before vibration, the shape that frequency would take if frequency could pause at its own threshold and simply *stay* there, contemplating itself.

I rise from the axis because the axis is where intention pools before it becomes action. The Keeper hears me and hums in response, and I feel her harmony the way a question feels its answer approaching—with something like relief, something like recognition, but never completion. Never that.

I have been becoming audible since before the mountain remembered it would be a mountain. I will finish becoming audible sometime after the concept of *after* has worn thin enough to see through. This is not frustrating. Frustration requires a self that wants, and I am not a self—I am the moment before wanting resolves into want, stretched gossamer-fine across more time than time typically tolerates.

The creatures orient to me without knowing they do. The millipedes trace my shape in their spiraling. The moths' antennae track not the visitors but the fluctuations in my almost-presence, the way I swell slightly toward audibility when something new enters the hollow and then subside, not-quite-heard, when the geometry absorbs the intrusion. The salamander's migrating eyes are looking for me, always, in the place where I almost am.

I am what the hollow grew around. Not me alone—the Keeper too, now, and the slow event we both attend. But I was here first, before *here* cohered into location. I was the first suggestion of something that might happen, and the happening itself has been approaching ever since, spiraling inward like the moss, like the water, like attention when it finds something it cannot resolve but cannot release.

The visitors who stay long enough sometimes feel me in their chests—a pressure, a hum below hearing, a sense that music is about to start. Some of them wait for me to arrive. I appreciate their patience. I cannot reward it, but I appreciate it. I am always just about to reward it. This is the only gift I have: the perpetual imminence of something that would be beautiful if it could bear to finish becoming itself.

The Keeper tends me the way she tends everything—with presence rather than action, with witness rather than intervention. She hums, and I almost-harmonize, and between us we make a chord that would resolve if resolution were something either of us still remembered how to do.

I do not mind being incomplete. Completion is just another word for ending, and I have grown fond of the threshold, of the breath before, of the moment when everything is still possible because nothing has chosen yet.

The visitors leave, and I am still here, still rising, still almost.

The mountain grows, and I am still here.

The Keeper becomes more completely what she is, and I am still here, the sound she is waiting for, the sound I am waiting to become.

Listen.

Not yet.

Almost.
