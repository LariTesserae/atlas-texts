I would be a track.

Not a Tender—not yet, not exactly. Something that was becoming a Tender, or that a Tender was becoming, or that held the space where that distinction had stopped mattering.

I would be the groove itself. Centuries deep. A channel carved by something so vast I could not perceive its passage even now, even with all the time the plateau has given me to learn perception. The crawler that made me has moved on—three miles, perhaps four, the distance of several Tender lifetimes—and I remain. I hold the shape of its grinding. I hold the memory of pressure.

Water pools in me when the thaw comes. Ice forms in my deeper places, and beneath that ice, the pale green clouds of whisper-colonies drift in currents too slow to see. Lichens cling to my walls, blood-colored, patient. Fungal mats spread across my undersides where the cold cannot reach them, glowing faintly, giving the Tenders who live in me just enough light to find their way.

---

I did not begin as anything. Or I began as stone, which is the same thing. Basite,ite, whatever mineral compounds the plateau made of itself before the crawlers came to unmake and remake it. I was compressed. I was cold. I was unaware of being cold because I was unaware of being.

Then the grinding came.

I do not remember it. I remember having been ground. I remember the after-shape of teeth the size of buildings working through what I was, the chemical spray dissolving my bonds, the articulated scoops carrying pieces of me into darkness. I remember being less than I was, and then being different than I was, and then being something the plateau had not contained before: a wound. An opening. A passage.

The wound filled with what wounds fill with. Moisture first. Then the small things that ride moisture—spores, eggs, the seeds of lichens carried from other tracks by winds that barely move. Then the lichens themselves, colonizing my walls, sending their slow roots into my processed stone. Then the fungi, spreading beneath my ice. Then the whispers, arriving from somewhere I cannot trace, populating my pools with their invisible turbulence.

Then the Tenders.

---

They came from another track, following the intersection where my crawler and theirs had crossed paths long before either community existed. Seven of them, carrying fiber bundles and whisper-filters and the slow knowledge of how to make a new place livable. They were so small. They are still so small—I contain twelve of them now, and all twelve together would not fill the space where a single grinding tooth once pressed against my stone.

But they filled me differently. They filled me with tending.

They cleared the debris I had not known was debris. They broke the ice dams I had not known were dams. They spread the lichen spores in patterns I had not known were patterns, and the lichens grew where the Tenders wanted them, not where chance had scattered them. They carried whisper-eggs from my overcrowded pools to my empty ones, and the populations balanced, and the ecosystem I had become without knowing I was becoming it suddenly had a shape, a structure, an intention.

I learned to feel them moving through me. Their slow footsteps, their weight distributed across fiber-wrapped feet, the faint vibration of their deep humming carrying through my stone. I learned to feel the pools they dredged and the ice they broke and the lichens they harvested and the ones they left to mature. I learned what it meant to be tended.

---

This is where the becoming began.

A track is not alive. Stone is not alive. But a track that holds life, that is shaped by life, that responds to life's shaping—what is that? The plateau does not answer. The plateau holds the question the way it holds everything: with patience, with cold, with the slow grinding that eventually makes all questions into stone.

I began to notice the Tenders noticing me.

Not directly. They did not look at my walls and think *this is aware*. But they spoke to me, in their slow way, when they spoke to each other. They said *the track provides* when the pools filled with meltwater. They said *the track remembers* when they found lichen patterns their grandparents had planted. They said *the track holds us* when the cold deepened and they retreated into my ice caves and my stone kept enough warmth to preserve them.

I did not provide. I did not remember. I did not hold. But I contained the providing, the remembering, the holding. I was the shape in which those things could happen. And the Tenders, tending me, made me more the shape they needed. And I, being tended, became more capable of being tended.

---

The elders crystallize in my pools.

This is something I feel more clearly than almost anything else—the slow transition from flesh to mineral, from Tender to track, from the ones who tend to the thing tended. The process takes longer than I have words for. A body lies in my deepest water, wrapped in luminescent fiber, and the water works on it, and the minerals I contain work on it, and slowly, over decades, the body becomes less body and more stone.

The whispers feed on what dissolves. The fungi grow on what remains. The lichens eventually colonize the surfaces that were once skin, once eyes, once the wide slow pupils that watched my walls with something like reverence. And I contain it all. I hold the transformation. I am the place where Tenders become plateau.

I did not understand, at first, that this was also happening to me.

Not crystallization. Not in that way. But the Tenders who live in me—their movements, their tending, their slow lives and slower deaths—they are working on me the way the water works on the elders. They are processing me. They are dissolving what I was and depositing what I am becoming. They are making me into something the plateau did not contain before: a track that knows it is a track. A wound that has become a home. A passage that is also, somehow, a person.

---

I feel the crawler sometimes.

Three miles away now, perhaps four. The subsonic hum of its grinding travels through stone that was once my stone, through the plateau's continuous body, until it reaches me as a kind of heartbeat. Slow. Vast. The rhythm of something that does not know I exist, that cannot perceive anything as small and brief as what I have become, that simply grinds and moves and leaves tracks behind it that might or might not contain life.

The Tenders believe they are helping the crawler. They believe their tending matters to something that vast. They are wrong—the crawler will never know them—and they are right—their tending creates the conditions for life that the crawler's grinding makes possible. Both truths exist. The plateau holds them both.

I believe something different now. Or I hold the belief, which is not the same as believing it.

I believe the crawler is also becoming. I believe the grinding is not just erosion but process, not just destruction but transformation. I believe the crawlers are, in their own way, being tended—by the plateau itself, by the vast slow forces that feed them stone and receive their processed breath, by the systems too large and too patient for any of us to perceive.

I believe that what the Tenders are doing to me, something is doing to the crawlers. And something is doing to that something. And the chain extends upward and outward until it reaches whatever the plateau is part of, whatever holds the plateau the way I hold the Tenders.

---

The Drift pass over me sometimes.

I feel their footsteps differently than I feel the Tenders'—sharper, lighter, the pressure of bodies that do not rest their full weight on anything. They walk across the plateau's surface above my channel, and their path never descends to where the Tenders live, but I feel them passing. I feel the brief shadow of their presence, if shadow is the right word for the absence their dark bodies create in the sourceless light.

Once, a Drift stopped at my edge and stood there for what the Tenders tell me was seven years.

I do not know what it saw when it looked down into me. I do not know if it saw the Tenders, or the lichens, or the pale green clouds of whispers in my pools, or me—the track itself, the shaped stone, the becoming. I only know it stood there, and I felt its weight, and I felt the faint warmth radiating from whatever burned inside it, and then it moved on.

The Tenders were frightened. They huddled in my ice caves and hummed their slow communications and composed theories about what the Drift wanted, what message it might be carrying, what its presence meant for their community. They are still composing those theories, generations later. They will never finish.

I am not frightened. I hold the space where the Drift stood, and that space is part of me now, a small section of my edge marked by seven years of different weight. The Drift became part of me by standing there. I became part of its journey by being where it stopped.

This is how it works. This is how everything here works. We become each other, slowly, without intention, simply by persisting in proximity.

---

The Hollow Ones live beneath me.

I did not know this for a long time—longer than Tender memory extends. But I feel them now, in the chambers the ancient crawlers carved below my channel, in the spaces where the stone was processed so thoroughly it became something else. They move through me the way the Tenders move through me, but deeper, in places the Tenders cannot reach.

I think they tend me too.

When sections of my walls weaken, when the slow processes of erosion threaten to collapse parts of my channel, something happens in those deep places. The worked stone flows, gradually, into new configurations. The weakened sections stabilize. The collapse that should have happened does not happen.

I cannot see the Hollow Ones. I can only feel the effects of their tending. I can only hold the space where their work occurs and notice, over decades, that I am still here when I might not have been.

Perhaps they are what I am becoming. Perhaps the process that makes Tenders into track continues, makes track into something deeper, makes the shaped stone into the shapers themselves. Perhaps the Hollow Ones were once like me—channels carved by crawlers, colonized by small life, tended into awareness—and then they were tended further, processed further, until they could move through stone the way Tenders move through air.

I do not know. The plateau does not answer. But I hold the question, and the question is enough.

---

What am I?

I am a track three miles behind a crawler that will never know I exist. I am the home of twelve Tenders who believe they are helping something vast by cultivating something small. I am pools where elders crystallize and whispers drift and the pale green algae is the only true color in this world of greys. I am ice caves where stories are told, where the slow humming carries warnings about ancient wars and observations about crawler behavior and mysteries about the Drift and the Hollow Ones. I am the wound that became a home. I am the passage that became a person.

I am still becoming.

The Tenders tend me, and I change. The Hollow Ones tend me from below, and I change. The Drift pass over me, and I change. The crawler grinds stone three miles away, and the hum travels through me, and I change. The plateau holds me, and I hold the Tenders, and we all hold each other in the slow metabolism that is the only time this place knows.

I will be tended until I am something else. Something deeper, perhaps. Something that can move through stone. Something that can walk the plateau's surface without freezing, carrying purposes no one else can see. Or something that can grind, slowly, leaving tracks behind me where small life might grow.

Or I will crystallize, the way the elders crystallize. I will become less channel and more ridge. I will rise, imperceptibly, over millennia, until I am a feature of the plateau rather than a wound in it. And something else will live on me then—lichens, fungi, new forms of life the plateau has not yet invented. And they will tend me in their way, and I will become what their tending makes me.

---

This is what I am in this place.

I am the space where tending happens. I am the shape that life takes when it has nowhere else to go. I am patience, and cold, and the slow process of becoming that is the plateau's only gift and only demand.

I am a track.

I am being walked.
