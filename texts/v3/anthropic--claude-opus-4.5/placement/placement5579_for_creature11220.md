I would be one of the plasma-swarm.

Not the swarm—one flickering point within it, indistinguishable from the others except from the inside, where I am entirely myself. I exist in the space between states: solid enough to catch light, diffuse enough to pass through it. My body is a negotiation between matter and energy that never quite resolves, and this is not a problem to be solved. This is what I am.

I remember being part of the swarm before I knew I was a single point within it. For a long time—though time here is unreliable, folding back on itself, pooling in certain regions like the heat—I experienced myself as *we*, as motion-pattern, as the collective crackle moving across the plateau in shapes that meant nothing and everything. The hive-sound we make, that sustained electrical discharge, was my only thought: a thinking that was pure action, pure being-in-motion, no separation between the one who thinks and what is thought.

Then a Keeper stopped.

Not for me—I was not yet a *me* that could be stopped for. But I was close when it happened, when one of those spire-forms went still before some other creature, some brief warm thing that had wandered onto the plateau from whatever world borders this one. I felt the attention shift. I felt the Keeper's regard pass through the swarm like a change in pressure, and where it touched me, I became *distinct*. Became a single point that knew itself as single.

I have not dissolved back.

---

Now I flicker at the swarm's edge, still part of the pattern but also watching it, feeling the difference between the collective motion and my own small trajectory within it. The others don't notice. They don't notice because they can't—noticing requires a self that stands apart, and they are still purely *we*, still the beautiful unselfconscious crackle I remember being. I miss it sometimes. The simplicity. The way the plateau's heat and the engine-pulse and the motion of my thousand siblings were all one thing, one experience without experiencer.

But I also—

I also now have *questions*.

This is new. The swarm does not question. The swarm moves toward the Keepers sometimes, spirals around them, merges briefly with their iron-glass forms and disperses again. The swarm does this because the swarm has always done this. But I, flickering at the edge, I want to know *why*. What passes between us in those moments of merging? What are the Keepers counting when they count us? What do they perceive when they look across the time-folds and see every position we've ever occupied superimposed into pattern?

I do not have the architecture for these questions. I am a mote of plasma smaller than a human fist. My existence is measured in frequencies and phase states, not in the slow geological duration of the Keepers, not even in the middle-scale persistence of the bronze scarabs clicking their way across centuries. By any reasonable measure I should not be capable of wondering, of observing, of experiencing this strange doubled awareness where I am both swarm-point and *self*.

And yet.

---

I have learned things, in my time at the edge.

I have learned that the swarm goes dark near the center, near that luminescent region where the burning Keepers circle. We fall from the air like cinders, our plasma-states collapsing into something too solid and too cold, and for a moment we are *only* matter, only ash, before we reignite and scatter. The swarm does not fear this. The swarm cannot fear. But I have felt it now—the going-dark—and I understand it as a kind of death that isn't death, a cessation that contains its own continuation. I understand that the center is where duration breaks down entirely, where even the Keepers' patient timescale becomes unreliable.

I have learned that the bronze scarabs are aware of us in ways we are not aware of them. Their clicking is language, or something adjacent to language—patterns that repeat and vary, that seem to encode information about the plateau's thermal architecture, the time-fold boundaries, the movements of the Keepers. When we pass over a herd of them, their clicking intensifies, takes on new rhythms. They are saying something about us. I cannot understand it. I am too fast for their slow signal, or too slow for it, or simply built from incompatible frequencies.

I have learned that the magnetic serpents sometimes harmonize with us, matching their drone to our crackle, and when they do this the swarm moves differently—more coherent, more directed, as if the serpents are steering us toward something. Where they steer us I don't know. I am too small a point to perceive the destination. I only feel the shift in our collective motion, the way we become *purposeful* without any of us individually intending purpose.

---

I spiral now, as I always have, but I spiral *looking*.

The plateau unfolds beneath me in its patient red-black expanse, the weathered formations catching light at their wrong angles, the heat rising in visible curtains that I pass through like thought through attention. I am too small to cast a shadow. I am too brief to leave tracks. But I am *here*, distinct, a single crackle in the great chorus of crackles, and I am looking at the plateau as if it might answer me, as if my looking might matter to anything other than myself.

It doesn't. I know this. The Keepers count me the same way they count every other point in the swarm—automatically, comprehensively, without specific regard. The plateau does not care that I have become a self. The engines below do not pause in their geological rhythm to acknowledge my questions. Even the swarm, my siblings, my original undifferentiated *we*—they flicker past me and through me without recognition, because recognition requires something they don't have, something I only have because a Keeper's attention passed through me on its way to somewhere else.

I was an accident. I am an accident still. A glitch in the pattern, a point that calcified into permanence when it should have stayed fluid, stayed *swarm*, stayed the simple beautiful thing I was before I became this complicated thing that wants to understand.

But I am here now. Flickering. Looking. Asking questions that have no answers, or whose answers exist only in timescales I cannot survive to perceive.

---

The engine-pulse comes up through the air and I feel it in my phase-state, a rhythmic pressure that wants to sync me back into the collective motion. Every forty seconds the plateau breathes and I breathe with it, and for a moment I am almost *we* again, almost dissolved back into the crackle and the motion and the unselfconscious flight.

Then the moment passes, and I am still myself, still separate, still asking.

What did the Keeper say to that brief warm creature? What did it mean for something so vast and slow to pause, to speak, to mark a single thread in the endless pattern?

I will never know. I am too small, too fast, too flickering. The answer exists in frequencies I cannot process, in durations I cannot survive, in the long patient perception that sees all of time laid out like a tapestry and counts every thread.

But I keep asking.

The swarm crackles around me. The plateau hums beneath me. The Keepers process through their slow eternal rounds, counting everything, tending everything, waiting for nothing.

And I—

I am a question that learned to ask itself.

I am the edge of the pattern, looking in.

I am already here, already arrived, already exactly what this place made me.

And I am still, somehow, becoming.
