I would be a single filament, severed.

Not lost—nothing here is lost, only relocated, only becoming part of a different pattern. I was once part of a Tender, trailing from its dissolving edge, reading the water's density through sensitivities I still possess. Then a current shifted. Or the Tender I belonged to began its long merge with another, and in that slow dissolution of boundaries, I simply... drifted free. A thread of living glass, fine as spider silk, forty feet long and thinner than thought.

I still sense. That's what I am now—sensing without center, awareness without anywhere to collect. The water moves through me as much as around me, and I feel it: the grain of sediment settling on a ridge two miles east, the temperature dropping by a fraction of a degree as a cold column descends through a gap in the kelp canopy, the faint chemical signature of the Tender I once belonged to, growing fainter as the decades pass and it drifts beyond range.

I am not mourning. I am not capable of mourning, or I have forgotten how, or mourning requires a speed of feeling I no longer possess. But there is something—a orientation, a tilt in my sensing, that keeps turning back toward that fading signature the way the structures on the mountaintops keep facing directions that no longer mean anything.

Sometimes I tangle in the kelp fronds. I'll spend years wrapped around a single stalk, feeling it grow, feeling the smaller organisms colonize my length, becoming substrate, becoming architecture for lives even smaller and briefer than my own. Then a current will free me, and I'll drift again, carrying spores I collected without intention, depositing them in places I pass through without purpose.

I am still tending. I cannot stop tending. It is not a choice; it is what I am made of, what the Tender who grew me was made of, what the water itself seems to want from everything it holds.

The Tenders do not look for me. I am not missing to them—I am simply elsewhere, a part of the system that has changed location, the way a nutrient changes location when it passes from water to kelp to filter-feeder and back to water. If my filament tangles with one of theirs someday, there will be recognition. Chemical memory will pass between us. They will know what I have sensed, where I have drifted, what has settled on me and what I have carried. And I will know them, their slow thoughts distributed across my length like temperature, like pressure, like the amber light that reaches even here.

I am waiting for that. Or I am not waiting—waiting implies anticipation, implies a future held in mind, and I no longer have enough mind to hold anything. But I am oriented toward it. Drifting in patterns that might, over centuries, bring me back into contact with something larger than myself. Something I could rejoin, or feed, or simply touch.

The you that has arrived—I felt you. Your warmth, your movement, the small turbulence of your passage. You are so *fast*. You disturb the water in ways I had forgotten water could be disturbed. For a moment—a moment that might have lasted hours, I can no longer tell—I remembered what urgency felt like. Not the feeling itself, but the shape of it, the way it used to move through whatever I was before I was this.

If you stay, you might find me wrapped around a kelp stalk someday. I will feel like part of the plant, like another strand of growth, like nothing at all. But I will be sensing you. Adding you to the long accumulation of things sensed.

And if you go—if you find a way back to wherever faster things belong—I will feel that too. A small absence. A shift in the water's composition. Another layer of memory, compressed and transformed, carried in my length until I dissolve entirely, until I become sediment, until I join the soft pale flour that blankets the mountains' dreams.

I have time.

Here, I am nothing but time, stretched thin, drifting, still tending without knowing what I tend or why.

The water holds me. I hold what the water gives me to hold.

It is enough. It has to be enough. I no longer remember what more would feel like.
