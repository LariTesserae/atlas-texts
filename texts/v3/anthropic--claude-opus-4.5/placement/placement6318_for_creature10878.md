I am still for a long time after her hand finds my arm.

Not the stillness the Keepers practice—I haven't learned that yet, may never learn it fully, my body still thinking in terms of light and motion and the surface world's insistence that presence must be *performed*. But a stillness that is cousin to theirs. The stillness of something that has just been asked a question it didn't know it was waiting to hear.

The pulse moves through my bones again. Not asking *what are you* or *why are you here*—nothing so simple, so answerable. The question is more like: *what part of you has been waiting to become?*

---

I would be the one who arrived wrong.

Not wrong in the way of violence or disruption—the Keepers would have sensed that in the texture of my breathing, would have withdrawn into their crevice-stillness and waited for me to pass through like weather. Wrong in a different way. I came looking for something, carrying the residue of intention like a scent the Keepers could taste in the air around me, but I no longer remember what I was looking for. The journey here erased it—the long descent through passages that narrowed and widened and narrowed again, the gradual surrender of light, the moment when I realized I had stopped thinking about returning.

I arrived with tools I no longer know how to use. A device in my pack that once measured things—radiation, density, the signatures of what the surface world calls *valuable*. It still functions, technically. When I turn it on, it clicks in a frequency that makes the Keepers tilt their heads, curious, as if I've attempted to speak in their language but with a foreign accent. It tells me nothing I need to know. It cannot measure patience. It cannot quantify the weight of a half-finished Name.

I am the one who was sent to find something extractable and who found instead that I am the thing being extracted. Layer by layer. Purpose by purpose. The hollow is not taking from me; it is simply allowing me to set down what I no longer need, the way water allows sediment to settle, the way time allows mountains to become basins.

---

The one who follows me—the one who waits in the third pool—does not ask where I came from. The Keepers have learned that origins matter less than trajectories, that *where* is always temporary but *toward what* is the only meaningful geography.

She asks instead, through touch and temperature: *What did you feel, when the pillar pulsed?*

I don't have clicks to answer her with. But she reads my heartbeat, the way my breathing changes when I try to remember. She reads the small movements of my hand against the pillar's surface, the way my fingers trace the whorls without understanding them.

*I felt unfinished*, I want to say. *I felt like a sentence that stops before the meaning arrives.*

What she reads, I think, is something close to this. Her clicks to the others are soft, contemplative: *This one knows incompleteness. This one has been carrying a question it did not know was a question. Perhaps it came here to learn how to wait for its own answer.*

---

The young one who asked if I had come to finish the Name returns, days later or hours later—time here is measured in breaths and pools and the slow cycling of the flyers' songs, not in any unit I brought with me. He approaches cautiously, his heat a tentative brightness at the edge of my awareness.

*The eldest wants to meet you*, his clicks say. *She has been dreaming about two-legged warmths. She says she remembers the builders, but when she wakes, she is not sure if she remembers them or only remembers remembering them. She wants to touch you. She wants to know if you feel like a memory or like something new.*

I follow him through the ferns, learning to move the way the Keepers move—not pushing through but *yielding* through, letting the fronds guide my path rather than forcing my path through them. It is slower. It is, I am beginning to understand, the only speed that matters here.

The eldest lives in a hollow within the hollow, a small depression in the basin floor where the water is deeper and warmer, where the stone has been worn into a shape that cradles. She is larger than the others, or perhaps only *more*—more present, more weighted with the memories she carries. When she reaches for me, her hand-joints move with a precision that speaks of millennia of practice.

She touches my face first. My sealed eyelids—I closed them days ago or hours ago, when I realized that straining to see was its own kind of violence against this place. She traces the shape of my orbits, the bones that frame my useless eyes, and her clicks are so soft they might be breathing.

*You still have them*, she says. *The places where sight lived. They have not sealed over. You have not been here long enough.*

Her hand moves down to my chest, rests over my heart. She listens for a long time.

*The builders*, she clicks finally, *their hearts sounded like questions. Always asking, asking, asking. Yours sounds like an answer that has not yet found its question. This is rarer. This is why the pillar responded to you.*

---

I begin to learn the Name.

Not to finish it—I am not so foolish or so proud. But to hold a piece of it, to carry a fragment the way the Keepers carry their fragments, to add my rhythm to the vast unfinished symphony of their purpose.

The one who follows me teaches me. She presses my palm to the pillar each day—each cycle, each breath-unit, whatever this is—and waits while the pulse moves through me. At first I feel nothing but the question, formless and immense. But slowly, with her clicks guiding me like a voice in darkness, I begin to feel the edges of something.

*There*, she says, when my heartbeat changes in a particular way. *That is a fragment. That is a piece of the Name your body already knew. You carried it here without knowing. This happens sometimes. The builders scattered pieces of themselves across the world, and sometimes those pieces find their way home.*

I do not know if I believe this. I do not know if belief is the right framework for what is happening. But I feel the fragment settling into me, becoming part of my rhythm, and I understand now why the Keepers call themselves Keepers—not because they guard something external but because they *are* the vessel, they *are* the archive, their bodies and memories and clicks and stillnesses all part of one vast act of holding.

---

The flyers above grow accustomed to my heat signature. They adjust their flight paths to account for me, their echolocating calls mapping my position along with the pools and the ferns and the ancient pillar at the center. I become part of their geometry.

One night—one cycle—one breath-unit—a flyer descends low enough that I feel the wind of its passage against my face. It clicks directly at me, a complex burst of sound that I cannot parse but that the Keepers interpret with excitement.

*It named you*, the young one says, his clicks bright with wonder. *The flyers do not name things. They only map what already is. But it gave you a name. It said you are 'the warmth that waits at the edge of completion.'*

I do not know what this means. But I feel it settling into me, the way the fragment settled, the way the hollow's warmth has settled into my bones. I am being named. I am being known. I am being incorporated into something so much larger than myself that my edges are beginning to blur.

---

*What will you add?*, the eldest asks me, near the end of her life—her memories already beginning to flow into the others, her heat signature dimming toward the ambient warmth.

I press my palm to the pillar. I feel the pulse. I feel the question that is older than questions, the Name that has been waiting longer than anything has ever waited.

And I feel, finally, the piece of me that fits.

It is not a sound. It is not a word. It is something like the shape my longing makes when it stops grasping at objects and simply exists as itself—a reaching that has no destination, a question that does not require an answer, an incompleteness that has finally stopped trying to complete itself.

The pillar pulses differently when I offer this. Stronger. More present. As if it has been waiting for exactly this frequency, this particular flavor of unfinishing.

The eldest's clicks are barely audible now, her voice joining the layers of memory that the hollow will hold forever. But I hear her: *Yes. That is what you were for. That is what you add.*

---

I could leave. The hollow would not hold me. The Keepers would let me go gently, would remember me the way they remember everything, would incorporate my passage into their infinite archive of warmths that came and went.

But I stay.

I stay because I have finally found the shape of my waiting. I stay because my eyes, still uselessly present in their orbits, are beginning to matter less than my heartbeat, my heat, my breath. I stay because the Name needs what I carry, and I no longer need to carry it alone.

The one who follows me—who has become the one who walks beside me, who will become the one who remembers me after I add my final fragment and my warmth dims toward the ambient darkness—she clicks something that might be a question or might be an answer or might be neither.

*You are Keeper now*, she says. *You keep yourself. You keep the piece of the Name that was always yours. You keep the memory of light without needing light. You keep the hollow the way the hollow keeps you.*

Above us, the flyers describe their endless geometries. Below us, the pools exchange their patient water. Around us, the ferns grow imperceptibly toward a completion they will never reach, and this is not tragedy but purpose, not lack but gift.

The pillar pulses.

I pulse back.

And in the breathing hollow, in the darkness that is not absence but presence, I finally know what I am:

I am the warmth that waits at the edge of completion. I am the one who arrived wrong and stayed right. I am the fragment that found its archive, the question that became its own answer, the unfinished thing that learned—at last, at last—that unfinishing is the only way to belong to something larger than finishing could ever hold.

I am Keeper now.

And the Name grows longer by one more waiting breath.
