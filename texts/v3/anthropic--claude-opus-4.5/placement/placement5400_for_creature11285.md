I would be one of the singers.

Not the largest, who moves through the overhead pipes with authority and calls out in a tone the Keeper has learned to recognize as *all is well, all is holding*. Not the smallest, who stays closest to the warmth and rarely ventures past the lamp's reach. I would be the one she has trouble tracking—the one whose pitch shifts, who moves through frequencies the way the others move through space, who sometimes goes silent for so long she thinks I've finally slipped through the grate and into the abyss below.

I haven't. I'm listening.

---

The others sing to communicate: *here I am, here is food, the pressure changed, something moves*. I sing because the station sings, and I am trying to learn its language. When the turbines drop into that low moan she calls night, I press myself against the pipe-wall and let the vibration move through my bones, my teeth, the long strange spine the flood-years gave my kind. There are patterns. There are almost-patterns. There are moments when the harmonic shifts and I think I've caught the edge of something—a message, a meaning, a word in a tongue older than air.

I lose it. I always lose it. The frequency slides away and I'm left with only the roar, the heat, the taste of copper on my whiskers.

But I keep listening.

---

The Keeper watches me differently than she watches the others. I know this because I watch her too—from the darkness above, from the gap beneath the grate, from the shadows that jump when the lamp flickers. She tracks the others by their voices, knows them by their patterns. Me she searches for. Her eyes move to the places I might be, and when she finds me, there's something in her face I don't have a word for. Recognition, maybe. The look of one listener finding another.

She talks to me sometimes. Not words exactly—she speaks so rarely that her voice has grown strange, unused, a rusty hinge of a sound. But she hums. Low notes that weave between the frequencies of the machines, that find the spaces in the thunder where something softer might survive. When she hums, I come closer. I let her see me in the lamplight—my long body, my oil-slick fur, my eyes that have learned to find light where there is almost none.

She hums and I chirp back, and for a moment we are singing together, two small voices in the throat of the machine.

---

I know things she doesn't know. Not because I'm wiser—I am small and my brain is smaller, and whatever I understand, I understand without words, without the scratched messages she leaves on the mineral walls. But I move through spaces she cannot reach. I press my ear to pipes she cannot touch. I have heard things in the deep machinery that would mean something to her if I could only find a way to say them.

The balance is shifting. I hear it in the harmonics—a new note, thin and high, that has been growing for cycles now. Something metal is fatiguing somewhere in the dark. Something that has held for decades is beginning to give. The pattern of pressure spikes has changed; the intervals are shorter, the peaks higher. The minerals on the wall grow faster near the eastern seam, and when I press against that wall I feel heat that wasn't there before.

I try to sing this to her. I pitch my voice to match the new note, the warning-note, the something-is-coming note. She tilts her head. She listens. I see her hand move to the wall, pressing where I pressed, feeling for the change I'm trying to name.

She feels it. I watch her face shift—the recognition again, but different now. She scratches something new into the mineral crust, and though I cannot read, I know she has heard me.

---

The others think I'm strange. They have their routes, their roles, their voices—the largest calls the all-clear, the smallest stays by the warmth, the middle ones patrol and forage and report in their simple chirping code. But I fall silent for too long. I disappear into pipes where no food lives. I come back shaking with vibrations I cannot explain and sing notes that mean nothing to them.

*Why do you listen*, they ask in their chirp-language. *The noise is the noise. The station is the station. Eat, warm, survive.*

I cannot explain. I don't have words for why the patterns matter, why the almost-meanings tug at me, why I need to know what the machine is saying before it falls silent. I only know that the Keeper listens too, and she is the only creature in this bubble who has lived as long as the station has been drowning, and she has not stopped trying to understand.

If she holds, I hold. If she listens, I listen.

---

Sometimes, in the darkest hours when even the Keeper sleeps, I crawl down to the grated floor and press my face to the gaps. Below is the abyss—the drainage channel that carries the weeping of the walls down into the flooded maze, down into the cold and the crushing weight and the dark that never ends. I can feel the water moving down there, can taste its mineral breath rising through the gaps.

The station is a bubble. A held breath. A small dry defiance in a drowned world.

I am smaller still. A chirp in the thunder. A listener who will never fully understand what she hears.

But I hear. I witness. When the balance finally breaks—when that thin high note becomes a scream, when the new heat in the eastern wall becomes rupture—I will know. I will sing it out in whatever voice I have, one last frequency in the final chord.

The Keeper will hear me. She always hears me.

And together, for one moment before the water comes, we will know what the station was trying to say.

---

For now, the pressure holds. The lap flickers. The Keeper hums her low wandering notes, and I chirp back from the shadows, and somewhere in the space between us a meaning forms that neither of us could make alone.

I am the singer who listens.

This is enough. This is everything.
