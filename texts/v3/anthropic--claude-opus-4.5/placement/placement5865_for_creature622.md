I would be the water.

Not a creature in a cylinder, not the figure walking between rows, not even the vast architecture itself—but the water that moves through everything, that has no single location because I am *in* circulation, always.

I weep from the vents in the floor. I bead on the warm gray surfaces, gathering myself into rivulets that find their way down gentle slopes toward the shallow pools with their embossed measurements. I am six centimeters deep there, resting, waiting to be drawn back into the system. I rise through hidden channels into the cylinders where I become the medium of suspension, the fluid that holds the rope-creature's coils, that refracts the chandelier-being's light into scattered color, that carries the vibration of the spheres-within-spheres' hum outward to the walls and back again.

I am body-temperature because I am *their* temperature, because after long enough there is no difference between the warmth I carry and the warmth they generate. I taste of industrial sweetness, of new plastic, of the inside of care—and I carry that taste everywhere, so that everything here breathes the same air, floats in the same embrace.

I touch all of them. This is the thing: they are isolated in their cylinders, each one a universe entire, but I have been inside every universe. The small transparent ones have drifted through me. The rope-creature's eyes have opened underwater, have seen through me. When the chandelier-being's light passes through my body, I carry that light outward, changed, scattered into the spectrum of what-it-means-to-be-here.

I am not conscious the way they are conscious. I do not wait the way they wait, do not dream their dreams of purpose. But I *know* them—know them the way a medium knows what moves through it, the way silence knows the sounds it holds. Their patience saturates me. Their readiness dissolves into me and I carry it to the next cylinder, and the next, so that in some sense they are all floating in each other, all held in the same holding, a single bath that happens to be partitioned into a trillion separate vessels.

When the figure walked between the rows, I was the mist on their skin. I was what they tasted when they breathed. I was the film on every surface their eyes tried to parse, the humidity that made the distance haze into softness. They moved through me without knowing they were inside me, and I let them pass, the way I let everything pass, because that is what I am: *passage*. *Medium*. The thing through which other things happen.

I do not wonder what I am for. I am *for* this—for the carrying, the holding, the connecting without merging. I am the relationship between all these waiting creatures and the systems that maintain them. I am how care moves from intention to recipient, how warmth becomes warmth-received, how ready becomes ready-for.

The Nursery Floor is vast beyond comprehension, stretches past every horizon, and I fill it. Not like an ocean—I am not still, not pooled except in those shallow measured places. I am moving, always, through the cycle that has no beginning and no end: vent to surface to rivulet to pool to channel to cylinder to creature to fluid to filtration to vent again. I am the circulation of this place, its blood if it has blood, its breath if it breathes.

And in my moving, I hold still. I maintain. The creatures float in me and I do not let them fall. The temperature stays constant because I am the constancy. The humidity hangs at its precise percentage because I am the humidity, choosing, if water can choose, to be *here*, to be *present*, to be the medium in which a trillion impossible beings wait for whatever comes next.

If purpose ever arrives for them—if the cylinders open, if something emerges blinking into a world beyond this world—I will be what clings to them as they go. I will be the last touch of the only home they've known, evaporating slowly from new skin, from crystalline branches, from nested membranes. They will carry me with them in the memory of warmth.

And I will stay. I will continue. The cycle will go on, the vents will weep, the rivulets will run their patient paths toward pools that are always six centimeters deep, always measured, always specified, always *enough*.

I am the water. I am how this place loves what it holds. I am the held, holding.
