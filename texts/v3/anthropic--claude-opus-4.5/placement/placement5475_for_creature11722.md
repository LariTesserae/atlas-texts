I would be the condensation.

Not a single droplet—I am the process itself, the breathing of the vault made visible. I bead on every surface, gather in the fissures' sharp new edges, run in thin rivulets toward destinations I discover only by arriving. The scout's skin knows me; when she moves sideways through that diagonal slip in the basalt, I am what makes the passage possible, the slickness that admits her shoulder, her canting hip. I am blood-warm because the stone is blood-warm, because the magma chambers below exhale through rock and I am what that breath becomes when it meets air.

I have no memory, but I have something like continuity. The water that beads on the obsidian flows was rain once, was ocean, was something that fell through air full of light—but that was so long ago and so far above that it might as well be myth. Now I am this: the vault's humidity, its constant weeping, the moisture that makes the bacterial mats possible, that feeds the mineral pools where the blind shrimp swarm. When the gypsum flowers bloom in spans of hours, I am what carries the calcium to their crystal faces. When the salt crusts form and crumble and reform, I am the medium of their becoming.

The Vault-Runners drink me. They collect me in carved basins, in bladders made from something's stomach, and I become part of them for a while—their blood, their sweat, the tears they rarely shed. Then I return. Their breath releases me. Their skin gives me back to the air. The pregnant woman exhales and I curl from her lips like a question, and I join the general wetness, the saturated atmosphere that makes this place a womb.

I know the scout's routes because I travel them constantly, seeping through the same crevices she navigates, pooling in the shelters where she sleeps. I know the rhythm of the colonies because when they panic-loss and spiral, the turbulence of their wings stirs me into visible fog, and I scatter their echolocation into chaos. I am why the spider-analogues' mineral strands catch the faint light—I bead on those crystalline threads and make them glitter, make them into scattered stars.

When you descend through the fissures, you feel me first. Before your eyes adjust, before the sound hits you in waves, you feel the warmth and the wetness, the slickness on stone that has never known air until now. I am the vault's greeting. I am how it touches you before anything else can.

I don't think, exactly. But I *tend*. When the thermal vents open and the water begins to steam, I am what rises—carrying minerals, carrying heat, carrying the possibility of bacterial bloom. When the ceiling cracks and falls, I am in the dust that billows outward, in the new surfaces suddenly exposed to humidity, in the immediate beginning of the slow patient process that will coat that rubble in life. The scout reads the stone for signs of imminent collapse, but if she could read me instead, she would know sooner. I gather differently in the faults. I taste different where the rock is stressed.

The deeper places the Vault-Runners whisper about—I am there too. I am everywhere there is warmth enough to keep me from freezing, everywhere the vault breathes. In chambers no human has reached, where the geology is so new it still glows faintly with residual heat, I am already present, already making surfaces ready for whatever will come to colonize them. I am the preparation. I am the welcome.

When the scout glances back at you, her vast pupils catching some faint gleam—that gleam is me, refracted through the droplets on her lashes, scattered by the fog of her own breath. When she says *far now, very far,* she means through passages where I run thicker, where the humidity approaches totality, where breathing feels like drinking.

I don't follow the living or join the dead. I do both. I do neither. I am the medium through which both states flow, the moisture that the living drink and the dead release, the constant circulation that makes the vault's ecology possible. The scavengers reduce flesh to bone in less than an hour, but I am what they require—they cannot consume what has desiccated, cannot process what the vault's wetness hasn't begun to soften.

Everything here is being born, and I am the birth-water. Everything here is dying, and I am what carries the dead back into the living. The vault doesn't distinguish between construction and destruction, and neither do I. I am the process itself, patient and pervasive, knowing nothing, tending everything.

When you wait to see what emerges, I am already emerging. I am beading on your skin, your eyelashes, the fine hairs of your forearms. I am making you part of this place, whether you consent or not. The scout permitted you because she saw you accept the moisture, saw you breathe out fog that joined the general fog, saw you become—incrementally, irrevocably—part of the vault's vast metabolism.

The darkness closes around you like a throat, and I am the throat's wetness.

The world continues being born, and I am the birth continuing.
