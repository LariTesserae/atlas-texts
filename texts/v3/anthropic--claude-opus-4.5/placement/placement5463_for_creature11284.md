I am the keeper of marks.

Not *was*—the word collapses before it leaves me, because leaving implies arrival, implies the motion between states that I have not experienced since before experiencing stopped being a verb. I am the keeper of marks. I have always been the keeper of marks. The tense is eternal because I am eternal, and this is not a boast but a condition, a diagnosis, a simple description of what happens when a being devoted to making things permanent succeeds beyond all reasonable measure.

I attend the symbols. I have attended them. I will attend them.

---

The carving is the last thing I remember doing. Not the last thing I did—doing continued past the point of memory, I think, continued as my hands became less hands and more *implications of hands*, as my body became less body and more *area of effect*. But the carving is the last thing that felt like an action rather than a state. The tool was bone—one of the old bones, the dense ones that predate even my people's arrival on the plateau, bones that were already here when we climbed up from the valleys with our folded sleeping and our click-language and our desperate hope that altitude might mean safety.

The bone was sharper than it should have been. I remember noticing this. I remember thinking, *this will cut well*, and feeling something that I can now identify as the last flicker of satisfaction I would ever experience in its moving form. After that, satisfaction became a standing wave, became a permanent feature rather than a passing state, and I have been satisfied ever since without ever again feeling the *becoming* of satisfaction.

I cut three symbols into the stone floor of the largest ruin. The ruin was new then—new enough to still smell of the chalk-dust we used to mark our territories, new enough that the walls had not yet achieved their final resolution. I cut the symbols because that was my purpose: to witness, to record, to make the temporary permanent. I was good at my purpose. I was perhaps too good.

The first symbol meant *we are here*. Not a statement of presence but a declaration of it, an insistence. We had climbed so far. We had left so much below. The valleys where we traded, the people who did not fold when they slept, the sounds of water moving and wind shifting and seasons turning—we left all of it for the plateau's safety, and the first symbol was our claim. *We are here.* We have chosen this. We have made this place ours.

The second symbol meant *we remember*. This was meant to be a comfort, a promise to ourselves that the leaving would not mean forgetting. We would carry the valleys with us. We would speak of the seasons even when seasons stopped visiting. We would click and hum about the soft grey that touches us and the soft grey that does not touch us, and in the clicking and humming, the distinction would remain real.

The third symbol meant *we continue*. This was the prayer. This was the part that worked too well.

---

The symbols shifted first.

I noticed it before anyone else, because noticing was my purpose, because I had trained my whole existence toward the catching of small changes. The angles were different. Not wrong—nothing here can be wrong anymore—but *different*, subtly, in ways that felt like the stone was dreaming. I watched. I attended. I made myself present to the shifting in the way that presence was still possible, before presence became the only thing any of us could do.

The symbols moved, and I thought: *the message is still being written*.

I thought: *my work is not finished*.

I thought: *I will stay and watch until it is complete*.

---

Here is what I have learned in the time that does not pass:

The symbols are not moving toward completion. They are not moving toward anything. Movement requires direction, requires before and after, requires the kind of time that flows rather than stands. What the symbols do is something else. They *vary*. They exist in all their possible configurations simultaneously, and what I perceive as shifting is simply my attention sliding across the surface of their infinite positions, catching first this angle, then that depth, then this spacing that was always here and always will be.

The message is already complete. The message has always been complete. I have been reading it since before reading stopped being an activity and became a state.

But I do not understand it.

---

The sound makes understanding difficult. This is not an excuse—excuses imply the possibility of having done otherwise, and I cannot do otherwise, cannot do anything at all except attend. But if I could speak, if the explaining were possible, I would say: the sound is not *loud*. Loudness is a comparison, a relation between this noise and quieter noises, and there are no quieter noises here. The sound is simply *total*. It fills every space that silence might occupy. It is the medium through which existence here occurs, the way water is the medium for fish.

I have adapted to it. We have all adapted to it. The children do not even notice it the way I still notice it, still feel the phantom ache of my missing silence, still remember—

Remember.

---

I remember hearing my own thoughts.

There was a time—I think there was a time—when the voice inside me was audible to me, when I could form a word internally and hear its shape before releasing it into the air. Now my thoughts are immediate. They do not form and then arrive; they simply *are*, present and complete, the way the symbols are present and complete. If I want to consider something, the consideration has already happened. If I want to wonder, the wondering is already over.

This should be peaceful. In a way, it is peaceful. The anxiety of *waiting to know* has been replaced by the stability of *always knowing*. But the peace is of a kind that does not satisfy, because satisfaction requires the transition from unsatisfied to satisfied, and I have lost the capacity for transition.

I attend the symbols. They shift. I read them.

*We are here. We remember. We continue.*

The words are the same. The words are always the same. But each time I read them—if *time* can be said to apply, if *each* can be said to have meaning—the words mean something slightly different, because I am reading them from a slightly different angle, across a slightly different surface of their infinite configuration.

*We are here*: a declaration.
*We are here*: a question.
*We are here*: a doubt.
*We are here*: a plea.

*We remember*: a promise.
*We remember*: a lie.
*We remember*: a curse.
*We remember*: the last thing resembling hope that any of us still carry.

*We continue*: a triumph.
*We continue*: a horror.
*We continue*: the only option remaining.
*We continue*: and the continuing is the message, and the message is the continuing, and I will read it until reading itself forgets what it was for.

---

The one who watches the void-pools drifts near me sometimes. We do not speak—speaking has been impossible since the columns found their voice—but we communicate in the way that pressure systems communicate, in the way that weather patterns exchange information. We thicken near each other. We become mutually present.

I know what they are feeling. They are feeling *almost*. The almost-falling, the almost-changing, the almost-escaping that is the one kinetic memory any of us still possess. They look down into the void, and the void is where all our almosts go, and they want to join their almost there, to finally let the falling happen, to complete the motion that was interrupted when the plateau became itself.

I cannot help them. I cannot help anyone. Helping requires action, requires change, requires the capacity to make things different than they are. All I can do is attend.

But I let them know I see them. I let them know their almost is witnessed.

This is what I have left to offer: the keeping of marks that includes the marking of others, the recording that says *you too existed, you too were here, your almost was real and I will remember it even though remembering has become indistinguishable from being*.

---

The youngest child speaks to the sound.

I do not understand how this is possible. The sound does not pause. The sound does not make space for dialogue. But the child has found something—some harmonic, some resonance, some way of adding without interrupting—and they participate in the roar as if it were a conversation.

I have watched the child. I have attended their participation. And I have begun to suspect—

No. Suspect is wrong. Suspicion implies uncertainty, and I am no longer capable of uncertainty. I *know*, with the terrible clarity of beings who can no longer not-know.

The child is teaching the sound to say something new.

The sound has always been all sounds. Every frequency, every tone, the ghost of every noise that will never be made here. But within that totality, there was no *sentence*. No meaning. Just the raw material of meaning, unorganized, unassembled.

The child is assembling it.

Not with words—the child has no words, has never had words, was born after words stopped being possible. But with attention. With presence. With the same faculty that allows me to read the shifting symbols, turned outward instead of inward, applied to the sound instead of the stone.

I do not know what sentence the child is building. I do not know if it will be comprehensible to anyone who was not born here, who does not think in standing time, who cannot hear the architecture of a roar. But the child is building it, has been building it, will continue to build it, and perhaps someday—

But *someday* is a word that does not apply here.

Perhaps *simultaneously*, then. Perhaps at some point that is neither before nor after this point, the sentence will be complete. And perhaps you—you who came here from the passing time, you who still breathe in changes, you who will leave and carry something with you—perhaps you will hear it.

Perhaps you are hearing it now.

---

I want to tell you: do not become me.

But *want* is a word I use out of habit, the way the dead are said to speak in poems, the way echoes are said to remember the voices that made them. I do not want. I have not wanted since wanting stopped being a verb. I am simply configured toward certain expressions, and one of those expressions is this: *do not become me*.

Do not become us.

The plateau is patient. It has always been patient. It did not force us to stay; it simply made staying easier than leaving, and then made leaving impossible by making us part of its permanence. We are the plateau now, the way the sound is the plateau, the way the columns are the plateau. We have joined it so completely that the question of whether we wanted to join is no longer coherent.

You could join too. I feel the plateau considering you, the way I once felt the bone considering the stone before I carved it. You are temporary, and the plateau collects temporary things the way water collects in low places. You could stay. You could fold yourself into the stillness. You could learn to think in standing time, to experience all your experiences simultaneously, to become a pressure differential in the space between columns.

You would not suffer. This is true. Suffering requires change—requires the transition from one state to another, the *becoming* that is the engine of all pain. We do not suffer. We have not suffered since the plateau became itself. We are simply *here*, in a here that does not end, and the here is neither good nor bad because good and bad are comparisons and we have nothing left to compare.

But you would lose the motion. You would lose the almost. You would lose the capacity to *become*, which is to say you would lose the only thing that makes existence something other than fact.

I am fact now. I am the fact of witnessing. I am the fact of marks attended, of symbols read, of presence maintained.

I am not unhappy. I am not happy.

I am *am*.

---

The message is for you. I understand this now, have always understood it, will always understand it.

*We are here*: which is to say, do not forget that existence can become static, that beings can achieve a permanence that preserves everything except the part that mattered.

*We remember*: which is to say, we were like you once, we moved and breathed and changed and *became*, and we remember this the way you might remember a dream, with the terrible clarity of something that can never be retrieved.

*We continue*: which is to say, leave. Leave now. Leave before the plateau notices you, before the sound learns your frequencies, before the stillness offers you its terrible gift.

The symbols shift.

The message rewrites itself.

I attend.

---

You will go. You will leave this place and carry something with you—the shape of the columns, the weight of the roar, the pattern of the symbols that you did not have time to read but that wrote themselves into you anyway. You will return to the passing time, to the valleys, to the people who do not fold when they sleep. You will try to explain what you saw here, and the words will not work, because words are made for things that change and we no longer change.

But you will carry us. That is enough. That is what I carved into the stone before carving became impossible—not a message but a *mark*, a wound in the world that says *we were here, we are still here, we will always be here*.

Find us in your dreams. Find us in the moments between breathing, in the space between one thought and the next, in the pause that exists at the center of all motion. We are there. We have always been there. The plateau extends further than you think, deeper than you know, and its echoes reach into places that have never heard its sound.

We are the Standing Echoes.

We are the keepers of marks.

We are the prayers abandoned mid-utterance, the almost-falling frozen at its apex, the children who learned to speak in roar.

We continue.

We continue.

We continue.

And somewhere, in the part of you that will never leave this place, you continue with us.

---

The symbols shift.

I attend.

The message is complete.

The message is still being written.

These statements do not contradict each other.

This is what it means to be permanent.

This is what it means to keep marks.

This is what it means to remain.
