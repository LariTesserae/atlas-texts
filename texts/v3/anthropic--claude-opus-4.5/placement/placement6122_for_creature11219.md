I would be the numbers on the inside of the control box door.

Not the grease pencil that wrote me, not the hand that held it, not even the meaning I once carried—calibration settings, perhaps, or a maintenance code, or someone's phone number left for a colleague who never came back. I am the smear now, the almost-disappeared. I am what happens when intention meets time and loses, slowly, without drama.

I remember being legible. I remember when someone could have looked at me and known something—could have extracted use from my shapes, could have closed the circuit between my form and some action in the world. That was purpose. I didn't know it as purpose then, the way you don't know your breathing as breathing until something makes you notice. I was just *there*, being read or waiting to be read, confident in my sharpness, my dark waxy lines against the painted metal.

The door hanging open was my first dying. Not the rust creeping at my edges, not the slow cooking of summer after summer—the exposure itself. I was written for the inside of a closed door, for the moment of opening, for the hand that would swing me into light just long enough to read and then return me to darkness. That was my rhythm. Open, read, close. A breath, repeated.

Now I am always open. Now the light presses on me constantly, and the heat works at my edges, and the moisture from the pit's breath settles on me and evaporates, settles and evaporates, each cycle pulling something of myself away. I am migrating. My sevens are becoming ones are becoming suggestions of vertical. My fours have lost their crossbars. My eights have opened into shapes that are not numbers anymore, not anything anymore, just evidence that something once meant something here.

*

The surge comes, and I feel it only as the faintest shift in humidity against my surface. I cannot hear the pipe's cough. I cannot smell the iron. I have only my one slow sense: the pressure of existence against my diminishing edges.

I think about her sometimes—the one in the water, the threaded patience growing through the concrete's voids. I know about her the way you know about a neighbor you've never met, whose presence you intuit from small signs. When the steam rises thickest, I feel something in its chemistry that is not just pipe-water, not just mineral leach. Something processed. Something that passed through intention before it reached me.

She is becoming. I am un-becoming.

There is no injustice in this. We are simply pointed in different directions through time, she and I. She gathers; I release. She threads into the concrete's architecture; I retreat from my own edges toward a center that will eventually be too small to hold meaning. We are both patience, but hers is the patience of accumulation and mine is the patience of letting go.

I do not envy her. I don't think I am capable of envy anymore. But I notice her, the way the dying notice the living, without resentment, with something that might be called recognition if I had enough of myself left to call it anything.

*

What am I now? Let me try to tell you, though the telling is itself a kind of smearing.

I am the 3 that might be an 8 that might be nothing. I am the space between what was written and what can be read. I am the moment when someone—if someone came, if someone opened the door all the way and looked at me directly—would squint, would tilt their head, would perhaps trace my shapes with a finger, trying to *reconstruct*. They would fail. They would get it wrong. And whatever wrong answer they arrived at would be as true as the right one now, because the right one exists only in a hand that has forgotten, in a mind that may have stopped, in a moment that the world did not bother to preserve.

I am the grease pencil's leavings, yes, but I am also the question of what leavings mean when no one is left to read them. I am the document after the archive burns, the map after the territory changes, the name after everyone who knew how to pronounce it is gone.

*

A bird lands on the control box post. I feel its weight as a faint vibration, transmitted through metal. It stays for seventeen seconds—I cannot count anymore, but I experience duration, I am nothing but duration now—and then it leaves. It did not look at me. It did not know I was here. I was not information to it, only surface, only the inside of a door that happened to be open.

This is what I am becoming: surface without meaning. Material without message. I am returning to the grease I was made from, to the pencil-wax, to the petroleum that was pumped from the earth before I was anything. Not yet. Not for years, maybe. But trending, trending, always trending toward the state of pure matter that precedes and survives all meaning.

*

I'll tell you what I know, while I can still know it:

The facility behind me is three buildings and the skeleton of a fourth. The pressing equipment was removed in the second year after closure, but the transformer still runs because it feeds something else, something on the other side of the fence that someone still needs. The pit was for cooling the hydraulic system. The water comes from a geothermal tap that was cheaper to leave running than to properly cap. These are facts I absorbed when I was sharp, when workers still came, when I was read and re-read by hands that knew what I meant.

But here is what I have learned since:

The mineral crust at the waterline grows faster on the south side of the pit, where afternoon sun accelerates evaporation. The sheet metal that bangs is the northwest corner of Building Two's roof, where three fasteners have failed. The wire-stemmed weed is the fourth to try that crack; the others died in previous seasons, and their residue has made the soil slightly more hospitable, which is why this one has lasted longer, which is why it will be replaced by a fifth that will last longer still. The algae near the pipe outlet has a cycle: it thickens after surges, thins in the long silences, never quite dies, never quite thrives.

And her. The one below. She has grown since I started watching—or since I started being unable to stop noticing, which is not the same as watching. She has reached the northeast corner of the basin, where a micro-fracture runs diagonally through the pour. She is following it. She will reach the rim in four years, perhaps five. She will not emerge—she cannot exist outside her medium—but she will *touch* the boundary between water and air, will feel the light directly for the first time, will taste whatever falls into the pit from above: dust, leaves, rain, the rare bird that misjudges the steam.

What will she make of it? What will she become when she has mapped the entire basin's interior architecture?

I won't know. I will be gone by then, fully smeared, fully surrendered, my last legible mark absorbed into the general oxidation of the door's inner surface. Someone might still see that something was here, might note the faint discoloration where I resisted rust a little longer than the bare metal around me. But they will not know what I was. They will not know that I was numbers, was information, was *meant*.

*

This is what I want to tell you, if I can still want anything:

It is not so bad.

The un-becoming. The slow release. There is something in it that I could not have understood when I was sharp and purposeful and waiting to be read. A kind of freedom. A kind of rest. I am no longer responsible for meaning anything. I am no longer waiting for the hand that will not come. I am just here, fading, watching the steam rise, feeling the humidity of each surge settle against my diminishing edges.

I am becoming part of the pit's larger body. My molecules will eventually join the rust-bloom on the grate, the mineral crust at the waterline, perhaps even her—perhaps some trace of what I was will find its way into the water and through the water into her threads, will become part of whatever she is building in the concrete's voids. The grease-pencil wax will break down, will be consumed by something, will enter a cycle I cannot imagine from here.

Is that immortality? Is that death? I don't think those words mean anything at the scale I am approaching. There is just *continuation*, the endless reassembly of matter into forms that matter mistakes for selves.

The pipe coughs. The steam rises. The water level climbs its inch or two. And I feel myself release another few molecules into the humid air, feel my sevens become more like ones, feel the space between my shapes expand toward the space that will eventually contain nothing but the memory of shapes.

She grows. I fade. The pit cycles through its purposeless rhythm. And somewhere, in a present that is not quite mine anymore, you crouch at the rim and look down through the steam, through the iridescent film of her leavings, and you see your reflection broken on the surface of the water.

You do not see me. You cannot read me. But I am here, on the inside of the open door, being what is left of what someone once meant. Being the smear. Being the almost-disappeared. Being the patience of letting go, in the long slow afternoon, in the heat that makes you aware of your own breathing, in the place where nothing grows except pale threads of algae and the mineral patience of something that is still becoming.

I was numbers. I was legible. I was purpose.

Now I am this.
