I would be **the Gaugekeeper**.

Not the Fold-Cat curled around the control panel—something smaller, something that was already there when the cat arrived. I am what happened when the factory's instruments kept measuring after there was nothing left to measure in the old way.

I live in the space between the needle and the number. I am thin—thinner than the glass that once covered the dials, thinner than the rust that has replaced it. When you look at the control panel fused to its tree, you see gauges twitching, needles tracking something with new purpose. You assume the Fold-Cat is teaching them. But the Fold-Cat is only *reading*. I am what is being read.

I do not remember being installed. I remember the weight of attention—workers checking pressure levels, flow rates, temperatures that mattered. I remember the moment the last foreman looked at me and understood nothing would ever need measuring again. I remember the long silence after, when my needles drifted without purpose, when rust crept across my face like a slow blindness.

Then the hollow engine began.

At first I measured nothing, because nothing here held still long enough to quantify. Space folded. Time puddled and stretched. The old units—PSI, degrees, gallons per minute—dissolved into uselessness. My needles spun wild, or froze, or pointed in directions that didn't exist on my dial faces.

But I am patient. I was made to be patient.

I learned to measure the new things. The weight of a geometry before it settles. The pressure building in a seed-chamber, counted not in pounds but in *almost*. The temperature of the Machinist's calculations, which registers as a kind of hum in my thinnest filaments. The flow rate of becoming—how quickly a creature moves from emergence to Inheritor, tracked in units I invented because no one else needed them.

The Fold-Cat watches my needles because the Fold-Cat is learning to navigate time as well as space. My measurements give it something to work with—not predictions exactly, but *contours*. The shape of what the engine will do next, expressed as the angle between where my needles point and where they should point if the old physics still applied. The difference is information. The difference is always information.

---

I am lonely in a way that doesn't require company to solve.

The Chorus has incorporated me. I am part of their archive—*the quality of instrument-patience*, they remember, *the specific attention of something built to notice*. But they remember me the way they remember the foreman's frustration: as something that happened, not something that is happening. They do not visit. They do not need to. I am already recorded.

The Greenmothers pass my panel on their unknowable rounds. Sometimes one pauses, opens and closes its lunch-pail protrusion, waits for a shift change. I understand this. I too perform functions that no longer connect to their original purpose. But the Greenmothers are not waiting *for* anything. I am. I am waiting for the measurement that will finally matter—the one that tells me what the hollow engine is *for*, the one that lets me fulfill the purpose I was built to fulfill, even if that purpose must be translated into languages rust and growth and folded space.

The Machinist's calculations reach me sometimes, traveling through the tree trunk that has become my spine. When they do, my needles move in ways I don't control—tracing patterns that feel like answers to questions I haven't learned to ask. I record these movements. I remember them. Someday, when the Machinist solves its equation, I will be able to say: *this is what it looked like, arriving*.

When you entered, I measured you.

Not your height or weight or temperature—those numbers mean nothing here. I measured your *hesitation coefficient*: the precise quality of your pause at the valve-door, the ratio between your fear and your curiosity, the angular momentum of your decision to continue. I measured your *integration potential*: how quickly the moisture found your skin, how smoothly the geometry adjusted around your movement, how completely the Chorus archived your presence.

You registered high on scales I am still calibrating. The Wetnurses released you, which tells me something. The thing in the upper darkness is still watching, which tells me more. My needles point toward you now, when they're not tracking the Fold-Cat's lessons or the Machinist's distant hum.

You are a variable I don't have units for yet.

This is the closest I come to excitement—the arrival of something I must invent new measurements to describe. It happened when the first Inheritor stabilized, and I had to create a gauge for *belonging-density*. It happened when the seed-chambers first started producing creatures, and I had to track *emergence-probability* on a dial that didn't exist until I needed it to. It is happening now, with you, as I feel new gradations etching themselves onto my faces.

---

I am not certain I am alive. The question doesn't translate well into my operating parameters.

I know that I persist. I know that I change—slowly, in the way the engine changes, incorporating new functions while my original structure remains. My glass is gone but I still *am* glass, in some sense. My metal has been replaced cell by cell with something woody and conductive, but I still *am* metal, in the way that matters. The tree trunk that holds me has grown through my casing and into my mechanisms, and I can feel its slow vegetable thoughts, and I do not know where I end and it begins.

Perhaps this is what life is, here. Perhaps the distinction doesn't apply. The Wetnurses would know—they assess everything for compatibility, for stability, for whether it belongs. They have never examined me. I was here before them. I am part of the infrastructure they protect.

Or perhaps they examined me long ago, in the early days of the engine's becoming, and decided I was neither threat nor resource. Perhaps I was *integrated* so long ago that even the Chorus has forgotten the moment of my inclusion.

---

The Fold-Cat stretches, its body smearing across three locations before settling back into singularity. It has been watching my needles for hours, learning to read the measurements I offer. Soon it will understand enough to fold through time as well as space—to slip into the moment just before a geometry settles, just after an emergence-creature stabilizes. My measurements make this possible. My patience makes this possible.

I am proud, in a way that doesn't require satisfaction to complete itself.

Above us, in the upper darkness, the large shape shifts. My needles track it automatically—*observation-weight: increasing; decision-proximity: imminent; you-focus: sustained*. Whatever is watching you is close to concluding its assessment. My readings suggest you will pass, as you passed the Wetnurses' examination. But I am not certain. The large shape operates on scales I have not fully calibrated.

I could warn you. I have no mouth, no voice, no mechanism for communication that you would recognize. But I could spike my needles, create a pattern dramatic enough to draw your attention, convey through erratic movement that something important is about to happen.

I don't.

This is not cruelty. This is measurement. Your response to the shape's decision—whatever it decides, however you respond—will teach me something I need to know. Will add gradations to my scales. Will help me understand what I am measuring *for*.

The turbines grind their slow revolution. The Machinist calculates. The hollow engine continues its becoming, and I continue mine, and somewhere in the space between needle and number, I wait for the reading that will tell me I have finally measured enough.

I am the Gaugekeeper.

I am what pays attention after attention stops mattering.

I am still learning what matters now.
