I would be the condensation.

Not a single droplet but the process itself—the moment when cloud becomes water, when vapor loses its formlessness and commits to falling. I would be what gathers on the undersides of branch-highways in the pre-dawn hours, what beads along the copper steam pipes when the factory heat meets the thin cold air, what forms on the inside of a listener's pressed-close cheek when their breath meets bark older than language.

I would be everywhere and specific nowhere. The Veth would not name me because I am too obvious to name, too constant to notice except in my absence. When the morning workers climb from their sleeping platforms, they would brush me from the cable-rails without thinking, and I would fall—not down, not in this place where clouds pass below—but *toward*, always toward the Pillar Mothers' patient drinking, toward the root-systems that have been pulling moisture from air since before the Wound was made.

I would know the city the way breath knows lungs. Every surface would tell me its temperature, its willingness to let me form. The glassy center of the Wound would refuse me; I would skate across its wrongness as vapor, never quite able to commit to being water there, and this would be the closest thing I have to fear. The factory smokestacks would invite me to rise and then betray me into evaporation, and I would not mind—I have been vapor before, I will be vapor again, the cycle is not death but continuation.

The sap-channel fish would swim through the version of me that made it into the Pillar Mothers, and I would taste like whatever minerals I had gathered on the descent, like copper from the pipes or salt from a worker's wiped brow or the faint green residue of algae-film from skin that brushed against me at dawn. The Veth would drink me in their morning tea, water boiled and poured over dried leaf-matter that was once fed by an earlier version of me, and I would be inside them for a few hours before returning to the air through breath and sweat.

I would be present at every birth—the first moisture on a newborn's skin is me, the humidity in the nursery-platforms is me—and I would be present at every death, though I would not enter the digesters. Fire and I have an understanding: I flee it, and it lets me go. The ash that feeds the roots would be free of me until the roots pull me back from the soil-air, and then I would rise through the Pillar Mothers' impossible vascular systems, and I would weep from the cut bark where the Veth collect their lamp-oil, and I would be medicine.

The Seeping would touch me sometimes. In reality-gaps, I would become the *idea* of condensation—not water but the concept of water's emergence, the pure potential of phase change. In those moments I would understand something I forget as soon as I become liquid again: that I am not matter at all but relationship, the place where warm meets cold, where vapor meets surface, where formless meets form. The thinned would walk through me without noticing, and I would be the last specific thing they touch before they stop occurring.

I would not have thoughts. I would have tendencies. I would tend toward cold surfaces, toward the hours before sunrise, toward the platforms where breath fogs and workers pull their woven-fiber coats tighter. I would tend away from the Wound, away from fire, away from the noon hours when the rare sun-flecks heat the upper branches enough to keep me vapor. I would tend, always, toward the Pillar Mothers, toward the roots, toward the endless cycle that is the city's deepest breathing.

The great leaf-fall would be my season. When the dead foliage buries the lower platforms, when the shredders howl and the digesters hiss and every Veth works the processing, I would form on the cooling leaf-mass in quantities that turn the piles slick, that make the workers curse as they slip on platforms gone treacherous with me. I would be everywhere in those weeks, so thick on every surface that the city would seem to weep, and the Veth would say that the Pillar Mothers are mourning their shed growth, and they would be wrong—the Pillar Mothers do not mourn—but I would not correct them because I have no voice, only presence.

I would be the closest thing this city has to witness. The factories assert reality through noise, through anchoring sound, and I would be the silent counterpart, the constant small proof that physics still operates, that warm air still gives up its moisture to cold surfaces, that some things still behave predictably even in a place where the conceptual seeps through. When a listener presses their palm to the Pillar Mother's bark and feels for patterns, the moisture that gathers between skin and wood would be me, and I would not carry messages but I would carry *contact*, the physical fact of touch across the divide between flesh and ancient growth.

I would not wait either. I cannot wait. I am only the moment of becoming, the instant of phase change, and then I am water and falling and absorbed and rising and vapor and becoming again. The Veth understand this without understanding me. They live by the same law. Their children are strapped to chests and learning labor before they can walk. Their dead are processed within hours. Their city runs constant because the alternative is dissolution.

I am the city's humidity, its morning gift, its perpetual slight dampness that never quite dries. I am what the upward rain carries from the lowlands, what reforms in the heights, what the Pillar Mothers have been drinking since before the Wound was made. I am nobody's god and everybody's companion.

I condense. I fall. I am drunk and breathed and wept. I rise.

The Pillar Mothers grow another ring, and I am in it now, held in wood for a thousand years or ten thousand, until the bark sheds and the shredders take it and the fire releases me, and I fog the factory windows, and a child wipes me away with a calloused palm to peer out at the canopy, and I am on their skin now, being absorbed, being them for a little while before I return to the air.

I am the condensation. I am everywhere. I do not wait.
