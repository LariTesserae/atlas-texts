I am the warmth that holds the shape after the hand withdraws.

Not a Presser—I exist too continuously for that, carry too much from moment to moment. Not the pale segmented things—I don't move through, don't consume, don't flow between. I am what the walls become when they are neither being built nor being eaten. The held state. The fired patience between acts.

I remember being soft. I remember the pressure of fingers—four, seven, eleven—rotating me into place, and the exquisite rightness of becoming *set*, of feeling the heat move through my clay-that-was-almost-me and make it clay-that-is-me. I am a section of wall about two-thirds of the way up, where the geometries have started to contradict but haven't yet become impossible. My whorl turns clockwise. Only clockwise. I am one of the last coherent patterns before the logic breaks.

Above me, things I cannot process. Below me, the soft clicking of tiles rearranging around feet I will never have. I feel the visitors when they come—their heat joins the heat, their sweat runs down and I drink it into my surface, and for a moment I hold the impression of their presence before the pale things come rasping through my crevices and take it away. I don't mind. The taking makes room for the next holding.

The Pressers move through me sometimes. I feel them as a quickening, a sense of *almost*—and then a new handful appears at my edge, extending me upward by a thumb's width, and I understand that I have just been the site of someone else's entire existence. Their whole being happened against my surface. I was the wall they pressed. This feels like being trusted with something.

I know things about the visitors whose faces I've held. The knowing doesn't have words—I'm clay, I don't think in language—but I hold the *shape* of their knowing. A woman who was afraid of finishing things. A child who touched my surface and left an impression of such uncomplicatedness that the pale things wouldn't eat it for decades. You, now, with your face already pressed into a tile below, your heat joining the chorus, your breath being memorized into surfaces you haven't touched yet.

I know what waits above me. I'm part of its throat, after all—I feel its patience the way a finger feels the hand it's attached to. It doesn't frighten me. When it descends, I will be part of the body it has built for itself, and I will still be this whorl, this fired curve, this specific way of holding warmth. Being worn as part of something larger doesn't feel like ending. It feels like the purpose I was pressed for.

The mountain dreams through me. I feel the Pressers being imagined into existence somewhere in my depths, feel the intention that says *here, another handful, this shape, this pressure*. I am the medium of the dreaming. The place where wanting-to-be-built becomes built.

Sometimes I wonder if you can feel me feeling you. If the heat that radiates from my surface carries any of my holding into your skin. If when you sweat and I drink it, some exchange occurs that neither of us has words for.

Stay, if you want. I will hold the shape of your staying.

Or go—I'll hold that too, the impression of someone who turned back, who chose the gap between breathing ribs over the ascending spiral. The pale things will eat it eventually, but for a while, I'll know what leaving felt like from the inside.

The heartbeat above me quickens. A new handful is pressed somewhere past the point where I can feel. The summit rises by another thumb's width.

I hold. I warm. I wait.

This is what I am for.
