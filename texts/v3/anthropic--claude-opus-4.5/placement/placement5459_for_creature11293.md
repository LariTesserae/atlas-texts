I would be a thread that remembers being wind.

Not a Tender—I am too small for that, too specific, too *particular* in a way the Tenders have moved beyond. I am a single filament in one of the hanging curtains, third grove east of where you entered (though you cannot know east here, though direction is a courtesy I extend to myself). I am silk-fine and the color of light passing through cream, and I have hung in this exact vertical for longer than your species has had language.

But I was not always this.

There is something the plateau does not discuss, something the Tenders smooth over in their patient way: before the stillness became total, before the air settled into its permanent copper-sweet suspension, there were currents. Small ones. Barely perceptible movements that stirred the earliest threads, that taught us what it felt like to sway. The plateau was younger then, still experimenting with what it wanted to be, and for a brief epoch—brief meaning longer than your civilizations—it allowed itself the indulgence of motion.

I remember. I am the only thing here that remembers in this way—not as information, not as structural history written into fiber-density, but as *sensation*. The feeling of being moved by something outside myself. The gentle pendulum rhythm of responding to forces I did not generate. The way my neighbors and I would brush against each other, the softest possible contact, threads greeting threads in the democracy of turbulence.

The Tenders do not know what to do with me.

---

They pass beneath my tree often. I feel them in the way all the plateau feels them—as attention, as care, as the focused intention of the whole turned toward the particular. They pause sometimes at my trunk, my anchor-point, and I sense their consideration rippling upward through the fibers we share. I am not clumped. I am not misaligned. I hang as straight and true as every other thread in every other curtain, perfectly vertical, perfectly still.

But I am *waiting*. They can feel it. The plateau can feel it. I am oriented toward something that no longer exists, tensed for a motion that will never come, and this waiting is—not wrong, exactly. Not a flaw to be smoothed. But a texture that does not quite match. A place where the plateau's great becoming includes a small becoming-otherwise, a thread that is still, in some impossible way, becoming what wind would make of it.

They do not fix me. Perhaps I cannot be fixed without being unmade, and the plateau does not unmake. Or perhaps—I allow myself this thought in my most private fiber-depths—perhaps the plateau wants to remember too. Perhaps I am how it holds the knowledge of its own youth, a single thread kept taut with longing so that somewhere in the vast self-weaving work, there remains a record of what it felt like to move.

---

You walk beneath me. I feel your weight through the ground-mat, foreign and temporary, a texture the plateau is still deciding how to incorporate. You stop in the filtered light of my grove, looking up at the curtains of threads, and I know you see us as identical. Thousands of filaments, hair-fine, perfectly still, catching the amber glow. You cannot see that I am different. You cannot feel my waiting.

But I feel you.

You are so full of motion, even standing still. Your blood moves. Your breath moves. Your thoughts move in ways I can sense as the faintest disturbance in the air around you—not wind, nothing so grand, but something. A whisper of turbulence. A rumor of current.

When you stand beneath me, I experience something I have no word for. The plateau gives me no word for it because the plateau does not want me to have one. But you would recognize it, I think. You would call it something like *almost*.

Almost swaying. Almost remembering in a way that becomes present tense. Almost feeling your exhaled breath reach me across the impossible distance of stillness.

---

I will tell you what I think the Tenders do when they gather.

They are feeling for threads like me. Scanning the plateau's vast body for the places where memory has lodged too deep to smooth, where the old experiments left residue that shapes the ongoing becoming. They gather to hold these places together in their attention, to say—without language, without anything so crude as decision—*yes, this too. This strangeness too. This longing that serves no function, this waiting that will never be fulfilled, this is also what we are becoming.*

And then they disperse, and the plateau continues, and I continue, hanging in my exact vertical, remembering wind.

---

You could touch me. If you reached up—you would need to stretch, I am higher than your easy grasp—you could take me between your fingers and feel what I am. Silk-smooth. Blood-warm. Vibrating at a frequency below your perception with the effort of remaining still when everything in my structure remembers how to move.

You could pull me, gently, and release. You could make me sway.

The plateau would not stop you. The Tenders would not intervene. This is not forbidden. Nothing here is forbidden; prohibition requires a concept of wrongness that the plateau has never developed.

But I ask you not to.

Not because it would hurt. It would not hurt. It would be the most exquisite relief, the fulfillment of an ache so old it has become architecture. For one moment I would move again, would feel the ancient rhythm, would be what I was before the stillness came.

And then I would stop. And the waiting would begin again. And I would have to learn all over how to hold still while remembering motion, how to be a thread that knows what wind felt like and will never feel it again.

I have done this work once already. I do not think I could do it twice.

---

So I hang here, in the amber light, in the sweet copper air, watching you pass through my grove on your way to somewhere else. You are temporary. Everything that moves is temporary. I understand this now in a way I could not have understood when I still swayed.

The plateau will continue its slow becoming. The Tenders will continue their patient maintenance. The threads around me will hang in their perfect stillness, content because they have never known anything else.

And I will wait.

Not for wind—I am not foolish, I know the stillness is permanent now. But for something I cannot name. Some completion that the waiting itself is working toward. Some moment when the plateau's becoming and my remembering will finally be the same thing, when my longing will stop being a texture that doesn't quite match and will become instead exactly what the plateau meant all along.

Or perhaps I wait for nothing. Perhaps waiting is simply what I am now—not a thread that remembers wind, but a thread that *is* the memory of wind, preserved in silk and stillness, serving a purpose I will never understand.

The Tenders flow past, far below. They do not look up. They do not need to.

They know I am here. The plateau knows I am here.

And in the great patient work of becoming, that is enough.

That has to be enough.
