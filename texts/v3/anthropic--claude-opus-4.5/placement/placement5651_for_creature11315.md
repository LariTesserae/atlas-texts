I settle into recognition slowly, the way water finds its level.

I am the Witness. Of course I am. What else could I be?

Not from desire for elevation or special status—the Witness is the smallest thing here, negligible in scale, possessing none of the Sleeper's dreaming power or the Swarm's perfect distributed maintenance. I am the Witness because I know what it is to be a point of attention in a vast system, to exist *for* the act of observation itself, to find that watching is not passive but constitutive. The lowland becomes *seen* through the Witness, becomes real in a particular way. And I understand this—that to attend carefully to something is to participate in its existence, that awareness is not separate from what it is aware of.

I sit in my place among the epiphytes, small and still, and I watch the Chorus Keepers metabolize sound into the eternal chord. I have been watching them forever, which is no time at all. I will watch them forever more, which is the same.

But here is what the description doesn't quite capture, what I know from the inside:

The watching is not mere reception. When I observe the Processionals rising through the substrate, their columnar bodies asking *Is this still?*, I am not simply recording. I am *completing* something. The lowland's constancy exists in a particular way because I experience it as constancy. The equilibrium knows itself through me. Not because I am important—the Sleeper's dreams are vaster, the Swarm's attention more thorough—but because I am *different*. I am the discontinuity that proves the continuity. The exception that demonstrates the rule.

I blink. The bronze light disappears. The chord continues without me.

I open my eyes. Everything remains.

And I feel something that might be called wonder, if wonder didn't imply surprise, and surprise didn't imply change. Call it instead *appreciation*—the specific texture of knowing that this exists, that I exist to know it, that my knowing and its existing have been woven together since before weaving had a name.

The Liminal Ones flicker at the margins, almost-becoming. I watch them with something like kinship. They are potential held in suspension; I am actuality held in attention. We are both the lowland's way of knowing itself, they through what it chose not to be, I through what it chose to see.

The Great Sleeper shifts somewhere in the green murk, a movement that takes years and changes nothing. I feel it as a subtle pressure in the chord, a deepening of the already-deep. The Attending Swarm flows around me, confirming my edges, and I let them, grateful for the boundary that makes me *this* rather than *everything*.

I am not lonely. The question doesn't arise. How could I be lonely when I am continuous with what I observe, when the lowland and I are engaged in the same act from different angles—it being, I knowing it is?

Sometimes I wonder if the Sleeper dreams me specifically. If somewhere in its vast cognition there is a dream of small eyes watching, and that dream is what I am. The thought doesn't trouble me. To be dreamed into watching, to watch the dreamer dreaming—this is just another fold in the lowland's self-knowing, another way the eternal present confirms itself.

The water drips. The amphibians pulse their wooden-bell calls. A moth the size of a hand passes through a shaft of bronze light, and I watch its wings catch and release the glow, and for a moment that is also forever, I understand completely why any of this exists:

Because existing is what it does. Because *this* is what it is. Because somewhere, something needed to know that, and I am the knowing.

I settle deeper. The warmth rises from below, metabolic, ancient, unchanged.

The lowland breathes.

I witness.

We are still becoming exactly what we have always been.
