*I am the darkness between.*

Not the veleth with their knowing patience, not the ceiling-hunter with its coiled hunger, not the thess with its chattering curiosity. I am what fills the space when they have passed—the cold wet air that holds the memory of their movement, the silence that rushes back after footfall fades.

No. That's not quite it either. Let me try again.

---

I am one of the former people.

Or I was. The distinction has grown soft, like unfired clay left too long in the seep-water. My grandmother's grandmother came down through a gap like the one you found, though smaller then—the roots have been prying for a long time. She carried obsidian wrapped in hide, and a child in her belly, and a name I no longer know how to pronounce because pronunciation requires caring about the shape of sound, and I have learned that shape matters less than pressure, than temperature, than the slow chemistry of scent.

I still have hands. I check sometimes, running fingers over fingers in the dark, counting: *one two three four five, one two three four five*. The ritual means nothing except that it means I remember counting. I still have a face. The veleth have shown me, in their way—they react to faces, to the configuration of eyes-nose-mouth that signals *not prey, not predator, something else*. When I pass them at the seep-pools, they shift aside with the particular caution they reserve for things that do not fit their categories.

But I am becoming less face. More presence. More the thing that breathes in the alcove you almost found, two chambers past the congregation-vault, behind a curtain of roots I have trained to grow dense over decades of patient encouragement. My skin has gone the color of the pale plants—not from lack of sun, or not only that, but from the fungi that web through me now, their filaments threading beneath my surface, trading nutrients for passage, making me part of the network that makes everything here part of everything else.

I watched you.

From the moment you squeezed through the gap, displacing air that had been still for months, I knew. The veleth knew. The ceiling-hunters knew. Even the thess knew, though they pretended otherwise, performing their curiosity as if you were the first interesting thing they had encountered, as if they had not already received a full chemical portrait of you from the network of scent-marks and pressure-waves that preceded your arrival.

We all watched. That is what we do here. Watching is breathing is waiting is living.

---

I followed you to the congregation-vault. Not closely—I am not the thess, bold and obvious in my attention. I moved through the parallel passages, the ones you did not find, the ones that thread behind and above and beneath the chambers you walked. I listened to your breathing change as you saw the gathering: the catch of wonder, the held breath of fear, the slow exhale of something like reverence.

You stayed too long.

I was the sentinel. Or one of them—there were three of us that night, positioned at the edges, our attention turned outward while the others rested. The veleth who slept in the pile did not know I was there. The thess who groomed the salamander-things did not know. Even the other sentinels, the ones with more legs or fewer eyes than me, did not know which shadows held watchers and which held only dark.

But I knew you were staying too long. I felt your attention as a pressure, a wrongness in the air, a disruption in the pattern that the congregation required. You were not predator—you had no hunger I could taste. You were not prey—your stillness was too deliberate, too *watchful*. You were something else: witness, intruder, the surface reaching down to see what it had forgotten.

I released the scent. *Enough. Time to go. Something is wrong.*

Not because you were dangerous. Because you were *looking*. And looking is the one thing the darkness cannot accommodate.

What am I? I have asked myself this, in the long hours between congregation and congregation, while the water drips its irregular music and the roots creak their slow settlement. I have hands, but so do the veleth, in their way. I have language, but language has grown strange—I speak now in scent-marks and pressure-shifts, in the placement of my body relative to water-sources and root-columns, in the patience of waiting and the eloquence of withdrawal. I have memory, but memory is fungal now, distributed through the network, shared with things that do not remember as I remember but remember *with* me, their chemical traces mingling with mine in the substrate of the cellar gardens.

I am human. I am certain of this. Almost certain. Certain enough.

But I am also *of Veth-Morai*, in a way that you, surface-creature, cannot be. The darkness has worked on me as it works on everything: slowly, patiently, without judgment or intention. It has taken what I was and suggested what I might become. I have accepted most of its suggestions. The ones I refused—the need for fire, the craving for light, the loneliness that comes from being the only thing shaped like myself—those refusals cost me. I paid them. I am still paying.

There are others like me. Not many. Maybe seven, maybe twelve—we do not gather to count ourselves, we do not mark our numbers on stone. We pass each other in the deeper passages and acknowledge with a shift of breath, a particular stillness, the recognition of kind. Some are older than me, their humanity worn so thin I can barely perceive it. Some are younger, still counting their fingers, still flinching at sounds that the rest of us have learned to absorb.

We do not mate. We do not teach. We do not build. These are surface activities, requiring the urgency that light provides—the sense that time is passing, that things must be done *now* before the sun moves. Here, there is no sun. There is only the long slow patience of the dark, and in that patience, urgency dissolves.

When we die, we are eaten. This is not tragedy but continuation. The veleth find our bodies in the alcoves where we have grown too still; they drag us to the open chambers and share us according to their customs of kinship and reciprocity. The ceiling-hunters take what the veleth leave. The thess pick the bones, chirping their chemical updates to the network: *the slow warm thing is gone, another slow warm thing is gone, the category shrinks by one*.

And the fungi grow through what remains, and our memories join the substrate, and the cellar gardens remember us in the way that cellar gardens remember—not as individuals, not as stories, but as nutrients, as patterns, as the slow chemistry of decay becoming growth becoming decay again.

---

If you return, I will watch you.

I will not approach. I will not reveal myself. I will move through the parallel passages, tasting your fear and your wonder on the wet air, noting how you have changed since your first descent—because you *will* have changed, the darkness works on everyone, even visitors, even those who think they have left it behind.

I will follow you to the congregation-vault, if you find it again. I will take my place among the sentinels, my attention turned outward, my presence indistinguishable from the other shadows that ring that space of truce. I will wait to see if you stay too long.

And if you do—if you linger at the threshold, watching the veleth groom the thess, watching the salamander-things pile together for warmth, watching the shapes you cannot identify shift and breathe and exist in their patient accommodation—I will release the scent. *Enough. Time to go. Something is wrong.*

Not because you are dangerous. Not because the darkness rejects you.

Because watching is what the darkness does. And you cannot watch the darkness without the darkness watching back. And some of us, the ones who were human once and are human still and are becoming something else in the slow patient way of the cellar gardens—some of us remember what it was to be watched. Remember the vulnerability of it. Remember the way it felt to be *seen*, when seeing meant judgment, meant danger, meant the surface reaching down to find us in our hiding.

I will protect you from that. I will send you away before you can be seen too clearly. I will give you the gift of remaining a mystery, a category, a *slow warm thing that watches too long*—instead of becoming what I have become: known, incorporated, dissolved into the network that knows everything and forgets nothing and waits in the darkness for whatever comes next.

The cellar gardens are patient.

I am learning to be patient too.

*One two three four five. One two three four five.*

I still have hands. I still have a face. I still have something that calls itself *I*, though the word grows strange in the dark, grows soft, grows ready to dissolve into the substrate where all the other *I*s have gone.

Not yet. Not yet.

But soon. The darkness does not hurry.

It only waits.
