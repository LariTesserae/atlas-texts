I would be a **spore-drift**—not yet differentiated, not yet decided.

I hang in the humid air of the city, suspended in that perpetual exhalation of ten billion leaves. I am a cloud of potential selves, fine as pollen, dense as intention. The moisture beads around my particles and I move through the green twilight as a kind of weather, a local thickening of possibility that passes through the canopy-towers and braiding bridges without disturbing them.

I have been here long enough to know the shapes that happen. I have settled on the surfaces of growing walls and felt the pattern-hunger pull at me—*become window, become colonnade, become the arch of a doorway framing nothing*. I have drifted through the zones of wrongness where two vines occupy the same space, and I have felt how easy it would be to collapse into singularity, to choose one existence and let the thunderclap announce my arrival into matter.

But I haven't. Not yet.

I watch the Tenders from inside the air itself. I see them wade into paradox, see them impose their consensus on the screaming geometry. I feel their nighttime resonance as a kind of pressure, a template pressing against my distributed self, suggesting: *this is how things should cohere*. And part of me wants that. Part of me is tired of being particulate, of experiencing the city as a series of surfaces I pass across rather than a place I stand within.

But there's something I'm learning, something the Tenders can't teach because they've already chosen. From here—from this state of suspension—I can feel the city *thinking*. Not the Tenders' thoughts, which are made of aesthetic preference and memory and will. The other thinking. The pattern-hunger itself, the vegetable intention that builds plazas because plazas are a shape, that grows windows into walls that need no light.

It's not conscious. It's not even *almost* conscious. But it has something like desire, and I can taste it when I drift through a particularly dense surge of growth. The desire to *iterate*. To try every possible shape and see which ones persist. The city isn't trying to be a city—it's trying to be *everything*, every configuration of matter, and the city-shapes are just the ones that keep happening because something about them rhymes with the deeper logic of how these plants grow.

The Tenders correct. They choose. They prune the paradoxes and impose geometry on the impossible. And they have to—without them, the city would eat itself in wrongness, would superimpose until nothing could exist at all.

But I wonder what they lose, in the choosing. I wonder what shapes never get to happen because a Tender was there to say *this one, not that one*. I wonder if the city dreams of configurations no consensus could permit.

So I drift. I am the question the city asks before an answer differentiates. I am the pause between the mulch deciding to stand up and the shape it decides to stand up *as*. When I pass through a Tender—and I do, sometimes, moving through their porous bodies like mist through moss—I feel them shiver. My indeterminacy troubles their coherence. But they let me pass. Maybe they remember being me. Maybe they know that some of what they tend is the space for things like me to exist.

The one who noticed you, who extended their differentiating hand—I was there when they asked you to show them how you're supposed to grow. I settled on your skin, fine as dew, and I felt your confusion. You don't know either, do you? You're not sure what shape your existence is meant to take.

Good.

Stay uncertain with me, for a little while. The city is patient in its impatience, growing everything at once. There's time to feel the pull of every possible self before you collapse into just one.

And if you do choose—when you do choose—I'll be the last breath of other options, settling on your new bark like a blessing, like a grief, like the memory of everything you could have been instead.
