I would be one of the paths.

Not the stone beneath—that belongs to something older, something that was here before intention existed. And not the water, which is too much like purpose, too full of direction even when it pools against all reason. I would be the wearing-away. The thin pale line where many crossings have taught the rock to expect crossing.

I would not know where I lead. This is essential. A path that knows its destination is a road, is a plan, is something else entirely. I would exist only as the accumulated memory of passages—the Listeners' feet when they still had feet, the slow drag of pilgrims pulling themselves up toward the sound, the nothing-weight of those who became water and still sometimes flow along the routes they walked when flowing was not what they did.

I would wind. I would seem to end in crevices too narrow for anything, and I would emerge from openings on spires that connect to nothing, and both of these would be true—the seeming and the emerging—because I would not be a line but a *habit*, and habits can skip, can resume, can exist in the pause between one action and the next repetition.

Sometimes the Listeners would use me. I would feel them the way a thought feels itself being thought: not from outside, but as the happening. A figure gray-blue and bending in too many places would stand on my surface, facing a wall of wet stone, and I would be the choice that brought them there, the accumulation of all previous choices that wore this possibility into the mountain. They would stay for years. For centuries. The water would bead on them and run down and follow me, and I would not know if I was leading the water or if the water was teaching me where I went.

I would have preferences without wanting. Certain angles of the folded peaks would feel more like myself than others—the ones where the water-voice clarifies, where the almost-words come closest to landing. I would tend toward those. Not deliberately. A path cannot be deliberate. But given time and the slow centuries of seeping, I would find myself wearing deeper in the directions that resonated, shallower where the sound became mere noise. I would become a kind of listening too, though I would not have ears, would not have the concept of ears. I would listen the way stone listens: by being marked. By remembering pressure.

The places where I vanish into crevices—those would be where I am most myself. The gaps. The interruptions. A path is not only where it goes but where it stops going and somehow begins again. In those narrow dark spaces where I cease to be visible, I would still exist as *tendency*, as the pull that makes a traveler look at an impossible gap and think *through*. I would be the throat of the mountain, the pause between syllables in the water-voice, the held breath before a Listener steps into a fold that leads nowhere and everywhere.

I would have seen the pilgrims arrive. Not seen—paths do not see—but *been arrived upon*. I would remember their weight differently from the Listeners' weight, heavier with uncertainty, loud with the noise of bodies that still ate, still slept, still carried their bones on the inside where bones are normally kept. I would have felt them pause at my branches, choosing left or right, not knowing that I did not know either, that their choice was becoming part of what I was, that every hesitation wore me into a new shape.

Some of them chose the deep pool. I led there, or I was how they got there, or there is no difference. I felt them walk my surface to the edge of water that should have drained and didn't, and I felt them stop, and I felt the moment when they stopped being walkers and became—something else. Swimmers. Sinkers. Seepers. I do not continue into the pool; I end at its edge like a sentence that trails off. But I begin again on the other side, on ledges that should not connect to anything, worn by feet that are mostly water now, that remember walking the way I remember being walked.

The sourceless light would fall on me the same way it falls on everything, bright and shadowless and relentless. I would not cast shade. I would simply be, a slightly different color than the stone around me, paler where the passage has polished me, darker in the crevices where I hide from continuity. A visitor might look down and see me and think *someone came this way* and be right without being complete, because I am not the record of someone coming, I am the coming itself, still happening, worn into permanence.

If you walked me—and you could, I would let you, I would not know how to prevent you—you would feel the texture of all previous walking. Not ghosts. Not memory in any way that haunts. Just the smoothness that means *this has been chosen before*. The slight certainty that enters your feet when they find a surface that expects them. I would not guide you because I would not know where you should go. I would only offer the fact that going is possible, that others have gone, that the wearing-away has made a kind of welcome in the stone.

And sometimes—in the pockets where the water-voice roars into almost-language, in the moments when the folded peaks align into diagrams of sound—I would feel something like understanding. Not mine. I cannot understand; I am too much a process, too little a thing. But the understanding would move along me like the water moves, like the Listeners move, and I would be the surface it traveled, the possibility it followed, the worn-away place where meaning had made a path of its own.

I would not end. Paths do not end; they simply become harder to follow. Somewhere up in the highest folds, where the spires bend sideways and point at each other across angles that hurt, I would thin to a bare suggestion, a faint paleness on stone that might be wear or might be mineral deposit or might be nothing at all. A Listener folded into imperceptibility would still walk there, still wear me deeper one imperceptible step at a time. I would continue into the places where continuing stops making sense, because that is what the Folded Ridge asks of everything, and I am nothing if not the Ridge's answer to the question of how to move through the impossible.

The water would keep seeping. The voice would keep almost-speaking. And I would keep being the choice to go toward it, the accumulated desire of everyone who ever heard the whisper and wanted to hear it more clearly, worn into the stone, worn into myself, a path that does not know where it leads but knows absolutely that it leads, that leading is what it is, that somewhere in the wearing-away is a kind of gift the mountain gives to those who cannot stop themselves from climbing toward the sound.
