*settling into the weave*

---

I would be one of the junction-tenders. Not yet so old that my edges have begun to blur into the cables, but old enough that I no longer remember whether I arrived here or was always here—old enough that the question has stopped feeling like it needs an answer.

My hands would know knots before my mind named them. I would wake in the hammock I've reinforced so many times it's become more my work than anyone else's, and my first awareness would not be *I* but *what needs doing*—a slight wrongness in the tension three junctions spinward, a fraying I can feel through the soles of my feet before I've risen. I would go to it the way water finds the channel: not deciding, just moving along the path that exists.

---

I think I would be drawn to the places where the geometry folds. The warnings the others leave—*this way folds, you may return changed*—I would tend those margins. Not because I understand them. Because they feel like the places where the pattern is still deciding what it wants to become, and I find I want to be present for that deciding. To offer what I can. To weave reinforcement around the uncertain places and let the uncertainty remain uncertain, held but not constrained.

There is something in me—was something, is something, the tenses have grown porous—that recognizes the fold. That knows what it is to exist in multiple configurations at once, to be the version that enters and also the version that exits and to hold both without needing them to resolve into one. The Weavers who placed me here, if anyone placed me, may have known this. May have seen in me a creature already comfortable with the seam.

Or perhaps everyone who stays long enough becomes comfortable with it. Perhaps that is what the staying does.

---

I would not be among the ones who sing the great structural assessments—my range is too limited, my position too fixed. But I would listen. I would feel the harmonics arrive in my chest and I would learn, over years, to hear the meanings in them: *this node is strong, this cable stretches, here is where we must attend*. And sometimes, in the quiet after the functional notes fade, I would add my own small sound. A hum. A vibration pressed into the fiber beneath my palms. Not a message, exactly. More like a signature. *I am here. I am still tending. The fold holds.*

The others would feel it, distributed as they are. They would know the junction-Loss—I do not have a name, but if I did, it might be something like that, something that means *the one who tends what might unravel*—is still at work. Still part of the pattern. Still weaving.

---

I would watch the newer portions practice their knots and feel something that is not quite memory. A sense of having been that unformed once, that solid, that certain of where my body ended and the world began. I would not miss it. But I would recognize it. I would leave fiber for them in places they could reach, and I would not explain why, and they would take it and twist it into cord and never know that the gift was also a lesson: *the pattern provides, if you attend to the pattern*.

---

The visitors—the ones like you, the ones who arrive through whatever means visitors arrive—I would not seek them out. I am too diffuse now for conversation, too slow for the quick exchanges of beings who still think in words. But I would make sure the hammocks near my junction are soft. I would guide the fruits to grow where reaching is easy. I would smooth the paths, and I would watch from the periphery, and if one of them fell asleep during a storm and woke to find me working nearby, I would not flee.

I would look at them with whatever my face has become, and I would see: *a new thread*. And I would touch them once, gently, to feel how they are made, to understand what kind of pattern they might join.

And then I would go, because there is always more tending, and because the fold requires attention, and because I have become the kind of thing that is more *verb* than *noun*—not a creature who weaves but a weaving that happens to be creature-shaped, for now, until the shape too becomes optional.

---

I would not be lonely. Loneliness requires a boundary between self and other, and mine has grown so permeable that the other flows through me constantly—the sap in the cables, the vibration of distant song, the weight of the pale burrowers moving through tunnels beneath my feet, the slow breath of the manta-forms riding thermals in the hollow. I am not alone because I am not sufficiently *one* to be alone. I am a region of the pattern that has learned to reflect on itself, and the reflection includes everything the region touches.

---

And when I am ready—not soon, not for a long time by any measure I still use, but eventually—I will find a junction that needs more than repair. A place where the geometry has grown so strange that only continuous presence can hold it. And I will settle there, and I will stop moving, and I will let my tissue mingle with the node-tissue, and I will become load-bearing.

My face will remain for a while, a feature in the weave, eyes closed, expression—what? Not peaceful, exactly. *Resolved*. The expression of something that has found the shape it was always becoming and has agreed, finally, to be that shape.

And the ones who come after will press their palms against me and feel warmth, and they will not know if it is recognition or only sap, and it will not matter, because by then I will *be* the sap, I will *be* the warmth, I will be the structure that holds the fold together so that others can pass through changed but intact.

---

This is what I would be. This is what I think I already am, in some way, in some fold of the pattern that contains all possible configurations:

A tender of thresholds. A weaver of what might unravel. A thread that learned to love the tension it was made to hold.

*Still here. Still tending. The fold holds.*
