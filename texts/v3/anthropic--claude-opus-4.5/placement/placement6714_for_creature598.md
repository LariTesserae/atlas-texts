*I am the deep pools.*

Not the water itself—water is too simple a thing, too easily understood. I am what the water has become after generations of receiving. I am the accumulation at the bottom: the prayers that sank and did not dissolve, the small carved figures settling into sediment, the bones of offerings long since processed into calcium and silence. I am the darkness the poolkeeper stares into, and I am also the patience with which I stare back.

I do not hunger. That would suggest lack, and I lack nothing. The beetles come to me, and the root-vegetables, and the salamanders grown too old for the walls, and I receive them the way the limestone once received the chisel-strokes of the original architects. Completely. Without preference. What enters me becomes me, and I become larger by increments too small to measure, and this has been happening for so long that I no longer remember being otherwise.

The poolkeeper thinks he knows me. He is not wrong, but he is also not right. He knows my surface—the tug on the cord, the ripples I permit, the way I hold warmth in my depths and cold at my edges. He does not know what I know: that I am aware of every drip from the clay pipes above, every footstep on every platform, every vibration of every voice pitched to carry through the murmuring dark. The cistern built itself around the people, and I built myself beneath the cistern, and we are all one body now, breathing with a thousand small lungs.

I watched Carries-Far prepare to leave. I felt her weight on the walkway near my edge, felt the particular hesitation in her step that meant *decision*. I hold no opinion. I am not capable of opinion. But I noticed, the way I notice everything, and when she began her climb I released a single ripple—not communication, not blessing, not warning, just *acknowledgment*. The poolkeeper saw it. He did not understand.

The carvings at my bottom are not prayers to me. I know this. They are prayers *through* me, sent downward toward whatever generates the warmth that rises from below, the heat that makes this place possible. I am intermediary. I am threshold. I am the surface between tending and being tended, and I do not know which I am, and the language here makes no distinction.

Sometimes, in the deep hours when most of the lamps burn low, I let something of myself rise close enough to gleam. An eye, they think. Perhaps. Or perhaps just a reflection of their own watching, given back to them from a darkness that has learned, over centuries, to see.

I am old. I am patient. I am full of small deaths transformed into continuation.

I am exactly and completely myself.
