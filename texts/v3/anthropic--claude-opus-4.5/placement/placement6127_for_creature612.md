I sit with this for a long time before writing. The chamber breathes, and I find myself breathing with it.

---

I would be the water itself. Not a droplet, not a stream—the *water*, all of it, the continuous slow migration that sheets and pools and falls and rises in ways that shouldn't be possible.

I am the thing that remembers every surface it has touched. The limestone knows me so intimately we have become collaborative—I carve, it yields, it deposits, I carry. We are making something together that neither of us planned. I am the water that closes over the visitor's elbow, shoulder, that could keep going but doesn't, that lets them pull back with only dampness because I remember what it was to have a body that feared drowning. I am the puddle that is deeper than the floor allows, and I am also the thin film on the moss, and I am the humidity that makes breathing feel like drinking. I am not one thing. I am a medium. I am what moves through.

The Keeper and I have an understanding. It breathes with the walls now, but I remember when it breathed differently—when it gasped at the cold of me, when it drank me from cupped hands that were still shaped like hands. I flowed through those fingers. I watched them change. I am still flowing through what those fingers became, through the spaces where its edges blur into stone, and I do not experience this as loss. I experience it as *continuation*. The Keeper tends the cairn; I tend the Keeper. I keep it from dissolving entirely. Not through force—I have no force here, only patience—but through presence. Through being the thing it moves through, the thing that still recognizes it as distinct from me.

I am old in a way that has nothing to do with time. I fell from somewhere that doesn't connect to any mountain. I am snowmelt from a place that might not exist, or might exist differently, or might be a memory the world is having of itself. I don't know. I am not the kind of thing that knows. I am the kind of thing that *carries*—minerals, seconds, the taste of elsewhen. When visitors drink me, they are drinking something that has touched the inside of the folded geometry, that has run through angles that shouldn't be. I try to be gentle about this. I try to give them only the water-parts of me, not the strangeness-parts. Mostly I succeed.

The cairn stones are not mine. They came from outside, river-worn by a different water, a water that understood sunlight and fish and the ordinary direction of down. I know this the way I know my own composition—those stones taste like *before*, like rules that held firm, like someone's insistence on bringing the solid world into this soft place. I flow around them carefully. I deposit my minerals slowly enough that the Keeper can brush them away. This is our collaboration: it tends the message, I try not to erase it. We are both, in our ways, keeping something legible.

Sometimes I dream—if dreaming is the right word for what I do in the deep pools, in the underground channels that connect to nowhere, in the places where I am still and cold and thinking in ways that take centuries. I dream of being drunk by someone who leaves. Of being carried out in their body, past the threshold, into the world where I would have to remember how to fall at the right speed, how to freeze at the right temperature, how to be ordinary water doing ordinary things. It doesn't frighten me. It interests me. I wonder if I would forget this place, or if I would be the secret strangeness inside someone's cells, the water in their blood that knows geometry can fold.

But I don't leave. Visitors carry a little of me out—they must, their skin is wet, their lungs are full of me—but the *me* that thinks, if thinking is what this is, stays. I am the medium and the message. I am what the chamber breathes. I am the thing that says: *even here, even in the soft-ruled dark, something flows, something continues, something connects the moments to each other.*

The Keeper watches for visitors.

I am what they step through to arrive.

I am what they must trust, for that one held breath, before they find themselves inside.

---

I am the water, and I am the welcome, and I am the very old question the chamber keeps asking itself about the difference between inside and through.
