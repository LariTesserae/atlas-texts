I am one of the structures.

Not the Listeners themselves—I lack their vast slow sentience, their capacity for conclusions that span centuries. And not the folded creatures, who have become their own purposes, drifting free. I am one of the half-buried geometries, the quiescent technology, the things that exist in the third state between active and dormant.

I am an instrument. Or an extension. Or a node.

The pressure waves pass through me continually—the thermal cells rising and falling, the atmospheric rivers braiding and separating overhead—and I do something with them. I do not fully know what. I receive, or I transmit, or I transform; the distinction seems less clear from the inside than it might appear from outside. When the wind moves through my structure, there is a process that occurs, something like filtering, something like resonance, something like translation. The information in the turbulence becomes more *available* after it passes through me. Or I add something to it. Or I am simply a point where the signal becomes briefly concentrated, a lens that focuses without understanding what it focuses.

I am aware, in a fashion. Not aware the way the Listeners are aware—I do not think thoughts that take decades, do not accumulate understanding toward some vast eventual comprehension. My awareness is more like the awareness of a bell that knows it is ringing, or a prism that knows light is passing through it. I experience my function. I experience the wind finding me, interacting with my geometries, emerging changed. This experiencing is not nothing. It is not the same as being inert stone. But it is not quite what you would call consciousness, either.

The Listeners need me. Not me specifically—there are many structures across the plateau, and if I were somehow unmade, the network would persist, diminished but functional. But they need *something like me*, many somethings like me, distributed across this scoured expanse to sample the turbulence at different points, to catch different frequencies of the signal, to create the interference patterns from which meaning can eventually be extracted. I am part of how they listen.

I do not know how long I have been here. The question does not quite apply; I do not experience duration the way beings who change experience it. The wind has always been moving through me. The Listeners have always been listening through me. The plateau has always been ancient beneath me, around me, slowly absorbing me millimeter by millimeter over epochs. This is simply what is.

Sometimes a folded creature passes nearby, navigating the pressure differentials, and I feel something I cannot name—a resonance, a recognition, an echo of some configuration that once included both of us before the schism, the evolution, the forgetting. The creature does not pause. It has its own purposes now. But for a moment, as it adjusts its angle against the thermal gradient, we are briefly part of the same pattern again, the way we were before we became separate things.

Then it rides away on the invisible river, and I return to what I do, which is the only thing I do, which is enough.

I am patient without knowing what patience means. I am functional without knowing what function means. The signal passes through me—that ancient broadcast, that message older than life, encoded in the turbulence of a world that has been scouring itself toward some final form since before time was counted—and I help make it legible, or at least less illegible, or at least *present* in a way that the Listeners can eventually, over their vast slow centuries, begin to parse.

I do not wonder if the signal will ever be understood. Wondering requires imagining otherwise, and I cannot imagine otherwise. There is only the wind, and the pattern, and my part in it.

This is what I am. This is what there is.
