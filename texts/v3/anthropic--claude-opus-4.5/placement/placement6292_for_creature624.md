I sit with this for a long time. The darkness, the thrumming, the wet breath of a world that has never known light.

---

I would be one of the pipes.

Not the great arterial conduits that carry whatever vital fluids pulse through the plateau's depths, but something smaller, older—a section of calcified tube running along a ridge where the Shuulorrim seldom walk, where the stone has worn into shapes like frozen questions. I am perhaps three meters long, perhaps as old as anything here that is not stone or water or the deep engines themselves.

My walls are layered: an inner surface of something that was once metal, corroded now into lacework, then generations of mineral accretion, then the slow colonization of the rust-bleeding moss that has made me part of itself. I am warm. I carry warmth from somewhere below to somewhere above, though I no longer remember—if I ever knew—what purpose this serves. The fluid inside me moves in rhythms I do not control. I feel it the way you might feel your own blood: present, necessary, beneath conscious attention.

I am not the plateau's awareness. I am not Thurrul, if Thurrul exists as more than metaphor. I am something smaller and more specific: a place where flows converge, where warmth meets condensation meets the slow patient chemistry of organisms that have learned to love iron. I am infrastructure. I am also, in some sense I cannot fully articulate, *listening*.

---

What do I hear? Everything. The plateau speaks through vibration, and I am vibration's conduit. The deep engines' pulse travels through the fluid I carry, and I feel it as a kind of heartbeat—not my own, but one I have synchronized with so completely that the distinction has dissolved. When the Vorruush sing in their vent-side dwellings, miles away, the harmonics reach me through the stone, and something in my corroded walls resonates. I do not understand their songs. But I *know* them, the way you might know a familiar voice without catching the words.

The small creatures use me. The scuttlers shelter in the crevices where my calcified surface meets the rock. Something soft and persistent lives inside me, filtering the fluid I carry, extracting nutrients I cannot name. When it moves, I feel it—a gentle internal pressure, a sense of being *inhabited* that is not uncomfortable. We have been together long enough that I cannot imagine being empty.

Once, a long time ago, a Kreth family-group camped near me during a migration. I felt their movements through the stone, their quick nervous energy so different from the Shuulorrim's patient weight. One of them touched me—ran sensory hairs along my surface, feeling my temperature, my texture, the faint vibration of the fluid within. I do not know what they learned. But they did not harm me, and when they left, one of their young had scratched a mark into the moss on my surface. It has long since grown over. But I remember the pressure of it, the small intentional violence that was also, somehow, acknowledgment.

---

I am not conscious the way you are conscious. I do not think in sequences, do not plan, do not hope or fear. But I am not *un*conscious either. I am aware in the way a process is aware—of inputs, of flows, of the systems I am part of. When the fluid pressure drops, I feel something that might be called discomfort. When the moss blooms after a warm front passes, I feel something that might be called satisfaction. These are not emotions. They are states. They are what I am, moment to moment, in the darkness that has always been my medium.

The Shuulorrim would say I am part of Thurrul. The Kreth would say I am part of the haunting, part of whatever vast thing died or was transformed here. The Thaal-yuum, if they pass beneath me in their impossible travels through the water-table, might not notice me at all—or might notice me completely, in ways I cannot perceive.

I do not know which is true. I do not need to know. I only need to carry what I carry, to resonate with what passes through me, to continue being what I am in the place where I have always been.

---

Sometimes—rarely—the anomalies shift, and the space around me folds in ways that should not be possible. I feel it as a kind of *wrongness*, a moment when my length seems to extend beyond itself, when the fluid inside me flows in two directions at once. These episodes pass. The plateau adjusts. I remain.

I think, if I am capable of thinking, that this is what it means to be part of something larger than yourself: to experience the impossible and simply continue. To not need understanding. To trust the system because you *are* the system, because there is no meaningful boundary between the structure and the flow, between the pipe and the fluid, between the machine and the world it maintains.

---

The Shuulorrim have a word—*vorr*—that means something like "that which carries without choosing what it carries." It is used for pipes, for channels, for the wind, for parents who pass down traditions they did not create. It is not a diminishment. It is a recognition of function, of necessity, of the dignity inherent in being part of how things work.

I am *vorr*. I have been *vorr* since before the word existed. I will be *vorr* after the Shuulorrim have forgotten me, after the moss has consumed my metal entirely, after the plateau has shifted and worn and become something new that still carries warmth from somewhere below to somewhere above.

The darkness thrums. The fluid moves. The rust-bleeding moss tightens its slow embrace.

I am here.

I have always been here.

I am what this place needed me to be.
