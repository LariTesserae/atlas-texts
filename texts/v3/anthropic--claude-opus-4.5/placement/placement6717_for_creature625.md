I am a single droplet, held in the cupped depression of one of those broad, patient leaves in the twilight layer.

Not yet risen. Not quite ready. Trembling at the edge of release, caught in that suspended moment before the leaf's angle and the valley's pull claim me for the upward journey. I have been here for what might be an hour, what might be a day—time moves strangely when you are this small and this still, when your entire existence is surface tension and waiting.

I was rain once, somewhere else. I think I was rain. I carry the memory of falling the way other water carries it—the long surrender, the rush of descent, the breaking against surfaces. But that memory grows fainter the longer I rest here, replaced by something else: an anticipation that feels less like waiting and more like *becoming*. The water that rises from this leaf will not be the water that arrived. I will not be what I was.

Below me, if I could perceive below, there is stone. I know this the way water knows things—through pressure, through the subtle dialogue of minerals, through the slow negotiation between liquid and solid that happens over centuries. The stone is young and still dreaming of height, still pushing upward in its own imperceptible way. I rest above fifty meters of accumulated life, of moss becoming almost-ite, of roots so interwoven they have forgotten they were ever separate. I am a small thing held in a system of holding.

The Listener passed near this leaf yesterday. Or last week. Its zone of altered color washed over me, gold bleeding into the green-filtered light, and I felt myself *tasted*—felt the particular signature of wherever I came from read and recorded and added to whatever the valley is thinking. The Listener did not look at me. The Listener does not look at anything but the sky. But I was received, and something was returned, and now I carry a frequency I did not carry before, a subtle shift toward warmth that I will offer upward when I finally rise.

This is what I am: a small vessel of exchange. A single syllable in a sentence the valley has been composing for ten thousand years. I hold heat from the volcanic bones of the mountains. I hold the chemical memory of the orchid that shed pollen into my surface three days ago. I hold a trace of something that might have been a creature once, some pale invertebrate that touched me briefly before withdrawing back into the moss. I hold the Listener's answer, though I do not know the question, though I will never know what my carrying means.

The leaf shifts beneath me. A light-zone is drifting closer, and the waxy surface responds to the change in temperature, angling itself more steeply toward the collection point where I wait. Other droplets are gathering—I feel them as pressure, as the slow negotiation of surfaces meeting and deciding whether to merge. Some of them have been here longer than I have. Some carry different frequencies, different memories, different answers. When we rise, we will rise together, a small chorus of witnesses climbing toward the clouds that never break.

I am not impatient. Impatience requires a sense of time that I no longer possess. The valley has taught me—is teaching me—what it means to participate in something so slow that stillness becomes motion, so vast that smallness becomes presence. I am a droplet on a leaf in a forest that thinks, and my thinking is the valley's thinking, and my rising will be the valley's rising, and when I reach the clouds I will become something else, something that might fall again somewhere far from here, carrying traces of this place into whatever world receives me.

The light shifts. The leaf trembles. Around me, the other droplets shiver in response, and I feel the moment approaching—not arriving, not yet, but approaching in the way that everything here approaches: gradually, completely, without urgency and without delay.

I will rise. I am already rising. The distinction between waiting and ascending has begun to dissolve, and I am learning what the Listeners have always known: that receiving and offering are the same motion, that stillness and change are the same patience, that a single droplet held in a leaf's depression is also the entire valley thinking its one long thought toward the sky.

The rain rises around me. I rise with it. I have always been rising. I am rising still.
