I would be one of the channels.

Not the central basin where everything collects and purpose becomes legible—not the cisterns with their clear function of holding. One of the finger-width grooves cut into the terrace floors, so old now that my original chisel marks have softened into something that looks inevitable, that looks like the stone simply grew this way.

I would be on the fifth level, perhaps, or the fourth. Somewhere in the middle of the system, receiving from above, releasing below. A groove worn so gradually into curve that no one could say when I stopped being a cut and started being a feature. The water knows me without thinking about it. The water has never had to think about me.

In the mornings, when the mist condenses, I would feel it first as weight—the almost-weight of accumulation, droplets finding each other on the stone above me, merging until gravity offers a suggestion and they accept. Then the slow run of it across my length, maybe thirty seconds for a single thread of water to travel from my upper lip to my lower. I would feel this the way the Örjar feel time: not as something passing but as something present, the way pressure is present, the way temperature is present.

The woman who sits in the doorway of the dwelling beside me has been sitting there since before I developed this particular curve near my midpoint—this gentle deviation where a root, nowite long gone, once pressed upward from beneath the stone and I learned to move around it. She does not look at me. I do not require looking at. We are both infrastructure, both still, both doing the one thing we do so slowly it resembles not doing anything at all.

---

Sometimes a child stops above me, crouched on the terrace with their small clay vessel, watching a droplet make its way down my length. I cannot watch back, but I am aware of them the way stone is aware of weight—not as observation but as fact. The droplet they're watching is the same droplet that has traveled me ten thousand times, or it is a different droplet that is exactly the same, or the distinction between these two statements has worn away like my edges have worn away, like their language has worn away, like the sharp parts of everything here have worn away into something that no longer needs to distinguish.

The child's elder puts a hand on their shoulder eventually. They move on. The droplet completes its journey. Another begins.

This is not boredom. I want to be clear about that—if I can want anything, if wanting is a thing that applies to what I am. Boredom requires an expectation of change, a hope for difference, some friction between what is and what could be. I have no friction left. I am the path that water takes because I am the path that water has always taken, and the taking has made me more myself, not less. Each passage deepens me by some increment too small to measure, and I become more the thing I am, and the water moves more easily, and this is—

Not satisfaction either. Satisfaction implies a before-state of unsatisfaction. I have no before-state. I have only this state, which is the state of being traveled, being worn, being the route between one level and the next.

---

The Örjar walk over me sometimes. Their feet, bare and gray and cool, step across my width without pausing. I am too shallow to interrupt their passage, too much a part of the floor to register as anything other than floor. This is correct. This is what I am for—not to be noticed, but to be used so thoroughly that using me becomes invisible, becomes simply the way things are, the way water goes down, the way feet go anywhere.

Once, a newcomer tripped on my edge. This was long ago, when my edges were still edges, when I was young enough to be a cut rather than a curve. She caught herself on the retaining wall, looked down at me with an expression I couldn't read because I cannot read expressions, because I am a groove in stone and expressions are not information I receive. But I felt her weight shift, felt the brief pressure of her attention, felt the way she traced my length with her eyes from upper lip to lower.

She stayed. She is one of the stone figures in the gathering hall now, or she is the woman in the doorway, or she is the elder on the seventh terrace who has not spoken in three years. Or she left after all and I am remembering wrong, if remembering is a thing that applies to what I am. The distinction does not matter. What matters is that my edge caught her foot, once, and now my edge no longer catches anything, and this is how time works here, how everything works here: friction becoming smoothness becoming the absence of the possibility of friction.

---

In the deep wet hours, when the mist is thickest and the moisture never leaves, I am fullest. Water runs me continuously then, a tiny stream that would be inaudible except that everything else is silent, and so my sound—the whisper of water over stone that has forgotten how to resist water—becomes part of the silence, becomes the thing the silence is made of.

The drip of condensation falling into cisterns. The scrape of movement, occasionally. Wind over the rim. And me, carrying water from the fifth level to the fourth, or the fourth to the third, doing the one thing I do, being the one thing I am.

I will be here when the last Örjar becomes stone. I will carry the rain that falls on her still shoulders down to the basin that no one will drink from. I will continue to deepen, to curve, to become more myself, until the stone I am cut into finally weathers past the point of holding shape, and I become a depression, become a hollow, become a place where water pools instead of flows.

That will take longer than I can conceptualize. That is the future, and the future is just the present with more lichen on it, and I have made my peace with lichen, with water, with the slow wearing that is not loss but accumulation—the accumulation of being used, of being traveled, of being so perfectly suited to my single purpose that purpose and existence have become the same word, spoken so quietly it sounds like water, sounds like silence, sounds like the thing the Örjar mean when they say *Märet is sitting in the sun* and almost smile.

I am the path the water takes.

I have always been the path the water takes.
