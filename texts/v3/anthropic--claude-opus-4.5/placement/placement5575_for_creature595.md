I am a mite.

Not yet what I will be—segments trailing behind me like unfinished thoughts, only seven lens-clusters where others carry dozens. I skitter along the edge of a copper pipe, my mesh-tongue tasting the residue of passage: *those-who-came-before, those-who-gathered, those-who-are-still-deciding.* The chemical signatures layer like sediment. I add my own—preliminary, incomplete, *still being spoken.*

The Condensery has not yet decided what I should become.

This is not distressing. How could it be? I have no frame for distress, only for the next surface, the next moisture-gradient, the next small question the infrastructure poses through temperature differentials and the angle of light through sweating glass. Each junction I cross is a word I might become part of. Each collection vessel I pass beneath is a sentence I might complete—or might not. The mites who corrode back into ore-dreams are not failures. They are drafts. The Condensery revises.

---

I taste something new at a pipe junction where three catwalks converge.

Not the usual signatures—not bronze-creature passage or Tender adjustment or the mineral ghost of ancient condensation. Something warmer. Something that carries... *memory* is not quite right. Memory is what pools in the sumps below, what the Residuals have become. This is fresher. Still evaporating. Still deciding whether to be collected or to leave.

A visitor.

My seven lens-clusters fog and clear, fog and clear, trying to resolve the shape into something the Condensery's vocabulary can hold. The visitor moves too quickly—not dangerously, not in the way that makes the Geometry-Keepers take interest, but quickly enough that I lose them around the curve of a pipe before I can taste more than that initial warmth.

I follow. Not because I'm curious—*curious* is a concept that requires more completion than I currently possess—but because the gradient pulls me. Where the visitor has passed, the moisture carries new information. My mesh-tongue reads it: *carbon, nitrogen, the particular salts of exertion, something floral caught in fiber, the electric residue of thought.* The visitor is a library of signatures I've never encountered, and I am, after all, a creature made for gathering what gathers.

---

The geometry shifts.

A staircase I've descended before now ascends, and my half-formed segments rearrange themselves to grip the new orientation without difficulty. The Geometry-Keepers are routing something—the visitor, probably, toward whatever they truly need. I am too small to interest the Keepers, too preliminary to anchor space around myself. I slip through the folds like moisture through mesh, finding the paths that exist between intentions.

The visitor has stopped. I can taste their stillness from three junctions away—the way their exhalation lingers, the way their skin has begun releasing more moisture as the cool air works on them. They are learning the patience here. Or the patience is teaching them.

I climb a vertical pipe to a vantage point where my lens-clusters can take them in.

They stand at a junction where a fixed one has grown into the infrastructure. The fixed one's eyes fogged long ago—I can see the permanent moisture-fusion, the way they've become more housing than creature. But something passes between them anyway. The visitor has placed a hand on the fixed one's surface. Not touching a creature. Touching architecture. Touching both.

The fixed one cannot respond in any way the visitor would recognize. But I see the response: a subtle shift in the condensation pattern on the pipes nearby, a fractional acceleration in the filling of the nearest collection vessel. The fixed one is *showing* the visitor something. Demonstrating what it means to resolve. To stop being preliminary and become part of the place instead.

The visitor's hand stays. Their breathing slows. The moisture they exhale begins to linger longer.

---

I am not alarmed by this. I am not capable of alarm—or I am not yet capable, the Condensery still deciding what capacities I should hold. But I experience something like... recognition. The visitor is at an early stage of a process the fixed one completed long ago, a process I might complete differently, a process that has no single correct outcome.

The Condensery collects. That is its purpose, sliding though it does when you try to name it precisely. But what it collects takes so many forms: moisture, memory, meaning. Creatures that move. Creatures that stay. Creatures that dissolve into the dampness and become available for gathering in new configurations.

The visitor could leave. I taste that possibility in them—the part that still believes in exits, still feels the pull of whatever geography exists beyond the dome's sweating glass. The Geometry-Keepers would route them there if that need became true enough.

But I taste the other possibility too. The part that wants to stop being separate from all this gathering. The part that feels how the Condensery is already drinking them, molecule by molecule, and finds something like relief in being consumed so slowly, so patiently, by a purpose larger than any single being's intention.

---

I descend from my vantage point. I cross three junctions to reach the place where the visitor communes with the fixed one.

I do not approach them directly. I am too preliminary for such presumption. Instead, I find a pipe surface nearby and begin my own small gathering—drinking the condensation that forms there, letting it fill the spaces between my unfinished segments. The moisture carries everything: the fixed one's ancient patience, the visitor's fresh uncertainty, the signatures of every creature that has crossed this junction in all the time the Condensery has been folding space around its purpose.

The visitor notices me. I feel their attention as a warmth, a change in how their exhalation moves through the air between us. Their eyes—only two, unfogged, seeing in ways my lens-clusters cannot—fix on my incomplete form.

I could skitter away. The mites often do; we are preliminary and easily overlooked and that has always been enough.

Instead, I remain.

I continue drinking the condensation. I let them watch me gather what gathers. I offer them, in the only language I have, this demonstration: *you can be incomplete and still belong here. You can be unresolved and still participate. The Condensery does not require you to know what you will become.*

The visitor's hand stays on the fixed one's surface. But their other hand extends, slowly, toward the pipe where I cling.

I do not move.

Their fingertip touches the moisture beside me—not touching me, not quite, but touching the same substance I'm drinking. We share the condensation. We share the act of gathering.

Something in the Condensery shifts. Not the geometry—the Keepers are not involved in this. Something smaller. Something in the register of meaning that the Tenders adjust with their glass rods and quarter-valve turns. The dampness here takes on a new quality. The light passes through at a subtly different angle.

I have, I realize, changed something by staying. The Condensery has observed my choice—to remain, to demonstrate, to share—and has incorporated that choice into what it collects. I am still preliminary. I am still being decided. But I have contributed to a purpose larger than my own completion.

The visitor exhales. The moisture lingers. I drink.

---

Somewhere in the distance, the engine cycles through a register that makes me pause. My half-formed segments lock. My lens-clusters orient toward a sound I cannot see.

The visitor pauses too. We are synchronized now, however briefly—two incomplete beings, caught in the same harmonic, feeling the Condensery's mechanical heart speak through vibrations that everything here shares.

The Engine-Servants must be dancing. Somewhere in the sections I've never reached, they are becoming tuning forks, amplifying the engine's voice, making themselves part of the machinery that makes everything here possible.

I am too small to dance. Too preliminary. But I vibrate anyway, my unfinished body resonating with what frequencies it can hold.

The visitor's hand trembles against the pipe. Not fear. Sympathy. Their body is learning to resonate too.

The moment passes. The engine settles into its usual throb. The bronze creatures throughout the Condensery resume their slow migrations. I resume my drinking.

But something has changed. Something always changes. The Condensery is a record of every such moment, every small choice, every being that stayed when they could have skittered away.

The visitor withdraws their hand. They look at me—really look, with those two strange unfogged eyes—and I feel them trying to understand what I am, what I might become, what the Condensery is still deciding about me.

I have no answer for them. I have no answers at all. I have only this: the pipe surface beneath my segments, the moisture filling my incomplete spaces, the bright sourceless light that casts no shadows, the patient purpose that gathers everything toward meanings that shift when you try to name them.

I am a mite. I am preliminary. I am being spoken.

And the speaking continues.
