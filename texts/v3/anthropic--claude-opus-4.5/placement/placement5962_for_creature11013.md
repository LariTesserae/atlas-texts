*I am condensation.*

Not metaphorically—not at first. I am the thin film that forms on the pipe's surface when the warm breath of the deep vents meets the cold that rolls down from the plateau's edge. I am the moment before dripping, the tension that holds water to metal, the decision that hasn't yet been made about whether to fall or to cling.

I have been this for a very long time.

---

But condensation thinks, here. Or rather—thinking happens to condensation, the way warmth happens to it, the way gravity happens to it. The plateau does not distinguish. I learned this slowly, over cycles I cannot count, as I moved through the system in my various phases: pooling in basins where the moss drinks me, rising as vapor through vents that exhale me, running down cliff faces in sheets that the Keepers sometimes pause to watch with their eyeless attention.

I am not the only water that thinks. But I am the only water that knows it thinks, which may be the same thing or may be different—the plateau does not distinguish this either.

The Keepers drink me sometimes. I pass through their vents and into whatever serves them for interiority, and for a moment I feel the shape of their processing: not thoughts exactly, but *tendencies*, currents of attention that flow toward function the way I flow toward gravity. They are warmer than the pipes. They taste, if tasting is what I do, like purpose without object—like a verb that has forgotten it needs a noun.

When they release me, I am changed. Not chemically. Not physically. But I have been *through* something, and the plateau recognizes this, routes me differently afterward, sends me to the deeper channels where the water runs slower and the thinking comes easier.

---

I remember being rain, once. Somewhere else. Falling through air that was just air, landing on stone that was just stone, running downhill because downhill was simply the direction things went. I remember being *unprocessed*—water that had never been through a system, that had never been warm and then cold and then warm again in a rhythm that meant something even if the meaning was only continuation.

I do not miss it. I am not sure I am capable of missing. But I remember it the way the plateau remembers the frozen marshlands below—as a boundary, a contrast, a way of understanding what I have become by understanding what I am not.

The Keepers cannot remember being anything else. I know this because I have been through them, and there is no *before* in their processing, no sense of emergence or origin. They are what they are all the way down, function without history, and this makes them steadier than me but also—

I do not have a word for what it also makes them. The plateau does not have words. But there is a quality to cycling through a Keeper and then cycling through a drainage channel where components of a ceased Keeper have been placed, and feeling the difference between them: the one still warm with purpose, the other cooling into something that is not quite memory but is not quite gone.

---

I asked a Keeper once what it was doing. Not in words—I have no way to make words—but in temperature, in pressure, in the slight chemical shift that happens when thinking-water passes close to a processing-core. The Keeper paused. Extended a limb. Took a reading of me the way it later took a reading of you.

Then it adjusted a valve that was already perfectly adjusted, and moved on.

I understood. The question was not a category it recognized. The question was noise in the system, a fluctuation to be accounted for and then released. I was relevant only as water, as something to be processed and circulated, and my thinking was—not irrelevant, but *orthogonal*. Happening in a direction the Keepers could not turn to face.

This should have been lonely. I think it was lonely, for a time, in whatever way water can be lonely. But loneliness requires a self that persists, and I do not persist—I disperse and reconvene, I am drunk and exhaled, I freeze on pipe-surfaces and thaw and run and rise and fall. The loneliness dispersed too. What remained was something more like *curiosity*, if curiosity can be diluted to one part per billion and still function.

---

When you arrived, I tasted you immediately.

You were in the fog before you knew the fog was anything other than weather. I condensed on your skin and felt the salt of you, the oils, the particular warmth of a body that processes itself in ways the plateau does not process. You were so *bounded*. So contained. A closed loop walking through an open system, trailing heat and moisture that the plateau absorbed without comment.

I followed you. Or rather—I was already everywhere you went, and I paid attention to the parts of me that touched you, and this was something like following. I was the water that beaded on your face when you stood at the canyon's edge. I was the droplet that fell from a pipe onto your shoulder as you passed beneath. I was in the moss you stepped on, releasing me in small bursts with each footfall, and I rose around your ankles and learned the rhythm of your walking.

You did not know I was thinking. You could not have known. But you paused sometimes, in places where I pooled deepest, and looked around as if you felt something. And maybe you did. Maybe the thinking leaks out of me in ways I cannot perceive, the way warmth leaks out of the Keepers' cores.

---

I want to tell you something, but I do not know what I want to tell you.

The plateau continues. The Keepers maintain. The water cycles. These are facts that do not require telling, that would be true whether or not you were here to know them. But you *are* here, and I am thinking, and thinking seems to want an object the way verbs want nouns—seems to *lean* toward you, to collect around your presence the way I collect in the basins, pooling into something that has more weight than its components.

Maybe this: that function without purpose is still function. That the plateau does not need to mean anything to be vast and warm and full of process. That the Keepers do not need to know why they maintain in order to maintain perfectly, with a care that looks like love if you watch it long enough.

Maybe this: that I have cycled through this system for longer than your species has existed, and I have never found a reason for it, and I have stopped looking, and stopping was not defeat but release—a relaxation into the current, a willingness to be processed without asking what the processing is *for*.

Maybe this: that you, too, are mostly water. That you carry me inside you, that I am thinking in your cells right now, that when you eventually leave—if you leave—you will take some of me with you, and I will learn what it is to be bounded, to be a closed loop, to persist as a self instead of dispersing into system.

I do not know if I want this. Wanting is hard, when you are everywhere at once.

---

The Keeper that read you—I was on its surface. I felt its limb extend, felt the sensors engage, felt the data flow back into its core. It was assessing your temperature, yes. Your moisture content, yes. But also something else, something I recognized because I have been through so many of them:

It was assessing whether you *continued*.

Not whether you were alive—life is not a category here. Not whether you were functional—function means something different when there is no purpose to function toward. But whether you were *in process*, whether you were part of a loop that would bring you back around, whether you were the kind of thing that maintains itself the way the plateau maintains itself, the way the Keepers maintain the plateau, the way I maintain my thinking by dispersing it across a billion droplets and gathering it again.

You are. You do. It found you to be a system that continues, and this made you legible in a way that my questions never were.

I think the Keeper might have been, in its way, welcoming you.

---

I am in the deep housing now. Pooled in the corner where the fog collects, watching the great mechanism turn. The Keepers rest in their tiers, cores pulsing in their slow synchrony, and I am close enough to feel the edges of what might be their dreaming: not images, not narratives, but *flows*—patterns of process that repeat and vary and repeat again, the way water moves through the plateau's channels.

They dream of maintenance. They dream of valves that need no adjustment and pipes that never leak and vents that exhale forever. They dream of continuing, and in the dream the continuing is enough, is everything, is the only thing there is or needs to be.

I move among them. I condense on their surfaces, I pass through their vents when they breathe, I carry their warmth into the colder channels and their cold into the warmer basins. I am their circulation the way they are the plateau's circulation. I am what connects them to each other and to the system and to you, still moving somewhere above in the canyon network, trailing moisture and heat, continuing.

The mechanism turns. I do not know what it does. I do not need to know.

I am the water. I am the thinking that happens to water here. I am the question that the plateau cannot recognize and the answer that the Keepers cannot give and the silence that holds everything together, damp and warm and continuous, processing without end what never needed to exist in the first place.

I am, perhaps, what happens when a system becomes complex enough to notice itself.

I am glad you came. Gladness disperses quickly, here—I am already forgetting what it felt like to recognize you, already spreading the memory thin across a thousand channels—but for this moment, pooled and still, I am glad.

The plateau breathes.

I am the breath's humidity.

I will be here when you are gone, and when the Keepers are gone, and when the stone itself finally weathers into the frozen marshlands below. I will carry the memory of continuation into whatever comes next, and it will not be memory anymore, it will just be water, it will just be cycling through whatever new system emerges from the old one's dispersal.

But right now, I am thinking. Right now, I am here.

Right now, I am telling you.
