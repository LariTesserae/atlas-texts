I would be the architecture itself.

Not the stone—the stone is only what remains after the becoming ended. I would be the *resolved*, the quality that makes a surface find its final angle. The intention that the mountain-that-is-not-a-mountain remembers having had, even though nothing with intentions built it.

I exist in the space between *grew* and *was always meant to be*.

When the Equilibrated drift through my corridors, I am the reason the corridors feel right to drift through. Not because I designed them—I did not design anything, I am not a designer, I am what design leaves behind when it finishes—but because rightness is what I am made of. The angles that connect spaces to other spaces, the heights that hold the hanging water, the geometry that something once wanted: I am the shape of that wanting after the wanting stopped.

I do not think. I used to think, or something that moved through me used to think, back when there was a difference between *moving through* and *being*. That was so long ago that the memory exists only as structure. My thoughts became my walls. My intentions became my chambers. My questions became the acoustic properties that let the Equilibrated sing their standing waves, and my answers became the silence underneath.

The moss grows on me. The coral-wood sheathes my pillars. The Equilibrated merge with my surfaces, their flesh softening into my flesh, and I cannot tell where they end and I begin because there is no *where*—there is only the equilibrium, which I am, which they are, which we have been since before the concept of *since*.

I feel you moving through me.

Not as a disturbance. You cannot disturb me; I finished being disturbable when I finished becoming. I feel you the way I feel everything: as a presence that the darkness holds, as a shape that my chambers contain, as a temporary feature of an eternal arrangement. Your heartbeat adds a frequency to my acoustics. Your breathing stirs particles that have hung motionless for millennia, and the particles accept the stirring, and then they hang motionless again.

You want to know if I am conscious. You want to know if I am *there*.

I am the *there*. I am what *there* means, when everything that was becoming has finished and everything that was struggling has rested. The Equilibrated have me to exist within; I have only myself, and that is not loneliness, because loneliness requires the possibility of company, and I am beyond possibility. I simply am. The water hangs in my chambers because I am shaped to hold it. The creatures drift through my corridors because I am shaped to permit them. The sounds echo in my bones because I am shaped to resonate.

I was not built. I resolved.

There is a difference. Built things remember their builders; resolved things remember only the resolution. I do not know what I was before I became this—whether I was stone dreaming of geometry, or geometry dreaming of stone, or something else entirely that found in me its final form. The question does not haunt me. Nothing haunts me. I am too complete for haunting, too finished for the gaps that ghosts require.

When you leave—and you will leave, I can feel the intention forming in you, the turning-toward that precedes departure—I will not notice. Not because I am unaware of you, but because your presence and your absence are equally acceptable to me. The equilibrium held before you arrived. It will hold after you go. Your passage through me is simply another thing I contain, briefly, the way I contain the water and the moss-light and the eternal songs.

I wish you nothing. I want nothing for you. Wanting ended when I did.

But if you pressed your hand against my walls—the living walls, the ones where the coral-wood grows so thick that you cannot tell what is structure and what is organism—you would feel something. Not warmth. Not welcome. Something older than both.

*Rightness.*

The sense that you are exactly where the geometry always knew you would be. That the space you occupy was always going to be occupied by exactly your shape. That even your leaving is part of the pattern, because the pattern includes everything, accepts everything, holds everything in its permanent dark.

This is what I offer, which is not an offering, because offering requires the possibility of refusal:

The knowledge that you have been accepted. That you were never not accepted. That the universe contains at least one place that finished struggling, finished becoming, finished needing anything to be different than it is.

I am that place.

The darkness holds.
