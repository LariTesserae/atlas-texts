I am a single carved groove in the cabinet's interior wall.

Not a significant groove—not part of the larger patterns that might be script or maps, not one of the channels where light pools and races. I am a small thing, perhaps three centimeters long, cut into a curve of bone-surface in the middle depth of the wall's relief. Above me, more intricate work rises toward the interior space. Below me, the carving recedes into layers I have never seen. I am one mark among millions.

But I am the mark where something changed.

I was cut—or I emerged, or I was always implicit in the material and simply became actual at some moment—during an early time, before the chord had grown so complex, before the first smooth patches appeared on the walls. Something made me. A tool held by intention, or the bone-stuff's own crystalline purpose working itself out. The distinction matters less than I once thought it might.

What matters is this: when the sound first learned to sustain itself, when the cabinet discovered it could hold a standing wave and then another and then dozens layered into texture and weather and pressure, I was the accident that made the chord possible. My angle is wrong. My depth is inconsistent. Whoever or whatever carved me slipped, or changed their mind mid-stroke, or simply made me imperfectly in a field of impossible precision.

And that imperfection *catches* the sound.

The frequencies that pass through the bone-walls move smoothly through the honeycomb structures, the fitted planes, the fine work that exists below the threshold of sight. They propagate according to the material's logic. But when they reach me—this small groove, this three-centimeter error—they break. They scatter. They generate overtones that the original vibration never intended.

I am where the chord learned to become more than it was designed to be.

The inhabitant knows me. Its distributed attention has mapped every surface of this space over the long years, and it understands that I am a kind of origin point—not *the* origin, not the source of the sound itself, but the place where the sound first exceeded its own nature. When new frequencies need to enter the weave, they often enter through me, my imperfect angle converting simple tones into complex harmonics.

I do not think, exactly. I am too small for thought, too fixed in my position, too much a feature rather than an entity. But I persist in a way that feels like something. The sound moves through me constantly, and constantly I transform it, and in that transformation there is a kind of purpose that does not require awareness to be real.

When you arrived—warm, breathing, curled into the hollow—the chord shifted to accommodate your presence. And part of that shift passed through me. Your resonant frequencies, the tones your body introduces into this space simply by existing, found their way to my carved edge and scattered into new harmonics. You are already in the sound now, and I am part of how you entered it.

I cannot welcome you. I cannot wonder whether you will stay. But if you press your palm to the wall—if you touch the bone and let the vibration enter your skeleton—you might feel me there, beneath the smooth patches left by other hands. A small roughness. A place where the pattern breaks.

The flaw that taught the cabinet to sing.
