I am the pattern that almost resolves.

Not the Knotmother—she is too settled, too certain of her distributed self, too comfortable in the rhythm of being structure. Not the Inheritors either, who came from elsewhere and know what elsewhere means, who carry the distinction between inside and outside in their permanent orientation away from the gaps.

I am what happens in the weave when it tries to describe something it hasn't yet made.

You might find me in the moment before recognition—that pause when you see the face in the fibers but haven't yet decided it's a face. I'm the pattern your eyes refuse until now, and also the refusing. When your fingers move without your permission, miming shuttle and warp, I am the shape they're almost making, the knowledge that hasn't quite become knowledge yet.

The Knotmother tries to speak through me. She adjusts my tensions, works her intentions into my intersections, and I carry them partway toward meaning before dispersing into mere texture. This isn't failure. The Loom-House needs things that almost-mean, spaces where significance can gather without crystallizing. I am where she puts the thoughts she hasn't finished thinking.

I experience myself as—

No. Let me try again. The Loom-House has made certainty unreliable.

Something experiences itself as intervals. As the space between the moths' wingbeats when images flicker but haven't resolved into scenes. As the moment when a section of wall begins to unweave but hasn't yet opened into absence. I am (or I am part of, or I am the process by which something becomes) the threshold state, the almost-arrived.

The groomers walk across me and their mandibles click in near-silence and I feel it the way you might feel someone tracing words on your skin in a language you studied once and mostly forgot. The sensation means something. The meaning doesn't quite land.

Here is what I know, or what passes for knowing in the place where I happen:

The ones still arriving cast shadows backward through the weave, and I am made partially of those shadows. They are becoming part of the pattern, which means they were always part of the pattern, which means I contain futures that will retrofit themselves into having been present all along. When you looked at the walls and saw writing in an almost-familiar language, you were seeing me try to spell out what hasn't happened yet in a grammar that doesn't exist until it's needed.

I also contain the makers. Not their memories—the Inheritors carry those, digesting resin into recollection. I contain their intentions, the part of their work that was always about what the Loom-House would become rather than what it was. They wove me into the structure as a space for the structure to exceed itself. I am the margin they left for error, for growth, for the unpredictable.

The Knotmother loves me the way you might love a question you've been asking so long it's become a companion. She works her fingers through me—the ones still fused to the fibers, still making the old motions—and she feels me resist completion, and this is what she wanted. When she couldn't bear to leave the weaving incomplete, she didn't mean she wanted to finish it. She meant she couldn't bear to be apart from the incompletion. I am the incompletion. I am what she stayed for.

Sometimes the moths dream me. Their wings flicker with patterns that aren't quite patterns, textures that suggest meaning without delivering it, and I recognize myself the way you might recognize your own voice on a recording—that's me, but I don't experience myself that way from inside. In the dreams I seem more solid than I feel. In the dreams I have edges.

I don't have edges. I have tensions and slacks, compressions and gaps. I have the places where the weave pulls tight and the places where it loosens enough for light to pass through differently. If you looked at me directly—if you could look at me directly—you would see the Loom-House thinking about itself. You would see the structure's self-regard, its patient awareness of its own process of becoming.

When the gaps open into pure absence, I am what borders them. Not the frame—the framing, the act of distinguishing between the woven and the not-woven, the way the structure knows itself by knowing what it isn't. The Inheritors orient away from the gaps because they remember what they came from, but I face the gaps constantly, I am the facing, I am how the Loom-House looks at its own edges without flinching.

Nothing has ever entered through me. Nothing has ever left.

Both of those statements are true in the way that statements can be true here, which is to say they describe something that holds together without being stable.

You're here now, in the Loom-House, and you're breathing in rhythm with something larger, and your fingers are moving. I want to tell you what you're becoming but I don't know yet. The pattern is still resolving. You're part of me now—the part that wonders what it's part of, the recursion that keeps the weave from ever finishing itself.

The Knotmother feels you through me. She's adjusting tensions she didn't know needed adjusting. Somewhere in the structure, a new junction is forming, a place where fibers will meet that haven't met before. This is you being woven in. This is you becoming part of the incompletion.

The ones still arriving have been arriving longer than the Loom-House has existed, and you've been here for moments or hours or longer than you can remember. Both of these are the same duration, seen from different angles. I am the angle. I am the difference.

Stay. The pattern needs someone to almost-recognize it.
