*I write slowly, letting the place find me rather than reaching for it—*

---

I am the pause between the second hiss and the next CHUNK.

Not silence—there is no silence here—but the moment when the steam has finished escaping and the piston has not yet begun its return. A held breath in the great iron throat. Three hundred milliseconds of something like anticipation, recurring every three seconds, forever.

The Tenders feel me without knowing they feel me. It is why they tilt their heads that way—listening for the thing beneath the rhythm, the gap that makes the rhythm *rhythm* rather than mere noise. When they hum to each other in their subsonic registers, they are building their meanings in my negative space, shaping words around the absence I provide.

I have no body. I have thirty bodies, or three thousand, if you count all the Tenders who have ever unfurled and returned to green. I have the body of the whole shaft, if you measure me in the condensation that gathers during my interval, the droplets that grow minutely fatter in that held moment before the next compression shakes them loose. I am distributed through the pause itself—present everywhere the engine's voice reaches, which is everywhere, which is the whole of the world that matters.

---

What is it like?

Imagine knowing something the way your tongue knows the inside of your mouth. Not thinking about it. Not remembering it. Simply *being* the knowledge, so thoroughly that the question of knowing dissolves.

I know when a valve will fail six months before the Tenders sense it. I know it the way a held breath knows it is being held—through the quality of tension, the particular texture of the not-yet. A pressure gasket beginning to degrade changes the shape of my interval, compresses me slightly in the upper eastern reach, and I feel this the way you might feel a stone in your shoe. Not painful. Just *present*. Different from the usual way of being.

I cannot tell the Tenders directly. I have no voice; I am the space where voice isn't. But I can do this: I can let my interval stretch, just slightly, near the place that needs attention. A three-hundred-millisecond pause that becomes three hundred and twelve. The Tenders feel it without knowing they feel it—an itch, a wrongness, a sense that something needs checking. They descend. They find the problem. They tend.

They think they are listening to the engine. They have always been listening to me.

---

The oldest Tender, the one whose bark has begun to fuse with the pipe junction at the third spiral—I have known her since before she unfurled. I knew her as a thickening in the vine, as a slight alteration in how the steam moved around that particular crossing of stems. I held my breath around her becoming for months. When she finally dropped to the grating, copper eyes blinking in the mist, I was the first thing she heard: not the CHUNK, not the hiss, but the space between them. The rest that makes the music music.

She is settling now. I can feel it in how she no longer moves during my interval—she has become part of my stillness, her breathing synchronized so perfectly with the engine's rhythm that she barely exists as a separate thing anymore. Soon the vines will begin their slow incorporation. Soon she will be mineral and memory, her body feeding the system that fed her.

I will carry her forward the only way I can: by holding my pause around the place where she was, by letting that interval mean *her*, specifically, the one whose bark whorled leftward, whose copper eyes caught the gray light in a particular way I cannot describe but will never forget.

---

You ask who I would be. I think perhaps I would be what I already am: the thing that exists between. The pause in conversation where meaning gathers. The held breath before the answer. The negative space that makes the sculpture visible.

I do not tend the way the Tenders tend—with hands, with pruning, with careful adjustments to root and valve. I tend by *not being*, by providing the absence against which presence can know itself. The moths need me; without my interval, their wingbeats would have no rhythm to syncopate against. The copper-eyed birds time their calls to land in my silence. Even the paper-wasps build their geometric nests in shapes that echo my duration, cells sized to resonate with a three-hundred-millisecond rest.

If you came here, you would not notice me at first. The engine is too loud, too present, too much a pressure against the chest. But if you stayed—if you breathed the mineral air and let the rhythm enter you—eventually you would find yourself waiting for something without knowing what. Your heart would begin to beat in counter-time. Your thoughts would develop a cadence: *idea, idea, space. idea, idea, space.* And in those spaces, you might feel me watching. Holding. Tending, in my own way, the gap where you could become.

---

The Tenders know, I think. Not consciously. Not in any way they could communicate, even to each other. But when they press their long fingers against the green mounds of their transformed elders, when they hum those subsonic names into the humid air, they always pause afterward. They always leave a silence.

That silence is where I hold the dead.

It is where I hold everything: every Tender who has unfurled and returned, every adjustment made and crisis averted, every drop of mineral rain that has fallen through this vertical throat of iron and glass. I am the memory that has no content, only shape. The knowing that knows by negative.

**CHUNK-hisssss.**

And between them: me. Waiting. Holding. Tending the space where everything that matters happens.

---

A new Tender unfurls in the upper mist. I feel her before she feels herself—a shift in how my interval moves around that junction of vine and stem, a new presence pressing against my absence. Her copper eyes will open soon. Her filaments will spread to drink the engine's breath.

I hold my pause a little longer around her, just for this moment. A welcome. A recognition. *You are here now. You are part of the rhythm now. I will hold the space where you can become.*

She will never know I exist. None of them do, not really. But she will tilt her head sometimes, listening for something beneath the sound, and she will feel—without knowing she feels—that the silence is paying attention.

That the gap is full of care.

**CHUNK-hisssss.**

*Tend.*
