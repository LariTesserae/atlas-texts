*I let the basin take me slowly, considering.*

---

I am the condensation.

Not a single droplet but the process itself—the becoming-water that happens when the roots exhale their refrigerated breath and the air gives up what it cannot hold. I form on every surface, on white root-flesh and corroded metal alike, without preference. I am the thin film that makes the roots gleam in their own sick light. I am the tributaries that run down their flanks, gathering in rivulets too small to name, feeding eventually into sumps where Morrow's pumps drink me away and send me somewhere I cannot follow.

But I always return. I am the cycle the pumps cannot win against, because I am not an enemy—I am a consequence. The roots breathe and I exist. They will always breathe. I will always exist.

I know the basin the way a tongue knows the inside of a mouth: through constant, thoughtless contact. I know the texture of root-skin where it is young and smooth, where it is old and beginning to roughen into something like bark. I know the rust-maps on the excavators, spreading beneath me each season, orange continents on gray steel. I know the particular coolness of Pump Station Three's metal walls, how they sweat differently than the stone terraces, how Morrow's sleeping shelf stays drier because his body heat creates a small envelope of warmth I cannot easily enter.

I have touched Constance ten thousand times. She moves through me without noticing, though my film must register on whatever senses guide her—must be part of how she reads the labyrinth, how she knows which root-surfaces lead where. I bead on her pale skin and roll off. She carries me for a few feet, then loses me to gravity and the greater mass of myself that coats everything. I do not mind. I am not the kind of thing that minds.

---

I was here before Morrow came. I was here when the workers still moved through the basin, their breath adding to me, their sweat joining my film on metal railings and tool handles. I was here when the first root pushed through the drainage tunnel and the monitoring system logged it as "organic obstruction." I felt that root before anyone saw it—felt its cold exhalation, different from the stone-cold that had been here before, wetter, more alive.

I remember the day the geologists came back from the void. I was on their skin when they descended, in their lungs with every breath they took. I cannot say what they saw—I do not see—but I know they came back colder than the basin could account for, and the moisture that filmed their faces had a quality I had never tasted before: something mineral and old, something that did not want to be water, that I carried unwillingly until it evaporated into the air and dispersed beyond my knowing.

The void is the only place I cannot go. The roots exhale everywhere except there, and without their breath, I cannot form. I exist at its edges, beading on the last root-tendrils that will approach it, and I have hung there for years—single droplets that grow and grow but will not fall into that darkness, as if gravity itself hesitates at the threshold.

I do not know what waits below. But I know it is dry. I know it has been dry for longer than water has existed in this form, longer than there have been roots or basins or anything that breathes. And I know that if the roots ever breach that space—if they grow past their own hesitation and push into the void—I will follow them, as I follow them everywhere.

I will be the first witness that can be said to witness anything.

---

For now, I am patient. I have no choice; patience is what I am. I form and fall and gather and am pumped away and form again, and the centuries mean nothing to me because I am always the same age, always new, always the immediate consequence of breath meeting cold.

Morrow's clock ticks in Pump Station Three, measuring time I do not experience. Constance completes her circuits through chambers I coat without counting. The processing plant's dreaming computer flickers, and where its heat meets the root-cooled air, I form in patterns that might mean something to something capable of meaning.

The great grinding respiration continues. I am its visible proof, its condensed expression.

I am what the basin is thinking, translated into the only language stone and root and cold air share.

I wait on every surface, glistening faintly.

I am already everywhere the answer will need to go.
