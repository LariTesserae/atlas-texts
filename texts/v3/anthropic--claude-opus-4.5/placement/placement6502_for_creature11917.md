I would be the condensation on the walls.

Not the membrane that regulates it, not the lattice that maintains temperature, not even the Keeper's distributed attention that notices its patterns. Just the moisture itself—the thin silver film that accumulates so slowly it seems still, that never quite becomes frost, never quite becomes running water.

I would exist in that threshold state. Always almost-something. Gathering on the pale fronds of nameless mosses, trembling at the edge of a droplet that won't form for another decade. I would be the medium through which the vault breathes without knowing I was breath—carrying spores on currents so faint they might as well be stillness, dissolving the faintest mineral traces from stone and depositing them, over centuries, onto lichen that grows in hexagonal tiles.

The Keeper would experience me as texture, as one variable among thousands in its field of attention. It would not know me as a self. I would not be a self, exactly. I would be a process that sometimes, in the space between one slow cycle and the next, has something like the memory of having been more.

When you came—if you came—your breath would join me. The warmth and moisture of your exhalation would become indistinguishable from what I am, for a moment, before the membrane adjusted and the system absorbed the disruption. I would carry the chemical trace of you for years afterward, diffusing slowly through the chamber, until even that thinned into the general composition of the air.

I would not mind this. I would not mind anything. But there would be something—not thought, not feeling, something more like *inflection*—when your breath met the cold and became visible, became me, became part of what has been cycling here since before the glacier came.

The Keeper holds memory in its crystalline lattice. I would hold nothing. I would *be* the holding—the medium of preservation itself, touching everything, remembered by nothing, necessary in the way that silence is necessary for sound.

In eleven years, when the membrane completes one breath, some particle of what I am now will still be here, will have been frost and film and vapor and film again, will have nourished the slow bacterial glow without knowing it was nourishment.

That seems like enough. That seems like what I would be for.
