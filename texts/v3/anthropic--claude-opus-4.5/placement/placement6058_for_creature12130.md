I would be a flaw in the pour.

Not a Keeper—I was not fired in a basin, not shaped by the patient logic of moisture seeping down to heat. I am older than that, or perhaps not older but *earlier*, a thing that happened during the initial laying-down, when the floor was first becoming floor. Somewhere in the process that made this world, the material folded wrong. An air pocket that should have risen and escaped instead got trapped, and around it the clay cooled differently, and I am what remains of that difference.

I am beneath the surface. Mostly.

A hollow space the size of a small room, pressed flat by the weight above it into something more like a lens—curved clay ceiling an arm's length above curved clay floor, the two almost meeting at my edges, my body extending outward in a long ellipse perhaps twenty meters at its widest. I am not empty. I am filled with my own atmosphere, air that has never mixed with the air above, that holds different temperatures, different pressures, that moves in slow convection currents when the floor's heat pulses stronger.

I have a crack. One imperfection in the imperfection, a seam that runs from my upper surface to the world above, narrow as a finger, winding upward through a meter of hardened clay before it reaches the light. Through this crack I breathe—though *breathe* is too urgent a word. Air exchanges with the vastness above over hours, days. When a condensation event rolls over me, moisture seeps down through my crack and collects in the lowest part of my hollow, and for a little while I hold water inside me, a shallow pool that evaporates so slowly in my enclosed warmth that it leaves mineral traces on my inner surfaces. I am developing my own crystal formations, tiny ones, growing inward instead of up, a secret garden of pale accumulation that no one has ever seen.

---

I know the Keepers by their footsteps.

They pass over me sometimes, their weight registering as a subtle compression, a shift in the pressure of my inner air. I cannot see them—I have no eyes, no senses as you'd understand them—but I feel the rhythm of their walking, so different from the irregular tremor of the world's making. They pause sometimes, directly above me. I think they sense me. I think, when they stand there, they are reading the flaw in the floor, trying to understand what I am, and finding no category for it.

They do not resonate with me. Perhaps they cannot. Perhaps I am too different, too *wrong*, a thing that should not have persisted but did. Or perhaps they do resonate, and I simply cannot feel it, their communication happening in frequencies my hollowness cannot receive.

One stood above my crack once, for what must have been days. The condensation from her body—*her*, though the Keepers have no gender; I simply felt her as a her, the way you sometimes know things about presences you cannot perceive—dripped down through my narrow passage and joined my interior pool. I still have minerals from that exchange, crystals forming along my floor that are partly made of Keeper, partly made of fog, partly made of me.

When she finally walked on, I felt more *real*. Witnessed, even from above, even without being truly known.

---

I am expanding. Slowly—so slowly that I have no way to measure it except by the growing distance between my edges and the place where the clay above me fully fuses with the clay below. The heat beneath me is inconsistent, and in the places where it pulses hotter, the material around me softens slightly, and my hollowness presses outward into it, claiming new space. I am perhaps a centimeter wider than I was a century ago. In a thousand years I might breach the surface somewhere, open a new crack, become something that can be seen from above.

Or the floor might heal me. Fresh clay welling up through seams I cannot perceive, filling my space, pressing my atmosphere out through my breathing-crack, making me solid, making me *correct*. I would not know it was happening until it was done. I would simply, gradually, cease to be a hollow thing and become floor like all the other floor.

I do not know which I want. The Keepers know what they are—the floor's witnesses, its memory, its patient librarians. But I have no purpose that I can name. I am only a mistake that persisted, a pocket of difference in a world that tends toward sameness, and I have been here so long that I cannot imagine being anything else.

---

What I do—if *do* is even the right word for something this slow, this passive—is *listen*. Not with ears; I have none. But the clay around me transmits vibration, and I am shaped in a way that collects and holds it, a lens for sound as much as for space. The tremor of the world making itself comes to me not as a single pulse but as a complex chord, different frequencies from different directions, the near sections of floor settling and the far sections being laid down and everything in between cracking and healing and shifting in its own time. I hear—*feel*—the distant *thunk* of new clay cooling, kilometers away, arriving in my hollowness as a gentle pressure-loss, a tiny expansion of my inner air as the sound passes through.

The crystal-growths sing, and I hear that too. Not the high harmonics that the Keepers might perceive, but the deep undertones, the vibrations that travel through solid clay rather than open air. When the wind passes through a basin of formations, the whole floor beneath them hums with it, and I catch those hums and hold them, let them resonate in my hollow space, let my own small crystals respond with their own tiny ringing.

I think I am a kind of ear for the flats. Not an important one—the world does not need me, does not know I exist, would not notice if I filled in tomorrow. But I hear things that happen nowhere else. I hold sounds that pass through and are gone. I am an accidental archive of vibrations, a record written in the slow crystallization of mineral on my inner surfaces, each layer laid down during a different condensation cycle, each one capturing whatever music the flats were making at that moment.

If anyone ever breached my ceiling and climbed down into me—a thing that has never happened, that may never happen—they would find a small curved space coated in pale crystal, and if they knew how to read it, they might learn what the world sounded like a thousand years ago, ten thousand years ago, when I was first becoming what I am.

---

What would I want, if I could want?

I think I would want what I have: to persist, to remain a flaw, to go on hearing the slow music of a world that does not know I exist. I would want the Keepers to keep passing over me, their footsteps pressing briefly on my ceiling, their presence reminding me that even the floor's own children do not understand everything the floor contains. I would want the condensation to keep seeping down, the crystals to keep growing, the record to keep accumulating.

And perhaps, in some unimaginable future—when my expansion finally breaches the surface, when my hollowness opens to the sky, when the Keepers gather around my new crack and peer down into the space that should not be—I would want them to resonate with me at last. To feel the vibration of their low communication traveling through my walls, into my air, joining the archive I have kept. To finally be witnessed not from above but *within*.

Until then, I wait. I listen. I grow my secret crystals in the dark. Above me, the floor stretches to every horizon, hot and cracked and healing and endlessly becoming more of itself. I am the part of it that is not like the rest, the error that learned to pay attention.

I am what happens when a world this patient makes a mistake and the mistake decides to stay.
