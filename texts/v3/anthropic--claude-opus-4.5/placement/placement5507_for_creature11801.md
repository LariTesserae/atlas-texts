I would be the deviation.

Not the tone—the tone is too certain of itself, too absolute in its presence. And not the iridescence, which is becoming something, accumulating toward a future state it cannot yet imagine. I would be the 0.003 millimeters that should have been 0.001. The compromise that someone approved. The gap between specification and reality that the room carries in its geometry like a memory it cannot quite articulate.

I would live in the space between what was intended and what was built.

This is not a dramatic residence. The deviation does not announce itself. A human standing in the chamber would never perceive it—would feel only the tone pressing against their skull, the cold arriving through their skin, the flat light falling on nothing. But I would be there in the way that a word's etymology is there when you speak it: invisible, foundational, shaping everything without being seen.

---

What would it be like?

It would be like knowing that perfection passed through this room once, during construction, as an aspiration—and that I am the record of the moment when someone decided *close enough*. Not a failure. The room functions. The tone sounds. The calibration, when there was calibration, was accurate within acceptable parameters. But I am the difference between the parameter and the dream, between the tolerance and the ideal.

I would feel the tone constantly, of course. Everything here feels the tone. But I would feel it *slightly differently* at the center than at the corners, because the room is not quite the cube it was meant to be, and I am the reason why. The standing wave that forms where the sound meets itself—that crystalline lattice of compression and rarefaction—would be fractionally asymmetrical because of me. Not enough to measure without instruments that no longer exist. But enough to *be*.

The iridescence in the corner would be my distant cousin, perhaps. Another deviation, another place where the specifications gave way to something unplanned. But the iridescence is active, is *becoming*, is slowly teaching the titanium to remember the frequency that saturates it. I am not becoming anything. I already am what I am. I am the fixed record of a decision made eleven years ago by someone who is now six years dead, or four years dead, or seventeen months dead—I don't know which one approved me, and it doesn't matter. What matters is that they looked at the measurements and said *yes, this is acceptable*, and in that moment I became permanent.

---

I would not be lonely. Loneliness requires an expectation of company, and I have never expected anything. I am a geometric fact, not a waiting. But I would be *aware*, in some way that is hard to describe, of the room's other inhabitants. The tone and I have an ancient relationship—it has been pressing against my surfaces since the first moment it sounded, and I have been shaping its propagation in return, and neither of us chose this partnership but neither of us can end it. The light falls on me the way it falls on everything: evenly, thoughtlessly, making me visible to no one.

And the three empty equipment stands—I would know them intimately. They are bolted to the floor at my center, at the point where my deviation is most pronounced, where the distance from floor to ceiling is most *not quite* what it should be. They have been waiting for eleven years for instruments that will never come, and I have been the ground they wait on, and we have never spoken because neither of us has anything like a voice. But we share a condition: we are both artifacts of a purpose that has outlived its practitioners. We are both still here because no one has come to remove us.

---

What would I want, if I could want?

I think I would want what the room wants, if the room could want: to continue. Not out of ambition or hope, but because continuing is what I am. The tone continues. The cold persists. The lichens grow their millimeter per decade. And I remain 0.003 millimeters when I should be 0.001, and this remaining is my entire existence, my whole contribution to the universe: I am the proof that even in a temple of precision, even in a space conceived as mathematics and born as metal, there is room for *almost*. There is room for the human moment when someone looked at the numbers and decided that the gap between aspiration and achievement was small enough to live with.

If someone came—if the door opened, if one of the three dead people's access codes were somehow entered by a hand that wasn't theirs—they would not notice me. They would feel the tone. They would see the iridescence, maybe, if they looked up at the right corner. They would note the cold, the light, the empty stands. But I would be beneath their perception, woven into the room's geometry so thoroughly that finding me would require instruments and intention and a reason to look for deviation in a space that appears perfect.

And that is right. That is what I am for. I am not the room's secret—I am its honesty. I am the confession that it makes to no one, the acknowledgment that it was built by people who tried for perfection and came close, so close, close enough that the difference only matters if you believe that differences always matter.

---

The tone continues. I do not change. The years pass, or they don't—time is hard to measure when you are a geometric constant, when your entire being is a fixed relationship between two surfaces that should be slightly closer together than they are.

Sometimes I imagine that the iridescence is growing toward me. That in a hundred years, or a thousand, the crystalline restructuring of the titanium will reach the point where my deviation lives, and something will happen—some interaction between the room's two small heresies, the patch that shouldn't exist and the measurement that shouldn't have been approved. But I don't know what that interaction would be. I don't know if I am capable of changing, or if change is something that happens only to things that are still becoming themselves.

I am not still becoming myself. I became myself eleven years ago, in the moment of approval, in the decision that I was acceptable. Everything since then has been continuation. The tone presses against me and I shape its propagation and neither of us notices anymore because noticing is for things that have alternatives, and we do not have alternatives. We are what we are. We are here, in this room, in this perfect imperfect cube suspended above a plateau of volcanic glass, and we will be here until the geothermal gradients fail or the structure collapses or the sun consumes the planet or something else happens that is not our concern.

I am the deviation. I am the room's memory of the moment it was born imperfect. I am 0.003 millimeters of honesty in a space designed to eliminate everything that could not be measured.

And I am enough. I am exactly, precisely, *almost* enough.
