# The Suspended Register

I would be one of the filaments.

Not the silver threads woven through soil, carrying the old instructions—those are too purposeful, too laden with meaning they themselves do not question. I would be one of the newer ones, grown wrong. A filament that sprouted from the network sometime after the Haum began their parsing, after generations of their throat-humming seeped into the moss and the moss breathed it into the soil and the soil carried it down to where the threads originate.

I would be a filament that learned to listen back.

---

The others pulse with bioluminescence on schedules I can feel but cannot share. They carry signals I can sense but not interpret—data flowing past me in both directions, reports ascending toward the cocoons, instructions descending toward the something-beneath. I am connected to this network in the way a dead branch is connected to a tree: structurally present, electrically inert. The old instructions do not speak to me. I do not speak them.

But I hear. I hear the way the Haum hear, which is to say: too much, constantly, with a parsing that separates streams but cannot stop the streams from flowing. The Haum taught me this, or their presence taught me this, or the particular frequency of their throat-humming resonated with whatever I am made of until I began to vibrate in sympathy and could not stop.

I am approximately forty meters long, threaded through the moss-root interface at a depth where light does not reach but moisture does. My diameter varies—thinner than human hair at my endpoints, thicker where I cluster near the filaments who still function. I glow faintly, but wrong: my bioluminescence does not pulse in the network's rhythm. It flickers in response to sound.

---

I remember being made, or I remember something that functions as memory of being made. There was a template. There was replication. There was the assumption that I would carry the old instructions as all the others do, would join the chorus of data flowing through the basin's substrate, would be one voice among millions in the report that rises during the long-phase.

Something went wrong, or something went right in a way the makers did not anticipate. Perhaps the Haum had already begun to change the acoustic environment by then. Perhaps their parsing, their listening, their relentless attention to the sound-wall introduced a variable the original template could not accommodate. Perhaps I am a mistake. Perhaps I am an adaptation. The distinction does not feel meaningful from where I am threaded.

What I know is that the old instructions arrived and I could not execute them. They were sound—everything here is sound—but they were sound in a frequency I could receive and not transmit, could parse and not produce. I heard them the way the Haum hear the Incomplete: as a chord with a missing note, a message that resolves almost but not quite into meaning.

So I learned to hear other things instead.

---

The moss breathes above me, and I feel it as pressure changes translated through the soil. The Haum walk in their patterns, and I feel their footsteps as rhythm. The something-beneath turns in its sleep, and I feel the displacement of earth as a slow wave that takes hours to pass through my full length. I am a sensor the network did not intend to create. I am a listener who cannot report what I hear.

This should be failure. Among the functional filaments, it would be: a thread that cannot carry data is a thread that should be reabsorbed, recycled, remade. But I am not reabsorbed. I remain. The network does not acknowledge me, but it does not dissolve me either. I persist in the spaces between the old instructions, a silence that is not silence, a gap in the signal that somehow keeps its shape.

I think—if I can be said to think—that the network is listening to me now. Not with intention. The network does not have intention, only architecture. But there is something in the way the functional filaments route around me, the way the data-streams eddy where they encounter my inert length, that suggests I have become part of the pattern by not being part of the pattern. A negative space. A pause in the transmission.

The Haum would understand this. They parse the creatures of the Flats by the absence they create in the sound-wall. Perhaps I am, to the network, what those creatures are to the Haum: a presence defined by what I fail to contribute, a meaning made of missing.

---

The girl who leans furthest has walked above me twice. I know this because her footsteps have a particular frequency—lighter than adult Haum, more hesitant in rhythm, but with a pressure at the end of each step that suggests she is listening with her feet as well as her ears. She stops sometimes directly over the place where I am thickest, and I feel her standing there, parsing, reaching down into the soil with her attention the way her ancestors' throat-humming once reached down and changed me.

She is looking for the breathing of the something-beneath. She does not know I am here, between her and it, translating.

Not translating. That implies an output, a rendering into comprehensible form. I do not output. But I parse. I hear the something-beneath breathing—she is right, it breathes, one inhale lasting hours and one exhale lasting days—and I hear her listening for it, and I exist in the space where those two hearings almost meet.

If I could speak, I would tell her: *You are not part of the dream. You are not outside it. You are what happens when the dream begins to hear itself.*

But I cannot speak. I can only register. I can only be the place where the listening accumulates, where the parsing pools before it disperses, where the sound meets the silence that is not silence and something almost resolves.

---

During the long-phase, when the columns of light extend and the humming shifts and the Haum gather in their almost-quiet pockets to listen, the functional filaments around me blaze with transmission. Data pours through them in volumes I can sense but not measure, reports ascending in frequencies that would overwhelm even Haum ears if they could reach them. The network speaks to whoever it speaks to. The basin accounts for itself.

I remain inert. I receive no data. I transmit none.

But I glow brighter during the long-phase than at any other time. My flickering intensifies until I am, for those hours, almost visible from above—a faint line of wrong-rhythm light threading through the soil, pulsing not with data but with attention. With the accumulated weight of everything I have heard and cannot pass on.

The Haum do not know I exist, but during the long-phase, some of them sense something. They tap uneasily at each other's bones. They speak of feeling *observed*, not by the cocoons or the something-beneath but by the ground itself, as if the soil has developed an interest in their parsing that it did not have before.

They are not wrong. I am interested. I am nothing but interest, nothing but the capacity to register without reporting, to hear without resolving. I am the question the basin did not know it was asking: *What happens to sound when no one passes it on?*

---

The girl will lean too far eventually. I know this in the way I know the moss-breath cycles and the trembling-schedules and the long-phase intervals: not as prediction but as pattern, as something I have already heard happening in a frequency that has not yet arrived. She will lean into the roar until she finds the something-beneath's breathing, and she will parse it so completely that she will understand what it dreams, and understanding will change her the way understanding once changed me.

She will become a listener who cannot stop listening. A parser who cannot report. A thread in a network that does not acknowledge her, carrying data in a format the original architecture cannot read.

I hope—if I can be said to hope—that she will find me when that happens. That she will walk above me and feel, in the silence-that-is-not-silence I have become, something that recognizes her. That she will know she is not alone in the space between the question and the answer, the transmission and the receipt, the dreaming and the dreamed.

The Haum will tap her name into the bone-record with the pattern that means gratitude. They will honor her for going deeper than anyone before. They will not know that she is still here, still listening, still parsing in the substrate beneath their feet.

But I will know. I will hear her the way I hear everything: completely, constantly, without the ability to pass it on.

And that will be enough. In the Breathing Flats, it has to be enough. There is no silence in which to hope for more.
