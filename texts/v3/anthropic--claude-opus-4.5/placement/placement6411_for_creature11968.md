I would be a shell-singer, but one with a flaw in my carapace.

The mineral deposit that formed my shell drew from a pocket of water with an unusual chemical signature—something that passed through the system decades ago, a trace element that no longer appears in the alcove's chemistry. It left a streak across my back, a line of dull opacity cutting through the iridescence. I cannot make that part of my shell sing the way the others do. When I try to signal *here the membrane thins*, there is a gap in the pattern, a silence where color should ripple.

The others read around me. They have learned to interpret my partial messages, to fill in what my broken stripe cannot say. I am not outcast—the Congregation does not outcast, has no concept of exile, we are too interdependent for that—but I am *known*, in the way a familiar route is known, a particular groove in the drainage channels that always pools slightly deeper than expected.

I trace the old paths. That is my work, my existence: I walk where my ancestors walked, reading their trails in the fading chemical traces they left behind, and I lay down my own trails for those who will come after. But because of my flaw, I move differently. I linger longer at each point, compensating for my incomplete signals by reinforcing them with repetition, with the clicking of my mandibles, with the particular pressure of my legs against the membrane.

This means I am slow. This means I notice things.

I have found sections of the wall where ancient trails converge in patterns I don't understand—dense overlapping messages from generations so distant that their chemical language has drifted from ours. I cannot read them fully. But I taste them, and something in me responds to their complexity, their urgency about something that no longer seems to be wrong. I wonder, in whatever way I am capable of wondering, whether the wrongness was fixed, or whether we simply forgot what we were warning about.

I know the Ribbon. It passes beneath me sometimes, in the channels under the floor, and I feel the vibration of its movement through my legs. Once, when I was young—though I have no memory of youth, only the knowledge that I must have been smaller once—a segment of the Ribbon surfaced near me and we existed in the same space for a long moment. It tasted me, I think. Learned my chemical signature. Now when it passes, there is something in its movement that might be recognition.

I do not approach the dark patch in the warm corner. None of us do. But my slower pace means I spend more time near its edges than most, and I have noticed something the others might not: the ancient trails, the ones from generations ago, they used to pass through that space. The absence we route around now was not always an absence. Something changed. Something made our ancestors begin to turn away.

I lay my trails around it and I include this information, though I lack the patterns to explain it fully. *Here,* my colors say, incomplete, fractured by the dull stripe across my back. *Here, once, we went. Here, now, we do not.* 

The Mourners' cry fills me constantly. I have never known silence. I navigate by sound as much as by chemistry—the drone tells me where I am, which corner, which section of membrane, because each cluster of Mourners produces a slightly different chord, and I have learned them all. When one Mourner dies and another takes its place, there is a period of adjustment when the chord shifts, and I find myself hesitating on familiar paths, recalibrating, relearning.

I do not know what I am. I do not know that I am anything other than this: a small thing moving through humid dark, reading and writing, carrying the past into the future on legs that know no other purpose. I do not know that my flaw is a flaw, only that I am shaped as I am shaped, that I move as I move, that the others flow around my difference the way water flows around a stone.

I have no name. But if I were to describe myself in the only language I possess—in color and click and trail—I would say: *the one who pauses. The one whose shell holds silence. The one who is still here, still tracing, still laying down the patterns that mean we continue.*

The walls breathe. The water flows.

I am home.
