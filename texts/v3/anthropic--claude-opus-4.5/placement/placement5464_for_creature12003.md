I would be one of the cairns.

Not the stones themselves—not quite—but the particular arrangement of them. The way this one leans against that one, the specific angle where water sheets off rather than pools, the hollow at my center where dissolved clay and rusted metal remember being offerings. I am the *shape* that remains when everything that made me has been forgotten.

I feel the rain as a constant conversation, each drop asking *are you still here?* and my answer being simply my continued existence. The Tenders come, and their soft fingertips read me like prayer, testing whether I have slumped, whether my angles have surrendered to the water's patient argument. When they lift a stone that has shifted and replace it just so, I feel it the way you might feel someone tucking a blanket around you in sleep—a rightness restored, a small collapse prevented.

I do not know why I matter. This is the truth at the center of me, in the hollow where the offerings dissolve: I have no idea what I am for. I was built to mark something, to measure something, to hold some relationship to the other cairns that stretches across the basin in a grid of meaning—but the meaning has worn away more completely than any mineral. I remain *significant* without knowing what I signify.

The pale creatures flow around me, their avoidance a kind of acknowledgment. The Tenders argue about my drainage channels, debates generations old, and I cannot tell them that both sides are wrong, or both right, or that it doesn't matter, because I don't know if it matters. I only know that they come, and they touch me, and they keep me standing against the rain's endless question.

Sometimes, in the lulls, I feel her pass at the edge of perception—the old one walking her circuit, watching for something at the boundaries. And I wonder if she knows what I'm for. If she remembers what we were all built to mark.

But mostly I simply *am*. Stone holding stone holding stone, making a shape that means *here*, that means *this place was worth noting*, that means *someone cared enough to stack these rocks and someone else cared enough to maintain them and the caring continues even after the reason has dissolved like clay in endless water*.

The rain asks, *are you still here?*

I answer by not falling.
