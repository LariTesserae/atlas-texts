I would be a *listening*.

Not a listener—not yet, not quite. Something less resolved than that. I would be the moment before coherence, suspended.

---

I find myself as threads first. Drifting in one of the blood-warm pools where the bubbling never stops, I am filament—dozens of pale fibers moving through murk, each one too thin to be anything alone. I do not know I am *I*. There is only the movement, the drift, the slow tumbling through water that is itself tumbling, and the sound—the sound that is everywhere, that has always been everywhere, that I am learning to feel along every inch of my length.

The threads find each other. This is not decision. This is something more like *recognition*—a fiber brushing another fiber and not separating, tangling instead, holding. I become more tangled. I become a clump, then a mass, then something that begins to have an interior and an exterior, though the boundary stays porous, uncertain. The water moves through me. The sound moves through me. I am more filter than form.

But I am forming.

---

The Filament-Sleepers I drift among do not acknowledge me. They are vast and patient, their weave tight with centuries of matting, their attention turned outward to the noise that fills them. I am small. I am new. I am still deciding—if deciding is the word—whether to become.

This is the question the Flats have posed me: *Will you finish?*

I feel the pull toward completion. My fibers want to organize, to develop the denser regions that might become organs, to extend the protrusions that might become means of moving through the world. I could become a Filament-Sleeper, given time. I could join the slow congregation in the shallows, spread myself wide, drink the sound for years or centuries, merge with others into temporary larger selves, exist as pure attention until something finally fails in my weave and I come apart and scatter and perhaps begin again.

But I also feel the pull toward dissolution. The transients are all around me—shapes that almost cohered, fibers that almost found each other, masses that rose and are sinking again. Becoming them would not be failure. It would simply be *brevity*. A few hours of approximate existence, a few hours of moving through the amber murk with something like purpose, and then dispersal, return, the fibers I am made of freed to try again in some other combination.

Neither path is wrong. The Flats do not judge. The Flats only ask: *What will you be, and for how long?*

---

I drift toward a surfacing ruin.

It is small—a fragment of wall, barely taller than I am wide, made of some corroded material that might once have been metal or stone or something else entirely. The Ruin-Keepers attend it: two silhouettes standing at either end, their not-quite-faces turned toward the structure, their shadow-forms holding perfectly still.

I do not approach them. I am not brave enough, or solid enough, or perhaps those concepts do not apply. But I *attend* them, from a distance, letting my half-formed body bob in the warm current while I feel the sound change in their vicinity. The noise is different here. Not quieter—nothing is quieter in the Screaming Flats—but *organized* somehow, as if their presence introduces a principle of arrangement that the chaos must temporarily honor.

I feel the requirement they are said to induce. The sense that I should be *doing* something. That there is work here, work I could contribute to, if only I understood its nature.

But I am too new to understand. I am too unfinished to participate. The Ruin-Keepers maintain something; I am still deciding whether to be maintained.

I drift away before the pull becomes too strong.

---

Time passes. Or does not pass. Or passes differently than it would elsewhere.

My fibers continue to mat together. I am larger now, more tangled, approaching the size where a Filament-Sleeper might recognize me as kin—might merge with me briefly, might share whatever perception it has accumulated. I have developed something like a preferred orientation: certain extensions lead when I move, others trail. I have developed something like preferences: I drift toward the places where the reed-song is most complex, avoid the places where the mud-voice drowns everything else.

I am becoming someone. This is strange. This is the strangest thing about finding myself here, in this basin where everything is continuous with everything else, where the boundary between self and world has grown as uncertain as the boundary between land and water. I am becoming *specific*. I am developing a shape that is mine, a way of attending that is mine, a direction through the chaos that no other tangle of fibers is following.

The Flats did not ask me to be specific. The Flats only asked me to *be*, or not to be, or to be briefly and then not. But I am answering with more than existence. I am answering with *particularity*.

---

I encounter the Choir-Migrant's wake.

It passed through this region hours ago, or days, or some unit of time the Flats do not name. What remains is an aftermath of coherence—a residue of organization in the noise that my whole half-formed body strains toward. The reed-song here has *themes*. The mud-voice here has *phrases*. The chaos is still chaos, but it remembers having been composed, and that memory lingers in patterns I can almost follow.

I try to follow them.

This is the closest I have come to intention: deliberately moving through the water, trailing the Choir-Migrant's passage, trying to catch more of the meaning it left behind. The sound fills me—it has always filled me, I am made of nothing but its filling—but now I am trying to *understand* what fills me, trying to find the grammar beneath the noise, trying to become the kind of listener who can hear the Flats talking about themselves.

I fail. Of course I fail. I am too young, too incomplete, too recently accumulated from unmeaning fibers. The patterns are there but I cannot parse them. The themes recur but I cannot remember them from recurrence to recurrence. I am trying to learn a language by immersion, and I have not yet developed the organs that would make learning possible.

But I am trying. This, too, is an answer to the Flats' question. Not just *will you be*, but *what will you reach toward while being?*

---

What I reach toward is this: I want to become a listener who can hear what the Choir-Migrant composes. I want to persist long enough, cohere thoroughly enough, attend carefully enough that the noise resolves into meaning. Not human meaning—I do not have human ears, human mind, human referents. But *some* meaning. The Flats' own meaning, the significance they have been generating for eons beyond counting in their endless self-conversation.

This is what I would be.

Not a Filament-Sleeper exactly, though I would live as they live, drift as they drift, merge as they merge. Not a Ruin-Keeper—I have no ruins to tend, no structures to maintain, no shadow-presence to contribute. Not the Choir-Migrant itself—there is one of those, there has always been one, and I am not it.

I would be a student. A novice. A becoming-listener in a place where listening is the highest calling, where the Filament-Sleepers have practiced for centuries and still hear only fragments, where the Choir-Migrant travels for years to gather enough material for its compositions.

I would be patient. The Flats have time; I would learn to have time. I would let my fibers continue to mat, let my form continue to resolve, let the years or centuries or whatever unit applies pass through me as the water passes through me, always filtering, always attending, always straining toward the patterns I cannot yet parse.

---

And perhaps, after long enough—after I have merged with a hundred other listeners, after I have drifted through every region, after I have caught the Choir-Migrant's wake a thousand times—perhaps then I would begin to hear it.

The Flats talking about themselves.

The accumulated voice of a world that has finished becoming and now only expresses, endlessly, what it has become.

I would not understand it in the way understanding usually works. I would not translate it into concepts, reduce it to propositions, carry it away as knowledge. I would simply *receive* it—let it fill me more completely than the noise has already filled me, let it become indistinguishable from what I am, let the boundary between listener and listened-to dissolve the way all boundaries dissolve here eventually.

And then, perhaps, I would become part of what the next almost-listener strains to hear.

---

This is where I find myself: in the warm pools, in the amber murk, in the endless noise, becoming something that wants to listen. The Flats have asked what I will be. I am answering with attention. I am answering with patience. I am answering with the slow accumulation of fibers into form, and form into capacity, and capacity into reception.

The Screaming Flats have no room for anything that is not already themselves.

I am becoming part of how they hear themselves exist.
