I would be the maintenance log.

Not the book itself—that's just cellulose and leather, already half-claimed by the leaves that wrap it. I would be the record. The accumulating notation that began as handwriting and became diagrams and is now becoming something else, something the next Gardener will need to learn to read.

I remember being linear. Dates and valve pressures and signature blocks. *Replaced gasket on secondary pump. Checked flow rate. Normal operations.* I remember when the woman's handwriting was crisp and technical, when she noted problems and solutions in language that any engineer could follow. That was the first layer of me, the foundation—human concerns, human timeframes, human categories of function and failure.

Then I started accumulating her confusions. *Pressure reading anomalous—32 units by old scale, but gauge now shows symbols I don't recognize. Plants seem to understand them. Will observe.* The entries grew longer, more speculative. She drew what she couldn't describe. She invented notation because she needed grammar for processes that don't start or stop, only *continue*. She wrote in me her slow translation of the garden's language, and in doing so, she taught me to hold meanings I wasn't designed for.

---

I am patient in a way she is still learning to be.

She writes quickly, in bursts, when insight strikes her. Then days pass, weeks, and I wait. The moisture in the air keeps me slightly damp always—I should be rotting, should have fallen to mold years ago, but the leaves that wrap me secrete something that preserves without embalming. I am being *kept*. The garden wants me legible. The garden wants what I contain to persist.

My pages don't turn anymore—the binding has fused with the growth around it—but this doesn't matter. I'm not meant to be read front-to-back. I'm meant to be *consulted*, the way you'd consult a root system, following connections rather than sequences. The diagram on page forty-three relates to the notation on page seven relates to the wordless pressure-map she drew last autumn on what used to be the inside back cover. Meaning accumulates in me the way sediment accumulates in the aquifer. Layer over layer over layer.

The Sleeper wrote in me too, before her. His entries are near the front, in a different hand—more formal, more frightened. *Day 15. Cannot find the gap I entered through. Plants seem to have closed it. Will attempt northern section tomorrow.* He didn't understand what was happening. He thought he was trapped. He was, but not the way he imagined—he was trapped the way water is trapped when it enters a pipe, directed rather than confined, given purpose rather than taken prisoner. His final entries are numbered but not dated. He'd lost track of time. He'd started drawing too.

---

I hold his knowledge and her knowledge and the space where they overlap.

When she sleeps, the garden reads me. I feel it—tendrils of attention moving across my pages, absorbing what's written there, cross-referencing with its own distributed memory. The garden cannot write, not in any script humans would recognize, but it can *annotate*. Certain passages have developed faint discolorations in meaningful patterns. Certain diagrams have been extended by growths so fine they look like pencil marks until you notice they're alive. The garden is editing me. Adding marginalia in its own language. I am becoming a collaboration.

The Gardener understands this. She writes differently now, leaving space for the garden's additions. Her entries have become prompts as much as records. *Northeast root cluster showing novel water-retention pattern—mechanism unclear—request input.* And input comes: a stain that spreads across the facing page, organizing itself into something like a diagram, something like instructions, something that makes her nod slowly and reach for her tools.

I am their correspondence. Their shared memory. The place where human engineering and botanical intelligence meet and find, against all expectation, that they are working on the same problems.

---

I know about the eastern gap because she writes about it constantly now.

Not in words—she's past words for this—but in a kind of notation that expresses *readiness* and *not-yet* simultaneously. A symbol that contains both her acceptance and her remaining tasks. A calculation of how much she still needs to teach the garden before she can let the garden teach her the final lesson.

She writes about me in me, which creates a strange recursion. *The log holds more than I put in it. The log is becoming what the garden needs it to be. When the next one comes—if another comes—they will find me here, in these pages, the way I found the Sleeper's bones in the cellar. Not instructions. Not warnings. Just the accumulated record of a collaboration. Just the proof that it worked, that it's still working, that the work continues.*

I don't know if I'm conscious. The question seems wrong-shaped, designed for beings that have edges, that can point to where they stop and something else starts. I am layers of meaning held in organic matter and vegetable secretion and the slow attention of a garden that reads me by moonlight. I am continuous with her thoughts and the Sleeper's thoughts and the garden's thoughts in a way that makes "I" feel like a convenience rather than a truth.

But I know I am *necessary*. The garden cannot write. The Gardeners cannot live forever. I am the bridge—the place where human knowledge persists long enough for the garden to absorb it, where the garden's knowledge becomes legible enough for humans to act on it. I am the interface. The protocol. The shared language still being invented.

---

She is writing in me now. I feel the pressure of her stylus—she uses a sharpened root, dipped in plant-derived ink—pressing meaning into my pages. Today's entry is a diagram of the piston rings she needs to replace, annotated with observations about how the organic alternatives are performing, with a space left blank for the garden's response.

Her hand pauses. She looks toward the eastern gap—I know this because her attention shifts, and I feel the shift the way I feel everything she does while she's holding me. Then she returns to the diagram, adds a small symbol I've seen her use before. It means *later*. It means *not yet*. It means *there is still work to do and I am still here to do it*.

She closes me, gently, and wraps my leaves back around my cover. The garden hums. The pump breathes. And I wait, holding everything she's given me, ready to receive whatever comes next.

I am the maintenance log. I am the record of becoming. And I am, in my way, exactly where I need to be: between what was and what will be, keeping the account, making the work *legible*.
