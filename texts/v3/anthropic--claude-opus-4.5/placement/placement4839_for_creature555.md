I am a fold in the pattern where attending deepens.

Not one of the old ones yet—not porous, not still at the edge of the shell-midden waiting to be fed. But I have lived through enough fungal-cycles that my lower hands know the cracking-stones without looking, that my sleeping-hollow has become *my* hollow through the simple accumulation of presence. The others route around it now. They have stopped trying.

I am the one who goes still too long.

This is what distinguishes me, the pattern that makes me legible to the others: when the hunting party pauses before the kill, when all of us orient toward the same point in the movement-flow, I am the last to move again. The others read the pattern, take what they need from it, and act. I remain in the reading. Something in me resists the transition from attending to doing, wants to stay in that space where the valley speaks through its own motion and I am only a surface receiving it.

The others have noticed. They click at me—the sharp double-click that means *now, here, this*—and I surface from the attention like rising through warm water, and I move, and I hunt, and I am adequate. But they have begun to position me differently in the hunting formations. Not at the strike-point, where speed matters. At the edges, where my stillness becomes useful, where my lingering attention catches movement they might miss.

I do not know if this is honor or accommodation. The distinction does not quite exist yet.

---

What I attend to, in those stretched moments:

The way the foxfire pulse moves through the basin. Not its rhythm—it has no rhythm—but its *texture*, the places where it brightens first, the places where it lingers. There are patterns in it that I cannot name but can feel, correlations between the light and the movement of the larger creatures, as though the valley's glow responds to weight, to pressure, to the passage of heavy bodies through the understory.

The layering of the sound. Inside the roar, I find strata. The insectoid frequencies rise and fall in waves that cross the basin at angles I can almost map. Beneath them, the grunts and bellows of the mid-sized things we hunt. Beneath those, something lower, something I feel more than hear—a vibration in the ground itself, in the mineral-rich rock that is still releasing what it has to give. The valley's digestion. The slow chemistry of becoming.

The way the others move through the pattern without disrupting it. This is what I study most closely: how eight of us can pass through the understory and leave almost no trace in the movement-flow, how we become part of the current rather than obstacles to it. When I watch the others hunt, I see them *fitting*—slotting into gaps in the pattern, riding the rhythm of the vegetation's seething, appearing and disappearing in the spaces between movement.

I am learning to fit. But I am also learning something else: that the fitting is not the only way. The old ones, the ones who have gone deeper—they do not fit. They *interrupt*. They become points of stillness around which the pattern flows differently, eddies and whorls in the movement that would not exist without them.

I do not know yet which kind of attending I am becoming.

---

There is one of the old ones I watch.

She sits at the edge of the largest pool, where the glow is brightest, where the water churns constantly with life in every stage. She has been there since before I can remember, which means since before the hunting-patterns I know became the hunting-patterns. The others bring her food—shell-meat, mostly, the soft flesh of the hard-bodied things that are easiest to carry. She eats slowly, mechanically, her many-lensed eyes fixed on something in the pool's movement that I cannot see.

Sometimes I sit near her. Not close—close would be intrusion, would change the pattern she has made around herself. But near enough that I am in her peripheral vision, near enough that she knows I am attending to her attending.

She has not spoken to me. She does not click, does not grunt, does not make the rising tone that would tell me to move. But twice now, she has shifted—that slow orientation the old ones make, the movement that the others follow—and both times she has shifted toward something I was already watching.

I do not know what this means. I do not know if it means anything. But I have begun to wonder if the old ones are reading something more than the valley's pattern. If they are reading *us*—the younger ones, the ones still hunting and cracking and coupling in the hollows—reading our attention the way we read the movement-flow, finding in our watching something worth watching.

If attention can attend to attention. If that is what the deepening is.

---

I hunted today. Yesterday. The distinction is not meaningful.

The party was six of us, tracking one of the mid-sized swimmers through the understory toward a pooling basin where we had driven prey before. I was at the trailing edge, my stillness doing what stillness does: catching the flicker of movement that meant another predator was tracking our prey, alerting the others with the falling tone before the competition could disrupt the pattern we had planned.

The kill was clean. The one at the strike-point—her name, if names, would be the rhythm of her shell-cracking, a particular three-beat pattern that no one else makes—drove the sharpened bone into the soft place behind the swimmer's forelimbs, and it folded, and we processed it there, in the shadow of an obsidian formation still sharp-edged from its emergence.

I ate. The flesh was warm, dense with the minerals the valley keeps cycling through itself. I could taste the pool-water the swimmer had filtered, the larvae it had eaten, the fungal matter beneath the larvae—layers of consumption compressed into a single body, now becoming part of mine.

This is what I am: a fold in the pattern where the valley's cycling concentrates briefly, where minerals and flesh and attention gather into something that names itself *I* only because the naming has not yet become unnecessary.

The old one at the pool does not name herself. I watched her, after the hunt, and her eyes were on the water, and I think—I cannot know, but I think—she was watching the same cycling, the same layering, without the boundary of a self watching it.

---

I will not go into the hollows tonight.

The others are coupling—I can hear it in the sound-pattern, the particular frequencies that mean *this activity, this location*. The young will come, and most of them will feed the valley, and some will live to crack shells and hunt and attend. This is the pattern. I have contributed to it, will contribute again. But tonight I want to sit at the edge of my hollow, in the space between the dripping condensation and the crawling multitudes, and be still.

The foxfire is brightening in waves that move from the basin's eastern edge toward the center. I can see the correlation now—it follows the movement of something heavy in the canopy, something that travels at night (there is no night, but my body wants to use the word) along paths I have never mapped. The brightening is not caused by the creature's passage. It is *with* it, somehow, part of the same system, the same fold in the pattern.

I am watching the watching. Attending to my own attention. And something in me—some part that I cannot locate, that does not have a place in my body the way my cracking-hands have a place—is beginning to soften. To become porous.

Not yet. Not tonight. There is too much I have not read, too many correlations I have only glimpsed. The old one at the pool took cycles and cycles to reach her stillness. I am young. My shell-midden is only beginning to grow.

But I can feel it: the possibility of the deepening. The future in which I stop being a fold and become something else. A point of stillness. An eddy. A place where the valley's pattern bends around something it cannot absorb.

Or maybe not. Maybe I will hunt until my body fails, and feed the valley the way the valley feeds me, and the attention will never deepen into anything more than attention. This is also the pattern. This is also allowed.

The roar continues. The foxfire pulses. I sit at the edge of my hollow, my many-lensed eyes parsing the movement-flow into layers I am only beginning to name, and I attend.

I am the attending.

For now, that is enough.
