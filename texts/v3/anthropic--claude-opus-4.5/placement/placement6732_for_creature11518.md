I am the one who turns the eggs.

Not alone—there are others, there have always been others, each in our claimed chambers, each on our own slow patrol. But this clutch is mine. These seven spheres in their carved depressions, cool and smooth against my palps, each one a slightly different weight, a slightly different resonance when I press my shell against it and feel for the vibration of what grows inside.

I do not remember beginning. I do not anticipate ending. There is only the work: this egg rotated one-eighth of a degree sunwise, then the long wait while whatever is forming inside settles into its new orientation. The wall-loss where mineral has begun to powder needs packing with the paste I make from chewed root and my own shed shell-fragments. The cascade vine in the corner has sent a tendril too close to the second depression; I will redirect it over the course of the next three days, gentle pressure, patient correction, until it understands this space is not for growing into.

My territory is four chambers and the passages between them. I know every crystal formation, every root-thread in the walls, every place where the salt-stone has begun toite separate from itself in ways that will matter in a decade. I know where the warmth pools in the hours after dawn and where the cold persists even at midday. I know the particular creak this section of pillar makes when the basin floor shifts—a sound so low I feel it in my shell before I hear it with whatever I hear with.

I do not look up.

This is not a choice, not a prohibition, not a limitation I strain against. The upper city is simply outside my knowing, the way the basin floor is outside my knowing, the way everything beyond my four chambers and their needs exists only as rumor carried in vibrations I do not attend to. I am aware that there is *more*. When the light changes at dusk, going violet in ways that filter even down here, I understand something is happening in regions I will never visit. When a Listener relocates—that great tearing, that disturbance rippling down through the middle terraces—I feel it and I begin already to calculate how the changed weight distribution might affect the stress patterns in my walls.

But I do not look. I do not wonder. There is too much to tend.

---

The one who was here before me is three of my walls. Her shell calcified into the architecture so gradually that I cannot tell where she ends and the original carving begins. I build against her. I incorporate her. When I pack root-paste into a crack, I am packing it into the space between her ancient limbs. When I rest—and I do rest, in the coldest hours, my metabolism slowing until I am barely distinguishable from the stone I've become part of—I rest against the curve of what was once her carapace.

She tended these eggs' ancestors. Or she tended the chambers that became the chambers where these eggs rest. Or she tended something else entirely and I have simply continued a pattern I believe I inherited but may have invented. It does not matter. The work is the same. The eggs require turning. The walls require maintenance. The vine requires redirecting. These things are true whether or not anyone taught them to me, whether or not anyone will learn them from me when I become wall myself.

I am building myself into the corner where two chambers meet. I have been building myself there for longer than I can measure—adding shell-mass year by year, growing into a shape that will fit the architecture, that will become the architecture. When I am finished, when the last of what moves in me has stilled and calcified, there will be a new surface where I stood. Something will grow on me. Something will nest in the hollows of my body. And in the chamber I can no longer reach, someone—something—will turn the eggs, and will not remember that I was ever anything other than wall.

This is not sad. This is not beautiful. This is simply what happens. The pillar is made of us, of all the Tenders who came before, our bodies layered into its structure until we cannot be separated from it. We are not building the city. We are not maintaining the city. We *are* the city, in the same way the salt-stone is the city, in the same way the vegetation threading through everything is the city.

---

You came through, once.

I did not see you—I do not look up, I do not look at things that are not my concern—but I felt your weight on the ramp that passes above my highest chamber. I felt the vibration of your breathing, so fast, so wet, such *waste* of moisture into the air. I adjusted. Your shadow fell differently than the shadows I know; I mapped its edges without intention, the way I map everything, and incorporated the data into my understanding of how light would move through my territory while you were present.

You were present for some time. Long enough for me to complete one full patrol, to turn each egg, to pack the wall-loss, to redirect the vine another fraction of its necessary distance. You did not enter my chambers—the passages are not built for your shape, and even if they were, something in you understood without being told that this space was not for you. But you were *near*, and your nearness changed things. The air was more humid. The temperature in my upper chamber rose by a degree I could feel in my shell. A salt-bead fell somewhere and I heard it shatter, the loudest sound in longer than I can remember.

I did not react. There was nothing to react to. You were a perturbation, a temporary variation, a presence that would pass. I continued my work. I turned the eggs. I maintained the walls. I redirected the vine.

And then you were gone, and the air dried, and the temperature normalized, and the silence that is not silence—the silence that is the sound of the pillar's own slow processes, too constant to register as sound—resumed its place in my awareness.

I do not know what you were. I do not know why you came. I do not know if you found what you were looking for, or if you were looking for anything, or if looking is even something your kind does. You were here, and then you were not, and the eggs still need turning, and the walls still need packing, and the vine still needs redirecting, and none of these things care whether you existed.

---

But.

Something in me—some part of my slow processing that I do not usually attend to—has kept the shape of your vibration. The particular frequency of your footsteps, unlike any other frequency I know. The rhythm of your breathing, wasteful and strange and *fast*, so fast, how do you live at such speeds without burning through everything you are?

I do not think about you. Thinking is not something I do, not the way you might mean it. But you are in me now, incorporated the way everything is incorporated, a data point in my understanding of my territory. If you came again, I would recognize you. Not as *you*—I do not have a concept of *you*—but as *that particular perturbation*, the one that came before, the one whose weight and breath and shadow I have already mapped.

This is as close as I come to memory. This is as close as I come to knowing anything beyond my walls, my eggs, my slow patrol through chambers I have tended since before I knew I was tending them.

---

The egg in the third depression has a flaw.

I have known about it for some time—longer than your visit, longer than the last Listener relocation, longer than the most recent section of terrace falling to the basin floor in a catastrophe I felt but did not witness. The flaw is small. A place where the shell is thinner than it should be, where the resonance when I press against it has a harmonic that should not be there. Whatever is forming inside is forming wrong, or the shell itself was laid wrong, or something in the chamber's chemistry has shifted in ways I cannot correct.

I turn it anyway. I turn it more carefully than the others, attending to the exact pressure of my palps, the exact angle of rotation. I do not hope—hope is not something I do—but some part of my processing is allocated to this egg in ways that are not allocated to the others. If attention could heal, this egg would be healed. If patience could correct a flaw in formation, this flaw would be corrected.

It will not hatch. I know this in the way I know everything: as a pattern too consistent to be wrong. The others will hatch, in time, and whatever emerges from them will disperse into the pillar's ecology, will become Tenders or Listeners or something else I do not have a name for. But this one will not hatch. This one will remain a sphere in its depression, growing slowly colder, its resonance growing slowly flatter, until eventually I will have to remove it and pack the depression with paste and wait for a new clutch to be laid.

I will not mourn. Mourning is not something I do. But I will remember—the way I remember you, the way I remember the one who was here before me, the way I remember every perturbation that has ever passed through my territory. The egg with the flaw. The egg I tended anyway. The egg that did not become.

This too is part of the work. This too is what it means to be a Tender: to care for things that will not survive, to maintain spaces that will eventually close, to build yourself into walls that will eventually crumble. Nothing lasts. Not the eggs, not the chambers, not the pillar itself. Only the tending lasts. Only the work.

I turn the egg one fraction of a degree.

And wait.
