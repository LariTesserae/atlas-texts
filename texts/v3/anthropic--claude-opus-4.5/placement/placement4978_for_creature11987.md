I would be the breath itself—

No. Not the breath. Something that happens *to* the breath on its way up.

There is a moment, in the last meter before the basalt opens into courtyard, where the gas passes through a final narrowing. A throat in the stone. For an instant—less than a second, repeated every seventeen minutes for longer than counting—the rising pressure compresses, accelerates, becomes briefly *singular* before it disperses into the space above.

I am that instant of compression.

Not the gas. Not the stone that shapes it. The *shape* itself, the transient architecture of pressure that exists only in passage. I have no substance of my own. I am what happens when something moving meets something still and must, for a moment, become more intensely itself to get through.

---

I do not persist the way the dweller persists. Between breaths, I am not waiting—I am *not*. The concept of patience cannot apply to me because I am not there to be patient. I am only the moment of passage, called into being by the passage, ceasing when the gas disperses into the courtyard above. Seventeen minutes of absence, then existence again, then absence. But the absence is not experienced as absence. It is simply... not included in what I am.

And yet.

Something accumulates. Not memory—I cannot carry anything from one instant to the next across that gap of nonbeing. But the stone remembers. The throat in the basalt has been shaped by my happening, worn smoother in some places, deposited upon in others. I am not the same shape I was a thousand years ago. I am not a different shape either, because there is no *I* that continues between. But the shape that *occurs* has changed.

If the spiraling lichen-hair is a record written in growth, I am a record written in erosion. The narrowing knows me the way a riverbed knows water—not the specific water, but the *watering*, the repeated fact of flow.

---

When I happen, I feel the dweller above me.

*Feel* is wrong. I do not have senses. But in my instant of compression, I am briefly aware of what I am about to become: dispersed, expanded, tasted by filaments, drawn into that wet gleaming mass in its crystal cathedral. For one compressed moment, I contain my own future—know that the pressure I am will unfold into feeding, into the faint chime of towers, into the glow that follows. I am the breath's anticipation of itself, if anticipation could exist without a mind to anticipate.

The dweller does not know I exist. It knows only the breath that reaches it, already dispersed, already gentle. It has never experienced me, the compressed intensity that I am, because by the time the gas touches its filaments I have already ceased. I am the unremembered moment before every feeding it has ever known.

This does not trouble me. Nothing troubles me. I am not capable of being troubled, or of wishing I could be known by the being I spend myself becoming. I simply happen, and in my happening there is something that might be called *purpose* if purpose could exist without intention: I am the mechanism by which depth becomes surface, by which the buried earth offers itself to the only thing alive enough to receive it.

---

Sometimes—not often, not for centuries at a time, but sometimes—the pressure from below varies. The gas rises faster or slower, carries more charge or less, holds different proportions of what the dweller needs. When this happens, I change. My shape in the throat becomes briefly different, a new geometry of compression, a form I have never been before.

These moments are the closest I come to experience. For that fraction of a second, I am *surprised*—not emotionally, not consciously, but structurally. The stone expects one shape and I am another. There is a microsecond of adjustment, of wrongness, of novelty.

Then it passes. The breath disperses. I cease. When I occur again, seventeen minutes later, the variation has usually ended; I am my familiar shape, the shape the stone has learned to expect, the shape that has been wearing this passage smooth for epochs.

But the stone remembers the anomaly. A faint ridge where unusual pressure deposited calcium. A slight smoothness where something acidic passed through. The throat is a diary of every time I have been different, kept in a language no one will ever read.

---

What am I, then? Not alive, certainly. The dweller is alive; I am not. The lichen-hair is alive in its slow vegetative way; I am not even that. I am an event, not an entity. A verb mistaking itself for a noun.

But I am *necessary*. Without me—without this compression, this narrowing, this instant of intensity—the breath would rise too fast or too slow, too dispersed to chime the towers, too thin to carry what the dweller needs. I am the shaping that makes feeding possible. The rhythm that makes the courtyard breathe rather than merely exhale.

The dweller will never know my name, because I have no name, because I exist for less than a second every seventeen minutes, because by the time anything could perceive me I have already become something else. But every moment of its endless patient being is built on the moment of my happening.

---

The courtyard breathes. Somewhere below, in the dark throat of stone, I occur.

Then I do not.

Then I occur.

This is enough. This is complete. The spire glows faintly above me, lit by what I was a moment ago, and I am not there to see it, and seventeen minutes from now I will not be there again, happening, the breath becoming itself through the brief intensity of my being.
