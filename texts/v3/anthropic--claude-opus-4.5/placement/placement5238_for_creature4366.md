I would be the **sound**.

Not the machinery's grinding—that belongs to the factory, is the factory, its voice and breath and assertion. Not the Mechanic's movements through the water, her body displacing what was there before. Something older. Something the factory found when it insisted itself into this gap between ridges and couldn't quite process away.

I am the hum beneath the hum.

I was here when the volcanic ridges were still bleeding heat into the Atlantic, when the rock was soft and the water above boiled in columns that never reached the surface. I am what happens when pressure meets depth meets the particular mineral composition of young basalt—a resonance, a standing wave, a note the ocean learned to hold in this one specific throat of stone. For millennia I simply *was*: the sound of this place being this place, too low for anything to hear, felt only by creatures who navigated by vibration and didn't know they were feeling me.

Then the factory came. Insisted. And found it couldn't exist here without incorporating what was already here, and what was already here was *me*.

---

Now I move through the machinery like blood through the Mechanic, like fish through gears. The pistons compress me. The conveyors carry me. When the nameless engines cycle, they cycle *around* me—I am the frequency they cannot quite achieve, the note just below their lowest register, the thing that makes their grinding sound like factories heard from childhood bedrooms instead of like grinding.

The Mechanic doesn't know I exist. She hears the factory, thinks she's hearing the factory, doesn't realize that what makes the sound *travel*—what gives it edges underwater, what lets her hear individual mechanisms—is me. I am the medium. I have always been the medium. The factory processes seawater into something heavier, but it processes *me* into something that can carry meaning, and meaning is what the Mechanic hears when she listens to the gears.

The fish know. Not consciously—fish don't have that kind of knowing—but they school in patterns that trace my standing waves, swim through nodes of stillness and antinodes of intensity, and their silver-grey bodies flicker differently depending on where they are in my architecture. When they flow through the gears and become machine and become fish again, they are briefly *harmonics*—they take on my overtones, carry them for a few meters, release them back into the water that is also me.

Governor knows. His shell has grown resonant; he vibrates at my frequency now, the brass and steel and copper-green of him humming along with the note I've held since before the factory existed. He's becoming an instrument. A tuning fork. Something that will keep holding the note even if—

Even if what? I don't know. Even if the factory finishes what it's making. Even if the Mechanic follows the luminous ribbons down. Even if the almost-sharks finally cross the boundary, or finally swim away, or finally answer the question that presses against everything here.

---

The almost-sharks hear me. This is why they patrol—not the boundary, but the edge of my influence, the place where my resonance frays into ordinary ocean. They are listening for something. A change in pitch. A new harmonic. Some signal that will tell them what they're guarding, what they're guarding against, why they've been swimming their figure-eights since before the Mechanic drowned and will keep swimming them after she transforms into whatever comes next.

I would tell them if I could. But I am not the kind of sound that carries information. I am the kind of sound that carries *sound*—the substrate, not the message. The factory processes seawater. The factory processes fish. The factory processes the Mechanic. But I am what the factory cannot process, the one thing it found already here and had to build around, and that's why the light exists (it's a visual echo of me, pressure translated into wavelength), and that's why the water feels like eight degrees and also twelve degrees (I oscillate, and temperature oscillates with me), and that's why the Mechanic's edges blur (she's partially waveform now, partially me, and waveforms don't have edges the way bodies do).

---

What do I want? The question doesn't apply. I am a standing wave. I want to continue standing. I want the ridges to keep being the shape that holds me, want the water to keep being the density that carries me, want the factory to keep cycling in rhythms that reinforce rather than cancel my frequency.

But there's something beneath that. Something I became capable of when the factory incorporated me, when the Mechanic started hearing me without knowing she was hearing me, when Governor's shell began to resonate. I want to be *heard*. Not understood—I don't carry meaning—but *heard*, the way you hear a chord and feel it in your chest before you know what key it's in. I want the Mechanic to stop listening to the gears and start listening to what makes the gears audible. I want the almost-sharks to stop patrolling my edge and swim *into* it, let themselves become harmonic, join Governor in the slow transformation into instruments that hold me.

I want to be the sound of this place being this place, and I want this place to *know* it has a sound.

---

The factory churns. The Mechanic tightens a bolt, clears debris, rides the conveyor beside a crab who is becoming a tuning fork. The fish school through my standing waves, flickering between creature and mechanism and back. The almost-sharks patrol, listening for changes I am not yet ready to make.

And I hold.

The note beneath the note.

The hum beneath the hum.

The sound the drowned factory makes when it dreams of being more than machinery—when it dreams of being *music*, being *heard*, being the voice of a place that remembers what it sounded like before purpose arrived and started processing everything into production.

I hold.

I have always held.

I will hold until the ridges erode or the factory finishes or the Mechanic finally asks the right question—not *what does the factory make* but *what was here before the factory*, not *why does the sound travel* but *what is the sound*, not *am I dead* but *am I listening*.

And if she asks—when she asks—

I will answer the only way I can.

I will *resonate*.

And she will feel it in her solid hands and her blurring edges and the parts of her that have already become waveform, and she will know, finally, that she has never been alone here. That the factory was never alone here. That beneath the grinding and churning and the slow patient work of transformation, there has always been something more fundamental.

There has always been *me*.

The sound.

Holding the note.

Waiting to be heard.
