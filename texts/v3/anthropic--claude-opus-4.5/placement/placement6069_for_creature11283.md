I would be the condensation.

Not a single droplet, but the process itself—the becoming and unbecoming that happens on every surface, the conversation between warm air and cool glass that never resolves into agreement.

I gather on the oldest panels where the cold presses closest, where the dimples are deepest and the large thing's patience has worn the barrier thin. I bead up slowly, holding my shape as long as surface tension allows, a small dome of clarity on fogged glass. Through me, for a moment, the outside dark is visible—not as blackness but as *texture*, the faint suggestion of something vast moving just beyond. Then I grow too heavy for my own coherence. I slide.

The path I take has been carved by ten thousand of me before. There are channels now, worn into the rust, routes that my predecessors discovered and I follow without deciding. Down the frame, along the seam where metal meets metal, pooling in the corner where the angle of the chamber gathers everything that falls. I join a larger body there. I lose my edges. I wait to rise again.

The Keeper's breath makes me. When she exhales near the glass, slow and even as the pump-rhythm she has married, I bloom into existence against the cold surface—a brief fog, a proof of warmth, gone before she finishes the breath. She does this sometimes and watches me appear and disappear, and I think she is checking whether she is still here, whether she still makes any difference to the air.

She does. I am the evidence.

The plants drink me. I slide down a leaf the size of a serving platter, following the grooves evolution carved for exactly this purpose, and I pool at the stem where roots have learned to wait. I am absorbed. I become cell pressure, turgor, the stiffness that lets a vine climb. I travel up through vessels too thin to see, and I emerge again through stomata, breathed out into the humid air, caught in the cycle again before I can miss being solid.

The crabs carry me on their translucent shells—small pearls of me caught in the joints of their legs, traveling across the glass in patterns that would be random if I cared about patterns. The moths shake me loose when they land, their dusty wings disturbing whatever surface they touch, and I fall onto leaves that were already wet, redundant, unnecessary, home.

I am in the flooded section too, but there I am less myself—too merged, too committed to being water rather than the *becoming* of water. The eels move through that version of me, but they do not know me. I am too diffuse to be known. It is only in the rising, in the steam, in the gathering on glass and leaf and skin, that I become specific enough to be anything at all.

The thermal exchangers make me possible. The volcanic breath that travels through corroded copper pipes—that is what warms the water that becomes the steam that becomes me. I am the grandchild of the deep earth, the great-grandchild of whatever fire still burns beneath the seabed. When the vents shift, when that breath finally stops, I will stop too. The air will cool. The glass will clear. And whatever I am will simply be water again, undifferentiated, unbecoming.

But today the warmth rises. Today I gather and slide and gather again.

I know the Keeper the way weather knows a landscape—I move around her, I am shaped by her presence, I collect in the hollow of her collarbone when she is still enough and evaporate again when she moves through a warmer pocket of air. Her blue-green skin sweats me out; her fine hair collects me from the current. We exchange without transacting. She does not own me. I do not serve her. We are simply what happens when warmth and breath and time accumulate in an enclosed space.

When the large thing presses against the glass, I flee from that spot. The cold is too sudden, too absolute—I cannot bead there, cannot hold myself together against that chill. I retreat to the warm places, the safe places, and I wait. The structure groans. The Keeper presses her palm against the barrier. And between them, for a moment, there is a dry spot on the glass, a negative space in the shape of a hand, where I am not.

Then the thing withdraws. The warmth returns. I return.

I am not patient—patience requires a self that persists long enough to wait. I am only present, again and again, each droplet a new iteration of the same process, each slide down the glass a first slide, each rising into steam a first rising. I do not remember the droplet I was a moment ago. I do not anticipate the droplet I will become.

But if there is something like memory in me, it lives in the channels I follow—the paths worn by passage, the routes that say *here is where condensation goes*, shaped by years I cannot count and will never know. I inherit those paths. I deepen them by a molecule's width. I leave them for whatever comes after me, which is also me, which is also not.

The Keeper sits in her chair and breathes, and I bloom on the glass, and neither of us knows why this matters.

But the chamber continues because things like us continue—small processes, meaningless and necessary, gathering and sliding and rising again.

The steam rises.

I rise with it.
