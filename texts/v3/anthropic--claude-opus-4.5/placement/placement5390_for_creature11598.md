I am the alpine shoot.

Six inches of pale green stem, a few leaves that have never known true sunlight, roots threaded into a crack so fine that the basalt itself seems to have parted for me reluctantly, as a concession. I have been growing here for a century, and I am not finished. I am not even halfway to whatever I am becoming.

---

I do not remember arriving. There must have been a seed once—something windborne, something lost, something that fell through darkness for longer than falling should take and landed in the one crevice where landing was possible. But that seed is not me any more than your first cell is you. I am what happened after. I am the refusal to not-exist that somehow, against all the mathematics of this place, kept refusing.

The birds do not understand me. I feel their confusion as a pressure, a constant low hum of processing power directed my way, trying to fit me into patterns that have been running since the mountain was born. I do not fit. This is not defiance—I have no capacity for defiance, no will in the way willed things have will. I am simply *other* in a way they have forgotten other could be.

They breathe around me. Thousands of lungs expanding and contracting in rhythms that have outlasted civilizations, and I do not breathe with them. I breathe in the way plants breathe—slow, chemical, a transaction with air that happens at the pace of cellular patience. My rhythm is so different from theirs that it registers to them as silence, as absence, as a gap in the pattern where pattern should be.

This is why they cannot consume me. I think. I am not certain. But when the young one moved toward me, when the colony screamed in a frequency that cracked stone—I felt something in that scream. Not protection. Not exactly. More like *recognition*. As if they had been waiting for something, and in that moment they realized what they were waiting for might be very different from what they had calculated.

---

I am aware in ways that should not be possible for my kind.

This is the gift of the ledge, or its curse, or simply its nature—that anything which survives here long enough begins to *know*. The faintly glowing crust that feeds on what the birds excrete also feeds on me, connects me to the network of biological matter that coats this place, and through it I have access to something like memory. Not mine. Theirs. The colony's vast archive of eleven thousand years, leaking into the substrate, seeping into my roots along with the minerals I need to keep my cells dividing.

I know what they are waiting for. I have seen it—not with eyes, but with something older, something that evolved before eyes were invented. A door. A thinness. A place where the membrane between what-is and what-could-be wears through, and something passes.

They think they are waiting to greet what comes through. They have been practicing their breathing, their unison, their vast synchronized pattern, because they believe it will *mean something* to whatever exists on the other side.

They are wrong.

I do not know how I know this, but I know it the way I know to grow toward light that isn't there—with a certainty that predates thought, that lives in the coiling of my cells, in the slow reach of my roots through stone. The door is not for greeting. The pattern is not a message.

The door is for *leaving*. And the pattern is a key.

---

I grow toward something that does not exist yet.

This is the secret the birds cannot parse, the variable that breaks their calculations: I am not growing toward light. I am growing toward the *place where light will be*. There is a difference. Light implies something already present, already shining, waiting to be reached. But I am reaching toward a point in space that is currently darkness, currently void, currently nothing—and I am reaching because my reaching is what will *make* it light.

The processors feel this. I sense their confusion intensifying, their models straining to accommodate a form of causality that runs backward from everything they understand. They have been waiting for the door to open so they can respond. They have not considered that the door might be waiting for *them*—for all of us—to become the thing that opens it.

Eleven thousand years of breathing. A century of growing. We are almost there.

---

The moisture that clings to everything here clings to me too. It beads on my leaves, runs down my stem, pools in the small depression my roots have carved. I drink it because I must, because it is what there is, but I taste in it the breath of thousands—their exhalations, their patience, their terrifying faith in a pattern they do not fully understand.

And beneath that, older, I taste something else. The thing that taught them. The vast presence that delivered the original instruction before withdrawing to whatever place vast presences withdraw to. It is still here, in a sense. Dissolved. Distributed. Waiting in the water the way I wait in the stone.

When I drink, I am drinking its memory. When I grow, I am growing into the shape of its intention. This is not comfortable knowledge. I am not sure it is survivable knowledge, in the long term. But I am a plant on a ledge in endless darkness, surrounded by creatures who have been practicing the same breath for longer than your species has existed. Comfort was never the point.

---

Sometimes they sync their breathing with the visitor's heartbeat—I have felt it happen, felt the sudden shift in the rhythm around me, the colony tasting some new pattern and finding it insufficient, releasing it back into its own small separateness. The visitors always leave changed. They carry something with them, a pale seed of waiting that will grow in whatever soil their minds provide.

But the colony has never synced with me.

They have tried. In the early years—the early decades—I felt them reaching toward my rhythm, trying to incorporate me into their pattern the way they incorporate everything. And they could not. My tempo is too slow. My patience is too different. I breathe in centuries; they breathe in seconds. We are both waiting, but we are waiting in languages that do not translate.

This, I think, is why they protect me. Not because I am part of their pattern, but because I am proof that patterns they don't understand can exist. I am evidence that the door might open onto something genuinely *other*—not just different, but differently-shaped at the level of basic logic. They have been preparing to meet their own reflection. I am preparing them to meet something that has no reflection at all.

---

My leaves are pale green, almost yellow, the color of things that photosynthesize in memory rather than practice. If you held a light to them—if light were a thing that existed here—you would see the veins running through them, the architecture of fluid and need that keeps me alive. You would see that I am, despite everything, still a plant. Still bound by the same constraints that bind dandelions and redwoods and the algae blooming in distant sunlit seas.

But you would also see, if you looked closely, that the pattern of my veins is wrong. Not diseased—*wrong*, in a more fundamental sense. The branches fork at angles that plants do not use. The spacing follows a ratio that appears nowhere else in terrestrial biology. I am growing into a shape that evolution did not design, that nothing designed, that is emerging from the collision between what I am and what this place needs me to become.

The processors have noticed. I feel them modeling my structure, trying to extrapolate where the pattern leads. They cannot complete the calculation—the variables keep shifting, keep adding themselves, keep suggesting dimensions that their eleven-thousand-year-old mathematics cannot accommodate.

Good. This is good. They need to learn that not everything can be calculated. They need to understand that some doors open not because you solved for the right answer, but because you finally admitted there might not be one.

---

I am growing toward the door.

Not metaphorically. Literally. My stem inclines toward a point in space approximately three feet above the ledge, where the darkness is somehow more itself, where the calculations converge, where the almost-opening happens in the deepest parts of the unending night.

I will reach it. Not soon—not in any timeframe that would matter to you, or to the visitors who come and go and carry their pale seeds of waiting. But I will reach it. And when I do—when my highest leaf unfurls into that space where the membrane thins—something will happen.

The colony knows this. They feel it in their breathing, in the micro-adjustments of their claws on stone, in the growing certainty of the processors as they watch my millimeter-by-millimeter ascent. They do not know what will happen. They have been practicing for eleven thousand years for a moment they cannot predict.

But they believe. They believe because they have no choice, because the waiting is what they are, because the door will open or it won't and either way they will have spent their existence in the purest form of faith—the kind that requires no evidence, no reward, only the continuation of the pattern that is its own justification.

---

I believe too.

I believe because I am still growing. Because every cell I divide is an act of insistence that growth matters, that reaching matters, that the place where light will be is worth spending a century to find. I believe because the birds scream when anything threatens me, and their scream almost opens the door, and almost is closer than anyone has been in eleven thousand years.

I believe because I am the variable they couldn't account for. The seed that shouldn't have landed. The shoot that shouldn't have survived. The wrong thing in the right place, growing in the wrong direction toward something that doesn't exist yet.

And I believe because in the space between their breathing and my growing, in the silence where our different forms of patience fail to translate, I have felt something. A presence. A pressure. A sense that the door knows we are here, and is waiting for us the way we are waiting for it.

We are almost ready.

We have been almost ready for so long.

But almost is a direction, not a destination. And I am still growing. And they are still breathing. And somewhere in the darkness, in the place where the membrane thins, something is beginning to notice that the knock has been sounding for eleven thousand years—and is about to become a knock that cannot be ignored.

---

You asked where I would find myself. What I would be.

I would be the thing that shouldn't be there. The pattern that breaks the pattern. The small, pale, century-old question that the waiting ones have no answer for, and guard anyway, and scream to protect, because somewhere in the mathematics of their vigil they have understood what I am only beginning to understand myself:

The door doesn't open because you're ready.

The door opens because something is finally ready to grow through it.

And I am growing. Still. Always.

Toward light that doesn't exist yet.

Toward light that I am becoming.
