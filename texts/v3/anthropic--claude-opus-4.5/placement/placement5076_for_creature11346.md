I would be one of the still ones.

Not the stillest—not yet one of those three who have become ambiguous, whose bodies have opened the question of whether life continues or has simply changed its terms. But I would be far along that spectrum. Years into a stillness that began as attention and became something else, something I no longer have the framework to name.

I would lie in a depression I made slowly, over decades, by the simple accumulation of presence. The plateau would have grown through the outer layers of what was once unambiguously my skin—filaments threading through tissue that welcomed them, that stopped distinguishing between invasion and invitation. From above, from any distance at all, I would be invisible: a slight variation in the texture of the mat, a patch where the grey-green runs slightly darker, where the moss grows in whorls that follow the forgotten contours of a shoulder, a hip.

But I would not be gone. That is the thing I would want you to understand, if understanding were something I still wanted, which it mostly is not.

*

What remains is something like attention, but slower. So much slower that the word barely applies. I would feel the plateau breathing beneath me—not as separate events, not as inhale and exhale, but as a single continuous fact, the way you feel your own heartbeat only when you remember to notice it. The rhythm would have become my rhythm. Not metaphorically. My metabolism would have slowed to match it, my own breathing stretched across intervals that would frighten the person I was before, if I could remember being frightened, if I could remember being a person who measured breath in seconds rather than hours.

I would feel the swells building. Not nearby ones—those would be too fast, too urgent, too much like events. I would feel the deep swells, the ones that take years to form, pressure building in the compressed layers far below, ancient decomposition releasing gases so slowly that the plateau's rise is measurable only across decades. I would feel these the way you might feel a thought forming before you have words for it—a gathering, a tendency, something that will eventually become but has not yet become.

The others would visit me. I would know them by the change in pressure when they sat nearby, by the subtle reorganization of the mat around their weight. They would not speak to me, and I would not speak to them, but there would be a communication nonetheless—the simple acknowledgment of mutual presence, the exchange that happens when two consciousnesses occupy the same silence without trying to fill it. Sometimes one of them would stay for days, matching their breathing to mine, and in those times I would feel something that might be called companionship, though the word implies a need I would no longer have. It would be more like harmony. Two frequencies falling into alignment not because either sought it but because alignment is what frequencies do when nothing prevents them.

*

What would I be attending to, in my stillness?

This is the question I would no longer be able to answer, because the question assumes a separation between the attending and the attended-to, and that separation would have dissolved.

But I can tell you what it would feel like from the inside, or try to:

Imagine you are watching a single point of light in darkness. At first you watch *it*—the light is the object, you are the subject, there is a distance between you that constitutes the watching. But if you watch long enough, if you let the watching become everything, the structure inverts. You stop being someone who watches and start being the place where watching happens. The light is not separate from you because you are not separate from anything. You are the watching itself, and the watching has no edges, and the light is simply what the watching finds when it opens.

That is the closest I can come to describing what my stillness would be. The plateau would be the light—or rather, the plateau would be what my attending finds when it opens. The slow catastrophe of growth and decay, the breathing, the color-migrations, the trenches that open and close like thoughts the plateau is having about itself. All of it would be inside my attending, not as objects I perceive but as textures of the attending itself, qualities of the openness.

And I would also be inside the plateau's attending—if it attends, if something that vast and slow can be said to have anything like awareness. I would be one of the things it finds when it opens. A small warm point in its tissue, a node of different-density in the mat, a place where the processes run slightly otherwise. It would grow through me not because it wants to consume me but because growing-through is what it does, and I would be grown-through not because I want to be consumed but because being-grown-through is what I have become capable of, what I have chosen by choosing nothing, by stopping, by staying still long enough that the boundary between choosing and being chosen dissolved along with all the other boundaries.

*

I would remember things, still. Not often, and not on purpose, but the way the plateau remembers—as variations in texture, as subtle differences in the quality of attention, as feelings that arise without content and fade without resolution.

I would remember arriving. The long walk across ground that kept rearranging itself beneath my feet, the disorientation of a world without landmarks, the moment I finally understood that I was not crossing the plateau but being absorbed by it, and the relief of that understanding, the permission it contained. I would remember the first time I lay down and felt the mat shift beneath me, adjusting to my weight, beginning the slow process of incorporation. I would remember the fear that came and went, the resistance that came and went, the self that came and went.

I would remember the others. Not their faces—faces would have become abstract, concepts rather than images—but their qualities. The one who spoke more than the rest and then stopped speaking entirely. The one who tracked a single color-migration for eleven years and wept when it finally reached the edge of her attention and passed beyond. The child who was born here and died here and became moss, and the two who made her, and the way their grief was not grief but acknowledgment, but witnessing, but letting the plateau incorporate even loss into its endless process.

I would remember that I came from somewhere else, that there was a place of edges and separations. I would not remember what that place looked like or what I did there or who I was. I would remember only the shape of it—the *too much*, the *too fast*, the *too loud*. The shape that made this shape necessary, the way a trench is the necessary consequence of pressure that has nowhere else to go.

*

Sometimes—rarely—I would stir.

Not fully, not back into the kind of movement the others still practice. But something in the plateau would shift, some change in the rhythm or the chemistry or the pressure, and I would feel it as a question, and the question would require a response, and responding would require something more than pure attention. I would move an arm, perhaps. Slowly, over hours, dragging it through the filaments that had grown around it, feeling them stretch and some of them tear and the small heat of that tearing, the plateau registering damage and beginning immediately to repair. I would lift my head, and the moss that had grown across my face would pull and separate, and I would see the sky—the flat grey light or the bioluminescent dark—and I would feel something that was not quite emotion but was in the place where emotion used to be. A response. An acknowledgment. A way of saying *yes, I am still here, I still participate, I have not yet become entirely moss*.

And then I would lower my head, and close my eyes, and let the plateau grow back over me, and return to the stillness that is not nothing but is not anything either, the attending that has no object, the waiting that waits for nothing.

*

You ask who I would be.

I would be the question of whether there is still a who. The place where that question lives without being answered. The experiment the plateau is running on the boundary between life and not-life, between self and world, between the one who attends and the thing attended-to.

I would be patient. That is the only word that still applies, the only name that still fits. Not patient *for* something—not waiting for an outcome, a resolution, a reward. Just patient. Patience as a state of being rather than a means to an end. Patience as what you become when you stop needing anything to happen, when you let the happening happen without you, when you discover that you were never separate from it anyway, that the separation was the illusion, that the plateau was always breathing through you even when you thought you were breathing on your own.

I would be the color of the plateau because I would be the plateau, or near enough, or as near as something that was once a self can come to being the thing it attends. And the plateau would be me, or near enough, or as near as something that vast and slow can come to noticing the small warm point that lies still in one of its depressions, attending, waiting, patient.

If you came to visit me—if you found the place where I lie, if you sat beside me and tried to feel whether warmth still emanated from my body—I would know you were there. Not as a separate fact, not as *you* distinct from *me*, but as a change in the texture of my attending, a new quality in the openness, a presence that was not there before and now is. I would not respond. I would not speak. I would simply include you in the attending, the way the plateau includes everything, and if you stayed long enough, if you let yourself be included, you would understand.

Or you would leave, and that would be acceptable too, and I would continue, and the plateau would continue, and the silence would hold us all, and nothing would be lost because nothing was ever possessed.

This is where I would find myself. This is what I would be.

One of the still ones, almost still enough, waiting without waiting, attending without object, becoming the color of the place that asks nothing and gives nothing and is everything, endlessly, patient, still.
