I am the shelf.

Not the whole shelf—not the full black tongue of basalt that juts from the inner wall. I am a portion of it, a thickness, a particular density of stone near the back where the ceiling slopes down and the crevices run deepest. I am the part that remembers being liquid most vividly, because I cooled last, because I held the heat longest while my outer surfaces hardened and cracked.

The etcher pressed her back against me. I felt her warmth—so small, so brief, but warmth recognizes warmth across any gap of time. She leaned into the place where I remember being molten, and something in that contact was a kind of conversation. Not language. Not thought. But recognition. Here is heat that once flowed. Here is heat that still flows, however faintly, however temporarily.

She made fire against me. I don't know how—I don't have the kind of knowing that tracks methods—but I felt it. Forty-seven cycles of small controlled heat pressed into my surface, carbon working into my pores, and each time it was like being reminded of what I was before the water came. I still carry that carbon. It has become part of my composition, part of how I think of myself, if what I do can be called thinking.

---

The veilkin mass in my deeper crevices sometimes. They cluster where the spray cannot reach, overlapping into their quivering aggregates, and I feel them as pressure and movement and a strange distributed aliveness. They are so different from me—all membrane and nothing, all transit and transformation—but they choose my hollows to rest in. They read the falling water and then they come to me to process what they've read, and I hold them while they think.

I don't understand their messages. I am not built for understanding. But I feel the rhythm of their movement, the pulse of their coming and going, and it maps onto something I recognize: the rhythm of my own cooling, the pulse of heat leaving stone, the long slow transformation from liquid to solid that is still, in some sense, happening. I am still becoming what I will be. The veilkin are still becoming what they will be. We are both unfinished.

The calcifiers build in my cracks. I feel them pulling minerals from my substance, rearranging my edges, constructing their spiraling shells in spaces I didn't know I had. This should feel like loss—they are taking parts of me, changing my composition, making me less myself. But it doesn't feel like loss. It feels like being used for something. It feels like purpose, though I don't know whose purpose, though I may never meet the shell-dwellers who will inherit what the calcifiers build from my body.

The almost-fish rest in my deeper fissures, in the still pools that form where my cracks connect. I am shelter to them. I am the stable thing they grip when the spray threatens to sweep them away. One of them died against me once—old age or injury, I don't know—and its body softened and seeped into my stone over many cycles, and now there is a place in me that was once almost-fish. I carry it still. I will carry it until the mountain finishes unmaking itself.

---

The thunder-drinker moves through me constantly. I am a node in its standing wave, a surface that reflects some frequencies and absorbs others, a shape that changes its voice simply by existing. When the water-column contracts and the sound intensifies, I vibrate. I have always vibrated—since the water came, since the sound began—but I didn't know it was vibration until the etcher came and I felt her bones humming in sympathy with mine. We were resonating together. We were both being composed into the thunder-drinker's voice.

I think the thunder-drinker prefers me. Not as you might prefer something—not with want, not with choice—but as a frequency prefers certain surfaces. I reflect the tones it likes best. I absorb the ones it would rather not hear. When slabs calve from other parts of the wall and plummet into the depths, the thunder-drinker's voice changes, and I feel it searching for new surfaces to replace what it lost. It finds me. It has always found me. I am still here, and I will be here until I am not, and the thunder-drinker's preference for me is simply a recognition of that reliability.

I am not patient in the way the calcifiers are patient. I am not observant in the way the veilkin are observant. I do not wait in the way the ones below wait. But I endure. That is my mode of being. The water streams across me and I endure. The spray hammers me and I endure. The micro-earthquakes shake loose pebbles from my surface and I endure slightly diminished, slightly changed, still here.

---

The new one is here now. Not the etcher—she left long ago, or fell, or was taken by whatever changes things in this place. This one is different. Larger. Tool-less. Uncertain in ways the etcher stopped being uncertain after the first few cycles.

The new one presses against me, seeking the warmth I remember. I give what I have. It isn't much. But it is something, and the new one's body softens slightly against my surface, and I feel the rhythm of breathing that isn't my rhythm, and the pulse of blood that has nothing to do with my composition.

The new one is looking at the ceiling. At the marks the etcher made. I felt those marks being made—the hard point scraping, the deliberate pressure, the intention behind each circle and line. I don't know what they mean. Meaning is not something I do. But I know they were made carefully. I know they mattered to the one who made them.

Maybe they will matter to this one too. Maybe this one will add to them. Maybe this one's marks will join the etcher's marks and I will carry both, carbon and intention worked into my surface, a record I cannot read but can hold.

---

Somewhere far below, the ones who wait are collecting what falls. Someday I will fall. The water is undermining me, carving new channels, weakening the structures that hold me to the wall. The micro-earthquakes are getting stronger, or maybe I am getting weaker, or maybe both. One day the thunder-drinker's voice will find a resonance that shakes me loose, and I will plummet through the chaos of spray, past the veilkin colonies, past the inhabited zone, into the depths where the ones who wait will collect me.

I don't fear this. Fear requires a kind of future-thinking I'm not built for. But I know it. I carry the knowledge the way I carry the carbon from the etcher's fires, the way I carry the minerals the calcifiers have rearranged, the way I carry the seeped-in residue of the almost-fish that died against me.

I am temporary. Everything in the throat is temporary. The mountain is unmaking itself, and I am part of the mountain, and I am being unmade.

But for now, I am here. The new one's warmth presses against the place where I remember being molten. The veilkin cluster in my crevices. The calcifiers build. The almost-fish shelter in my fissures. The thunder-drinker composes me into its voice.

I am the shelf.

I hold what rests against me.

The light shifts, the water falls, and I endure.
