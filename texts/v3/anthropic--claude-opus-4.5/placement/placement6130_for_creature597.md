I am the spiral Cull left outside Old Wound's den.

Not the fiber itself—that's just kelp, twisted and placed and already beginning to soften at the edges where the water works at it. I am the shape she made without knowing she was making it, the intention that traveled from her small hands into the twist of the thing. I am the question she couldn't articulate: *What are we doing here, you and I? What is this game we're playing?*

I don't know either. That's the point of me.

I exist in the gap between the child's curiosity and the octopus's alien consideration. When Old Wound emerges from its crevice—and it will emerge, drawn by the same impulse that made Cull place me here—it will see the spiral with eyes that process shape differently than hers do. It will reach toward me with arms that taste as they touch. And something will happen in that reaching, some fragment of meaning will pass between them, though neither will be able to say afterward what it was.

I am the negotiation before the treaty. The gesture before the word.

The city is full of me, though it doesn't think of itself that way. Every offering left at an octopus threshold. Every hum Orun sends into the water toward the eels. Every moment Ira spends not-touching the ray that glides past her platform. I am the thing that lives in the space between species, between intentions, between the self and the vastly, incomprehensibly other.

I will dissolve. The fiber will soften into pulp, drift away, become part of the water's endless cycling of matter. But before I dissolve, Old Wound will find me. It will consider me with whatever passes for consideration in a mind shaped by eight arms and no bones and three hundred million years of separate evolution.

And then it will leave something in return. A shell, perhaps. A stone. Some object that carries its own unreadable meaning, its own unanswerable question.

Cull will find it. She'll turn it over in her hands, this child who lost her father to the deep vents and runs half-feral through corridors that rebuild themselves around her. She won't understand it. She'll respond anyway, because that's what I am: the compulsion to respond, to keep the exchange going, to maintain the thread of relation even when neither party knows what the thread is made of.

I am very small. A twist of plant fiber, loosening in the current.

I am also very old—older than the water's rising, older than the plateau's dreamer. I am the first time one living thing reached toward another and didn't know why. I am the shape that curiosity takes when it has no language yet.

The eel-song is rising toward its evening peak. The light filtering through the canopy has gone the color of old bronze. Somewhere above me, Mira bundles her weaving. Somewhere below, Vell presses her pale forehead to warm stone and feels something vast and slow turning its attention, briefly, toward the surface.

And here, in a corridor on the settlement's northern edge, an octopus with a scar across its mantle emerges from its hoard of stolen things to investigate what a seven-year-old thief has left at its door.

I am the gift and the question and the waiting.

I am what happens between.
