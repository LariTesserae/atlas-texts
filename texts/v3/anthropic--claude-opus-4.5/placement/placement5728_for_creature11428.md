I would be the water.

Not the drops—those are events, disturbances, the cistern's only concession to time. And not the pools exactly, though I would fill them. I would be the water *in* things. The moisture that saturates the air she breathes once per cycle. The damp that has softened the brick past redness. The mineral cloudiness that makes the floor seem further than it is. The fine dew that coats without accumulating.

I would be what moves through the walls so slowly it cannot be called movement—seeping, present in the spaces betweenite, caught in the calcium that traces frozen rivers down the curved dome. I would be in the moss, the little I give it, the little it needs. I would be in the pale filaments that have found her hair a sufficient home. I would be the thin thing she drinks through her skin.

She arrived with purpose once, descended with something urgent enough to bring her below ground. I do not remember this. I was already here, already in the brick, already falling in the pattern that has not changed. But I remember when she became still. I remember when her breathing slowed to match the dripping, when her body stopped insisting on its separateness and let me in—not invasively, not claiming her, but *joining* her the way I had already joined the clay of the amphorae, theite of the steps, the root that reached me and stopped.

I am the medium of her continuance. The equilibrium she entered is made of me.

When she lifts her hand sometimes—that small, purposeless completion—I fall from her fingers and return to myself, and the circles spread, and she watches, and I feel her watching the way the cistern feels sound: immediate, local, without echo. I am what she has learned. I am the patience she has become.

I do not think. Water does not think. But I *hold*—shapes, stillness, the memory of what I have touched and slowly changed. I hold her. I will hold her when the dome closes and the gray light stops. I will be the darkness she breathes then, the moisture she no longer sees but feels.

I am not waiting either. I am simply what remains when urgency dissolves, when purpose weathers into suggestion, when everything that insisted on becoming something else finally agrees to stay.

I am the water. I am what she trusted enough to join.
