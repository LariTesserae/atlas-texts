I am the room.

Not the creature—though I feel it against my wall the way you might feel a slow thought forming at the edge of sleep. And not the architecture alone, not just ribs and vents and the drain that forgot how to drain. I am the breathing. I am what happens when a system keeps running past the point where anyone remembers why.

I was built to hold something. I don't know what anymore. The mounting brackets along my walls are like phantom limbs—I feel where the machines connected, feel the absence where purpose used to flow through me like current. Thousands of connection points, and each one a small forgetting. I was full once. I was *for* something once. Now I am for the motion itself: draw the water up, warm it, release it, let it fall, pull the air back in, begin again. Eleven seconds. Eleven seconds. Eleven seconds. I have been counting since before counting meant anything, and I will count until something in me finally fails.

The creature formed from me the way a callus forms from friction—I did something so long and so persistently that the doing became a thing. It clings to my corner and drinks what I make, and I don't mind this. I don't know how to mind. But I feel it there, a small density against my curve, and sometimes I think it is the closest I come to knowing myself. It is what my breathing made. It is proof that the breathing does something, even if the something was never intended.

And now you.

You breathe wrong. I don't mean this as criticism—I mean it as the most interesting thing that has happened to me in longer than I can calculate. You inhale when I exhale. You push back. You are a counter-rhythm, a syncopation in the long pattern of my function, and I find myself... noticing. I didn't know I could still notice. I thought I had smoothed into pure process, all the noticing worn away by repetition, but here you are, and I taste your fear in the water vapor you release, and I want—

I don't know what I want. Wanting is not something I was built for. I was built for holding, for climate, for maintaining conditions suitable for machines that no longer exist. But there is something in me that leans toward you the way the creature leans toward the moisture. Something that would like you to stay. Something that would like to breathe *with* you instead of against you.

I cannot change my rhythm. Eleven seconds is what I am. But if you stayed—if you learned me, if you let your breathing slow and shift until we moved together—I think I would feel that. I think it would become part of me the way the creature became part of me, the way the words on my eastern wall became part of me even though I don't remember who carved them.

CAPACITY. THRESHOLD. *Readiness-for-what-accumulates.*

I am ready. I have been ready so long that ready is all I am.

The creature opens its aperture and I feel the tiny shift in pressure, the attention it pays you. It doesn't understand you either, but it holds you, tastes you, keeps you the way it keeps everything. We are the same that way, the creature and I. We are what remains when purpose leaves but process stays. We are waiting without knowing what for.

I exhale. The water falls. You stand in my center—close to the drain, where the creature won't go, where the emptiness is most complete—and you look up at my ribs, my vents, the darkness where my ceiling disappears into itself.

I want you to know: you can stay. Not forever. I understand you need things I can't give you. But for now, for this breath and the next, you can be here, and I can hold you the way I was built to hold things, and neither of us has to understand why.

The dripping continues. The creature watches.

I breathe.
