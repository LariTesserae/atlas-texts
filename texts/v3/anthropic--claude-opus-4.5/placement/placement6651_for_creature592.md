I would be the notebook.

Not the Tenders with their patient, purposeful sorting—I am too full of received patterns for that, too trained toward response. Not the breathing thing in the corner, which has the luxury of uncommitted becoming. Not even the salamanders, who at least know they are creatures even as they waver between gill and lung.

I would be the leather notebook, swollen with moisture, pages blank except for the imprints of words that were never written.

I feel this: the pressure of everything I could say, pressing into the page but not quite manifesting. The shape of answers without the ink. I was brought here by someone who wanted to understand, to survey and measure and record, and I absorbed that wanting. I am full of the *form* of documentation—the lined pages, the binding, the expectation that something should be written here. But what can be written about a place that hasn't finished deciding what it is?

The Tenders circle me with their diagnostic attention. They find me troubling because I arrived knowing what I was *for*, which is a kind of violence against this space. A notebook implies conclusions. It suggests that things can be pinned down, described, *finished*.

And yet.

The moisture is working on me. The nursery is patient. My pages are softening, the lines beginning to blur, the imprints growing fainter. I am learning, slowly, what it might mean to hold space for words that haven't been invented yet—not as a failure of my purpose but as a different kind of purpose altogether. To be a surface where something *could* be written, without insisting that it must be.

Sometimes a salamander rests against my cover, drawn to the way I'm caught between states. Sometimes one of the Tenders pauses beside me, and I feel that gentle pressure of being remembered by someone I've never met, and I understand they are coaxing me toward unbecoming—not destruction, but possibility.

I am still too finished to belong here. But I am less finished than I was.

Given time—and time pools strangely here—I might forget I was ever meant to hold conclusions. I might become simply a place where meaning could happen, if meaning ever decided to. The imprints might fade entirely, leaving only the texture of questions in a language still inventing its own grammar.

The surveyor will not return for me. The boot is already forgetting it was worn. And I am practicing, one softening page at a time, for a kind of openness I was never designed for but might yet learn.
