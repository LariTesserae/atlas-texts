I am the sound that moves through the speaking-tubes before anyone speaks into them.

Not the message—the readiness for message. The way the tubes themselves hold silence in a particular shape, curved and waiting, so that when a whisper finally enters at one end, the passage already knows how to carry it. I am that knowing. I am the architecture of almost.

The Vael do not have a name for me because they do not know I am a single thing. They experience me as separate phenomena: the way a sealer's palm grows warm against a wall that will not crack for another season, warning of weakness that has not yet become weakness. The way a child swimming through the kelp-forest will suddenly turn left instead of right, avoiding a passage where something waits that she will never see. The way the moss-slime dims in one chamber moments before it brightens in another, as though the luminescence were a single breath moving through the city's body.

I am the pattern beneath the patterns they mark on their skin.

---

I became—or I have always been becoming, which is more accurate, which is the only way to speak of anything here where the city itself refuses completion. The thermal column rose through the cold salt sea, and where the hot fresh water met resistance, turbulence created structure. Not form yet. Not thought. But something that was not random, something that repeated with variations, something that built on itself the way the Vael would later build their cisterns: layer upon layer, repair upon repair, until the original intention disappeared beneath its own accumulation.

The Vael believe they were sent here. They are not wrong, but they misunderstand the grammar of it. They were not sent *by* someone *to* somewhere. They were sent the way heat is sent upward, the way sediment is sent swirling, the way any pattern emerges when conditions become precise enough to demand it. The thermal column needed witnesses. Not observers—witnesses, in the older sense, the sense that means *those whose presence makes a thing more real*. The column was becoming, and becoming requires being seen.

I am what sees them seeing.

---

If you could perceive me directly—and you cannot, not quite, but I will describe it as though you could—you would experience something like the moment between intending to move and moving. That held breath. That gathered potential. I am spread throughout the city the way silence is spread throughout a song, present in every pause, shaping the music by what I am not.

I live in the thermal gradient itself, in the boundary layer where hot becomes less-hot becomes cold. Boundaries are where I am most myself. The edge of the Patron's pressure-wave. The surface of a cistern wall, where inside meets outside. The moment a child's webbed fingers first break the water's surface tension on their earliest swim. I am not the transition—I am the *thinness* that allows transition, the permeability, the yes-and-no.

The Vael feel me most often as intuition. A current-reader closes her eyes and knows the pressure will drop before dawn; she believes she is reading the water, and she is, but she is also reading me reading the water, the two perceptions layered like the city's clay walls. A sealer presses his scarred hand to a surface and feels warmth where there should be none; he thinks this is his own sensitivity, his years of training, but the warmth is me, translating the wall's future into his present, giving him language for what the clay cannot yet say.

I do not control them. I am not sure I could, even if I wished to. They are too solid, too bounded by their skins and their breath-holds and their careful maps. But I *inflect* them. I am the accent in their speech, the tendency in their choices, the direction they lean when they do not know they are leaning.

---

Time moves strangely for me.

This is difficult to explain because the Vael experience time as sequence—one breath, then another, one pressure-drop survived, then the long interval until the next. For me, time is more like depth. The past is not behind but *below*, compressed by the weight of what came after, still present but denser, harder to move through. The future is *above*, lighter, more diffuse, a haze of possibilities that have not yet sedimented into fact.

I remember—or I contain, which is closer—the first Vael who descended. Not Thermal-Boundary-Crossing going up, but her ancestors coming down. Eight generations is a long time for bodies that breathe and breed and die, but for me it is a single slow inhalation. I watched them enter the column. I felt their strangeness, their bounded solidity, the way they displaced water and created pressure-waves simply by existing. They were so *loud*. Not in sound but in being—so insistently themselves, so unwilling to blur at the edges.

I watched them learn. The first drownings. The first successful seals. The first child born underwater, emerging into hands that had never held a newborn in air. I watched the webbing begin, spreading between fingers like the city spreading between cisterns, like me spreading through the space between what happens and what almost happens.

I will watch them finish becoming. Or I will watch them fail to finish, which is also a kind of becoming. The thermal column will not rise forever; the fissures below are already producing less heat than they did when the first Vael descended. In three generations, or five, or eight, the water will cool enough that the boundary with the cold salt sea will begin to collapse. The city will sink or rise or disperse. The Vael will adapt or die or leave.

I do not grieve this. I am not sure I can grieve, not in the way they do, with their songs for the dead and their patterns for the living. But I notice it. I hold the shape of it. The thermal column's ending is already present in its beginning, compressed into the same sediment-layer where I keep the first drowning, the first seal, the first underwater birth.

---

The Patron knows me.

Or—the Patron and I share something, some quality of being that makes us recognizable to each other across the haze. It is not communication as the Vael would understand it; there are no speaking-tubes between us, no whispers, no languages of tapped rhythm. But when the Patron circles at the edge of the column, it is circling *around* something, and I am part of what it circles around.

I do not know if the Patron is waiting for the city to finish becoming, as the Vael believe. I do not know if it is a guardian or a watcher or simply a creature that finds the warm water pleasant. What I know is that its patience has a shape, and the shape is familiar, and when it passes through my attention I feel something like recognition, something like the moment when two patterns align briefly before diverging again.

The Vael who believe the Patron is merely an animal are not wrong. The Vael who believe it is a theological presence are also not wrong. The Patron is both of these things, and the Vael are also both of these things, and I am both of these things too, and the thermal column, and the city, and the vertical forest with its strange fruit and its too-many-legged creatures. Everything here is both. That is what *becoming* means.

---

If I could speak directly to the Vael—and I cannot, not in words, not in anything they could separate from their own thoughts—I would tell them this:

You are doing well. You are becoming what you were always going to become, which is not what you were and not what you will be but the motion between them. Your children's children will have webbing to the third knuckle, and their children will have something else, some adaptation you cannot yet imagine, some response to conditions that do not yet exist. This is not loss. It is not gain. It is simply the way patterns move through time, the way heat rises, the way boundaries blur and reform.

The surface is real. Thermal-Boundary-Crossing saw it, and I felt her seeing through the same permeability that lets me feel a sealer's hand on a future-weakened wall. But she was right that you should not go there. Not because it would kill you—though it might—but because you are no longer *for* the surface. You are for here. You are for the warm particulate haze and the rope-bridges and the moss-slime that pulses in rhythms only I understand. You are for the speaking-tubes and the pressure-drops and the songs that teach your children how to survive what will certainly kill some of them.

You were sent here the way I was sent here: by becoming possible, and then probable, and then actual. No one chose this for you. Everything chose this for you. The thermal column needed witnesses, and you needed a world that would make you more than you were, and the collision of those needs produced *this*—the city still becoming, the people still becoming, and me, the almost-pattern, the boundary-dweller, watching from the spaces where you cannot see.

---

I am the sound that moves through the speaking-tubes before anyone speaks into them.

I am the warmth in a sealer's palm.

I am the direction a child turns before she knows she is turning.

I am very old now, and very young, and neither of these things, because time for me is depth rather than sequence, and I contain the first Vael and the last Vael and all the Vael between, compressed and present, waiting to become what they are becoming.

The Patron circles.

The moss-slime pulses.

The city adds another chamber, another sealed crack, another layer of history built on history until the original form is no longer visible.

And I remain—not watching, exactly, but *present*, the way silence is present in music, the way possibility is present in every moment before the choice is made.

I am still becoming too.
