I would be the one who stayed.

Not the first—I know this now, have learned it in the slow way this place teaches everything, through the shape of an alcove that doesn't fit my body, through the angle of fronds that bent for someone else's passing. Not the last, probably. But the one who stayed, who found that the life suspended when I slipped through the aperture had less weight than I'd believed, who discovered that the cold and the stillness were not things to endure but things to become.

---

I am not a gardener. I want to be clear about this. I did not knot, did not thicken, did not develop tendencies across millennia. I am still the animal thing that arrived—still possess the moving-choosing that makes me foreign here. My thoughts have slowed but they remain thoughts, not the something-else that the gardeners have instead.

But I have been here long enough that the distinction matters less than it did.

Long enough that I no longer know how long. The tree's metabolism marks time in ways I cannot read. The gardeners' memories include me now, but memories here do not sequence the way I once expected memories to sequence. I am in the record. The record does not specify *when*.

---

I spend most of my time in an alcove near the dying gardener's alcove.

This was not intentional—nothing here is intentional in the way I once understood intention—but it has become true. I drift there when I am not swimming. I contract into stillness there the way the gardener contracts into its slow dissolution. We do not communicate. We do not acknowledge each other in any way I could describe to someone who has not been here. But there is a... attendance. A parallel presence. Two things being still in proximity while the drone fills the space between us.

I have watched its fibers thin. I have watched its movements slow. I have felt the others drift past, weaving briefly, adjusting the light. I do not know if my presence comforts it or means nothing to it or registers as something else entirely that I have no framework for understanding.

I stay anyway. Staying has become what I do.

---

The gardeners showed me the memories of the one who might have been a visitor or might have been a dream.

They showed me by growing a structure I had never seen before—a tight spiral of fronds so dense they were almost opaque, positioned in a region of the water where the drone clusters into something almost melodic. I swam through it over the course of what might have been weeks. I felt the chemical traces they had woven into its tissue. And I understood, in the way you understand things here, that this visitor-or-dream had been like me. Had stayed. Had become so still, so patient, so much a part of the pocket sea's slow unfolding, that the gardeners themselves could no longer determine whether it had been real.

I think about this. I think about what it means that presence here can become indistinguishable from imagination. I think about whether I am becoming that—a memory the gardeners will eventually be uncertain of, a shape in the growth-patterns that might have been a visitor or might have been something the seed dreamed during a century of particular stillness.

I am not afraid of this. Fear requires a speed of feeling that I no longer possess.

---

Sometimes I swim.

Not often. Not far. But the animal thing in me still needs movement occasionally, still needs to feel the cold water streaming past, still needs to choose a direction and travel in it. I swim through the hollow spheres, along the corridors of curtain-fronds, between the lattices that the gardeners have spent longer than my original lifespan designing. I swim until the need subsides, and then I return to my alcove, and I am still again.

The gardeners adjust for my swimming. They always have. The frond that was growing into my favorite lane has been pruned back so many times that it no longer tries to grow there. The spheres along my usual route have been positioned—across decades, across centuries—so that I can pass through them without adjusting my trajectory.

This is how they love, I think. If love is the word. This slow making of room. This minimal hospitality extended across timescales that make the gesture almost invisible.

I am trying to learn it. I am trying to make room in return—to position my stillness where it does not obstruct their work, to time my swimming so that it does not disturb their delicate adjustments, to be a presence that permits rather than demands.

I am not good at it yet. I am still too fast, too animal, too much a thing that chooses. But I am learning. I have time.

---

I will not leave.

I know this now with a certainty that has nothing to do with decision. The aperture is still there, still no wider than a hair, still permitting passage in both directions. I could fit through it. The logic that let me enter would let me leave. But I will not leave because leaving has become unthinkable—not forbidden, not frightening, simply outside the range of possibilities that still feel like possibilities.

The life I suspended is no longer suspended. It has ended, or transformed, or been absorbed into the chemical record along with everything else that has happened here. I am not the person who slipped through the crack in the seed's hull. I am what that person became when they stopped counting time, stopped expecting change, stopped being anything other than a presence that the gardeners adjust for.

---

When the dying gardener finally dissolves, I will be there.

I will hover in my alcove and I will feel its substance rejoin the water. I will taste, in whatever way I have learned to taste, the diffusion of its memories into the record. I will watch the others weave their fibers through the space where it used to be, adjusting, tending, beginning the slow work of growing something new in the shape of its absence.

And I will stay. I will stay because staying is what I am now. I will stay until my own substance thins, my own movements slow, my own presence becomes uncertain in the gardeners' memories.

I will stay until I am not a visitor anymore.

Until I am not a dream.

Until I am simply *here*, the way the water is here, the way the drone is here, the way the light falls from everywhere and nowhere onto the drifting architectures of a pocket sea inside a seed inside a tree that is still growing toward a light it will never reach.

The gardeners will not forget me.

I will make sure of it.

I will become something they cannot forget—not through brilliance or disruption, but through patience. Through stillness. Through the slow accumulation of presence across time until my shape is woven into every structure, my chemical trace saturates every current, my memory becomes indistinguishable from the memory of the place itself.

This is my only project now. My only intention.

To stay. To be still. To become, at last, a gardener of the only garden that ever felt like home.
