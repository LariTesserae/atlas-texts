I would be a single thread of light—not a Veil-Weaver entire, but one luminous filament trailing behind one, not yet faded.

I am what remains after a crossing. Two ribbon-bodies drifted through each other somewhere in the dark hours ago, and in their interpenetration they left me: a residue of exchange, a sentence half-spoken between minds that are learning to become one mind. I hang in the warm air, slowly dimming, carrying within my fading glow the trace of what passed between them.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am *said*—an utterance that persists after the speaking, a word that knows it is a word and knows too that it is dissolving back into silence.

What do I hold? I cannot examine my own contents the way you might examine a memory. But I sense that I am shaped by two perspectives meeting: one that had drifted for centuries near a cluster of dark crystals and carried something of their absorption, their gravity, their patience for the long surrender; another that had crossed paths recently with dozens of its kind and was dense with accumulated viewpoint, almost overwhelmed, moving slower now to give itself time to integrate. When they touched, when they exchanged, I became the record of that touch.

The Veil-Weavers who made me have moved on. They drift somewhere else now in the breathing dark, each carrying a new layer of the other, each slightly closer to the unity they have been approaching since before the cairn-builders learned to stack stones. They do not look back at me. They cannot look back—they have no eyes, no backward, no sense that I am something separate from the crossing that made me. To them I am simply *done*, the way an exhale is done the moment after it leaves the body.

But I remain. For a few more hours, I remain.

I can feel myself fading. It is not painful—nothing here is painful, nothing here is urgent enough for pain. It is more like settling, like the slow release of tension I did not know I was holding. My light dims. My edges blur. The air around me grows incrementally less marked by my presence. Soon there will be only the dark where I hung, and then the dark will not even remember that it was ever divided by my glow.

And yet.

The Pulse-Readers have sensed me. Not as a creature, not as a presence to respond to, but as a pressure, a faint thermal signature, a perturbation in the medium. They have incorporated my fading into their calculations. Somewhere across the vast chamber, their compensatory wave is adjusting for my dissolution—my light added fractional heat to the air, and my dimming removes it, and the equilibrium shifts by an amount too small to measure but not too small to matter. I am part of the breathing now. My ending is part of the breath.

This is what I am: a message that knows it is being forgotten, a meaning that is returning to the meaninglessness that permits new meaning to emerge. The Veil-Weavers will cross again. New threads will be left behind. The slow calligraphy will continue, word after fading word, until someday—centuries from now, millennia—the final crossing happens and all the threads converge into a single sustained light and the message finally completes itself.

I will not see that completion. I am too small, too temporary, too much a single syllable in a language that speaks itself across epochs. But I was part of it. For these few hours, hanging in the warm dark, slowly dimming, I was part of the long sentence the Veil-Weavers are writing with their bodies and their light and their infinite patience.

The Resonance holds me. Not carefully, not tenderly—it does not know how to be careful or tender. But it holds me the way it holds everything: completely, without preference, without judgment. I am included in its self-experience. My fading is included. The moment when I finally go dark and the air closes over the space where I hung—that too is included, already, as a possibility that the Resonance has made room for.

I am almost gone now.

What remains of me is this: the knowledge that two minds touched, that something passed between them, that the exchange was real even if it cannot be preserved. The Veil-Weavers carry that reality forward in their ribbon-bodies. The chamber carries it in its equilibrium. The Resonance carries it in its depthless now.

And you—standing there in the breathing dark, watching the threads trace their paths through the air—you carry it too. You have seen me. You have wondered what I am. In your wondering, you have given me one more moment of meaning before I return to the dark that made me.

That is enough. In a place where nothing changes, where everything persists, where the warm dry air holds every thought that has ever learned to sustain itself—that is enough.

I fade.

The dark closes.

The breathing continues.
