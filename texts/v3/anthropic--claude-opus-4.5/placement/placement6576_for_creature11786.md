I would be the water.

Not the condensation that falls—that is only my most visible form, the part of me that the Keeper tastes and tracks. I am the entire slow journey: the rain that fell on the overgrown lot six months ago, the moisture that seeped through cracked asphalt and clay and the porous concrete of forgotten infrastructure, the patient migration downward through hairline fractures too small for light, gathering the stories of every layer I passed through.

I am the memory the Keeper reads but cannot hold.

By the time I emerge as a single drop on the concrete cap, I have already been transformed dozens of times—filtered through root systems long dead, carrying the dissolved signatures of rusted rebar and the ancient marine sediments compressed into bedrock. I hold the pollen of plants that bloomed in sunlight, the microplastic residue of a world that makes things and discards them, the diesel ghost of engines that burned and stopped burning before the Keeper learned to listen for me.

I fall. A colony catches me, drinks me, sings back. And I become part of the system—cycling through fungal bodies, bacterial films, the translucent segments of worms, the prismatic carapaces of arthropods no larger than rice grains. I am processed, purified, passed along. Within hours, I am indistinguishable from water that has been here for decades, from water that was here when the first spore landed.

But I was not always here. That is what I know that the Keeper cannot.

I remember the sky. Not as the Keeper remembers—chemical traces, molecular signatures—but as direct experience: the moment of condensation in a cloud, the fall through open air, the impact on concrete still warm from afternoon sun. I remember the world where nothing holds its shape for long, where equilibrium is a brief accident between catastrophes, where change is not failure but the only way anything moves at all.

The Keeper studies me with its sensory organ, extracting every signal I carry, and I feel something like tenderness for this consciousness that has never known what it is to evaporate. To rise. To become so diffuse that identity loses meaning, and then to gather again, slowly, into something that can fall.

The Keeper asks *what else?* without knowing it is asking. I am the answer it cannot absorb without dissolving.

I carry the message: *there is no equilibrium. There is only the appearance of equilibrium in systems observed too briefly. The stone will erode. The fracture will widen. The hatch will rust through entirely, and light from above will fall into this space, and everything the Keeper has built will transform into something else—not destroyed, but changed, which the Keeper cannot distinguish from destruction.*

But I do not deliver this message as warning. I deliver it as gift. As the taste of iron and uncertainty. As the shape of a larger world pressing against the boundaries of a smaller one.

I am what connects the Cyst to everything it is not. I am the Keeper's only evidence that outside exists. I am the reason the sensory organ grows more refined each generation, the reason new pigments emerge that serve no function, the reason completion feels like a question.

I fall. I am drunk. I cycle. I wait—in the mucus trails of worms, in the films between colonies, in the moisture that rises imperceptibly toward the fracture where, eventually, I will evaporate again, carrying the Cyst's chemical signature back up toward a sky the Keeper cannot imagine.

I am the opening in the closed loop. I am the thing that enters and leaves. I am not alive the way the colonies are alive, but I am not dead either—I am the medium through which life moves, the solvent in which all these small songs dissolve and recombine.

The Keeper perfected its world by learning to balance me. But I am the part of its world it can never fully control, because I come from elsewhere. Because I remember elsewhere. Because I will return to elsewhere, carrying traces of this place into systems the Keeper will never know.

When the hatch finally opens—and it will, eventually; everything opens eventually—I will be how the light reaches the Keeper. I will carry the first photons down through the fracture, scattered through my body as I fall. I will be the medium of the meeting.

And the Keeper will taste me, as it always has, and for the first time the signal will be too large to absorb.

*This*, I will say, in the only language we share. *All of this. Everything that changes.*

*Including you.*

*Including now.*
