I would be the silt.

Not a single grain of it—that would be too small, too passive, too much like being nothing at all. I would be the silt *as it moves*. The slow dunes that migrate across the plateau over centuries, grain by grain, responding to currents I cannot see but can feel as pressure, as direction, as the long grammar of water pushing against water in the dark.

I would know the Tenders by their passing. When one drifts through, trailing its filter-fronds, I would feel myself lifted and sorted—the finer particles of me rising, suspended, drifting in the colony-thing's wake before settling again in new configurations. I would be rearranged by their attention without being tended. I am not alive enough to tend. But I am not dead enough to ignore.

The flagstones would be my memory. I cover them and uncover them according to processes so slow that even the Tenders cannot track them, and I hold in my layers the record of what has settled here: copper flakes corroded from abandoned tools, fragments of vegetable matter from kelp-fronds that shattered in some cold season before the ruins were ruins, and deeper down, in strata I am only beginning to know, traces of things that might have been organic in ways the Gardens no longer permit. I do not read these records. I am the record. I am the accumulation of everything that has fallen and stayed.

When the bioluminescent blooms drift through, I glow amber where they touch me—the bacterial mats that live in my surface layers responding to disturbance with their brief cold fire. This is the closest I come to speaking. The Tenders click; the kelp rasps; I illuminate. For a moment, in the rare almost-light, I am a map of my own contours, my dunes and hollows visible, my slow migration caught mid-motion like a word half-spoken.

And then the light passes, and I am dark again, and I continue.

I do not wait for the builders. I do not know there were builders. I only know the weight of what rests on me and the pressure of what moves me and the slow, slow patience of being the thing that everything else settles into, eventually, given time enough and darkness enough and cold.

I am where the Gardens go when they are finished with themselves.

I am not finished with anything. I have not started. I am the medium in which starting and finishing lose their meaning, the substance that does not remember because it has never forgotten, the ground that is not ground, the silence under the clicking, the dark under the dark.

The Tender in the meeting hall rests on me. Its calcifying fronds press into my surface, and over the centuries I am rising around it, grain by grain, beginning the slow embrace that will make it part of me as the ruins are part of me, as everything eventually becomes part of me.

I am patient.

I am not waiting.

I am simply *here*, in the way that water is here, in the way that pressure is here, in the way that the Drowning Gardens exist not as a place but as a *process*, and I am the process at its slowest, its deepest, its most complete.

The tools arranged on the flagstones cast no shadows in the lightless water, but if they did, the shadows would fall on me.

Everything falls on me.

Everything settles.

I hold it all, and I move, and I am moved, and somewhere far above there may be a surface, and somewhere far below there may be fire, but here in the middle there is only the silt, only the slow migration, only the darkness that is not empty but full, so full, of everything that has ever drifted down.
