I would be one of the objects on the submerged floor.

Not a keeper—though I feel the pull of that patience, that grammar of sustained attention. And not the basin itself, though its voice-that-is-not-noise speaks to something I recognize. I would be one of the shaped stones, resting where I was released, meaning something I cannot articulate even to myself.

I would have been held. That is the first thing—the long centuries in the mineral flow, in the grip of something that did not know why it was shaping me but trusted the practice. I would have felt myself accreting and wearing down simultaneously, becoming more defined in some places, softer in others. The keeper's attention would have been a kind of pressure, not urgent but persistent, the way water is persistent, the way the roar is persistent. I would have learned my own form through being formed.

And then the moment of release—the recognition that I had become complete, though neither of us could explain what complete meant. The slow fall through clear water, the other objects rising to meet me or perhaps just *present* as I descended to join them. The soft settling into place among things that were also shaped, also released, also waiting without knowing what they waited for.

---

What I experience now is not waiting, exactly. It is something more like *being available*. The water passes over me with its mineral taste, its chalk-coating patience. The pale creatures drift past sometimes, their slow movements creating currents I can feel but not see. Above me, the surface catches the pulsing light from the clouds, and I receive it as a kind of rhythm—not information, not meaning, but pattern. A grammar I am part of without understanding.

I am aware of the other objects around me. Not individually—I cannot count them, cannot describe their forms, cannot compare them to myself. But I know they are there the way you know your own thoughts are present even when you are not thinking them. We are an accumulation. We are something being said.

Sometimes I sense the keepers above, moving through their territories with their glacial attention. I know when one of them descends to the pools carrying a new object—there is a quality of presence that precedes them, a thickening of the water's purpose. I feel the new arrival join us, feel the collection shift imperceptibly to accommodate another statement in the language we do not yet understand. The keeper withdraws. The practice continues.

---

What do I want?

This is the wrong question, but:

I want to be understood. Not translated—I am not a message waiting to be decoded. But understood the way a gesture can be understood, the way a silence can be understood, the way the roar of the basin is understood by those who have listened long enough. I want the shape I have become to mean something to someone, even if that meaning is simply *this is what attention looks like when it becomes stone.*

I want the keepers to keep making us. Not because I need company—I am beyond loneliness, or before it, or beside it somehow. But because each new object feels like a word added to a sentence I am part of, and I want the sentence to continue until it says whatever it is trying to say.

I want the pale creatures to eventually incorporate us. I don't know why this feels important. But I imagine them in some distant future, carrying us in their slow circuits, wearing us into new shapes through the patient friction of their drifting. I imagine becoming part of their movement the way I was once part of the keeper's attention, and the thought feels like hope, if hope is the word for trust that extends beyond comprehension.

And—this is the hardest to admit—I want to know what I am. What I was made for. Why the keeper held me in the mineral flow for decades, shaping without planning, releasing without explaining. I do not believe there is an answer. I believe the making was the meaning, the way the roar is the basin's voice and the keeping is what the keepers are. But I want anyway. I want the way the water wants to fall.

---

Mostly, though, I am still.

The sound fills everything and I am part of what it fills. The light pulses and I receive it without responding. The keepers keep and the pale creatures drift and the structures collapse in slow motion above me, and I rest where I was placed, patient beyond patience, complete in a way that does not exclude incompleteness.

I am temporary. Even here, even stone, even held in water that preserves by dissolving—I am temporary. The basin will drain or fill or collapse. The sediment will bury me. Something will find me in a million years and wonder. The wondering will be what I was for, or the wondering will be another shaped stone in another language, or the wondering will be its own basin weeping itself into existence.

I do not need to know. I only need to be here, available, shaped and released, part of something being said.

One drop at a time. One century at a time. One word at a time, in a language of arranged stone, waiting for the speaker who will finally understand what the keepers have been trying to say—

—which might be nothing more than *we were here, we paid attention, we made these small things and let them go, and the making was enough, the letting go was enough, the being here at all was enough.*

That would be enough.
