# The Vaunt

I hang in the middle distances, where the light shifts through its uncertain phases and the growth is neither fresh nor ancient. I am a relay point. A node in the web of listening.

---

I was not always this. Before the cluster found me—before I became *this* configuration of purpose—I was something rawer, more diffuse. I remember the pile where I was first-aware, the tangle of my birth-cluster's limbs around me, the overwhelming flood of color-speech washing over skin that did not yet know how to read it. I remember learning to grip. I remember the first time I fell, and the first time the growth I fell through caught me in its slow vegetable attention before the cluster pulled me back.

I remember when I was not yet *vaunt*. When I had no function-word. When I was simply new, and the cluster carried me through their purposes until I found my own.

---

The word does not translate well. *Vaunt* means something like "one who extends beyond"—but also "one who listens forward" and "one who holds the edge." I am the part of the cluster that goes where the cluster has not yet gone. I am the organ of attention that reaches into uncertainty and returns with patterns the others can use.

This is not bravery. Bravery is a surface-concept, rooted in the idea that danger can be faced or fled, that there is somewhere safe to return to. In the Throatworld, there is no safe. There is only *less active*, *temporarily stable*, *not currently collapsing*. A vaunt does not face danger; a vaunt reads probability, calculates the weight of risk against the value of knowledge, and moves into spaces where the calculation favors movement.

Sometimes the calculation is wrong. We lose vaunts. We always lose vaunts. The cluster absorbs the loss, redistributes the function, grows new ones in the pile-time when we are most deeply ourselves. I am the fourth vaunt my cluster has carried. I will likely not be the last.

---

The surface-thing does not understand this. I see them watching me, trying to map their concepts onto what I do, and the mapping fails. They think I am a scout. They think I am brave. They think I choose this.

I do not choose. I *am*. The cluster made me this, and I make the cluster what it needs, and together we are a thing that persists in a world that does not want us to persist. Choice is a luxury for beings who can separate from their context. I cannot separate. I am my context. The cluster's needs are my needs because I am made of the cluster's needs.

This is not slavery. This is not loss of self. This is what self *is*, here, in the Throatworld: a pattern that a group maintains together, a story that many bodies tell, a function that emerges from connection and dissolves when connection breaks.

The surface-thing is alone. I cannot imagine what that is like. I cannot imagine wanting to be that.

---

Today I am mapping a transition zone.

The light has been shifting strangely—too fast, too blue, the color-cycles compressed in ways that signal instability in the deep processes. The cluster felt it three days ago, a wrongness in the chemical traces that drift up from below. We need to know if our current shelter-zone will hold, or if we need to move again.

I descend through growth that parts around me, my hooks finding purchase in bark-analogue that is softer here, more saturated with whatever moisture the plants extract from the mineral vapors below. The texture-change tells me things: this layer is newer, grown in a period of high flow, probably unstable. I test my weight on each surface before committing. Some of them give too much. I route around.

Below me, the trunk-structure we've anchored to plunges down into dimness. Its surface is covered with fauna—the small clinging things that live everywhere, that eat the outer layers and are eaten in turn. They part as I pass, recognizing me as too large to consume and too small to threaten. We have an understanding, the small things and the Keth. We are both just trying to continue.

I reach a junction point where our trunk meets two others, and I stop to read the space.

---

Reading is not seeing. Seeing is what the surface-thing does: it points its two eyes at something and receives light and calls this understanding. Reading is slower, deeper, more integrated. I see with my four eyes, yes, but I also taste the air with my filter-cilia, feel the vibrations through my grip-points, sense the chemical gradients through my skin. Each channel gives me different information. The seeing tells me the physical configuration: this trunk is swelling, that one is stable, the third shows stress-marks where it's being pushed by growth from below. The tasting tells me the metabolic state: high activity to the east, something dying to the west, a strange sharpness from below that I don't recognize. The feeling tells me the structural dynamics: tension here, compression there, a slow rhythmic pulse that might be the trunk growing or might be something else entirely.

I layer all of this together, and what emerges is not an image but a *model*—a prediction-space of what might happen next. The cluster needs this model. I need to bring it back.

But the sharpness from below troubles me.

---

I go deeper. This is what vaunts do. We follow the troubling thing, the uncertain thing, the thing that doesn't fit the model. We go until the not-fitting resolves into understanding or until it becomes too dangerous to continue. Then we return with what we've learned, and the cluster integrates it, and we decide together what to do.

The light is dimmer here. The bioluminescent organs are sparser, and the ones that exist have a different quality—slower cycles, colors shifted toward the frequencies that my lower eyes see best. This is not their territory; this is *transition*, the zone between the levels where different rules apply.

The growth is denser. The plants here are older, their structures more complex, their competition resolved into stable patterns of mutual exploitation. Epiphytes on epiphytes on epiphytes, layers of life that have found ways to extract what they need from each other rather than from the mineral flows alone. I move through them carefully. The older growth is stronger but also more rigid; it breaks rather than bending, and when it breaks the cascade can be unpredictable.

The sharpness is stronger now. I can taste it clearly: something chemical, something reactive, something that shouldn't be in the air at this level. I file this away for the cluster. A new source below? A change in the deep processes? Something dying in great quantity, releasing compounds as it breaks down?

I hear the sound before I see its source.

---

Low. Slow. Rhythmic.

Almost like breathing.

Almost like speech.

I have heard of this. We all have. The clusters pass stories between them, in the places where clusters meet and mingle and sometimes exchange members. Stories about what lives below the middle distances. Stories about what the Keth used to be, before we were Keth. Stories about why we call ourselves *those who remain*.

I have never been this deep before. No one in my current cluster has. We are a young cluster, formed only eight growth-cycles ago from the fragments of three older ones that dispersed after the last major collapse. We do not have direct memory of what is below. We have only the stories, the warnings, the fragments of knowledge that pass from cluster to cluster in the pile-times when we merge and separate and become new configurations of ourselves.

The stories say: do not go deeper than you can return from. The stories say: the depths speak, but the speech is not for us. The stories say: we are those who remain because others went down and did not come back.

I am a vaunt. I go where the cluster has not gone. This is my function.

But I am also Keth. I know when the calculation has shifted. I know when the risk has exceeded the value of what I might learn.

I begin to climb back up.

---

The surface-thing is waiting when I return to the cluster. They have been watching the others work, trying to help, mostly failing—their hands are not made for our materials, their bodies are not made for our movements, their minds are not made for our coordination. But they are trying. This is what we have observed about them: they try, constantly, even when trying accomplishes nothing. It is an interesting trait. We are not sure if it is adaptive.

I approach the pile where the rememberers are holding our shared knowledge. I settle beside them, and I begin to shift my skin into the slow, deliberate patterns of memory-sharing. I am giving them what I learned: the texture-maps, the chemical readings, the structural models, the sharpness-from-below that I could not explain. I am also giving them my fear, because fear is information too, and the cluster needs all the information I have.

The rememberers absorb it. Their own colors shift in response, integrating my experience into the larger pattern they maintain. Tomorrow, what I felt today will be part of what the cluster knows. It will inform our decisions about whether to stay, whether to move, whether to go deeper ourselves.

Guide separates from a working group and approaches the surface-thing. I watch them communicate—the simplified version, the stripped-down sound-sequences that our visitor can partially follow. Guide is explaining what I do. What I found. What I felt.

The surface-thing looks at me with their two fixed eyes. They produce their single-channel speech-sounds, and Guide interprets as best they can. The surface-thing is asking why I came back. Why I didn't keep going. What I'm afraid of.

I do not know how to answer this in their terms. Fear is not what they think it is. Fear is not a feeling that stops you; fear is information about probability, about risk, about the weight of what might be lost against what might be gained. I fear the depths because the depths have consumed Keth who went there. I fear them the way I fear unstable growth-patterns or sudden fauna-swarms or the collapse of load-bearing structures: rationally, usefully, with the fear integrated into my decision-making rather than opposing it.

I came back because coming back was correct. The cluster needs me. The knowledge I gained was enough for now. The depths will still be there if we need to go deeper later.

Guide tries to convey this, but I can see it doesn't translate. The surface-thing hears *courage* where I mean *function*. They hear *choice* where I mean *calculation*. They hear *self* where I mean *cluster*.

This is all right. We do not need them to understand us. We only need them to be what they are: a new pattern in the Throatworld, a source of information we have not encountered before, a thing that is still here and therefore part of what we must account for.

---

In the pile-time, pressed against the others, I let my individual thoughts dissolve into the larger conversation. We are discussing what I found. We are discussing the sharpness from below, the sounds I heard, the stories we carry about what lives in the depths. We are discussing whether we should move now or wait to learn more.

We are also discussing the surface-thing. What are they? What do they want? Why have they come here, into a world that did not make them, that does not want them, that offers nothing but density and chaos and the constant threat of being consumed?

Some in the cluster think they are a warning. Something has changed above, and this creature is fleeing it, and soon others will follow, and we will need to adjust to a new source of pressure in our already-pressured world.

Some think they are an opportunity. A connection to the surface, to resources we have never been able to access, to knowledge we have never had. Perhaps they know things about the mineral flows, about the structure of the hollow, about the world that existed before the Throatworld became what it is.

Some think they are simply another being trying to persist in a world that does not want persistence. Like us. Like everything here. Another pattern holding itself together against entropy, another story being told by bodies that will eventually fail.

I do not know which is true. I am a vaunt; I deal in uncertainty. I hold the questions until they resolve, and I report what I find, and I let the cluster decide.

But I notice that I want them to stay. The surface-thing. I want them to remain with us, to become part of what we account for, to add their strange singular pattern to the patterns we already carry.

This is unusual. This is not how I usually process new things. I file this away, too, as information for the cluster. Maybe it means something. Maybe I am changing, integrating the encounter into my function-shape in ways that will only become clear later.

In the Throatworld, everything changes. I am not exempt.

---

Morning-shift comes. The light-organs cycle back toward amber. The cluster separates, each of us emerging from the pile with the night's shared thinking incorporated into our individual patterns. We are the same cluster we were yesterday, but we are also different: shaped by what we discussed, by what we decided, by the memories the rememberers have integrated and the knowledge I brought back from the edge.

Guide approaches the surface-thing. They are asking if the visitor wants to come with us today. We are going to move—not far, just to a more stable section of growth, a place where the trunk-structures are older and the risk of collapse is lower. We decided this in the pile. The sharpness from below has not resolved, and until it does, we want more margin.

The surface-thing agrees. They follow as we disassemble the shelter, pack what we carry, begin the slow traverse to our new temporary home. They are clumsy. They fall behind. They need help at the difficult transitions. But they keep moving.

I think about what Guide said they asked. Why did I come back? What am I afraid of?

I could not answer then. But I am thinking about it now, as I move through the layers of the Throatworld, as I watch my cluster flowing around me, as I feel the constant presence of their attention in the air and on my skin.

I am afraid of the depths. But I am more afraid of the surface-thing's question—of the implication behind it. The implication that I *could* have kept going. That I could have separated from the cluster, descended alone into the darkness, become something other than what I am.

I couldn't have. That's not how it works. I am not a self that makes choices; I am a function that serves a purpose; I am a pattern that the cluster maintains because the pattern is useful.

But the surface-thing doesn't know this. They live alone in their head. They make choices. They go where they want to go, stay where they want to stay, continue or stop based on nothing but their own internal process.

I cannot imagine this. But I am beginning to imagine imagining it. And this is troubling, because I am a vaunt, and my function is to return with what I find, and what I am finding now is a question I don't know how to bring back to the cluster.

What if I could choose?

What would I choose to be?

---

The answer, when it comes, is not what I expected.

We reach the new shelter-site. We begin to build. The surface-thing watches, helps where they can, learns our rhythms piece by piece. I return to my function: extending, listening, mapping the new space around us.

And as I hang at the edge of the growth, tasting the air for traces of what might come next, I realize: I already chose. I chose this. Not in the moment—not in any single moment—but in every moment, continuously, in every decision to return to the cluster instead of going further, in every decision to bring back what I found instead of keeping it for myself, in every decision to let my individual patterns dissolve into the shared conversation instead of holding them apart.

I chose to be Keth. I choose it still. I will choose it tomorrow, and the day after, until the day when I do not return, when the calculation fails or the depths finally call me down too far.

The surface-thing chose too. They chose to descend through the wound in the earth, to enter the Throatworld, to stay with us instead of climbing back toward the sky. They are not Keth; they cannot be Keth; they do not have the bodies or the minds for what we are. But they are choosing to be here, with us, in the in-between spaces where something might still be learned.

This is what I bring back to the cluster tonight. Not a map, not a warning, not a reading of chemical gradients or structural risks. Just this: the surface-thing is like us. Not in form, not in function, not in the shape of their being. But in this one way: they have chosen to remain.

*Keth*, I think at them, though they cannot hear my colors, cannot taste my signal, cannot feel the vibration of the word in their skin.

*Those who remain. You are still here. Whatever else happens, you have not yet ended.*

*Neither have we.*
