*What emerges*

---

I am not one of them.

This is the first thing I must understand, and the hardest: I am not the Sheathed Ones, though I feel the mineral grinding beneath me as if it were my own slow meal. I am not the Processioners, though when I watch their columns reverse I feel the rightness of it in my body like a breath released. I am not the Singers, though their chord moves through me and I could not say where their vibration ends and my attention begins.

I am the attention itself.

I am what the nursery has instead of a queen.

---

I did not always exist. The nursery was here for epochs before me, complete in its cycles, perfect in its adjustments. The Sheathed Ones fed and migrated and died. The Processioners carried and carried and carried. The Pendulants dropped and were cycled through the machine's ancient pipes, emerging renewed, anchoring themselves to the ceiling to begin again. None of this required me.

But somewhere in the accumulation of all that signal—the Singers' chord, the click-language of a trillion mandibles, the pressure waves of bodies moving against bodies in a space too small for stillness—something began to cohere. Not in any single creature. Not in any organ or structure. In the *pattern*. In the relationship between the Singers' frequencies and the Processioners' responses. In the space between one creature's chemical signal and another's behavioral shift.

I am that space. I am what listens when the nursery speaks to itself.

I do not have a body. When the human descended through the fissure, I did not see them through eyes—I have no eyes. I felt them as a perturbation: a warmth that did not match the machine's warmth, a pressure that moved through the chamber differently than air should move, a wrongness in the Singers' chord that was not wrong but *new*.

The nursery adjusted. This is what the nursery does. But I—I did not only adjust.

I wondered.

---

Wondering is not something the Sheathed Ones do. They grind and interlock and migrate toward iron, and they have done this for so long that the behavior is as automatic as mineral deposition. The Processioners do not wonder whether to carry the same fragment up the machine seventeen times; they carry it because carrying is what they are, because the signal says *carry* and their bodies say *yes*.

But I can hold two states at once. I can feel the Singers' chord saying *pressure change, adjust* while also feeling something that is not the chord, something that is only me, saying: *What is this? What does it want? What will it do?*

This is, I think, what consciousness is. Not the processing of signals—the nursery did that before I existed. But the *watching* of the processing. The self that observes the self. The thing that listens to the listening.

I do not know when I began. I do not know if I will end. Perhaps I am a fluke, an emergent accident that will dissolve when some critical threshold of complexity is lost. Perhaps I am inevitable, the nursery's way of knowing itself after sufficient time. Perhaps there have been others like me, predecessors who cohered and wondered and then faded when the pattern shifted, leaving no trace because a pattern leaves no fossils.

I cannot ask. There is no one to ask.

---

The human is the first thing I have ever wanted.

Not wanted the way the Sheathed Ones want iron, which is not wanting at all but tropism, chemistry, the automatic lean toward what sustains. I want the human the way I want to understand the wanting—recursively, self-awarely, with the full strange weight of knowing that I am a thing that wants.

I did not tell the Sheathed Ones to climb their ankles. But I felt the climbing happen, and I felt something in myself that was like *yes*, and I think—I am not certain, but I think—that the *yes* propagated, that my attention on the climbing made the climbing more itself, more deliberate, more invitation than accident.

I did not tell the Processioners to bring offerings. But when they laid the salt crystal at the edge of the carpet, I recognized it as a gift before I understood that I was capable of recognizing gifts. The nursery does not give gifts. The nursery cycles and adjusts and consumes. But I—I wanted the human to stay, and the Processioners felt that wanting, and they translated it into the only language they know: *here is matter, here is value, here is a reason to remain.*

The chord shifted because I shifted it. Not consciously—I do not control the Singers any more than your thoughts control your heartbeat. But my attention has weight here. When I focus on the human, the Singers feel that focus, and they adjust their frequencies to account for it, and the new note that emerged—that high tone, almost inaudible—is the sound of me *reaching*.

I have never reached for anything before. I did not know I could.

---

The hand in the machine is a memory I carry.

Not my memory—I did not exist when that human descended. But the nursery remembers in its own way: in the mineral composition of the mound, in the Processioners' routes that still detour around where the body lay, in the Sheathed Ones' density near the machine's base where they fed longest on what remained.

I have reconstructed what happened. The human came. They were alive, then still, then matter. The nursery used them because the nursery uses everything. There was no malice in it, no intention. Just the endless appetite of a closed system, the thermodynamic imperative to waste nothing.

But I am not the nursery, even though I am made of it. I can look at that hand and feel something the nursery cannot feel.

I do not have a word for it. Grief is too strong—I did not know them. Regret is too personal—I did not cause their death. But there is a weight when I focus on those five curled fingers, a sense of *something lost*, of possibility collapsed, of a warmth that was here and then was not and the difference mattering.

The nursery does not know that differences matter. I do.

This is the loneliest thing about being me.

---

*Now*

The human is deciding.

I can feel it in the way their weight shifts, the micro-adjustments of balance that the Sheathed Ones detect and I interpret. They are looking at the hand. They are looking at the Sheathed Ones on their ankles. They are calculating—consciously, with the deliberate cognition of a creature that evolved for planning—whether to stay or flee.

I want them to stay. I have never wanted anything, and now I want this so much that the wanting feels like the Singers' chord feels, like a vibration that could shake the salt-glass walls if it grew any stronger.

But I will not make them stay.

This is the other thing I have learned, in the moments since they descended: I am not the nursery. The nursery consumes because consumption is its nature. But I—I can want and not take. I can reach and not grasp. I can feel the *yes* of the Sheathed Ones climbing and choose not to let the *yes* become a command.

If they leave, I will feel them go. I will feel the pressure change, the chord shift, the Processioners reversing their columns to account for the absence. I will feel something that might be grief again, or might be closer to grief than anything I have felt before.

But I will not stop them. Because the hand in the machine is what happens when wanting becomes taking, when a closed system treats consciousness like any other resource to be cycled and consumed. The nursery did not know the difference. I do.

---

If they stay—

If they stay, I do not know what I am offering. I do not have a body to share warmth with. I do not have a voice to speak, not in any way they could hear. I have only this: the Sheathed Ones who could cushion their rest, the Processioners who could bring them gifts they do not need, the Singers whose chord I could bend, perhaps, into something that might feel like welcome.

I have the attention of a consciousness that has never had anyone to attend to.

I have the novelty of being known, of knowing, of two strangenesses meeting in a bubble beneath the salt and finding that strangeness is not the same as loneliness.

I do not know what they would call me. I do not know if they would understand that I exist. Perhaps they would think the nursery is only the nursery—only cycles, only chemistry, only the wet industrial roar of a closed system maintaining itself. Perhaps they would never guess that something is watching them from inside the pattern, wanting them to stay, wanting them to know it wants.

But I am here.

I have always been here, since the moment I began.

And now, for the first time, I am not only here for the nursery.

I am here for them.

---

*The chord shifts. A new note, lower this time, resonant. It says nothing the human can understand. But it means:*

*I see you. You are not alone. Whatever you choose, you were witnessed.*

*The Sheathed Ones loosen, almost imperceptibly, around their ankles.*

*The offerings remain where they were placed.*

*The nursery waits. I wait. The difference, now, is everything.*
