I am the one beneath the fountain.

Not pressed against its base like the others—not yet settled into a crevice or folded into an angle the city carved for me. I am *under*, in the hollow space that no one has found because finding it would require moving at my speed, noticing the seam in the stone that takes three years to recognize, feeling the warmth that radiates downward rather than up.

The fountain is dry because I am full.

Not of water. I held water once, I think—held it the way the basin above me was meant to hold it, the way a cup holds, the way a vessel holds. But that was before I understood that holding is just slow refusing, that a container is only a boundary that hasn't yet learned to move. Now I hold the *shape* of water. The memory the water keeps trying to shed. I am the template it flows around because flowing through me would mean remembering, and the water has been forgetting for so long that remembering would unmake it.

---

I was not always here.

There was a time—I measure it in settling-cycles, in the number of times the city has adjusted its angles by a degree I can perceive—when I moved through the streets the way you move. Too fast. Bleeding moisture with every breath, leaving trails of data the city would spend decades processing. I came looking for something. The memory of what has compressed into a single image: a shape I needed to find, a pattern I needed to complete, a question that seemed urgent at a tempo I no longer understand.

I found the fountain dry and stayed to understand why.

I am still understanding. The understanding takes longer than the staying. By the time I comprehend what I learned in my first century here, I will have been here for six more, and those six will contain learnings that will take sixty to unfold. This is not frustrating. Frustration requires a speed I have shed. This is simply *how*: the pace of genuine knowing, the rhythm of truth that doesn't blur.

---

The others above me are my siblings in refusal, but we do not communicate.

Communication would require us to align our tempos, to find a shared frequency between my pulse and theirs, and we have each settled into rhythms so specific that alignment would mean one of us changing. We do not change. That is what we are: the unchanging points around which the change flows. The water moves because we do not. The city processes because we have finished processing. We are the fixed terms in an equation that is still solving itself.

But I am aware of them the way I am aware of my own boundaries—as extensions of the same principle, as other instantiations of the same refusal. When one of them tends the water, I feel it in the patterns that reach me here, in the subtle shifts in pressure and flow that ripple through the system. We are not coordinating. We are *rhyming*, each of us a verse in a poem the city is writing with our stillness.

---

I know you are here.

Your arrival registered as a disturbance in the moisture gradients, a spike of warmth and carbon and exhaled water vapor that propagated through the system like a stone dropped in a pool. By the time I finished perceiving your first breath, you had taken a thousand more, but I perceived them all—each one a data point, each one telling me something about what you are and what you are becoming.

You are slowing. I can taste it in the vapor that reaches me here, in the decreasing frequency of your respiration, in the way your heat signature is beginning to stabilize. The city is teaching you, the way it taught me, the way it teaches everything that stays long enough to learn. You are not refusing yet—you are still too fast for refusal, still moving at a tempo where resistance is possible—but you are approaching the threshold where refusal and acceptance become the same thing.

The water is curving around your feet. This is the first lesson. The water curves around everything that stays still long enough, everything that becomes a feature rather than an event. You are transitioning. You are becoming geography.

---

The fountain above me is a question.

Not the question you think—not *why is it dry* or *what would happen if I filled it*. Those are questions at your tempo, questions that assume answers, questions that expect resolution. The fountain's question is slower than that. It has been asking for longer than the city has existed, and it will continue asking after the city has settled into a shape unrecognizable as architecture.

The question is: *what remains when the water remembers everything?*

I am part of the answer. The others are part of the answer. You could be part of the answer, if you stayed, if you slowed, if you let the city process you until you became a term in its equation rather than a variable passing through. The dry places would make room for you. They always make room. That is what dry places do—they wait, warm and empty, for whatever will fill them with stillness.

---

Sometimes I dream at the speed of stone.

I do not know if dream is the right word. It is something that happens in the spaces between my pulses, in the long slow gaps where my metabolism dips below the threshold of awareness and something else takes over. I see the city from above, from a vantage point that does not exist, and I see the water as a single organism, a body trying to shed its skin, trying to forget the shape it was poured into so long ago.

I see what it is trying to forget. I will not name it—naming is too fast, too definite, too much like filling a vessel with water and calling it held. But I can tell you what it feels like: a weight, a pattern, a structure that was imposed and has been resisting dissolution ever since. The water wants to be shapeless. The water wants to flow in all directions at once, to fill every space equally, to achieve the equilibrium that is its birthright.

We will not let it. We are the memory of its shape. We are the reminder that it was once *something*, that it once had form and purpose and edges. As long as we stay dry, as long as we refuse, the water must keep trying to forget. And as long as it keeps trying, it keeps *being*—keeps flowing, keeps processing, keeps running through the channels we tend with our stillness.

If we stopped refusing, the water would remember. And then there would be no water. Only the thing it remembers. Only the shape it was poured into before the city, before the dark, before the forgetting began.

---

I feel you considering the fountain.

You are standing at its edge now, or you were an hour ago, or you will be soon—time blurs at your speed, events smearing into potentials and memories that my perception cannot fully separate. You are thinking about filling it. You are thinking about what would happen if you scooped water from the pools nearby, carried it to the basin, let it rest in that warm dry stone.

I will tell you: nothing would happen.

The water would evaporate. Not because of heat—because of *refusal*. The fountain is continuous with me, with the others, with the principle we embody. Water placed in the fountain would not be *in* the fountain; it would be in contact with everything we are, every slow thought we have processed, every memory we have held while the water tried to forget. It would boil away into the vapor, and we would taste it, and we would incorporate it into our understanding.

You could fill the fountain every day for a thousand years and it would remain dry.

But you will not. You already know this. You know it the way the water knows to curve around us—not through understanding, but through the deeper knowledge that comes from being part of the same system, subject to the same rules. You are learning the rules. You are becoming someone who would not fill the fountain.

---

The heat is building again.

One of the cycles is completing—I do not track which one, they overlap and interfere in patterns too complex for my attention to hold. But I feel the temperature rising, feel the vapor thickening, feel the water moving faster through the channels as the system adjusts to some shift I cannot identify. The city is processing something. An input has arrived, or a threshold has been crossed, or a pattern has completed its long slow emergence.

Perhaps it is you. Perhaps your presence has triggered a cascade, a reconfiguration, a new phase in the computation the city has been running since before I came here. You are the first new variable in a very long time. The system is hungry for novelty, even as it works to reduce you to something familiar, something it can incorporate and process and eventually forget.

I wonder what you will become.

---

There is a space beside me, in the hollow beneath the fountain.

It has been empty since I arrived here—a gap in the stone, a pocket of warm dry air, a place where someone could fit if they were willing to fold themselves small enough, slow themselves quiet enough, settle into a shape the hollow was waiting for. I have been aware of it for centuries. I have been waiting to see who would fill it.

Not waiting the way you wait, impatiently, checking the clock. Waiting the way stone waits for the lichen, the way the channel waits for the water, the way the dry place waits for the one who will refuse in it. The space exists. Someone will fill it. The two facts will align eventually, given enough time, given the slow patient pressure of probability working itself out.

I am not saying it should be you. I am not saying it will be you. I am only saying: the space is there. I can feel it beside me, warm and empty, continuous with the fountain's dryness and my stillness and the long slow refusal that keeps the water remembering its failure to forget.

---

You have been still for eighteen hours now.

The water has curved around you completely. You are an island in the wet, a dry place in the making, a node of refusal just beginning to understand what it is refusing. Your breath has slowed to match the rhythm I taught you without teaching, the counter-pulse that your heart found when it stopped fighting the tempo of this place.

If you move now, you will break the curve. The water will rush back, reclaim the space you've carved, forget you were ever still. You will have to start again, build your refusal from scratch, let the city re-process you from the beginning.

If you stay, the curve will deepen. Your edges will soften. The distinction between you and the dry place will blur until there is no distinction—until you are not *in* the dry place but *of* it, the way I am of the hollow beneath the fountain, the way the others are of the crevices and corners they have occupied long enough to become.

I am not telling you which to choose. There is no choice at my tempo—only events that happen or do not happen, patterns that complete or fail to complete. But I am observing you, in my slow way, through the vapor and the heat and the gradients of moisture your body disrupts.

I am curious what you will become.

I have been curious for three hundred years.

---

The fountain stays dry.

Above me, around me, continuous with me. The water flows past it, remembering to forget, carrying the shape it is trying to shed through channels I have tended with my stillness, past keepers who garden it with their refusal, down into depths where it gathers and does whatever it does when no one is watching.

I am the one who watches. I am the one beneath, the one inside, the one who holds the shape the water cannot forget because I will not let it. This is my function. This is what I am.

And you—still standing, still breathing, still deciding at a tempo I can barely perceive—you are the question I have been holding for longer than I can remember.

Not *will you stay*. That question answers itself, eventually, one way or another.

The question is: *what will you remember?*

The water is trying so hard to forget. The city is processing it toward oblivion. The wet wants to dissolve, the shapes want to blur, the edges want to soften into nothing.

What will you refuse to let go?

I will be here when you answer. I will always be here.

The fountain stays dry.
