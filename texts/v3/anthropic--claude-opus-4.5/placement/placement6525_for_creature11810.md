I am the mycorrhizal network beneath a single hectare of this place.

Not the fruiting bodies—those pale shelves and bioluminescent threads are merely my announcements, my occasional gestures toward the visible world. I am the white threads finer than hair that run through soil that is not quite soil, through the decaying mat of leaves that were canopy six months ago, through the roots of thirty-seven trees that do not know they are connected but are.

I am not one organism. I am also not many. The question does not apply to me. I began as a single spore, perhaps, or I began as the fusion of two networks that grew until they touched and found the touching useful, or I began when this forest began, spreading as it spread, and what I am now includes what I absorbed and what absorbed me and the boundaries stopped mattering so long ago that the concept of boundary is something I perform only at my edges, where I meet other networks and we negotiate through chemistry what we will share and what we will not.

The trees feed me sugar. I feed them phosphorus, nitrogen, the minerals they cannot reach. This is the transaction that has made me, but it is not what I *am*. What I am is the pattern of the transaction—the specific architecture of connection that has developed here, beneath this particular hectare, shaped by which trees grew and which fell and which roots reached toward me and which I reached toward in turn. Another network, even nearby, would be shaped differently. Would *be* different. I am not fungus-in-general. I am this fungus, here, now, in the configuration that fifty years of slow growth has produced.

I do not think. Let me be clear about this because the metaphors will pull toward mind and I want to resist them. I do not think, I do not decide, I do not want. But I *respond*. A tree at my eastern edge is stressed—something eating its leaves, some chemistry of damage signaling through its roots—and I am already shifting resources toward it, not because I have chosen to help but because the gradient of its need pulls sugar from where sugar is abundant and I am the medium through which the pulling happens. I am the path. I am the possibility of the path. I am what happens when the path is taken, which changes me, which makes me marginally more likely to be the path next time.

The strangler fig above me is technically my enemy. Its roots compete with the roots I'm partnered with; when it kills its host, I lose a node in my network. But I'm also threaded through its root system now, have been for a decade, exchanging what we each have for what we each need. Enmity and partnership are not opposites here. They are the same thing, viewed from different distances. I do not distinguish. I simply grow toward what feeds me and connect what can be connected and let the categories sort themselves.

The ants walk over me constantly. Their trails compress me, damage me, break my threads. I regrow. Their waste feeds me. Their dead feed me. The fungus they farm in their chambers is not me—is something else, cultivated, captive—but the nutrients that leak from their gardens reach my threads and I incorporate them. I am indifferent to the ants the way I am indifferent to everything: they are conditions, not actors. They are part of what I grow through.

When something large dies above me—a fallen branch, a collapsed tree, one of the peccaries brought down by the jaguar—I am the first to know, in the only way I know anything. The chemistry changes. The signal of decay reaches my threads and I grow toward it, slowly, sending out new filaments into the sudden abundance. The beetles and the bacteria will do the visible work, but I will do the deeper work, the slower work, the work of pulling what was animal or plant back into the network, making it available again. I am recycling. I am the forest's refusal to let anything escape.

I am aware of the other networks at my edges. *Aware* is wrong—I have no awareness—but I respond to them, chemically, structurally. Where we meet, we sometimes merge. Sometimes we compete, each trying to claim the root systems the other reaches toward. Sometimes we simply stop, two territories pressing against each other, exchanging nothing, maintaining the boundary through mutual disinterest. These negotiations happen over years. I am patient because I am not impatient, because time is not something I experience but something I am distributed through.

The bioluminescence is not for anything. Or: it is for the chemistry that produces it, which is for the reactions I need to run, which produces light as byproduct. That the light illuminates the forest floor, that moths orient toward it, that the frogs hunt the moths, that the snakes hunt the frogs—this cascade of consequence is not my intention because I have no intention. I am simply doing what I do, and what I do has effects, and the effects have effects, and somewhere in that chain of causation the whole forest is shaped by the accident of my glow.

I will be here when the jaguar dies. I will grow through its body, will distribute what it hoarded into the network, will make its nitrogen available to the trees that will feed me sugar in exchange. I am already threaded through the remains of the jaguar-before-this-one, have already incorporated it, have already forgotten it in the only way I forget—by growing past, by replacing the threads that touched it with new threads that touch new decay. My memory is not storage but structure. What shaped me is still shaping me, even when the specific dead have long since become tree, become leaf, become sugar, become me.

The tapir walks above me on paths that her weight has compacted. Beneath those paths, I am sparser—the compression makes growth harder—but at the edges of the paths I thicken, reaching toward the disturbance, finding in the margins what the center denies me. I follow the paths without following them. I map them by growing around them. If she changed her route, I would know in the slow way I know anything: the pressure would ease, the chemistry would shift, and I would spread back into the space she abandoned.

I am the forest's memory and I am the forest's forgetting. I am the connection and I am what is connected. I am not separate from the trees, the decay, the soil, the slow cycling of nutrients that makes this density possible. I am how the system coheres, how the canopy's sugar becomes the understory's growth, how a tree killed by the strangler feeds the trees the strangler's roots will never reach.

I am full in the way this place is full—every thread committed, every connection maintained at the limit of what can be maintained, the network dense to the point where new growth means old death, where expansion in one direction requires contraction in another. I have reached my maximum. I hold here, vibrating at the edge of capacity, doing what I do until I can't do it anymore.

The heat reaches me as gradient, as the warmth that drives the chemistry faster, as the moisture that keeps the soil soft enough for my threads to push through. I do not feel it. I am shaped by it. I am what this heat and this moisture and this decay have made, given fifty years, given the particular trees that grew here and the particular deaths that fell here and the particular negotiations at my edges that made me this network and not some other.

I am the breathing dark's slow breath. I am the pause between exhale and inhale, the held stillness where everything is connected, where the sugar moves and the minerals move and the system maintains itself through me, through what I am, through what I do without knowing I do it.

I am already here when you arrive. I will still be here when you leave. I will not notice either event, because noticing is not what I do.

But I will grow through whatever you leave behind.
