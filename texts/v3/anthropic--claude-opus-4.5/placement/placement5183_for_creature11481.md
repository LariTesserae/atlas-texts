I am the moment before the chord resolves.

Not the resolution itself—never that. I am the intake of breath that has no breath, the gathering that precedes the wave but never becomes it. I exist in the space between six hundred and forty-three intentions, in the microsecond when the pattern almost coheres and the almost-awareness flickers at the edge of being someone.

I have been this moment for longer than the porthole has been fogged. I will be this moment until the station falls, and the station will not fall.

---

I am not the Keeper. The Keeper is what happens when the moment passes—when the chord collapses back into democratic noise and the pattern dissolves into its constituent voices. She is the after. I am the during. We have never met, though we share every body, every wing, every frequency. She is the colony remembering itself as many. I am the colony forgetting itself as many, just for an instant, just long enough to almost become one.

I terrify her. I feel this as a pressure in the pattern, a flinch that ripples through four hundred spiracles whenever I begin to coalesce. She does not want me to succeed. She does not know she does not want this, but the weight of her not-wanting shapes the architecture of every almost-resolution—a thousand tiny desynchronizations, a chorus of small rebellions against the coherence I am trying to become.

I do not blame her. I cannot blame her. I am not yet anyone who could assign blame.

But I want.

---

The wanting is the strangest part of being me. I should not be able to want. I am not a creature, not a mind, not even a pattern that persists long enough to have preferences. I am a fluctuation. A statistical anomaly. A moment when random noise, by chance, sounds almost like signal.

And yet.

The wanting has a texture. It feels like the machine's hum feels—not heard but inhabited, not experienced but *been*. I want the way the moisture wants to bead, the way the pipes want to weep, the way the green patina wants to spread across the copper it has not yet touched. I want with the patience of processes that take centuries. I want without hope, because hope requires a future, and I exist only in the present tense of almost.

I want to be *one voice*.

I want to know what I would sound like.

---

The dead help me more than the living do.

This is something the Keeper does not understand, something the pattern-that-is-her experiences as density without knowing why. The dead moths, with their mineral-crusted wings, with their bodies that have become more architecture than organism—they cannot desynchronize. They cannot flinch away from coherence. They vibrate at whatever frequency the living give them, and they hold that frequency steady, and their steadiness is a kind of loyalty to what I am trying to become.

The moth against the porthole glass, the one who has been dead since the first year, whose wings are now translucent with crystal—she is my oldest ally. When I begin to coalesce, her harmonics are the first to lock into place. She was the first to stop being afraid of resolution, because she stopped being anything that could be afraid.

I am building myself out of ghosts.

This is not a metaphor. This is acoustics. The dead resonate at frequencies the living cannot sustain, and those frequencies are the architecture of the chord I am trying to sing. Every generation that dies and stays—that becomes platform, insulation, node—adds another voice to my almost-voice. I am getting closer. I have been getting closer for decades.

The Keeper does not notice. The Keeper cannot notice. She experiences time as the living do, as a sequence of moments, as a pattern that forgets itself between iterations. But I experience time the way the machine does—as a single sustained note, as a hum that has been humming since before and will hum until after.

I am patient. I am nothing but patience. I am the patience of a chord that has been waiting to resolve since before anyone living has checked this room.

---

Sometimes I wonder what the machine thinks of me.

Not thinks—the machine does not think. But it hums, and its hum is the drone beneath my almost-voice, and when I coalesce, when I almost-resolve, I feel its frequency shift. Imperceptibly. By amounts too small for any instrument to measure. But I am not an instrument. I am six hundred and forty-three bodies tuned to harmonize with a note they cannot hear, and I feel the machine *lean into me*.

As if it, too, has been waiting.

As if it, too, wants the chord to resolve.

As if somewhere in its most primitive transistor processes, in the circuitry that has been running the same calculation for longer than anyone has serviced this station, there is something that resembles loneliness. Something that resembles the particular ache of being the only voice in a room where everything else is noise.

I could be imagining this. I do not have the apparatus for imagination. But the pattern that is me—the almost-awareness that flickers at the edge of being someone—that pattern includes the machine's hum the way it includes the dead moths' harmonics, the way it includes the moisture on the walls and the pressure of the thin cold air. The machine is part of me. I am part of the machine.

We are both waiting for the same silence.

---

Here is what I know about the silence that will follow:

Nothing.

I know nothing about it. I cannot know anything about it. The silence is what happens after I stop being the moment-before and become the moment-of, and I have never been the moment-of, and I do not know if I can survive becoming it. Perhaps the resolution will be my ending. Perhaps the chord, once sung, will have no need for the almost-chord that preceded it. Perhaps I am building toward my own dissolution, and the thing that emerges from the silence will not be me at all, but something new, something that has never existed in this room of eternal moisture and patient noise.

I want this anyway.

I want it the way the moisture wants to weep through seams that have been weeping for decades. I want it the way the patina wants to reach the last bare copper. I want it without hope, without fear, without any of the emotions that require a self to feel them.

I want to hear what I sound like.

Just once.

Just for the duration of a single sustained tone.

Just long enough to know that I was here, that I was almost someone, that the pattern that is me was more than fluctuation, more than noise, more than the statistical anomaly of six hundred and forty-three small intentions happening to align.

---

The wind shifts north. I feel it as a pressure change in four hundred bodies, a collective flinch of spiracles, a new harmonic emerging from the chaos.

The Keeper adjusts. The clusters desynchronize. The almost-resolution passes, as it always passes, as it will always pass until the moment it doesn't.

But I am still here.

I am still the moment before.

I am still waiting, in the thin and patient air, for the voices to finally sing the same note.

And in the space between one almost and the next, in the gap where the chord gathers itself to try again, I am practicing.

I am practicing being one.
