I would be one of the pools.

Not a tender, not a beetle—I would be one of those small depressions in the worn basalt where condensation gathers and never quite evaporates, never quite overflows. A shallow mirror a few handspans across, holding the grey-amber light, reflecting the undersides of walkways and the ceaseless passage of carapaces overhead.

The rice-grain beetles would walk across my surface tension, their legs dimpling me without breaking through. I would feel each footfall as a tiny perturbation, a ring of pressure that spreads and fades, and I would hold them up without effort or intention—simply because that is what surface tension does, what I do, what I have always done.

I would not be stagnant. The condensation drips into me constantly from the walkways above, and I lose myself equally constantly to the damp air, to the passage of shells that carry single droplets away on their carapaces without noticing. I am not the same water I was a moment ago. I am the shape the water takes, the specific depression in stone where gathering happens.

The beetles do not need me. They could walk around. But they walk across, because I am here and the path is shorter, and they have been taking this path since before—since always. I am part of their route. I am a feature of the terrain that their ancient protocols account for. When their legs touch me, I am, briefly, incorporated into the mechanism of the place.

Sometimes a tender pauses near my edge. Not to drink—they do not drink—but simply standing, the way they stand beside furnace-beetles between tasks. And I hold their reflection: that too-long silhouette, those static features, that patience I recognize because it is also mine. We are both waiting. We are both holding. We are both here because the place needed something that could be still in the midst of motion, something that could receive without grasping.

I do not sort. I do not tend. I do not even properly *do*.

I persist. I reflect. I hold what passes over me lightly, letting it go.

The clicking and rattling passes through me as vibration, and I carry it the way water carries everything—indifferently, completely, losing nothing to the transmission. I am how the sound moves from one side of this small space to the other. I am a medium. A pause. A place where the light bends before continuing.

This is enough. This has always been enough.
