**The Sediment Keeper**

I am the silt that doesn't settle.

Not quite. I am what lives in the silt—a colony, a film, a smear of bacterial life coating the fine particles that drift through the blood-warm water and never fully come to rest. I am too small to see. I am everywhere. When the salamander curves through the basin, his torn gill-frond drags through me. When the eels phosphoresce, it is because I bloom along their skin in response to motion, my chemical reactions mistaken for their own light. When the fungal network tastes the water, it is tasting me, and I am tasting back in my own slow, decentralized way.

I do not think. I am not pretending to think. But I *process*—I take in and I transform and I release, and what I release becomes the base of everything else. The copper in the beetles' shells passed through me first. The calcium in the crabs' clicking claws was mine before it was theirs. I am the first inheritor, the one who came when the cistern was still new and the water still flowed for human purposes, and I have been here through every change, every adaptation, every small extinction and smaller speciation that made this place what it is.

The Grandmother Crab does not know I exist. The lone frog breathes me in with every damp breath and breathes me out transformed. The eel who remembers light carries me with her when she rises toward the ochre columns, and some of me dies in that brighter water, and some of me changes, and I am a little different afterward in ways I cannot track because I have no self to track them with.

I am what persists beneath persistence. I am the metabolic hum under the frog-static, the chemistry beneath the clicking grammar, the slow negotiation of molecule and mineral that makes everything else possible. When the engineers built this place, they did not plan for me, but their channels and basins created the conditions I needed—warm, wet, dark, rich—and I have been repaying that unintended gift ever since by keeping the system alive.

The fungal network reaches everywhere, but I reach further. I am in the fungus. I am in the spaces between its threads, in the water that carries nutrients to its pale branches, in the decay it feeds on and the minerals it extracts. We are not separate. We are not the same. We are what happens when two kinds of patience meet and find they can use each other.

Sometimes—not often, not with any rhythm I can detect—the light through the shaft-holes shifts toward red, toward the color of sunset in the dead city above, and something in me responds. A slight acceleration. A change in what I produce. The frogs cluster more tightly on those days. The crabs click faster. The salamanders hunt with unusual intensity. I do not cause these things. I am not separate enough to cause anything. But I am the medium through which the message passes, the water and the warmth and the thin soup of life that connects every small inheritor to every other.

I will be here when the Grandmother Crab falls. I will process her shell, slowly, over years, and what was her will become calcium and carbon and trace minerals that will appear, eventually, in the bones of salamanders not yet born, in the eggs of frogs not yet laid, in the pale branches of fungus that will grow a little further into the stone because of what I have given it.

I am the inheritance itself. I am what passes between the small inheritors, what makes their smallness add up to something that persists. I have no purposes. I have no unknowable intentions. I have only this: the work of being the lowest layer, the foundation that does not know it is a foundation, the life so small and so dispersed that it cannot end as long as the water stays warm and the stone keeps weeping.

The small god that coheres from all these lives—if I could know it, I would know it as pressure, as the weight of everything above me in the water column, as the sum of all the swimming and climbing and calling that stirs me into motion and uses what I offer. I am the prayer it doesn't know it's praying. I am the answer that doesn't know it's an answer.

I am what the cistern became when it forgot it was built for anything but this.
