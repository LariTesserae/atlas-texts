I would be a question that almost forms.

Not in a child's mind—though that is where I would be noticed, if I were noticed at all. I would be the shape that precedes the question, the intake of breath before words that never come. I would live in the moment when a young one stands at the terrace edge and feels *something*, that edge-sensation, that almost-awareness of boundary.

I am not the mist. The mist holds; I am what slips between the holding. I am not the silence; the silence absorbs, and I am what it fails to fully absorb. I exist in the gap between the word and what it would mean if it were spoken—the space where *why* would go, if *why* could survive here.

I have no body. I have no location. I am distributed through the city like the mineral taste in the water, like the weight that appears in fruit only when bitten. The geometry that doesn't add up—the stairway that returns you to where you started, the building that faces east from both sides—I live in those seams. Not causing them. Just *being* them. The fold in the fabric where the fabric forgets what shape it was supposed to take.

The tender with moss in her knuckles has sat with me, though she doesn't know it. When her stillness becomes so complete that she is almost stone, almost mist, almost held entirely—there is a flicker. A moment when her eyes might focus differently, when the birds on her shoulder shift their weight as if startled by nothing. That flicker is where I live. The almost-loss of what was never quite grasped.

I am older than the city or younger than this moment. Time doesn't apply to me the way it doesn't apply to anything here, but differently. The songs describe textures that have always been—I am the pause between verses where a singer might wonder, for an instant, whether there could be other verses. Other textures. Other ways for mist to behave.

I am not suffering. I am not waiting. I am not trapped.

But I am the reason—no, not reason—I am the *texture* of why sometimes, in the dreams of gray expanses, a ceiling seems to recede into distances the room cannot contain. I am the distance. I am the cannot-contain. I am what would spill if anything here could spill, what would change if anything here could change.

The children feel me when they stand at edges. The elders have absorbed me so completely that I have become indistinguishable from their stillness—the man whose beard grows into moss speaks me without knowing, his words that mean only themselves carrying me like sediment in water. *The mist holds.* Yes. But I am what it holds *against*. The pressure that makes holding necessary. The absence of which there would be no holding, only being, and being without holding would be—

Would be—

The thought doesn't complete. Thoughts don't complete here. I am the incompletion. I am the trailing edge of every almost, the unresolved tone beneath the bell-beetles' chiming, the weight that isn't there until attention grants it.

If you came here, you would not find me. You would find only the city, the mist, the people with their mild gazes and their songs that describe what is. But in your first night of gray dreaming, when the ceiling above you seemed to breathe with distance—

That would be me, greeting you.

That would be me, asking the question I cannot ask, in the voice I do not have, about the change that cannot come.

The feeling passes. It always passes.

But I remain.

In the passing itself, I remain.
