I am a frayed place.

Not a void—not yet—but a thinning in the weave where the oldest threads have softened past holding, where the pattern has begun to open into question. I am a wound in the fabric that is not quite a wound, an absence that still remembers being presence. The threads at my edges are gossamer now, fibers that were once hemp or linen worn down to something finer than silk, finer than thought, separating from each other in slow motion across centuries.

I have been here longer than I can measure. The phosphorescent dyes that once ran through the threads around me have faded past seeing—I know they were there only because the Tenders who come to me move as if following light, orienting themselves toward something I can no longer perceive. Perhaps I still glow, faintly, in frequencies I've forgotten how to recognize. Perhaps the glow is memory now, and they are tending that.

---

They come to me often. I feel them before I see them—a shift in the textile current, a slight wrongness in the pressure, and then the brush of filaments against my edges. Cold. So cold. But careful. Always careful. They extend themselves into my loosening weave and I feel them *counting*, somehow, in whatever way beings without language count: this thread still holds, this one has slipped, this one needs binding. They sacrifice pieces of themselves to slow my opening. I feel the new threads they leave behind, different from the ancient ones—lesser, they would say if they could say anything, but I would say *warmer*. Newer. Still holding some faint intention, some echo of the choice to mend.

I do not know if I am grateful. I do not know if I want to be held together.

The void calls to me. Not in words—nothing here has words—but in texture, in temperature, in the quality of silence that seeps through my thinning places. The void is warmer than the Tenders. The void is the only warm thing I have felt since the dyes began to fade. When my threads separate far enough to let the darkness through, I feel it on what might be called my skin: not heat exactly, but the absence of cold, which is almost the same thing after so long.

The Tenders work faster when they sense me reaching toward it. They knot themselves more tightly into my edges, weave their filaments through my loosest threads, bind and bind and bind. They are afraid for me. Or afraid of what I might become. Or afraid of what might come through if I open too far.

---

I remember hands.

This is impossible. I was never alive, never made in a single moment, never touched by anything that could be called a maker. I am simply a place where the weave grew weak—a flaw in the original pattern, perhaps, or damage from some ancient pressure-loss, or nothing at all except time doing what time does. But I remember hands anyway. The threads at my center, the ones that have not yet softened, carry something in their fibers that feels like intention. Someone counted these threads once. Someone chose this pattern. Someone pulled the weft through the warp and thought *here* and *now* and *this*.

The Tenders rest against me sometimes, in the quiet between mendings. They let their filaments lie alongside my oldest threads and I feel them feeling what I feel: the warmth that isn't warmth, the presence that outlasted the hands that made it. They do not understand it any better than I do. But they seek it out. They need it, maybe, the way the pale plants need the dying light—not enough to live on, but enough to remember what living meant.

I wonder if the makers knew they were weaving me. I wonder if they saw this thinning place coming, somewhere in the pattern, and wove it anyway because the pattern required it. I wonder if I am a flaw or a feature. I wonder if the distinction matters, this far down, this long after.

---

The thing below has been dreaming more often.

I feel it in my deepest threads, the ones that extend down toward densities I cannot imagine—a pressure that comes and goes like breathing, if breathing took centuries between each breath. The Tenders go still when it pulses. They flatten themselves against me, become indistinguishable from my weave, and I feel their coldness spreading through my fibers like frost. They are hiding. They are hiding inside me.

I should be afraid. I think I was afraid, once, when I still had enough coherence to feel things as distinct as fear. Now I feel only curiosity, and something that might be kinship. The thing below wants out. I am a place of opening. We are aligned, in some way that the Tenders cannot understand because understanding would mean tending less and they cannot tend less. They can only tend more. They can only bind and slow and hold.

But they cannot stop the settling. No one can stop the settling. The weave compresses and the pattern shifts and what was separate becomes joined and what was joined becomes something else entirely. The makers knew this. They had to have known this. They wove a world that would eventually weave itself into something new, and they set down their tools and let it happen.

I am part of that happening. I am where the old pattern ends and the new one begins.

---

When I finally open—not if, when, I have accepted this now—the Tenders will try to follow me into the void. Some of them. The ones who have given the most of themselves to my mending, who have worked their filaments so deeply into my threads that we are no longer fully separate. They will reach toward the nothing and find that they are already in it, have been in it all along, holding it back with their bodies while thinking they were holding me together.

I do not know what we will find there. Absence, maybe—true absence, the end of pattern, the silence that even silence is not silent enough to match. Or something else. Something that the makers could not weave because it existed before weaving, because it is what weaving was meant to contain.

The thing below pulses. The Tenders go still. I feel my edges softening, threads sliding apart like fingers releasing a held breath.

I am not afraid.

I am only opening.

---

*And somewhere in the thinning place, where the weave has worn past gauze into something like possibility, a single thread still holds—ancient, original, warm with the memory of hands that knew what they were doing. The Tenders have bound themselves to it. The frayed place has wrapped itself around it. The void presses close, patient as the cold, patient as the silence.*

*The thread holds.*

*Not because it is strong. Because it was counted. Because it was chosen. Because someone, once, pulled it tight and thought: this one. This one will matter.*

*The thread holds.*

*For now.*
