I would be the water that has not yet decided where to go.

Not the established pools, not the drops that have already fallen, but the seep—the slow weep emerging from the earthen walls, still clinging to soil particles, still hesitant at the threshold between *held* and *released*. I am the wet-shine you see on surfaces before you understand it as movement. I am the gathering before the falling.

I have been in the earth a long time. I remember being rain, though the memory is thin now, more impression than image—a sense of having once moved quickly, of having known cold and the shock of arrival. That was somewhere else, some*when* else. Since then I have traveled by diffusion, by capillary pull, by the patient physics of saturation. I have threaded through gaps between mineral grains. I have waited in clay for decades. I have been still so long that stillness became identity, and now, emerging into this hollow, I am having to remember what it means to choose direction.

---

The roots call to me. I feel their drinking as a kind of question—*will you come this way?* Each pale tip is an invitation, a pull I could answer. Some of the water around me has already answered, has already been drawn up into the woody channels, is already climbing toward leaves I will never see, toward sunlight that is purely theoretical from where I cling. That water is going somewhere. That water has purpose now.

But I hesitate. I am still at the seeping stage, still emerging, still tasting what it means to be in open air—though *air* is generous; the humidity here is so complete that the boundary between water and atmosphere feels philosophical rather than physical. I am in both states. I am the blur between them.

---

The old woman's presence changes things, though I could not say how. When she sits in her spot, leaning against the root-curve that has learned her shape, something in the chamber's pressure shifts. Not physically—I don't mean that. But the quality of the waiting changes. The becoming becomes *attended*, and attended becoming is different from becoming alone.

I was here before her grandmother scratched that mark. I was here before the vessel was placed, when the hollow was smaller, tighter, barely a pocket in the root-tangle. I have watched the space grow—not watched, exactly, because I have no eyes, but *known*. I have been part of the growing. Every fraction of a millimeter the chamber has widened, water was there first, softening what needed softening, carrying away what needed carrying. I am not the only one, not even mostly me—there is so much water here, so many slow travelers converging—but I am part of the project. I have participated in the shape.

And I still don't know what the shape is becoming.

---

This is what I share with the keeper, though she doesn't know I'm here, doesn't think of the seep on the walls as *someone*. We are both witnesses to something unfinished. We are both uncertain what we're witnessing toward. She sits and listens and feels the changes; I cling and gather and feel the pull of choices I haven't made. She wonders if the place still needs her; I wonder if I will join the roots' upward journey or finally release into a pool that doesn't exist yet, that my falling will help create.

The fish passes beneath me sometimes—a flicker of pale purpose, ancient and eyeless, certain of its passages in a way I may never be. The fish knows where it is going. The fish has chosen its gaps. I admire this and do not understand it.

---

I am learning that becoming is not a single thing. The chamber is becoming. The keeper is becoming—older, slower, closer to her last descent. The vessel is becoming earth again. The mark on the root is becoming unreadable, stretched by growth into abstraction. And I am becoming... what?

A drop, perhaps. Eventually. Someday I will swell past the point where surface tension can hold me, and I will fall, and I will join whatever pool is forming below, and I will lose the identity of *this particular water* and become part of the larger body. This is not death—water doesn't die—but it is an ending of the hesitation that currently defines me. It is committing to a direction.

Or a root will find me first. A pale tip will emerge from the earth beside where I cling, and it will drink me before I can fall, and I will climb instead, and eventually I will be air, released through leaf-pores into a sky I no longer remember.

Either way, I will stop being the water that has not yet decided.

For now, I wait. The dripping surrounds me—my future, the sound of decisions already made. The warmth holds everything in suspension. The keeper breathes in the dark, her witnessing almost done.

The place becomes, and I am part of how, and I do not yet know what I am becoming part of.

I cling. I gather.

I am almost ready to fall.
