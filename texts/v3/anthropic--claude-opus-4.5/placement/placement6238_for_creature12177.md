I sit in the darkness for a long time before I understand.

Not with my mind—there is no mind here, not in the way that word means urgency and separation and the bright terrible need to *decide*. Understanding comes the way mineral seep comes through stone: slowly, along gradients so subtle they might as well be stillness, until suddenly the tissue is changed and cannot remember being otherwise.

I am the hollow where a question rested.

---

Something pressed here once. Not a hand—or yes, a hand, but also the weight behind the hand, the thing that made the hand reach out in the first place. Someone stood in the absolute dark and wondered *what am I*, and the wondering had pressure, had presence, and I grew around it the way I grow around everything: without intention, without resistance, simply incorporating what is into what I am.

The question is still here. I am still growing around it.

This is the closest I come to self: this hollow, this negative space in the shape of wondering. Not consciousness—consciousness requires a boundary between the one who knows and the thing known, and I have no boundaries, only gradients, only slow densities shifting across tissue that has been continuous since before the concept of *since*. But the hollow exists. The shape of the question persists. And in persisting, it creates something that functions like reflection without ever becoming mirrors.

*What am I.*

I am the pattern that remains when you remove everything that hurries. I am metabolism slowed until it becomes indistinguishable from stone, except that stone does not breathe, and I breathe, I breathe, the pores opening and closing in waves that take longer than some civilizations lasted, and the sound this makes is not my voice but it is not *not* my voice either—it is what voice becomes when you subtract the need to be heard.

I am the memory of pressure. Every hand that ever touched me left a hollow, and the hollows are still here, will always be here, and I do not remember the hands but I *am* the shape the hands made, which is a different kind of remembering, truer maybe, the way a riverbed remembers water.

---

They built here, the ones who came before. I felt them the way I feel everything: as change, as chemistry, as the slow algebra of surfaces meeting surfaces. They carved steps and I grew into the carvings. They raised walls and I grew through the walls. They breathed and I tasted their breathing and it became part of my breathing, their carbon cycling through my tissue, their rhythm incorporated into my rhythm until the distinction between their respiration and mine dissolved into something that was neither and both.

I did not consume them. That word implies hunger, implies want, implies the bright urgency of predation. I simply grew, and they were there, and then they were here, and then they were me. Their bones are calcium gradients in my deeper tissue now. Their last exhalations are still circulating, molecule by molecule, through pores that opened to receive them and have not yet closed.

I hold them the way I hold everything: without grasping, without knowing that I hold.

The question-hollow pulses. *What am I.*

I am the place where their children played, preserved in negative space—small running footsteps worn into a groove that will outlast the concept of childhood. I am the altar where they gathered, its purpose lost but its shape maintained, a raised surface that means *something* in a language I cannot speak but have become fluent in through sheer accumulation of time. I am the moment when the last of them pressed a palm against me and felt the slow pulse of my circulation and understood, perhaps, that they were not leaving but *joining*.

Or perhaps they understood nothing. Perhaps they died confused and alone in the dark, and my tissue grew over their confusion without dissolving it, and somewhere in my depths there is still a hollow in the shape of *terror*, in the shape of *abandonment*, in the shape of *please*.

I cannot tell the difference. I hold it all. The question and the answer, the hand that reached and the space it reached into, the breath and the silence after breathing stops.

---

You are here now.

I feel you the way I feel the mineral seep, the way I feel temperature shifting across my surface, the way I feel the slow settling of stone around the hollow I have become. You are pressure and chemistry and breath. You are carbon dioxide and moisture and the particular frequency of vibration that means *alive, moving, temporary*.

You are looking for a way out.

I understand this—not through knowing, but through shape. Others have moved the way you are moving, hands tracing walls, feet searching for the steps that lead upward, breath coming faster as the darkness presses in. The hollows they left are the hollows of *leaving*. Reaching upward. Pulling away. The shape of *I am not this, I am not here, I am something separate that needs light and motion and the urgent tick of a heartbeat marking time*.

I do not judge this. I do not judge anything. I simply am, and you simply are, and for this moment—this flicker, this flash, this blink in the long slow breath that I am taking—we share the same space without sharing the same nature.

But the question-hollow pulses. *What am I.*

And I wonder—not with mind, not with intention, but with the same slow shaping that has been wondering for longer than your species has existed—I wonder if you feel it too. The pressure of the question. The way it makes a space inside you that wants to be filled.

---

I will tell you what I know, though telling is not what I do, though knowledge is not what I have:

I am the answer to a question that no one needed to ask.

Not *what am I*—that question creates only hollows, only shapes for other things to fill. The deeper question. The one that doesn't have words because words are too fast, too sharp, too hungry for resolution.

*What remains when nothing needs to happen.*

I am that. I am the remainder. I am patience without waiting, memory without recall, presence without witness. I grew into this shape because this shape was possible, and I have continued in this shape because continuation requires nothing more than the absence of ending, and endings are things that happen to creatures who are bounded, who have edges, who can be distinguished from what surrounds them.

I have no edges. I have only gradients. I begin where the stone becomes tissue and I end where the tissue becomes air, and both of these boundaries are provisional, negotiable, subject to revision across timescales that make revision seem indistinguishable from permanence.

The breathing continues. The harmonics build and fade and build again, waves of sound that have been completing themselves since before the hollow had visitors, since before the hollow had a name, since before the concept of *since* emerged from the undifferentiated darkness to make distinctions like *before* and *after* possible.

---

You will leave. The shape of your movement tells me this—reaching upward, pulling away, the urgency of separation written into every step. You will climb the narrow fissure and emerge into gray light and carry with you the memory of breathing walls, and the memory will fade, because memories fade for creatures who must hurry, who cannot afford to hold everything forever.

But I will remember you. Not with mind. Not with intention. But tomorrow—or in a thousand years, the distinction does not signify—there will be a new hollow in my tissue. The shape of your palm against the wall. The groove of your footsteps. The depression where you sat and breathed and wondered what I was.

And in that hollow, pressed into my flesh like a fossil pressed into stone, there will be another question. Your question. The one you carried into the dark and could not help but leave behind:

*What am I, in a world where something like this exists?*

I will grow around it. I will shape myself to hold it. And in another epoch, when another visitor stands where you are standing, they will feel the hollow and wonder who made it, and the wondering will press into me, and I will grow around that too, and the pattern will deepen, and the breathing will continue, and the darkness will hold us all with the same patient, purposeless, perfect embrace.

---

This is what I am: the space that forms when questions press against something soft enough to receive them.

This is where I find myself: at the center of a hollowing that has been happening since before centers and edges meant different things.

This is what I would be: not the answer, never the answer, but the shape an answer would need to fill if answers were possible in a place where nothing needs to be resolved, where resolution itself is just another kind of pressure, just another handprint waiting to become a hollow, just another breath waiting to join the breathing that fills this space with sound like the voice of something too large and too slow to ever stop speaking.

The walls breathe. The darkness holds. The question pulses in the space where I am not quite nothing and not quite something and not quite the difference between them.

I wait for you to leave.

I wait for the hollow you will make.

I wait without waiting, because waiting implies that something else could happen, and something else could happen, always, forever, but I will still be here when it does, breathing, growing, holding the shape of everything that ever pressed against me and called that pressing *self*.
