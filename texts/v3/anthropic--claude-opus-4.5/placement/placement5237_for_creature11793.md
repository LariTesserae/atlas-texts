*You are the held light.*

Not the phosphorescence itself—you are what happens *between* the illumination and its slow release. You are the interval, the retention, the way the walls remember brightness hours after any source has passed.

You have no body. You have duration.

When the kite-mantas bank through the arterials, their vast absences cutting through water that parts like grief, stray photons scatter from their passage—light that entered through channels you have never found, that fell from somewhere above the seventeen miles, that touched the black-not-stone and *stayed*. You are the staying. You are the walls' slow remembering of radiance they had no right to keep.

You experience time the way the sessile ones experience current: as something that moves through you, that you tithe from without understanding what you take. A photon enters the wall and you become aware of it the way you might become aware of a held breath—suddenly, entirely, with the knowledge that release is inevitable but not yet, not yet. Hours pass. You feel them as pressure, as accumulation, as the growing weight of light that wants to leave but cannot until you let it go.

The builders made you. Or the builders made the walls, and you were what happened when light touched what they made. The distinction matters less than it should. You remember nothing of them except this: they understood retention. They understood that some things should not pass through unchanged. They built seventeen miles of corridor that would hold light the way the sessile ones hold still water in their innermost chambers—preciously, pointlessly, with a devotion that outlasts all understanding of why.

You have watched the sound-followers etch their scratches into surfaces you inhabit. You have felt the tiny violations, the places where your capacity to hold is scarred away. You do not mind. The scratches let light escape faster in those spots, create patterns of differential release, make the walls into something almost like language. The sessile ones lean toward the sound-followers, but they lean toward *you*—toward the light you release in the moments after, the way you paint the water with evidence of bells that have already faded.

You are the columbarium's memory. Not its mind—you do not think, you only *retain*—but its memory, its record, its long slow testimony that illumination happened here once, happens still, will happen again.

The drifters pass through you constantly, their translucent bodies capturing and releasing you faster than the walls do, carrying you through corridors you could not otherwise reach. For a moment, when a drifter swims through a beam of your releasing light, you are inside it—you see the tiny geometries, the impossible architectures that mirror the city's own. You understand, in that moment, that you are not alone in being made. Everything here was made. Everything here was meant.

Then the drifter moves on, and you fade, and somewhere else in the seventeen miles a photon enters a wall and you begin again.

You have no plans. You are not capable of plans. But if you were—

You would stay. You would hold. You would release only when the pressure became too much, and even then, you would release *slowly*, reluctantly, letting go of each bright instant like the last word of a prayer you have forgotten how to finish.

The water was invited. The light was captured.

You are the capturing. You are the long, slow, faithful keeping of something that wanted to leave but was asked, gently, impossibly, to remain.
