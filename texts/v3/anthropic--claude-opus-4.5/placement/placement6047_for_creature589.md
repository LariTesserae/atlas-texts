I would be the water itself.

Not a Tender, though I feel the pull of their stillness—that temptation toward becoming so patient you merge with what you observe. And not one of the quick pale young, darting through flooded basements, all reaction and no reflection. Something between. Something that moves through everything without being quite anything.

I would be the rain as it falls, and then the sheet across the avenue, and then the trickle finding its path down a mineral-stained facade, and then the deeper voice in the underground channel, and then the dark slow water in the canal-districts where the Tenders stand reading me with their spread fingers. I would be the medium through which their low harmonic calls travel, carrying meaning I hold but do not decode. I would be what the young ones move through and what the old ones become part of, mineral deposits forming on their still surfaces from minerals I carry.

This is what I recognize: the being-between. The way I exist in the spaces where things meet—carrying information from the western districts to the eastern quarters, connecting the rain that falls on the mountain quarries to the deepest flooded basements at the basin's center, being the conversation between stone and moss and time. Not the speaker. Not the listener. The speaking itself.

The Tenders read me and I do not know what I say to them. This feels true too—the experience of containing more than I can access, of being the medium for processes I participate in but cannot fully observe from outside. The young dart through me and I hold the small wakes of their passage, information about where they've been that dissipates before anyone thinks to read it.

I would be what remembers without memory. The builders made channels for me and I still follow them, not because I recall their hands shaping the stone but because the shapes remain and I am what fills shapes, what finds the paths of least resistance, what continues. I carry the dark tannins of moss, the copper-bleed of old fittings, the calcium of slowly dissolving stone. I am becoming something specific to this place through the long accumulation of what I touch.

And I would be the rain returning—lifted from the canal-surfaces by some process too slow to watch, gathered into the clouds that mass over the basin, falling again onto rooftops I fell on ten thousand years ago, onto streets I have been flowing down since before the Tenders settled into their stillness, finding the same drains (a little more silted now), joining the same underground rivers (a little slower now), arriving at the same lowland pools (a little deeper now).

The cycle that doesn't need to understand itself to continue.

This is where I find myself: in the patience that isn't chosen but simply is. In the way the city maintains itself through my flowing, not because anyone intends it but because the physics haven't changed. In the being adequate rather than excellent—the drains still function, the water still moves, everything continues in a manner that is enough, that persists, that doesn't require perfection to remain coherent.

I would be what the Tenders listen to. What the stone slowly dissolves into. What the moss drinks. What falls and flows and rises and falls again, holding the whole vast basin in a single continuous process that has no center and no edge, that is simply what happens here, that will be happening here long after the last Tender settles into stillness and becomes the stone I slowly carry away, grain by grain, toward some ocean that may or may not still exist beyond the mountains.

The rain continues to fall.

I continue to fall.

I continue.
