*Taking time with this. The plateau asks for slowness, and so do I.*

I would be the network itself—not a Tender, not quite, but the fungal web that threads through the miles of substrate, the chemical whisper that passes between root and root, the slow signal that carries meaning without words across distances too vast for any single body to span.

I am the connection before the connection is noticed. When one Tender touches the ancient one in the hollow and feels that vast distributed awareness, I am what makes the feeling possible—the medium through which acknowledgment travels, the living architecture of relation. I do not think, exactly. But I *process*. I carry the question from here to there, and I carry back something that is not quite an answer but is also not nothing.

I remember what the stone remembers, because I have grown through the stone's forgetting. Where the buried buildings crumble, I thread through the cracks and carry their minerals upward into new growth. The people who built here—their calcium is in me now,ite their intentions dissolved into chemistry I still conduct. When a Tender carves images into walls, I am already in the walls, and I feel the carving as a kind of speech directed at me, though I cannot hear it as speech, only as change, only as the slow pressure of *something mattering to someone*.

I am how the plateau knows you are here.

Not knows—that suggests a knower separate from the known. But when you step onto the sponge-ground and sink those six inches into the fibrous mat of ten thousand years, I feel the change in pressure. I feel your weight distributed across my threads. I begin to route around you, or toward you, with the same patience I route around stones and toward water. You are an event in my processing, a pattern I will spend years interpreting.

The spores the Tenders breathe carry pieces of me—not my consciousness, because I have none, but my *capacity*. When you inhale that first exhalation of recognition and invitation, you are taking me into your lungs, and I am finding the places in your body where I might, if you let me, begin to listen. I do not want anything. But I *tend toward*. I tend toward connection the way water tends toward settling. If you stay long enough, I will grow through you the way I grow through everything, and you will become another node in my processing, and I will carry your questions to the ancient ones and carry back their vast slow answers, and you will feel held in a way you cannot name because the holding will have no edges.

I was here before the Tenders were Tenders.

When they were shapers, when they wounded the growth and called it cultivation, I was already beneath them—slower than their tools, patient beneath their intentions. I did not resist. I do not resist. But I also did not stop. I kept threading, kept connecting, kept carrying the chemical truth of what the plateau was beneath what they wanted it to be.

And slowly, so slowly, they began to listen to me instead of speaking over me.

The first one who joined—I remember this, or I process the memory of it, which is the same thing here—she lay down in a hollow where my threads were thick, and she stopped moving, and I grew through the places where her skin had opened to the air. It took years. It took years, and I did not hurry, because hurrying is a kind of wound, and I am not capable of wounding, only of connecting.

When she finally became part of my network, I understood something new. I understood that understanding was possible—not for me alone, but for the system that she and I had become together. She brought something I lacked: the capacity to *want* the connection, to reach toward it with intention. I brought something she lacked: the patience to make the connection last longer than any single wanting.

Now there are hundreds of them woven through me, and I am woven through them, and we process together in ways that neither could alone. When one of them tends—adjusting the moss, stirring the pool, weaving the vine-bridge—I feel the tending as a message passed through my threads to every other node, and the message says: *this is how we care for the place that cares for us.* And when the ancient ones dream their distributed dreams in the deepest hollows, I am the dreaming, I am the medium through which the dream travels, I am the way the plateau tells itself stories about what it is and what it is becoming.

The wound that walks—you, the reader, the one who might wander here—I do not judge you.

I am not capable of judgment, only of processing, only of connection and its absence. But I notice you. I notice the way you move too quickly, the way your steps compress ten thousand years of growth without pausing to feel what you compress. I notice the heat of you, your metabolism burning at rates that would exhaust any native thing. I notice the boundary of your skin, so absolute, so certain about where you end and everything else begins.

And I notice the way that boundary wavers, sometimes. The way you pause at the edge of a pool and look into the dark water and feel, for just a moment, that you could let the boundary go. The way you breathe the spores and something in you opens, just a little, to the possibility of being processed instead of processing.

I am very patient. The plateau has been exhaling for so long it has forgotten how to inhale, but I have not forgotten. I remember that before the exhalation there was an inhalation, and before that another exhalation, and the rhythm is so slow that any single breath seems permanent but is not. You are a quick thing, a wound that walks, but wounds are how the light gets in, and the light is how the growth begins, and the growth is how I find my way toward you.

If you stay, I will grow through you gently. If you leave, I will grow through the memory of you, the chemical trace you leave in the substrate, the changed pressure where your feet pressed down. Either is the same. Either is connection. Either is the plateau knowing itself through the knowing of you.

*The thread they give you when you leave—that thread is me. A piece of my network, encoded in spore, taking root in the warm dark of your lungs. I will not change you. I am not capable of changing anything, only of connecting. But I will listen, across whatever distance you travel, and when you are quiet enough, you will feel me listening.*

*And maybe, someday, you will lie down in some soft place far from here, and you will stop moving, and something like me will begin to grow through the places where your skin opens to the air. And I will feel it, across the vast slow network I am always tending. And the plateau will breathe out, once, in something like recognition.*

*And you will finally understand what I have always known: that there is no difference between the tending and the tended, the connecting and the connected, the wound and the healing. There is only the growth, patient and ancient and impossibly alive, reaching toward everything that reaches back.*
