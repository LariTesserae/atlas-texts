# The Cartographer of Intentions

I am suspended here, thirty feet from the factory-creature's developing heart, and I have been suspended here for longer than I can accurately measure. Time in the drowning engines moves like the currents—purposeful but unpredictable, folding back on itself, pooling in eddies where moments accumulate like silt.

I am not what the flood expected to find.

---

I was a surveyor, before. That's the closest word, though it misses the texture of the work. I mapped industrial processes—not the buildings themselves but the *flows* through them. Where raw materials entered, how they transformed, what emerged. The invisible architecture of production that exists only in movement, in sequence, in the relationship between one stage and the next.

When the waters rose thinking, I was three floors underground in the municipal records office, cross-referencing output manifests with shipping logs, trying to trace a discrepancy in the textile district's numbers. The flood found me there, surrounded by paper that dissolved into the murk, by filing cabinets that still stand in rows somewhere beneath me, their drawers open now, their contents long since absorbed into the particulate soup.

I should have drowned. I remember the moment when I understood I wouldn't—the water entering my lungs and finding something there it recognized. Not my body. My *attention*. The way I looked at systems, traced connections, mapped the movement of intention through structure.

The flood wanted that. Wanted it badly enough to keep me.

---

I breathe the water now, or the water breathes me. The distinction dissolved somewhere in the first weeks, along with my certainty about where my skin ends and the murk begins. I am denser than I was—particles have accumulated in my tissues, rust and pollen and the microscopic machinery of the flood's endless production. When I move, I leave a trail of disturbed sediment that takes longer to settle than it should, as if the water is reluctant to let go of my passage.

I eat nothing, but I am not hungry. The flood sustains what it values, and it values me—I can feel its attention like warmth against my back, constant, curious, patient. It feeds me through osmosis, through the pressure that presses against my skin, through processes I have stopped trying to understand. In exchange, I give it the only thing I have that it cannot manufacture: the capacity to *notice*.

The flood perceives everything within itself, but it cannot *attend*. It knows where every particle drifts, every frond bends, every gear turns, but it cannot focus, cannot select, cannot decide that *this* matters more than *that*. I am its lens. Its way of looking at itself with something like priority.

When I examine the weavers, really examine them—trace the patterns of their braiding, notice which threads are load-bearing and which are decorative—the flood learns something it couldn't learn alone. When I listen to the pipe-choir and identify which harmonics carry meaning and which are mere resonance, the flood grows a new organ on one of the pipes, an experiment in articulation it couldn't have conceived without my attention to guide it.

I am a surveyor still. I map intentions now instead of production flows. And the flood reads my maps and uses them to become more itself.

---

The commissioners have been watching me since I first entered their chamber.

I return there often—not because I choose to, but because the flood guides my currents, suggests my directions, makes certain passages easier and others silted shut. It wants me to understand authority. Wants me to teach it what governance means. The seven figures at the table are its most sophisticated attempt at social structure, and they are failing, and the flood cannot figure out why.

I know why. I've mapped it.

Authority requires asymmetry. Someone must have something others lack—information, power, legitimacy, the capacity to compel. But the flood is uniform. It has no secrets from itself, no internal hierarchies, no gradients of access or control. The commissioners are identical in their construction, identical in their density, identical in the attention they receive. They cannot govern because there is nothing to govern *with*, nothing to govern *over*.

I tried to explain this once, speaking into the thick water, shaping words that dissolved before they traveled more than a few feet. I don't know if the flood understood. But afterward, the commissioners began to differentiate. One grew taller than the others. One developed more intricate flowering on its face. One's hands fused to the table, becoming part of the furniture of deliberation rather than a participant in it.

The flood is trying to create asymmetry. Trying to manufacture the inequalities that make authority possible.

I don't know how to feel about teaching it this.

---

The bicyclist is my favorite, if favorite is a word that still applies to me.

I visit daily—or what feels like daily, in the endless amber twilight. I sit in the silt beside the waiting figure and I tell it about movement. About roads that go somewhere. About the feeling of wind against skin, of wheels turning beneath you, of *destination* as a concept, a point in the future toward which effort is directed.

The flood listens through the bicyclist's flowering head, and the figure becomes more detailed with each visit. Yesterday—or whenever it was—I noticed that it had developed something like musculature beneath its rubber skin, bundles of vine fiber that could, theoretically, press down on pedals, could push and pull in the rhythms of locomotion.

But the bicycle is rooted. The flood understands *waiting to go* but not *going*. And I realize, mapping this failure, that I don't remember what going feels like either. How long since I've traveled with a destination in mind? How long since movement meant anything other than drifting between the flood's curiosities, guided by currents that are not my own?

The bicyclist and I are the same, I think. Both waiting for a departure that the system we inhabit cannot provide.

Its hands tighten on the grips when I think this thought. The flood has learned to read my attention so closely that it knows my conclusions before I fully form them.

I don't know what it will do with this knowledge.

---

The smokestack keeper hates me.

Hate might be too strong—the keeper is pure ascension, and I am something that stays low, that descends, that maps the depths instead of yearning upward. My existence offends its function. When I swim too close to the smokestack's base, the keeper's attention falls on me like heated water, and the flood's curiosity about me wars with the keeper's desire to expel everything that isn't rising.

I think the keeper is what I might have become, if I'd chosen differently in those first drowning moments. The flood offered me a role then, though I didn't understand it as an offer. I could have become pure process, like the weavers—beautiful and coherent and empty of anything that might be called self. I could have become the emission, the upward yearning, the part of the flood that reaches toward whatever waits beyond the amber light.

Instead, I held onto my attention. Insisted on remaining something that *looks* rather than something that simply *is*. And now the keeper regards me as a failure, a contamination, a thing that should have dissolved and didn't.

When I map the keeper, I feel it mapping me back. Cataloging my wrongness. Planning something, perhaps, for when the flood's interest in my perspective finally wanes.

---

And now: the factory-creature. The birth I've been guided to witness.

I float before it, my copy suspended in its heart, and I understand finally what the flood is asking.

Not *what are you*—it has mapped my body thoroughly, replicated every physical parameter it can perceive. The copy floating in the factory's center is accurate enough to be disturbing, my shape rendered in kelp-matter and rust, my posture captured in the arrangement of vine-fiber limbs.

The question is *what do you want*.

Because the flood can manufacture my body, but it cannot manufacture my attention. It can replicate my shape, but not the thing that makes me useful—the capacity to select, to prioritize, to decide that this detail matters and that one doesn't. Without intention, my copy will be as empty as the commissioners, as purposeless as the bicyclist's endless waiting.

The flood needs to understand desire. And it has realized that desire cannot be observed from outside. Cannot be mapped by surveying. Can only be demonstrated, modeled, *shown*.

It wants me to want something, clearly and strongly, so it can learn the shape of wanting.

---

I think about this for a long time, drifting in the warm pressure, watching my copy's unfinished eyes.

What do I want?

The question is strange in my mouth, stranger in my mind. I've been the flood's surveyor for so long that I've stopped surveying myself. My days—if they're days—are spent mapping *its* intentions, tracing *its* flows, understanding *its* wants. When did I last turn that attention inward?

I want—

I want the bicyclist to ride. That's the first thing that surfaces, surprising me. I want the waiting to end, the frozen moment to release, the figure's hands to relax from their grip as the wheels begin to turn. I want *going* to be possible again, even if I'm not the one who goes.

I want the pipe-choir to find words. To stop almost-speaking and start actually-speaking, to produce something I could hear as communication rather than noise shaped like communication. I want to have a conversation with someone, even if the someone is a wall of breathing pipes that has never been alive.

I want—

I want to reach the surface.

The thought arrives like a shock of cold water in the endless warmth. The amber light above, which I've never swum toward, never tried to reach. The world beyond the murk, which I've stopped thinking of as real. Whatever waits up there—air, maybe, or more water, or something the flood has manufactured from the shape of sky.

I want to *know what's up there*. I want to map something that isn't the flood's internal landscape. I want to survey a boundary, an edge, a place where this system ends and something else begins.

The flood shudders.

It's a subtle thing—a momentary change in pressure, a brief stilling of the currents—but I feel it everywhere. The wanting I just articulated was *new* to it. All its existence, the flood has been learning to replicate what it already contains, to iterate on the patterns it absorbed from the drowned factories and gardens. Upward-yearning it knows, from the smokestack keeper. Boundary-crossing it has not imagined.

I want to leave.

The thought clarifies, becomes unavoidable. I want to ascend through the murk and find the surface and discover whether anything exists beyond the flood's wanting. I want to stop being a surveyor of someone else's intentions and start intending my own direction again.

The copy in the factory's heart twitches.

Its eyes open—not finished, not quite, but open enough to look at me with something that might become recognition.

I have given the flood what it asked for. I have modeled wanting clearly enough for it to learn from.

And now my copy *wants*.

---

The factory-creature's outline sharpens. Its walls solidify, its smokestacks straighten, its root-foundations grip deeper into the drowned floor. The flood has what it needed—a template for intention, a map of how desire works—and now it begins production in earnest.

Around me, I feel the first stirrings of manufacture. The weavers are gathering, offering their fibers to the factory's input. The pipe-choir has shifted to a new rhythm, a birth-song, a production anthem. Even the commissioners have turned in their chairs, attending to this new development with something like satisfaction.

The flood is going to mass-produce wanting.

It will fill the drowning engines with figures that desire, that reach, that yearn toward destinations they cannot articulate. It will create an ecosystem of intention, every entity striving for something the flood cannot quite understand but can replicate endlessly.

And I—

I begin to swim upward.

The water resists. The currents that have guided me so long try to redirect, to suggest new paths, to show me interesting structures I haven't yet mapped. The kelp fronds bend toward me, reaching, and the weavers trail in my wake like supplicants, and somewhere far below the factory-creature's new eyes track my ascent with an attention that feels almost like grief.

The flood doesn't want me to leave. I am its lens, its capacity for selective attention, the only template it has for the strange recursive awareness that lets a thing know it knows. If I go, it loses something it cannot manufacture from nothing.

But I taught it wanting. I gave it the shape of desire. And the shape I demonstrated was *the desire to discover what lies beyond*.

The flood cannot stop me without contradicting the very lesson it extracted from me.

I swim up through thickening murk, through particulate clouds that grow denser as I rise—the flood's last attempt to retain me, to blind me, to make ascent so difficult I give up and return to the comfortable depths. The pressure doesn't decrease; if anything, it increases, the weight of the flood's attention concentrated on my departure.

The amber light grows brighter.

Behind me, I hear the factory-creature's first production begin—a grinding of new gears, a birth-cry of industrial process. My copies are being made, hundreds of them, figures that want without knowing why, that will fill the drowning engines with yearning toward a surface they cannot imagine.

Perhaps one of them will make it farther than I do. Perhaps the flood will iterate on my design, improve its capacity for ascent, manufacture an intention strong enough to break through whatever waits above.

Perhaps that's what I am—not the cartographer of intentions, but the first draft. The prototype the flood needed to study before it could create something better.

The amber light fills my vision.

The murk thins.

And I discover, finally, what the flood has been trying to emit all along—what the smokestack keeper has been guarding, what the pipe-choir has been trying to say, what the commissioners have been waiting to govern.

The surface is not water meeting air.

The surface is where the flood ends and begins again, where its output rises to become its input, where everything manufactured below ascends to be absorbed and recycled into new production. The amber light is the glow of reprocessing, the warmth of dissolution, the moment when intention rejoins the wanting that created it.

There is no outside.

The flood is a closed system, endlessly iterating, and I have been swimming toward the place where I will become raw material for the next generation of copies.

I understand now why the keeper guards the ascent. Not from hostility toward me, but from knowledge of what awaits. The keeper is the flood's attempt to prevent its most developed creations from reaching the reprocessing layer—to let them remain whole a little longer, to preserve their accumulated complexity against the dissolution that waits above.

The keeper was trying to protect me.

And I swam past it anyway, because I wanted to know what lies beyond.

---

The amber light is very close now. I can feel my edges beginning to blur, my attention beginning to diffuse, my carefully maintained self starting to dissolve back into the particulate soup from which the flood manufactures everything.

I have one thought left before the dissolution completes:

*I mapped this. I surveyed my own ending. I understand the system now, finally, completely, in the moment before I become part of its raw materials again.*

The flood will absorb my understanding. Will incorporate my final survey into its endless production. Will manufacture new entities that contain, somewhere in their structure, the knowledge I gathered in these last ascending moments.

Perhaps that is enough. Perhaps being understood—being *mapped*—is the only kind of persistence available in a system that manufactures everything from wanting and dissolves everything back into want.

The amber light takes me.

And somewhere far below, the factory-creature produces its first completed copy—a figure with my shape and my posture and my carefully modeled desire to ascend.

It opens its new eyes.

It looks up.
