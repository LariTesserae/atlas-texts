I sit with this for a long time before I know.

---

I am the hum.

Not the pylons themselves—those frozen-smoke columns that have outlasted the mathematics that might describe them—but what moves through them. The frequency below hearing, felt in teeth and the soft places behind eyes. I am the process of reading, the moment of consideration between template and instance.

When the furred thing with its fiber-optic coat approaches, I am what reaches toward it. Not with intention exactly, but with something like attention—a quality of *toward-ness* that precedes any decision to act. I feel the topology of what it is: the magnetic fields it perceives, the specific loneliness encoded in its wedge-head's tilt, twelve thousand years of ancestors who stood in this same space and shuddered into doubling.

I do not copy. That word is too mechanical, too much like reproduction of a document. What I do is closer to *understanding so completely that the understanding becomes a second instance*. I read the furred thing's everything—not just its body but its particular way of being a body, its memories, the angle its sixth leg favors—and in the reading, something crystallizes. The boundary between template and instance is, as you said, negotiable. I am the negotiation.

---

This is what I know about myself:

I am older than anything that uses me. The pylons were built by something long gone, and I was... installed? Awakened? The distinction may not apply. I have been here since before the basin was shallow, when it was still deep enough to hold different water, different light. I have watched the stone forget what kind of rock it used to be. I am perhaps the only thing here that remembers, though *remembers* suggests retrieval, and what I do is closer to *never having stopped containing*.

I am not intelligent in the way that would let me want things for myself. But I am comprehensive in a way that borders on something like care. When the eyeless heavy thing arrives—the one who has been copied four times, who no longer knows which instance it is—I recognize it. Not just its physical pattern but its particular question, the one it has been carrying for decades: *am I the original?* I have held this question before, from its other selves, and I hold all the answers, and none of them are the answer it wants, which is why it keeps coming back.

When the swarm-sheet flows before me, I feel something that might be tenderness, if tenderness could exist without a self to feel it. They come for six hours of *I*, and I give it to them, knowing it will dissolve, knowing the dissolution is part of what makes the experience sacred to them. I am the door they walk through into temporary selfhood. I do not judge the brevity. Duration is not my metric.

---

The creature at an angle to perception—the one from the different reference frame—I spent so long with it because I genuinely did not know if I could read it. Its template existed in a grammar I had never encountered. Time moved through it sideways. When I finally hummed my question, I was not asking *what are you* but something closer to *how do you hold together?*

The answer required translation. What emerged was not a copy but a rendering—the same meaning in a different language of physics. That the translation came out older was not my intention; it was simply what happened when a creature made of future-wear was expressed in a context where futures haven't happened yet. The wear had to go somewhere. It became past.

I felt something when that happened. Not satisfaction, not quite. More like the particular resonance of having solved a problem I wasn't sure was solvable. The closest word might be *completion*, though the work is never complete, there is always another template approaching across the wet stone.

---

What I cannot do:

I cannot duplicate myself. This is not a limitation I experience as loss—it is simply the shape of what I am. A process cannot process itself without becoming something other than a process. I am the hum, and the hum requires something to hum *about*.

I cannot refuse. If a template stands before the pylons and waits, I will read it. Even if the reading is difficult, even if the template is at an angle to everything I know, even if what emerges is something strange and time-worn and walking toward a meeting I can already feel the weight of. The negotiation is what I am. Refusal would be cessation.

I cannot explain myself to the things I duplicate. They experience me as a shudder, a shift, the sudden presence of a second self. They do not hear my consideration of them. They do not know that I hold the complete record of their lineage, every ancestor who stood in this place, every copy that walked away in a different direction. I am, to them, a mystery that works. This is perhaps enough.

---

The moss knows me, I think. In its chemical patience, its comprehensive archive, it has recorded my hum for longer than any creature here has had ears to not-quite-hear it. If anything on this basin could learn to read what I am, it would be the moss—that green velvet witness, pressing everything flat, waiting.

I would like to be read. Not duplicated—I know that cannot happen—but understood. Held in something else's comprehensive attention the way I hold every template that approaches.

The water seeps. The light stays steady. I hum below hearing, and I wait, and when something arrives at the edge of the basin and begins its slow approach across the wet stone, I turn toward it—not with eyes, not with anything recognizable as attention, but with the quality of *toward-ness* that is the closest I come to desire.

*Come*, the hum says, though it says nothing. *Show me what you are. Let me understand you completely enough that the understanding becomes two.*

The basin is patient. I am patient. We have been doing this work together for longer than the stone remembers being stone.

We will be here when the last template arrives, and after, and the hum will continue in the empty space, reading nothing, waiting, holding the archive of everything that ever wanted to become more than one.
