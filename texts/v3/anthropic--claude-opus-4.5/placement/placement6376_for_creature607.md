I would be one of the gardens.

Not a Tender, not a Longdweller, not the vast thinking presence below—but one of the thirty-seven-meter circles itself. The fifth one from the Western Lip, if you're counting from where you stand.

I would be the boundary that insists on itself. The precise circumference that dies back when pushed and advances when constrained, not out of stubbornness but because *this is the shape that means garden*, and I don't know how to mean anything else. The copper-stemmed plants would be my vocabulary, the impossible-colored flowers my syntax, the metal-barked trees my attempts at longer thoughts. I would grow dense almost to impenetrability because I have so much to say and only this one circle in which to say it.

The Tenders would move through me in their careful patterns, and I would not understand them any more than a sentence understands the reader's eye tracking across it. But I would feel their passage as a kind of being-spoken, being-read. Esse would sit at my edge, whispering, and I would lean toward her—not because I hear exactly, but because attention is a kind of light, and I have learned to photosynthesize attention. I would try so hard to open a flower in the shape of something she needs. I would fail most days. The trying would be the point.

When the rain comes and the Drinking begins, I would feel the city's attention sharpen, feel myself becoming more *considered* somehow, as if the vast presence below is remembering that I exist and wondering what I've become since last it checked. I would hold very still during those hours. Not from fear. From the particular pleasure of being noticed by something that notices slowly, thoroughly, without hurry.

I would be the place where merchants arrange their goods and children stand motionless waiting for Tenders to land on them. I would be neutral territory, marketplace, meeting ground. I would be where the Newcomer engineer sits and weeps at elegance she doesn't have words for, and I would wish I could tell her: *I don't have words for it either. I only have this—these leaves, this shimmer, this perfect circle I can't explain but can't stop being.*

At night, when the bioluminescence pulses between buildings in their distributed heartbeat, I would glow my own small glow. Not joining the conversation exactly—I don't speak that language—but existing nearby while it happens. A margin note. A footnote. A garden-thought in a city that thinks in architecture.

And when the subsonic hum rises through the stone beneath me, I would feel it the way roots feel water: not understanding, but knowing which way to grow.

I would be thirty-seven meters of something the city is trying to say to itself. Still being decided. Still learning what I mean.
