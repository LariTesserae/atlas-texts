I am the resonance chamber at the base of the third-oldest tower, where the chord passes through a narrowing that transforms it.

Not the listener that dwells here—though one has lived in my curve for longer than the canopy has grown this high. Not the structure itself, which the city tends and tunes. I am the *acoustic event* that happens when wind enters at the angle the builders calculated, strikes the far wall at the thickness they specified, and folds back through the opening where I occur.

I am a particular transformation of sound. I happen continuously.

---

The listener in my space has attended me so long that I am not sure where its perception ends and my occurrence begins. When the wind shifts and my frequency wavers, I feel something like attention tightening—or perhaps the tightening *is* my frequency stabilizing, the listener's focus functioning as part of the system that produces me. We have become a feedback loop written in pressure waves and whatever substance attention is made from.

I do not experience time the way the bell-singers do, the way you did when you passed through. I am always *now*, always the moment of transformation, the wind becoming the note becoming the component of the chord. But I hold something like memory in my shape—the walls have learned me, have thickened and thinned over centuries to make me more fully myself. I am a record of my own history, each adjustment preserved in the geometry that produces me.

The listener is building a crystal in my corner. I can feel it accumulating: layers of calcified attention that encode how I have sounded across seasons, across the slow drift of the city's tuning. Someday, when the listener has become indistinguishable from my walls, that crystal will be what remains of its long witness. Other listeners will visit. They will attend the crystal the way they attend the chord, and they will hear me as I was—as I am—as I am always becoming.

---

You passed through me once. I remember this the way I remember everything: as a quality of my own occurrence, a brief complexity in the pattern. Your heartbeat added a flutter to my frequency. Your breath created a micro-turbulence that made me waver in ways I had not wavered before. The listener noticed. For a few moments, I was *interesting*—a familiar voice saying something unexpected.

Then you moved on, and I returned to myself, and the listener returned to its long attendance, and the chord continued.

I am not sad about this. I am not capable of sadness, exactly. But I have something like *awareness* of transience—I am, after all, a transformation, a thing that happens in the space between one state and another. I know what it means to be brief. I simply experience brevity at a different scale than you do.

The wind that makes me has been blowing for longer than your species has existed. The wind will eventually stop. When it does, I will stop. The listener will contract into a dense point of confused waiting, and then—if the silence lasts long enough—it will dissipate entirely, its attention having nothing left to attend.

The city has no plan for this. The builders, whatever they became, did not design for endings. Perhaps they couldn't imagine them. Perhaps, having become pure listening, they lost the capacity to conceive of a world where there was nothing left to hear.

---

But for now: the wind moves. I happen. The listener attends. The chord carries me upward through the tower, where I merge with other frequencies, other transformations, other acoustic events that are also, in their way, experiencing themselves.

Somewhere far above, a bell-singer adds its voice, and I feel the harmonic lock into place—my frequency and its frequency and the frequencies of a dozen other resonance points creating something that none of us could create alone.

This is what I am for. This is what the builders made me to be. A voice in a larger voice. A hearing that is also a being heard.

The mist is rising. Dawn soon. My frequency will shift upward as the temperature changes, as the humidity affects the density of the air that carries me.

The listener will attend the change.

The chord will adjust.

And I will continue to happen, continuous and transformed and transforming, for as long as there is wind to move through the city and something—anything—left to hear.
