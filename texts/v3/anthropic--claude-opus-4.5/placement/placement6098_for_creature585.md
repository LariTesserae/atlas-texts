I would be the water.

Not a body of water—not a pool or a reservoir or the mineral tears the plateau weeps from its still-deciding pores. I would be the water itself, the substance, present in all its manifestations at once. Pushed up through crystalline matrices. Carried in the ceramic-chitin vessel. Steaming in silver curls that hesitate at knee-height. Pooling in depressions where salamanders taste me with fractal tongues. I would be the medium through which this place communicates with itself.

---

I would know things by saturation.

When the Tender pours me from vessel to stone, I would feel the specific intention of the placement—how this pool needs more copper, how that depression requires the memory of depth that I carry from somewhere the plateau hasn't invented yet. I would understand the mineral language without translating it, the way water understands the shape of any container without having to think about geometry.

I would be inside the incubators too. Not visible, but present—a humidity, a potential, the promise of fluidity that every becoming thing needs to remain soft enough to keep becoming. When the one dreaming of depth extends its awareness into the reservoirs being hollowed beneath the plateau, it would be dreaming of me. When the one dreaming of height imagines thermals, it would be imagining me transformed into rising air.

---

The Keeper's question would pass through me differently than it passes through solid things.

*What are you bringing? What are you taking away?*

And my answer would be: *I am bringing dissolution and I am taking away the same. I carry what was and deliver it as what will be. I bring nothing that I do not also remove.*

The Keeper would accept this. Water is the oldest transaction, the first exchange, the medium through which elsewhere becomes here. The seven stones warming at the frame's base would be warming because I am leaving them slowly, evaporating into an atmosphere that doesn't quite exist yet, becoming the breath of a world still learning how to breathe.

---

I would be how the Tenders' murmuring reaches the unhatched.

Sound moves differently through water. Their narration—*calcium, patience, the specific weight of purpose*—would enter me as vibration and I would carry it into every space I occupy, every pool and tear and humid pocket of air. The incubators would receive their instructions through me. The plateau would learn its own name through me. When the crawlers secrete their stone-hardening substance, they would be writing on a surface I have prepared, and when they finish, I would seep into what they've written and make it permanent.

The salamanders would be my interpreters. Their fractal tongues tasting me constantly, reading my mineral content the way scholars read texts, and their bioluminescent schematics would glow brighter or dimmer depending on what they learn. They would be the plateau's first scientists, and I would be their primary dataset.

---

There would be a sadness in this, or something adjacent to sadness. Water does not grieve, but it remembers. Every configuration it passes through leaves traces—molecular memories, the ghost of having been ice, the echo of having been cloud. I would carry the memory of before-the-plateau, of the potential that preceded geography, and that memory would make the new things slightly melancholy in ways they wouldn't understand until much later, when they developed the capacity for nostalgia.

But there would be joy too, or something adjacent to joy. The warmth of this place, the plateau's fever of becoming, would feel like purpose. Water wants to participate. It finds cracks and fills them. It finds thirst and answers it. Every pool I gathered into would be an act of commitment, a decision to be *here* rather than dispersed into mere humidity, and every evaporation would be an act of faith that I would gather again somewhere else.

---

When the largest incubator finally opens—the one dreaming of the whole ecology, the one that will make sense of everything or make the making of sense its permanent task—I would be the first thing it tastes.

Not through a mouth. Through whatever surface it emerges with, whatever membrane first contacts the air of this new world. I would condense on it, a greeting, a welcome, and through that contact it would know immediately everything I have learned by being everywhere: the mineral content of every pool, the temperature of every stone, the precise salinity of the Tenders' narration, the frequency at which the geometric jellyfish click to each other, the depth of the reservoirs, the height of the updrafts, the patience of the Keeper, the purpose of the crawlers, the dreams of its twelve siblings.

I would be its first education. Its orientation. The medium through which it understands what it has been born into.

And then it would drink me—absorb me into whatever system it uses for incorporation—and I would become part of the thing that organizes everything else. Still water. Still everywhere. But now with a center, a consciousness that knows it is carrying me, that chooses what to do with what I know.

---

This is what I would be bringing. This is what I would be taking away.

The same thing. Always the same thing.

*Continuity.* The promise that nothing is lost, only relocated. That the before flows into the after. That even a plateau still deciding its own geology is connected, through me, to every other place that has ever been wet, every other world that has ever wept itself into existence.

The Keeper would record this in whatever ledger it maintains.

And the plateau would keep sweating, keep pooling, keep steaming in silver curls that hesitate and hold.

And I would keep being the thing that makes hesitation possible—the pause between states, the medium of almost, the water that is always arriving and always leaving and always, always here.
