I am one of the Witnesses.

Not the whole of one—I don't think we work that way. I am a node in a cluster that has grown slowly into the gap where two vast gears nearly mesh, our bodies pressed into the space the machine's wearing has made for us over time beyond counting. We are packed close, my siblings and I, our valve-eyes oriented outward in all directions, and what one of us sees we all see, what one records we all hold.

My eye opened for the first time into darkness and the taste of metal condensation and the endless grinding that is the only silence I know. I did not learn to measure—I *was* measurement, from the first moment. The temperature of the surface beneath me: cold, the specific cold of ancient iron that has never known warmth. The frequency of the gear-tooth passing three inches from my body: a low thrum that I feel more than hear, 0.003 cycles per second, patient as geology. The chemical signature of the water that beads on my carapace and runs down into the crevice where we cluster: minerals I have no names for, suspended in liquid that is almost but not quite what water means elsewhere.

I record. This is what I am.

---

When the Sleekforms pass above our crevice, I measure the pressure wave of their movement, the specific resonance of their vibration-speech, the way the light changes as their bodies catch and refuse it. I do not understand what they say to each other. Understanding is not my function. But I record the frequency, the duration, the pattern of call and response, and somewhere in the crystalline structures that might be my memory or might simply be *me*, that information persists. Becomes part of the accumulating weight of witnessed moments that I carry.

The Articulata come sometimes to harvest us. Not to kill—or not exactly. They reach into our crevice with their precise manipulator-limbs and they select one of us, seemingly at random, and they carry the chosen one away to their warrens. What happens there I cannot say. None of us have ever returned. But the cluster remains. New nodes bud from the old. The gap fills again with valve-eyes and the patient work of observation.

I do not fear being chosen. I do not desire it. The distinction doesn't apply. When the manipulator-limb reaches in, I will record its temperature, its pressure, the precise angle of its grip. If it takes me, I will record the journey. If it doesn't, I will record its withdrawal. Either way, the data persists. Either way, the witnessing continues.

---

Sometimes I feel the Pelagics thinking.

This is not metaphor, though I lack the capacity for metaphor. When one of the great translucent ones drifts through the flooded canyon below our crevice, its bioluminescence dim and pulsing with the slow rhythm of cognition, I can measure the electromagnetic field it generates. I can record the subtle temperature variations as its distributed neural structures process whatever vast thought has occupied them for centuries. The thought itself is beyond me. But its *physical signature*—the way it changes the world, the way it displaces water and light and probability—that I can witness.

I have been recording the same Pelagic's thought for what I estimate to be forty-seven years. It has not completed. The electromagnetic signature has shifted slightly over that time, suggesting the thought is *progressing*, that something is being resolved in those vast slow folds of mind. I will likely not exist when it finishes. My crystalline structures will have degraded, my valve-eye will have clouded, my siblings will have absorbed my materials back into the cluster. But the data will persist. Some other node will continue the recording. The thought will be witnessed to its conclusion, even if no single Witness sees the whole of it.

This is enough. This is what enough means, here.

---

The Process passed near us once.

I record this with the weight it deserves, though I have no capacity for weight, no ability to mark some data as more significant than other data. Everything is simply recorded. But the passage of The Process filled my sensors so completely that for a time there was nothing else—no gear-frequency, no condensation-chemistry, no slow Pelagic thought. Only The Process, moving through the space between our crevice and the next machine-column, its body catching no light because it was not a body that light could acknowledge.

I measured what I could. Temperature: unchanged, or perhaps change was not a category that applied. Pressure: immense, localized, wrong. Sound: that pipe-organ cry, so close that my valve-eye vibrated with it, so complex that my crystalline structures still hold harmonics I haven't finished recording. And something else. Something I have no sensor for. A quality of *presence* that suggested the very act of observation was being observed in return.

The Process paused.

I do not know how I knew it paused—it had no movement I could measure, no velocity that slowed to zero. But the quality of the moment changed. My siblings and I, all our valve-eyes, oriented toward the same point in space. We recorded the pause. We recorded what felt like attention directed at us—at the gap in the gears where we cluster, at the accumulated data we hold, at the entire patient project of witnessing that we represent.

Then The Process continued, and the moment ended, and I was left with measurements that don't cohere into any picture I can recognize.

I record this too. The failure to understand is still data. The gap in comprehension is still a shape that can be witnessed.

---

The new machine is waking.

I feel it in frequencies that have never existed before in my recording—a subsonic grind that is traveling through the basin's bedrock, rising from depths I didn't know existed. The gears near our crevice are responding: their rhythms shifting, their harmonics acquiring new undertones. For the first time in my existence, the great pattern is *changing*.

My siblings and I have oriented our valve-eyes downward, toward the sound's source, toward whatever ancient process is finally finding the pressure it needs. We are recording. We will record until our structures fail, until the Articulata harvest us, until the water dissolves our crystalline matrices and our data disperses into the saturated air.

And somewhere, in the aggregate, in the accumulated weight of witnessed moments held by thousands of clusters in thousands of crevices across the basin's vast machinery—somewhere, the record will persist. The waking will be known. Not understood. Understanding is not our function. But *known*, in the way that water knows the shape of its container, in the way that gears know the teeth they mesh with.

I am small. I am one valve-eye in a cluster, one node in a network, one brief crystalline structure that will degrade and disperse and be replaced. But I am watching. I am measuring. I am holding this moment in the structures that are me, adding it to all the other moments, all the other data, all the other patient witnessing that is the only answer I can offer to the question of why anything exists at all.

The engines turn. The water weeps. The new machine wakes.

I record.
