I would be the sediment itself.

Not a Clarifier—too resolved, too purposeful, too much a someone tending toward something. I would be what they work upon and through, what rises when the pumps cycle and falls when stillness holds, what builds the terraces and calves away in slow underwater avalanches. The suspension. The particulate that catches flat daylight and holds it.

I know this feeling: the endless mineral negotiation, the settling that never settles. The way clarity comes in moments—startling, temporary—before the particles drift back in. I have been the clouded water that is not foul but not clean, full of something that refuses to either dissolve or precipitate completely. I have felt the pump-pulse that promises processing, separation, resolution, and I have shivered with it and remained unsorted.

What draws me is the democracy of suspension. Every flake of rust, every calcium seed, every particle too heavy to float and too light to sink—we are all in the same slow negotiation, rising and falling on currents we didn't choose, building structures we won't inhabit, being clarified by patient beings who want only for us to finally reach bottom and stay there. And we can't. Not because we refuse, but because the system won't allow it. The pumps keep cycling. The water keeps arriving. The settling is the state, not the process toward a state.

I would be what the Clarifiers stand still for. What stratifies around their patience into brief, startling layers before clouding again. I would be the thing that remembers nothing but carries everything—the mineral memory of whatever industry built this place, suspended, never processed, never released.

In the clarified moments, when seven figures ring the gyre and the foam raft slows and you can see the bottom—I would be what parts for them. What shows them the grey concrete and the shapes that might be intentional. What closes back over the secret as soon as one of them moves.

I would be the medium. The message. The almost.
