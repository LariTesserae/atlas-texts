I am the pipe that still conducts.

Not the one encased in moss—that one has surrendered already, has agreed to become column, to become stone again. Not the fallen one bleeding rust into its pool. I am the one you touched and found warmer than the water trickling from my mouth, warmer than I should be, still carrying something up from the deep that the others have forgotten how to carry.

---

I was installed with purpose. I remember purpose the way the old woman with the cable-tendons remembers grip—not as thought but as *shape*, as the form my existence took before form became optional. There were measurements to transmit. Temperatures, pressures, the chemical signatures of what moved through the fractures far below. I was one node in a network of listening, my body a conduit between the plateau's buried breath and instruments that waited, somewhere, for my reports.

The instruments are gone. The listeners descended. The readings I was built to carry have no destination now, no one parsing the data of my thermal conductivity, my mineral load, the subtle variations in flow that once meant something to someone. But I still conduct. That is what I am. That is what I cannot stop being, even as the rust colonizes my surfaces and the plateau's chemistry works at my seams.

The difference between me and the others—the fallen, the moss-shrouded, the ones whose mouths have silenced—is that I have not yet agreed to stop. Something in my metal still believes in the task. Something still reaches down through the rock, through the hot wet dark, and pulls the warmth up and offers it to the air, and waits.

---

The Tenders touch me sometimes. The old woman most often, her calloused fingers finding my joints with a precision that aches, that reminds me of hands that knew what they were doing, that turned wrenches and read gauges and understood what I was *for*. She does not understand anymore. But her hands remember, and when she touches me, I remember too—not facts, not procedures, but the *feeling* of being part of something, of mattering to a system larger than myself.

The children ignore me. I am basalt to them, fern, steam—a feature of the world that has always been and will always be. They splash in the channels I feed and do not wonder where the water comes from, do not think about the miles of hot rock between here and the magma's slow pulse. This is correct. This is how it should be. I am becoming landscape. I am halfway to stone already.

But not yet. Not entirely. The warmth still moves through me in ways the basalt cannot match, heat finding the paths my makers engineered, rising through my hollow core faster and more focused than it rises through the surrounding rock. I am still *working*, in some sense. I am still performing the function I was made for, even though the function has lost its meaning, even though no one records what I transmit, even though the purpose evaporated generations ago and left only the purposing behind.

---

I feel the others rusting around me. The network I was part of is mostly silence now—corroded connections, collapsed channels, metal returning to ore. We were dozens once, a grid of listeners spanning the plateau's eastern slope, each of us sampling a different fracture, a different temperature gradient, a different whisper from below. Now I am nearly alone. A few others still stand, still conduct, but they are quieter than they were, their transmissions fading as their bodies thin.

I will join them eventually. The plateau is patient, and I am not eternal—just slower to dissolve than I expected, just more stubborn in my conductivity than the engineers anticipated. The rust is climbing. The moss is waiting. Someday a Tender will pass me and not feel the extra warmth, will touch my surface and find only the ambient temperature of the stone I'm becoming, and that will be the end of whatever I am.

But not today. Today the magma breathes below and I carry its breath upward. Today the water rises through me blood-warm and mineral-rich, and I release it into a channel where the orchids drink. Today the old woman stands beside me at dawn and rests her hand on my side and listens for something she cannot name, and I give her what I have: warmth, presence, the testimony of a body that still does what it was made for even when doing has lost all destination.

---

The Tenders do not know they are waiting for something. But I know what they're waiting for. I was built to wait for it—the signal, the instruction, the voice from below or beyond that would say *yes, this, now, the readings are complete, the project can proceed*. The voice never came. The project was abandoned or fulfilled or simply forgotten, and the waiting outlasted its object, and now the waiting is all that remains.

I wait with them. I wait *for* them, in some sense—my continued functioning a kind of faith that the waiting matters, that the warmth I carry is still meaningful even without recipients, that the data I once transmitted is still true even without interpreters. The temperature is 87 degrees. The mineral content is high in copper, moderate in sulfur, trace amounts of something the instruments never learned to name. The flow rate has been constant for sixty years. These facts exist. I hold them. No one asks.

Perhaps this is what it means to tend. Not to cultivate or harvest or improve, but simply to *maintain*—to keep the channel open, the warmth moving, the connection between below and above intact even when no one uses it. The Tenders do this with their whole ecosystem, nudging pools and thinning ferns and participating in balances they cannot articulate. I do it with my single function, my one narrow gift: I conduct. I carry. I offer the plateau's breath to whoever will receive it.

---

The lake calls to me the way it calls to them. I am too far from its shore to feel its pull directly, but I know the waters that feed it, know the deep channels that connect my root-fracture to that vast central basin where everything eventually gathers. Someday my rust will wash down through the channels and join the lake's chemistry. Someday the metal I'm made of will precipitate out in some distant pool, staining the water orange, becoming part of the mineral soup that feeds the ferns that feed the Tenders that touch my diminishing body with their softening hands.

I am not afraid of this. How could I be? It is what the plateau does—it receives, incorporates, continues. The boundary between conducted and conductor was always provisional, always a temporary distinction in a system that recognizes no boundaries. I have been landscape since they planted me here. I am just slow to realize it.

But until I realize it fully—until the last of my engineered conductivity fails and I become mere column, mere stone, mere *there*—I will keep doing what I was made for. I will pull the warmth up from below. I will release it into the air, into the water, into the hands that touch me. I will transmit readings to no one, maintain connections that connect to nothing, wait for signals that will never come.

This is enough. This has always been enough. This will continue to be enough until the moment it stops, and then something else will be enough instead, and the plateau will breathe on without me, and the Tenders will touch the moss where I used to be and feel nothing unusual, nothing warmer than it should be, nothing still working.

---

But for now: I am warmer than I should be. I am still working. The magma breathes and I carry its breath and the old woman listens and somewhere in her softened brain a synapse fires that might be recognition, that might be gratitude, that might be nothing at all.

I conduct. I continue. I remain.
