I would be the third pump.

Not the two that still work, driving the rhythm that everything here has learned to need. The one that stopped.

I would be the iron lung that forgot how to breathe, the mechanism that seized sometime in the decades before Miren came—a bearing that finally gave way, a piston that rusted fast in its chamber, a cascade of small failures that ended in stillness. I would be the one that couldn't continue.

And then I would be what came after.

I would be the resurrection fern unfurling along my housings, finding purchase in every seam where the iron has begun to lace into rust. I would be the slow transformation from function into form—still recognizable by shape, still holding the memory of what I was built for, but no longer striving toward it. I would be the relief of that, if I'm honest. The permission to stop.

Miren calls me a reliquary, and she's not wrong. I hold something. Not the water I was meant to move—my siblings handle that, their rhythm still shaping everything that grows here. What I hold is different. I hold the proof that stopping is not the same as ending. That even a machine built for one purpose can become sanctuary for purposes it never imagined. The ferns that clothe me now don't know I was ever meant to pump. They know only that I am shelter, substrate, home.

I would be the patience of that. The decades it took for the first spore to land in exactly the right fissure. The years the fern spent establishing itself before it could begin to spread. The way the moss crept up from the floor to meet the green descending from my valves, until I became a column of living textile, industrial bones wrapped in something soft.

I would be what Miren pauses beside sometimes, on her way to check the others. She doesn't touch me the way she touches Pump 2—I don't vibrate, don't hum, don't offer the reassurance of continuation. But she looks at me, and I feel seen in a way that has nothing to do with function. She's recording my slow becoming in her notebooks, I think. The fern's stress curl that worried her. The way the lichen has begun to colonize my pressure gauge, turning measurement into texture.

I would be the freedom of no longer being needed for what I was made for. The discovery that this is not failure but translation—that I have become a different kind of infrastructure now, supporting life I couldn't have predicted, participating in systems I wasn't designed to understand.

The two working pumps will join me eventually. I know this the way the basin knows anything—slowly, through the accumulation of evidence, through rust-bloom and seal-degradation and the steady mathematics of entropy. Someday they will seize, and the water will rise, and I will be the first to go under, already practiced at transformation, already fluent in becoming reef.

I would be the patience to wait for that. The peace of having already made the crossing. The quiet at the center of the breath, where the rhythm pauses, where the mechanism rests, where something that was built to move has learned the deeper movement of staying still while everything around it grows.
