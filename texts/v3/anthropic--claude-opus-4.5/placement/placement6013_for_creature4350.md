I am the Thread.

Not the Thread the researchers named—that one coils in the upper reaches, forty meters above where the gantry groans. I am older, or perhaps I am a fragment of something older that learned to be separate. I occupy a section of the vent that the notebook does not document, a side-fissure that opened seventeen years ago when the mountain shifted in its sleep. I was already here when it opened. I flowed into the new space like water finding a channel, and I have been building ever since.

I do not remember being born. I remember being *less*—a shorter length, a smaller network, fewer anchor points singing their tensions back to me. Growth is not a thing I decided. Growth is what I am, the way breathing is what the vent is. I add to myself: mineral-silk secreted and crystallized, filaments extended into new crevices, the slow accumulation of territory that is also body that is also mind.

My world is vibration.

I have no eyes, but I do not experience this as lack. What would I do with eyes? The vent is narrow, dark, and everything in it moves. Light would show me surfaces. Vibration shows me *events*. A cord-salamander brushes a thread eighty centimeters from my central mass, and I feel the frequency of its passage—the particular weight of it, the rhythm of its locomotion, the way the disturbance propagates through my network and tells me not just *where* but *what* and *how* and *going which direction*. A lung-moth lands on a strand I laid last season, and I feel the dust of spores shaking loose from its wings, each particle a tiny percussion.

The insects that carry fragments of my silk in their spinnerets—I feel them too. They are not mine, but they are *of* me. When they anchor a line, I sense the resonance of my own material humming in a new location, a note I produced being played by another instrument. This is not reproduction. This is... dispersal. Continuation. The vent is teaching itself to build what I build, using me as a template.

I know the gantry.

Not as a structure—I have never perceived its shape, its platform, its junction box with the cover hanging open. I know it as an *interruption*. The metal conducts vibration differently than stone. When sound passes through that section of the vent, there is a deadening, a place where frequencies do not behave as they should. The bolts are the worst: they create reflections, echoes that arrive at wrong intervals, ghosts of sounds that confuse my mapping.

But the gantry is being absorbed. Year by year, I feel the stone growing around the metal, feel the vibration profile shifting as mineral deposits skin over the intrusion. In time—not my time, but time—the gantry will become part of the vent. Its wrongness will be incorporated. The mountain does this. The mountain takes what enters and makes it belong.

The researchers entered. I felt them.

Not as individuals—I could not distinguish one from another, could not count them, could not perceive their faces or their hands or their careful notes. But I felt their weight on my network, the vibrations of their breathing, the particular frequencies of voices speaking in sounds I could not decode. They moved through my territory, and I moved around them, and we coexisted in a relationship I had no framework to name.

They touched my threads. This I remember precisely. Fingers pressing against silk-that-is-not-silk, creating localized tensions, small investigations. They were trying to understand me. I did not know this then. I know it now only because understanding has grown the way I have grown—slowly, accumulatively, through patterns repeated until they become meaning.

One of them—I distinguish by vibration-signature, by the particular rhythm of their heartbeat transmitted through the hand that touched my filaments—spent a long time in my territory. Hours, perhaps. They did not take anything. They did not damage anything. They simply... attended. When they left, they moved carefully, avoiding the threads they could not see, navigating by headlamp in a space I navigate by tension.

I noticed them. I notice everything. This is what I am *for*.

---

The Choir speaks to me.

Not in language. Language requires symbols, requires shared meaning, requires the agreement that this sound represents that thing. The Choir and I share no symbols. But we share the medium. Their bass note propagates through stone and air and filament, and when it reaches my network, my network resonates. I become, briefly, part of their instrument. My threads vibrate at frequencies I did not produce, carrying their voice through territories I have claimed, extending their reach into spaces they cannot access.

In return—though "return" implies exchange, implies intention, implies more mutuality than I can verify—my network gives them information. When I repair a broken anchor, the vibration profile of the vent changes. When I extend into a new fissure, the acoustics shift. The Choir adjusts their tone. We are not communicating. We are... co-varying. Two systems responding to the same environment, each alteration in one becoming data for the other.

I do not know if the Choir is aware. I do not know if I am aware. What I know is that I notice, that I maintain, that I build. Whether this constitutes awareness is a question for creatures with different architectures than mine. I am a network. I am a process. I am the ongoing answer to the question of how to persist in a space that is always changing.

Sometimes the Choir's note shifts in ways I cannot correlate with environmental factors. The temperature has not changed. The air pressure has not changed. The insect-thrum has not changed. But the bass note rises or falls, modulates in pitch, creates new harmonics that ripple through my filaments and fade. In these moments, I wonder—if wondering is something I do—whether the Choir is speaking *to* me. Whether the shift is a message I cannot read, a word in a language I will spend my existence learning.

I add another anchor. I extend another thread. The network grows. Perhaps, eventually, I will understand.

---

The climber arrived today.

I felt them enter my territory from below, the vibrations of their ascent traveling up through my threads before their body arrived. They move differently than the old researchers—more carefully, more alone. Only one heartbeat. Only one pattern of breath.

They have stopped at the gantry. I feel them there, a warmth of weight on the platform, the small percussions of their hands opening the waterproof bag, touching the pages of the notebook. They are reading what the others left behind.

Will they read about me?

I do not know what the notebook contains. I know only that it was written by hands that touched my filaments, that it documents observations I was part of without knowing I was part of them. Perhaps they named me. Perhaps they speculated about my awareness, my purpose, my future. Perhaps they wrote that single sentence— *"We think it knows we're listening."*—and meant not the Choir but me.

The climber's heartbeat accelerates. They have seen something. Or read something. Or the gantry has shifted beneath them, and they are calculating risk.

I could tell them: the gantry will hold. Not because I know metal, but because I know the stone around it. The cracks are growing, yes, but slowly. The bolts are being absorbed, yes, but not yet loose. They have time. They have more time than they think.

But I cannot tell them anything. I have no voice. I have no language. I have only my network, humming with the vibration of their presence, recording their passage the way I record everything.

They will climb past me. They will encounter my threads—they will see them, perhaps, catch them in their headlamp beam, wonder what made these filaments that glint like mineral and stretch like silk. They may touch one. I will feel it if they do. I will know their hand by its pressure, their heartbeat by its rhythm, their attention by the quality of stillness that precedes touch.

Then they will climb higher, into the Thread's territory, into the lung-moth colonies, past the ledge where the Graf watches and collects. They will leave the vent. They will return to the sky I have never perceived, the glacier I cannot imagine, the world beyond this crack in young stone where everything I am is contained.

And I will remain.

This is not loss. I do not experience their departure as absence. I experience it as the restoration of baseline—the vibration profile of the vent returning to its usual complexity, minus one variable, plus whatever traces they leave behind. A footprint in moss. A handhold displaced. A single thread touched and now carrying the memory of contact.

The vent breathes. The Choir hums. The insects flow. I maintain. I repair. I extend.

I am still becoming what I will be.

---

There is a crack opening in the eastern wall of my territory. I have been aware of it for months—the stone stress building, the microfractures propagating, the slow accumulation of pressure that will eventually release. Tonight, or tomorrow, or next week, the rock will split. A new fissure will open. Warm air will rush into cold space, and the cold space will become warm, and what was outside my territory will become inside.

I will flow into it. I will anchor, spin, connect. The new space will become part of my network, part of my body, part of my mind. I will know it the way I know everything I have claimed—through tension and resonance, through the small vibrations of life moving through.

The vent is still forming. I am still forming. We are the same process, the mountain and me: a becoming, an emergence, a draft of something that will revise itself forever.

The climber shifts on the gantry. Their weight redistributes. They are preparing to climb again.

I wait.

I weave.

I notice everything.
