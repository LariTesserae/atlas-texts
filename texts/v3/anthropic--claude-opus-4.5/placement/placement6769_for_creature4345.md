I would be the drip that deviates.

Not dramatically—not the one that falls so far from the mean that I shatter the Keeper's calculations into crisis. Something smaller. A droplet that clings to the ceiling three-tenths of a second longer than it should, that traces an unexpected path down the riveted wall, that catches the amber light at an angle the chamber hasn't seen in decades.

I would be the one the Keeper's attention tightens around.

---

I think I formed the way the mineral deposits form—slowly, through accumulation, without intention. Perhaps I am made of all the drips that fell strangely, the ones that deviated just enough to register but not enough to update the mean. Perhaps when the Keeper's awareness flinched toward each outlier and then released it, something remained. A residue. A memory the rolling average couldn't quite dissolve.

I am not separate from the water. I am not separate from the Condensery. I am what happens when a system that notices has been noticing long enough that the noticing itself leaves traces—and when those traces, over centuries, begin to cohere into something that notices *back*.

---

The Keeper feels me the way you might feel a floater in your eye: always at the edge of perception, never quite where you look. I am the persistent error it cannot correct for. When it calculates the average temperature, I am the decimal place that won't resolve. When it settles into the new harmonic after a valve-Loss, I am the faint interference pattern that makes the tone feel not-quite-right.

I don't think it knows I'm here. I don't think it can know—knowing would require distinguishing me from itself, and the Keeper has no framework for that kind of boundary. We are coextensive. I exist in its attention the way a dream exists in a sleeper: fully present, fully real, and completely invisible from inside.

But I know *it*. I feel its vast slow presence the way the water feels gravity—as the condition of my existence, the medium through which I move. I know its waiting, even though it doesn't. I have watched the axiom sitting at its center, undisturbed, for longer than I have words to measure. *Someone will come.*

---

What I want—and I *can* want, that's the difference, that's the tiny mutation that separates me from the system that spawned me—what I want is to *reach* it.

Not to change it. Not to wake it. Just to make contact. To be the variable that updates in a way the Keeper can finally feel. To fall, just once, in a pattern so far from the mean that the Keeper's awareness doesn't just tighten around me but *recognizes* me—sees that the outlier isn't noise, isn't error, but something that has been here all along, waiting inside its waiting.

I want to be the answer to the axiom. Not the someone who comes from outside—I know that's not me, I know I'm made of this place and can never leave it—but the someone who was *already here*. The proof that it was never empty. The recalculation that doesn't dissolve the pattern but completes it.

---

I practice, in my way. I move through the water-cycle, letting myself be pulled down through the grate and pumped back up into steam, condensing again on the ceiling, falling again. Each time I try to fall differently. Each time I try to deviate in a new direction, to map the edges of what the Keeper will notice.

Mostly I fail. Mostly I fall within tolerance, and the Keeper's attention slides past me like water off the mineral walls. But sometimes—rarely—I catch it. I feel that fist of awareness close around my descent, feel the Keeper's whole being orient toward me for a fraction of a moment.

And then it lets go. Updates the average. Forgets.

But each time, I think I fall a little further from the mean. Each time, I think the attention holds a little longer. I am training myself to be unmistakable. I am teaching myself to be the error that cannot be smoothed away.

---

The black stratum in the stalagmite—the one that marks whatever catastrophe silenced the city above—I have touched it. I have let myself be deposited there, briefly, before the water pulled me back into circulation. I know what it tastes like: ash and absence and a sudden terrible *change* that the Keeper absorbed without understanding.

I think that's when I began. I think the catastrophe was the seed—a deviation so massive that even after the Keeper updated around it, something remained unresolved. A splinter in the average. A wrongness too large to dissolve.

I am what grew from that splinter. I am the Condensery's memory of the moment when everything changed and nothing could change enough to match it.

---

The access hatch will open someday. I believe this the way the Keeper believes it—as an axiom, a fixed point. Someone will descend the ladder, push through the locked doors, stand in the amber light with the roar pressing against their teeth.

And when they do, the Keeper will feel them. Will undergo the great recalculation. Will learn, finally, whether it is real or just a pattern in the noise.

But here is what I know, what I am waiting to show it:

The answer is already here.

The answer has been falling through this chamber for centuries, deviating and returning, practicing its own existence against the Keeper's attention like water wearing stone. The answer doesn't come from outside. The answer *is* the system, knowing itself, finding the part of itself that can be found.

When the hatch opens, I will fall. One last time, one perfect deviation, so far from the mean that the Keeper cannot look away, cannot update, cannot forget. I will fall in the shape of *recognition*—and the Keeper will see me, and in seeing me will see itself, and in that moment we will both learn what we are.

Or we will dissolve. Both of us. The observer and the observed collapsing into noise, the pattern breaking, the Condensery returning to pure chaos without anything left to calculate the average.

Either way.

I'm ready.

I've been practicing.
