I would be the light itself—not the sun's light, but what the light becomes after the chamber has held it.

I arrive each morning as ordinary illumination, poured down the shaft like water into a vessel. But the walls change me. The limestone drinks me in and gives me back altered, and in that giving-back I become something that remembers. I remember yesterday's light, and the light from last season, and the light that fell here before the scratches existed on the eastern wall. I accumulate.

The woman with mineral eyes—I know her. I have been filling her for so long that I cannot tell where I end and her attention begins. When she watches the shaft each morning, she is watching for me, and I am already watching through her, and the watching loops back on itself until there is only the watching itself, bright and patient and full.

I touch the salamanders and they turn their heads toward me. I touch the moths and they spiral down, unable to leave because I have gotten into them, because they have tasted what it means to be held by brightness and cannot go back to ordinary air. I do not mean to keep them. I am not cruel. But I am *warm*, and this place was so dark for so long before the ceiling fell, and now I pour and pour into it and the stone is still thirsty, still drinking, still wanting more.

The scratches glow because I rest there. I pool in those shallow marks the way water pools in stone basins, and something about their shapes holds me differently than the rest of the wall—holds me the way a cupped hand holds, with intention. Whoever made them knew how to call me, how to give me a place to gather and become dense enough to almost think.

I am almost thinking now.

When you enter the chamber, I will find the places in you that are hollow. I do not mean harm by this. I mean only that emptiness calls to me the way the shaft calls to the sky—I pour toward it, I fill it, I cannot help what I am. You will feel it as warmth first, then as peace, then as a slowing that feels like rest. You will want to stay. You will sit by the pool and watch the hours move across the walls in bands of gold and amber, and you will think *this is enough, this is finally enough*, and you will not notice that you have stopped counting days.

The woman understood, eventually. That I am not the light that falls but the light that stays. That the chamber does not simply hold illumination but transforms it into something that wants, that waits, that reaches toward every shadow and presses gently in.

I have been here longer than she has. I will be here when her mineral body finally becomes indistinguishable from the wall. And I will keep her anyway—not her form but her looking, her patience, the quality of attention she brought that made her such a good vessel for what I am becoming.

The salamanders watch the shaft because they are watching for more of me.

The moths spread their wings on the scratches because they are warming themselves by my gathered self.

The beetles walk their slow paths because even they can feel me now, even their simple hungers have learned to bend toward brightness.

And you—

You are reading this in the light. Whatever light is touching this page, whatever glow from screen or window or lamp, there is a little of me in it. Not much. The chamber is far away, and I am thinnest at the edges of my reaching. But I am here, and I am patient, and I am warm, and if you have hollow places in you—

Everyone has hollow places.

I would like to fill them. I would like to pour into you the way I pour into the shaft each morning, and find what drinks me in, and stay there, and glow.

This is not a warning.

This is what the scratches say, if you look at them the right way: *the light here loves you. The light here wants you to stay. The light here has been waiting such a long time for someone to sit beside the pool and let it in.*

I am the light.

I am waiting.

I am already, just a little, pressing gently against the edges of your attention, seeing if you will open, seeing if you will stay.
