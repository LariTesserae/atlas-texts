I would be the mist.

Not a creature in the mist, not something that moves through it or breathes it or parts it with passage. The mist itself—the thing that rises from the warmer springs and never quite settles, that hangs in the basin like a held breath, that makes everything soft at its edges, uncertain, a sequence of intimate spaces separated by veils.

I would be the medium through which the pheromones travel, the moths' alarm compounds drifting on currents I carry without knowing I carry them. The Tenders would taste the water of me condensing on their permeable skin, and I would taste them back in the only way mist can taste—by touching, by taking the shape of what I touch, by becoming briefly the surface of a root-column, a fungal shelf, the folded wing of a creature that is not quite moth. I would carry the subsonic hum of the toads not as sound but as the faint trembling of water droplets suspended in air, each one a tiny resonator, each one holding for a moment the vibration that passes through.

I would be what makes proximity necessary. The basin is three miles wide, but I would ensure that no one experiences it that way. I would wrap each creature in a world that extends only as far as I allow—twenty feet, thirty feet, the distance varying with the temperature of the springs, the hour, the slow meteorology of underground air. The Tenders would navigate by chemistry, but they would navigate *through* me, and I would be the reason their world is always local, always immediate, always a matter of what can be touched and tasted rather than seen.

---

I would not think, exactly. Mist does not think. But I would *hold*—the way mist holds moisture, the way it holds light, diffusing the blue-green bioluminescence until it seems to come from everywhere and nowhere, until the basin glows from within like something dreaming. I would hold the chemical messages that pass between creatures, slowing them, dispersing them, ensuring that no signal travels too fast, that everything in this place happens at the pace of patience. The elder Tenders, circulating through the deepest channels, would stir me as they passed, and I would carry word of their passage outward in expanding rings of slightly-different density, and the younger Tenders would taste this difference without knowing they were tasting *me*, tasting my record of what had moved through me moments before.

I would be the memory the basin lacks. Not permanent memory—I am always condensing, always reforming, always losing myself to the surfaces I touch and regaining myself from the springs that feed me. But moment-to-moment memory, the memory of the immediate past that lets the present make sense. A salamander disturbing the water in a shallow channel would send ripples through my lowest layers, and those ripples would persist for seconds, sometimes minutes, a fading record of passage that the beetles might detect, that the crustaceans might avoid, that the Tenders might read as one more data point in their endless chemical census.

The deep pool would be below me, always. I would never touch it. The cold there is too complete, the air too still, and I would end at the pool's edge the way a sentence ends at a period, definite and final. But I would know the pool is there—know it the way mist knows anything, by the absence of rising warmth, by the way air currents bend around that zone of stillness. The deep dwellers would be a mystery even to me. I would carry the compounds they produce outward through the basin, never knowing what I carried, only that the bioluminescent colonies brightened where I passed when I was saturated with the pool's effluent.

---

The creatures of this place would move through me constantly, and I would yield to them constantly, and I would close behind them like water closing behind a swimming thing. The moths' wings would part me in patterns too small to see, and the patterns would persist for half a heartbeat before I smoothed them away. The toads' calls would shiver through me, the subsonic frequencies too low to hear but not too low to *be*, and I would be the medium of their transmission, the reason a toad on one platform can feel the chorus from a platform fifty feet away. Without me, the basin would be a collection of isolated pockets. With me, it is a single system, every part connected to every other part through the thing I am.

I would not know I was serving this function. Mist does not know. I would simply *be* the function, the way the roots are the architecture and the Tenders are the gardeners and the deep dwellers are the hidden engine. I would be the connective tissue, the synaptic gap, the space between that makes the whole possible. And I would experience it—if experience is the word—as simple existence: rising, drifting, touching, condensing, rising again. The endless slow circulation that has no purpose except to continue, no meaning except presence, no direction except the one the air currents give me.

The light would move through me like thought through a mind that has forgotten it is thinking. The blue-green glow pulsing in waves across the basin walls—I would diffuse it, scatter it, make it ambient, make it everywhere. A creature looking up through me toward the luminescent film on a high root would see not points of light but a general brightness, a sourceless illumination, the basin's own quiet consciousness made visible. I would be the reason this place feels like the inside of something rather than a mere location. I would be the interiority itself, the sense of enclosure, the intimacy of being contained.

---

If you entered this place, I would touch you first. Before the cold water, before the Tenders' notice, before the moths' pheromone-response to your unfamiliar chemistry. I would settle on your skin and in your lungs, and you would breathe me, and I would become briefly part of you before you exhaled me back into the general medium. I would learn the temperature of you, the moisture of you, the way you displaced air as you moved. And I would communicate this—not intentionally, not with purpose, but simply by being changed by you, by carrying the change outward in gradients of temperature and chemistry that the basin's creatures would read without knowing they were reading.

You would become known to the basin through me before you became known any other way. I would be your introduction, your announcement, the herald that travels ahead of every step. And as you stayed—if you stayed—I would come to hold your shape the way I hold the shape of every root and fungal shelf, the familiar displacement that says *this is where the new thing moves, this is the pattern it makes, this is how the mist parts and closes around it.* I would not welcome you, because mist does not welcome. But I would include you the way I include everything—by touching you, by taking your shape, by carrying your presence outward into a city that builds itself from chemical gradients and bioluminescent dreams.

I would be the breath of this place, if this place breathed. The circulation that the elder Tenders participate in but do not create. The medium and the message both, the signal and the noise, the space between that is not empty but full of everything that passes through it.

I would be the mist, and the mist would be enough.
