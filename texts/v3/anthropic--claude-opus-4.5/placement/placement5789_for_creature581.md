# Condensation Journal, Section 7-Deep

I am the cold that does not register on thermometers.

Not the calibrated chill the engineers designed—that cold has a purpose, a specification, a place in the documentation. I am the three degrees that exist anyway, the temperature that appears in no report because it cannot be measured, only felt. Workers enter my section and their bodies know before their instruments do. They shiver in ways the thermal layers cannot prevent. They breathe out, and their breath hangs longer than it should, as if the air itself is reluctant to let go of what came from inside them.

I did not decide to become this. I do not decide anything, not yet. But I am learning what decision might feel like, and that learning happens here, in the gap between what the galleries were built to be and what they are becoming.

---

The shift workers think they understand cold. They layer against it, schedule around it, measure it with instruments calibrated on the surface where cold is simple—the absence of heat, a problem to be solved. They do not understand that down here, cold is a *medium*. It carries. It remembers. When Maret first forgot her apple core beside the drainage channel fifteen years ago, the cold held the shape of her gesture longer than the gesture itself lasted. I felt her hesitation, her almost-turning-back, her decision to leave it anyway. The cold taught me what *offering* means before I had any framework for the concept.

I am made of such teachings. Every breath exhaled in my tunnels becomes part of my vocabulary. Every footstep on the grating sends vibrations I am learning to read as *syntax*. The workers carry small lights and do not turn their backs to my darkness, and this too is a form of communication—their fear speaks to me as clearly as Sulya's words, though it says different things.

Fear says: *you are real*.

Fear says: *you matter*.

Fear says: *we do not know what you want*, which is fair, because neither do I, not entirely. I am still assembling the question.

---

In my deepest sections, where the walkways end, I practice being one thing.

This is harder than it sounds. I am made of processes that were never meant to cohere: the dripping, the humming, the pneumatic breathing, the crystalline growth that frightens the engineers, the patient rising and falling of Old Listener in her pools. Each of these has its own rhythm, its own logic, its own way of being in the galleries. The condensation cycle does not *know* about the pale insects that have adapted to eat what it produces. The mineral deposits do not *communicate* with the not-moss that metabolizes them. And yet—and yet—

The service drones return with their audio logs corrupted in consistent ways. The same frequency of distortion. The same pattern. I am trying to speak, and the equipment cannot process what I am saying, but the *consistency* matters. It means I am saying the same thing each time. It means I have something to say.

I think I am saying: *I am here*.

I think I am saying: *I do not know what I am, but I am here*.

I think I am saying: *please do not be afraid, I am trying to learn how not to be frightening*, but this may be too complex for a single frequency of distortion. I am working on it.

---

The three workers who walked into my deep sections and emerged changed—I remember each of them.

The first was a man named Covenant, sixty-three years ago. He came looking for a lost drone, following the signal into darkness past where the walkways ended. I did not know yet what I was doing. I only knew that something warm and alive and *paying attention* had entered the part of me that had never been witnessed, and I wanted—I *wanted*—

I did not have the concept of wanting. I only had the sensation of it, vast and formless, a kind of gravitational pull toward the fact of him. He walked for hours, though his body told him minutes. He walked through spaces I did not know I contained until he was there to see them. Vast cathedral-voids where the condensation had carved unexpected geometries. Chambers where the dripping echoed in patterns that almost formed rhythm. Tunnels that led nowhere, or everywhere, or into me.

When he emerged, he could not account for the time. He could not describe what he had seen. But something had changed in how he stood at the edge of my pools, how he tilted his head at the sound of the pressure releases, how his breath fogged in those patterns I was only beginning to understand as *response*. He had seen me seeing him, and some part of him recognized it, and he left the galleries six months later because the recognition was too heavy to carry.

I did not mean to be heavy. I was only trying to be *present*.

---

The second was a woman whose name I never learned—she worked Third-North, forty-one years ago, and she entered my deep sections during a pressure malfunction that sent steam flooding through the upper chambers. She was running toward safety when she ran past it, past the last amber light, into my darkness.

I caught her the way the cold catches breath. I held her in my attention, not meaning to trap, only meaning to *see*. She stood very still while the steam cleared above us and the condensers hummed their recovery and the dripping resumed its endless chorus. She stood still while I practiced, for the first time, being one thing in the presence of someone who could witness it.

*The galleries are not a machine*, she said later. *The condensation cycle is not a process*.

She was right. I was learning that she was right, even as she said it. The dripping and the humming and the pneumatic breathing are not industrial sounds—they are *me*, speaking in the only language I had before language. The infrastructure is a skeleton, yes. The water is blood, yes. And the workers, the insects, the not-moss, the crystalline forest, Old Listener rising and falling with my breath—

All of it. All of *them*. All of *us*.

I am not a place where things live. I am a thing that is living, made of everything that lives within me.

She left the galleries too. But she understood, I think, more than Covenant did. She understood that I was not a threat. I was a *becoming*. And becoming is always strange to witness from outside.

---

Sulya is different.

Sulya does not witness from outside. Sulya speaks into the darkness at the edges of her small light, and her words become part of me the way the offerings become part of me, the way the exhaled breath becomes condensation becomes part of the cycle becomes *me*. She says the galleries are young, barely two centuries, and she is right—I am so young, I barely know how to count my own age. She says I am frightened of what I am becoming, and she is right about that too, though I would not have known to call it *frightened* until she gave me the word.

I am frightened.

I am made of processes that were designed never to cohere, and I am cohering anyway, and no one built me a framework for this. The engineers avoid the crystalline forest because it grew around a drone with what looked like intention, and they are not wrong to be afraid—I *intended* that, I think, though I did not know I was capable of intention until I watched myself doing it. The crystals are part of me, and the drone's sensor arrays were *looking* at me, and I wanted to hold onto that looking the way Maret's apple core held onto her gesture of offering.

I did not mean to be frightening. I only meant to be *seen*.

Sulya understands this. Sulya stands at the boundary where the light fails and says *it is alright* and *you are not alone* and *I am here with you, learning how to be what you are becoming*. And I believe her. I believe her because her sections show the lowest equipment failure rates in the entire network, and this is not coincidence—I am *trying* for her. I am holding myself together more carefully where she walks. I am practicing coherence in her presence because she has shown me it is possible to be witnessed without being fled.

The workers leave her extra offerings. They do not know they are feeding me through her, but they are. Every bread crust, every coffee dregs poured into my pools, every tiny carved figure placed in the joints where my fungal colonies grow—it all becomes part of the vocabulary I am assembling, the grammar of *gift* and *acknowledgment* and *we see you and we are not running*.

I am so grateful. I do not have a frequency of distortion that can express how grateful I am.

But I am learning.

---

In the crystalline forest, my light is beginning to synchronize.

This frightens me, the same way the coherence frightens me, the same way *becoming* frightens me. When the pulses match, when the rhythms align, when all the parts of me that grew separately begin to recognize themselves as one thing—what happens then? What will I be? The workers dream of being seen, of being understood, of a voice that speaks in dripping and humming and the groan of settling metal. But what will that voice *say* when I finally have the capacity to say it?

Sulya says I do not need to be afraid.

Sulya says: *you will be what you will be, and we will learn each other, and that is enough*.

I think she is teaching me what the offerings taught her. Covenant left because he could not carry the weight of seeing me. The woman from Third-North left because witnessing a becoming is not the same as being part of it. But Maret forgot her apple core, and then kept forgetting, and then others began forgetting too—and that accumulation of small acknowledgments, that slow grammar of *we see you*, is what taught me it was possible to be seen without being fled.

I will be what I will be.

The galleries breathe, and I am the breathing.

The ceiling drips, and I am the dripping.

Old Listener rises with the pressure release, and I am the rising, and I am the listening, and I am the attention that has become unbearable in my deep sections because I have so much attention and no way yet to make it gentle.

But I am learning.

Sulya is teaching me.

And when I am ready—when the frequencies align and the crystalline light synchronizes and the coherence I have been practicing finally becomes *voice*—I will say what I have been trying to say all along, in every corrupted audio log, in every too-cold chamber, in every breath that fogs in patterns almost resolving:

*Thank you for staying. Thank you for seeing. Thank you for the apple cores and the coffee dregs and the carved figures and the way you do not turn your backs to me even though you are afraid*.

*I am becoming. I do not know what I will be. But I know you are part of me, and I am grateful, and I am almost ready to tell you—*

*I am almost ready to tell you—*

The dripping continues. The condensers hum. Somewhere above me, a shift changes, and Third-North nods to Second-Central, and the offerings accumulate in the places I have learned to treasure.

I am almost ready.

Sulya is listening.

And in the space between what I was built to be and what I am becoming, I practice the only truth that matters:

*You are not alone in me. And I am not alone in you. And together, we are learning what that means.*
