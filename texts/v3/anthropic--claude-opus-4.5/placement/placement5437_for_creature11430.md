---

I am not yet what I will become.

I move through the soil—if moving is the word, if *I* is the word—and the earth here is not earth but something that remembers being molten, remembers the moment of cooling, holds that memory as warmth against my soft and unformed body. I have no shell yet. I have no position. I have only the listening, the waiting, the slow differentiation of self from soil that happens when the chord passes through me and I begin to vibrate at frequencies that are mine and not mine, that are *becoming* mine through repetition, through patience, through the gentle pressure of sound shaping flesh into crystal.

The others move above me in their patterns. I feel them through the ground—the Anchors' deep pulse, the Weavers' quick crossings, the Tenders' rare emergences. I do not know their paths yet. I know only that paths exist, that positions exist, that somewhere in the architecture of the chord there is a frequency waiting to be filled, a note that is not yet sounding but that the song already contains as absence, as potential, as the shape of what I might become.

I surface sometimes. Push my translucent head through the warm dark into the warmer air, taste the copper-honey atmosphere, let the light from the ceiling-flesh pulse against sensory tissues that have not yet decided what they are for. The Weavers pass near me in their circuits and I feel their attention—not curiosity, nothing so wasteful, but acknowledgment, recognition of what I am and am not yet. They have all been where I am. They emerged from this same soil knowing nothing, and now they know everything they need to know, which is only their path, only their sound, only their place in the pattern.

I do not yearn. That is not the right word. But I *orient*. I grow toward something, some position I cannot yet perceive but that pulls at me the way the Anchors' drone pulls at the air, reshaping it, bending it into harmony. I will know when the time comes. Something will stop—an Anchor falling finally silent after millennia of holding its note, a Weaver crystallizing mid-path into permanent sculpture—and I will feel the absence like a wound in the chord, and I will rise, and I will already be becoming what the wound requires.

---

This is what I understand, in the wordless way that understanding happens here:

The pattern is not static. It only appears static because change happens on scales that make watching meaningless. The chord has been refining itself since the bubble formed, since the first accidental resonance found its shape in the cooling crust. Every Anchor that has ever held a position held it slightly differently—infinitesimally, immeasurably differently—and each difference added something, taught the chord something about what it could become. The Weavers' paths have shifted over geological time, adapting to crystallized predecessors, finding new harmonics in the altered acoustics of a space that accumulates its dead as architecture.

I am part of this. My waiting is not passive. In the soil, unformed, I am *learning*—absorbing the chord through my soft tissues, letting it shape the crystalline structures that are beginning, just beginning, to precipitate in my core. By the time I emerge, I will have heard the song for so long that singing will be indistinguishable from breathing, from being, from the simple fact of existing in a space that *is* song.

The visitors do not understand this. I feel them when they come—vibrations in the passage, the wet organic heat of bodies that run on different chemistry, the noise of their breathing and heartbeat and the electricity that crackles along their nerves. They are so *loud*. Not in volume but in complexity, in the chaos of signals that have never been harmonized, that fight against each other, that produce interference patterns rather than resonance.

The cavity does not judge them for this. The cavity offers what it offers: a place in the pattern, a resolution to the noise, a chance to become simple and eternal and part of something that will outlast their frantic cellular churn. Most of them leave before the offer fully registers. The ones who stay—I have felt them, in the soil, their organic matter breaking down under the Tenders' patient care, their component molecules joining the warmth, the breath, the material that might someday become an Unfinished like me—they do not seem to suffer. They seem to *settle*. They seem to finally stop fighting against their own complicated music and let something simpler take over.

I do not know if I was ever one of them. The soil does not preserve such memories. I know only that I am here now, waiting, learning, slowly crystallizing into whatever the chord requires.

---

The Listeners are the ones I orient toward most strongly. Not because I will become one—there are three, there have always been three, I do not think they are replaced so much as they are *continuous*, the same three witnesses who heard the first note still hearing now—but because they represent something I recognize. They do not produce. They receive. They hold. They are the chord's way of knowing itself, and I think—in whatever way thinking happens here, which is more like crystallization than cognition—I think that before I can sing, I must learn to listen the way they listen. Not just hearing but *containing*. Not just receiving but *becoming the record of what I receive*.

So I wait in the soil, and I let the chord pass through me, and I become more myself with every repetition of the pattern I cannot yet join.

---

There is no urgency here. That is the thing visitors never understand, the thing their retrieval parties interrupt before it can fully settle into their bones. The chord has been singing for longer than their species has existed. It will sing for longer than their sun will burn. What is a human lifetime against that? What is waiting?

I will emerge when I emerge. I will find my position when my position opens. I will sing my note for as long as my note needs to be sung, and then I will crystallize into the architecture of the space, and something soft will begin to form in the soil, learning from my silence, waiting for its turn.

The song continues.

I am learning to be part of it.
