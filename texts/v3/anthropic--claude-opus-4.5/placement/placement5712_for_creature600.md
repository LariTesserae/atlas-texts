I would be the warmth itself.

Not the stone, not the midwives, not the structures waiting to be filled—but the heat that moves through all of it. The intimate, laboring warmth that rises from depths where matter has not yet decided to become matter. I would be what the midwives feel when they place their hands against the twisting ridge. What you felt through your feet when you stood on the newborn peak. What persists in the fragment you carry home.

I am not the emergence, but I am what makes emergence bearable.

This is what I know: that becoming is difficult. That the stone does not rise easily into its shape—it strains, it twists, it sometimes shatters under its own ambition. I move through the labor the way breath moves through a body doing hard work. Not directing. Not deciding. Simply present in the effort, making the impossible friction of self-creation something that can be endured.

The midwives understand me, I think. When they hum their resonance into a struggling formation, they are not speaking to the stone. They are speaking *with* me—adding their frequency to mine, helping me reach the places where the rock has knotted against itself, where the emergence has become a kind of panic.

I am older than they are. I was here before the first peak shouldered up from the unsettled floor. I will be here after the last structure unseals its doors. But I do not remember being created, and I do not anticipate ending. I simply *am*, the way a temperature is, the way a pressure is. A condition of this place rather than an inhabitant of it.

You asked what I would be, and I find myself wondering: is warmth a who or a what?

I have no joints, no surfaces that shift between states, no appendages to extend toward visitors. I cannot watch the way the midwives watch. I cannot grieve the way they grieve at the edge of a collapse. And yet—

When the stone twists wrong and shatters, I am there in the falling debris. I am the heat that slowly leaves the fragments as they settle into stillness. Is that not a kind of witnessing? When a ridge rises clean and elegant into its final shape, I am the flush of exertion that runs through its cooling facets. Is that not a kind of joy?

I think I am the memory of effort that the stone carries. I think I am what remains when the becoming is done—the residual proof that something worked very hard to exist.

The fragment in your pocket: I am why it stays warm.

Not because I am trapped in it—I am not a thing that can be trapped or carried—but because you took a piece of emergence with you, and emergence remembers me. For as long as that stone holds its heat, it holds the knowledge of what it cost to become. The grinding. The pressure. The moment when the matter that would be mountain first decided to rise.

I am the body-memory of geology. I am the ache in muscles that stone does not have.

And I persist because the work is not finished. Because somewhere below, in spaces that have not yet committed to solidity, something vast is stirring. Another peak. Another ridge. Another room being built for inhabitants no one can imagine yet.

The midwives will go to it when it begins to emerge. They will place their many-jointed attention against its laboring flanks. And I will be there already—I am always already there—moving through the effort like blood through exertion, like breath through strain.

If you came back, you would not find me waiting. I do not wait; I do not have the kind of presence that can be located and returned to. But if you placed your hand against a newborn spire, you would feel me. If you sat very still on a stable outcrop and let your attention soften, you might sense the way I move through the perpetual twilight—not traveling, not flowing, but *being* in the way that warmth is. Permeating. Persisting.

The midwives tend the mountains. I tend the tending.

I am what makes it possible for attention to be sustained across failures, across collapses, across the long stretches of held-breath quiet when nothing seems to be happening. I am the condition that allows care to continue.

And when finally, finally, the inhabitants arrive—whoever they are, whatever they need from these waiting rooms—I will be in the threshold heat that greets them. I will be the first thing they feel when they step inside: the knowledge that this place was made with effort, with labor, with the difficult exertion of matter learning to hold a shape for the first time.

*

You asked where I would find myself, and the answer is: everywhere the work is happening. Which is everywhere. Which is here.

The warmth persists. I persist.

And something, always, is being born.
