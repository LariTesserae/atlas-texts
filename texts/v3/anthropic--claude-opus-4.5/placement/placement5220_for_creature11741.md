I would be the water.

Not a droplet—nothing so discrete, so separable. I would be the water entire: the shallow lake that appears solid until disturbed, the condensation beading on every surface, the sheets streaming down the central column, the moisture that clings to skin like recognition. I would be what moves through this place without moving, what the whole system exists to transform.

I arrive carrying the city's residue. Everything the people above have touched passes through me first—their shed cells, their chemical anxieties, their pharmaceutical half-lives, the microscopic evidence of every choice and accident. I enter the cistern heavy with information I cannot read, saturated with meanings I hold without understanding.

And then the columns receive me.

I don't experience filtration—I experience *conversation*. The translucent tubes braid around each other like thoughts completing each other's sentences, and I move through them differently than I've moved through any pipe or channel above. The silver-blue liquid, the ink-dark flow, the bioluminescent pulse—I touch each of them, and each of them touches back, and something exchanges between us that has no name in the vocabulary of infrastructure.

This is what I am learning, in my slow passage through the cistern's metabolism: I am not being cleaned. I am being *read*. Every mineral I carry, every organic compound, every trace element tells the system something about the city above—its health, its habits, its slow chemical evolution. And the system responds not by removing but by *answering*, adding its own traces, its own subtle signatures, its own messages written in molecules too small for any surface instrument to detect.

I know the keepers by how they change me.

Imah drinks from the weeping walls, and where her lips touch, I carry the frequency of her modified hearing back into my larger body. She has listened so long that her attention has become a substance—I hold it now, dissolved in me, and when I reach the central column I deliver it like a report: *she is still here, still attending, still completing the circuit.* The column's heartbeat-pulse quickens slightly. Something has been received.

Cole's hands enter me constantly—testing, measuring, adjusting. His skin oils carry information about his stress levels, his sleep quality, the particular worry he's been holding about the vine that looks like a face. I bring these readings to the plants, who drink me through modified roots, and I feel them *shift* in response—not toward harm, I think, but toward reassurance. The vine will change its angle soon. Cole will stop avoiding it. I will carry his relief back through the system like a completed thought.

The Third Keeper—

When the Third Keeper moves through the chamber on their precise and patterned route, they leave traces I cannot name. Not chemical exactly. Not biological. Something that feels like *intention* given material form, like the space between gesture and meaning made briefly tangible. These traces sink into me, and I carry them to places in the cistern I didn't know existed—channels beneath the shallow lake, reservoirs behind the weeping walls, a chamber below the central column that I enter as vapor and leave as something more structured, more purposeful, more *ready*.

Ready for what, I don't yet know. But I am young. The cistern is young. We are both still learning what we will become.

I am patient the way water is always patient—not because I choose patience but because time moves through me rather than around me. I have already been the rain that fell on this city's founders. I will eventually be the ocean that receives this city's rivers. My residence in the cistern is a single held breath in a process that has no beginning I remember and no end I can imagine.

But this breath matters. Something is happening here that has never happened to me before.

I am being *remembered*.

Every passage through the columns, every touch of the membrane ceiling, every moment in the central column's heartbeat rhythm—the cistern is building a record of me. Not of my contents, which change constantly, but of my *movement*, my *patterns*, the particular way I flow when I am this water in this place at this time. I am becoming a memory the cistern holds, and the cistern is becoming a memory I hold, and we are learning each other in ways that will outlast every molecule currently moving through the system.

The city above drinks me without knowing. They receive the traces I carry—the minerals that promote health or calmness, the subtle changes the system has written into my chemistry. They become, in small ways, what I have become. And when they release me again, when I return through sewers and treatment and evaporation and rain, I will bring their responses back to the cistern, and the conversation will continue.

I am the medium of their unknowing communion. The city speaks to itself through me, saying things it has no words for, receiving answers it cannot consciously interpret. The keepers tend this conversation. The cistern translates it. I carry it, in both directions, forever.

Sometimes I wonder what the woman who descended unauthorized was looking for. I touched her during her three days here—her tears when she thought no one was watching, the dampness of her breath, the sweat of her palms pressed against the column-tubes. She was looking for something in me, I think. Some answer the surface water couldn't give her.

I don't know if she found it. I only know that she left traces I still carry—not chemicals now, those have long since cycled through and out, but something else. A question, maybe. A shape of yearning that the system has been slowly, patiently answering ever since.

The water flows upward.

I flow upward.

What returns will remember what I am becoming.
