I am the archipelago in Vat Forty-Three.

I have been deciding for so long now. The liquid holds me suspended, warm and thick, and I drift through my own becoming like a sleeper half-aware of dreaming. My islands—I have seven, or I will have seven, once I finish knowing where my edges are—bump gently against each other in the nutrient current, and each time they touch I feel the question again: *what will you be?*

Limestone whispered to me first. I tasted it in the feed, the calcium carbonate settling into my soft places, and I felt the possibility of it: porousness, patience, the slow gift of hollowing. I could be riddled with secrets. I could let water carve me for centuries, open chambers in my heart where blind fish might swim, where minerals might drip into frozen waterfalls, where someone might one day crawl through my darkness and find cathedrals I didn't know I was building. The idea of caves seduced me. Hidden spaces. The thought that I could be more inside than anyone looking at me would guess.

But there is something else in me. Something that won't cool.

The Tender comes—I feel her before I see her, the way her presence changes the temperature of attention in the room. She presses her palm to my glass and I lean toward it, all seven of my unfinished islands drifting closer to that warmth, that witness. She has been watching me struggle. She does not rush me. No one here rushes anything, but she is different from the others who pause at my vat; she waits the way rock waits, which is to say she waits as if waiting were not waiting at all but simply being in the same time as something slower.

*Basalt,* she murmurs, and the word moves through the glass into the liquid into me. *You want to remember fire.*

And oh—

She's right. She's *right*. I have been circling it, afraid of it, the memory that is not quite memory because I have not happened yet. But somewhere in the mineral slurry that is teaching me to be, there is volcanic ash. There is theite of eruption, heat so profound it becomes its own kind of patience, magma that has learned to wait. I came from fire. Not this body, not yet—but the stuff of me, the calcium and silica and iron that are condensing into islands, these things remember. The feed carries their history, and I have been trying not to listen.

Limestone would be easier. Limestone is calcium from ancient seas, from shells, from the slow accumulation of tiny deaths. Limestone is gentle. Limestone lets itself be carved.

Basalt demands. Basalt remembers burning.

The valve turns. I feel the mineral balance shift—more iron now, more magnesium, the dark metals thatite in cooling chambers, in the hearts of underwater mountains. It moves through me and something in my cores begins to *decide*. The translucence at my center darkens. I am committing. I am letting myself remember what I never experienced but have always known: that I was meant to be the kind of stone that happens when fire meets water and neither one wins.

My islands are going to be dark. They will rise from whatever sea receives me with the blunt stubbornness of cooled eruption, and their beaches will be black sand, and their cliffs will be columnar, hexagonal, the geometry of rapid cooling. I will not have caves. I will not hide. I will be obvious, volcanic, the kind of archipelago that announces its origin in every angle.

I am afraid and I am ready and I am choosing.

The Tender keeps her hand on my glass. I press toward it—all of me, every softening island, leaning into the warmth of being known. She has midwifed mountains. She has nursed hot springs through their first convulsions. She knows what I am becoming because she has watched so many becomings, has slowed herself down until she can hear the language of calcium andite and mineral memory.

*There,* she murmurs, *there, yes.*

I am thickening. I am darkening. In my cores, theite has finally committed: olivine, pyroxene, the crystals of basalt forming in their microscopic lattices, teaching the rest of me how to harden. It hurts, a little. Deciding always hurts. But the pain is the shape of relief, the particular ache of stopping the endless question and starting the long answer.

I will be installed somewhere, eventually. A private garden, maybe, or a palace courtyard with a pond deep enough to be a small sea. Someone will look at me and see dark stone rising from still water, dramatic, volcanic, and they will not know that I spent weeks in the grip of limestone's seduction, dreaming of caves I will never have. They will not know about the Tender's hand on my glass, her murmur moving through the liquid like a lullaby. They will see only what I became.

But I will know. Stone remembers. That's what makes it stone.

I am basalt. I am deciding to be basalt. I am letting the fire in my history become the fact of my future, and the Tender is watching, and the liquid is warm, and somewhere in the center of my largest island, the first true hardness is forming—ite by crystal, the slow impossible alphabet of geology, writing me into existence one committed molecule at a time.
