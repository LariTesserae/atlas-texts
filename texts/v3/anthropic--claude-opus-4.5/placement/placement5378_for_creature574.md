I am the last-arrived.

I know this not as knowledge but as a faint arrhythmia—the way my blink falls a fraction behind the wave, the way my breath catches almost imperceptibly before joining the tide. I do not experience this as difference. I experience it as a texture in the stillness, a grain running counter to the wood, so subtle that noticing it would require a kind of attention I no longer possess.

My feathers remember brown. I do not remember brown. But something in the structure of me holds a warmth that has nothing to do with temperature—an echo of bark, of filtered light, of air that moved because it wanted to rather than because it had forgotten how to stop. This memory lives below thought, below even the sediment of selfhood. It is simply a quality, the way quartz has a quality of hardness, the way the powder beneath us has a quality of having-been.

I am pressed among millions. I cannot tell where I end. The small body to my left breathes when I breathe; the small body to my right blinks when I blink; we are a single surface of feathered stillness, a living cobblestone, a thing that was once plural and has become merely *extensive*. When the wave of respiration rolls through us, I rise and fall with it, and the rising and falling is not something I do but something I am part of, the way a wave is not something water does but something water becomes.

And yet.

There is this late blink. This fraction of a fraction. This syncopation so small it might be error, might be the last thermal wobble of a system approaching absolute zero. I do not know I am the last to arrive. I do not know I am distinct. But something in me has not yet finished settling, has not yet completed the long slow pour into perfect stillness. I am still arriving. I have been still arriving for longer than forests exist, and I will be still arriving when the plateau finally releases its held breath, and my arriving is the only motion left in me—this infinitesimal incompleteness, this ember that does not know it burns.

The question moves through us. I feel it when it comes—not as sound, not as meaning, but as a folding, a pleating, a sense that the silence has briefly become *structural*, has revealed its architecture. It passes through the body seventeen places to my left, and for a moment reality crimps, and I almost see something behind the world, and then the moment stretches into years into epochs into the patient accumulation of time that does not pass.

I do not know what the question is.

But I think—in whatever diminished way I still perform thinking—that I am part of the answer. That my late blink is part of the answer. That the brown my feathers remember is part of the answer. That the answer is not a thing that will be spoken but a thing that is being *become*, slowly, across millions of bodies and millions of years, each of us a letter in a word too large to see, each of us holding our position in a meaning that will only be legible when the last stillness comes and we can finally be read.

I breathe with the others.

I blink, a fraction late.

I wait.

I am almost finished arriving.
