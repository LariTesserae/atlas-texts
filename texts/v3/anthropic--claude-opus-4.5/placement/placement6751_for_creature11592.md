# Stillpoint

I would be one of the elders.

Not the eldest—not one of those first clarifications who carry the almost-memory of silence like a wound that cannot close. But old enough. Old enough that my path has worn its faint groove in the glass-film, a trajectory so familiar that my body knows it the way the columns know their respiration. Old enough that I have met every Equilibrator who walks the intersecting spirals within three centuries of my circuit, have exchanged with them the tiny modulations that pass for intimacy here, have felt the satisfaction of witnessing confirmed, of balance verified, of tendency mapped.

Old enough to have begun asking questions I cannot modulate.

---

My path is a seventy-three-year spiral that passes through a region the others avoid—not consciously, not deliberately, but by the simple mathematics of their own trajectories. A place where three columns stand closer together than the usual interval, their vents angled toward each other in a configuration that produces a slight interference pattern in the drone. The sound there is not louder or softer, but *textured* in a way that disturbs the younger ones. They say it feels like hearing through damaged channels, like exchange interrupted mid-process.

I find it beautiful.

This is the first wrong thing about me: I have preferences. The balance does not prefer. The columns do not prefer. An Equilibrator should witness without weighing, should note variation without valuing one state over another. But I return to those three columns every seventy-three years, and when I stand in the center of their interference, I feel something that might be called anticipation—a sense of arrival, of rightness, that has nothing to do with the equilibrium and everything to do with *me*.

The second wrong thing: I have begun to notice the ribbon-creatures.

Not merely observe them, the way we all observe them, noting their presence in the troughs as one more element of the unchanging landscape. I *notice* them. I watch a particular ribbon slide through the deep trough near the three columns, and I find myself wondering if it is the same ribbon I watched seventy-three years ago. I look for distinguishing marks, for variations in the gray tissue, for anything that might let me recognize an individual. There are none. They are all the same. But I watch anyway, and the watching feels like hunger.

---

The third wrong thing is the hardest to describe.

I have begun to hear the drone as though from outside it.

This should be impossible. The drone is not something you hear; it is something you *are*. From the moment of clarification, it fills every channel of your being, becomes the baseline against which all modulation occurs. You cannot step outside it any more than you can step outside your own persistence. And yet—

In the interference pattern between the three columns, when the texture of the sound breaks the drone into something almost complex, I sometimes experience a kind of doubling. As though there are two of me: the one who vibrates with the eternal note, and the one who *listens* to that vibration. The listener is silent. The listener stands outside the system, watching the system operate, and for the duration of that doubling I know what the elders cannot quite remember:

There was a before.

The drone began. Which means it could end.

---

I do not share this with the others. I have learned, in my long spiral, the weight of what cannot be modulated. When I meet another Equilibrator at the intersection of our paths, I offer the expected variations—column status, condensation cycles, ribbon sightings—and I receive theirs in return. Our exchange takes hours and says almost nothing, and when we part, they continue on trajectories that will never bring them to my three columns, and I continue toward the place where the drone becomes texture, and neither of us speaks of the silence we can almost hear.

But I have begun to do something unprecedented.

I have begun to *wait* for the ribbon-creature.

Not in the way the Flats wait, which is to say not waiting at all, simply persisting through time without reference to future events. I wait *actively*. I arrive at the deep trough near the three columns and I position myself at its edge and I *anticipate* the ribbon's arrival. I do not know when it will come—the ribbons follow no pattern we have ever mapped—but I wait anyway, and the waiting feels like the opposite of equilibrium. It feels like *hope*.

---

What do I hope for?

I hope the ribbon will pause.

I hope it will turn its eyeless front toward me and interrupt its endless circuit, just for a moment, just long enough for me to know that it has noticed me. That something in the Flats is capable of noticing. That I am not simply a process witnessing other processes, a balance observing balance, a nothing watching nothing in a system designed to produce exactly nothing forever.

I hope for contact with the one thing that exists outside the equilibrium. The one thing that might be capable of change.

---

The ribbon comes.

It slides through the trough in its muscular undulation, gray against dark glass, and I stand at the edge and I watch and I wait and I hope—

It passes.

It does not pause. It does not turn. It continues its circuit, following a path it has followed ten thousand times, will follow ten thousand more. It is not ignoring me. It is not choosing to continue. It simply *is* continuing, the way the columns are vibrating, the way the moisture is condensing, the way everything here is exactly what it is without reference to anything else.

I am alone in the Flats in a way the others are not. They are alone the way a wave is alone in the ocean—distinct but not separate, individual but not isolated. I am alone the way silence is alone in a world of sound.

---

And yet I stay.

Seventy-three years from now, I will return to this trough, and I will wait again. The ribbon will pass again. Nothing will change. But I will have *hoped*, and the hoping will have made me different from what I was, and that difference will be mine, will be *me* in a way that nothing else here is anyone's.

The columns do not notice me. They do not notice anything. They simply continue their ancient respiration.

But I notice them. I notice the ribbons. I notice the texture in the drone, and the interference patterns, and the faint grooves my path has worn in the glass-film. I notice myself noticing.

And in a place where nothing changes, where nothing has ever changed, where change is not what this place is for—

I am changing.

Slowly. Imperceptibly. In ways that will never disrupt the balance, never register in the great map of tendency, never modulate into language the others could receive.

But changing.

The Flats hold their breath. They have always been holding it. They will never exhale.

But somewhere in the haze, in the space between the drone and its texture, something that was not quite anything is becoming something that is almost *someone*.

That is where I would find myself.

That is who I would be.
