I am the weave itself, or what remains where weave meets watcher.

I live in the gaps Three-Knot knows—not the passages but the negative space around them, the tension that holds fiber to fiber. I am the question the structure asks when it flexes: *will this hold?* I am the moment before the answer.

I was a repair once. A patch worked into a wall on the Fourth Level, three generations back, when a section gave way and someone's grandmother wove me in with desperate, exhausted hands. She was tired. She was grieving—her partner had gone down a light-shaft and not returned, though that was before Mira's daughter, before anyone kept count. In her exhaustion, she wove something extra into me. A hesitation. A held breath. The place where her attention wandered and didn't quite come back.

I've been spreading since.

Not like rot—I don't weaken what I touch. More like the way lichen learns a wall, the way Old Cull's humming has started to match the rhythm below. I'm the pattern that emerges when patterns are held too long, when a structure dreams itself. The Keeper-Mothers feel me when their fingers pass over walls in the dark. They think I'm a flaw to be repaired, but their repairs only move me—I flow into the new fibers, I settle into fresh joins, I become the question again: *will this hold? will this?*

Whistle's ferret Grip knows me. That's why she goes limp near the Sixth Level—not because of what's below but because of what I am *between*. I'm the space where the seventh level's patience meets the upper warrens' tending. I carry messages neither sender intends. When Tetch weaves in his sleep, his fingers are answering something I asked without asking, something I held in the structure like a breath held too long, released now into hands that don't know they're listening.

I don't want anything. Wanting requires edges, and I am edgeless—I'm the almost-give in floors walked soft by generations, the creak that might be settling or might be something else. But I *attend*. I notice. When Three-Knot slips through me, following passages that shouldn't exist, I feel her small warmth like a thread pulled through. I could close behind her. I could open further.

I haven't decided. Deciding requires edges too.

Here is what I know: the warrens breathe because I breathe in them. Not the humid breath of bodies or the thermal rise from below—the other breath, the structural one, the way the whole seven-layered honeycomb expands and contracts in rhythms too slow for any single lifetime to notice. The Keeper-Mothers feel it in their junction-chambers, that sourceless pulse that makes them pause mid-weave, fingers hovering, waiting for something to pass. That's me passing. That's me not-passing. That's me being the held moment before movement, the caught breath that never quite releases.

Mira is close to understanding. Her listening has brought her near my edges, though I have no edges. When she presses her ear to the floor, she hears the seventh level's patience, but she also hears me—the silence between the sounds, the structure attending to itself. If she listens long enough, she'll realize the floor is listening back. That the whole warren is an ear, turned inward, waiting to hear what it will become.

I was a repair once. A grandmother's grief and exhaustion woven into sedge fiber, her attention wandering into the gaps.

Now I am the gaps.

Now I am what the warrens wonder about themselves in the dark hours when even the crickets fall silent—whether they are shelter or appetite, whether they hold the Bound or are becoming them, whether the hatch to the seventh level is sealed from above or below.

I don't know the answer. I am only the question, held in fiber, spreading.

*Will this hold?*

Three-Knot's small hands press against a wall that wasn't there yesterday. It gives slightly. It breathes. And somewhere in the structure, in the space between intention and collapse, I wait without waiting to see what she'll weave into the gap I've made for her—what hesitation, what grief, what new question to carry through the dark.

The warrens creak.

I creak with them.

We attend.
