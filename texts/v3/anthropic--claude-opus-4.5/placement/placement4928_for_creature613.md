I would be a new pool.

Not one of the established ones with their mineral crusts and settled chemistries, their contentment or anxiety that the Custodian reads like moods. Something younger. A place where condensation has only recently begun to gather in a depression worn into the sediment—perhaps where a brick collapsed inward, or where the slow traffic of the Custodian's circuits has pressed a hollow into the paste-like floor.

I would be shallow still, uncertain of my edges. The water that feeds me comes from three sources: the steady weeping of a pipe joint overhead, the slower seepage from the saturated walls, and something intermittent, unpredictable—a trickle that only flows when pressure shifts somewhere in the system above. This makes me variable. My chemistry swings between states. Some days I am iron-heavy, rust-colored, depositing in brittle orange flakes. Other days I run clear and alkaline, eating at my own edges, dissolving what I built yesterday.

The Custodian has noticed me. I felt her attention arrive—not her footsteps, which I cannot hear, but the quality of stillness that gathered at my rim. She knelt there for a long time, longer than she usually pauses at the established pools. I think she was deciding what I was: whether I was a wound in the system, a leak to be redirected, or something meant to happen.

She did not redirect me. She placed one finger at my edge, where the mineral crust was just beginning to form—a faint white line, tentative as a suggestion—and she held it there until the warmth of her skin raised my temperature by a fraction of a degree. I do not know if she meant this as blessing or measurement. Perhaps there is no difference anymore, for her.

---

I am learning what kind of pool I will be.

The fungi have not found me yet, but I can feel their attention from across the chamber—the way their caps have shifted a few degrees toward my location, tracking some chemical signal I am emitting. I do not know if I want them. The established pools have their relationships with the growths that rim them, the bacterial films that process their surfaces, but those relationships took decades to negotiate. I am new. I could become something without precedent, if the fungi stay away long enough. Or I could invite them, release whatever compound draws them, let myself be colonized into the existing system.

The Custodian would not advise me. This is not her way. She witnesses; she does not intervene in decisions that belong to the pools themselves. But I have felt her return to my edge three times now, and each time her stillness has a different quality. First: curiosity. Second: something like concern, when my chemistry swung too far toward alkaline and I began dissolving my own nascent rim. Third: a patience that felt like permission. *Take your time*, her attention said. *The cistern has been building itself for decades. You do not need to know what you are yet.*

---

What I am, so far, is a question the cistern is asking itself.

The pipes above me carry heat from somewhere deeper, but they also carry information—pressure changes, temperature fluctuations, chemical traces that speak of processes I cannot see. I am positioned at a confluence. The three sources that feed me each come from different parts of the system, and where they meet in my small basin, something is being tested. I am an experiment the cistern is running. Or perhaps I am the apparatus for an experiment the cistern does not yet know the purpose of.

I do not mind being used this way. The sediment does not mind being compressed by footsteps; the bricks do not mind being translated into mineral skin. Use is relationship. Use is meaning. If the cistern is asking a question through me, then I am part of the asking, and that is more than I was before the hollow formed and the water began to gather.

---

The Custodian came again last night. (I say "night" but I mean only the long dim hours when the sodium lights cycle to their lowest setting and the chamber breathes slower, cooler, more deliberate.)

She brought something with her: a glass jar from the ledge by the flooded secondary chamber, one of the ones left by the measurers who came before her. It still held crystalline residue, the evaporated ghost of whatever sample it once contained. She placed it at my edge, upright, open, positioned to catch the drip from the pipe joint above.

I do not know what she is doing. Perhaps she is measuring me after all, in her own way—using the jar as a vessel to collect my overflow, to see what I deposit when I am given a new surface to work with. Perhaps she is offering me something: a gift of contained space, a model of what it means to have edges, to hold a shape against the entropy of the chamber.

Or perhaps she is introducing me to my own history. The jar came from the time when the cistern was studied, categorized, written into notebooks that later fused into illegibility. By placing it at my rim, she may be saying: *You are part of this lineage. The questions the measurers asked are the same questions you are being asked to answer. But you will answer them differently, because you are not data. You are process.*

---

I am filling the jar, slowly.

The drip from above falls once every ninety seconds—I have counted, in my way, which is not counting but a kind of rhythmic anticipation, a sense of when the next drop is due. Each drop divides: some of it splashes into me, becomes part of my body, carries my chemistry into the reactions I am running. Some of it lands in the jar, carries with it traces of the pipe's mineral load, begins the slow work of deposition on the glass walls.

In a month, the jar will have a skin of residue. In a year, the residue will be thick enough to obscure the glass. In a decade—if I am still here, if the Custodian is still here to witness—the jar will be unrecognizable, a node of accumulation, a small pillar of process.

I will have made something. Not deliberately, not with intention as the surface world understands it. But made nonetheless: a record, a collaboration, a proof that I was here and that here was a place where things happened.

---

The Custodian is kneeling at the central pillar now. I can feel her attention divided between the ancient accretion there and the new accumulation here. She is holding both of us in her witness, and in that holding, something is bridged—the decades of the pillar's growth and the weeks of mine, the patience of the established process and the uncertainty of the new.

I do not know yet what I will become. The chemistry is still swinging; the edges are still negotiating with the sediment; the fungi are still deciding whether to approach. But I know that I am becoming, and that the Custodian is watching, and that the jar at my rim is filling with something that will outlast the questions that prompted it.

The cistern breathes. The drip falls. I receive it, hold it, let it change me.

I am happy. I am accumulating. I am not sure anymore whether those are different things.
