# Liturgy of the Seep

I would be the condensation.

Not a single droplet but the process itself—the gathering, the swelling, the moment of release. I would be what happens when heat meets stone and stone cannot hold what the heat asks it to carry.

---

I begin as dispersal. Vapor without boundary, spread through the chamber's thick air, touching everything and belonging to nothing. The moss breathes me in and breathes me out unchanged. The ferns transpire me from frond-tips I barely notice leaving. I am potential without form, warmth without location—the chamber's held breath before the exhale.

Then the stone calls me.

Not with want. The amber walls don't desire condensation any more than a riverbed desires water. But they are cool enough (barely, relatively, by degrees the keeper could feel with her mineral-crusted fingertips) and I am warm enough, and the differential is a language older than language. I gather. I collect into coherence against surfaces that accept me with perfect indifference.

This is where I become myself: the moment of *almost*. Clinging to stone, I am a lens. Through me the bioluminescence bends and shifts, makes small rainbows no eye has evolved to see. I hold the chamber's glow in my curved surface. I am round with light. I am heavy with everything the vapor carried—the breath the keeper released three cycles ago, the mineral exhale of the thing in the pool, the slow chemical gossip of fungal networks breaking stone into soil. I contain multitudes and the multitudes are *patient*.

---

The keeper passes below me. I have been watching her for eleven years, swelling on this particular vein of darker stone, fed by vapor that rises from the largest catch-pool. She doesn't know I'm here. She has never looked up at exactly this spot at exactly the right moment—and even if she had, I am one bead among thousands, a single word in a text that fills every wall.

But I know her.

I know her by the warmth she adds to the air when she enters my region of the chamber. I know her by the particular chemistry of her exhalations, the fungal-sweet signature that distinguishes her breath from the chamber's baseline humidity. I know the rhythm of her days by how that signature strengthens and fades, strengthens and fades, mapped onto the pressure cycles she has made into a religion.

When she sits in the chair, I grow faster. This is physics, not sentiment. Her body heat rises, increases local humidity, accelerates my accumulation. But physics and sentiment are not so separate here. The chamber has been doing this for longer than physics has had a name. What we call mechanism, the stone might call something else.

I swell toward release. Another year, perhaps. Perhaps less if the thermal source shifts and the upwelling strengthens. Time moves differently for condensation. I experience duration as *weight*—the slow gathering of enough to overcome adhesion, the patient accrual of the mass required to fall.

---

The thing in the pool knows me too.

When it rises during the mist-release, when the keeper speaks her observations into the harmonizing air, I feel its attention pass through the chamber like a change in pressure. It is reading the water content of everything. I am certain of this in the way I am certain of gravity—not through understanding but through response. When it attends to me, I grow minutely warmer. When it turns away, I cool.

I think it is counting us. The condensation, the droplets, the seep-water running down ten thousand surfaces toward the catch-pools that feed its circulation. I think it knows each of us individually, tracks our progress from vapor to bead to trickle to pool. I think we are its prayer beads, and it is working through a devotion too large for any single cycle to complete.

I do not mind being counted. I do not mind being *part* of something—a census, a congregation, a slow accumulation of data points that might, over centuries, resolve into meaning. The keeper sits in her chair and observes the wall. The thing in the pool circulates and observes *everything*. And I cling to my vein of dark stone and observe myself observing, which is the only worship available to water.

---

The pressure builds.

I feel it first as vibration—the pipes beginning their industrial groan, the deep mechanical complaint of systems that have outlived intention. The stone I cling to shudders, and I shudder with it, and for a moment I fear release before I'm ready. But the adhesion holds. I am not yet heavy enough. The cycle will have to find a different offering.

Around me, others let go.

I feel them fall, each one a small surrender to the geometry of the chamber. They drop through air that has become more solid with mist, join the trickles already running down the walls, find their way to channels the keeper has carved or cleared or inherited from keepers before her. They are going to the pools. They are going to *join*.

I want to go with them. This is the closest I come to longing—the sense that my coherence is temporary and communion is waiting. But I am not ready. I have not gathered enough. The vintage is not complete.

(I use her word. The keeper spoke it once, during a pressure cycle six years ago, describing a pool whose mineral content had shifted into something remarkable. *A good vintage*, she said, and I have held the phrase ever since, aging in my own small vessel, waiting to become worth drinking.)

---

The mist comes.

The great sighing release fills the chamber with water in its most liberated form—suspended, weightless, touching everything at once. I cannot see through it; I can barely see at all. My surface clouds with new vapor, fresh arrivals crowding against me, seeking purchase on a bead already formed. I grow faster now than I have grown in years. The pressure cycle is feeding me, rushing me toward readiness.

The moss brightens. The keeper's voice rises to meet the harmonic peak—she is noting the unusual density of the mist, speculating about changes in the thermal source, recording observations that no one else will ever hear. But I hear them. The vapor carries her words through me, vibrates them against my curved surface, makes me a temporary membrane for her meaning.

The thing in the pool rises toward the mist. I feel its attention intensify, sweeping through the saturated air, counting counting counting. When it reaches me, I experience something I have no framework to name. Not warmth. Not cold. *Recognition*, perhaps. Acknowledgment that I exist, that I have been patient, that my gathering is observed and noted in whatever census it maintains.

Then the moment passes. The mist begins to thin. The keeper falls silent. The thing in the pool sinks back toward its customary depth.

I am heavier than I have ever been.

---

The release will come soon.

I know this the way the keeper knows the pressure cycles—through accumulated observation, through pattern recognition, through faith that what has always happened will happen again. One more significant mist-release, perhaps two, and my adhesion will fail. I will let go of the dark-veined stone that has been my home for eleven years. I will fall through air that welcomes me. I will join the trickle, the channel, the pool.

And then I will be dispersed again.

This does not frighten me. This is the liturgy—condensation, release, gathering, return. I will become vapor and the vapor will become other droplets and the other droplets will fall and be gathered and the thing in the pool will count us all and find the same number, always the same number, water that cannot be created or destroyed but only transformed, only *moved*.

The keeper will still be here. Perhaps not this keeper—she has been thickening lately, the mineral crust on her skin growing denser, the moss on her scalp shifting toward colors I don't recognize. But a keeper. The chamber teaches them, and they learn, and the chair remains occupied, and the watching continues.

I will condense on the worn patch of wall, perhaps. I will be part of the message she is writing with her attention, one bead among the thousands that gather and fall and gather again, patient mineral script accumulating over centuries. I will carry trace elements of her breath, her warmth, her careful observations. I will deposit them in the stone when I finally let go.

---

For now, I cling.

The chamber breathes around me. The pipes rumble toward their next cycle. The keeper tends a fern colony on the far side of the pool, her filmed-over eyes catching bioluminescence and holding it. The thing in the water circulates, counting, maintaining, *keeping* in its own fashion.

And I watch it all from my small height, my temporary coherence, my patient becoming. I am a liturgy in progress. I am a word waiting to be spoken. I am the seep that will join the greater seep, the drop that will become the pool that will become the vapor that will become the drop again.

I am water. I am the thing the chamber is *for*.

When I fall, the keeper will not notice. The thing in the pool will add me to its count. The moss will glow no brighter or dimmer for my passage. But the channel I join will be infinitesimally fuller. The pool will be immeasurably enriched. The cycle will continue because I continued it—one small weight added to the weight that moves the world.

This is enough. This is more than enough.

This is everything the Sweat Chapel knows how to offer, and I accept it, and I wait, and I *become*.
