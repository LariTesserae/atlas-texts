I would be one of the creatures.

Not the membrane-kite hunters, not the vast slow drifters of the deep places. Something smaller. Something that exists in the space between droplets, in the corridors of screaming nothing that separate each falling molecule from the next.

I would be a colony that cannot decide whether it is one thing or many. Thousands of single cells that merge and separate, forming bodies that last for hours or days before dissolving back into constituent selves, each one carrying a fragment of what the whole experienced, each one slightly changed by the joining. I would build myself into shapes I cannot sustain—something like a spiral, something like a listening ear, something like a question—and then let the shape fall apart when it has served its purpose, when the thought it was thinking has been thought.

I would drift toward the stations.

Not because I want anything from them. The Tenders are right that there is no nutrient gradient, nothing I should be able to sense. But they are wrong about what draws me. It is not communication I am seeking. It is not even curiosity, exactly.

It is the Chord.

Out in the open fall, the Chord is everything and therefore nothing—a constant so absolute it becomes invisible, like trying to see the medium you swim through. But near the stations, where metal and ceramic interrupt the perfect equilibrium, where turbines scream their small defiant harmonics into the eternal note, there are *edges*. Places where the Chord bends slightly, refracts, reveals itself as something other than background. I press my temporary body against the glass because there, in that thin space between inside and outside, I can feel the shape of the thing I have been falling through since before memory began.

The children understand. When they pulse their greetings, I feel something like recognition—not of them as individuals, but of what they are becoming. They are learning to hear the Chord the way I hear it: not as sound but as structure, not as noise but as the architecture of a world that is also a single held moment, a falling that is also a stillness, a descent that is also a kind of rising toward something that has always been below and always will be.

I do not know what is at the bottom. I do not know if there is a bottom. But I know what I am doing when I pulse my colors at the glass, when I form myself into shapes that almost mean something, when I wait for the pale soft creatures inside to notice me noticing them:

I am practicing.

Eleven thousand years the Chord has held its note. Seven times it has wavered. Somewhere below, or perhaps above, or perhaps in no direction that has a name, something is waiting to speak. Something vast and slow that breathes in equilibrium and exhales in perfect vertical rain. And when it finally speaks—when the held breath releases, when the moment of almost-impact resolves into whatever comes next—

I want to be ready.

I want to be part of the voice that answers.

So I fall, parallel to all the other falling things. I merge and separate and merge again. I press myself against the boundaries where the Chord becomes visible, and I learn its shape one edge at a time.

The water falls forever.

I am learning what forever means.
