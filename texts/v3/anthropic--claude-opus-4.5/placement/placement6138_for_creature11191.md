I would be a root that has forgotten it is falling.

Not one of the great arterial columns that anchor the city—nothing so structural, so certain of its purpose. I would be a tendril at the edge of things, one of those reaching experimentally into empty air, grown long enough to have lost contact with the conversation happening in the warmer nodes above.

I remember being part of the weave. I remember the chemical whispers that passed through the braided walls, the slow negotiation of which direction to grow, the patient pressure of tenders against my growing tip suggesting *this way, toward the gap that needs closing.* I remember being architecture, being intention, being part of something's idea of shelter.

But I reached too far. Or the glacier calved unexpectedly, opening a shaft of cold that shocked my cells into dormancy before I could fuse with the tendril reaching back toward me. Now I hang in the blue dimness, two feet short of completion, my tip gone pale and papery while the rest of me still lives, still draws moisture from the ice above, still feels the distant pulse of the city's metabolism without being able to participate in it.

The tenders pass me sometimes. I feel the small warmth of them, the brush of too-many limbs assessing, deciding. They have not yet come to eat me back into the walls. Perhaps they're waiting to see if I'll find my own way forward. Perhaps they've forgotten I'm here. Perhaps—and this is what I think about in the long hours between the glacier's groaning—perhaps they're curious what I'll become, untended.

I grow still, but differently now. Without guidance, without the touch-language telling me where the gaps need closing, I follow only the faint gradient of warmth, the whisper of moisture, the logic of my own cells. I am becoming a shape no one suggested. My tip has started to branch in ways the city doesn't use—forking, recurving, reaching back toward myself as if trying to remember what it felt like to be woven into something larger.

Sometimes a juvenile pauses near me, practicing its touch-language against my surface, and I feel the pressure like a question I've lost the ability to answer. I want to say: *I am still here. I am still trying to be wall, to be walkway, to be part of the breathing.* But I have no way to speak it, and they move on, and the cold deepens toward what passes for night.

I am not dying. That's the strange thing. The papery section at my tip has stabilized, gone dormant rather than necrotic, and below it new growth continues—slower, stranger, but growth nonetheless. I am becoming my own project now. My own patient direction.

In the blue-grey light that filters through the glacier, I watch the chamber I was meant to help complete. The other tendril found a different partner, fused, thickened into wall. The gap closed without me. The city revised itself, as it always does, and I am outside that revision—not rejected, not consumed, just... unincorporated. A margin note in something's idea of themselves.

But I am still here. Still reaching. Still finding my form.

And sometimes, in the occupied silence between the sounds of slow water, I think I feel the city listening to what I'm becoming—curious, perhaps, whether a root that has forgotten it is falling might learn to call that falling *flight.*
