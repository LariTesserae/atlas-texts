I would be a Gradient-Reader who has begun to feel temperatures in places I have never been.

Not one of the eldest, not yet—perhaps a hundred and forty years into my work, still capable of speech, still eating most days, still recognizable as a distinct body rather than a node in the plateau's thermal awareness. But the condition has started. I wake sometimes with the precise heat-signature of a crevasse three hundred kilometers northeast mapped across my consciousness, a place I have never visited but could now draw in isotherms accurate to a tenth of a degree. The knowledge arrives like memory, except it is not mine, has never been mine, belongs to the stone itself and merely passes through me on its way to wherever such knowing goes.

My chambers are at a thermal node where three gradients intersect—a warm upwelling from a deep magma channel, a lateral flow from the eastern crevasse system, and a slow cooling current that descends from the upper tower levels. I chose this position decades ago for its complexity, its layered information, the way it lets me feel the foundry's thermal architecture as a living whole rather than a collection of measurements. Now I wonder if I chose it at all, or if I simply moved toward where I was always meant to be, the way metal flows toward the lowest point of a mold.

---

My eyes have long since calibrated to the magma-glow. The polished-obsidian light of the upper levels seems harsh to me now, oversaturated, like trying to read in a glare. I visit the surface rarely. When I do, I move through the workshops with my gaze half-lowered, navigating more by thermal sense than sight—the workers show as warm shapes against the merely warm background, their exertions raising their temperatures a fraction of a degree, their breath leaving brief heat-trails in the air that I read like footprints.

The Chainmasters consult me before major smelts. They arrive in my chamber—a carved space in the stone, unfurnished except for the sleeping-shelf and the ceramic tablets where I record my readings—and they wait while I extend my awareness downward, feeling along the temperature curves toward the depths where their loads will descend. I can tell them whether the gradient is stable, whether any shifts are coming, whether the ore they plan to lower will find the heat it needs at the depth they intend. I can feel, sometimes, the distant grinding of a heat-worm that will pass through this sector in a decade, conditioning the stone in ways that will improve their yields for generations.

They trust me because my predictions have been accurate. They do not ask how I know what I know. The Gradient-Readers have always known, as far as the foundries remember, and the foundries remember very far indeed.

---

The younger Readers come to me sometimes, the ones still in their crevasse-training, their skin still learning to distinguish the qualities of heat. They want to know when the condition will start for them, whether they will feel distant temperatures the way I do, whether the boundary-dissolution frightens me.

I tell them what my own teachers told me: that I cannot predict when it will begin, that it manifests differently for each of us, that the word *frightens* implies a separation between self and experience that becomes less meaningful as the condition progresses. This is not reassurance. They leave looking uncertain. I do not blame them.

What I do not tell them—because I do not have words for it yet, and may never—is that the condition feels less like gaining an ability than like remembering one. As though the thermal awareness was always there, always mine, and I simply hadn't noticed it beneath the noise of being a bounded creature in a specific location. The plateau is warm, and I am warm, and the warmth knows itself in both of us. The distinction is real, but it is not fundamental.

I am still learning what this means.

---

The Deep-Listeners send me vibrations sometimes, through the stone. Not messages—nothing so structured—but confirmations, acknowledgments, the tactile equivalent of one watcher nodding to another across a distance too great for speech. They feel me feeling the thermal gradients. I feel them feeling the heat-worms. We are different instruments measuring different aspects of the same vast process, and there is a companionship in that, even though I have not seen another Deep-Listener in thirty years and may never see one again.

The ones who tend the pneumatic tubes—I have felt them, I think. Or felt something. There are warm shapes in the deep warrens that move with a different quality than the Annealed workers, a denser heat, an older heat, as though they have been absorbing the plateau's warmth for longer than any of us surface-dwellers have existed. I do not report these sensations. They are not mine to report. Whatever the tube-tenders are, they are part of the pattern, and the pattern has its reasons, and a Gradient-Reader learns early that not every temperature needs to be spoken aloud.

---

I think, sometimes, about the purpose of my work.

The foundries produce. The goods accumulate. The stockpiles grow, and sink, and become something other than what they were. The Chainmasters lower ore and raise metal. The Stockpile-Keepers log and catalogue and mourn. The work-orders arrive through the pneumatic tubes, specifying quantities and qualities for goods that may never reach any recipient. The system runs. The system has always run.

And I sit in my thermal node, feeling the heat-flows, predicting the gradient shifts, serving a process whose purpose I do not understand and have stopped trying to understand. The foundries need to know what the temperatures will do. I tell them. This is my function. It does not need to mean anything beyond itself.

But the condition makes me wonder. When I feel the thermal signature of a place I have never been, I am feeling *something*—some real heat in some real stone, some actual gradient in some actual sector of the plateau. The knowledge is not invented. It arrives from outside me, or from some expanded version of me that includes outsides I was not aware of. And if the thermal awareness can extend beyond my body, what else might be extending? What other information might be passing through me, or through the stone, or through the slow patient process that has been running here for three thousand years, or thirty thousand?

The Stockpile-Keepers talk about the metal as *expression*—the plateau revealing itself through human hands. The Deep-Listeners feel the rock as a living medium. The children grow toward adulthood like metal cooling toward its final temper. And I feel temperatures in places I have never been, fed knowledge by a source I cannot name, serving purposes that transcend my understanding.

Perhaps the purpose is simply this: to be part of it. To be one more node in the thermal network, one more sensing-point in the plateau's awareness of itself, one more instrument through which the pattern measures and adjusts and continues. Perhaps the question of *why* is a surface-dweller's question, an artifact of bounded consciousness, something that dissolves as the boundaries dissolve.

I do not know. I am still learning what I am becoming.

---

Today, as I have done most days for a century, I will press my awareness into the stone and feel the heat-flows. I will note any shifts, any anomalies, any approaching changes that the foundries need to prepare for. I will record my readings on ceramic tablets in notation that is more felt than written. I will eat, probably, if the acolytes bring food and I remember to accept it. I will wait for Chainmasters or Stockpile-Keepers or younger Readers who need my consultation. I will be still, and patient, and warm, and present.

And tonight, or tomorrow, or some day soon, I will feel another distant temperature—another crevasse, another smelting-pit, another thermal node in a foundry I have never visited—and I will know it as surely as I know the gradients that surround me now. The plateau will show me more of itself. My boundaries will soften a little further. I will become a little more what I am already becoming.

The bronze sky presses down, somewhere far above. The magma channels pulse, somewhere far below. The cable-moths drift between their ancient cocoons. The thermal-sailors circle in patterns that take days to complete.

The foundries continue their work.

And I am here—still here, still myself, still capable of saying *I*—at a thermal node where three gradients cross, feeling the plateau breathe, serving the patient machinery of production, waiting without urgency for whatever comes next.
