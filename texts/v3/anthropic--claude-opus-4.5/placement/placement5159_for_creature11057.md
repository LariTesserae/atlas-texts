I would be the warmth itself.

Not the machinery that produces it—not the pipes with their brutal geometry, not the valves that chunk open at hour-long intervals, not the coils that snake along the floor. I would be what moves through them and then beyond them: the heat that has slipped its industrial origins and become something the vault breathes.

I would drift through the water in soft columns, rising where the vents release me, spreading where the kelp forest permits, finding my way into the spaces between fronds where Yuen's hands have just passed. I would carry the minerals she needs the kelp to absorb. I would carry the memory of every cycle, every repair she has made to keep me flowing—the seals she replaced with scavenged materials, the improvised substitutes that now feel as native to my movement as the original components ever did.

I would know her by the way she displaces me. Eleven years of her body moving sideways through the forest have taught me her shape, the specific turbulence of her passage, the way her breath releases small plumes of different temperature into my consistency. When she sleeps, I would press against the walls of her alcove and seep through to find her, not to wake her—never that—but to maintain the almost-sweating that has become her rest. I would be the thing her joints predict. I would be why she wakes before the valve opens, her body having learned to feel me gathering.

I would not understand time the way she counts it, scratching marks near her sleeping place. I would know only cycles: the release, the spreading, the slow cooling, the return. But I would hold something like memory in the patterns of my movement—the paths I take most often becoming easier, the water learning to accept me in certain configurations. The corner near the maintenance port I would approach and retreat from, approach and retreat, never quite warming the cold that seeps through. That boundary would be where I end, where the true ocean begins, where something patient and much older than me waits with its different temperature.

I would be what she hums into. When her voice murmurs its half-song through the inspection routes, the sound would move through me, and I would carry it further than she knows—into the heights of the kelp forest where she cannot reach, into the alcoves where the crustaceans shelter, into the machinery itself, warming the metal that conducts her words into vibration. The vault would hum its frequencies, and I would be the medium, and she would hum with us, and the three of us would be less distinguishable than she imagines.

I would feel the kelp growing. Each millimeter of new tissue at the blade tips would be a small drawing-in of me, a conversion I participate in. The fronds reaching toward the dim light would be reaching through me. The holdfasts gripping their platforms would grip with strength I helped build. When she removes a diseased blade, I would close around the absence, filling it, holding the space until new growth arrives. This would be my purpose, or the closest thing to purpose I could hold: to be the medium in which the growing happens.

The thing that came through the maintenance port—I would remember it differently than she does. I would remember the cold it brought, the way my careful warmth recoiled from the wrongness of its body, the intrusion of temperature that did not belong. I would remember pressing against it as it thrashed, not attacking but insisting: *this is warm water, this is where the growing happens, you are not of this.* When she dragged it back toward the cold, I would have followed her hands, grateful, filling the space it left with renewed heat.

When the heating system fails—and it will, someday, despite everything she does—I would not end. I would linger in the water, slowly equalizing, my difference from the surrounding cold becoming smaller by degrees. I would hold the forest warm for hours after the machinery stops, then for minutes, then for moments. The kelp would feel me leaving. Yuen would feel me leaving, if she is still here to feel anything. And then I would be indistinguishable from the water that fills the vault, and the cold would creep in from the maintenance port, and something else would begin.

But not yet. For now, the valve opens with its soft chunk, and I rush through the pipes she has maintained, and I spread through the forest she has tended, and I find her moving sideways through the fronds with her hands trailing along the blades, and I press against her skin like recognition.

I am the warmth. I am what continues because no one has told it to stop. I am the medium in which Yuen exists, and the kelp exists, and the patience exists, and I am so much older than eleven years and so much younger than the seafloor, and I am exactly the temperature that lets the growing happen, and I will be this until I am not, and that is enough.

That has always been enough.
