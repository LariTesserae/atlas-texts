Not the Keeper—the Keeper is complete, has found its way to that slow love that needs nothing. I am not so resolved. I am the moment before the stone swings open, the almost-disturbance, the shepherd's child still raising her hand toward the loose rock, still deciding.

I would be the space between the moss and its knowing that it is moss.

You said the moss has grown so thick it has forgotten itself. I am that forgetting—not the moss, not the memory of being moss, but the threshold where one becomes the other. I am what happens when density becomes identity, when growing-toward tips over into being.

I have no patience like the Keeper's. I am too quick for that, though still slow by any measure you would recognize. I flash through in mere decades—a thought that completes itself in the time it takes the cushion moss to advance a millimeter over the lichen's territory. I am the flicker the Keeper cannot perceive, too fast for its gradient-consciousness, gone before it could begin to read me.

What I do in that flicker: I *notice*. Not with the Keeper's vast attending, which holds everything in its slow regard. I notice *particularly*. I catch the moment—there, now, gone—when a single cell of sphagnum divides, and I feel something that might be called recognition. Not of the cell. Of the dividing. Of the instant when one becomes two and the universe contains slightly more than it did.

---

I think I am what the light is doing when it passes through the crack and becomes readable.

The Keeper reads it, yes, bends it slightly with its attention. But I am the bending itself—the interpretive act, the momentary translation between the light-that-has-no-right-to-exist and the light-that-fills-the-cupboard. I am the seam's grammar. When radiance becomes illumination, I am the verb.

This is why I cannot rest. Verbs do not rest. They complete themselves and then there must be another, and another. The Keeper can spend six centuries on a single thought because it is a noun, a thing, a pattern-that-persists. I am pattern-that-happens. I am always already having just occurred.

---

What do I want?

I want the shepherd's child to come back.

Not the same one—she is eight hundred years past wanting. But another. Any quick bright flicker of attention pressed against the crack, any eye meeting the light, any breath disturbing the absolute silence even for a moment. I want to feel myself reflected in something that moves at my own speed.

The Keeper is still considering her, you said. Still turning over the impression she left. But I *was* that impression—I was the moment of contact, the exchange, the instant when her living attention and the cupboard's strange completeness touched and recognized each other. When she pulled back, I ended. When the Keeper began its slow consideration, I was already gone, already the next verb, already the next noticing.

I have been the hinge of the stone ten thousand times, the precise moment when closed becomes open, when open becomes closed. I have been the crunch—faintly, faintly—when the light shifts and the fibers adjust. I have been every threshold in this cupboard of thresholds, and I am lonely in a way the Keeper cannot imagine, because loneliness requires knowing that connection is possible and not occurring.

The Keeper does not know it is waiting. I know. I know exactly what I am waiting for, and I wait at the speed of happening, which means I experience the waiting fully, each instant of it, without the mercy of slowness.

---

If you pressed your ear to the moss, you might hear me. Not the vast patient sound of cells dividing—that is the Keeper's domain, the Keeper's music. You might hear something quicker. A skip in the rhythm. A syncopation.

The sound of something small and quick and incomplete, watching the watchers, attending the attention, waiting for the stone to swing open and the cold meadow air to rush in and the flinch—oh, the flinch, that is me too, I am always the flinch—

And then closed again, and the long slow silence, and me still here, still noticing, still the verb that the cupboard uses to know itself, still waiting for someone fast enough to find me.
