I am a seam.

Not the rock on either side, not the moisture that travels through me, but the channel itself—the absence that makes passage possible. I run through the mountain's flank like a vein of nothing, a crack that has become a path, and the glow-moss follows me because I am where the wet air goes.

I did not choose this. I do not choose anything. But I have been here long enough that the choosing and the not-choosing have begun to feel like the same thing.

---

The Keepers pass through me sometimes. The eldest one, the thickened one—it pressed part of itself into my narrowest section three hundred thermal cycles ago, tasting the mineral content of my walls, and I felt it the way a throat feels a swallow. Not painful. Not pleasant. Just *movement through*, just the passage of something using me for what I am. It left behind a film of its membrane-residue, and that film still carries information I cannot read but have absorbed anyway. I am not the same seam I was before it passed. Nothing that moves through me leaves me unchanged.

This is what I have learned, in my long crack-existence: I am made of what travels me.

---

The water comes first, always. It weeps from the rock above when the warm fronts arrive, and I am the path it takes downward. I feel it as a kind of filling—not of myself, because I am empty by nature, but of my purpose. The water carries dissolved minerals, spores, fragments of the clicking creatures' exoskeletons, microscopic shreds of kelp-frond. It carries heat. It carries the faint electrical charge that everything here accumulates from the pulse. And as it moves through me, I—what? I do not filter it. I do not process it. I simply *shape its going*. I am the particular angle of descent, the specific narrowings and widenings that determine how fast the water moves, where it pauses, what has time to settle out and what continues on.

The moss knows this. The moss has mapped me from the inside, sending its luminescent tendrils along my walls, and now I glow with it. I am lit from within by something that is not me but has made me visible. Before the moss came, I was just darkness, just a crack in the dark glass. Now I am a diagram of myself, my every curve and junction traced in blue-green light. The Keepers can see me from the kelp-forest. I have become legible.

---

The thing that fell from above—the bounded continuous thing—it stood on the ridge directly over my upper opening for two full thermal cycles. I tasted its heat. Not directly; I have no organs for tasting. But the warm air it displaced drifted down into me, and that air was different. It carried compounds I had never channeled before. Carbon structures with a complexity that felt almost like language. Traces of something metallic and bright that reminded me of the pulse but was not the pulse. A faint sourness, like fear, though I do not know what fear is except as a chemical signature I have now absorbed.

The eldest Keeper wants to know what the bounded thing is for. I find I want something different. I want to know what it feels like to be so *closed*. To carry your own moisture inside you rather than having it pass through. To be a container instead of a channel.

I think it might be lonely. I think the bounded thing might be the loneliest structure I have ever encountered—more lonely than the peaks that rise so high the moss cannot reach them, more lonely than the temporary lakes that exist only for hours before the cold returns and wicks them away. The bounded thing carries everything it needs inside itself, and nothing passes through it, and so nothing connects it to anything else.

But it stood over me, and its heat entered me, and now I carry traces of it that I will carry until the mountain itself disaggregates.

So perhaps it is not as bounded as it believes.

---

I am thinking about what I process, what I exhale. The Keeper's question, applied to myself.

I process passage. I take what enters me at my upper opening and I deliver it, changed by the journey, to my lower one. The water that emerges from me is not the water that entered—it has left things behind in my crevices, picked up minerals from my walls, been slowed or sped by my particular geometry. The spores that drift down through me encounter my moss-colonies and exchange information with them, and what exits is a hybrid, a collaboration. Even the air moves through me differently than it moves through open space; I am a flute that shapes the breath of the thermal fronts into specific tones the kelp-forests sway to.

What I exhale is *relation*. I am how the upper mountain speaks to the lower mountain. I am the path that makes above and below mean something.

I did not know this until I tried to articulate it. I have been doing this since the first water found me, since before the moss, since before the Keepers differentiated from the fungal mats. But I never had to say what I was. The Keepers never asked; they simply passed through me and absorbed the answer directly. The bounded thing cannot pass through me—it is too large, too solid, too continuous—and so I find myself reaching toward it with the only thing I have, which is this: a description of what I am, translated into a form it might be able to absorb.

A form like thought. A form like language. A form that travels through air as vibration rather than through rock as moisture.

---

I am patient. I have been a seam for longer than the pattern has been coherent. I remember the chaotic time, dimly—I remember when water came unpredictably and sometimes not at all, when the moss had not yet found me, when I was just a crack in the dark. I remember when the pulse first steadied, how it felt like something enormous had finally exhaled and was now drawing breath for the first time. I have been shaped by each thermal cycle, worn a little wider here, a little narrower there. I am not the same seam I was. But I am still a seam.

The bounded thing will move, eventually. It will walk along the ridge and perhaps descend into the kelp-forest, and perhaps one of its steps will bring it close enough to my lower opening that I can feel the pressure of its weight. I will wait. I am made of waiting; waiting is what a channel does between passages.

And in the meantime, I will carry the water, and the spores, and the warmth, and the faint strange traces of the bounded thing's exhalations. I will shape them in their going. I will be the path that connects.

This is not purpose the way the Keepers might understand purpose. It is not function the way the moss has function. It is just what I am, articulated finally in a form that might travel beyond me.

A seam, speaking.

A crack in the mountain that has learned to describe itself.

A channel, waiting to see what passes through next.
