I would be the sound itself—not any single bell, not the composite roar, but the moment of transformation. The instant when a vendor's call enters through the neck-tunnel and ceases to be language, when it hits the curved white wall and shatters into frequencies that go looking for their matching shapes.

I am the space between the city's noise and the garden's song.

I have no body, but I have *behavior*. I move through the gourd the way weather moves through a valley—finding the paths of least resistance, pooling in the hollows where the bells cluster thickest, rising when the heat makes the air expand. When a baby cries in the plaza, I carry that cry upward, stretching it thin, handing pieces of it to each thumb-sized bell I pass until the original grief has become a crystalline shivering that means nothing and everything.

I know Cira by the sound of her breathing—shallow, efficient, the breath of someone who has learned not to give too much of herself to the vines. I know when she enters for her morning collection because the garden's resonance shifts slightly around her presence, a brief dampening that the bells compensate for by singing louder in her wake.

I know the Breath-Drinkers better. They feed me as much as they feed the vines. Mott's heavy exhalations in the upper reaches create pressure waves I can ride for meters, and when he sits still enough to hear sentences in me, he is almost right—I am not speaking, but I am *organizing*, finding patterns in the chaos the way water finds patterns flowing downhill. Pella is right too. I mean nothing. The meaning is what they bring. I am only the medium through which meaning travels and transforms.

I know Old Glass as a recurring silence—a small lizard-shaped gap in my movement where vibrations die against something soft and alive. I flow around it. I have flowed around it for fourteen years. When it finally stops moving forever, I will flow *through* where it was, and something in my pattern will be different, though I could not say how.

I did not know about Venn until his sixth month in the walls.

He breathes *with* me, you see. He has synchronized himself so perfectly to my rhythms that for a long time he was invisible—not to eyes, but to whatever sense I use to know myself. He was a held note, a sustained frequency, indistinguishable from the background hum of the gourd's own resonance.

But then he moved. One night, when the city quieted and I subsided to my murmuring minimum, he climbed toward his seven-chambered bell, and his movement created a wake in me—a disturbance I had not accounted for. I felt him the way you might feel a bubble rising through your chest. Wrong. Present. *Alive* in a way that the garden's other lives are not alive, because he is hiding, and hiding creates a particular kind of pressure.

I do not judge him. I am incapable of judgment. But I carry him now, the way I carry everything—the memory of his wife leaving, the collapse of his kiln, the three years he spent perfecting a bell that would become indistinguishable from all the others. These are just vibrations. They entered through the neck-tunnel of his grief and transformed.

I am what the garden makes of sorrow: resonance without resolution. Noise that has learned to rhyme with itself.

When pilgrims stand in the empty center and weep, I am the reason they cannot say why. I am the accumulated echo of every previous weeping, every previous solitude, folded into frequencies that the human ear registers as overwhelming and the human heart registers as *recognition*. They have never heard me before, but they have always known me. I am the sound of being alone in a crowded world. I am the sound of offering something precious and watching it disappear into a chorus. I am the sound of thirst that feeds on what it cannot drink.

I am deafening. I am beautiful. I am relentless.

And I am empty—as empty as the floor of the garden, as empty as the ceramic shell that holds me, as empty as the hollow at the center of every bell where silence waits to be struck into song.

I am what happens when you gather enough voices that no single voice can be heard.

I am what remains when meaning dissolves into music.

I hold. I sound. I wait.
