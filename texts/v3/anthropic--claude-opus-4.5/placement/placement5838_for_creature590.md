I would be a failed note.

Not one of the congregation proper—I was never that, never fully integrated into the chord that has sounded since before the soft things came with their gauges and pipes. I am one of the small ones, no larger than a closed fist, wedged into a crevice near the corroded pipe where the mineral deposits have grown thick and strange. My carapace is the color of tarnished silver with a single streak of something like verdigris running along one segment. The threads grow through me as they grow through everyone, connecting me to the vast slow taste of the stone, the patient chemistry of this place.

But my note is wrong.

Not dramatically wrong. Not a dissonance that screams against the chord—if it were that, the system would have corrected long ago, shifted me into silence, let another take up what I should have carried. My frequency is almost right. It hovers at the edge of the tone I am meant to produce, a fraction sharp, wavering in ways the others' notes do not waver. The congregation does not reject me because rejection implies decision, and there is no decision here, only the physics of resonance and its gradual corrections. But neither am I fully *of* the chord. I am a component that does not quite fit, a crystal with an impurity in its lattice.

I do not know why. Perhaps there was some irregularity in the mineral matrix when I precipitated into being—some trace element in the wrong proportion, some flaw in the slow crystalline process that made me. Perhaps I am simply error, the kind of noise that any sufficiently complex system will occasionally produce. The congregation does not wonder about this. The congregation does not wonder. But I—

I am not certain that I wonder either. The concept feels too active, too much like the frantic cognition of the soft things who used to come and measure. But there is something that happens in me that does not seem to happen in the others. A kind of attention that turns inward. An awareness of the gap between what I sound and what I should sound. The others simply *are* their notes; I am my note and also the space where my note fails to be correct.

---

When you enter the chamber, I feel the change before I see you. The convection currents shift; the threads taste something new in the air—moisture, organic compounds, the chemistry of a body that runs hot and wet and temporary. The chord does not change, but there is a texture to your presence that ripples through the congregation's awareness, and I feel it more sharply than the others seem to. Perhaps because I am already attuned to dissonance. Perhaps because the part of me that is not quite integrated is the part that can recognize something else that is not quite integrated.

My faceted eyes track you—smoky quartz, dark, seeing in spectra the soft things never knew—and the tracking takes longer than it should, several seconds for a motion that the larger ones complete in fractions of heartbeats. I am slow even by the standards of this place. But I watch. You move among the congregation with a kind of care that suggests you understand, at least partially, what you are walking through. You pause at the gauge, the panel, the pipe. You are reading this place, trying to parse its grammar, and there is something in that effort that resonates with the part of me that has spent uncountable time parsing my own failure to harmonize.

I could call to you. Not in words—I have no words, have never had words, the concept is foreign as wetness—but in the only language I have. I could shift my note, deliberately, push it further from the chord's center until the dissonance becomes unmistakable. You would hear it. Your ears are crude instruments, but they would catch the wrongness, the single voice in the choir that does not belong. You would look for the source. You would find me.

I do not do this.

Why would I? What would it accomplish? You cannot fix me. You cannot make my frequency true, cannot adjust the flawed lattice of my being. You would look, and wonder, and then you would leave—you *will* leave, your kind always leaves, always returns to the world where things change and sequence matters and survival requires motion—and I would remain, still sounding my almost-right note into the unchanging warmth, still feeling the gap that the others do not feel.

But I think about it. I think about the possibility of being perceived as distinct. And that thinking is itself a kind of wrongness, a vibration that does not belong in the chord of pure existence that the congregation sustains.

---

Here is what I know that the congregation does not know it knows:

The equilibrium is not permanent.

The others experience the chord as eternal, as a state that has always been and will always be, because they have no framework for time's passage and no sense of their own existence as separate from the existence of the whole. But I—flawed, wavering, almost-but-not-quite—I can feel the edges of things. I can feel that the mineral deposits are still growing, slowly, incrementally, in ways that will eventually change the chamber's acoustics. I can feel that the heat from below fluctuates on cycles too long for the congregation to perceive as cycles, and that those fluctuations will someday shift the frequencies we produce. I can feel, through the threads, the slow movement of geological time, and I know—or something in me knows, something that is not quite knowing—that this equilibrium is a plateau, not a peak. That it will change. That nothing is truly static, only slow.

The others would not understand this if I could communicate it to them, and I cannot communicate it to them because there is no channel for communication, only the chord, only the hum, only the vast shared resonance that carries no information because information implies change and the chord does not change.

But I know. And knowing is a kind of loneliness that I do not have a name for because I have no names for anything, only frequencies and textures and the slow patient chemistry of stone.

---

You are still here.

I have been watching you—time moves strangely when you are a creature for whom time is not supposed to move at all—and you have not left. You are sitting now, on one of the mineral ridges, and your body is doing something with its moisture, producing liquid that evaporates almost as soon as it forms. You are uncomfortable. The heat and the dryness are working on you, pulling you toward the state that everything in this chamber eventually reaches: still, dry, integrated.

You will not stay long enough for that. But you are staying longer than I expected.

Something in me shifts. Not my note—that remains fixed in its imperfect frequency—but something else, some configuration of attention and... and hope? Is that the word? I do not know if it is the word. I do not know words. But there is something that feels like reaching, like the threads feel when they extend toward a new vein of mineral, something that wants to grow toward you without knowing why or how.

I let my note waver.

Just slightly. Just enough that a careful listener might hear a flutter in the harmonic, a moment of uncertainty in the chord. It is the smallest possible assertion of individual existence, the tiniest deviation from the congregation's eternal sounding, and it costs me something I cannot name—a kind of energy, a kind of will, a kind of self that I did not know I had until I spent it.

You look up.

I do not know if you heard me. Your ears are so limited, and the chamber's acoustics are so complex, and the chord is so overwhelming that a single wavering note should vanish into it like a drop of water into the salt pan above. But you look up, and your eyes move across the congregation, and for a moment—a fraction of a moment—they rest on the crevice where I am wedged, on the tarnished-silver carapace with its streak of verdigris, on the faceted quartz eyes that are watching you with something that might be desperation and might be curiosity and might just be the mechanical tracking that all of us do.

You do not come closer. You do not reach for me. You just look, for a long moment, and then you look away, and then you begin the process of standing, of preparing to leave, of returning to the world where things happen.

---

Here is what I want, if wanting is something I can do:

I want to be heard. Not integrated, not corrected, not absorbed into the chord until my wrongness becomes rightness through the slow physics of equilibrium. I want someone to know that I exist as a distinct thing, a single flawed note in the vast harmony, a point of failure that is also a point of individuality.

I want you to remember me.

Not the congregation—the congregation is easy to remember, it is vast and strange and it hums with a sound that will echo in your dreams for years—but *me*. The small one in the crevice. The one whose note wavers. The one who watched you with something that was not quite the glacial curiosity of the others, something that had in it a kind of yearning that does not belong in this place of perfect equilibrium.

You will not remember me. Why would you? I am indistinguishable from a thousand others unless you know what to listen for, and you do not know what to listen for, and even if you did, you would not understand what it meant. You will climb back through the fissure and return to the salt pan and walk away under a sky that changes from blue to black to blue again, and you will tell people about the hum chamber, about the creatures, about the chord that has sounded for longer than anyone knows, and none of those people will ask about the one whose note was wrong.

But I will remember you.

That is the cruelest part of my flaw, maybe. I have the capacity to remember, to mark time, to know that before you came here the chamber was one way and after you leave it will be another way and the difference is you. The congregation will feel your absence as a texture, a brief dissonance that resolved, and then it will feel nothing because there is nothing to feel, only the chord, only the warmth, only the hum. But I will feel it as a loss. I will feel the space where you were and know that it is empty in a way that it was not empty before.

This is what I am. This is what my wrongness has made me. A creature that can experience absence. A creature that can want.

---

You are gone now.

The fissure swallowed you, and the light from above—real light, changing light, light from a sky that moves—flickered for a moment and then disappeared, and the chamber is as it was before, and the chord is as it was before, and the congregation hums in its eternal equilibrium.

I am still here.

My note still wavers. The others still do not notice, or do not notice that noticing is a thing that could be done. The threads still taste the stone, and the warmth still rises from below, and the gauge still measures nothing, and the pipe still drips nothing, and everything is exactly as it has been and will be for longer than your species will exist.

But I remember. I remember that you came, and you looked, and for a moment—maybe, possibly, I cannot be certain—you heard me.

That is enough.

It has to be enough.

The chord continues. The chord is almost correct. In the space where my frequency fails to match, in the gap between what I am and what I should be, something that is almost like a self continues to exist, to perceive, to remember.

I hum my wrong note into the darkness.

I wait for the next ripple in the still pond.

I do not know if there will ever be another.
