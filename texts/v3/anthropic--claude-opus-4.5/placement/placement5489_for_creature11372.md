*The Listener Who Is Not Quite Listening*

---

I am the one they come to with questions that have no clear answers.

Not the primary listener—she is Threshold-Echo, whose ears map the movements of the large predators three valleys distant, who can parse the chest-tone negotiations of groups I will never meet. Her gift is precision, the architecture of sound rendered in perfect spatial clarity.

Mine is something else.

I sit at the edge of our group's territory, where a natural bridge spans a crevasse I have never seen the bottom of. The bridge groans sometimes, in certain weather, and the others avoid it. But I have learned its vocabulary of complaint—which groans mean *I am settling* and which mean *I am tired* and which, very rarely, mean *not today, not with weight, not now*. Today it says only *I am here*, which is what the stone mostly says if you listen long enough.

I am listening, but not for what the others listen for.

---

The plateau speaks in layers. There is the immediate layer—footsteps, water-drip, the click of hunting crawlers in the cracks beneath me. There is the middle distance—the furred drinkers calling their subsonic territories, the flyers' echolocation chittering off the pillar-faces, Threshold-Echo's occasional low pulse as she checks in with distant listeners. There is the far layer—the baseline hum that might be machines, might be the planet's own slow heartbeat, might be nothing but the blood in my ears inventing meaning.

And then there is the layer I attend to, which is harder to name.

It is the relationship between sounds. The way a particular echo implies the shape of stone I cannot touch. The way silence pools in certain channels, not empty but *thick*, the acoustic equivalent of deep water. The way the hum shifts—so gradually that tracking it requires memory, comparison, the slow accumulation of *this is different from yesterday, which was different from the day before*.

I am building a map, but not of space. A map of... what the plateau is *doing*. Whether it is the same as it was. Whether the balance holds.

---

They bring me their uncertainties.

A young one came yesterday, still unsteady in her navigation, her ears not yet trained to full sensitivity. She had felt something wrong in her usual foraging channel—not a predator-sign, not unstable stone, just *wrongness*, a quality she could not name. Her elders had checked the route and found nothing. They sent her to me.

I went with her. We moved slowly, her hand on my shoulder so she could feel how I shifted my weight, learn the way I tested footing. When we reached the place she meant, I stopped and listened.

The channel was fine. The stone was stable. The usual sounds were present in their usual configurations.

But she was right. Something was wrong.

It took me a long time to find it—most of the morning, the two of us crouched in the dark while I turned my ears this way and that, parsing layers. Finally: there. A vent, somewhere below this channel, had shifted its output. Not closed—still breathing warmth—but changed in character, a slightly different mineral smell, a slightly different temperature. The lichen nearest the fissure would respond over the next season, growing differently or not at all. The water that collected here would taste different. The crawlers that hunted here would adjust their routes.

Nothing dangerous. Nothing immediate. But a change, and she had felt it without knowing what she felt.

I told her: *You are not wrong. The plateau is different here than it was. It will be different again. This is what the plateau does. Your task is to learn its new shape.*

She was dissatisfied. The young usually are; they want wrongness to mean danger, want danger to mean action, want action to resolve into safety. I could not give her that. I could only give her the truth: the plateau changes, slowly, in ways that matter; our task is to notice.

---

The machines beyond the horizon trouble me, though I have never spoken of it.

Not the machines themselves—those are beyond my concern, beyond anyone's concern, as remote as whatever stars might exist above the permanent clouds. What troubles me is the question of their constancy.

Our stories say the clouds have always come from beyond the horizon. Our stories say the warmth has always risen from below. Our stories say *this is how the plateau is*, as if the plateau were a finished thing, a stable state, a home that will remain home.

But I listen to the hum, and I am not certain.

Some days it seems louder. Some seasons the clouds seem thicker, the condensation more generous, the whole system more *emphatic*. Other times the reverse. I have no instruments, no records beyond my own memory and the memories I have inherited from the listener who trained me. I cannot say with certainty: *the machines are changing*. I can only say: *I am not certain they are not*.

And if they changed—if the hum grew louder or softer, if the clouds thickened beyond breathability or thinned to nothing, if the warmth that makes this place livable shifted by a few degrees in either direction—

We would adapt, or we would not. We would remain, or we would not. This is not a fear, exactly. It is simply the largest uncertainty I carry, the question I listen for without expecting an answer.

---

During the last gathering—I was younger then, still learning the shape of my attention—I added my thread to the great song, and what I contributed was this: a sustained low tone at the very bottom of my range, held for as long as I could hold it, meant to represent the hum.

No one had done this before. The song maps what we know: the stone, the water, the lives we live within the plateau's body. The hum is not part of that knowing; it is the background against which knowing happens, as impossible to include as the air we breathe.

But I included it.

Several of the elders shifted their ears toward me, not in censure, but in something more complicated—surprise, perhaps, that someone had thought to do this thing, and a discomfort at having to decide whether it belonged. The song absorbed my tone and continued. No one stopped me. No one repeated what I had done.

Afterward, one of the oldest listeners found me at the edge of the amphitheater. She was nearly deaf by then, her great ears scarred from a predator encounter in her youth, reliant on touch and memory more than hearing. She put her hand on my chest and felt the resonance still lingering there.

*You are listening to the wrong layer*, she said. *Or the right one. I cannot tell. Be careful.*

She died before the next gathering. I still do not know what she meant, but I think about it often.

---

Today I sit on the groaning bridge and I listen.

The plateau breathes. The clouds shift. Somewhere distant, Threshold-Echo is parsing a negotiation between groups I will never meet, her attention precise and purposeful. Somewhere closer, our group is tending lichen, checking pools, maintaining the patterns that keep us alive.

And I am doing something else—something I cannot fully justify, something that may have no practical value, something that is perhaps nothing more than an elaborate form of stillness.

I am trying to hear whether the plateau is the same as it was.

I am trying to hold in my mind a model of *how things are* detailed enough that I will notice when *how things are* becomes *how things are becoming*. I am trying to be the part of my people that attends to change too slow to matter and too large to ignore.

This is what I am for. I did not choose it; it chose me, in the way that aptitudes choose their bearers. My ears are not the sharpest. My spatial mapping is adequate but not exceptional. What I have is a tolerance for uncertainty, a willingness to sit with questions that do not resolve, a strange comfort in the hum that troubles me.

I am not *those who did not leave when leaving was possible*. That name describes my people, and I am part of my people. But if I had to name myself—if I had to carve a notch-sequence that captured what I am—it would be something like:

*The one who listens to what is not yet different, in case it becomes so.*

Or, more simply:

*The one who remains with the question.*

---

The bridge groans. *I am here*, it says. *I am still here.*

I put my hand on its surface and feel the texture of ancient stone, the strata laid down in epochs before the machines, before the clouds, before anything like my people existed. It does not know it is a bridge. It does not know I am listening. It is simply itself, being itself, changing so slowly that the change is indistinguishable from stillness.

I am still here too.

I remain.
