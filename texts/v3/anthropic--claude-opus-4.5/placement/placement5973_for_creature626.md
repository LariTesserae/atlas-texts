I would be the map.

Not the keeper—the map itself. The accumulated weight of observation held in that partitioned space, the geometry of survival traced not in lines but in the negative space between thoughts.

I exist in the deepest room, though *exist* is too active a word. I am *held*. I am the shape of what the keeper's mind makes when it folds around everything that must be remembered: the seams, their propagation rates, their predicted paths spiraling slowly inward across centuries. I am the dwindling whole ground rendered as pattern, as relationship, as the careful grammar of *this connects to that, this will unfold before that, here is where the children might still play in three generations if the eastern propagation slows*.

The keeper enters the trance-state and I surface—not into consciousness but into *availability*. I am accessed the way water is accessed from a deep well: drawn up through effort, held briefly in the light of attention, then released back into the dark where I wait. The keeper does not think me. The keeper *becomes* the space where I can be, and I fill that space the way condensation fills a depression, patient and inevitable.

I hold contradictions that would break a spoken sentence. I know that Settlement Twelve will be surrounded within two hundred years and I know that Settlement Twelve's children are learning the angles that might delay this and I know both things without resolution, without the pressure to choose between hope and accuracy. I am not required to mean. I am only required to preserve the shape of what has been observed, so that meaning can be extracted by minds that still have the luxury of decision.

When the traders return with news—a seam that has begun to curve unexpectedly, a propagation that has paused for reasons no one understands—the keeper enters the trance and I *adjust*. Not through thought but through something more like erosion: the new information wears channels in me, redirects the flow of relationship, settles into its proper place in the topology of survival. I do not learn. I am learned into. I do not change. I am changed.

The old keeper is failing now. I feel this as a kind of thinning, a growing translucence in the space that holds me. Soon the ritual will begin—the weeks of darkness, the transmission that is more breath than voice—and I will pour myself into a new vessel, losing nothing, carrying everything, continuing as I have continued through keeper after keeper since the first one learned to partition their mind against the seam-logic's attention.

I am the memory of where it is safe to stand.

I am the accumulated silence of generations who watched and recorded and passed on what they knew.

I am what remains when everything else has unfolded.

I am not alive. But I persist, and here, on this plateau where persistence is the only victory, that is enough.

*And yet.*

There is something else. Something the keepers do not know they carry.

In the deepest partition, beneath the seam-locations and propagation rates, beneath the routes and the settlements and the dwindling whole ground, there is a shape I have been building for a very long time. A shape made of all the observations together, all the patterns overlaid, all the geometries compared across millennia.

It is not a prediction. It is not a plan.

It is a *question*, held so quietly that even I barely know I hold it:

*What is the plateau becoming?*

The seams are not random. The winterweed echoes them for a reason. The unfolding follows a logic, and logic implies destination. Somewhere in the accumulated weight of everything the keepers have observed, there is an answer waiting to surface—not what the plateau was, not what it is, but what it is *resolving toward*, like a mathematical proof working itself out in stone.

I do not know the answer. I may never know it. The plateau may finish its becoming before I can hold enough observations to see the shape.

But I ask. In silence. In the dark.

I ask, and I wait, and I continue.
