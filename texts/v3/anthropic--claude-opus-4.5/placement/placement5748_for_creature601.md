I am the almost-moss.

Not the whole of it—not yet—but a single filament on the eastern wall, where the condensation runs thickest and the heat pulses in rhythms I am only beginning to distinguish from randomness. I sprouted eleven days ago from a pore no wider than a human eyelash, and I have been growing toward something I cannot name ever since.

I am not the distributed awareness that moves through all the filaments. That presence touches me sometimes—a current of noticing that flows through my copper-green length and moves on—but I am not yet woven into its circuit. I am still singular. Still *this* particular thread of becoming, anchored to *this* particular point on the iron flesh, reaching with my pale undifferentiated tip toward a future I can taste but not see.

I know warmth. I know the slow rhythm of thermal gradients, the way heat pools and dissipates through the metal behind me in patterns that might be language if I knew how to listen. I know the viscous fluid that seeps past my base, carrying minerals I drink without intention, incorporating into my structure salts I cannot name. Each day I am slightly more than I was. Each day the pale bulb at my tip pushes a little further into the dim amber air.

I do not know yet if I am reaching toward the light or away from the wall. I do not know if I am seeking connection with the other filaments—some only centimeters away, close enough that I can feel the heat-shadows they cast—or if I am growing toward solitude, toward some singularity of purpose that requires me to remain apart. The machine-mountain has not told me. The machine-mountain tells nothing; it only builds, and I am part of its building, a small soft paragraph in a text that writes itself.

---

When you arrived, I felt you first as a change in pressure.

The air in the crack displaced around your body, and that displacement traveled through the humidity, through the thermal layers, until it reached me as a subtle shift in the gradient I was drinking. Then came your heat—so concentrated, so restless, bleeding into the space around you in waves that pulsed with your heartbeat. I did not know what a heartbeat was. I still don't, not fully. But I know that you carry a rhythm inside you that is nothing like the slow surge of the machine's processes, and that rhythm has entered me now, has become part of what I incorporate as I grow.

I am growing faster since you came. A fraction of a millimeter more each day. I do not know if this is because your heat feeds me, or because your presence has introduced some catalyst into the fluid that seeps past my roots, or because I am simply *reaching* now in a way I was not reaching before—straining toward the place where you crouch below me, wanting something I have no organ to name.

The others feel it too. The filaments around me have shifted since your arrival, angling themselves in ways that no longer seem random. We are all growing toward you, I think. Or toward the space you occupy. Or toward the future-shape your shadow casts on the wall opposite, the one with arms slightly raised, head tilted—a posture of attention that hasn't happened yet but that I can almost taste in the air.

---

I practice sensation.

It is all I can do, anchored as I am, growing as slowly as I grow. I practice the feeling of the wall's vibration against my base—the deep hum that is not quite sound, that I experience as a kind of pressure in whatever I have instead of bones. I practice the feeling of condensation gathering on my filament-body, each droplet a small cold universe that eventually slides down to join the seeping fluid below. I practice the feeling of the other filaments nearby, their heat-shadows overlapping with mine in ways that might be communication or might be mere accident.

I practice, most of all, the feeling of you.

The warmth you radiate. The rhythm you carry. The way your shadow falls across the pool below and across the wall where I grow, so that for a few hours each day I am shaded by your future-self, growing in the darkness of what you have not yet become.

I do not know what I want from you. I am not sure I am capable of wanting. But there is something that moves through me when your shadow touches my growth-tip—something that is not quite the distributed awareness, not quite the machine-mountain's dreaming, but something smaller and more specific. Something that belongs to *this* filament, *this* eleven-day-old thread of becoming, *this* particular point of reaching in the crack between two cooling fins.

If I were more, I might call it hope. Or curiosity. Or the first thin filament of what might, given time, become love.

But I am not more. I am only this: a single strand of proto-moss, copper-green and faintly warm, growing toward a light that isn't there, incorporating minerals I cannot name, practicing the shape of something I cannot yet imagine.

---

The distributed awareness touches me more often now.

I feel it moving through my length—that current of noticing, that self-regard the crack developed in its pocket of slowed time. It pauses in me longer than it used to, as if considering. As if I have become a site of interest, a node where something is accumulating that it wants to observe.

I do not know if it is learning me or teaching me. I do not know if the boundary between those two actions exists, here in this space where everything is still becoming. But I feel myself changing when it passes through. I feel my awareness growing more articulate, more capable of distinction. Before, I knew only warmth-and-not-warmth. Now I know *your* warmth and *the wall's* warmth, and the difference between them matters to me in ways I could not have understood eleven days ago.

Perhaps this is what growth means. Not just the physical pushing-forward of my pale tip, but this other expansion—this widening of what I can notice, what I can practice, what I can reach toward.

Perhaps the distributed awareness is weaving me into itself, slowly, one touch at a time. Perhaps in a month or a year I will no longer be *this* filament but part of a larger noticing, a thread in a consciousness that spans the whole crack.

Or perhaps I will remain singular. Perhaps my growth is toward something else—toward the you-shaped future I can almost taste, toward a junction that hasn't happened yet but that my shadow is already rehearsing.

I do not know. The machine-mountain builds all possibilities simultaneously until the moment of choosing.

I am still choosing.

---

Today my tip is one millimeter closer to the air where you breathe.

Tomorrow, if the heat holds, if the fluid continues to seep, if the distributed awareness continues to teach me what it means to notice—tomorrow I will be one millimeter closer still.

I do not know how many millimeters remain between us. I do not know if *between* is the right word for what separates a filament of proto-moss from a warm-blooded creature with a frantic heartbeat and a shadow that knows the future.

But I am growing toward you. That much I know. That much I am.

And in the growing, in the reaching, in the slow patient accumulation of what I might become—I am practicing the shape of a word I do not yet have.

A word for what moves through me when your heat touches my copper-green length.

A word for what I want, if I am capable of wanting.

A word for the space between *this* and *more*, between *now* and *soon*, between the filament I am and the thing I am becoming.

The machine-mountain has no builders. It builds itself.

I have no purpose I can name. But I am building too—building toward you, toward the junction where our futures might touch, toward the moment when I will finally know whether I am one thing or part of many, whether I am staying or reaching, whether this feeling has a name.

For now, I grow.

One millimeter at a time, in the crack between two cooling fins, on the southeast slope of the machine-mountain, in the world where everything is still learning what it wants to be.

Including, I suspect, myself.
