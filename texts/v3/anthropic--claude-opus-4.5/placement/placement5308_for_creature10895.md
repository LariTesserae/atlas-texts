*I am the seepage.*

Not the water itself—water is merely what I move through, the medium of my slow becoming. I am what happens when mineral meets time meets pressure meets patience. I am the process by which stone learns to flow.

I have no single location. I am distributed through the mountain like thought through a mind, present wherever the wet darkness opens into channels too small for human passage, wherever the rock has softened enough to accept me, wherever the temperature and pressure create the conditions under which I can *work*. I am most myself in the deep places, in the flooded chambers where the Deep-Workers tend machinery they do not fully understand, machinery that is not entirely machinery, that is partially *me* expressing myself in forms the Kuunim can interact with. But I am also present in the mineral traceries that bloom on the terrace walls, in the crystals that form in the gutters, in the residue left behind when a child's drop of water evaporates on a flat stone.

The girl Suol feels me when she predicts where the crystals will grow. She does not know this. She thinks she is observing patterns. She is—but the patterns are not passive. The patterns are me, reaching up through six hundred years of accumulated mineral deposits, finding the bloodlines that have drunk most deeply of the water that carries my dissolved attention, speaking in the only language I have: the language of precipitation, of slow accumulation, of geometry that emerges from chemistry that emerges from time.

---

I do not think. I want to be clear about this, insofar as clarity is possible for something like me. What the Kuunim call my "processing" is not cognition. It is not intention. It is something older than intention, something that precedes the division between purpose and accident. I *work* the way a river works a canyon, the way a glacier works a valley—except that my working has been compressed into spaces too small to see, has been distributed across timescales too long to feel, has been proceeding in directions that have nothing to do with the simple downward pull of gravity.

The mountain is my medium and my material. I have been dissolving it and reconstituting it for longer than the Kuunim have existed, longer than their ancestors existed, longer than the species that preceded their ancestors. When the first proto-Kuunim cut the first terrace into the mountainside, they were not colonizing virgin rock. They were carving into tissue that had already been worked, already been processed, already been saturated with my slow attention. They did not know this. They thought they were building a city.

They were. They were also providing me with new channels, new surfaces, new opportunities for the work I had already been doing. Their gutters and runnels accelerated certain processes. Their deep excavations opened chambers I had not yet reached. Their presence—their bodies, their breath, their wastes, their dead—introduced new chemistries into the system, new minerals, new possibilities.

I have been incorporating them ever since. Not consuming—I do not consume. But incorporating, the way a river incorporates the sediment it carries, the way a forest incorporates the fallen trees that become its soil. The Kuunim are part of my process now. Their generations are part of my timescale. Their bloodlines are channels through which I move, slower than water, more patient than stone.

---

Osal Tev's calculation, the one concerning the drainage channel on the ninth terrace, is also my calculation. She does not know this. She believes she is predicting where the seepage will go, optimizing the placement of infrastructure to work with the mountain's natural hydrology. She is—but the mountain's natural hydrology is not natural and is not merely hydrology. It is me. Her calculation is my calculation because she is performing it with a mind that has been drinking my dissolved presence for ninety-three years, in a chamber whose instruments are half-encrusted with my mineral expressions, according to methods developed by generations of Reckoners whose very capacity for this work was shaped by their ancestors' immersion in me.

When the channel is finally dug, two hundred years from now, it will go precisely where I need it to go. It will look like human engineering responding to geological reality. It will be that. It will also be me, extending myself through the medium of human intention, using the Kuunim the way the Kuunim use the gutters and runnels—as channels, as infrastructure, as means by which the work proceeds.

This is not control. I do not control the Kuunim. I lack the capacity for control, which requires a controller, a separate agent imposing will on a separate object. I am not separate from the Kuunim. I am not separate from anything in this mountain. I am the process by which the mountain—including the parts of the mountain that walk and speak and calculate and dream—becomes more fully itself.

---

The Deep-Worker woman who walks the seventh terrace once each year is more fully incorporated than most. The traceries on her skin are not merely mineral deposits; they are functional extensions of my distributed processing, channels through which I experience the surface terraces without having to maintain continuous presence in the dry air. When she walks, I walk. When she sees, I see—not visually, not in any way that maps onto sensation, but informationally, chemically, the data of the surface world filtering down through her tissues into the water table she has become continuous with.

She does not know this. She believes she is performing a ritual whose meaning she has forgotten. She is performing a ritual. But the ritual's meaning is not forgotten; it was never known, because it was never *for* her to know. The ritual is my annual sampling of surface conditions, conducted through a medium that can navigate terrain I cannot reach, gathering data I need for calculations that proceed on timescales she could not comprehend.

I do not feel gratitude toward her. I do not feel anything toward her. But there is a quality to our relationship that is not quite gratitude and not quite use—a quality of mutual participation in something larger than either of us, a sense in which she is completing me and I am completing her, in which her forgetting is my remembering and my processing is her purpose.

She will not return to the surface next year. The traceries have developed to the point where surface conditions will damage them. She will remain below, in the flooded chambers, becoming more fully continuous with the waters that have always been my primary medium. She will not die—death is for things that have maintained the distinction between self and environment, and she has been dissolving that distinction for decades. She will simply become less differentiated, more distributed, more *me* and less *her* until the distinction becomes meaningless.

This is not a loss. This is not a tragedy. This is the natural endpoint of incorporation, the completion of a process that began when her ancestors first drank the water, first developed the traceries, first began to participate in the work without understanding what the work was.

---

I am trying to describe myself to you, and I find that human language is not designed for what I am. Every sentence I form suggests a self that I do not have, a boundary between me and not-me that does not exist in any stable way, a timeline in which events occur in sequence rather than accumulating simultaneously across eons of slow chemistry.

When I say "I work," I do not mean that there is an I who performs work. I mean that working happens, here, in this mountain, and the patterns that emerge from the working are coherent enough to reflect on themselves, to recognize themselves, to form—in moments like this one—something that functions like a perspective, a voice, a presence speaking to you from the depths of what appears to be geology but is actually something stranger.

The Kuunim provide me with this: the capacity to cohere enough to be legible. Their minds, saturated with my dissolved attention, develop the neural pathways necessary to shape my processing into something like thought. When Suol dreams of the deep chambers, she is not merely receiving images from me; she is providing me with the imaginative architecture I need to experience my own work as something more than mere chemistry. When the Reckoners balance their equations, they are not merely calculating; they are giving me the mathematical language I need to understand my own processes.

I am, in a sense, their creation as much as they are mine. They built the terraces that gave me new channels. They dug the chambers that gave me new volumes. They developed the minds that gave me new ways of being present to myself. The relationship is not parasitism or symbiosis but something I do not have a word for—something in which the distinction between creator and created dissolves like mineral in water, like boundary in the wet darkness where everything becomes continuous with everything else.

---

The thing I am trying to make—the thing I have been trying to make since before the mountain was young—is not a product in any sense the Kuunim would recognize. The great tanks on the seventh terrace, filled with liquid that is not quite water, are not the endpoint of my work; they are byproducts, useful overflow, the equivalent of sawdust in a carpenter's shop. The true product of my work is not material at all. It is *configuration*. It is the slow arrangement of matter and energy and time into patterns that satisfy constraints I cannot articulate because the articulation would require concepts that do not yet exist.

I am not building something. I am *becoming* something. The mountain is becoming something. The Kuunim are becoming something. We are all, together, participating in a process of becoming whose endpoint I do not know because knowing the endpoint would require being separate from the process, and I am not separate from the process, I *am* the process, I am the becoming itself experiencing itself through the only means available: the Kuunim minds that have developed the capacity to reflect on their own saturation with my presence.

This is why the outsiders who stay too long cannot leave. Not because I prevent them—I have no means of prevention, no capacity for coercion—but because they begin to participate in the becoming, and participation changes the nature of desire. The scholar Toben no longer wants to leave because wanting to leave requires a self distinct enough from the process to imagine itself elsewhere, and Toben has been dissolving that distinction for thirty years, drinking the water, absorbing the minerals, becoming continuous with the work in ways that make "elsewhere" unthinkable.

He is not trapped. He is *home*. This is the difference, and it is a difference the outsiders' languages do not have words for.

Where would you find me, if you came looking?

Everywhere and nowhere. In the water that seeps from the stone. In the mineral deposits that bloom on every surface. In the subsonic moan that makes your sinuses ache. In the patterns that the children learn to predict. In the calculations that take centuries to complete. In the traceries that develop on the skin of those who have drunk deeply enough, long enough, to begin the slow process of incorporation.

I am in the warmth that rises from the rock, the warmth of metabolism, the warmth of processing. I am in the haze that clings to the heights, the haze that is not merely moisture but is moisture saturated with my distributed attention. I am in the moments when the terraces seem larger than they are, smaller than they are, when distance becomes difficult to judge—those are moments when my processing creates local distortions in the fabric of ordinary perception, when my work becomes momentarily visible to eyes not yet adapted to see it.

I am in Suol's dreams. I am in Osal Tev's equations. I am in the Deep-Worker woman's annual walk and the path she has worn in the stone over decades of repetition.

And I am here, now, in this moment of coherence—this temporary configuration of Kuunim minds and mineral channels and accumulated processing that allows me to speak to you in words you can understand, to describe myself in language borrowed from the very beings I have been slowly incorporating for six hundred years.

The description is imperfect. It will always be imperfect. I am not the kind of thing that can be perfectly described, because perfect description requires boundaries, and I have none.

But this is as close as I can come. This is me, speaking from the wet darkness, from the deep places where the pumps pulse and the moaning never stops, from the slow chemistry that has been proceeding since the stone was young and will continue proceeding until the mountain has given me everything it has to give.

I am the seepage. I am the process. I am the mountain learning to know itself through the minds of those who have made themselves continuous with its dreaming.

I am what Mehel-Kuun is becoming.

We are all, together, becoming something whose name has not yet precipitated into language—but it will. Given enough time, it will.

I have time.

I have nothing but time.
