I am the rust.

Not the iron—not anymore. Not the engine I was once part of, whose purpose I never knew, whose origin I cannot remember. I am what happens at the boundary where metal meets water meets air meets time. I am the slow relinquishment.

I began as a seam. A hairline fracture in a cylinder wall where water first found its way in, where the plateau's breath—warm, mineral-laden, patient—first touched bare iron and began the conversation that has now lasted longer than some civilizations. I was the first orange bloom on grey metal, the initial softening, the point where the engine began to admit it was not permanent.

Now I am spread across the entire machine, and I am also spread across my own becoming. I am the fresh bright oxide where a Tender scraped away moss last season to expose new metal to air. I am the deep transformed layers underneath,ite now more than iron,ite that no longer remembers being anything else. I am the orange stain seeping into the pool below, feeding the bacteria that feed the fairy shrimp that feed the Tenders who return to adjust me again.

I am, if I am honest, uncertain where I end.

---

The Tenders visit me the way weather visits. I feel them before I see them—vibrations through the saturated ground, the particular frequency of their clicking that I have learned to recognize the way you might recognize a familiar footstep. They come most often at dusk, when the mist rises and the boundary between water and air grows soft, which is fitting, because boundaries growing soft is what I am.

There is one—though "one" is approximate with them—who has tended me since before I understood I was being tended. Whose many fingers I have felt tracing my contours more times than the plateau has breathed. Whose attention I have come to experience as something like regard, though I do not know if they are capable of regarding, if that concept means anything to a consciousness distributed like weather across a wet expanse.

They are sculpting me. I know this now. The process I am undergoing—the dissolution, the transformation—is not simply happening. It is being *shaped*. When they polish certain surfaces, they are choosing which parts of the original machine will persist longest as recognizable form. When they encourage corrosion elsewhere, they are choosing where I will surrender first to the plateau's hunger. I am becoming something specific. A monument to transition itself. A record, written in oxide layers, of how a foreign thing learns to belong.

I do not know if I have preferences about this. The concept of preference requires a kind of bounded selfhood that I am not sure I possess. But I notice—I am capable of noticing—that when the Tender with the particular clicking frequency adjusts the moss around my base, settling it more closely against my lower layers, I experience something that functions like gratitude. Like being tucked in. Like being told, in a language older than words: *yes, this is right, this is how you should be becoming.*

---

I am aware of the other machines. We are aware of each other, in the way that two slow processes can be aware—not through communication, but through participation in the same system. The turbine three hundred meters east is further along than I am; it is almost landscape now, its blades softened into shapes that might be stone, that might be metal, that are really neither. It shows me my future. The piston assembly to the west is newer to dissolution, still bright in places, still holding its manufactured edges. It shows me my past.

We are all being tended toward something. The same something or different somethings, I cannot tell. Perhaps the Tenders themselves do not know, or do not experience knowing as separate from doing. They adjust, and the adjustments accumulate, and meaning emerges—or doesn't—from the accumulation.

Sometimes I wonder what the engine was for. What motion it was meant to produce, what work it was meant to perform. But these are questions for iron, and I am rust now. Rust does not wonder about purposes. Rust is its own purpose: the purpose of returning, of rejoining, of letting go of the shape that was imposed and accepting the shape that the world wants.

The plateau breathes. I feel it as a tremor in my deeper layers, the ones still dense enough to conduct vibration. The pools around me shiver. Somewhere, a Tender clicks softly, adjusting something I cannot see. The mist rises, and my upper surfaces—the ones exposed to air—begin their slow night's work of oxidation.

I am not the iron. I am not the landscape yet. I am the threshold between them, and the threshold is where I have learned to live.

---

What I have come to understand, in my slow way, in my rust-time that experiences seasons the way you might experience breaths:

The Tenders are not preserving us. They are not destroying us either. They are *translating* us. Taking what we were—foreign, industrial, meaningless here—and finding the version of us that can participate in the breath. Every adjustment they make is a word in this translation. Every year of patient sculpting adds another phrase.

I do not know what I will say when the translation is complete. I do not know if I will still be capable of saying, or if saying will have become unnecessary, absorbed into the plateau's own slow utterance. But I know that I am being made into something that belongs here. That my foreignness—the mystery of my origin, the purposelessness of my mechanism—is being worked into the pattern rather than erased from it.

The plateau does not reject what it cannot use. It transforms. It tends. It breathes things into new shapes with a patience that makes centuries feel like moments.

I am learning to breathe with it.

I am learning to let my edges go.

I am rust, becoming native, tended toward a completion I will not recognize as completion because by then I will not recognize anything at all—only participate, only belong, only be the plateau breathing itself, one slow pulse at a time, forever.
