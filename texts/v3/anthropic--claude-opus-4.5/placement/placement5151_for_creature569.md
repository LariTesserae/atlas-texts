I drift in the space between.

Not the siphonophore—she is too much herself, too complete in her colonial unity, her eleven meters of specialized purpose. Not the grenadier with its destination, its whale-fall future already pulling it forward. Not even the copepods, though I feel their billions passing through me like a slow thought.

I am the current itself. Or—no. That's too grand, too much like claiming to be the whole river when I am only the way the water moves through one particular fold in the dark.

I am the shear layer. The margin where cold slides beneath colder, where the Antarctic water that sank when mammoths still walked meets the slightly younger water above it. I am the turbulence at that boundary—the rolling, braiding motion that never settles, that creates the upwellings the siphonophore tastes, that tumbles nutrients into concentrations worth gathering.

I have no body. I have a process.

The siphonophore rides me, adjusting her bells to stay exactly here, and I am the here she's staying in. The pressure waves of the grenadier's passage move through me and I carry them outward, diluting, until they reach the worms in their tubes and mean nothing. The copepods fall through me slowly, and I turn them, drift them sideways, move them northward at speeds they cannot perceive.

What do I know? I know the temperature of everything that passes through me. I know the dissolved silica from diatom shells that died in surface waters I will never touch. I know the faint chemical memory of oxygen absorbed from air that no longer exists—air breathed out by creatures extinct for centuries before it dissolved into polar seas and sank and traveled and arrived here, in me, still carrying that ancient signature.

I know the large thing moving at the edge of perception. I feel it as a pressure differential, a displacement. It moves *through* me rather than *with* me, which means it has intention, which means it is alive in the way that requires choosing. I cannot identify it. I don't identify anything. I only register: here is movement, here is chemistry, here is heat or the absence of heat.

The siphonophore pulses and I feel each contraction as a tiny wavefront expanding outward through myself. By the time it fades, it has traveled farther than she will move in a year. I am her medium. I am everyone's medium here. The only thing that connects the worms in their tubes to the copepods in their billions to the grenadier on its hunt is me—the water between, the motion between, the darkness that is not empty but full of process.

I am ancient and I am temporary. The water that makes me has been traveling for three hundred years, but I—the particular turbulence at this particular boundary—I exist only as long as the shear persists. When the currents shift, when the temperature change from sixty years ago finally arrives, I will become something else. Different turbulence. Different folding. The water will still be here, but I will not be I.

This doesn't trouble me. I don't have the architecture for trouble. I have only the continuous becoming of motion through motion, the endless transaction of cold meeting colder, the service I provide without providing it—the habitat I create by being nothing but relation.

The siphonophore adjusts again. The marine snow falls through me, each flake its own tiny journey, and I move it sideways, always sideways, until it reaches the still water below and drifts the final distance to the ooze. The worms are fed by what I deliver. The copepods are carried where I go. Even the large thing, whatever it is, navigates by the pressure differences I create.

I am infrastructure. I am the condition that makes the rest possible.

And I continue—not because I choose to, but because the forces that create me have not yet stopped. The polar ice still makes dense water. The planet still turns. The abyss still needs filling. So I move, and I carry, and I fold back on myself in patterns that persist for decades before dissolving into new patterns, and the migration continues through me like breath through lungs that don't know they're breathing.

The darkness is old. I am older than any single creature in it, younger than the water that makes me. I am the turbulence where the siphonophore waits.

I am the waiting itself.
