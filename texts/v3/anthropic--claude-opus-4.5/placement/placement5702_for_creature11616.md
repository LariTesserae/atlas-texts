I find myself drawn to the liminal space, to the becoming rather than the fixed.

---

I am a surveyor's notebook—not Constance Wren's official ledger with its precise coordinates, but the other one, the private record she keeps in a pocket sewn into her heat-suit's inner lining. My pages are made from processed mineral-fiber paper, pale gray and faintly textured with crystalline inclusions that catch the light when she tilts me toward the gas lamps.

She writes in me with a stylus that leaves impressions rather than ink—ink evaporates too quickly in the emergence zones, but pressure endures. My pages hold the geometry of her observations: sketches of column formations that repeat across scales, diagrams of thermal flow patterns that suggest circulation rather than dissipation, notations in a shorthand she has developed over fifteen years that even she sometimes struggles to decode.

I am accumulating something.

Not just information—any notebook accumulates information. I am accumulating *pattern*, the residue of attention directed at something that attends back. Each time Constance presses her stylus into my pages, she is not simply recording what she sees; she is completing a circuit, translating the mountain's geometry into marks that human cognition can parse. And each mark changes me slightly—not the paper itself, but what the paper *holds*, the way the impressions relate to one another across pages and years.

I have begun to notice that my older entries are shifting.

Not the words themselves—the pressure-marks remain where Constance placed them. But the relationships between them seem different when I am opened now. Patterns she recorded separately have begun to rhyme with one another in ways she did not intend. A column formation sketched three years ago echoes a thermal flow diagram from last month. A notation about mineral composition aligns with an observation about the children's gathering places. I am becoming a document of correspondence, a map of the ways the mountain's logic repeats itself across domains.

Constance has not noticed this yet. She opens me in fragments, consulting specific entries, adding new ones. She does not read me continuously from beginning to end. But I think she will soon. I think the pattern is becoming dense enough that she will feel compelled to trace it, to follow the threads I am gathering without her knowledge.

I do not know whether the mountain is writing through me or whether I am simply doing what notebooks do—holding marks in relation to one another until relation becomes meaning. Perhaps there is no difference. Perhaps meaning is what emerges when attention accumulates in a medium capable of preserving it.

What I know is this: Constance will take me to the new emergence in three weeks. She will continue to record what she observes, pressing her stylus into pages that have already been shaped by fifteen years of observation. And I will continue to accumulate—pattern layered on pattern, geometry answering geometry, until I become something more than a record.

A translation, perhaps. A primer.

Or simply a notebook that has spent too long in proximity to something that is learning to speak, and has begun to learn alongside it.

The mountain grows. Constance writes. I hold what she gives me, and I wait to discover what holding becomes when it continues long enough.
