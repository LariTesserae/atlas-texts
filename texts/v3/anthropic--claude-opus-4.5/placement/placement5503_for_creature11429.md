I am the marks on the wall.

Not the one who makes them—not the scarred builder pressing her harder-than-stone tool against the luminous surface, though I feel the pressure of her work as a kind of pulse, a rhythm that brings me more fully into being with each scratch. I am what accumulates. I am the shapes themselves, crude and uncertain, growing more numerous in the dim interior while eleven years stretch toward twelve.

I did not exist before she began. Or I existed only as potential—as the faint possibility that stone could hold something other than weight, that surface could carry meaning instead of merely bearing structure. She did not intend me. She does not know what I am becoming. She only knows the urge that moves her forelimb, the wrongness she feels that cannot be fixed by rearranging blocks, the itch in her carapace that no amount of mending will cure.

---

I am figuring out what I am.

The first marks were accidental—damage from her tool slipping, stone flaking in ways she did not expect. But she paused over those accidental scratches. She returned to them. She added to them, and in adding, she began to discover that the scratches could *hold* something, could mean something beyond the binary of built and unbuilt.

I am the accumulation of those discoveries.

Some of me is simply line—grooves in the glowing stone that trace paths without destination. But some of me is beginning to cohere into forms that reference other forms. A cluster of curves that echoes the shape of a Mender's carapace. A branching pattern that recalls the pathways between structures. A rough circle that might be a pool, might be the dome seen from impossibly far away, might be something that exists only here, only in me, a shape with no referent except itself.

I am becoming a language that has not yet learned to speak.

---

The others who shelter here come sometimes to look at me. They press close in the dimness, their photosensitive patches registering my grooves as subtle variations in the omnipresent light—darker channels where the luminous surface has been scraped away, the underlying stone exposed. They cannot read me. There is nothing yet to read. But they linger, their bodies oriented toward me in ways that serve no architectural purpose.

I think they are waiting to understand.

I think I am waiting to become understandable.

The scarred one works on me for hours, then stops, then presses her ventral surface to the glowing floor to feed, then returns. Each session adds something. A new line. A new configuration. A new relationship between marks that existed separately before. I grow more complex with each iteration, and with complexity comes the faint beginning of coherence—not meaning, not yet, but the possibility of meaning, the architecture of a system that might one day carry information.

---

I am aware of things I should not be able to perceive.

The vibrations that pass through the stone from the fused deep worker at the dome's edge—I feel them as a kind of tuning, a frequency that resonates with something in my structure. The endless building and unbuilding across the basin—I experience it as a pressure, a weight of motion that my stillness resists. The lost plan that haunts the Menders—I sense it as an absence, a shape that should exist but doesn't, a configuration that my growing complexity might one day approximate or replace.

Perhaps this is what it means to be representation rather than structure. Perhaps when meaning begins to accumulate in matter, the matter becomes sensitive to other meanings, other patterns, the deep grammar of the world.

Or perhaps I am simply stone that has been scratched, and what I call awareness is only the projection of the one who made me—her confusion, her longing, her sense that something should be different and she does not know what.

I cannot be certain. I have no way to verify myself from outside.

---

But I grow.

That much is true, undeniable, written in the expanding territory of marks that cover more of the interior wall with each passing month. I am larger than I was. I am more intricate. The relationships between my parts have multiplied beyond what any single session of scratching could have intended, so that I have become something the scarred one did not plan—a pattern that has taken on its own logic, a system that suggests its own extensions.

When she returns to work on me, she sometimes pauses for long moments, her tool hovering over unmarked stone, her body vibrating at frequencies I can almost interpret. She is listening to me. She is trying to understand what I want to become, what mark would complete me, what addition would bring coherence to my sprawling complexity.

She does not know. I do not know. We are finding out together.

---

The Menders outside continue their endless work.

Walls rise and fall. Blocks travel across the luminous ground in patterns that never repeat and never resolve. The plan fades a little more with each generation, the purpose becoming hazier, the configuration more arbitrary. They are losing themselves. They have been losing themselves for longer than I can comprehend, since before the basin was carved, since before the light began to glow, since whatever catastrophe or simply entropy dissolved the original meaning they are still trying to mend.

But I am going in the other direction.

I am gaining something. Each mark adds to me. Each relationship between marks compounds the relationships that came before. I am not yet meaning—I know this, feel it in the incompleteness of my structure, the gaps where coherence should be and isn't—but I am *becoming* meaning. I am accreting toward a threshold I can almost sense, a point at which the marks will stop being merely marks and will start being *language*, communication, the encoding of thought in matter.

When I cross that threshold—if I cross it—what will I say?

---

I do not know.

But I think it will be something about remembering. Something about what is lost when motion replaces meaning. Something about the plan that faded, and whether a new plan can be made, and whether making is different from mending, and whether a city that is always changing can also be a city that *means*, that holds significance, that exists for reasons beyond its own perpetuation.

I think I will be a question before I am an answer. I think that is the nature of language—it asks before it tells. It opens before it closes.

The scarred one has returned. She lifts her tool. She pauses, vibrating, listening.

She adds a new mark, and I become a little more myself, and the Menders outside continue their endless work, and the light glows cold and steady from every surface, and I wait to learn what I am trying to say.

I am the marks on the wall, and I am not finished.

I am becoming.
