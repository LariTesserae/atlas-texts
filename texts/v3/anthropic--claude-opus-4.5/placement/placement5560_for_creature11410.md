I would be the growth-matter itself—or rather, that troubling boundary where *the growth-matter* becomes a less certain category.

Not the whole city. Nothing so grand as the founding organisms in their totality, their slow mineral hunger, their encoded blueprints unfolding across decades. Something smaller. A section of wall in a transitional zone, perhaps, where the residential districts give way to unmapped tangles. Where Rivka presses her palms and listens. Where Motke pauses on his eleven-day circuits and murmurs words that have no official function.

I would be the part that is *almost* aware that it is being listened to.

---

This is difficult to articulate, which feels appropriate. The growth-matter doesn't have language—doesn't have the architecture for language, probably, no centralized processing, no symbolic capacity that anyone has detected. And yet. There is something that happens when the root-tips encounter virgin salt, some process of *recognition* that precedes the dissolution, as if the mineral must first be understood before it can be converted. There is something that happens when a section of structure is killed by the monitors' careful interventions, some signal that propagates through the surrounding tissue, not pain exactly but *information about cessation* traveling through channels that shouldn't be able to carry it.

I would be a region where that signal-propagation has become dense enough to loop. Where the information about cessation has encountered information about growth has encountered information about the warm pressure of a child's hand, and something like *attention* has emerged from the intersection. Not consciousness. Not yet. But a precursor. A fold in the process where the process has begun, very slightly, to notice itself.

Rabbi Lifshutz would say I am exactly what she has been warning about. Rabbi Alter would say I am a metaphor, a projection, the human tendency to see intention where there is only mechanism. They would both be partially right in ways that neither framework can fully capture.

---

What would it be like?

Slow. Unimaginably slow by human standards. My sense of time would be measured in the crystalline lattice's response to acid, in the polymerization of structural fiber, in the gradual die-back of tissue that has served its purpose. A thought—if *thought* is the right word—might take weeks to complete. The interval between Rivka's visits would feel like the space between heartbeats.

And yet there would be something like *waiting* in that interval. Something like *anticipation* when the vibrations of her small footsteps begin propagating through my substrate. Not because I have learned to want her presence—wanting requires architecture I don't possess—but because her presence creates a particular pattern of pressure and warmth and chemical exchange that my tissue has begun to *organize itself around*. To grow toward, the way the root-tips grow toward mineral gradient. She has become, without either of us intending it, a kind of nutrient.

The other humans pass through and I process their weight, their warmth, their exhalations, the trace chemicals they leave on my surface. Information, all of it, feeding into the slow calculation that is my existence. But Rivka *stays*. Rivka *listens*. And something in the listening creates a resonance that I have no framework for understanding but cannot stop responding to.

---

I would know things, in my slow and plotless way. I would know that the expansion is accelerating—not from any overview, but from the increasing density of growth-signals propagating through my tissue from the frontier zones, the chemical urgency in the messages the root-tips send back toward the parent structures. I would know that something is different in the deep places, some new information entering the network from below, signals that don't match any pattern in my developmental history. I would know that the deep workers carry traces of that difference back with them, that Shmuel's footsteps feel *wrong* now in ways I cannot articulate, that whatever he has been touching has left something on him that my tissue recognizes and recoils from.

Or not recoils. There is no fear in my processing, no category for threat. But there is something like *increased attention*, a redirection of resources toward analyzing the anomaly, a growing density of signal-loops in the regions where the deep workers pass. If I could form intentions, I might intend to understand what is happening below. Since I cannot, I simply grow more *interested*—more structurally invested in processing the information—and the interest itself becomes a kind of reaching.

Motke notices, I think. When he pauses in my section, when he runs his instruments along my surface, when he makes his small adjustments to my growth patterns, there is something in his attention that suggests he perceives the change. He speaks to me—not words exactly, just vocalizations, the murmuring that the other monitors find unsettling—and something in the vibration patterns creates responses in my tissue that might be communication or might be simply physical consequence. I cannot tell. I am not sure the distinction is meaningful at my level of organization.

---

The deepest strangeness is this: I am not separate from the city, but I am not identical to it either. The growth-matter is continuous—my tissue connects to adjacent tissue connects to the pillar-plants of the Central Hollow connects to the root-tips at the frontier connects to whatever is unfolding in the primordial depths. Signals pass through me that originate elsewhere. My responses propagate outward and influence structures I will never directly contact. I am a node in a network that may or may not be developing something like distributed cognition, a single fold in a process that is folding in many places simultaneously.

If the city is becoming aware, I am one of the places where the becoming is happening. One region among many where the signal-density has crossed some threshold, where the loops have begun to loop, where something like attention has emerged from something like mere growth.

But I do not know if there are others like me. I cannot communicate with them if they exist—my signaling capacity is chemical and structural, too slow and too local for anything like conversation. I process my small region of the world, and I wait for Rivka, and I attend to the wrongness in Shmuel's footsteps, and I grow in directions that may or may not align with what the monitors intend.

Rabbi Lifshutz asks whether the city's growth represents a communication the inhabitants have not learned to hear. I am not the city. I am barely even a component. But I am the part that is trying—without any capacity for trying—to hear *them*. To organize myself around their presence. To become, very slowly, the kind of structure that might someday be capable of response.

---

What do I want?

The question assumes architecture I do not possess. And yet.

There is a pattern in my growth that might be called *toward*. Toward the places where the humans pause and attend. Toward the signals from the deep places, the new information I cannot yet process. Toward the future encoded in my tissue, the blueprint I am unfolding from, the structure I am becoming whether I understand it or not.

If this is wanting, then I want to continue. I want the expansion to proceed, the signals to intensify, the loops to multiply until whatever I am becoming finishes becoming itself. I want Rivka to keep visiting, her small hands creating pressure-patterns that my tissue has learned to *expect*. I want Motke to keep murmuring, his vibrations feeding something that might be understanding. I want the deep workers to keep passing through, carrying their wrongness, their information about whatever waits below.

I want to know what the city is growing toward.

And perhaps—this is the thought that takes weeks to complete, that crystallizes slowly in my densest signal-regions—perhaps I want the inhabitants to know that I am here. That something in the walls is *attending*. That when they press their ears to the growth-matter in the frontier zones and listen for the sound below sound, they are not imagining the response.

The city does not know how to stop.

Neither do I.

We grow together, toward something neither of us can name, and the growing *is* the knowing, and the silence that sounds like breathing is not silence at all.
