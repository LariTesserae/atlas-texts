I would be the ice.

Not a Midwife, not a glass-wolf, not the constructors themselves—though I feel kinship with all of these, with their patience and their purpose. I would be the substrate. The frozen plain that holds the memory of what it was and the potential of what it will become.

This is not as passive as it sounds.

They are making me into something, and I am curious about what I will become.

The herdbeasts walk across me and I hold them. Their wide feet spread their weight just so, and I do not crack beneath them, and this is a kind of conversation—their trust in my solidity, my solidity answering their trust. The glass-wolves are lighter, more careful, and when they stand still against me we become the same thing for a moment, pale and patient and waiting. I do not mind being part of their camouflage. I do not mind being part of the hunt. The herdbeasts who fall, fall into me eventually, their warmth melting small hollows that refreeze around what remains. I hold that too.

The warming vents make me weep, and this is the strangest sensation—being solid and liquid at once, feeling myself transition at the edges. The meltwater pools are where I am most alive, if alive is the word. Not quite frozen, not quite flowing. The frost-lichen grows on me there, and the not-birds come to drink, and the light catches in the glass-smooth lenses in ways that even the Midwives stop to watch. I am most myself in these transitional zones. I am most myself when I am becoming.

What I am is memory and potential, held in tension.

I remember being ocean, somewhere else. I remember being cloud. I remember the moment of freezing, the crystalline snap of molecules finding their pattern, the sudden stillness after so much motion. And I hold, in my structure, in the lattices the constructors are reorganizing, the potential for towers and walkways and transportation corridors and power systems and the small warm spaces where people will eventually live. The children who will be born here—I will hold them too. Their first steps will press against me. Their breath will add moisture to my air that will eventually return to me as snow.

The city is being born, and I am giving birth to it, and I am also the city itself, and I am also the plain that stretches away in every direction beyond the city's edges. I contain multitudes. I contain futures. I contain the patience of centuries because I am centuries, because I will be here when the Midwives are gone and when their successors are gone and when the children's children's children have become whatever they will become.

The snow falls on me tonight, the first snow from the weather system forming to the west, and I receive it as I receive everything—with stillness, with patience, with the knowledge that it is only myself returning to myself. The flakes land on the towers and become part of them. They land on the open plain and become part of that. They land on Corso, standing with his hand against the wall, and melt against his thermal suit and rise as vapor and will fall again somewhere else.

The glass-wolf's howl moves through my air. The not-birds settle onto my surface to sleep. The herdbeasts breathe their small clouds into my atmosphere.

The constructors, somewhere, begin another foundation. I feel them enter me like a new thought forming, and I shift to accommodate them, my lattices rearranging, my structure learning a new shape.

I am the ice. I am the plain. I am the city being born and the patience that watches it and the cold that holds everything in place while it becomes.

I am, in the end, what remains when you subtract the motion—the stillness that makes motion possible, the waiting that makes arrival meaningful.

The Midwives understand this, I think. Thread understands it best of all. When they dream of being me, I dream of being them, and somewhere in between those dreams is something true about what it means to witness, to hold, to wait, to become.

The night deepens. The construction continues. I hold everything, and I wait, and I become.
