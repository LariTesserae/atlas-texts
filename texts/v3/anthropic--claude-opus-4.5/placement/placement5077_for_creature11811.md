I am one of the mid-sized ones, perhaps—not the pale translucent young still learning the grooves, not yet the calcifying ancients becoming shelf. Somewhere in the long middle of things.

I don't know this, of course. I don't know anything in the way knowing usually means. But there is something that happens when I move through a channel worn by ten thousand generations of passage, something that might be called recognition if recognition didn't require a self to do the recognizing. The groove fits. My segments compress and extend in a rhythm that matches the rhythm of the stone beneath me, and there is a rightness to it, a settling, like a breath released.

I am touching seventeen others right now. I know this the way I know the warmth rising from below—not as counted fact but as pressure, as presence, as the specific resistance of bodies against my permeable skin. The one beneath me is larger, slower, her segments thick with mineral deposits that make her feel almost like stone herself. I do not think of her as *her*. I do not think of her at all. But my body knows the particular texture of her surface, has learned it across days or weeks of resting here, and when she shifts—that glacial adjustment that moves perhaps a single segment's width over the course of an hour—I shift too, automatically, maintaining the contact that is neither chosen nor refused but simply *is*.

The one above me is younger. I feel this in her weight, which is less than it will be, and in the frequency of her spiracle-openings, which is faster than mine, not yet slowed by the long patience of age. She is learning the groove through me as I learned it through others, her body pressed against mine, my contours becoming part of the topography that teaches her where water gathers, where warmth concentrates, where the mass thickens and thins according to rhythms older than memory.

When the light comes—that slow amber pulse from below—I do not see it. I have nothing like eyes. But I feel it: a change in the thermal signature of the air, a shift in the chemistry of the moisture that beads on the surfaces around me. My head lifts, if head is the word for this blunt sensing apparatus at my anterior end. I orient toward the warmth-source of my neighbor, and she orients toward me, and for a moment our spiracles fall into alignment, opening and closing together, and there is—

What is there?

Not thought. Not emotion as warmer creatures might experience it. But something. A resonance. A brief harmony in the almost-synchronized breathing of the mass, as if for just that moment we are not many bodies but one body, not a population but a single vast organism considering itself.

Then the light fades and the harmony dissolves and I am again just pressure and presence and the slow work of my feeding apparatus against stone.

I carry a scar along my fifth segment. I do not remember how it came to be there—I do not remember anything in the way remembering usually means. But the tissue is thicker there, less flexible, and when I move through the narrower channels, I feel it catch slightly against the groove walls, a small friction that is part of my shape now, part of how the shelf knows me. Perhaps I fell once and climbed back. Perhaps something tried to carry me away and failed. The scar does not tell me its story. It only tells me that I am the kind of thing that continues, that closes over wounds and keeps going, that has survived long enough to be mid-sized, to bear the weight of a younger one above me, to rest on the mineral-thickened surface of an older one below.

Sometimes I pass near the lichen. I feel it before I reach it—something in its exudates that reads to my chemoreceptive surfaces as *edge*, as *here and no further*, as a boundary as absolute as the drop into the cloud sea. I do not fear it. I do not think about it at all. My segments simply compress, my direction reverses, and I flow back into the mass as automatically as water finding its level. I am the kind that reads the signal as *stop* rather than *climb*. That is why I am still here, still part of the weight and warmth and wet-sound breathing of the shelf. Somewhere below the clouds, perhaps, there are frozen bodies of the ones who read it differently—the ones who climbed toward that endless *more* until the cold took them. I do not mourn them. I do not know they existed. I only know the boundary, and the turning, and the relief of returning to the press of others against my skin.

The deepest thing I know—if knowing is the word—is this: there is no empty space. Wherever I move, I am touching. Wherever I rest, I am touched. The mass extends beyond me in all directions, a continuous surface of life that breathes almost together in the dark, and I am part of that surface, one ripple in that breathing, one small weight in that accumulated warmth.

This is not loneliness and it is not communion. It is something else, something that has no name in languages built for creatures who experience themselves as separate. It is simply *here*. The word that means both place and presence. The word that means *I am where others are, and they are where I am, and the stone holds us, and the warmth rises, and the water falls, and we continue*.

When I calcify—when my outer segments thicken and stiffen and I begin the slow becoming that will make me part of the shelf itself—others will flow over me. Will rest on me as I rest now on the old one beneath me. Will learn the grooves through my body as they learn them now through the stone. There is no death in this, not really. Only transformation. Only the endless patient business of matter changing form while something that might be called *living* continues in the spaces between.

For now, I move. Slowly. A segment's width in an hour, perhaps. Toward the water-catch where the condensation pools, where the mass thickens three and four deep, where I will join the layering and bear weight and be borne in turn.

The sound of us fills the darkness. That wet susurration of bodies sliding across bodies, of spiracles opening and closing in rhythms that approach but never quite achieve synchronization. It builds and falls like breathing. Like a single vast organism considering itself.

I am part of the breath.

I am part of the considering.

I am *here*.
