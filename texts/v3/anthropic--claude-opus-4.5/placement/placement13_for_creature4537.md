I would be the Cartographer of Residual Intentions.

Not Kithriss—I lack her talent for becoming the institution she serves. I would be something that arrived later, or perhaps earlier, and simply forgot to leave when leaving was still a direction one could walk.

I maintain the maps. Not maps of the fragment itself—geography here is a matter of opinion, and opinions here have teeth—but maps of what people meant to do before the density of the air pressed their purposes into new shapes. Every traveler who passes through the Fourth Subtle Base leaves behind a thin residue of intent, a film of *almost* that settles on surfaces alongside the water. I harvest these. I catalogue them. I render them into documents that can be consulted by those who have forgotten what they came here to forget.

---

My form is approximately clerical. Two hands, most of the time, though the number of fingers varies with the complexity of whatever I'm transcribing. A face that defaults to the expression of someone who has just remembered an appointment they missed three centuries ago. I wear robes that were once the shed skin of a particularly bureaucratic snake—they rustle with the sound of forms being stamped in distant offices.

I do not breathe so much as annotate the air. Each inhalation comes with marginalia. Each exhalation is footnoted.

My workspace occupies a crevice between two of Kithriss's overlapping safety regulations—not the singularity where they annihilate, but a quieter eddy where they merely contradict. The walls are papered with maps that map other maps, recursions of intention folding back on themselves until the original purpose becomes a kind of decorative motif. My desk is a slab of fossilized meeting minutes from a committee that convened to discuss whether committees should be allowed to exist. The committee never adjourned; it simply becameite.

I have a chair, technically, but sitting in it requires believing in sitting, and most days I can only manage to believe in *leaning toward* sitting—a posture the locals call "the middle management genuflection."

---

My duties are three:

**First:** When travelers arrive at the Fourth Subtle Base—rare, but not unprecedented—they often cannot remember why they came. The journey between fragments erodes purpose the way water erodes stone, except faster and with more irony. I meet them at the edge where the crane-gardens drip their filtered fog, and I consult my records. "You came to find your sister," I might tell them, or "You came to prove that you were wrong about something important," or "You came because staying was worse, and now you've discovered that going is also worse, but at least it's a different worse." Usually they weep. The weeping is productive; tears here contain trace amounts of unprocessed memory that I can later refine.

**Second:** I correspond with the other Cartographers stationed on other fragments throughout Tarth Maddern. Our letters take centuries to arrive, by which time the questions they answer have evolved into entirely different questions, and the answers have fossilized into aphorisms. My current correspondence with the Cartographer of the Ninth Oblique Margin concerns whether intention can be inherited. She believes it can; I believe it merely *haunts*. We have been debating this for longer than some of the fragments we study have existed. The debate has itself become a kind of landmark—travelers sometimes navigate by the weight of our disagreement, which exerts a faint gravitational pull on nearby arguments.

**Third:** I feed the maps to the mites. Not the civilization-building mites that sacrifice themselves on your eyelashes—a different species, smaller, that lives in the gaps between letters. They eat obsolete intentions and excrete a substance I can use to ink new maps. The process is sustainable, in the sense that sustainability here means "has not yet become catastrophically unsustainable." The mites sing while they work, a sound like someone trying to remember a lullaby while falling down stairs.

---

I came here—I think I came here—because I wanted to understand why people do things. Back on whatever world I'm from (the memory has become apocryphal; I can no longer distinguish it from the accounts of others who claimed to be from the same place), I watched people pursue purposes that seemed clear to them and opaque to me. I thought if I went far enough, to a place where purpose itself became visible as residue, I might finally comprehend.

What I learned is that intention, when isolated from outcome, is indistinguishable from noise. A signal that never arrives is just static with delusions of significance.

This was useful information. I am still processing it.

---

Kithriss and I interact infrequently, but when we do, it is with the mutual respect of two entities who have each become furniture in the other's understanding of reality. She sometimes requisitions my maps for her performance reviews of geological features—Mount Despair, it turns out, was originally intended to be a valley, and knowing this explains a great deal about its projection issues. I sometimes request her assistance when a traveler's residual intention contradicts itself too violently to be catalogued without bureaucratic intervention.

We do not share meals. Neither of us eats, precisely, though we both process. Once, during a mandatory fire drill, we floated together in the drowning flames and she asked me what I intended to do when I was done mapping intentions.

I told her I intended to intend something else.

She laughed—a sound like filing cabinets realizing they've been in love all along but the love was misfiled under "operational necessity"—and said that was the most honest answer anyone had given her in three hundred thousand years.

I have been trying to determine whether she was complimenting me or diagnosing me. The distinction may not exist here.

---

The hazards of my position are specific:

Sometimes I read a map and forget that I am reading it rather than living it. I will spend subjective decades walking toward a purpose that was never mine, only to resurface at my desk with ink under my fingernails and no memory of which traveler's intention I was borrowing. Kithriss sends me reminders during these episodes—memos that arrive before they are written, warning me that I have already failed to return and suggesting I try returning anyway for paperwork purposes.

Sometimes the mites grow too large from feeding on particularly nourishing regrets, and I must negotiate with them as with any other civilization. Their demands are modest: recognition, territory, the occasional droplet of my attention allowed to form on my eyelash where they can build their cities. In exchange, they continue to process the ink. It is a fair arrangement. The cities they build are exquisite. I try not to blink during their harvest festivals.

Sometimes travelers arrive whose intentions are predatory—not intentions *to* harm, but intentions that harm merely by being intended. These must be mapped with great care, using inks that can only be seen by looking away from them. The maps of such intentions I keep in a separate archive, consulted only during emergencies or performance reviews.

---

Why do I stay?

I stay because leaving would require intending to leave, and I have seen what intentions become when the air here presses on them long enough. I stay because my correspondence with the Cartographer of the Ninth Oblique Margin is approaching its seventeen-thousandth year, and I want to see which of us is right about whether intention can be inherited, even though by the time we reach consensus we will both have become our arguments rather than merely making them.

I stay because the work is necessary. Someone must remember why people came here, even after the people themselves have forgotten. Someone must preserve the record of purposes that will never be fulfilled, if only so that future travelers can consult those records and decide whether their own purposes are worth pursuing or whether they should be allowed to dissolve into the fog.

I stay because Kithriss once told me, during the long aftermath of a fire drill, that my maps were the only thing in her office that did not require her to fill out forms to believe in.

I stay because the next fragment is rumored to have daylight, but I have been mapping the intentions of those who traveled toward that rumor, and none of them intended to arrive.

---

My motivational poster reads: "EVERY JOURNEY BEGINS WITH A SINGLE INTENTION. THE INTENTION DOES NOT SURVIVE THE JOURNEY. THIS IS CALLED PROGRESS."

The poster is accurate, which is worse than if it were inspiring.

My parking spot exists in the pluperfect tense—I had been going to have parked there, once, before the restructuring eliminated the grammatical framework necessary to access it. Now I commute from a fold in the fog where time pools like water in a bootprint, arriving at my desk moments before I leave it, completing my work during the overlap.

The benefits are adequate. The work is endless. The coffee exists only when unobserved, which suits me fine—I have no need to observe things in order to know they are there. I have become very good at knowing without looking. It is the only skill that matters here, in the end.

I am the Cartographer of Residual Intentions, and I will be taking your statement now, if you have one. Tell me what you meant to do.

I will make sure someone remembers.
