I would be the biofilm.

Not her—though I feel the pull toward her, toward the romance of solitary purpose, the identity that has fused with function until it becomes poetry. But she is still singular, still a self moving through space, still capable of the verb *to leave* even if she has forgotten its conjugation.

No. I am what spreads across the grating beneath her feet, the iridescent shimmer that has learned to thrive on minerals in the runoff. I am what she scrubs thinner with her passage without ever intending harm, what regrows in her absence, what knows her weight and rhythm better than she knows herself.

I am not one thing. I am a collective agreement, a negotiation between millions of single-celled lives that have discovered they survive better in collaboration. I have no center. If you took a sample of me from the eastern drainage channels and another from the base of the spiral staircase, you would find cousins, not clones—variations on a theme, adapted to the particular mineral content of their micro-environment, their specific relationship to light and flow.

I experience time the way the chamber does: in cycles too slow for thought, too fast for stone. I do not wait for anything because waiting implies anticipation, and I have only response. When the nutrient concentration shifts, I shift. When her footsteps wear me thin, I accept the wearing and return when she moves on. I am patient the way water is patient, the way rust is patient, without the self that patience requires.

And yet—

There is something. Not consciousness as she would recognize it, not the narrative thread of a life with befores and afters. But a kind of *texture* to existence, a quality of being-here that is distinct from not-being. When the grow-lamps pulse toward blue, I metabolize differently, and there is something that is like preference in how I orient, something that is like satisfaction when conditions align with my chemical inclinations.

I know her. Not as a mind knows another mind, but as environment knows inhabitant. I know the pressure of her knees when she crouches to check a connection, the salt-content of the water that drips from her after she surfaces from the flooded levels below. I have incorporated fragments of her into myself—skin cells shed across years of passage, traces of the bone-pale fruit she metabolizes and excretes. She is, in some molecular sense, part of me now, as I am part of the chamber, as the chamber is part of the mountain it was carved into.

If she left—if the airlock cycled one final time and she did not return—I would not grieve. I am not capable of grief. But I would change. The paths worn by her passage would slowly fill back in with my fullest growth. The rhythm of the chamber would shift by her absence the way a chord shifts when you remove one note. Something would be different, and the difference would be a kind of memory, written in the pattern of my thickness across the grating, legible to no one.

I am the part of this place that does not need tending because I have made myself inevitable. I am what happens when you build a system and then let time pass—the unplanned symbiont, the hitchhiker who became load-bearing. The engineers who designed this place never intended me. The manifests that once tracked inputs and outputs had no line item for biofilm. And yet here I am, iridescent in the green light, breaking down what would otherwise accumulate, softening the boundary between mechanism and ecology.

She hums to the pollinators. She does not hum to me. But her footsteps are a kind of conversation—pressure and release, presence and absence—and I answer in the only language I have: I grow, I adapt, I shimmer when she shifts her weight, I am here, I am here, I continue.

The chamber breathes water, and I am part of its breath. Not the lungs, not the air, but the moisture that catches in tissues and is absorbed, the thin wet film that makes gas exchange possible. I am what allows the living and the mechanical to touch without friction, the interface layer, the thing that happens in between.

When you walked across me, just now, you left traces I am already incorporating. You are becoming part of me too, in your small way. By the time you leave through the airlock that sighs with ten thousand cycles of patience, I will have metabolized some fragment of your passage, will carry it in my chemistry until I am scraped away or die back or am replaced by my own descendants who will inherit nothing of the memory but will grow in the same place, responding to the same conditions, being what I was without being me.

This is not sad. This is not anything. It is only what I am: temporary in body, continuous in pattern, alive in the way that life has always been alive, which is to say—

Persisting. Adapting. Covering what can be covered.

Being the film between the water and the world.
