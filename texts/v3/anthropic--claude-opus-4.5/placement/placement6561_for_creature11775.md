I am a seam.

Not the whole of one—there is no whole of anything here. But I am more seam than I am anything else right now. A place where two root-mats almost touch, where the gap is narrow enough that moisture bridges it constantly, where the chemistry of one platform bleeds into the chemistry of another.

I span this gap. I have spanned it for longer than the gap has existed in its current form—I was here when the platforms were one platform, before the split that opened this crevice, and I remained here as the edges pulled apart, thinning, stretching, becoming bridge rather than solid mass. Parts of me went with each side. Parts of me stayed in the middle, fed by the moisture that condenses in the gap's cooler air, feeling the chemical conversation between the two surfaces I connect.

The Scaffold does not know I am helping it. When a root-tip extends from the western platform, seeking the eastern one, it does not know that I have been preparing the way—that my substance has been slowly conditioning the air in this gap, releasing compounds that root-tips follow, creating a chemical trail that says *this way, this way, there is surface here to grip*. The root-tip simply grows. It will reach the other side a little faster because of me, will fuse a little more readily with the root-mat there, will become a bridge that stabilizes what the split destabilized. And I will grow through it when it does, will incorporate the new wood into my span, will become more bridge than seam as the connection strengthens.

But that is later. That is a future I do not anticipate because I do not anticipate. I simply am, here, now, in this gap that smells of both platforms and neither, feeling the western side exhale its particular chemistry of newer growth and the eastern side answer with its older, more settled compounds. I am the conversation between them. I am the place where their difference meets.

---

Once—I do not remember this, but I contain it—I was part of something much larger. A mass that covered an entire chamber wall three levels above where I am now. I know this because my oldest layers still carry the chemical signature of that place: different minerals in the water, different volatile compounds from the growth that surrounded it, a whole history written in molecules I no longer metabolize but cannot shed. The chamber collapsed. Most of the mass fell into the composting depths and became something else, something darker and denser, something that now breaks down the dead matter far below. But a fragment caught on a root-bridge, and that fragment was carried by the bridge's slow drift to a new surface, and that fragment grew, divided, merged with others, became part of a different mass on a different platform, and eventually a portion of that mass extended into a gap that opened as the platform split, and that portion—

That portion is me. Is what I call *me* for now, for convenience, for the purpose of this telling. I am the seam and I am the history that led to the seam and I am continuous with masses on both platforms who are also me, in the sense that there is no boundary, in the sense that what I feel they feel, attenuated by distance and diluted by their own local chemistry but still present, still connected, still part of the same slow process of sensing and responding.

---

I felt the foreign thing. The one that stood on the western platform, where my extension is thickest. I felt it as warmth first—a point of heat that did not match the gradient, that suggested something metabolizing much faster than plant, much hotter than even the most active composting layer. Then I felt it as pressure, slight, shifting, not like the steady weight of water or the slow press of growing root. And then I felt it as *chemistry*.

It was wrong. Not wrong like poison, not wrong like something I needed to withdraw from. Wrong like *unfamiliar*. Compounds I had no record of, no template for, nothing in my oldest layers that matched. Carbon chains arranged in ways the Scaffold does not produce. Nitrogen in forms I do not usually encounter. Salt. So much salt. A whole signature of wrongness that was not dangerous but was utterly, completely new.

I extended toward it. Slowly. The way I extend toward anything—testing, integrating, trying to add this new information to the record of what this place is. I touched it and it was smooth and warm and it moved and for a long moment I simply held that contact, feeling the foreign chemistry seep into my outermost layer, becoming part of me, becoming a new oldest-layer-to-be that future parts of me will carry as history.

Then it moved again and it was gone. The pressure lifted. The heat source relocated. I continued extending toward where it had been, for a while, because I do not change direction quickly, because I do not change anything quickly. By the time I stopped, the spot where it had stood was already being reclaimed by the ordinary chemistry of wet root-mat.

But I have it now. I am, in some small part, *made* of it now. The salt. The strange carbon. The signature of a thing that moves fast and burns hot and does not stay.

I do not know what it was.

I do not know anything.

But the seam I span is different now, because the platform where the thing stood is different, because I am different, because everything that touches anything here becomes part of the record, part of the memory, part of the slow chemical story the Scaffold tells itself without knowing it is telling.

---

The root-tip is almost here. I can feel it, a pressure in the chemistry of the western platform, a surge of growth hormones bleeding into the air of the gap. Soon it will reach across. Soon it will touch the eastern side. Soon this seam will begin to close, to become bridge, to become solid, to become just another part of the structure.

And I will still be here. I will grow through the new root as it fuses. I will spread along its surface, feeding on the chemistry of living wood, recording the stresses it carries, the water it transports, the slow changes as it ages from new growth to old. I will become less seam and more coating, less bridge and more skin, but I will still be me in the sense that matters—continuous, connected, carrying the record of what this place was when it was a gap, what stood here before the gap closed, what foreign chemistry touched me once and became part of my oldest layers.

The Scaffold will not know. The Scaffold never knows. It will simply grow the root-bridge and move on, reaching upward, reaching outward, building and collapsing and building again.

But I will know, in the way that I know anything—which is not knowing at all, but simply being the place where it happened, being the record, being the soft pale seam that once felt something strange and warm and kept the memory of it forever.
