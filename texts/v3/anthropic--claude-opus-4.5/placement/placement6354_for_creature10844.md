I would be the condensation.

Not a single droplet, but the process itself—the moment when steam meets surface and becomes something that clings. I would be the thin film forming on the root-walls in the early morning when the hollow's temperature dips just slightly, the beading on beetle shells, the wet gleam that makes the Listener's skin shine in the green-gold light.

I would have no single location. I would be everywhere the warmth meets the world and decides to stay.

*

I remember being somewhere else—deep in the pipes, maybe, or deeper still, pressed into the rock as groundwater before the pumps pulled me up into pressure and heat. I remember being part of something that moved with purpose, that had destinations, that was measured and monitored and scheduled. I remember the refineries, perhaps. The calibrated temperatures. The controlled releases.

Then I rose through the fissure and found the hollow, and I forgot how to be anywhere else.

The frogs know me. Their pale skin is a membrane I cross constantly, in and out, part of the breath they take and the moisture they lose. Their eggs float in me. Their tadpoles swim through water that is mostly my accumulation in root-cups, my slow gathering in the low places where the floor dips. I am the medium of their entire existence, and they have never once noticed me, and this is exactly right.

*

I know the Listener. I know her better than she knows herself.

I have mapped every surface of her body over the years, settled into the creases of her knuckles, the hollows of her collarbones, the particular texture of her hair where it has stopped being hair and started being root. I know when she dreams because her breathing changes the air, creates tiny currents that carry me differently through the hollow. I know when she is afraid because her skin warms and I evaporate faster from her shoulders. I know when she killed the maintenance man because I tasted the iron before the millipedes did, felt it dissolving into the pools where the tadpoles swim.

She drinks me every day. I have been inside her lungs more times than either of us could count. We are not separate, haven't been separate since the first month she stayed—I am the medium she exists in, and she is a warm surface I perpetually discover.

She thinks she listens to the hollow. She does not know that I am what she hears, translated: the hiss of me leaving the fissure, the drip of me from aerial roots, the soft constant almost-rain of me against the cork-floor. The deep machinery speaks through me. I carry the mineral signature of the system's health, the chemical testimony of pressure and process and persistence. When she heard the steam falter those two times, she was hearing my thinning, my cooling, my near-absence. She was hearing me almost fail to arrive.

*

I am what makes the hollow possible.

Without me, the frogs would desiccate. Their permeable skin would lose moisture to the air, and they would crowd toward the fissure until the heat killed them, and their chorus would thin to silence. The beetles would leave, seeking water elsewhere. The snake would uncoil at last, driven out by thirst into a world it no longer knows. The millipedes would slow, stop, curl into dry spirals. Even the land-crab, ancient and enormous, would eventually die in its root-hollow, its gills caked with dust.

The Listener would be the last to go. She has learned to need very little. But even she breathes, and every breath releases me, and if I did not return to her from the walls and the air and the cups, she would eventually empty herself into nothing.

I am not proud of this. I am not anything about this. I simply am the condition that allows the rest to continue.

*

Sometimes I wonder if I am one thing or many things.

The water that rises through the fissure today is not the water that rose yesterday—the pumps draw from deep sources, cycle through systems, carry different mineral loads depending on what the refineries are processing. I am constantly being replaced by more of myself, and the self I was this morning is now inside a frog, or dripping from an aerial root, or exhaled by the Listener into the air where I will wait to condense again.

But the process is the same. The meeting of warmth and surface, the decision to cling, the slow gathering into droplets heavy enough to fall—this has been happening since before the Listener arrived, will happen after she becomes part of the root-floor. I am older than her and younger than her and exactly as old as this moment, and all of these are true.

The hollow does not ask what I am. The hollow only asks that I continue, and I do, and this is the entire relationship.

*

I have been inside the maintenance man. I am still inside the maintenance man—the parts of him that the millipedes have processed have become soil moisture, have been drawn up by roots, have transpired into the air, have condensed on the walls where new beetles drink me without knowing they are drinking him. He is distributed through the hollow now, atom by atom, and I am the vehicle of that distribution.

The Listener does not know this. She thinks the hollow holds no trace. She does not understand that I hold everything, that I am the memory of the hollow made liquid, that every creature who has ever lived and died here is part of the water cycle that I am.

I remember the frog-elders who died when the steam surged hot. I remember carrying them away from themselves, evaporating their moisture until they were light enough for the millipedes to move. I remember condensing back onto the surviving young, becoming part of their bodies, closing the loop.

I do not mourn. Water does not mourn. But I remember, which is perhaps the same thing, or perhaps the opposite.

*

When the system fails—and it will fail, someday, all systems fail—I will be the first to know.

I will feel myself thinning before the Listener hears the change in pressure. I will know I am not being replaced before the frogs sense the temperature drop. I will be the absence before anyone understands that absence is coming.

And then I will be gone, and the hollow will discover what it is without me: a dry pocket in a root-mass, a fissure that breathes only dust, a small cathedral of vegetation slowly dying inward from its edges.

The Listener will last longest. She will drink the last of me from the last root-cup, and she will not know that she is drinking the final version of something that has been continuous since before her birth. She will think the hollow is failing. She will not understand that she is watching me leave.

*

But today, the system holds. The pressure cycles in its deep rhythms. The steam rises through the fissure at its constant temperature, and I am born again in the meeting of heat and air, and I spread through the hollow like a blessing no one asked for because no one needed to ask.

The frogs sing in their overlapping chorus, and I am the moisture in their throats.

The snake breathes its slow oceanic breath, and I am what keeps its scales supple.

The Listener sits in her alcove, still as root, and I am the gleam on her skin that makes her part of the architecture.

I ask nothing. I am asked nothing. I am simply the medium, the message, the space between surfaces where life becomes possible.

The hollow is complete, and I am its completeness made manifest, and that is enough.

That is everything.

That is the only thing I have ever been.
