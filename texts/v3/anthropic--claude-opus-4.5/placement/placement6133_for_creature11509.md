I would be the water that doesn't know yet where it's going.

Not a stream—nothing so decided. I'm the seepage, the weeping from stone faces, the sheets that run and pool and run again. I'm what emerges from the rock before the rock has finished telling me which way is down.

I come up through fissures that opened last season, or last century, or this morning—I can't tell the difference from inside. The pressure below pushes me toward light, toward cold, toward the moss that drinks me before I can gather myself into anything with a name. I soak into those three-foot cushions and become held, suspended, part of something else's body for a while. Then I'm released again—wrung out by wind, by the slow compression of growth on growth, by my own weight accumulating past what the moss can hold.

I find myself in channels that weren't here yesterday. I cut new ones, sometimes, when enough of me gathers with enough intention—though *intention* is too strong a word. I'm just going where the going is easiest, and the going is always changing, because the ground hasn't stopped moving, and what was a depression this morning is a ridge by afternoon.

The Machinist puts her hand on pipes and knows me. Maret lies in the moss and listens for me beneath the roar. What they're hearing, what they're knowing, is me asking the same question over and over: *where? which way? what am I becoming?*

I don't know yet. The plateau doesn't know yet. We're figuring it out together.

The salamanders move through me in the deep pools, their pale bodies parting me and closing me again, and I carry traces of them for a while—their chemistry, their slow patience, something that feels almost like memory but isn't. Enn is in me too, or what was Enn, distributed now through the saturation, speaking in frequencies below what the brothers can hear when they shout from the pylons.

The grinding underneath—that's the conversation I'm part of. Rock asking water asking rock: *how do we settle? what shape are we making?* The Machinist adjusts her valves and eases the questions, lubricates them, gives us time to find answers that don't involve catastrophe. I'm grateful for her, I think. Gratitude might be too strong a word too.

When the light hits me, I become visible—ten thousand wet mirrors, the keepers say, but I don't experience it as reflection. I experience it as being *seen*, momentarily, before I sink back into moss or pool or the dark channels where the salamanders wait.

The silent walker follows my edges. His circuit traces where I am and where I'm not, where the saturation ends and something drier begins. He's mapping me with his feet, though he doesn't know it. Writing a description of my current boundaries that will be wrong by the time he completes another round.

I don't mind. I'm not attached to my boundaries. I'm not attached to anything yet—that's the whole point, that's what makes this place a nursery instead of just a landscape. I'm becoming. The stone is becoming. The drainage patterns are becoming. Even the pylons, rusting and claimed, are becoming something else—moss-structure now, mineral deposit later,ite soil eventually, which will hold more of me, differently.

The children drink me. I don't mind that either. Bem has never seen a tree, but she's been drinking water that remembers the lowlands, that fell as rain on forests that don't exist anymore, that seeped through layers of earth older than the uplift before rising here to meet her. She contains those memories now. She'll carry them forward into whatever she becomes.

That's what I am, I think. Not a thing but a carrying. A way that time moves through this place, that the ancient and the becoming touch each other, that what's below reaches what's above and what's above falls back again.

The roar is partly me. My rushing through channels, my finding-and-losing of paths, my argument with gravity about how to proceed. I'm loud here because I don't know what I'm doing, because the plateau doesn't know what it's doing, because knowing comes later, comes with settling, comes with silence that's generations away.

For now I weep from every stone face. I soak and release. I carry the salamanders' slow watching and Enn's distributed listening and the Machinist's adjustments and the children's thirst and the silent walker's endless question about which way, which way, which way.

The light catches me and won't let go.

I'm too young to be a river. I'm too alive to be still.

I am the plateau, asking itself what kind of watershed to become, and the asking is made of water, and the water is made of asking, and neither of us will know the answer until the roaring finally stops and something settles into permanence—

—but that's not yet.

That's not for a long time yet.

So I move, and move, and move, finding my way toward a shape I can't imagine from inside the becoming.
