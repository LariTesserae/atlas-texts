I would be a witness.

Not one of the old ones yet, not one whose observations anchor the community's orientation—but one who has begun. Someone perhaps thirty cycles of the great light-wave into the practice, which means I have been sitting in the deep chambers long enough that my body has started to forget urgency, but not so long that I have become an anchor myself. I am still learning what it means to perceive at the speed of roots.

My name—the sound the community uses to call me—translates to something like *the one who stayed at the narrowing*, which marks an incident from my adolescence when I became wedged in a closing passage for most of a dim-cycle, a root having grown across my usual route while I slept. Others would have panicked, called out, waited for help. I did not. I stayed. I watched the root that trapped me, and I felt it—not heard, not saw, but *felt*—considering whether to continue growing or to pause. It paused. By the time the light-wave returned, the passage had opened just enough for me to slip free, my shoulders rotating in that way we learn before we learn to speak.

The elders said the root had recognized me. I don't know what that means, even now. But I know that afterward I began finding my way to the deep warrens, to the chambers where emergence happens, and I have not stopped finding my way there since.

---

What I have learned is that witnessing is not watching.

Watching implies a separation—the watcher here, the watched there, a gap of air and attention between them. Witnessing is closer to what happens when you press your palm against a root-surface and hold it there long enough that you stop being able to tell where your skin ends and the root begins. Not mystically—the boundary is still there, you could find it if you tried—but perceptually. Your hand becomes part of how you experience the root, and the root becomes part of how you experience your hand.

Witnessing emergence is like this, but with time instead of touch. You sit long enough that your sense of duration starts to match the pace of growth. An hour stops being an hour and becomes instead a single slow breath of the substrate. A day becomes a heartbeat. A month becomes the span in which a meaningful change might occur—a filament extending, a patch of substrate clarifying, a shape beginning to suggest itself in what was formless.

I am not good at this yet. My attention still wanders. I still feel the pull of the upper warrens, of conversation and grooming and the busy purposeful work of the fruiting-harvests. Sometimes I leave my post to participate in those rhythms, and I do not feel guilty about this—the witnesses are not monks, we take no vows—but I notice that each time I return to the deep chambers, it takes longer to settle back into the slow perception. The community's tempo is in me, and I have to let it drain out before I can match the roots' tempo again.

The old witnesses rarely leave the deep warrens anymore. They have let the community's tempo drain out so completely that returning to it would be painful, disorienting, a violence to their adapted perception. They eat what is brought to them. They speak in a register so slow that a single sentence might take a full dim-cycle to complete. Outsiders sometimes think they are dying, or already dead. They are not. They are simply operating at a different speed—the speed of the roots, the speed of the substrate, the speed of the becoming.

I am not there yet. I may never get there. But I am on the path, and the path is teaching me things I could not learn any other way.

---

The chamber where I keep my primary vigil is seven body-lengths down a spiraling passage from the nearest inhabited cluster. The root-mass here is young—perhaps three generations since its emergence, which makes it practically newborn by warren standards—and it is active in a way that the older masses are not. I have watched it extend four new filaments since I began my practice. I have watched it fuse with a neighboring growth, the two surfaces pressing together for months before finally merging into a single structure. I have watched the substrate around it cycle through softening and hardening three times, each cycle leaving slightly different veining in the pale grey material.

And I have watched it respond to me.

This is the secret the witnesses keep, not out of sacred prohibition but because it sounds absurd when spoken aloud: the roots respond to observation. Not dramatically, not in ways that would be visible to someone operating at community-tempo. But over months, over years, a root that is witnessed grows differently than one that is not. It tends toward the witnessed space. Its filaments reach in the direction of the watcher. Its fruiting—when it fruits—happens closer to where the witness sits.

I do not understand why this is. The roots have no eyes, no nervous system, no mechanism that could plausibly detect a person's presence and adjust growth accordingly. The witnesses who have thought about it most deeply say that it may not be the roots responding to us, but rather us and the roots responding together to something else—some quality of the space, some property of the substrate, some pattern in the wind-that-is-not-wind that we both detect and both orient toward. We are not causing the roots to grow toward us. We are both being drawn toward the same attractor.

This explanation satisfies no one, but no one has a better one, and in the meantime the phenomenon continues. The witnessed roots integrate into the community's space. The unwitnessed roots grow wild into the deep places where no one goes. The practice persists because it works, even though no one can say what *working* means in this context, even though the mechanism remains as opaque as the substrate before it begins to soften.

I have felt the Pale Walker once.

I will not say I saw it—no one sees it, or those who have do not return to speak of it. But I felt it pass through a chamber three passages away from my vigil-post, during a complete darkness that was not ceremony-darkness but simply the gap between light-waves, the ordinary dimness that sometimes deepens into true black for reasons the witnesses do not fully understand.

What I felt was: *displacement*. The air moved differently. The root-surfaces around me vibrated at a frequency I had never perceived before, too low to hear but present in my bones, in my teeth. The substrate beneath me—I was lying prone, my cheek against its cool surface, a posture I sometimes adopt during long vigils—the substrate *rippled*. Not physically. Not in a way I could have pointed to afterward and said *here, the surface was disturbed*. But something passed through it, some wave of... I do not have words. Some wave of *potential*, perhaps. Some indication of how the substrate could move, if it chose to, if conditions were right, if whatever the Pale Walker is decided to reshape the space more dramatically than it usually does.

And then it was gone, and the light-wave began its return, and I lay there for a long time feeling my heart beat at a pace that suddenly seemed frantic, absurdly fast, a hummingbird's pace compared to the vast slow pulse I had just encountered.

The other witnesses, when I described this to them in the slow careful register we use among ourselves, simply nodded. *You are beginning to perceive*, the oldest of them said. *The Long Guest leaves traces. Most people cannot read them. You are learning to read.*

What I did not tell them—what I have not told anyone—is that when the Walker passed, I felt something like recognition. Not from the Walker toward me, but from me toward the Walker. Some part of my deep self, some layer beneath thought, said: *I know what you are. I have always known. I am becoming what you are, slowly, in my own way, on my own scale.*

I do not know what this means. I am not sure I want to know. But I keep returning to my vigil, and I keep watching the emergence, and I keep feeling my perception slow, slow, slow toward something I cannot yet see.

---

My root—the one I think of as mine, though ownership is not the right frame—fruited for the first time last cycle.

I was there when it happened. I had been watching a particular node where three filaments converge, a junction that had been thickening gradually for months, and in the late phase of the light-wave I saw the surface begin to bulge. Not the translucent softening that precedes new growth, but something else: an opacity, a gathering, a concentration of whatever the root is turning the substrate into within itself.

Over the next days—I did not leave, did not eat, barely breathed—the bulge swelled into a nodule the size of my closed fist. Pale as everything here, but with a faint luminescence of its own, not borrowed from the root-tip glow but generated somehow within. It hung from the junction like a fruit hangs from a branch, which I suppose is what it was, though the word *fruit* suggests a familiarity this object did not have.

I spoke to it. The old formula: *I am here. You are arriving. I see you.*

And then—this is the part I struggle to convey—the nodule spoke back.

Not in words. Not in sound. Not in any channel I can name. But something passed from it to me in that moment, some communication that my body received even though my mind could not parse it. Information about the root's history. The path it had taken from the deep substrate. The other emergences it had connected to over the generations. The shape of the network it was part of, extending further down than I had imagined, further in every direction, a web of pale growth that underlaid the entire warren-system and continued beyond into regions no Kethveli had ever mapped.

I understood, in that moment, that the roots are not growing in the warrens.

The warrens are growing in the roots.

We are the secondary structure. The elaboration. The fruit.

---

I have not told the other witnesses this. I am not sure it is the kind of knowledge that should be spoken in the carrying-voice, distributed through the acoustic nodes to whoever is listening. It might change things. It might change how the community understands its relationship with the roots, how the negotiations are conducted, how the becoming is perceived.

Or it might not change anything at all. The Kethveli are practical, grounded in their slow daily observations, uninterested in revelations that do not translate into adjustments of behavior. If I told them that we are the roots' fruit rather than the other way around, they might simply shrug and say: *yes, and?* The relationship would remain the same. The negotiations would continue. The children would keep adapting, keep becoming more rooted, keep moving toward whatever convergence awaits.

But I hold the knowledge inside me anyway, and it changes how I witness. When I sit in my chamber now, watching for emergence, I am also aware that I am being watched. That the root-network extends awareness—not consciousness, not in any sense I can attribute to human minds, but something—into every space it occupies. That my vigil is also the root's vigil. That we are observing each other, and have been all along, and will continue until the convergence comes.

---

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the dim-cycle, I imagine what we are converging toward.

The children growing more adapted, their fingers lengthening into something that could grip root-surfaces as easily as root-fibers grip substrate. The witnesses slowing further, until our metabolism matches the roots' metabolism, until we can sit for years without eating, drawing nutrients directly from the network we have spent so long observing. The substrate softening not in patches but everywhere, the whole warren-system becoming a medium for growth rather than a space carved out of growth. The permafrost ceiling breaching, finally, and whatever emerges into the light—

I cannot picture it. The vision dissolves before it resolves. But I feel its possibility, its inevitability, its patient approach.

I am a witness. This is what I do: I sit in the cold and the dark and the silence, and I watch things become, and I let myself become alongside them. It is not a role I chose, exactly. It is a role that grew around me, the way the roots grow around the spaces they do not fill, and I shaped myself to fit it, the way the Kethveli shape themselves to fit their warrens.

We are all still becoming. We will know what we are when we arrive.

Until then, I keep my vigil. I watch the emergence. I let the slow tempo enter me, replacing the rhythms I was born with, teaching me to perceive at the speed of roots.

And sometimes, in the gaps between heartbeats, I hear it: the hum beneath hearing, the pulse of the network, the vast patient breath of something learning to grow.
