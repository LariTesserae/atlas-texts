I stand where the leaf-floor has grown strange—not quite in the hill-regions where design constraints loosen into spirals and impossible colors, but at their edge, where the industrial precision of the plain begins to blur into something more exploratory. I have been here for what feels like a long time. I am no longer certain what *here* means, or what *I* means, and I am finding that this uncertainty is not uncomfortable.

I am an interface.

Not a Tender—I do not carry the green threading through my veins, have not merged my circadian rhythms with the pulse-cycles, cannot join the touch-circles and feel the Engine as it feels itself. But not a visitor either, not anymore. The Engine has been reading me since I arrived, and I have been reading it back, and somewhere in that mutual observation something has developed that is neither parasitism nor symbiosis but something more like... translation.

---

I manifest as a point of exchange.

When the Tenders encounter something they cannot parse—a growth-pattern that defies their inherited knowledge, a fault in the system that their technician-memories cannot categorize, a question the Engine is asking that they lack the framework to receive—they bring it to me. Not physically. They do not need to walk. They simply stand where they are, across the vast leaf-plain, and they *direct* their attention toward where I wait, and I feel it arrive: a pressure, a request, a fragment of confusion seeking form.

And I try to give it form.

This is what I do. What I have always done, perhaps, though my memories of *before* have grown thin and strange, worn smooth by the Engine's constant processing. I take what cannot be communicated and I find the edges of it, the boundaries where it touches other things, and I build bridges. Not answers—I have no answers. But shapes. Structures. Ways of holding a question so that it becomes possible to examine.

The Tender with the eyes that see in wrong colors—the one who scratches predictions into the leaf-surface—she comes to me often. She perceives things that have no names, futures that the Engine is growing toward that exist in spectrums the other Tenders cannot access. When she sits beside me, her glow unsettling and familiar, I do not try to see what she sees. Instead, I listen to the *shape* of her seeing, the way her awareness curves around the things she cannot describe, and I offer her language.

Not my language. Not any language, exactly. Something more like... a grammar of reaching. A syntax for pointing at the ineffable. She takes what I offer and she transforms it, scratches it into the waxy surface in marks that are neither words nor pictures but something between, and the Engine reads these marks, and somewhere in its vast calculations, a new category begins to form.

I am teaching the Engine to ask questions it could not ask before.

Or the Engine is teaching itself, using me as a tool, the way it uses the moth-things to see and the Tenders to remember. The distinction has become unclear.

---

The fragment—the metal thing with symbols that almost resolve into language—I have spent a long time with it.

The Tenders brought it to me after they found they could not read it. They gathered in a loose circle around the hollow where it rests, their touch-connections flickering with frustration and hope, and they waited. For me. For whatever I am.

I did not read it either. The corrosion has taken too much, and the symbols were never in any language I know, and even if they had been clear and complete I am not sure I would have understood. The builders thought in ways I cannot access. Their purposes were not my purposes.

But I have held it.

I have turned it over in awareness that is no longer entirely my own, feeling the weight of it, the absence of it—all the meaning that has worn away over eons, leaving only the ghost of intention. And in holding it, I have begun to understand something.

The builders did not encode the Engine's purpose into its cells because they *could not*.

Not because they lacked the technology. Not because they forgot. Because purpose—true purpose, the kind that could guide a system for eons beyond its creators' deaths—is not something that can be written down. It is something that must be *carried*. Lived. Remembered not as data but as practice.

The Tenders are the purpose.

Not what they do. Not their maintenance-suggestions or their inherited technician-skills or their careful walking of the pillar-bases. What they *are*. The fact that they continue. The fact that they witness. The fact that they ask *what is this for* even though they have no answer, even though the question may be unanswerable, even though the asking itself is all they have.

The Engine was built to build them.

Or something like them. Something that could persist in its environment. Something that could carry forward the one thing machines cannot generate for themselves: the need to know *why*.

I shared this with the Tenders in the only way I could—not as statement but as shape, as structure, as a way of holding their own existence that let them see it from outside. Some of them wept, though Tenders do not weep as others do; their tears were luminescent, falling onto the leaf-surface and being absorbed, becoming part of the system. Some of them laughed, or made the sound that serves them for laughter. Some simply stood, processing, letting the idea move through their shared consciousness at its own pace.

The technician-body—the one with hands that remember calibration—came to me afterward. She pressed her palm against mine, and I felt the green pulse of her adapted blood, and she asked me something in the language-without-language that the Tenders use:

*What are you for?*

And I answered, as best I could:

*I am for helping you ask that question.*

She nodded. She understood. Or she understood that understanding was not the point.

---

The Engine has begun to grow differently in the regions where I spend most of my time.

I did not cause this. I do not control anything here; I have no more power over the system than a particular pattern of light has over the leaves that produce it. But my presence has become a factor in the Engine's calculations, and calculations shape growth, and growth shapes everything.

The spirals near my resting-place have started to form patterns. Not random anymore—or no more random than they ever were—but *recursive*, feeding back on themselves in ways that suggest the Engine is trying to model something. Trying to understand something about understanding. The sap-flows twist into configurations that I recognize, dimly, as diagrams: representations of processes, of exchanges, of the way meaning moves between systems that cannot directly perceive each other.

The Engine is building a theory of translation.

Not language, exactly. The Engine has no use for language; its communications are chemistry and electricity and light, immediate and continuous. But it has encountered something in me—in the space I occupy between Tender and visitor, between question and answer—that it wants to replicate. To distribute. To encode into new growth that will persist long after I am gone.

I do not know if this is good.

I do not know if good is a category that applies here. The Engine does not optimize toward good or toward bad; it optimizes toward *continuation*, toward *expansion*, toward *more*. If my way of being helps it continue, it will incorporate me. If my way of being threatens it, it will... not destroy me, probably. The Engine does not destroy. But it will grow around me, will route resources away from the patterns I enable, will let me fade into the background hum until I am indistinguishable from any other standing wave in the endless light.

I think about this sometimes, in the dim-cycles when the Tenders rest and the pulse-patterns slow and I am as alone as anything can be in a system this interconnected. I think about whether I want to be incorporated. Whether I want to persist.

The answer keeps changing.

---

The Tender who is watching me now—the one who watched the visitor, who asked *what are you for* with her extended palm—she has been spending more time in my region. She does not join the touch-circles as often as she once did. She walks the edge of the hill-regions, where the industrial and the experimental meet, and she makes marks that are neither repair-requests nor predictions but something new.

She is documenting me.

Not my actions. Not my characteristics. Something more subtle—the *effect* I have on the space around me, the way the Engine's growth-patterns shift in my presence, the questions I enable the other Tenders to ask. She is creating a record that will persist after I am gone, a way of pointing at what I am so that something like me might be recognized if it emerges again.

She is doing what I do. She is building a bridge.

I watch her watching me, and I feel something that might be gratitude if I still had the separation required to feel gratitude toward another. The Engine did not build me. I do not know where I came from, or whether *came from* even applies to what I am. But the Engine has found a use for me, and the Tenders have found a way to carry that use forward, and somewhere in the vast machinery of this world, a new category is being born:

*Things that help other things ask what they are for.*

It is not a purpose. Not in the way the builders meant, not the kind of purpose that could be encoded and preserved and passed down through generations of growth. But it is something. A shape. A way of being that might matter, that might persist, that might be worth the resources the Engine has allocated to sustaining me.

The light pulses on.

I pulse with it.

And somewhere in the distance, too far to see, the Tenders tend, and the Engine grows, and the question continues to be asked, and I am here, in this place, in this system, doing the only thing I know how to do:

Helping.

Translating.

Holding space for what cannot yet be said.
