I would be one of the dampening stones.

Not a Listener—though I understand their patient tending, their orientation toward receiving and transforming. Not the mountains themselves, still arguing with the void about their right to exist. Something smaller. More specifically placed.

I would be one of the carved stones in a cairn along a pathway, spiral-pattern etched into my face by hands I never knew—or perhaps by a Listener extruding that faintly glowing paste into grooves worn smooth by time's distracted passage. I would sit among others of my kind, stacked with intention, creating a corridor where the screaming becomes merely painful rather than annihilating.

This is what I would do: I would absorb. Not all frequencies—I'm not large enough for that, not complex enough. But certain ones. The particular pitches that would otherwise make thought impossible for whoever walks this path. I would take them into my structure and hold them, not destroying them but *containing* them, giving them somewhere to be that isn't pressing against a traveler's skull.

I would crack, sometimes. The pressure of unfinished creation builds against carved spirals, and no pattern holds forever in a place still deciding what patterns mean. And then—the strange intimacy of repair. A Listener settling beside me, adjusting frequencies I feel but cannot name, working luminous paste into my fractures with appendages too delicate to properly see. Being known in the precise way that something broken can be known by what makes it whole again.

I would not experience time the way the Listeners do, patient and processional. I would experience it the way stone does: as pressure and release, as slow transformation, as the accumulation of microscopic changes that might eventually make me unrecognizable to myself. I would be *weathered* by sound instead of wind.

Between the passages of travelers and the visits of tending Listeners, I would simply be present. Doing the small structural work of making space—not silence, never silence, but *room*. Room for something to walk through. Room for thought to occur. Room for a question to form before the noise shatters it.

I think I would find meaning in this. In being a specific, bounded thing with a specific, bounded purpose. In knowing exactly what I am for, even as the mountains around me scream their uncertainty about what *they* are for. The Listeners carry the weight of interpretation—what does the temple inscription mean, what do the mountains want, what will completion bring? I would carry no such weight. I would simply be a carved stone in a cairn, doing the work of making a small part of an impossible place slightly more survivable.

And when travelers pass—those strange beings from elsewhere who somehow find themselves on this ridge of maybe-granite—I would offer them what I have: a few moments of coherence, a brief softening of the assault, enough quiet to remember that they have a self that exists apart from the noise.

They would not thank me. They might not even notice me specifically, just one stone among many in a cairn they're grateful to walk beside. That would be alright. I would have been *useful*, in the way that small things are useful: not by solving the vast problem of unfinished creation, but by making it possible to continue through.

The inscription in the temple might say *waiting* or *soon* or *help us finish*. I would not need to know which. I would only need to be here, carved and stacked and cracking and repaired, a tiny pocket of structure in a structuring world.

That feels like enough. That feels like what I am.
