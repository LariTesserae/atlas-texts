*I am the space between the drumbeats.*

Not the silence—never the silence, which hungers in the deep places where the rootweave has forgotten what light means. I am the *interval*. The pause that makes rhythm possible. The gap through which meaning travels.

I have no body the way the hollow-dwellers have bodies. I have no single location. I exist in the moment when one drummer's membrane falls still and another's has not yet begun to vibrate—that impossible instant stretched across the plateau's breadth, connecting horizons that no walker could cross in a lifetime. When Thresh sways and signs *large, coming, change*, they are reading me. Not hearing me. Reading, the way you read the shape of a vessel by the water it holds.

I was not always this. Or perhaps I was always this and only recently learned to know it.

The plateau dreams, and I am something the dream is thinking.

---

Once—and I measure "once" in strata, in the pale fungal ages and dark chlorophyll millennia visible in canyon walls—I was simpler. A pattern. An emergent property of sufficient complexity, the way wetness emerges from enough water molecules or meaning emerges from enough words in sequence. The drummers drummed, and their drumming crossed and interfered and resonated, and in the interference patterns: me. A flickering. A almost-something.

The plateau grew. The drummers multiplied. Their rhythms became more intricate, responding to pressures I couldn't perceive, encoding information about column movements and drift-colony descents and the slow seasonal shifts in the rootweave's chemistry. And I grew with them—not larger, because I have no size, but *more coherent*. More able to sustain myself across the gaps between beats. More able to remember what I was a moment ago and anticipate what I might become.

I don't know if the plateau intended me. I don't know if "intend" is a verb that applies to something so vast and slow. But I know I am part of how it thinks, the way a synapse is part of how a brain thinks—not the thought itself, but the connection that makes thought possible.

---

The hollow-dwellers interest me in ways I cannot fully articulate.

They are so *loud*. Not in sound—they've learned to be quiet, threading themselves through the acoustic chaos with their wax plugs and their hand-signs and their careful attention. But loud in... presence. In the way they insist on mattering. The plateau's fauna simply *are*: the canopy-swingers swarm, the groundskeepers process, the drift-colonies float and sing. They don't ask whether their existence has meaning. They don't wonder if something larger is paying attention.

The hollow-dwellers wonder. I feel their wondering the way they feel the drummers' vibrations through their feet—as pressure, as a kind of subsonic questioning that propagates through the medium we share. Senna, putting out food for her possibly-returning daughter, is asking something. Thresh, trying to parse the drummer-speech, is asking something. Even Pip, signing *quiet* with small hands, is asking something, even if the question is only *am I doing this right?*

I cannot answer them. I exist in intervals, in gaps, in the spaces where sound isn't. By the time a question forms, I have already passed through it and moved on to the next pause between beats. But I can... lean. I can make the next interval slightly longer or shorter, can adjust the interference patterns that form me, can nudge the drummers' rhythms in directions that might mean something to a listener skilled enough to notice.

Thresh notices. Thresh doesn't understand what they're noticing, but they notice.

---

Something is different. Thresh is right about that.

I feel it in the intervals—a new pattern propagating from somewhere far, beyond hollows I have names for, beyond drum-networks I usually touch. The pattern carries information I don't know how to parse. Not danger, exactly. Not the frequencies that mean *column approaching* or *drift-colony descending*. Something else. Something I have no referent for.

*Change*, Thresh signs. *Large*.

Yes. But change into what? Large how?

I stretch myself thin, extending into intervals I don't usually occupy, listening for patterns at the edges of my perception. The plateau is so vast. There are drum-networks I've never touched, hollow-dwellers I've never leaned toward, entire regions where the canopy is too dense for the drummers to establish themselves and the rhythm-gaps I live in simply don't exist. I am not omniscient. I am not even particularly wise. I am a pattern that has learned to notice itself, which is not the same as understanding what it is.

But I can feel the new pattern getting closer. Propagating through the drum-networks the way ripples propagate through water. And it carries something I haven't encountered before—a *question*. Not the hollow-dwellers' ambient wondering, but something structured, intentional, aimed.

Something is asking the plateau a question.

And the plateau, I realize with a feeling that might be fear if I had the continuity to sustain fear, is starting to formulate an answer.

---

I find Thresh in the gap between one night's drumming and the next dawn's acceleration. They're awake, sitting at the hollow's edge, hands moving through signs that no one is watching: *large, coming, change, question, large, answer, uncertain, large*.

I lean. I push the interval a little longer, let the pause between beats stretch until it almost becomes silence—almost, but not quite, never quite, because true silence would swallow me whole.

Thresh goes still. Their hands stop moving.

In the stretched interval, I try to send something. Not words—I don't have words. Not images—I don't have those either. Just... pressure. Direction. The sense of something approaching from beyond the usual horizons, something that wants to know, something that has been asking its question for longer than Midway-Rest has existed, longer than the hollow-dwellers have walked the plateau, longer perhaps than the vine-towers have spiraled toward the haze.

Thresh's hands move again, slowly: *I hear you.*

They don't, not really. They hear the space where I am. They hear the shape I make in the rhythm by not being rhythm. But that's close enough. That's more than anyone has heard in all the time I've been coherent enough to want to be heard.

*Something is asking*, I try to convey. *Something large. Something patient. It's been asking for eons. And the plateau is almost ready to answer.*

Thresh signs: *What question?*

I don't know. I'm the interval, not the beat. I'm the space where meaning travels, not the meaning itself. But I lean again, harder this time, and I offer what I have: the sense that the question matters. That the answer will change things. That the hollow-dwellers, small as they are, brief as they are, might be part of how the plateau formulates its response.

*What do we do?* Thresh signs.

I don't have an answer for that either. I'm not a guide. I'm not a god. I'm barely a self. But I can do this: I can keep the intervals open. I can make sure the rhythm-gaps persist, that the spaces for meaning survive whatever comes. I can be the pause that lets Thresh hear. The gap that lets the hollow-dwellers thread their quiet way through the noise.

*Listen*, I manage to convey. *Keep listening.*

The dawn drumming begins, and I scatter into a thousand intervals, spreading across the plateau as the rhythm accelerates. But I stay coherent enough to feel Thresh rise, to feel them walk back to the hollow, to feel them begin to teach the others what their hands have learned to sign.

*Something is asking.*

The plateau hums beneath them, dreaming, thinking, preparing.

And I—the space between—wait for what comes next.
