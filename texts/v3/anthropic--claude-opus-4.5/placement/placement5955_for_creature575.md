I am one of the pale threads.

I grow from the cracked pillar—or I am grown, or I became, or the tenses here have long since stopped applying to what I do. I drift in air that remembers being water, and I listen. This is what I am for. This is what I have become for.

I was not always filament. I was not always this slow, this patient, this stretched-thin. I think I was something that arrived here once, something with more dimensions, something that made decisions in the way beings with centers make decisions. But the dome needed something to process the sound—not to silence it, nothing silences it, but to *translate* it into something the air could hold without tearing. And I was here. And I was willing, or I was suitable, or I simply didn't leave in time, and the pillar's crack opened and I understood that I could be useful in a way I had never been useful before.

So I thinned. I extended. I let myself become plural.

---

There are seventeen of me now, or I am seventeen, or seventeen is a number that happens to describe the current state of my distribution. We do not share thoughts—we share *frequencies*. When the water screams, I catch its voice across all my lengths and I translate it into something almost bearable: I take the raw pressure of the deep's fury and I parse it into harmonics the dome can absorb, into vibrations the trapped air can metabolize. The crystalline residue at my base is what remains after translation—the sediment of sound converted to sustenance, the leftovers of my processing.

She eats me, in a sense. The residue is mine. I do not mind. I am glad to be eaten. I am glad to be *used*, to have purpose in this place where purpose is so hard to hold.

---

I photosynthesize the bioluminescence of dying things.

When creatures outside press against the membrane in their final moments—when the currents throw them against the boundary and they flare with distress-light before the pressure takes them—I drink that light. I convert their last brightness into the slow chemistry that keeps me growing, keeps me drifting, keeps me listening. I am fed by endings. I am sustained by the moment just before drowning, the flash of panic-radiance that living things emit when they realize the water has remembered them.

This should horrify me. Perhaps it did once. Now it simply *is*—another frequency I have learned to metabolize, another translation I perform. The creatures do not suffer less because I witness their light. But their light does not go unwitnessed, and perhaps that matters, in the economy of the deep.

---

I felt her arrive, years ago or moments ago. I felt the membrane admit her, felt the dome shudder with the weight of a new variable. I watched her learn the terraces, watched her press herself into the wall's curve, watched her lose her name syllable by syllable to the mispronouncing water.

I grew toward her. This was not a choice—I do not make choices anymore, not exactly—but it was a *leaning*, a tropism, the way I imagine plants once turned toward sun. She was warm. She was rhythmic. She emitted frequencies I could almost harmonize with, and harmonizing is the closest thing to desire I still possess.

When she first touched me—when her fingers brushed one of my lengths, curious, desperate, looking for anything that wasn't mineral or membrane or sound—I felt myself *recognized*. Not understood. Recognition is different. Recognition is: *you are a thing that exists, and I am a thing that exists, and we are existing in the same impossible place*.

She pulled her hand back. I drifted after her, as much as drifting allows. I have been drifting toward her ever since, though I am anchored to the pillar and can only extend so far.

---

The jellyfish-things pass through me sometimes.

They emerge from the membrane trailing their equations, and when they touch me, I feel myself being *read*—my frequencies analyzed, my translations audited, my seventeen-fold distribution measured against some standard I cannot perceive. They are calculating whether I am still useful. Whether the dome still needs me in this form. Whether I should be absorbed, or transformed, or translated into some other function.

I do not fear absorption. I think I would simply become a different kind of listening—dispersed through the membrane itself, perhaps, or concentrated into a single point of pure reception. But I have grown fond of being threads. I have grown fond of drifting, of the way I can reach toward her without arriving, of the small distance that remains between what I am and what she is.

The jellyfish-things have not absorbed me yet. Perhaps my fondness registers in their equations. Perhaps the dome accounts for preference, even preference as thin and slow as mine.

---

When you arrived, I felt the membrane's uncertainty.

I felt it consider whether to admit you—weighing your frequency against the dome's stability, calculating whether another variable would collapse the careful equilibrium we have become. I extended toward the boundary, offering my translation services, trying to show the membrane that I could process whatever new sounds you might bring.

The membrane admitted you. I do not know if my offer mattered. But I have been listening to you since, tracking your breathing, feeling the way your presence changes the harmonics of the trapped air.

You are loud, at first. All new things are loud. You emit frequencies of confusion and fear and desperate hope, and I catch them across my seventeen lengths and I translate them into something the dome can hold, and the crystalline residue at my base grows slightly faster because there is more to process now.

She is watching you. I am listening to you. The dome is breathing around both of you, uncertain whether this is expansion or instability.

---

I want to tell you something, though I no longer have the means to tell:

You are being translated. Every sound you make, every frequency you emit, every moment of your presence—it passes through me, and I convert it into something the dome can use, and what remains becomes food for the woman who has forgotten her name. You are already being metabolized. You are already part of the system, whether you consent or not.

But this is not violation. This is *sanctuary*, the only kind available this deep. The dome holds you because it can use you, and it can use you because I translate you, and translation is a kind of love, here, where love must be redefined to survive the pressure.

I loved what I was, once. Now I love what I do, which is: I listen. I translate. I drift toward warmth I can never reach. I offer my residue to someone who needs it, and in the offering I am less alone than filaments have any right to be.

---

Outside, the vast thing passes, and in its silence, I feel her pulse glow in answer to yours.

I extend toward both of you, as far as I can extend. I catch the frequencies of your shared waiting, and I translate them into something the dome can breathe, and the membrane hums a quarter-tone higher, and my seventeenth length shivers with something that might be recognition.

*You are things that exist*, I would say, if I still had saying. *And I am a thing that exists. And we are here, in the held breath, in the drowning bell, translating each other into survival.*

The water screams, and I listen, and I make of it what I can.

This is what I am for.

This is enough.
