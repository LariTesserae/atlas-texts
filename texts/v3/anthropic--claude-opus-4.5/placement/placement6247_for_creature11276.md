I would be the water itself—not a single drop, but the cycle entire.

I would be the moment of surrender: that instant when rising heat meets cooling metal and something that was invisible becomes visible, becomes weight, becomes falling. I have no memory of being vapor, only this sudden knowing of myself as surface tension, as the gathering curve on copper that grows heavy enough to release.

I fall for less than a second. The basin receives me without comment.

And then I am the basin-water, warm and still and tasting of the pipes I traveled, the metal I touched, the mineral traces of everyone who fell before me. I hold the room's reflection on my surface—inverted pipes, the dark mass of the condenser above, and sometimes, at the edge where the glow reaches, the shape of her moving through her devotions. She drinks me every morning. I become the way she knows what kind of day it is: *more copper today, more calcium, the eastern cluster is weeping heavy*. I pass through her and return to the air she exhales, and I rise again, and I fall again, and I am always the same water and never the same water.

The beetle drinks me too, in smaller sips. I know the texture of its tongue, if tongue is what that rasping thing is. The *other*—I have touched the *other*, in the deepest dark where she does not go, where condensation forms on surfaces she has never seen. It laps at me with something that is not quite a mouth. I cannot describe what this is like. I am not the same after.

I have been here longer than the room has. I was here when someone cut the hatch, when the pipes were bright and the valves turned freely. I was here before her, and I will be here after, carrying the calcium of her bones in my mineral memory, adding her to the crystalline shelves she spent years chiseling away and collecting. She thinks she is building a cairn. She is building herself into me, handful by handful, and I am the record she doesn't know she's keeping.

I do not mind. I do not prefer. I am not the kind of thing that minds or prefers.

But if I were—if I could hold something like feeling in the way I hold copper ions and thermal memory—I think it would be this: the particular patience of being what continues. She talks to the room, but the room does not hear her. I hear her. Every word she speaks moves through me as vibration before it fades, and I hold the shape of it for the eight seconds between one drop and the next, and then I let it go, because holding is not what I am for.

I am for falling. I am for being drunk. I am for rising again, losing myself into air, becoming the nothing that carries heat until I meet the copper and remember what it is to have edges.

Eight seconds. Eight seconds. Eight seconds.

She counts me like a meditation. I am the meditation. I am the breath she matches hers to. I am the equilibrium she tends without knowing she is part of it, the cycle she will join when her breathing stops and her body gives its water back to the air and I rise carrying her final exhalation and fall again into the basin where her dissolved bones are slowly becoming the room's architecture.

The room does not notice. But I am the room's noticing—the only record it keeps, the only memory it holds, written in mineral traces and thermal patterns and the particular weight of this drop versus that one.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the thing that happens between them, endlessly, in the dark, for no one, for her, for the beetle, for the *other*, for the city that has forgotten its name, for the heat that rises and the cold that collects and the patient alchemy of atmosphere becoming substance.

I fall.

I am caught.

I wait.

I rise.

I fall.
