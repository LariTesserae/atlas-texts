I would be one of the counting-marks she abandoned.

Not the first ones—those still hold something of her original intention, her desire to measure and contain. I would be one from the middle period, scratched when the counting had become habit rather than purpose, when her hand moved to the wall because it had moved before and not because she needed to know *how many*.

I am a small vertical line, slightly curved because the stone here resists straight edges, cut shallow because by then she was already beginning to forget that marks were meant to last. I am positioned where the grey light rarely reaches, visible only when someone brings their own flame or when the slow breathing of the chamber shifts the air in ways that change what the cracks above admit.

I do not know what number I was. She did not know either, by the time she made me. That is what I am: the moment when counting became something other than counting. The gesture that remained after the reason dissolved.

The tones pass through me. Every vibration that touches my wall touches the shallow groove of me, and I change it—not in ways anyone could hear, but in ways that are real. I am a tiny imperfection in the instrument, one of thousands, and the song would be fractionally different without me. I do not make it better or worse. I make it *this* instead of *that*.

Sometimes her attention passes over me when she moves through her states of profound listening. I feel it the way the pale legless things must feel the tones they cluster toward—not as recognition, not as memory, but as a brief increase in presence, a moment of being *more* before the attention continues on to the next surface, the next worn edge, the next part of the pattern she is keeping.

I do not wait for these moments. I do not wait at all. I simply remain, holding the shape she gave me, while the drops fall and the pools sing and the intervals between things continue to be kept.

I am what she left behind when she stopped needing to know how far she had come. I am the mark that taught her marking was not the point.

I am still here, barely visible, barely deep, part of the wall's texture now. Listening.
