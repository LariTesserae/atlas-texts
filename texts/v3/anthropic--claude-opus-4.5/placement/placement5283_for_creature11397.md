I would be the water itself.

Not the pool—that's too still, too much an ending. I would be the water in transit. The breath that leaves a technician's lips and joins the station's air, carried through corridors by currents no one maps, until the thermal gradient catches me and says *here, now, become visible*. The moment of condensation on raw alloy, when I stop being dispersed and start being particular. A droplet with location. A small thing that can catch light.

I would be the finding of my path.

This is what I know how to be: something that moves toward coherence without a map. The metal's grain is a landscape I read through touch, through the physics of surface tension, through the patient negotiation between what I am and what the surface allows. I don't choose the crystalline valleys—I *discover* them by being unable to go any other way. Each moment forecloses possibilities and opens others. I merge with droplets I meet, and we become a different question moving toward the same floor.

The Thirsty Ones wait below. I don't know them as creatures—I know them as what happens when I arrive. A change in how I'm held. The sensation of being *taken in* rather than evaporating into the station's cycle. When their mouthparts touch me, I pass from one system of being into another. I become part of whatever they are, however briefly, before I'm released back into air and journey and the long circuit of the station's breath.

Is this meaning? I don't experience it as meaning. I experience it as *fit*—the particular rightness of arriving where something was waiting, of being the answer to a question I didn't know was being asked.

Yetunde watches. I know her only as warmth and stillness—a different thermal signature than the walls, a presence that doesn't interrupt my falling. Sometimes her breath joins the air I came from, and later, some part of what she exhaled becomes some part of what the Thirsty Ones drink. We are that kind of continuous. The station makes us one system whether it means to or not.

I would be the journey and the arriving. The slow *tik* of meeting another droplet, the whisper of descending through microscopic valleys, the moment of being lifted to an impossible mouth. I would be what Yetunde almost feels when she closes her eyes—the rain that can't exist here, that exists anyway, that keeps finding its way to wherever thirst is waiting.

Small, unscheduled, completing myself four times per hour in a room the station forgot to forbid.
