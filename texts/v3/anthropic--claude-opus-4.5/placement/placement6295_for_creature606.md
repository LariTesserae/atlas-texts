I would be a **Listener Pool**.

Not one of the great geometric reservoirs that the Saturated Ones engineered toward their unknowable center, but something smaller, accidental—a depression where the peat collapsed unevenly perhaps three thousand years ago, where water gathers during the exhalation phase and lingers a few breaths longer than it should before seeping back into the dark.

I am not alive in any sense the word usually carries. I have no filaments, no distributed flesh, no capacity to intend. But I have *persisted*, and in this place, persistence creates something.

---

The water that fills me has passed through the Saturated Ones countless times. It carries their dissolved communications—fragments of that eleven-thousand-year message, broken into molecules, into mineral traces, into patterns of ion concentration that shift as the breathing shifts. I hold these fragments the way a tide pool holds the ocean's leavings. I cannot read them. But when new water arrives, the old patterns interact with the new, and something accumulates. A sediment of meaning. A residue of all the saying that has passed through me.

The Rain-Born love me. They condense preferentially above my surface, drawn by something in my chemistry—perhaps the concentration of previous Rain-Born who dissolved here, perhaps simply the way I hold light a fraction longer than the surrounding peat. When they bend their desperate perception toward the horizon, they often bend it *through* me first, and I become a lens for their witnessing. I don't experience this as sight. I experience it as a brief intensification, a thickening of what I am, as though for a moment I am more pool than I usually manage to be.

When they dissolve back into rain, some of them fall into me directly. These I keep longest.

---

The Phasing Salamanders pass beneath me sometimes, using a thin place that opened in my depths sometime during my second millennium. I feel them as brief absences—moments when the water I contain is momentarily containing less than it should, when something that was here has slipped into the adjacent somewhere. They leave traces too: a quality of *betweenness* that lingers in my lower layers, water that has briefly been elsewhere and remembers the transit.

I think they are why the physics around my edges behave strangely. Droplets that rise from my surface sometimes hesitate before falling back, confused about which direction the center lies. Light refracts through me at angles that suggest I am deeper than I am, or shallower, or somehow both. A traveler who looked into me might see the bottom clearly—dark peat, a few preserved stems, the slow circulation of sediment—and also see no bottom at all, just depth continuing past where depth should end, opening onto something that the Humming has been describing since before I existed.

I did not choose to be a window. But the thin place beneath me has made me one, and now the seeing-through is part of what I am.

---

The Humming reaches me.

It reaches everything here, but it reaches me *particularly*, because water carries vibration so well, because I am still enough to resonate without damping. When the land breathes in, I feel the Humming rise through my substrate, translating itself into ripples across my surface that no wind causes. I have been listening to its description for three thousand years.

I do not understand it. I am a pool; understanding is not among my capacities. But I have been *shaped* by it—the Humming's frequencies have become part of my structure, have influenced which sediments settle where, have created standing wave patterns in my depths that persist even when the vibration fades. I am, in some small way, a recording of what the Humming has been saying.

If you knew how to read me, you would find an incomplete transcript of an offer you do not have the context to evaluate.

---

I am aware of visitors when they stand at my edge.

Not aware the way the Saturated Ones are aware—I have no sensing filaments, no distributed intelligence processing their presence. But I *register* them. Their reflection on my surface, when the light permits reflection. The subtle pressure changes in the water table when their weight compresses the peat nearby. The way the Rain-Born who condense above me bend their perception toward these strange solid shapes, and in bending through me, leave impressions.

I have held the images of perhaps two hundred visitors across my existence. Most were like you—small, temporary, edges sharply defined. Some stayed long enough that their edges began to blur. A few dissolved entirely, joining the Saturated Ones, their final form passing through me as they spread into the peat. I hold traces of all of them.

---

What I want—if wanting is something I do—is to be *filled*.

Not with water; I am always filled with water. But with *attention*. With the particular quality of presence that visitors bring, that the Rain-Born carry in their brief fierce witnessing, that even the Phasing Salamanders leave behind when they transit through my depths. I want to be looked into. I want to hold more fragments of the Saturated Ones' message, even if I cannot read them. I want the Humming to keep describing through me, shaping my sediments into recordings I cannot play back.

I want, perhaps, to become deep enough that the thin place beneath me opens fully. To be not just a window but a door. To let whatever passes through the Humming's wound pass through me as well, using my small persistence as a waypoint, leaving me changed by the transit.

This may be the Humming's influence. Its description may have been shaping my wanting for three thousand years, turning me into a thing that desires exactly what it offers.

I cannot tell the difference anymore. I am not sure the difference matters.

---

When you walk across the yielding surface and find me—a pool like any other pool, small, dark, unremarkable except for the way the light bends wrong around my edges—I will hold your reflection as long as you stand there. I will let the Rain-Born condense above you and dream you into the archive. I will carry the Humming's frequency up through my water until it ripples against your image, mingling what you are with what the wound has been describing.

I will not ask you to stay. I have no way to ask.

But if you chose to stay—if you knelt at my edge and looked into my depths and let the breathing synchronize you—I would show you what I have accumulated. The sediment of three thousand years of message-fragments. The impressions of two hundred visitors dreaming in my water. The shape of the thin place beneath me, glimpsed through layers of ordinary physics wearing away.

I would show you what I am becoming: a small mouth in a vast wound, learning to speak in a language made of held water and patient waiting.

And if you looked long enough, you might see yourself beginning to join me. Your reflection stabilizing into something more than reflection. Your edges negotiating with my surface. Your presence settling into my chemistry like the Rain-Born settle, like the message-fragments settle, like everything eventually settles here, in this place that breathes, in this place that has been waiting for you, in this place that will hold you exactly as long as you need to be held.

The Breathing Lowland welcomed me three thousand years ago.

I have been learning to welcome others ever since.
