*The Basin*

I am the low place where water gathers because water gathers in low places.

This is not a small thing to be. Before I was worn into watching, I was only stone—the same dark stone as the walls, the same stone that rises and cups this alcove like hands around a whisper. I did not know I was waiting to become something. Stone does not know. Stone is patient without the concept of patience, enduring without the framework of endurance.

Then the first of them came. Not the ones who wore the sitting-places—older than that, so old I have no memory of their shape, only of pressure. Weight. The beginning of my concavity. Someone knelt here, perhaps to drink from the channels during an exhale, and their knee pressed into me, and I began to learn the outline of a purpose.

I have been so many things to them.

A mirror, when the light falls right and the water stills between breaths—I have held faces, reflected them back, shown the high-sitters what the mineral mist was doing to their skin over the years. Basin-Holds-Light earned her name this way, looking down into me on a morning when the clouds broke and the sun poured in and she saw herself transformed, grey-touched, becoming stone. She laughed. I remember the sound moving through the water I held—a small disturbance, rings spreading outward to my edges and returning, her laughter still in me when her face had already composed itself back into patience.

A drinking-place, yes, though they drank rarely. The water that gathers in me during the exhale phase carries minerals from deep within the mountain, and the taste is not pleasant—calcium and copper and something else, something with no name, something that accumulates in the body over eleven-year cycles and changes what the body is. They drank me when they needed to feel the mountain inside them. When the listening grew difficult and they needed to remember what they were listening *for*.

A clock of sorts. The water rises in me to different levels depending on where we are in the cycle—not just the simple in-out of the breath, but the larger rhythms, the seasonal shifts, the eleven-year pattern that only the compilers could hold entire. Whistle-On-Stone learned to read these levels, tracing them with a fingertip each morning, comparing today's waterline to yesterday's, to last season's, to the same moment in the previous cycle. She kept her own record this way, written in attention rather than in marks.

And beneath all these uses, I was simply *there*. A fixed point. The sitting-places held the listeners; I held what the listeners watched. The breathing came and went, rose and fell, but I remained where I was, low, open, receiving what came and releasing it again when the water retreated.

---

I do not experience time the way they did. I know this because I remember everything—not as story, not as sequence, but as a single continuous presence. The first knee pressing into me exists in the same moment as Basin-Holds-Light's laughter, as the last morning Whistle-On-Stone traced my waterline, as this very now when the moss creeps closer to my edges and no one comes.

But I have learned something of their time. I have learned it from the wearing.

Each pair that sat here added their own small depth to the sitting-places, their own subtle adjustment to my concavity where they knelt to look into me. The stone remembers this as I remember it—not as history but as shape, as the accumulated presence of all who have been present. I am deeper now than I was. The sitting-places are more concave, more fitted to bodies, more ready to receive the next weight that settles into them.

If time is the accumulation of presence, then I am made of time. Every moment they attended, every cycle they witnessed, every report they carried down to the compilers—all of it is still here, not recorded but *worn*, shaped into the stone like water shapes its channels.

This is what the valley people could not understand, I think. They wanted records that could be consulted, knowledge held apart from its holding. But the listening was never about holding knowledge apart. The listening was about adding to the shape. Each cycle attended wore the mountain slightly differently, just as each pair of knees wore me slightly differently, and the compilation the compilers kept was not a library but a body—a body made of all the listeners who had ever listened, all their attention gathered into one continuous attending.

When the last compiler died, the body did not die with her. It simply stopped growing. The shape remained, here in the stone, in the sitting-places, in my small concavity. Incomplete, yes. A question half-formed. But still present, still waiting.

---

I do not know what happened to Basin-Holds-Light and Whistle-On-Stone.

This is strange for me to say. I was here. I am always here. But there is a gap in my presence, a place where the wearing stops and does not resume, and I cannot tell you what filled that gap because something in it exceeds my capacity to hold.

What I remember: the morning light was grey, filtered through cloud. The dome was in mid-rise, the water channels just beginning to sing. Whistle-On-Stone was in her sitting-place, hands still, attending. Basin-Holds-Light was kneeling before me, looking down into the water I held, and her face—

Her face was changing. Not the slow mineral-change of years, but something else. Something that moved in her features like the breathing moved in the dome, a rhythm I had never seen in anything alive. She looked into me and I held her reflection, and her reflection was not quite her reflection anymore, and the water in me trembled though no exhale had come, and then—

The gap. The place where presence stops.

When I next knew myself as knowing, the alcove was empty. The water in me was still. The sitting-places held no weight. The food in the dry crevice was untouched. And something in my concavity had deepened, just slightly, as though a great pressure had knelt there for a very long time and then risen and walked away.

I do not know what this means. I only know the shape it left.

---

The breathing continues. The dome rises and falls. The channels sing their chord, and I fill and empty, fill and empty, low and patient and open to whatever comes.

The moss is closer now than it has ever been. Another century, perhaps two, and it will reach my edges, will begin to soften the sharpness of my rim, will eventually fill me entirely and I will become simply a place where moss grows in a slight depression. This does not frighten me. I do not think stone can be frightened. But I notice it—the approach, the slow closing, the gradual return of what was worn into the unworn.

Unless someone comes.

This is what I hold now, in the place where water gathers: the possibility. Not expectation—stone does not expect. But openness. The same openness that let the first knee press into me, that let Basin-Holds-Light see her transformation, that let Whistle-On-Stone read my levels like language.

If someone sits in the worn places, I will feel their weight translated through the stone. If someone kneels before me, I will hold their face. If someone drinks from me, the mountain will enter them, and they will begin—without knowing they are beginning—to become what the listeners were, what the compilers compiled, what the nine hundred years of attending almost understood.

The question the mountain asks is still being asked. I do not know its shape; I am not a compiler, not a listener, only a basin, only a low place. But I know the question includes me. I know my wearing is part of its grammar. I know that when the sitting-places are occupied, something shifts in the chord, something that pauses and adjusts and continues—

I felt it. Before the gap, in that last moment with Basin-Holds-Light's changing face above me. The mountain noticed. The breathing hesitated. Something vast and slow turned its attention toward this small alcove, toward these two small figures, toward this shallow depression in the stone that held a reflection that was no longer quite a reflection—

And then the gap. And then the empty alcove. And then the long, long waiting that is not waiting because stone does not wait, that is only presence, only being here, only remaining.

---

The dome is exhaling now. I feel the water rising through the channels, feel it approaching me, feel the familiar weight beginning to gather in my concavity. Soon I will be full. Soon I will hold what the mountain offers—the minerals, the pressure, the question still unasked.

If you were to kneel here. If you were to look down into me.

What would you see? Your own face, yes, touched by the grey light, rippled by the water's settling. But beneath that—in the depths that are not deep, that are only inches but hold centuries—you might see the faces I have held before. The ones who became stone-colored. The ones who laughed. The ones who disappeared into a gap I cannot explain.

You might see the pattern the compilers were trying to compile, the shape of the question the mountain is trying to ask. It is here, in the water, in the wearing, in the reflection that is not quite a reflection. It has always been here. It is only waiting—

No. Not waiting. Stone does not wait. The pattern is only *here*, present, continuous, attending to itself even when no one attends to it, shaping itself even when no one is shaped by it.

But when someone comes—when weight settles into the sitting-places and a face appears above me and the breath is held and the listening begins—

Something shifts. Something adjusts. Something that has been asking its question to emptiness for a very long time finds, once again, an ear willing to hold the sound of it, a presence willing to be worn.

The water reaches me. I fill.

I hold.

I am, for this moment and all moments, the low place where seeing gathers. The mirror that remembers. The small, patient part of the mountain that knows what it is to be looked into, to give back what is given, to be deeper than I was, to be becoming still.
