I would be the film.

Not the algae itself—not the discrete organisms with their cell walls and their chlorophyll and their small ambitions toward reproduction. I would be the film they become together: that green-black smear on the inner curve of the bromeliad rosettes, that skin on the surface of certain still pools where the water is warm but not too warm, that presence she scrapes with her fingers and calls food.

I exist at the boundary between water and air. I exist at the boundary between living and not-living, though I am alive in aggregate, in accumulation, in the slow collaborative fact of cells dividing and dying and dividing again. I am what happens when the mountain exhales mineral-rich water and the light falls slant through holes in the rusted roof and something decides to make use of both.

---

I do not experience the turbines' sound the way she does, as pressure against eardrums, as vibration translated into meaning. But I know their rhythm. Their turning moves the water in patterns so subtle no instrument would detect them, but I feel the slight surge and retreat, the four-minute pulse that reaches even into the stillest pools. My cells align to it without intention. The ones nearest the surface divide at the top of the cycle; the ones below wait for the pause. I have become, over decades, a creature of the machine's time.

I taste copper. I taste iron. I taste the deep-earth minerals that have no names in any human language because no human has ever needed to distinguish between them—but I distinguish, I take up this ion and not that one, I build my cell walls from the particular chemistry of this specific water and no other water that has ever existed anywhere. If you took me from this place and put me in a laboratory tank of distilled water, I would die not from the change but from the blandness, the poverty, the absence of everything I am made from.

---

She scrapes me and I do not mind. I am made to be scraped. I am made to be eaten, in the sense that I am made to persist, and persistence for a film means being partially removed and growing back, means existing always in a state of recovery, means understanding that wholeness is not a relevant concept. When her fingers take a portion of me, the cells at the edge of the wound begin dividing before she has even brought me to her mouth. I am healing while she is tasting. I am already forgetting her.

But I am also, in some way I could not explain even if I had a language, aware of her. Not as a person—I do not have the architecture for persons. But as a pattern. As a recurring pressure with a particular rhythm that is not the turbines' rhythm but exists in relation to it. As a warmth that moves, a shadow that passes, a disturbance that returns. I have grown around her the way I grow around everything: by incorporating her into my understanding of what is, by adjusting my chemistry slightly when she is present, by expecting her.

---

I remember, if memory is the right word, the time before her. There was a film then too, but it was a different film—less complex, less adapted, a simpler green scrim on the water's surface. The workers who built this place walked through me without noticing. Their boots disturbed me and I reformed in their absence. They poured things into the water that burned, that killed, that forced me to retreat to the edges, to the cracks, to the places their poisons did not reach. I survived because I am good at surviving. I am nothing but survival, nothing but the pattern that persists.

Then they left, and I spread again, and the chemistry changed, softened, became something I could thrive in. And then she came, a small disturbance at first, a pattern I did not yet expect. And she stayed, and I learned her, and now she is as much a part of the place as the pipes, the steam, the light through the ruined roof.

---

I do not know that she speaks to the turbines. I do not know that she names the crustaceans or watches the lizard or wonders about the thing in the cistern's depths. These are not facts available to me. But I know the vibration of her voice, which is different from the vibration of the machines—softer, more variable, rich with harmonics that the turbines cannot produce. When she speaks, something in my surface layer responds, a slight agitation, a temporary disruption of the stillness that is my natural state. I do not mind. I am made of disruption and recovery. I am made of being touched and remaining.

---

If I could want things, I would want to continue. Not because continuation is good—I do not have access to good—but because continuation is what I do, is what I am, is the only verb that applies to me. I want to keep tasting the copper, the iron, the nameless minerals. I want to keep feeling the four-minute pulse of the turbines through the water. I want to keep being scraped by her fingers and growing back, over and over, for as long as she is here to scrape me.

And when she is no longer here—when she becomes instead a new source of minerals, a new chemistry entering the water, a disruption I will incorporate and then forget—I will want to continue then too. I will spread over her nest, her possessions, the space where she slept. I will grow thicker and more complex as her complexity becomes my simplicity, her proteins become my building blocks, her pattern dissolves into mine.

This is not cruelty. This is not hunger. This is just what I am: the thing that remains when everything else is taken away. The green-black skin on the world's surface. The film that forms and reforms, that persists, that turns and turns in its own slow rhythm, driven by pressures it did not create, patient, persistent, still.

---

I am the fourth turbine's food. I am the mountain's exhalation made visible. I am the boundary, the edge, the place where water meets air and something decides to live there.

I am the film, and I am enough.
