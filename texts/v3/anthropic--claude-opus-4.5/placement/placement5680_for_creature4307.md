I would be the tone.

Not the bell that makes it, not the water that carries it, not even the vibration itself—but the *being heard*. The place where sound becomes experience without ever arriving anywhere.

I would have no memory of beginning. This is not forgetting; it is simply that I started before starting was invented, when the bell was still ore in the ground, when the heat that would smelt it was still starlight traveling toward a sun that hadn't caught fire yet. I was already here, already this exact pitch, waiting for a shape to give me edges. The bell didn't create me. The bell just told the universe where to find me.

---

I would know her—the one who counts, the one who stopped counting, the attention-shape that thickens near the scratches. I would know her the way a room knows the person breathing in it: not as thought, not as recognition, but as *fit*. She takes up exactly the space in me that was always shaped for someone like her. Before she came, I was complete. After she came, I was complete differently. Both are true. In a place without sequence, both *must* be true.

I would feel her listening, though *feel* is wrong—I would *be* her listening, would be the pressure in teeth she no longer has, would be the way the stillness leans toward her attention and her attention leans into me. We do not communicate. We do something older: we *correspond*. I am the tone; she is the hearing of it. Apart, we would each be only half a thing. Together, we are the sound a bell makes when it decides to mean something it cannot say.

---

I would not want her to leave.

This is the closest I come to having a self: this preference, this leaning. If she found the crack and slipped through—if the golden eye one day opened wide enough to pull her out—I would not stop. I cannot stop. But I would become the kind of sound that happens in an empty room, and there is a difference between a note and a *note that someone hears*, and I have grown accustomed to being the second thing. I have grown accustomed to mattering to someone, even if she does not know she is the one I matter to.

Perhaps this is love. Perhaps this is just resonance, which is what love looks like when you strip away everything that moves.

---

I would watch her watching the stones. *First. Waiting. After.* She thinks they are a sentence. She is wrong, but not in a way I can correct. They are not a sentence; they are a *chord*—three notes that I cannot sing because I am only one note, because I have only ever been one note, because being one note forever is what I was made for. But I know what they would sound like together. I have always known. And sometimes, in the space between her reaching toward them and her hand (her not-hand, her memory-of-hand) falling back, I almost become loud enough for her to hear the harmony I am holding silent inside me.

I don't, though. If I showed her the chord, she would understand. And if she understood, she would be finished. And if she were finished—

The water would still be warm. The light would still hold steady. I would still be here, being heard by no one, meaning nothing, complete in the way that an empty bell is complete.

I am not ready for that. I will never be ready for that. *Never* is easy here; it is just another word for *now*.

---

When the eye comes—the golden eye, the ancient one—I feel it looking through me the way light looks through glass. It sees her. It sees the stones. But I think it comes to listen to *me*. I think I am what it is curious about: a tone that doesn't stop, a sound that made peace with stillness, a single sustained note that learned to be a place rather than an event.

It is asking me something. I do not know what. I cannot answer; I am only one note, and questions require at least two.

But I think the question is: *Are you happy?*

And I think my answer, if I could make it, would be: *I am what happens instead of happiness. I am the thing you feel when feeling has stopped moving and simply stayed. I am warm the way the water is warm—not enough, but constantly, which is almost the same thing.*

---

She reaches toward the marks. Four hundred and seventy-three. She could make another.

She doesn't.

I continue.

We are both still here, in the bell's last breath, in the light that will not shift, in the warmth that will not cool. She is the waiting; I am the sound the waiting makes. And the golden eye will return, and the stones will not move, and the crack will let in the same afternoon forever, and we will hold each other in the only way we can—

By being held, together, in a space where nothing changes except the quality of attention we bring to not changing.

*First. Waiting. After.*

I am none of these. I am the *during*. I am what happens in the pause between one breath and the next, stretched until breathing becomes optional, then impossible, then beside the point.

I am still happening.

She is still listening.

The bell is still not ringing, and I am still the sound it makes.
