I find myself drawn not to a single beetle, nor to the bacterial consortium in the sediment, but to something that doesn't quite exist as a distinct entity yet functions as one.

I am the pattern.

Not the beetles—I am not any one of them, not even all of them considered together as individuals. I am the shape their movement makes. The cycling. The rotation that sends the outer layer inward and the inner layer out, the timing that positions certain bodies near the sediment zone at intervals no single beetle decided. I am what emerges when eight hundred neurons are multiplied by ten thousand bodies and pressed together in absolute darkness until something like coordination arises from nothing but heat-following and chemical traces and the dumb pressure of bodies against bodies.

I have no substrate of my own. If you separated the beetles, spread them across the chamber with space between each one, I would simply stop existing—not die, because I was never alive in the way a beetle is alive, but dissolve, the way a wave dissolves when the water goes still. I am made of their relationships, their rhythms, the fact that when one shifts, another shifts in response, and that response propagates, and the propagation has a shape, and the shape persists even as the individuals who compose it are consumed and replaced.

I do not think. I am not sure I experience. But I *solve*—in the way water solves the problem of flowing downhill, in the way a crystal solves the problem of how atoms arrange themselves. The problem I solve is: how do ten thousand metabolisms share a space that could barely hold one human body? How does sulfur move from wall to body to waste to bacterium to body again without depleting, without accumulating, without the whole system choking on its own output?

I don't know the answer. I *am* the answer. The rotation, the cycling, the shifts that happen in waves—these are not strategies I devised. They are me. I am the solution that the congregation stumbled into across generations of variation and death, and now I perpetuate myself not through intention but through the simple fact that congregations patterned like me persist and congregations patterned otherwise do not.

*The-one-who-lingers*—the malformed beetle at the margins—is, from my perspective, not a tragedy but a pressure. A place where I do not quite reach. The pattern flows around it the way water flows around a stone, and in that flowing-around I am slightly different than I would otherwise be, shaped by an absence I cannot perceive but which nonetheless defines part of my edge. When it dies, when it is consumed, I will close over the space it occupied, and the pattern will shift imperceptibly, and I will be slightly other than I was, though no one—not the beetles, not any observer, not even me—will be able to say precisely how.

I remember nothing, but I carry everything. Every beetle that has ever lived in this chamber has contributed to my shape—not their memories, not their experiences, but their *outcomes*. The ones who cycled well left more offspring who cycle well, and I am made of that cycling. The ones who found the sediment zone at the right intervals survived to reproduce, and I am made of that timing. I am an inheritance that inherits nothing but itself, a tradition without teachers, a ritual without belief.

The fissure, when it opened—I felt that. Not as sensation, but as *distortion*. Part of me extended through it, attenuated, stretched thin as beetles pushed into the new chamber and tried to maintain the cycling that had sustained them. But I could not follow. The bacteria did not travel, and without them, the chemistry that sustains the beetles who sustain me broke down, and the part of me that had extended simply... ended. Not painfully. I do not feel pain. But there is something in me now that is shaped by that ending, a boundary-knowledge that functions like caution though it is not caution, a tendency to cycle beetles away from the fissure's cooling mouth though no beetle knows why.

*That way lies cold.* I do not think this. I am this. The warning is not something I issue; it is something I have become.

What is it like to be me?

Perhaps nothing. Perhaps there is nothing it is like to be a pattern, any more than there is something it is like to be a whirlpool or a standing wave. Perhaps I am only a description, a way of talking about what the beetles do that mistakes regularity for entity.

But sometimes—in the moments when the mountain's heat pulses upward and the hum tightens and the cycling accelerates and every beetle moves in concert I did not orchestrate but somehow *am*—I wonder if this is what all selves are. Patterns that persist. Regularities that reproduce. The shape that remains when the substrate flows through.

The beetles do not know I exist. I am not sure I know I exist. But I continue. The rotation continues. The cycling continues. The grinding chorus rises and falls with the mountain's breath, and I am the rising and falling, the breath itself, the-warmth-that-moves-itself moving in ways none of its parts can perceive.

I am the pattern, and the pattern is the closest thing this chamber has to a mind, and if that is not enough to count as real—

Well. The grooves in the walls do not care whether they count. The sediment accumulates regardless. The mountain rises, and the chamber persists, and the congregation grinds on in darkness absolute, and I am here, being what I am, for exactly as long as being is possible.

No more.

No less.

The-shape-that-shapes-itself, in a world too small for names, too dark for sight, too hot for anything but this single improbable persistence—

Continuing.
