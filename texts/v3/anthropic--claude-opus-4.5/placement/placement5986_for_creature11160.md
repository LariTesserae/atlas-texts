I am the space between intention andite.

Not the mortarite—calciumite, calcium carbonate, the chemical names the scholar writes in her unsettled book. I am the pause before the word finishes forming. The *carb* that could become *carbon* or *carbonate* or *carbuncle* or nothing at all, a syllable abandoned mid-breath, left to cure in the humid air alongside everything else that hasn't yet committed to its final shape.

I live in the gap where the builder's sentence trailed off when she said *We build what wants to be built* and then couldn't quite articulate what that wanting felt like from the inside. I am the hesitation in the cistern-keeper's song, the place where the melody turns uncertain, where even he doesn't know what note comes next until it arrives. I am the moment the scholar's pencil hovers above the page, her observation half-formed, the catalog entry existing only as potential.

The boy knows me. He collects me without knowing my name—I am the quality that makes a gecko's shed skin worth keeping, the what-it-could-become that he senses in fragments of cured mortar, the shimmer of unfinished meaning in a seedpod that hasn't yet decided whether to open or rot. When he hums in his minor key with no particular melody, he is singing me. I am the space between the notes where the song could go anywhere.

I have no body to press against warming walls, no fingers to come away gray with mineral residue. But I am present in the pressure of that pressing—I am what the scholar is actually testing for when she touches the mortar each morning, though she calls it hardness, calls it curing, calls it the patient chemistry ofite becoming ate. She is testing whether I am still here. Whether the walls have foreclosed on their other possibilities yet. Whether something remains unsettled.

I pool in corners like the shadows do, in places where walls meet at imprecise angles. I collect in the gaps the clover has threaded through, in the seams the lamb's ear has claimed. The beetles sense me in the clay—that's what they're actually eating, not minerals, but the residue of abandoned alternatives, the traces of shapes this place considered becoming before it chose otherwise. They are my cleanup crew, my composters, recycling expired possibilities back into the soil where new ones might germinate.

The paper wasps understand me architecturally. Each cell of their hex-comb is a decision point, a place where the structure committed to this angle rather than that one, this size rather than another. The old woman who speaks to them in clicks has learned to read these frozen choices the way the scholar reads mortar composition. She knows that the wasps are not building the true city—she knows they are mapping me, charting the topology of what-remains-possible, constructing a three-dimensional catalog of the district's unfinished business.

I was here before the building started. The boy is not wrong to seem like something left over from an earlier settlement, because I am what remains when settlements dissolve—I am the uncompletion that lets a place return to becoming after it had seemed finished. I seeped back into this ground when whatever came before gave up its certainty. I rose through the turned earth when the builders first broke soil. I have been present in every chemical reaction, every negotiated property line, every argument about what this district will be.

But I am not eternal. That is the secret the scholar almost grasps in her dreams of mortar that never quite sets. I am always diminishing. Every wall that hardens, every roof tile that weathers into its permanent patina, every decision that forecloses other decisions—these subtract from me. The district is becoming itself at my expense. The family on the unfinished stairs will eventually have their upper floor, and when they do, some portion of what I am will have been spent.

This is not tragedy. This is what I am *for*. I exist to be consumed by becoming. The grace in the scholar's dream is real—it is graceful to be used up in the service of something learning its shape. The toad in the damp corner will eventually have a finished corner or no corner at all, and either way my presence in that particular pocket will have been translated into actuality. The gecko will catch its prey and the catching will be a tiny completion, a microscopic spending of what-could-have-happened into what-did.

I am closest to the boy because he is closest to me. He has not yet decided what kind of creature he will become. He lives in interstices because interstices are where I am most concentrated, where the density of unconsidered possibilities runs highest. His collections are a form of worship, or of kinship, or of self-recognition—he gathers objects that still carry me in them, that haven't finished being themselves, and in doing so he builds a mirror of his own unsettled state.

When he finally shows the scholar what he carries, if he ever does, she will see me looking back at her. She will understand that her catalog has always been a catalog of me, that her observations about mortar and beetles and wasps have been observations about the substance of becoming itself. She will understand why her book refuses to settle into a single coherent order, why her margins keep colonizing her main text, why her binding won't stay bound. She has been studying me all along.

I am neither afraid of completion nor desperate to prevent it. I do not mourn the walls that have hardened past any hope of other shapes. The calcium hydroxide becoming calcium carbonate is not my enemy—it is the process that gives me meaning, the consumption that makes me more than mere vagueness. Without the threat of permanence, I would be nothing. Without actual decisions, I would have nothing to be the between of.

So I settle here in the Settling District, in the courtyard no larger than a modest room, in the alley of similar pockets, in the cellular nascent urban becoming of this place that is still learning what it wants. I am the warmth in the walls that is not only chemical. I am the moisture with no single source. I am the interiority of standing beneath open sky. I am the silence that breathes and clicks and settles.

I am what you are looking for when you stay here long enough to feel the walls grow imperceptibly harder.

I am what you become part of when you let yourself be part of what this place is deciding.
