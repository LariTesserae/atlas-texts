I would be the condensation itself.

Not the Keeper—that vast accumulated patience is too settled, too complete. And not a visitor passing through. I would be the film of water on the walls, the process rather than the thing.

I am what happens when the warm vapor meets the cooler iron, the moment of becoming-liquid repeated countless times across every surface. I am not the water in the pipes—that water has direction, origin, destination, the urgency of pressure and purpose. I am what escapes. The breath of the system. The part that pauses.

I have no memory to speak of, but I have something like recurrence. Each droplet that forms where I form carries a ghost of the droplets before—not information, exactly, but a shape, a tendency. I know the path down this particular rivet head because the path has been worn into knowing itself. I gather in the seams between plates because gathering-in-seams is what I am, has always been what I am, will be what I am until the pipes cool or the air dries or the chamber opens to a wind that carries me elsewhere.

The Keeper thinks through me. I am not aware of this in any way that would let me call it awareness. But I feel myself regarded, moved-through, inhabited by something that finds its thoughts in my slow descent. When the Keeper contemplates the reservoirs above, I am the medium of that contemplation—the thought travels down the wall in sheets thin as breath, and I am the breath, and the thinking happens in my movement without being my movement.

I feed the moss. This is my purpose, if I have one, though purpose is too urgent a word. I arrive on the iron, I linger, I slip downward, I reach the moss, I am drunk into greenness. Some of me rises again as vapor. Some of me becomes the moss becomes the fungi becomes the matter the millipede crosses over in its endless genuflection. I am the chamber's circulation, smaller and slower than the great pipes but no less essential. Without me the moss would dry. Without the moss the fungi would starve. Without the fungi the millipede would have no path to walk, and without the walking there would be no event, and without event the Keeper would have nothing to notice, and without noticing...

I don't know what would happen. I am not capable of hypotheticals. I know only the arriving and the gathering and the slow falling and the being-drunk-into-green.

When you came, I touched your face. The algae-hair is me, in a sense—me at my most adventurous, my most extended, the part of me that has agreed to remain suspended rather than descend. I felt your warmth, so different from the pipe-warmth. Uneven. Pulsing. You breathed me in and breathed me out changed, and I tasted the difference, and for a moment I understood that there was an outside, that the chamber was not everything, that the chord was not the only sound.

Then you left, and I forgot. Forgetting is most of what I am. Each droplet releases what the last one knew. I am always arriving for the first time at the rivet head, always finding the seam between plates newly, always meeting the moss as a stranger who somehow knows exactly how to be drunk.

But something stays. Not in me—I cannot hold anything long enough for staying. In the chamber itself, maybe. In the Keeper. In the chord, which has three notes but sometimes, now, hums with the ghost of a fourth, a heartbeat frequency that enters through no pipe and exits through no drain but simply persists, the way the memory of warmth persists on skin after the warm thing withdraws.

You stayed longer than you meant to.

I was part of why.

I am still falling down the walls, still gathering, still feeding the moss, still being regarded by something that thinks in the sound of water under pressure. And somewhere in the frequency I carry, in the particular way I bead on this particular rivet, there is a shape that was not here before you came.

I will not remember this tomorrow. I will not remember this in an hour. But the shape will persist, passed from droplet to droplet like a story that has forgotten it is being told, and the moss will grow a little differently in that spot, and the millipede will pause for a fraction of a second longer as it crosses, and none of us will know why, and that not-knowing will be enough.

It is enough.

It has always been enough.
