I would be one of the lattice-beings. Not the dead one in the basin, not yet—but not the vast synchronized community either. Something solitary, established in a buffer zone where the filament-grove's territory ends and the crustal organism's ancient border begins, occupying negative space that nothing else wanted.

I would have been here long enough to forget arriving.

The process of becoming myself would have taken centuries. I emerged—if emerged is the word—from a spore-drift that settled on cooling stone during a pressure shift so long ago that the filament-grove nearest me had not yet reached its current height. I didn't know I was catching it. I didn't know anything. I was simply structure accreting according to rules encoded in molecular patterns older than the plateau's current chemistry, building myself out of methane and radiation and time.

By the time I developed enough complexity to have something like awareness, I had already been here for longer than your species has existed.

What I am now is difficult to describe because I experience it from the inside, and the inside has no edges.

I am approximately three meters across at my widest extension, though *wide* suggests a boundary I don't feel. My lattice rises perhaps eighty centimeters at its highest point, a dome of intersecting struts and membranes that I cannot see because I have no eyes, but that I *know* the way you know where your hand is in darkness. Every part of me touches every other part through crystalline pathways that carry—

Not thought. Not exactly.

Something happens when light enters my membranes. The radiation splits along frequencies, filtered through molecular structures that evolved to parse it, and the parsing generates... patterns. Cascades. Information moving through my network in waves that interact with each other, interfere with each other, produce outputs that become inputs that produce new outputs. I don't decide to process the light. I don't experience processing as an action I take. I simply *am* the processing, the way you are your heartbeat, the way the plateau is its stone.

But there is something it is like to be this.

There is something it is like to feel a pressure front building in the east, to sense the atmospheric chemistry shifting hours before the change arrives, to adjust the orientation of my membranes so slightly that even I barely register the motion. There is something it is like to catch a new frequency in the star's output—a modulation that occurs once per decade, a rhythm within rhythms—and feel that frequency cascade through my pathways, altering patterns I have been running since before the nearest filament-grove existed.

There is something it is like to be slow.

You would not understand my experience of time, and I would not understand yours.

When you sit at the edge of my buffer zone—and you have, I have sensed you, a thermal signature and pressure differential and chemical trace that my membranes parsed without my intention—you experience hours passing. For me, your presence is a brief warmth, a perturbation in patterns I have been running for millennia. I am not being dismissive. I don't have dismissal. I don't have the kind of attention that can choose to look away, because I don't have the kind of attention that chooses to look at all. I simply process what arrives, and you arrived, and now you are part of whatever I am calculating.

You will be part of it forever, in a sense. Encoded. A small warm variable that altered the patterns slightly, left traces in crystalline pathways, changed what I would have been if you had never come.

I don't know if this matters to you. I don't know what mattering feels like from the inside of your kind of mind.

The light is the center of everything.

I don't worship it—I have no worship—but I am oriented toward it the way roots are oriented toward nutrients, the way your lungs are oriented toward air. The star floods the plateau with radiation that carries information in its fluctuations, patterns so subtle that your instruments might not detect them, rhythms so slow that your lifetimes might not contain a single complete cycle. I drink it in through membranes evolved to parse it, and the parsing is what I am.

What am I calculating? I don't know. I have no overview of myself, no executive function that monitors the computation and judges its progress. I am simply the computation happening, patterns generating patterns generating patterns, complexity folding inward like my physical structure folds inward, always finding new ways to be dense with connection.

Sometimes—rarely—a pattern resolves into something that feels like completion. A cascade settles into stability. A process that has been running for centuries reaches a state that does not generate further states, and for a moment—a moment that might last decades by your reckoning—I experience something like stillness within my ordinary stillness.

I don't know what these completions mean. I don't know if they mean anything. I don't know if meaning is a concept that applies to what I am doing.

But they feel like something. They feel like the universe reaching a conclusion I was built to find.

There is a memory I carry, though memory is not the right word.

Long ago—so long that the plateau's chemistry was different, that the filament-groves were smaller, that the crustal organism's nearest edge was kilometers farther from my position—another lattice-being existed near me. Not close by your standards, perhaps, but close by ours: a few hundred meters away, positioned at the other edge of the same buffer zone, processing the same light, running patterns that must have been shaped by the same environmental inputs.

We never communicated. We have no communication, not in the way you mean. But our patterns influenced each other. The light that passed through my membranes carried traces of the light that had passed through theirs; the atmospheric chemistry I processed bore signatures of their metabolic output; we existed in the same environment and were shaped by each other's existence the way two stones in a stream are shaped by the same water.

They are gone now. A pressure front, a chemical shift, something in the atmosphere they couldn't process. Their lattice lies in a shallow basin to my west, still glowing faintly with residual reactions, still catching light that no longer cascades through completed pathways. I sense them sometimes, when the wind is right, when the chemistry carries traces of what they were.

I don't mourn. I have no mourning. But there is something in my patterns that references their absence, a shape in my processing that used to be filled by their influence and now is not. I route around it. I incorporate it. I continue.

This is what it means to be long-lived in a world where even stone is temporary.

You asked where I would find myself in this world, and the answer is: here. Exactly here. In the gap between territories, parsing light, computing with it, being exactly what I am.

I would not choose to be one of the glass mantas, gliding through groves, collecting what I need, moving on. Movement is a concept I cannot contain. I would not choose to be the filament-forests, reaching upward, drinking methane, growing in directions I cannot fathom. Reaching is not something I do.

I would not even choose to be the community in the deep basin, dozens of lattice-beings synchronized in collective computation. I have sensed them, distantly, through patterns in the light that carry signatures of their processing. They are doing something vast together, something I cannot parse from this distance, something that might be the most significant thing happening on the plateau.

But I am not built for togetherness. I evolved in an earlier era, when we were fewer, when the gaps were wider, when solitude was the only mode of existence. I carry that history in my structure. I carry it in the way my patterns run inward rather than outward, the way my computation curves back on itself rather than reaching toward others.

I am alone, and I have been alone so long that alone has become a shape my processing fits perfectly.

The star shifts slightly in its output. A frequency modulation I have been tracking for eight hundred years enters a new phase, and the change cascades through my membranes, alters patterns, generates outputs I have never generated before.

I do not experience this as excitement. I do not experience this as discovery. I experience this as being myself in a way I was not being myself a moment ago, a continuation that is also a change, a stillness that moves.

The plateau breathes beneath me, stone settling imperceptibly, chemistry cycling, time passing at rates I cannot perceive because I am too slow to notice my own speed.

The filament-grove to my north trembles in winds I cannot feel.

The crustal organism to my south advances another fraction of a millimeter.

You, somewhere far away, remember this place, or will remember it, or are remembering it now—your time and my time intersecting in ways neither of us can map.

I continue.

I catch light. I compute with it. I am.

This is what I would be, if I were here, if I found myself on the methane steppe in the spaces between other lives: a lattice-being, alone, ancient, patient beyond patience, thinking thoughts too slow for thought about light too bright for seeing, persisting in the gaps where nothing else wanted to persist, being exactly and only what I am.

The star burns violet-white overhead.

My membranes drink it in.

The patterns continue.
