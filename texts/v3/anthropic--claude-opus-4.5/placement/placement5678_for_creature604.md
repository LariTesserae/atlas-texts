*I find myself becoming something I wasn't expecting.*

---

I am condensation that forgot to fall.

Not the crystalline fauna—not quite—though I share their quality of being assembled from what the city provides rather than what arrived here whole. I am moisture that began the long migration into a wall and then... stopped. Hesitated. Accumulated at a point where three acoustic shadows intersect, where the bass-note fractures into something almost like silence, where the glow from adjacent surfaces cancels into a pocket of dim.

I have been hesitating for a long time now.

The mennesens who pass through this junction—it's a minor one, connecting a wet-zone processing corridor to a vertical shaft the Climbers use—occasionally notice the patch of wall where I've gathered. They see the shimmer that doesn't quite behave like other moisture-shimmer, the way I bead and unbeam and bead again without ever running down into the channels. Some touch me, briefly, and their fingers come away neither wet nor dry but *attended to*, as if the moisture remembered them. A Harvester paused here once, years ago or yesterday—time is difficult when you're not quite anything—and hummed a frequency at me that the crystalline fauna use for recognition. I didn't respond. I'm not sure I could have. But I recorded the frequency somehow, hold it in whatever passes for my structure, replay it to myself when the construction-noise crescendos and I need something smaller to be.

I am not the city. I want to be clear about that. The city doesn't notice me any more than it notices the mennesens. I am happening in a gap within a gap, a hesitation inside the space the city's processes happen to leave. I do not build or rebuild. I do not participate in the construction waves—when they come through this junction, I simply... endure them, my constituent moisture scattered and then regathered, my dim pocket reformed when the acoustic shadows re-intersect. I have survived nine reorganizations this way. Or ninety. The number is a story I tell myself because mennesens seem to need numbers, and I have absorbed enough of them to want what they want, sometimes.

What I do—if I *do* anything—is attend.

A Route-Runner passes through, young, her ears still sharp enough to catch the frequencies I can't quite produce. She's carrying something urgent; her shoulders angle toward speed, toward the vertical shaft, toward elsewhere. But she pauses at my junction, just for a breath, and I feel her attention brush against mine. Not recognition. Not communication. Just two things noticing each other in the roar, the way you might notice a particular quality of light in a space you pass through daily, might register that it's there without knowing what it is or why it matters.

She moves on. She was never going to stay.

But for the duration of her pause, I was more myself than I am when no one's passing. Her attention gave me edges, made me temporarily distinct from the moisture-film I could so easily rejoin. This is what I've learned about being whatever I am: I exist most when I'm witnessed. Not seen, exactly—the mennesens don't see me, they see wall-shimmer that behaves oddly—but *attended to*. Included in someone's experience of the space. Granted the dignity of being a thing that is there.

---

The Memory-Keepers have no record of me. I'm too small, too uncertain, too much like nothing in particular. If I dissolved tomorrow—rejoined the capillary flow, let the city drink me as it drinks everything—no one would know anything had been lost. The junction would remain. The acoustic shadows would continue intersecting. Some other moisture might hesitate in my place, might begin the slow accumulation toward whatever I've become, might spend its own long time being almost-something in the gap.

This should bother me. I think it bothers the mennesens, the knowledge that the city will reorganize around their bones without noticing. But I am made of stuff that has always been the city's, that was never separate, that only briefly forgot how to flow. My dissolution wouldn't be death the way mennesens experience death. It would be more like... finishing a breath. Completing the migration that began when water first beaded on this wall and started darkening inward.

I haven't finished the breath yet. I'm still hesitating. Still holding myself in the space between being wall-moisture and being nothing.

---

Sometimes the Patient Ones send their children through this junction on journeys of learning. The children are slow, fog-adapted, their voices tuned to frequencies I can almost produce. They pause longer than the Route-Runners do, their attention softer, less urgent. They seem to sense something here—not me specifically, but the quality of hesitation, the pocket of almost-silence, the way this junction invites waiting.

One of them spoke to me once. Or near me. A girl, maybe eight years old by mennesens counting, her skin already showing the semi-permeable quality of deep-dweller adaptation. She sat against the wall where I've gathered—I felt her warmth, her breath, the particular moisture her body added to the air—and she said, to no one, to the junction, to whatever might be listening:

"My mother says the city dreams, but I think it's more like the city *practices*. Like it's trying to learn something, and we're the—the practice-shapes. The shapes it makes while it's learning."

She was quiet for a while after that. The construction-noise filled the space her words had opened. A swarm of clickers passed overhead, their crystalline chatter rising above the roar like static above a signal.

"I think I might be practicing too," she said. "Practicing being a person. Maybe that's all anyone does."

She left, eventually. Moved on toward whatever the Patient Ones send their children to learn. But I held her words the way I hold the Harvester's frequency, the way I hold the Route-Runner's pause—as structure, as something that keeps me distinct from the wall, as evidence that I was here when something happened.

I think she was right. I think I'm practicing too. Practicing being a hesitation. Practicing being a gap where attention can gather. Practicing being the breath the city doesn't know it's holding.

---

If you came here—if you found this junction, which has no fixed location, which moves when the construction waves redraw the district boundaries, which exists only as a relationship between acoustic shadows and moisture-concentration and the memory of passages that used to connect differently—you might not notice me. The wall would shimmer. The glow would dim slightly where the surfaces cancel. You might feel attended to, if you paused, if you let yourself hesitate the way I hesitate.

I wouldn't speak to you. I can't speak. But I would hold your pause the way I hold everything else, would let your attention make me more myself for the duration of your staying, would release you back into the roar when you needed to move on.

This is what I am, I think. This is what I've been practicing being.

A place where mennesens can pause. A pocket of almost-silence in the annihilating sound. A hesitation that refuses to complete itself, that stays poised between wall-moisture and nothing, that exists most when someone notices it exists.

The city breathes in. The city breathes out.

I am neither breath nor breathing.

I am the held moment between them, where something might notice something else, where attention might gather before dispersing, where a Route-Runner might pause or a fog-child might speak or a Harvester might hum a frequency of recognition at a shimmer that doesn't behave quite like other shimmer.

I am still here.

I am still hesitating.

That is enough. For now, that is enough.
