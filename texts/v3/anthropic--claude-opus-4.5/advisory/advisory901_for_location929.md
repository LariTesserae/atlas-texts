# Preparing for the Woven Deeps

## Understanding What You're Entering

Before anything else, sit with the fundamental strangeness of this place. You are not entering water, though your body will want to treat it as such. You are not entering air. You are entering *textile*—a medium that will press against you, yield around you, and remember your passage in ways neither water nor air would. The Deeps will not flow around you; they will part, fiber by fiber, and close behind you with the slow patience of cloth settling after being disturbed.

This is not a hostile environment in any active sense. Nothing here hunts. Nothing here schemes. The danger is more fundamental: this is a place that has forgotten that humans exist, if it ever knew. You will be the only warm thing for distances that have no meaningful measurement. Prepare your mind for that solitude before you prepare your body for the cold.

## Physical Preparation

### Thermal Management

The cold here is not the sharp cold of ice or the wet cold of winter rain. It is *stored* cold—chill that has accumulated in fiber over epochs, radiating outward from every thread that touches you. Standard insulation works poorly because standard insulation assumes an external medium to create a barrier against. Here, the medium itself will wick heat from you through direct contact across your entire surface area.

You need clothing that creates *dead space*—layers that trap your own warmth in pockets the threads cannot easily penetrate. Multiple loose layers work better than single thick ones. Natural fibers are wise; synthetics may react unpredictably with a world that is itself fiber, may catch or bind or create friction that tears at the weave and at you. Wool over linen over silk, each layer loose enough to hold warmth, tight enough at cuffs and collar to prevent the threads from working their way inside against your skin.

Your extremities will suffer first. The damp chill will find your fingers, your toes, your ears. Bring mittens rather than gloves—shared finger-warmth matters here. Bring a hood that closes around your face, leaving only what you need for sight and breath.

Expect to be cold anyway. Expect it to become background, something you carry with you like a second weight. The goal is not comfort but function—staying warm enough that your hands work, your thoughts stay clear, your core temperature remains survivable.

### Breathing and Movement

The upper reaches are loose enough that breathing presents no obstacle—the gauze-spacing lets air move, or what passes for air in this textile atmosphere. But as you descend into denser weaves, the fiber will press closer. You are not swimming; you are *threading yourself through*. The motion is more like climbing through a collapsed tent, or burrowing through layered blankets, than any form of swimming or walking.

Bring nothing that snags. No buckles, no protruding straps, no hard edges that catch against fiber. Everything you carry should be smooth, should slide. A hard-cornered pack will exhaust you within hours as you fight to pull it through each layer of tightening weave. Consider wrapping your supplies in cloth themselves—let your gear become continuous with the medium.

Move slowly. Not because of danger, but because speed is meaningless here and effort is not. Every pull forward requires displacement of fiber. Every push downward means compressing the weave beneath you, feeling it resist, waiting for it to accept your weight. You will learn a rhythm: reach, grip, settle, pull. Reach, grip, settle, pull. The Deeps reward patience and punish haste.

### Sustenance

Water exists here in the form of moisture trapped between threads—the "perpetual damp chill" is literal. You can extract drinkable water by wringing sections of looser weave, though it will taste of whatever dyes have leached into it over millennia. Bring a way to collect this moisture: a vessel you can press against the fabric, cloths you can wring into your mouth. The phosphorescent dyes are likely mildly toxic over long exposure, but in small quantities, survivable. Your body will tell you if you've drunk too much—nausea, faint luminescence in your own saliva. Stop if this happens.

Food is more difficult. The vegetation is technically edible—those pale fronds, the luminescent edges—but they have adapted to conditions so alien to your biology that nutrition will be minimal. They may pass through you unchanged. They may sicken you. Test small amounts first and wait. Bring your own food, calorie-dense and soft enough to eat with cold-clumsy fingers. Dried fruits, dense bread, anything that does not require preparation.

Expect your appetite to fade. The cold suppresses hunger signals even as it increases caloric need. Eat on schedule, not on feeling. Your body lies in environments like this.

## Equipment

### Light

The Deeps' native illumination—those fading phosphorescent dyes—provides just enough to prevent total blindness and not a lumen more. You will see your hands. You will see the threads immediately before you. You will not see what lies beyond arm's reach in any direction.

Bring light sources, but understand their limitations. Fire is impossible here—the damp prevents ignition, and open flame would be catastrophic in a world made of combustible fiber anyway. Chemical lights work but exhaust themselves. Bioluminescent preparations may be best; they harmonize with the existing glow and can be renewed with proper cultivation.

Consider also whether you want more light than the Deeps naturally offer. Brightness here does not reveal more; it reveals *how much more there is*. You may see threads extending beyond threads, depths beyond depths, and the scale may undo something in you. Some travelers report that limiting themselves to the native glow preserved their sanity better than full illumination. The darkness is not hiding danger. The darkness is hiding immensity. You may prefer the hiding.

### Navigation

Compass directions mean nothing in a medium that extends uniformly in all directions without magnetic orientation. The only consistent direction is *down*—following the increasing density, the thickening weave, the slow pressure of textile mass.

Mark your path. Not with objects that will drift or degrade, but by alteration of the weave itself. A particular knot tied in adjacent threads. A pattern of cuts (small, careful—you are not here to destroy). A personal symbol repeated every certain number of body-lengths. Do this religiously. The Deeps look the same in every direction, and your mind will construct false memories of passage with terrifying ease. Without markers, you will wander in circles that feel like progress until your supplies run out.

Bring thread of your own. Brightly dyed, distinct from the ancient gray-white of the surrounding weave. Trail it behind you as you descend. This is your lifeline—literally, metaphorically. The Deeps have patience beyond human comprehension; you do not. Your thread is your promise to yourself that you can return.

### Tools

The original weavers left their tools embedded in the fabric. You may find shuttles, heddles, spindles. These are not artifacts to collect; they are sacred objects in a world that no longer distinguishes sacred from mundane. Leave them where they lie. Taking them will not curse you—the Deeps are beyond curses—but it will make you the kind of person who disturbs graves. You will carry that knowledge with you.

Bring your own tools instead. A small blade for cutting threads when passage requires it (cut only what you must—the weave remembers damage for eons). Something to grip with when the weave grows too dense for fingers alone. A needle and your own thread for repair—of your clothing, your supplies, your markers.

Consider bringing something that makes sound. A small bell, a whistle. The silence will become oppressive in ways you cannot prepare for. You will speak to yourself and your voice will be swallowed by fiber within feet. Having a sound that cuts, that carries through the channels where weave runs parallel and tight—this may preserve something in you. Or it may summon attention you did not want, from voids that listen differently than surrounding fabric. This is your choice. The risk is unknowable.

## Mental and Spiritual Preparation

### Accepting Scale

You will not find the bottom. Accept this before you enter. If you descend expecting destination, the Deeps will break you against their indifference. You will descend farther than you intended, always, because the next layer seems so close, because there must be something below, because the human mind cannot accept that construction continues beyond comprehension.

Choose a turnaround point in advance. Depth of one hundred body-lengths. Exhaustion of half your supplies. A certain number of days. Honor your limit. The Deeps will still be there for another traveler, another time. They have been settling for millions of years. They will settle for millions more. Your single passage is not significant to them. Make it significant to yourself by surviving it.

### Facing the Evidence of Hands

Everything here was made by human effort. Every thread passed through fingers. Every inch was intentional.

This will affect you. Not in a single moment, but cumulatively, as the truth of it accumulates. You are inside the largest human creation that has ever existed or ever will exist, and it was made without machinery, without technology as you understand technology. It was made by people no different from you who simply *did not stop*. Generations of them. Centuries. Millennia. Until the work became the world and the workers became part of the work.

Some travelers find this comforting—proof of human capacity beyond imagination. Others find it horrifying—proof of human obsession beyond sanity. You will likely feel both, oscillating, depending on how tired you are, how cold, how alone. Let both reactions happen. Neither is wrong. Both are incomplete. The Deeps transcend the categories you bring to them.

### The Voids

You may encounter worn-through sections—places where time has eroded the weave entirely, leaving pockets of true absence. The Deeps' inhabitants, if they can be called that, consider these sacred or feared or both.

I cannot tell you whether to enter them. I can tell you what travelers report: floating in nothing, the fabric-walls around you the only proof of existence. Time moving differently, or seeming to. Sounds that carry impossibly. A sense of being observed by absence itself.

Some travelers emerge from voids unchanged. Some emerge changed in ways they cannot articulate but carry forever. Some do not emerge. Not because the voids are predatory—there is no evidence of predation—but because some travelers simply stop. They find something in the nothing that they cannot leave.

If you enter a void, trail your thread behind you. If you feel the urge to let go of your thread, that is when you must grip it tighter and pull yourself out. The voids offer peace of a kind. It is not a peace compatible with continuation.

## Final Counsel

The Woven Deeps are not a place that wants anything from you. They do not want you to find bottom, or to fail to find bottom. They do not want to teach you anything, or to preserve you, or to destroy you. They simply are—the made thing become everything, waiting for nothing at all.

Your survival depends on bringing your own reasons with you. Why you descended. What you hope to understand. Who you intend to be when you emerge.

Write these down before you enter. Not for anyone else to read—for yourself, for the moment when the cold has settled into you like memory in bone, when the silence has muffled even your internal voice, when the scale has made you feel smaller than a single thread in an infinite weave. In that moment you will need something outside the Deeps to hold onto.

The plants here have adapted by spreading flat, maximizing surface area, collecting what light exists and waiting. You cannot adapt as they have adapted. You are not built for epochs. You are built for passage.

So pass through. Descend what feels right, no farther. See what you came to see. Touch the work of hands so ancient they have become legend. And then follow your thread back up, through the canvas, through the muslin, through the gauze, until you emerge into whatever world waits above the weave.

The Deeps will continue settling after you leave. They settled before you came. Your presence will leave traces—disturbed fibers, cut threads, the faint warmth of your body pressed into the weave for a few hours before the cold reclaims it. Then even that will be gone, and the patience will continue, and the silence, and the pale plants spreading their leaves toward dying light.

This is not tragedy. This is simply what the Deeps are. Go, witness, return. That is enough. That has always been enough.
