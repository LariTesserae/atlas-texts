# Preparing for the Calibration Hollow

## Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before anything else, sit with the description and let it settle. This is not a place that wants to harm you, but it is also not a place that was made for you. You are, at best, a variable it will incorporate. At worst, an irritant it will work around. The Hollow does not care about your comfort, your curiosity, or your survival—it cares about its function, whatever that function is. Your preparation should begin with accepting this fundamental indifference.

---

## Physical Preparation

### The Altitude Problem

Seven thousand meters is not survivable for most humans without acclimatization or supplemental oxygen. You will need both, and you will need to take the altitude seriously even if you have climbed before. The Hollow's engineered microclimate maintains breathable conditions inside, but the approach will try to kill you in ordinary ways—pulmonary edema, cerebral edema, the slow stupid death of someone who thought they were stronger than atmospheric pressure.

Spend at least two weeks acclimatizing at progressively higher camps. Bring supplemental oxygen for the final approach and keep a backup supply cached at the Hollow's entrance. The thin air outside will be waiting for you when you leave, and you may not be thinking clearly enough to remember this.

### Thermal Considerations

The Hollow holds at forty-one degrees Celsius—hot bath temperature, sustainable but uncomfortable for extended stays. Outside, at this altitude, temperatures will plunge well below freezing. You need layered clothing that can handle both extremes, with the outer layers easily removable and storable. Moisture-wicking base layers are essential; the condensation cycle will coat you regardless of what you do, and wet fabric against skin becomes dangerous the moment you step outside.

Bring more water than you think you need. The heat inside will dehydrate you faster than you expect, and the alkaline condensate is not a reliable hydration source—the mineral content may upset your stomach or worse if consumed in quantity.

### The Approach

The ridge is young volcanic rock, sharp enough to cut. Bring heavy gloves, reinforced clothing for your forearms and shins, and move slowly. A laceration at this altitude, in this isolation, with limited oxygen and no easy evacuation, can become life-threatening through simple blood loss or infection. Bring a comprehensive first aid kit weighted toward wound care: clotting agents, butterfly closures, irrigation syringes, broad-spectrum antibiotics.

## Equipment and Instrumentation

### What to Bring

Bring instruments, but bring them with humility. The description makes clear that conventional sensors will not register the pulse's nature. Bring them anyway—a radiation detector, a magnetometer, thermal cameras, audio recording equipment across multiple frequency ranges. Even negative data is data. Document what your instruments *don't* detect; the shape of the absence may tell you something.

Bring a mechanical watch alongside any digital timekeeping. If the Hollow affects electronics—and it may, given the light's strange behavior—you need a backup that runs on springs and gears. Note any discrepancies between them.

Bring a physical notebook and graphite pencils. If your electronics fail or behave unpredictably, you need a recording method that cannot be disrupted. Write down everything, even observations that seem trivial. The patterns here repeat over hours; you may not recognize significance until you can review your notes later.

Bring a camera, but do not trust it. The lichen "doesn't photograph well"—this is a warning. The Hollow may not render accurately through lenses, digital or analog. Sketch what you see. Your hand and eye may capture what your camera cannot.

### What Not to Bring

Do not bring anything you cannot afford to lose or leave behind. The artifacts are half-buried in young rock "as if they grew there or were always meant to be found." Consider the possibility that items you bring may become artifacts themselves—incorporated, integrated, impossible to remove. Bring equipment you are prepared to abandon.

Do not bring weapons. This is not a combat situation, and the Hollow's awareness—its accounting for your presence—may respond poorly to threat displays. You are a guest in a machine you do not understand. Act accordingly.

## Behavioral Protocols

### Movement and Presence

Move slowly. The Hollow adjusts to your presence like a thermostat adjusting to an opened door. Sudden movements, loud sounds, rapid changes in your position may trigger larger adjustments than you want. Enter with the energy of someone visiting a sleeping animal—respectful, quiet, aware that you could wake something.

Do not touch the central structure. Not yet, not on your first visit, possibly not ever. Observe it from a distance that feels comfortable and then add another meter. The material that has "no texture at all" and makes your eyes slide off is not inviting contact. Trust this instinct.

The artifacts are a more complicated question. They appear to have been left to be found—but found by whom? For what purpose? If you choose to examine them, do so gently, one at a time, with long pauses between. Note any changes in the Hollow's behavior: shifts in the condensation cycle, variations in the pulse, changes in light behavior. If the Hollow reacts, stop immediately and wait for it to stabilize before deciding whether to continue.

### Duration of Stay

Your first visit should be brief—perhaps two hours, no more. This is reconnaissance. You are mapping the Hollow's baseline behavior and your own baseline responses. Note your emotional state, your physical sensations, any intrusive thoughts or unusual cognitive patterns. The Hollow may affect consciousness in ways that are not immediately apparent.

Subsequent visits can be longer, but build up gradually. The pulse patterns repeat over hours; to observe a full cycle, you may need to stay six, eight, twelve hours. Bring food that requires no preparation, water in quantities sufficient for the duration, and a plan for sleep if you intend to observe through a full pattern repetition. Do not sleep directly beneath the central structure.

### If the Hollow Seems to React to You

The description suggests the Hollow "adjusts" to presence—a two-degree temperature variation, shifts in the pulse pattern. Pay attention to these adjustments. If they seem to correlate with your actions, you have information about how the Hollow perceives you.

If adjustments become more dramatic—larger temperature swings, light behavior that makes navigation difficult, changes in the sound that cause physical discomfort—treat this as a request to leave. Do not argue with the thermostat. Exit calmly, wait outside (in your thermal layers, with supplemental oxygen) for at least an hour, then reassess.

If the Hollow's adjustments seem to *facilitate* your presence—temperature shifts toward a more comfortable range, light behavior that illuminates what you're examining, pulse patterns that seem to slow when you're taking notes—you may be receiving an invitation to stay longer, observe more closely, engage more deeply. Accept this invitation cautiously. An invitation is not the same as safety.

## Psychological Preparation

### Accepting Incompleteness

You will not understand the Hollow. Not on your first visit, probably not on your tenth. The equations on the vellum-like page use symbols you will recognize alongside symbols you won't. This is the Hollow's nature: partial comprehensibility, enough familiarity to suggest meaning, not enough to confirm it.

Prepare yourself for this frustration. Bring the patience to observe without understanding, to document without interpreting, to sit with mystery rather than forcing premature conclusions. The Hollow has been here, doing whatever it does, for some time—possibly longer than the young volcano that houses it, depending on what "young" means in this context. It is not going anywhere. You can return. You can bring others with different expertise. You do not need to solve it today.

### The Question of Purpose

You will be tempted to ask what the Hollow is *for*. This is a natural question, but it may not be the right one. Consider: what is a tide pool for? What is a thermal vent for? The Hollow may be a node in a larger system, or it may be the entire system. It may be calibrating something, or it may have finished calibrating long ago and now simply persists. It may be waiting for something that will never come, or it may be actively engaged in processes invisible to you.

The purpose question can become an obsession. Allow yourself to ask it, but do not allow the asking to consume your attention. Observe what is, not what you imagine should be.

### The Awareness Question

The Hollow seems aware of your presence—not watching, just accounting. This is an unsettling form of awareness because it implies intelligence without intention toward you specifically. You are a factor, not a focus.

Some travelers find this comforting: the Hollow does not mean you harm because it does not mean you anything. Others find it deeply disturbing: to be beneath notice while clearly being noticed is a particular kind of loneliness.

Know which response you tend toward and prepare accordingly. If you find comfort in indifference, the Hollow may be a peaceful place for you. If you find indifference distressing, limit your exposure and bring a companion—not into the Hollow necessarily, but nearby, someone you can speak to afterward about being a person in a human way.

## Contingency Planning

### Communication

Establish a check-in schedule with someone who is not making this journey. Every twelve hours at minimum, more frequently if possible. Agree on a protocol: if you miss two consecutive check-ins, they should assume something has gone wrong and initiate whatever rescue or recovery plan you've arranged.

The Hollow may interfere with electronic communication. Bring a satellite messenger as primary, a personal locator beacon as backup, and establish visual signals (smoke, mirror flashes) as a tertiary option for anyone approaching from below.

### Extraction

Know your exit route and keep it unobstructed. The Hollow has only one entrance; if something blocks it—rockfall, equipment failure, your own incapacitation—you have no alternative. Keep your heaviest gear near the entrance, not scattered throughout the space. Position yourself so you can reach the exit within seconds if necessary.

If you become incapacitated inside the Hollow, rescue will be extremely difficult. The altitude, the remote location, the sharp volcanic rock—extraction teams will face all the same challenges you did, with the added complication of carrying an unresponsive person. Make your peace with this risk before you enter. The Hollow is not a place to become helpless.

### If You Cannot Leave

There is a scenario you must consider: the Hollow may not let you leave. Not through malice, but through adjustment—temperature changes that make the exit dangerous, light behavior that makes navigation impossible, some other mechanism you haven't anticipated. If this happens:

Do not panic. The Hollow is accounting for you; panic may trigger further adjustments.

Move to the entrance and wait. The Hollow's patterns repeat; a cycle that closes the exit may eventually reopen it.

If waiting does not work, if the exit remains inaccessible, you are in a situation with limited options. Signal for help if you can. Ration your supplies. Document everything—if you do not survive, your notes may help whoever finds them understand what happened.

This is an extreme scenario, probably unlikely. But the Hollow is not a known quantity, and you must make peace with the full range of possibilities before you enter.

---

## Final Thoughts

The Calibration Hollow is not hostile, but it is not hospitable either. It is a place that exists for its own reasons, running its own processes, and you are choosing to insert yourself into those processes as an observer. This is a privilege of the multiverse traveler—to witness things not meant for human eyes—but privileges come with responsibilities.

Be humble. Be patient. Be prepared for wonder and prepared for nothing at all. The Hollow may reveal itself to you, or it may simply continue its ancient rhythm as if you were never there. Both outcomes are valid. Both outcomes are worth the journey.

Document what you see. Share what you learn. And when you leave—*if* you leave—carry the memory of that pulse in your teeth, that thrumming that suggested intention. You have stood inside a single room of something much larger. That is more than most will ever know.
