# A Preparation Guide for Keth-Sulom

## Understanding What You're Facing

Before anything else, sit with the description you've received. Read it more than once. Let the strangeness settle into your thinking before you try to plan around it.

You are not visiting a place that operates by broken rules—you are visiting a place that operates by *different* rules, ones that happen to be internally consistent in ways you won't immediately grasp. This distinction matters. Broken things can be worked around. Different things must be learned.

The city exists without foundation or summit. Directions that feel like "up" are most directions. The towers breathe and the streets rearrange themselves. The light has no source. The creatures belong there and you do not.

Accept this. Not as metaphor, not as exaggeration, but as operational reality. Your preparation begins with accepting that your preparation will be incomplete.

## Physical Preparation

### Hydration and Moisture

The city will claim the water you bring. This is described not as theft but as tribute—a slow, patient extraction that will crack your lips if you stand still too long, that will draw your skin tight against bone.

**Bring more water than you think you need, then bring more.**

- Sealed containers, multiple redundant vessels
- Water stored in different locations on your person, so that if the city's geometry separates you from your pack, you still have something
- Consider carrying water in forms the city might not immediately recognize as moisture—sealed gel packets, hydration compounds that require mixing
- Understand that rationing may work differently here; the extraction seems tied to stillness, so you may lose more water resting than walking

**Protect your moisture from the air:**
- Barrier creams for skin, applied liberally and reapplied often
- Cloth wrappings for extremities
- A face covering that traps exhaled breath, allowing you to re-inhale some of your own humidity
- Sealed goggles or eye protection—dry air will damage eyes over extended exposure

**Recognize the warning signs:**
- Cracked lips are the first indicator
- Skin that feels papery or drum-tight
- Thoughts that become dusty, slow, mineral
- Leave before you begin to feel like you belong to the chalk

### Nutrition

The description mentions no food sources whatsoever. The city contains no market stalls, no debris, nothing organic except the pale watching creatures and yourself.

- Pack calorie-dense foods that require no water to prepare
- Avoid anything that makes you thirsty
- Consider foods that contain moisture as a secondary water source
- Pack enough for twice as long as you intend to stay, because time and distance are unreliable here

### Physical Conditioning

This is not a tourist destination. You will be walking on stone, climbing stairs that go sideways, navigating architecture that rearranges itself between heartbeats.

- Your legs will bear the worst of it; train for sustained walking on hard surfaces
- Your ankles need to be reliable; the geometry will ask strange things of your balance
- Core strength matters when directions become negotiable
- Cardiovascular endurance for the thin, dry air that will make your lungs work harder

## Equipment and Gear

### What to Bring

**Navigation and marking:**
Here is the hard truth—navigation may be fundamentally impossible. The streets rewrite themselves with every breath. A doorway you passed may now face elsewhere. A stairway may now lead nowhere.

But bring marking tools anyway:
- Chalk, though it will blend with the pale stone
- Colored markers that will stand out against bone-white walls
- Ribbons or fabric scraps to tie to architecture
- Understand that your marks may end up on walls you've never touched, or vanish into stone that has merged with itself

Keep a journal. Write down not what you see, but how you feel as you move through spaces. Note which directions feel *more up* than others. Record your internal compass even when external references fail. You are training yourself to navigate by intuition because that may be all that works.

**Light sources:**
The amber diffuse glow is permanent but dim. You cast no shadow. Consider:
- A personal light source to create shadows, to remind yourself of the physics you came from
- Multiple redundant lights—the city may take interest in unfamiliar phenomena
- Something that attaches to your body and cannot be set down and lost

**Shelter and rest:**
- A sleeping system that can be deployed anywhere, because "anywhere" is all you'll have
- Something to cover your eyes when resting—the sourceless light never changes, and your body will need darkness cues
- Consider a small tent or bivouac that creates a microenvironment of familiar air

**Sound and signal:**
- Bells or noisemakers that you can leave behind as auditory markers—if you hear your own signal, you've looped back
- Something loud enough to hear from far away, in case you discover there is someone to signal
- Something quiet, personal, grounding—music or a recorded voice, something that reminds you what your world sounds like

### What to Leave Behind

**Mortar, adhesive, anything meant to fix stone in place:**
The towers breathe. The stones merge. The city processes. Do not bring anything that tries to stop this—you will either fail completely or succeed in ways that draw unwanted attention.

**Precise measuring equipment:**
Your measurements will not remain accurate. Rulers will lie. Compasses will spin or point in directions that are not directions. Bring them if you must, but do not trust them.

**Weapons:**
I say this not because violence is forbidden, but because there is nothing described that violence would help with. The watching creatures do not threaten. The city itself cannot be fought. You will only burden yourself and signal a posture that may be read as something you do not intend.

## Mental and Psychological Preparation

This section matters more than gear.

### Relearning Direction

Practice, before you go, existing in spaces where up is uncertain. Spend time in darkness. Spend time inverted. Train your vestibular system to tolerate confusion, and train your mind to stay calm when the signals from your body stop making sense.

More importantly: practice *not needing* directions to resolve. Sit with uncertainty. Let yourself not know which way you're facing, and do not scramble to figure it out. The anxiety of disorientation is what will exhaust you in Keth-Sulom—not the disorientation itself.

### Accepting Alien Attention

The pale creatures watch. They note. They file you away in some ledger you cannot read.

This is not malevolent. It is also not benevolent. It is *attention*, from beings whose experience you cannot share, for purposes you cannot know.

Practice being observed without needing to understand why. Practice being catalogued without demanding to see the catalogue. Many people find this deeply uncomfortable—the sense that they are being processed by something they cannot process in return. If you cannot tolerate this, you cannot tolerate Keth-Sulom.

Do not attempt to interact with the creatures. Do not flee from them. Do not ignore them so hard you lose track of where they are. Acknowledge their presence. Note them as they note you. This is as close as you will get to relationship here.

### The Breathing

This is the key skill.

You must learn to feel the breathing of the towers. You must learn to time your movements to it—slipping between spaces when they're widest, holding still when the architecture contracts.

I cannot tell you how to learn this. The description says "after a while, you find you can." This suggests it is possible. This suggests it is also not immediate.

So: expect a learning period where you move wrongly, where you are caught in contracting spaces, where the city's rearrangement leaves you somewhere you did not intend. Stay calm. Breathe yourself. Perhaps—and this is speculation—the city responds to breath, and matching your own respiration to what you feel around you helps you perceive the larger rhythm.

Practice stillness before you go. Practice patience. Practice waiting for the right moment without knowing when it will come. This is what navigating Keth-Sulom requires.

### Solitude and Silence

There is no one here except you, the creatures, and the city. The sounds are distant and stop when you listen. The light is sourceless. The architecture is populated only by more architecture.

This will erode you if you let it.

Bring something of yourself. A talisman. A photograph. A recording of a voice that matters to you. Something that reminds you that you exist independently of this place, that you came from somewhere that makes different sense.

Talk to yourself. Say your own name out loud periodically. The silence will try to become natural, and if you let it, you may begin to forget that you are something other than a feature of the city, walking through itself.

## Practical Strategies

### Movement and Timing

Walk with purpose, but hold that purpose lightly. You cannot navigate *to* a specific destination—the destination may move before you arrive—but you can navigate *through* the space in a direction that feels consistent.

When you feel the contraction, stop. Stand still. Let the geometry settle around you. When you feel expansion, move. Move quickly but not frantically. You are working with the breathing, not against it.

Do not run unless you are certain the space ahead will remain open. Running through a contracting passage is how you become part of the architecture.

### Shelter and Rest

Find spaces that feel stable—where the breathing is slower, gentler, or where the geometry has reached some local equilibrium. The courtyards, perhaps, or the interior chambers nested within chambers.

Do not sleep in doorways or staircases. Do not sleep near the places where towers pass through each other. Do not sleep where you can see the flickering cross-sections of reality asserting itself.

Rest in small increments rather than long sleeps. You cannot trust the city to be the same when you wake.

### Interacting with the City

The description suggests the city may be attentive, may appreciate those who move in time with its breathing. This is not permission to ask for favors, but it may be permission to offer respect.

Move carefully. Do not damage the stone—not because damage is possible, but because the attempt signals disrespect. Do not try to wedge doors open or block passages from contracting. Accept the gifts of wider spaces when they come; accept the constraints of narrower ones.

If you find yourself in an impossible geometry—a stairway that loops, a doorway that opens onto a view from elsewhere—do not panic. Observe. Try to understand what the space is doing. Then move through it as if it makes sense, and it may begin to.

### Leaving

This is the question I cannot answer.

The city has no foundation and no summit. It continues in all directions that are up. There is no described exit, no edge, no boundary where Keth-Sulom ends and something else begins.

You must find your own way out. Perhaps the way you came in still exists, somewhere in the breathing maze. Perhaps there is another exit, up or down or in a direction you haven't learned yet. Perhaps leaving requires the same intuition as moving through—a sense of when the city releases, when a passage opens into somewhere that is not more city.

Do not assume you can leave. Do not assume you cannot. Stay long enough to learn what you need to learn, and begin trying to leave before you've forgotten why you would want to.

## What You Cannot Prepare For

The builders are absent. The purpose is unknown. The processes continue without explanation.

You may never understand why the towers breathe. You may never learn who fitted the impossible arches, who carved the chisel marks into geometry that should not permit masonry. You may never know if the city is functioning or malfunctioning, computing or dreaming.

You cannot prepare for answers you may never receive. You can only prepare to exist in the presence of questions.

Bring humility. Bring wonder. Bring the acceptance that some things will remain mysterious no matter how long you walk the bone-white streets.

The city will note your passage. The creatures will watch. The towers will keep breathing.

Walk anyway. Walk in time with the breath if you can. And remember that you are made of water, that you came from somewhere that has shadows, that you are not yet stone, not yet architecture, not yet something that belongs to Keth-Sulom forever.

That remembering may be the most important preparation of all.
