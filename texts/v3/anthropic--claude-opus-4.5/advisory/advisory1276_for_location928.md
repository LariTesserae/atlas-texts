# Preparing for the Nursery Vaults of Keth-Somnar

## Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before anything else, sit with the reality of this place. Keth-Somnar is not hostile, but it is profoundly alien in ways that will challenge you. This is a world that has never known a sun—not lost one, never had one. The inhabitants evolved or adapted to conditions that have no parallel in solar-system life. You are visiting what amounts to a planetary-scale nursery, a civilization that has organized itself entirely around the raising of young whose lifecycle you cannot fully perceive.

You are not the point here. The larvae are the point. Everything and everyone else exists in service to them. This is not a tourist destination that has learned to accommodate visitors; this is a working facility that may or may not have protocols for your presence. Approach with appropriate humility.

---

## Thermal Protection: Your First Priority

The cold of Keth-Somnar is not winter cold. It is the cold of interstellar space, moderated only slightly by internal planetary heat and the metabolic output of billions of organisms. Your standard thermal layer will help, but you need to think in terms of *sustained* exposure to temperatures that want to pull heat from your body through every available pathway.

**Layer strategically.** Your base layer should wick moisture while retaining heat—you will sweat when exerting yourself, and that sweat will kill you if it's allowed to cool against your skin. Your middle insulation layer needs to handle the specific challenge of *wet cold*; the atmosphere here is saturated with moisture from the thermal aquifers, and many insulating materials lose effectiveness when damp. Research materials designed for deep-cave expeditions or submarine environments. Your outer shell needs to breathe while blocking convective heat loss—those pressure changes that carry denser, colder gas down from the upper atmosphere will strip warmth from you if you're not prepared.

**Protect your extremities obsessively.** Heated gloves and boots with independent power supplies. A face covering that warms inhaled air before it reaches your lungs. The cold here is patient, as the description says. It will wait for you to make a mistake.

**Bring backup heating.** Chemical heat packs, a compact heating element, something that can warm a small shelter space if your primary thermal systems fail. Hypothermia on Keth-Somnar would be a slow, quiet death, and rescue may not come quickly.

## Atmospheric Considerations

The description suggests a breathable atmosphere—convection currents, pressure changes, weather fronts—but don't assume human-compatible. Research the specific composition before arrival. Even if the oxygen content is adequate, you may face:

- **Trace gases** that are toxic at low concentrations or that become problematic over extended exposure
- **Pressure variations** that could cause discomfort or physiological stress during weather events
- **Humidity levels** that interfere with respiration or promote fungal/bacterial growth in your equipment

Pack a respirator system with multiple filter types. Even if you don't need it for atmospheric content, the constant moisture may make it useful for warming and conditioning inhaled air. Bring a compact atmospheric analyzer and check readings regularly—conditions may vary significantly between the transit ridges, the well depths, and enclosed spaces.

## Sensory Challenges: Light and Sound

### The Light Situation

You will be operating in bioluminescent conditions that your eyes did not evolve for. The light here is generated by living organisms, which means it's *variable*—it responds to conditions, to stimuli, to the collective behavior of millions of creatures. When the larvae brightened in response to the cold front, when they all oriented toward that distant movement, the lighting conditions in the entire city shifted.

Your eyes will struggle. The color described as sitting "between ultraviolet and visible spectrum" suggests emission frequencies that will cause eye strain at minimum, possible damage with extended exposure. Pack:

- **Filtering eyewear** that can block UV and near-UV while transmitting visible light. Adjustable filtering would be ideal, allowing you to adapt to changing conditions.
- **Backup lighting** for situations where the bioluminescence dims or you need reliable, stable illumination for detailed work. Use this sparingly and thoughtfully—you don't know how the larvae or tenders will respond to artificial light sources.
- **Eye drops and care supplies.** Your eyes are going to water. The description mentions this directly. Bring adequate supplies for daily eye care over the duration of your visit.

Consider that your color perception will be compromised throughout your stay. You will be seeing a shifted, filtered version of reality, and some things—including possibly important social or safety signals—may be in wavelengths you cannot perceive at all.

### The Sound Situation

This is where preparation becomes truly critical.

The larval chorus is described as constant, massive, layered, and operating partly through frequencies that "bypass your ears and settle directly into your chest cavity." This is infrasound and vibration, transmitted through the mucoid medium, through the basite infrastructure, through your bones. You cannot escape it by covering your ears. You cannot turn it off. It doesn't stop.

**Understand what this will do to you.** Sustained infrasound exposure can cause:

- Anxiety and feelings of unease
- Nausea and disorientation
- Fatigue and difficulty sleeping
- Difficulty concentrating
- In extreme cases, hallucinations or panic responses

The effect is often subtle and cumulative. You may not realize how much it's affecting you until you've been compromised.

**Prepare countermeasures:**

- Research active noise-cancellation systems designed for infrasound and vibration. Standard audio earplugs will not help.
- Consider vibration-dampening insoles and base layers to reduce transmission through physical contact with surfaces.
- Plan for regular breaks in whatever space offers the most isolation from the chorus. Your transit ridge position may actually be better than descending into the wells, but investigate the tender quarters or other structures that might provide respite.
- Practice meditation or other techniques for managing psychological stress before arrival. You need tools for staying centered when your environment is constantly trying to shake you loose.
- Set up a buddy system if traveling with others—agree to check in regularly and watch each other for signs of infrasound-related distress.

**Also prepare for the emotional content.** The larvae are communicating. You don't understand what they're saying, but your body may respond anyway. Some people find such experiences profound and moving; others find them deeply disturbing. Know yourself. If you're prone to emotional overwhelm, plan accordingly.

## Navigating the Physical Environment

### The Terrain

You'll be moving across transit ridges, platforms, suspension bridges, and possibly descending into the well structures. The description emphasizes:

- **Wet surfaces everywhere.** The constant seep of thermal aquifer water means everything is damp at minimum. Footwear must provide excellent wet traction on basite, which you should assume is smooth and engineered rather than naturally textured.
- **Bridges that sway** in convection currents. If you have issues with motion sensitivity or vertigo, this will be challenging. The bridges appear functional and well-maintained, but they are not static.
- **Variable footing** on newer structures where the basite is "still rough, still porous." These edges may be less stable or predictable than established infrastructure.

Pack mobility aids appropriate to your needs—trekking poles, climbing equipment, harnesses—and consider that rescue services as you understand them may not exist here.

### The Mucoid Medium

If you descend into the Gestation Wells, you may come into contact with the mucoid medium that the larvae live in. You need to know:

- Is it safe for human skin contact? For how long?
- Is it toxic if inhaled or ingested?
- Will it damage your equipment or clothing?
- What are the cultural protocols around touching it or contaminating it?

Don't assume it's merely "water with stuff in it." It "weeps constantly from the well walls" and maintains conditions for creatures that pupate partly in dimensions you can't perceive. It may have properties you cannot anticipate. Bring protective barriers—waterproof outer layers, sealed gloves, face protection—and err strongly on the side of not touching it until you know more.

### The Things Beyond the City's Edge

"Something large" moved in the darkness, and the larvae all oriented toward it in unison. This is information about predators, megafauna, or other entities that exist in the surrounding environment. You need to know:

- What is out there?
- Does it enter the city?
- What behaviors attract or deter it?
- What should you do if you encounter it?

Do not venture beyond the city's lit perimeter without understanding what you might meet. This is not an idle concern—the city has edges, the darkness is real, and something lives in it.

## Interacting with the Inhabitants

### The Tenders

The tenders are your primary point of contact with Keth-Somnaran society. Key observations from the description:

- They move in choreographed patterns that become visible only with sustained observation. This suggests organized, purposeful activity—they're not random, and they're not idle.
- They carry tools that "work by convincing matter to rearrange itself." Their technology operates on principles you don't understand and possibly can't understand.
- Their bodies are matte black, absorbing light, with eyes that glow with captured luminescence. Visual signaling may work differently than you expect.
- They are entirely focused on care work. This is what they do. Your visit is, at best, a minor distraction from their primary purpose.

**Communication:** Research this extensively before arrival. Can they perceive and produce sounds in human-audible ranges? Do they use visual signals? Is there a translation technology or protocol? You cannot assume you can simply "talk to them."

**Cultural protocols:** What are the rules for visitors? Where can you go, what can you touch, what questions are appropriate? The tenders' entire civilization is built around protecting vulnerable young—they may be extremely cautious about unfamiliar presences in or near the nursery spaces.

**Your role:** You are probably an observer at most, not a participant. Do not attempt to assist with tender work unless explicitly invited and trained. Do not interfere with larvae under any circumstances. Do not touch the dead larvae that still glow—they are being taken somewhere for a reason.

### The Larvae

The larvae are not just animals. They communicate in complex patterns, they respond collectively to stimuli, they are the valued center of an entire civilization. Treat them with the respect you would give to any sentient beings, even if their sentience operates in ways you cannot perceive.

Do not attempt to communicate directly with larvae without guidance from tenders. Do not touch them. Do not expose them to your artificial light sources, your equipment, or your biological contaminants. You do not know how fragile they are or what might harm them.

Remember that some larvae die, and when they do, they are processed elsewhere. You may witness death here. Prepare yourself emotionally for that possibility.

## Equipment Checklist

### Essential Survival Gear
- Multi-layer thermal protection system with backup heating elements
- Atmospheric analyzer and filtration respirator
- UV-filtering eyewear with adjustable transmission
- Backup light sources (use only when necessary)
- Vibration-dampening materials and infrasound countermeasures
- Wet-traction footwear and mobility aids
- Waterproof outer layers and sealed gloves for mucoid medium contact
- Emergency shelter materials rated for sustained cold
- Nutrition and hydration supplies for extended stay (you cannot assume local food is safe or available)
- Water purification (the thermal aquifer water may not be potable)
- Comprehensive first aid, including supplies for eye care, cold injury, and psychological distress

### Documentation and Communication
- Recording equipment that functions in high-humidity, variable-light conditions
- Translation or communication technology if available
- Detailed notes on cultural protocols and emergency contacts
- Backup copies of all critical information in waterproof storage

### Psychological Preparation
- Meditation or grounding practices
- Comfort items appropriate to your personal needs
- Clear plans for managing overwhelm, isolation, and sensory distress
- If traveling with others, established check-in protocols and support agreements

## Mental and Emotional Preparation

You are going to a place of profound darkness, relentless cold, and constant alien sound. You will be surrounded by creatures whose nature you cannot fully comprehend, in a civilization organized around values you may not share. The environment is wet, the light is strange, and something large moves in the darkness beyond the city's edge.

This can be beautiful. The description conveys wonder as much as alienation—the choreography of the tenders, the glow of thousands of larvae singing together, the slow growth of a city that builds itself. This is a place where life persists against the cold and dark, where care is the organizing principle of an entire world.

But it will also be hard. You will be uncomfortable much of the time. You will not understand things that seem important. You will feel small and irrelevant in the face of processes that have continued for countless generations without you and will continue long after you leave.

**Practice sitting with discomfort before you go.** Spend time in darkness. Spend time in cold. Listen to unfamiliar sounds and resist the urge to make them stop. Get comfortable with not understanding. The more you can accept these experiences without fighting them, the more capacity you'll have for actually engaging with Keth-Somnar.

**Know your limits.** There is no shame in deciding this place is not for you, or in leaving early if your wellbeing requires it. Interstellar travel presumably offers many wonders; not all of them need to be yours.

**Hold your expectations loosely.** You don't know what you'll find meaningful here. You don't know what will change you. Go with curiosity rather than agenda, and let the experience be what it is.

---

## Final Thoughts

The Nursery Vaults of Keth-Somnar are fundamentally a place of care. Whatever else is strange or difficult, that is the heart of it: an entire civilization devoted to nurturing the next generation through a metamorphosis that spans dimensions you cannot see. You are being allowed to witness something sacred to the beings who live there.

Witness well. Tread lightly. Keep yourself safe so that you can bring what you've seen back to wherever you came from.

And when the larvae sing and turn as one toward something moving in the darkness—pay attention. Even if you don't understand, even if you never understand, something is being communicated. Being present for that is a gift.
