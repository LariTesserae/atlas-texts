# The Keepers of the Dry Stone

They are not awake in the way you understand waking.

The first one you encounter—though *encounter* suggests a meeting, and this is more of a gradual realization—is pressed into the corner where two walls join a ceiling. It has been there long enough that the condensation flows around it the same way it flows around the fountain: a body-shaped absence in the constant wet. Its skin, if it is skin, has taken on the texture of the dark stone, though when you look directly at it you can see the faintest movement, a slow pulse that takes nearly a minute to complete its cycle.

It is not sleeping. It is not dead. It is *processing*, the same way the city processes, running at a speed that makes your heartbeat seem frenetic, violent, impossibly rushed.

You realize you have been looking at them for hours without seeing them. They are in the walls. In the spaces between buildings. Curled in the dry hollows beneath bridges where the water cannot reach. Always in the dry places—not because they fear the water, but because they are *continuous* with the fountain, with the refusal, with whatever principle keeps that basin empty against all the pressure of the endless wet.

---

Their bodies suggest something that was once more differentiated. You can trace, if you look long enough, the memory of limbs—four, or six, or perhaps four that each bifurcated at some point in the long settling. The bifurcations have fused back together in most of them. They are returning to a simpler shape, the way the stones have lost their edges, the way the water has learned its patterns. Evolution here runs backward, or sideways, or in directions that have nothing to do with survival and everything to do with *fitting*.

They fit the dry places. That is their function: to occupy the spaces where water cannot go, to ## the fountain's emptiness with their presence, to be the warm nodes in the wet network. The water does not avoid the fountain because of the fountain. The water avoids the fountain because *they* are continuous with it, and they have been here long enough that the water remembers them the way it remembers the shape it once held.

They are the memory. They are what the water is trying to forget.

---

The one in the corner has opened something that might be an eye.

It does not look at you—you are moving too fast for it to track, a blur of motion across a visual field adapted to geological time. But it has registered you, or will register you, or is currently in the process of registering you through senses that operate on a different schedule than your own. By the time its perception completes, you will have aged. Not much. A few hours, perhaps. But you feel the attention like a weight, like the air growing thicker around you, like the heat increasing by a single degree.

It is tasting you through the vapor. Your breath, your sweat, the moisture you exhale with each too-quick heartbeat. You are a burst of data, a spike in the signal, something the city will need to process for years after you leave. If you leave. If leaving is a thing that happens here.

---

There are more of them near the fountain.

Not around it—they do not gather, do not cluster, do not do anything so active as *approaching*. But the density of dry places increases as you move toward the plaza's center, and each dry place holds one of them, folded into angles that should not accommodate a body, pressed into crevices that seem to have been carved for exactly their shape. Or perhaps their shapes have been carved by the crevices. The causality here flows in both directions and neither.

The ones near the fountain are older. You can tell by their simplicity—fewer traces of what they might once have been, more integration with the stone that holds them. The oldest one, pressed into a crack at the fountain's base, is barely distinguishable from the rock itself. Only the pulse gives it away, that slow rhythm that you have started to feel in your own chest, a counter-beat to your heart that pulls at your tempo, trying to slow you down, trying to teach you the pace of this place.

You understand, suddenly, that they did not build the city. The city built them. Or they are building the city, still, through the slow pressure of their presence, through the weight of their refusal to be wet. The fountain is dry because they are dry. The channels curve because they curve them, have been curving them, will curve them, for as long as the water runs.

---

One of them moves.

The movement takes eleven hours, and you watch all of it, unable to look away, your own metabolism slowing in sympathy or submission. An arm—if it is an arm—unfolds from a torso—if it is a torso—and extends toward a pool of water that has been creeping closer to its hollow. The water retreats. Not quickly, not dramatically, but definitely: a slight recession, a new curve in its boundary, a small adjustment to the flow patterns that will persist long after the arm has returned to its resting position.

They are gardening. That is the word that surfaces now. They tend the water the way a gardener tends soil, shaping its paths, teaching it where to go and where not to go. The channels that wind through the city are not architecture—they are cultivation, the slow patient work of bodies that have all the time the water has and more.

The plants that wave in the wrong direction are responding to them. Following movements you cannot see because they happen too slowly. The vegetation of this place has adapted to track motion at their speed, the way sunflowers track the sun, the way roots follow moisture. Only here, the moisture follows them.

---

You have been in the city for three days, or three weeks, or three hours stretched beyond recognition.

The keeper in the corner of your shelter has completed its registration of you. Something in its posture has shifted—a welcome or a warning, you cannot tell which. Perhaps both. Perhaps the distinction does not exist at their tempo.

You find you are breathing slower. Your heart has found a new rhythm, one that sits between your old pulse and theirs, a compromise frequency. The heat does not bother you the way it did. The darkness has become navigable, not through sight but through some other sense that is emerging, some awareness of the dry places and the wet places and the boundaries between them.

The fountain calls to you. Not with sound—with *absence*, with the shape of the empty space at the center of all this water, with the promise of what you might become if you pressed yourself into the warm stone and let the moisture flow around you, learned to refuse it, learned to remember what the water wants to forget.

You find you are considering it.

You find you have been standing still for six hours, and the water has begun to curve around your feet.
