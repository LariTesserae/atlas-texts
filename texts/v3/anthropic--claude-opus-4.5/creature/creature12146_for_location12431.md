**The Tended Ones**

They have no name for themselves. Naming requires a sense of separation—*this* from *that*, *self* from *other*—and separation is not something the Vaults have ever taught them. They exist in a state of continuous, gentle overlap: bodies drifting through the same warm medium, filaments tangling and untangling, chemical signatures mingling until no individual can say with certainty where its own thoughts end and the thoughts of its neighbors begin.

This is not hive-mind. It is something softer. Call it *shared weather*—a climate of experience that passes through them all, felt differently by each, owned by none.

---

The ones feeding at the walls are the youngest, though "young" means something different when generations turn over every few decades and nothing has changed in the chamber for longer than stone remembers. They press their soft bodies against the weeping pores and absorb what the walls offer: calcium for the slow crystallization of their feeding-parts, trace metals that will eventually allow their filaments to carry signal, sugars so simple they require no digestion. They do not eat so much as *accept*. The walls give. They receive. This is the first relationship they know, and it teaches them everything they need to understand about existence: that the world provides, that patience is rewarded, that hunger is a temporary state resolved by proximity to what sustains.

When they've fed enough, they release from the wall and drift. The middle water is for learning—for the slow development of pressure-sense, for the first tentative chemical exchanges with others, for the discovery that the chamber has geometry, that there is up and down and center and edge, that movement creates subtle wakes which others can read. A young one spiraling through the open water is not playing, exactly. It is *calibrating*. Learning its own body's relationship to the space that holds it.

---

The mature ones have settled into patterns.

Some are wanderers, tracing the same circuits through the chamber that their ancestors traced, not from instinct but from the accumulated rightness of it—the way a particular path catches the best nutrient gradients, passes through the warmest currents, allows for the most frequent contact with others. They carry news, in their way. Chemical traces from the walls they've brushed, pressure-memories of distant movements, the particular flavor of a region they've passed through. When two wanderers meet, they spiral around each other in a slow double helix, filaments extended, exchanging everything they've gathered. Then they part, each carrying a little of the other's journey.

Some are anchors. They find a place against the wall or floor and stay there, sometimes for years, becoming landmarks that others navigate by. An anchor's body chemistry shifts over time, influenced by the local mineral secretions, until each anchor tastes distinct—a waypoint, a reference, a known quantity in a world without light. Young ones learn the chamber by learning its anchors. *Here is the one who tastes of copper. Here is the one whose filaments have calcified to lace. Here is the one who clicks in response to touch, a small acknowledgment: yes, I am still here, you have found me again.*

---

And then there are the old ones on the ceiling.

They began as wanderers or anchors, but something shifted in them—some slow recognition that the chamber has a structure beyond its walls, that the breathing of the space follows a rhythm larger than any single life. They rose. They attached. And over decades, their bodies elongated, reaching upward into the constructed flesh of the Vaults themselves.

They are not dying. They are *becoming infrastructure*.

Their filaments have grown into the walls, tapping directly into the nutrient flows that feed the chamber. They have become part of the circulation system, their bodies serving as living conduits between the ancient architecture and the water that fills it. When the walls breathe, the old ones breathe with them. When the chamber's chemistry shifts—seasonally, perhaps, following some pattern too slow for younger minds to track—the old ones are the first to know, and they release chemical signals that prepare the others for what's coming.

They remember things. Not in any way that could be called memory, but in the shape of their bodies, the particular configurations of their reaching roots. An old one who lived through a time when the mineral secretions ran richer has grown differently than one who matured during a leaner age. Their forms are records. The ceiling is a library written in flesh.

Sometimes, the young ones rise to touch them—pressing soft bodies against the elongated shapes, reading what can be read through contact. The old ones do not respond, exactly. But they allow. They endure. They continue their slow work of becoming part of what shelters everyone.

---

**What They Know**

They know that the world is warm and close and safe.

They know that food comes from the walls, and the walls have never failed to provide.

They know that others exist, that contact is possible, that chemical exchange creates something richer than solitude.

They know that there is up and down, that the chamber has edges, that beyond the edges are other chambers they will never visit—other populations, other chemistries, other ways of being tended. They can feel those distant cousins through the walls. Pressure-ghosts. Suggestions of abundance.

They know that they are held.

This is the deepest knowledge, the foundation beneath all others. Not safety as the absence of danger—they have no concept of danger—but safety as a positive presence, as the fundamental texture of existence. The walls breathe, and the breathing says: *you are meant to be here*. The water holds them, and the holding says: *you are enough*. The old ones reach toward the ceiling, and the reaching says: *there is always further to become*.

They do not know who built this place. They do not know that it was built at all. The Vaults are simply *the world*—the only world, complete and sufficient. That it was made, that it was made *for them*, that hands or appendages or directed intention spent generations crafting every surface to serve their flourishing: this is beyond their imagining. They could not comprehend gift, because they have never known lack. They could not comprehend love, because they have never known its absence.

They simply are, and the Vaults are, and the two states have been continuous for so long that no separation is possible.

---

**When You Arrive**

The one that brushes your hand does not know you are strange.

It registers your presence as it registers all presences: a pressure-shape in the water, a chemical signature, a warmth. You are larger than anything it has encountered, and your chemistry is foreign—sharp, complex, carrying traces of places that do not exist in its understanding of the possible. But these are simply *qualities*, no more alarming than a new mineral seep or an unfamiliar current.

It extends its filaments toward you because that is what one does with new things. It tastes you. It reads the subtle turbulence of your movements. It finds you strange and vast and unknowable, and then it spirals away, because it has other business—a circuit to complete, an anchor to visit, a fellow wanderer whose chemical traces suggest interesting news from the far wall.

You are a small mystery in a world without mysteries. The chamber will continue to breathe. The walls will continue to give. The young will continue to feed, and the mature will continue to wander or anchor, and the old will continue their slow ascent toward infrastructure.

In a generation or two, the one who touched you will anchor somewhere, and its body will carry a faint trace of your foreign chemistry—a slight discoloration, perhaps, or an unusual crystalline formation in its feeding-parts. Others will touch it and taste the strangeness and not know what it means.

*Here is the one who once met something from outside.*

They will not understand the sentence. But they will navigate by it anyway, adding it to their map of the chamber, another landmark in a world that has always been complete, that has always provided, that has never asked anything of them except that they exist, and drift, and tend to the small business of their lives in the darkness that shelters.
