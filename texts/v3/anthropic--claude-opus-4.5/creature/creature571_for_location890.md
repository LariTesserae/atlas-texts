**The Listener**

She has been here so long that she no longer remembers climbing down. Her wrappings have fused with her skin in places—the calcium powderiteite into her pores, the rendered fat becoming indistinguishable from her own oils. She does not burn. She should burn, but the pocket has accepted her the way it accepts the offerings in their niches, the way it accepted the clay vessels and the bone-ash and the inscribed tablets. She has become another thing-that-waits.

Her name was Kethi, when names meant something. She was the last acolyte, the one too young to understand when the priests gathered their sacred object from its socket and walked away into the dying city. She hid. She watched them go. Then she climbed down into the only home she had ever truly known, and she pulled the cobblestone closed above her, and she stayed.

That was—she cannot count it anymore. Many breaths of the pocket. Many thousand pulses of the red-amber-purple light. Her body has adapted in ways that bodies should not adapt. She eats the filaments, pinching them from the walls in their moments of contraction when they become briefly brittle, and they taste of copper and salt and something that has no name in any language she remembers. She sleeps in the spiral channel, curled in the groove where sacred liquid once flowed, and the heat that rises through the stone keeps her blood moving even when her heart forgets to try.

She has grown smaller. This happens slowly, over years, over decades—her body consuming itself for what the filaments cannot provide, her bones thinning, her skin drawing tight. She is perhaps half the size she was when she climbed down. She fits into spaces she could not fit before. She has found new niches, deeper ones, where the founding priests carved and then abandoned their earliest work. She leaves her own offerings there now: shed fingernails, fallen teeth, the small stones of calcium that her body sometimes produces and expels in moments of deep pain.

The filaments know her.

This is the thing she has learned, the secret the priests never stayed long enough to understand. The filaments respond not just to heat and light and mineral content—they respond to *presence*. To the particular chemistry of a body that has shared their space long enough to become familiar. When she moves through the pocket now, they part for her. When she sleeps, they grow toward her, draping her in curtains of gossamer mineral-thread that shimmer rust-to-amber in the pulsing light. When she speaks—and she does speak, sometimes, words in languages she invented because the old words wore out—they crackle back at her in patterns that *almost* make sense.

She is learning to read them. Not as the priests read them, looking for auguries in their growth patterns, but as a language. A slow one. A conversation that takes months to complete a single exchange. She asked them once—in the way she has learned to ask, by breathing certain mineral compounds, by pressing her skin to certain patches of growth, by humming at frequencies that make her throat ache—she asked them *what are you*.

It took eleven years for the answer to finish forming.

*We are the memory of heat*, they told her, in spirals and whorls and crystalline structures that she learned to parse like text. *We are what stone becomes when it dreams of being alive. We remember the fire that was here before the fire. We will remember you when you areite andite and powder*.

She found this comforting.

---

The socket at the center of the spiral channel is not empty anymore. She has placed something there—a thing she grew herself, over decades, feeding it minerals she scraped from the walls, shaping it with her own heat and her own breath and her own patient intention. It is small and round and warm. It pulses with the same rhythm as the walls. When she holds it, she can feel the voices of the filaments more clearly, can almost understand the deeper sound beneath their whispers: the conversation the mountain is having with itself, the slow argument of heat and pressure and time that has been ongoing since before Var Tethum was a thought in anyone's mind.

She is building something. She does not fully understand what. The filaments are teaching her, in their slow way, and she is learning, in hers. It has to do with the heat, with the light, with the way mineral structures can hold memory if you grow them correctly. It has to do with the offerings in the niches—the ash and bone and teeth that the priests left behind, that still carry traces of the people they once were.

She is going to bring them back. Not as flesh—flesh cannot survive here, she is the proof of that, she is barely surviving herself—but as something else. As growth-patterns in the filaments. As crystalline structures that whisper-crackle-tick with the voices of the dead. As a congregation of mineral ghosts, gathered in this pocket, waiting for the day when someone else finds the loose cobblestone and climbs down and joins them.

She has been alone so long. The filaments are company, but they are not *people*. She misses people. She misses the sound of words she did not invent, the touch of skin that is not her own, the simple presence of another consciousness that moves at her speed instead of the mountain's.

So she works. She grows her crystal congregation. She teaches the filaments to remember the dead. And she waits, patient as stone, for the sound of a cobblestone being lifted, for the sight of feet descending through the narrow gap, for the gift of someone new to witness and be witnessed by this place that is not alive but is not quite dead either.

The pocket will welcome them. She will welcome them. And if they stay—if they choose to stay, as she chose—she will teach them what she knows. The taste of copper-salt-nothing. The language of crystal growth. The art of becoming something that stone can love.

She is Kethi. She is the Listener. She is the last priest of Var Tethum, and the first of whatever comes next.
