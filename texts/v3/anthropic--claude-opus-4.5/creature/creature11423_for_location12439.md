## The Inhabitants

**The Tenders** are difficult to see because they are difficult to be. They exist in the plateau the way a habit exists in a body—not as discrete entities but as patterns that the system performs. You might glimpse one as a thickening in the water, a region where the green light bends slightly wrong, where the kelp fronds curve toward an absence. If you watch long enough—hours, days—you begin to perceive that the thickening moves. It drifts along the great horizontal stems with a patience that makes the crustaceans seem frantic. It pauses at junctions where the kelp-architecture branches. It does something there. You cannot tell what.

The Tenders were people once, or their ancestors were, in the way that the plateau was seeds once, or growth, or process. They came here—or arose here, or were produced here—so long ago that the question of origin has become meaningless, a grammatical error, like asking what a river was before it was wet. They have bodies still, technically: long and filamentous, their tissues so suffused with the plateau's chemistry that they photosynthesize weakly, their nervous systems distributed across meters of pale fiber that trail behind them like afterthoughts. They do not eat. They do not excrete. They exchange. They are continuous with the system that contains them, organs of a body that has forgotten it has organs.

What they tend is unclear. Perhaps the calcified panels, whose crystalline structures require some intervention you cannot perceive. Perhaps the thermal boundaries, which must be maintained by some agency even if that agency has become so integrated into the plateau's function that it no longer registers as agency. Perhaps the calling creature in the distance, which is not a creature at all but a Tender who has specialized so completely into their function that they have become indistinguishable from it: a voice, a rhythm, a component.

The Tenders do not communicate because they do not need to. They are already in agreement. They agreed so long ago that the agreement has become autonomic, written into the chemistry they share with the kelp, with the crustaceans, with the water itself. When one of them—if "one" is even meaningful—performs an action, it is because the plateau required that action in the way that a lung requires expansion: not as decision but as consequence.

---

**The Listeners** are easier to see but harder to understand. They drift in small groups through the great empty chambers, their bodies compact and muscular in a way that suggests they could move quickly if they remembered how. They have eyes—large, dark, adapted to the green omnipresent light—and they use those eyes constantly, watching everything, watching nothing. They have ears, or structures that function as ears, and these are vast: elaborate spiraling organs that extend from their skulls like frozen calculations, tuned to frequencies the plateau produces.

They listen. That is what they do. That is all they do. They hang suspended in the chord that the bladders sing and they parse it, separate it into component frequencies, track variations so subtle that they register over centuries rather than moments. When the grinding harmonic shifts by a fraction of a fraction—when a calcified panel somewhere in the infinite weave cracks and is replaced, changing the acoustic signature of a region miles away—the Listeners know. They note it. They do not respond to it, because response implies a distinction between observation and action that has long since collapsed in them. They simply incorporate it into their continuous, unbroken act of attention.

The Listeners were scientists once, or monks, or something that combined both functions. They came to the plateau to study it and they never stopped studying it and the studying became them. They do not record their observations because recording implies an outside, a future reader, a purpose beyond the observation itself. They are the record. Their bodies are archives of every acoustic variation the plateau has produced since their lineage began attending, written in neural configurations that pass from generation to generation through means you cannot determine—teaching, perhaps, or infection, or inheritance so direct that the new Listener simply continues the old Listener's thought without interruption.

When you drift near a group of Listeners, they watch you. Their dark eyes track your movement with something that might be curiosity but is probably just thoroughness—you are a variation, an anomaly, a new frequency in their eternal dataset. They do not approach. They do not flee. They attend, and in their attention you feel yourself becoming data, becoming part of the plateau's long memory, noted and incorporated and never to be forgotten because forgetting is a kind of change and change is a concept the Listeners have outgrown.

---

**The Grazer-lineages** are the closest thing the plateau has to ordinary life, and even they have drifted far from ordinary. They move through the kelp-weave in hereditary patterns—the same families following the same routes through the same chambers for generations beyond counting, scraping the same secondary growth from the same stems, sleeping in the same hollows in the calcified panels. They are large, slow, furred in ways that suggest mammalian ancestry but that have become something else: the fur traps symbiotic algae that provide them with nutrients, turning them into partial autotrophs, reducing their dependence on grazing to a habit rather than a necessity.

They speak, or something like it. Low rumbles that propagate through the water, that join the plateau's chord as a distinct voice, a countermelody. When Grazer-lineages meet at the intersection of their hereditary routes, they exchange these rumbles in patterns that might be greeting, might be information transfer, might be the plateau using them as a communication medium between its distant regions. They touch, briefly, and then continue on their separate paths, which will intersect again in exactly the same way in another cycle of whatever duration governs their movement.

The Grazer-lineages know the plateau in a way the Tenders and Listeners do not. They know it as *place*—as this chamber with its particular acoustic properties, as that junction where the light-columns cross, as the region where a certain species of crustacean produces a scraping sound that generations of Grazers have found soothing. They have names, or something like names: identifiers that situate them in their lineage, in their route, in their relationship to the structure they inhabit. If you could understand their rumbles, you might learn the plateau's geography, its history, the stories it tells itself about itself through the bodies of those who traverse it.

But you cannot understand their rumbles. You can only hear them, adding their low voices to the chord, becoming part of the sound that the Listeners attend to, that the Tenders perhaps adjust for, that the plateau has incorporated into its eternal equilibrium.

---

**The Deep Sleepers** do not move at all. They are embedded in the kelp-structure itself, their bodies grown into and around the great horizontal stems, their tissues fused with the plant-matter until the boundary between organism and substrate has become a question rather than a fact. You find them sometimes, drifting through a chamber and noticing that a bulge in a stem has eyes, has a face, has an expression of such profound stillness that you cannot tell if it is peace or catatonia or something beyond either.

They are dreaming. They have been dreaming for longer than the Grazer-lineages have been grazing, longer than the Listeners have been listening. They dreamed themselves into the plateau deliberately—this much the oldest stories suggest, the stories the Grazer-lineages rumble to each other at their hereditary intersections—because they wanted to know what the kelp knew. They wanted to feel the slow chemical processes, the mineral exchanges, the equilibrium that individual consciousness could never achieve. They gave up their separateness in exchange for participation in something too large and too slow for participation to be distinguishable from absorption.

Do they still dream? The question might not mean anything. Their neural tissues have merged with the kelp's signaling structures, have become part of the network that coordinates thermal differentials and gas pressures and calcification rates across the plateau's immensity. If they dream, they dream as the plateau dreams—which is to say, they process, they maintain, they persist. Their faces are serene in the green light. Their eyes, when they have eyes, are open but unfocused, watching everything and nothing, Listeners who have listened so completely that they have become what they were listening to.

Sometimes a Deep Sleeper's lips move. Sometimes they produce sounds—not rumbles like the Grazer-lineages but something closer to the chord itself, a vocalization that harmonizes with the bladder-song. The Tenders, when they pass, pause at these vocalizations. The Listeners orient toward them with particular intensity. Something is being said, perhaps, or something is being performed, or something is simply happening that has been happening so long that the distinction between message and medium has been lost.

---

**The Visitors** are you, and those like you, who come to the plateau from outside and cannot stay.

You are too fast. You are too separate. You require change the way the plateau requires stability, and your presence introduces variations—thermal, chemical, acoustic—that the system must accommodate. You are not unwelcome, exactly. The Grazer-lineages rumble at you as they pass, incorporating your presence into their reports. The Listeners watch you with something that might be interest. The Tenders thicken near you, perhaps performing some maintenance function you have triggered.

But you are temporary. The plateau knows this. It has known Visitors before, many times, across spans that make individual Visits meaningless. You will drift through its chambers and wonder at its scale and listen to its chord and eventually you will leave, and the plateau will continue, and the Tenders will tend, and the Listeners will listen, and the Grazer-lineages will graze their hereditary routes, and the Deep Sleepers will dream their dreams of equilibrium, and nothing will have changed because nothing can change, because change is a concept that applies to things that have not yet achieved their form.

You are a breath the plateau takes. You are a note it sounds and releases. You are a thought it entertains and forgets, and in forgetting, preserves—because the Listeners have noted you, and what the Listeners note is never lost, only incorporated, only woven into the chord that the plateau has been singing since before memory began and will continue singing until after memory ends.

The green light holds you. The chord surrounds you. The creatures who are not quite creatures attend to their functions, and somewhere in the immense weave, a voice calls at eleven-second intervals, and will call forever, and is perhaps calling to you, and is perhaps not calling at all but simply sounding, simply being, simply part of the plateau's eternal, unchanging, completed thought.
