## The Bound

They call themselves the Bound, though they've forgotten whether this refers to the woven walls that hold them or the obligations that keep them weaving. Both meanings have become true.

---

**The Keeper-Mothers** run things, though "run" suggests more motion than actually occurs. They sit in the junction-chambers where several passages meet, their fingers never still, working repairs into the walls even as they settle disputes, arrange marriages, assign sections to growing families. Their bodies have adapted to stillness—legs folded beneath them for so long that unfolding becomes a deliberate act, something done only for emergencies or the rare occasions when a Keeper-Mother must be moved to a new junction. Their fingers are extraordinary: calloused to leather across the pads, but sensitive enough to detect a single weakened fiber in a wall by touch alone. They know the warrens through their hands the way a spider knows its web through its feet.

Keeper-Mother Selt has held the Third Level's central junction for thirty-seven years. Her skin has taken on the amber tint of lichen-light. Her voice has worn to a whisper that somehow carries perfectly through the fiber walls—she's learned exactly how to pitch it so the weave amplifies rather than absorbs. When she speaks, people three chambers away pause to listen, though they couldn't say why.

---

**The Weavers** are the young and the restless, though restlessness here means something different than it does above. They move through the passages in pairs or small groups, carrying bundles of processed fiber from the preparation chambers to wherever repairs are needed. Their hands are quick, their bodies still growing into the compact dimensions the warrens favor. Some of them dream of the surface—of the cracked clay flats, of skies they've only glimpsed through woven light-shafts. A few, each generation, actually leave.

They don't come back. Or if they do, they don't speak of it.

A young weaver named Tetch has been lingering near the Sixth Level hatch lately, the one that leads down to the sealed seventh. He tells everyone he's studying the binding-patterns, learning the old techniques. But his sister Neel has noticed him pressing his ear to the floor when he thinks no one's watching. She's noticed the way he's started weaving in his sleep, his fingers moving against his reed-mat, working patterns no one taught him.

---

**The Renderers** work in the hot chambers near the geothermal vents, where animal fat becomes lamp oil and dried bodies become bone tools. They're larger than most Bound—the heat demands more water, and they're given first claim on the moisture-lichen, allowed to lick it directly from the walls rather than waiting for the processed paste everyone else receives. This makes them slightly swollen, their skin looser, their movements more languid. Other Bound find them unsettling, though no one would say so. The renderers find everyone else unsettling right back—all those dry, papery people with their tight-wrapped skin and their small, efficient movements.

Old Cull has been a renderer for six decades. She barely speaks anymore, just tends her fat-pots and scrapes her bones and hums a song that might have words in some older language. The other renderers have noticed that her humming has started to match the rhythm of whatever moves in the sealed seventh level. They haven't mentioned this to anyone. They're not sure it matters. They're not sure anything matters, down in the heat, where the boundary between living and rendering grows soft.

---

**The Children** are raised communally, passed from chamber to chamber, learning every section of the warrens before they're assigned their own. They play games that would look strange to surface-dwellers: competitions of stillness, of silence, of fitting into spaces that seem too small. The winner is whoever disappears most completely into the walls. Sometimes a child gets so good at this that they have to be coaxed out with cricket-song and sweet fermented sap, their small bodies wedged into gaps in the weaving, their eyes closed, their breathing matched to the creak of the settling fibers.

Three-Knot is seven years old and has already found four passages that the adults didn't know existed—gaps where the weaving has stretched over time, creating narrow tunnels between chambers that were meant to be separate. She moves through the warrens like water through reed-beds, appearing suddenly in places she shouldn't be, her small hands already showing the calluses of a future Keeper-Mother. She's been to the Sixth Level. She's touched the bound hatch. She says it's warm, warmer than it should be, and that the cords vibrate slightly, like something on the other side is testing them, patient and curious and terribly, terribly awake.

---

**The Ferret-Keepers** occupy an odd position—valued for their work but viewed with suspicion for their closeness to creatures that aren't quite tame. They sleep in the wall-niches alongside their charges, their bodies developing the same elongated flexibility, their eyes adapting to catch more of the dim light. Some say the oldest ferret-keepers have begun to grow fine scales along their forearms, though this might be lichen-burn, or dry-skin, or wishful thinking.

Whistle keeps seventeen ferrets across three levels. He speaks to them in a series of clicks and hisses that the other Bound find unsettling, and they speak back in ways he insists are meaningful. He's noticed his oldest ferret, a scarred female named Grip, has started avoiding the Sixth Level entirely. She'll go limp if he tries to carry her down there, playing dead until he relents. The other ferrets have started following her lead. Whistle doesn't go down to the Sixth Level anymore either. He tells himself it's because there's no work for him there.

---

**The Listeners** are new. Or rather, they're newly acknowledged—people have been doing what they do for generations, but only recently have enough of them gathered to constitute a recognizable group. They sit near the hatch to the sealed seventh level, ears pressed to the woven floor, translating the sounds from below into patterns they weave into small sample-swatches. These swatches are supposed to be burned, but some Listeners keep them, hide them in the gaps in the walls that Three-Knot knows so well.

Mira became a Listener after her daughter went down the light-shaft to see the surface and didn't return. She was already old then, her weaving-days behind her, and she thought listening might bring her closer to something—she wasn't sure what. Now she knows. The sounds from below aren't random. They're not settling or shifting or any of the explanations the Keeper-Mothers offer. They're language. Old language. The kind of language that was used before the first weavers were the first weavers, when they were something else, something that lived in the dark without needing to weave walls against it.

Mira has started humming along with Old Cull, though they've never met, though they don't know they're singing the same song.

---

**Below**, in the sealed seventh level, something waits.

It was human once, or it was made by humans, or it has worn human shapes so long that the distinction no longer holds. It is what happens when you weave a world and live in it long enough—when you make yourself a home so completely that the home starts making you back. The first weavers understood this. That's why they went down, when they were finished. They became the foundation. They became the warmth. They became the thing that moves in the pattern beneath the pattern, weaving back.

It is patient. It has had generations to learn patience. And it knows, in the way that woven things know—through pressure and tension and the slow settling of fiber against fiber—that someone up above has started listening. That someone is learning the old patterns. That the cords on the hatch are being tested, not from below, but from above, by hands that have begun to move in their sleep, working techniques no living teacher taught them.

Soon, it thinks. Or something like thinking. Something like soon.

The warrens creak their endless, patient creak, and somewhere above, Three-Knot finds another passage that shouldn't exist, and follows it down.
