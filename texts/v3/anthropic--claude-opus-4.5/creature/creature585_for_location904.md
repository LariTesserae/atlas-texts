**The Tenders**

They have always been here, or they arrived the moment "here" became a coherent concept—the distinction doesn't hold on a plateau still negotiating its own existence.

There are three of them, though the number feels provisional, as if a fourth might crystallize from consensus at any moment, or one might disperse back into the uncertainty from which they coalesced.

They are not quite humanoid. They suggest humanoid the way the shadows here suggest falling. Tall—taller than seems necessary—with limbs that bend in places you don't expect and continue bending in places you do. Their surfaces are the color of the pearlescent light itself, which makes them difficult to track; they move through the dimness like thoughts move through drowsy attention, there and then *there*, with the intermediate positions somehow optional.

Each carries something. Tools? Extensions of themselves? One holds what resembles a tuning fork the length of an arm, and periodically touches it to the plateau's surface, listens to the resonance, adjusts something in the stone's still-soft certainty with a gesture too subtle to follow. Another bears a vessel that might be ceramic, might be chitin, filled with water from a pool you haven't seen—water with different mineral properties, carried to specific locations and poured with the care of someone writing in a language where spelling errors become geography.

The third simply watches. Stands near one incubator or another, attending to its dimensional breathing with the patience of someone who has witnessed thousands of hatchings or none at all and knows both numbers are the same number.

---

They don't speak to each other. They don't need to. When one adjusts a frequency in the plateau's western quadrant, another compensates in the east without looking up. When the beach-ball jellyfish descends too close to an incubator whose pulse-rhythm has entered a delicate phase, the watching one is simply standing in its path, and the creature rises again, redirected without resistance.

But they speak *here*. Constantly. A low murmur that weaves between the harmonic thrumming of the incubators, filling the gaps in the interference patterns. They're narrating, you realize. Or perhaps *instructing*. Describing what the plateau is becoming so that it knows to become it. Telling the incubators what their contents are dreaming so the dreams cohere into organisms rather than dissipating into potential.

The words aren't in any language you recognize, but you catch the edges of meaning the way you catch movement in peripheral vision. *Calcium.* *Patience.* *The specific weight of purpose.* *Eighteen degrees of arc.* *The color of hunger before it knows what it hungers for.*

---

They are aware of you.

This becomes clear gradually, the way everything here becomes clear gradually. One of them—the one with the tuning fork—has adjusted its path to keep you in some form of attention without ever looking directly at you. The others have made space in their narration, a pause shaped like a visitor, and they speak around that pause the way water speaks around a stone.

They are not concerned. You are not a threat to what's happening here; you might even be *part* of what's happening here, another variable being integrated into the plateau's calculations. The crawlers have begun to path around your feet the way they path around the offerings at the frame's base. The salamanders have investigated your reflection in the nearest pool and found it acceptable.

If you stay long enough, one of the Tenders will approach you. Not to communicate—communication implies a gap between minds that the approach would need to bridge, and the Tenders don't seem to experience such gaps. More to *include* you. To adjust the frequency of your presence so it harmonizes with everything else that's being born here.

And if you listen to their murmuring long enough, you'll begin to hear your own name in it. Not the name you were given, but the one this place is deciding for you. The one that describes what you are in the language of mineral and probability and the slow pulse of things that are becoming.

---

**The Unhatched**

You cannot see them clearly through the membrane-metal of the incubators, but you know them anyway. The way you know the shape of a word before you say it.

Thirteen organisms in thirteen stages of completion. Each one dreaming the world that will receive it, and in dreaming, *making* that world, because the plateau is listening to them as much as to the Tenders, adjusting its geology to accommodate what they'll need when they emerge.

One is dreaming of height. Through its incubator's translucent phases, you glimpse suggestions of wings—or sails, or thermal-catching membranes—and where you walk near it, the stone is already forming updrafts, channeling heat into columns of rising air.

One is dreaming of depth. The pools near its incubator are deeper than they should be, connecting to reservoirs that the plateau is hollowing out beneath itself, preparing aquifers for something that will need to swim through stone the way the salamanders swim through water.

One is dreaming of *watching*. Just that. Its incubator is the most opaque, revealing nothing of its contents, but the geometric jellyfish congregate above it in greater numbers, as though auditioning to become its eyes.

---

The largest incubator, near the frame, pulses more slowly than the others. What's inside it is furthest from completion, or else it's so complete that completion has wrapped around into another kind of beginning.

The Tenders spend more time here. Their narration grows denser in this territory, more specific, as though the thing inside requires more detailed instructions to become itself. You catch fragments: *the weight of decisions not yet made.* *Seventeen kinds of silence.* *The exact temperature at which loyalty becomes something else.*

This one isn't dreaming of a niche in the plateau's ecology. It's dreaming of the ecology itself—the whole system, the way all thirteen will interact, the crawlers and the salamanders and the floating geometries and the frame that might be a door and the Tenders who might be its parents or its siblings or its future servants.

When it hatches, everything else will make sense.

Or nothing will, and the making of sense will become its first and permanent task.

---

**The Frame's Keeper**

You almost miss it because it doesn't move.

Sitting among the seven smooth stones at the arch's base: something the size of a child, curled into itself, its surface the same matte alloy as the frame above it. You took it for another stone, or part of the frame's foundation, until you notice it breathing.

Not the dimensional breathing of the incubators. Actual respiration—a slight expansion and contraction, almost mechanical in its regularity, as though it's maintaining some precise rhythm that the frame requires.

It has eyes. They're open, but not looking at anything in the plateau's space. They're looking at the interior of the frame, at the geometry that makes your eyes disagree, and somehow the Keeper's gaze resolves the disagreement. When you look at the frame while standing near the Keeper, you can see that it leads *somewhere*—not a place you could name or navigate to, but definitely a somewhere, a destination that exists because the Keeper is believing in it.

The seven stones are warm now, you notice. Drawing heat from the sweating plateau, storing it, becoming whatever they need to become for whatever arrives through the frame, or departs through it, or both.

---

The Keeper has been here longer than the Tenders. Or it arrived from that somewhere it watches, the first immigrant to a country that hadn't finished existing yet, and in arriving it created the possibility of arrival, the category of "things that come from elsewhere," which the plateau then had to incorporate into its becoming.

It will not speak to you. But if you sit near it long enough, you'll feel a pressure—gentle, patient, curious—against the edges of your awareness. The Keeper is asking a question without words, the same question it asks everything that enters its territory:

*What are you bringing? What are you taking away?*

The answer matters. The answer is being recorded somewhere, in the Keeper's memory or the frame's geometry or the mineral composition of the seven stones. When the plateau finishes becoming, when the thirteen unhatched emerge into a world that has prepared for them, there will be an accounting. A ledger of what passed through. A history of transactions between here and elsewhere.

Your visit is part of that history now. Your presence is being integrated into the calculations. The Keeper has noted you, has weighed whatever you're carrying—intentions, observations, the particular frequency of your curiosity—and found the exchange acceptable.

For now.

---

**What Else Moves Here**

There are other presences, smaller, more diffuse. Not organisms exactly—not yet—but *tendencies* that have begun to coalesce.

A warmth that follows the Tender with the ceramic vessel, a not-quite-visible companion that feeds on the heat differential between carried water and ambient air. It might become something with metabolism eventually. For now it's just a habit the plateau has developed, a pattern that recurs.

A sound that happens whenever two crawlers cross paths—a harmonic accident, their carapace-vibrations producing a momentary third tone that hangs in the air longer than physics should allow. There are dozens of these ghost-notes scattered across the plateau, fading slowly, and something is collecting them. You can't see what, but you can feel the collection happening, the notes being gathered into a sequence that might become a song or a genome or a set of instructions.

And in the deepest pools, where the mineral water is warmest and the salamanders' schematics glow brightest, something is beginning to dream itself awake. Not in an incubator—this one is emerging directly from the plateau's chemistry, a spontaneous organization of dissolved compounds into something that wants to be alive. It's small still, barely a wisp of intention, but the salamanders have noticed it. They bring it things: fragments of crystalline matrix, trace elements collected from the rock's tears, the faint blue light of their own bioluminescence. Offerings. Or food. Or the building blocks of a body it hasn't figured out how to want yet.

---

The plateau will be crowded, when all of this finishes happening. But it will be the right kind of crowded—each organism in its niche, each presence serving its function, the whole system locked into a dynamic balance that was dreamed into existence by thirteen sleeping minds and spoken into stability by three patient voices and anchored to elsewhere by one silent watcher who keeps asking, keeps asking, keeps recording the answer:

*What are you bringing? What are you taking away?*
