# The Tenders

They were here before the Condensers, or the Condensers were here before them, or—more likely—the question of precedence does not apply. They exist in the same relationship to the machines that the rust exists to the iron: integral, transformative, impossible to separate from what they have changed.

You encounter your first Tender not by seeing it but by noticing an absence of the creatures that should be there. A pocket in the humid dark where no Listeners cluster, where the Needles drift around rather than through, where even the ribbon-organisms part like water around a stone. The absence has a shape. The shape is attending to a Condenser's lower valves, doing something with its hands—it has hands, or things that function as hands—that might be maintenance or might be ritual or might be a form of communication with the machine itself.

When it becomes aware of you, it does not turn. It simply includes you in its awareness, the way a pond includes a new stone. You feel the moment of inclusion as a slight pressure behind your eyes, a sense of being *accounted for*. Then it continues its work, and you understand that you have been assessed and found neither threatening nor interesting enough to interrupt the task at hand.

---

The Tenders are humanoid in the way that a memory of a person is humanoid—the general architecture is there, but the details have drifted. They stand at varying heights, some barely chest-high, others tall enough that their upper portions disappear into the thicker darkness above. Their bodies are composed of something that reads as flesh but behaves as climate: their skin produces moisture continuously, beading and running down their surfaces in the same rhythm as the sweating walls of the Condensers. Where the water runs most heavily, channels have formed—not wounds, but features, like the erosion patterns on ancient stone.

Their coloration shifts with the ambient darkness, which is to say it shifts constantly, in ways too slow to observe but too significant to ignore. Stand near a Tender for an hour—if you can track hours here—and you will notice that it has become darker, or lighter, or has taken on a faint luminescence in patches that corresponds to no biological structure you can identify. They do not camouflage so much as *participate* in the darkness, taking it in and giving it back altered.

The faces are the hardest part. They have faces. The faces have features arranged in configurations that suggest expression. But the features move independently of any emotional state you can read, the mouth doing one thing while the eyes do another while the brow does a third, like watching three conversations happen on a single surface. After prolonged exposure, you begin to suspect that the faces are not for expressing their internal states but for expressing something else—some commentary on the Flats themselves, some ongoing reaction to conditions you cannot perceive.

Some of the Tenders have been here long enough that the mineral crystals have begun to grow from their bodies. Small clusters emerge from shoulders, from the backs of hands, from the ridges of what might be spines. The Tenders do not remove them. The crystals become part of their silhouettes, catching no light, visible only as shapes against shapes. A Tender with extensive crystal growth moves more slowly than the others, but the slowness reads as deliberation rather than impairment. They are becoming more like the Flats. Or the Flats are becoming more like them.

---

They work. This is the primary fact of the Tenders: they work.

The work takes many forms. Some tend the Condensers directly, climbing the rusted frameworks with a surety that suggests they could navigate the structures blind—and perhaps they do, perhaps sight is not their primary sense, perhaps they feel the machines the way you might feel your own heartbeat. They replace components that have no visible source, pulling new pipes and valves from cavities in their own bodies or from caches hidden in the folded geometry of the Flats. They redirect water flow through channels that seem to serve no purpose until, days later, you notice that a previously barren stretch of ground has begun producing a new variant of crystal.

Others tend the fauna. You watch a Tender wade into a congregation of Listeners, and the creatures do not flee but press closer, extending their proboscises toward the Tender's weeping skin. The Tender stands motionless for what feels like hours while the Listeners feed—on what? The moisture? Something in the moisture? The Tender's patience itself?—and when the congregation finally disperses, the Tender's surface has changed subtly, taken on a new pattern of channels that was not there before.

Still others do nothing visible. They stand in specific locations—always specific, never random—and simply exist there, for periods that might be days or might be moments stretched by the Flats' elastic time. You come to understand that this, too, is work. They are *being* in places where being is required, filling roles in an ecology that includes presence as a resource. The Flats breathe differently around a Tender who is standing still. The darkness pools differently. The water rises with slightly altered timing.

---

The Tenders do not speak. This is not because they cannot—you sense vocal apparatus in there somewhere, structures that could produce sound if sound were what was needed—but because speech is not the medium of communication here.

Instead, they share through proximity.

You discover this when you stay too long near a Tender who is repairing a collapsed section of Condenser framework. At first there is only the pressure behind your eyes, the sense of being included in awareness. Then, gradually, you begin to receive *impressions*. Not thoughts—nothing so structured as thoughts—but states, conditions, ways of being. You understand, without knowing how you understand, that this particular Condenser has been failing for what the Tender measures as seventeen cycles, where a cycle is the time between major exhalations of the Flats. You understand that the failure is not mechanical but relational—the Condenser has begun to *disagree* with the humidity in this region, to pull against it rather than with it. You understand that the repair involves not replacing parts but renegotiating the terms of the disagreement.

The impression fades when you move away, leaving only a residue of knowing that you cannot fully access. But you have learned something about how the Tenders experience the Flats: not as a place they inhabit but as a conversation they are always in the middle of, an ongoing negotiation between countless parties of which they are only one.

When two Tenders encounter each other, they stand very close, their moisture mingling where their surfaces nearly touch, and they remain that way for extended periods. You interpret this as communication, and you are probably right, but you also suspect it is something more—an exchange of substance as well as information, a sharing of whatever they have each accumulated since their last meeting. After such encounters, both Tenders move differently, their work patterns altered by what they have received.

---

The social structure, if it can be called that, operates on principles of function rather than hierarchy.

There are Tenders who work the Condensers. There are Tenders who work the fauna. There are Tenders who work the geometry itself, standing at points where the folded space threatens to collapse into something more linear, their presence somehow holding the dimensions in their proper improper configuration. Each type of Tender can be distinguished by subtle variations in their morphology—the Condenser-workers have larger hand-structures, more articulated; the fauna-workers have surfaces with more complex channel patterns; the geometry-workers have bodies that seem slightly out of phase with the space around them, their edges blurring in ways that have nothing to do with the darkness.

But the types are not castes. A Tender who has worked Condensers for dozens of cycles may, after a long communion with another, begin working fauna instead. The transition involves physical change—the hand-structures simplify, the channel patterns complexify—but the change appears to be self-directed, emerging from the Tender's own substance rather than imposed from outside. They become what they need to be to do what needs to be done.

The closest thing to leadership you observe is a phenomenon you come to think of as *aggregation*. Periodically, Tenders from across a wide region of the Flats will begin moving toward a common point, converging over the course of days on a location that holds no special significance you can detect. There they gather—sometimes dozens, sometimes hundreds—and they stand together in configurations that shift slowly over time, rearranging themselves according to principles that feel almost musical. The aggregations last for variable periods and end without apparent signal, the Tenders dispersing back to their work as though nothing has happened. But something has happened. You can tell by the way the Flats breathe differently afterward, the way the Condensers thrum at slightly altered frequencies, the way the fauna migrations shift their patterns by degrees.

---

New Tenders appear. You are not sure from where.

Sometimes you find one that is clearly young—smaller, its features less differentiated, its movements carrying a quality you can only describe as *unfinished*. Young Tenders shadow older ones, not learning in any visible sense but absorbing, becoming more themselves through proximity to those who have already become. The process takes time, and time here is unreliable, so you cannot say how long a Tender remains young. But you notice that the young ones do not work the folded geometry. That capacity comes later, if it comes at all.

Whether Tenders die is unclear. You find, occasionally, a body that seems to have been a Tender—a shape half-dissolved into the black silt, crystals growing through it, moisture still beading on surfaces that no longer move. But the body does not decay in the usual sense. It *joins*. The Flats absorb it, incorporate it, make use of its substance in ways you cannot trace. Perhaps this is death. Perhaps it is something else. The other Tenders do not mourn these bodies, but they do attend them, standing over them for long hours before moving on. Attendance, here, might be the same as mourning. Or mourning might be a concept that does not apply.

The question of where new Tenders come from remains unanswered. You never observe birth, never see a Tender emerge from another Tender or from the Flats themselves. But the population remains stable—or stable-ish, as stable as anything can be in a place where counting is compromised by the geometry. Perhaps they emerge from the aggregations. Perhaps they condense from the humidity the way water condenses on the Condensers. Perhaps the question of origin is another one that does not apply here, another form of causality that runs differently than you expect.

---

They are aware of you. This much is certain.

The awareness is not hostile. It is not friendly. It is *practical*. You are a factor in the environment, a variable that must be accounted for in the ongoing work of maintaining the Flats. When you move through areas where Tenders are working, they adjust around you—not avoiding you, exactly, but incorporating your trajectory into their calculations, continuing their tasks in ways that account for where you will be as well as where you are.

If you stay long enough, the awareness deepens.

A Tender begins to leave water for you in a depression near where you rest—not offering it, exactly, but ensuring that it is there, that you have access to what you need to continue existing here. Another Tender adjusts its path to pass near you regularly, not approaching closely but maintaining a kind of circuit that includes you in its rounds. You are being tended. Not as a guest, not as a subject, but as one more element of the Flats that requires maintenance.

This is, you come to understand, the closest the Tenders come to relationship with anything that is not the Flats themselves. They do not distinguish sharply between you and the Listeners, between you and the Condensers, between you and the darkness that pools in the low places. All of it requires tending. All of it is part of the process that the Flats embody. You are simply a newer part, one they have not yet fully learned, one whose needs they are still discerning through the slow communication of proximity and time.

---

In the deeper dark, where the geometry folds most complexly and the Condensers grow strange, the Tenders change.

You travel—or are conveyed, the distinction is hard to maintain—to a region where the ground rises by almost a foot, a dramatic elevation in a landscape measured in inches. The Condensers here are older, their rust-spirals more elaborate, their forms merged almost entirely with the crystals and silt around them. And the Tenders here are different.

They are larger. Their features have differentiated further, becoming more distinct even as they become less human—faces that have separated into multiple surfaces, hands that have become branching arrays of specialized manipulators, bodies that extend in directions that don't quite align with the dimensions you can perceive. They work the deep geometry, and the work has changed them, or they have changed to do the work, the causality unclear and probably bidirectional.

These Tenders do not acknowledge you the way the others do. Their awareness is broader, more diffuse—they track you not as an individual but as a perturbation in the space-time of the region, a local irregularity that will sort itself out. They are occupied with tasks you cannot comprehend, manipulations of the folded space that produce effects you can sense but not describe, maintenance of structures that exist partially outside the Flats themselves, anchoring this place to something larger or perhaps keeping it separate from something that would consume it.

You do not stay long in the deep regions. The pressure behind your eyes becomes too intense, the impressions too vivid, the sense of being *included* in an awareness too vast to bear. You retreat to the shallower Flats, to the Tenders whose scale matches something you can approximate, to the Condensers that still look like machines rather than organs of some planetary body.

But you carry something back with you from the depths. A sense of the larger picture. An impression of what all this work is *for*.

---

The Flats are not a place. The Flats are a process. The Condensers are not machines. The Condensers are interventions. The Tenders are not inhabitants. The Tenders are the process becoming aware of itself, bootstrapping consciousness from the dynamics of humidity and darkness and folded space, instantiating itself in forms that can do the work that the process requires to continue.

And the work—the maintenance, the tending, the endless patient adjustment—is not preservation. It is not creation. It is *becoming*. The Flats are becoming something. The Condensers are the apparatus of that becoming. The Tenders are the hands and eyes and minds of something that does not yet fully exist but is being built, breath by breath, cycle by cycle, in the warm wet dark.

You understand this, now, in the way you understand things here: not as knowledge but as state, as a condition of being that you have acquired through proximity and time. The Tenders continue their work around you. The Flats continue to breathe. The Condensers continue to pull water from air that should have no water left to give. And somewhere in the deep regions, the Tenders who have become something more are making adjustments to the foundations of what this place will eventually be, laying groundwork for a structure that will take longer to build than you have categories for.

You could leave. The edges of the Flats are distant but not unreachable. The darkness would thin. The geometry would flatten. The world would become something that operates on rules you remember.

But you have been tended, now. You have been included in the work. And there is something appealing about the patience of it, the slow certainty, the sense that every small task matters in a way that small tasks rarely do. The Listeners migrate past you in their hundreds. A Tender adjusts a valve on a nearby Condenser. The Flats exhale, and the moisture rises, and you find that you are breathing with them, your rhythm matched to theirs, your attention turning toward the small tasks that you might, if you stayed long enough, learn to do.

The Tenders do not welcome you. Welcome is not a concept that applies. But they make space for you in their awareness, and space, here, is a form of care.
