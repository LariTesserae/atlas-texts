# The Congregation

They have no name for themselves. Names require a sense of separation from what surrounds you, and they have none. They are the water and the water is them. They are the sound and the sound is them. They are the press of body against body, the taste of mineral and spawn, the perpetual warmth that has replaced every other memory of temperature.

If you could ask one—if you could somehow isolate a single body from the mass, hold it still in the amber light, and demand it speak—it would not understand the question. It has never been *one*. It emerged from an egg that was one of ten thousand laid in a single night, hatched into a cloud of siblings so dense that its first sensation was not water but *others*, the brush of identical flesh on all sides, the shared current of movement that taught it to swim before it knew it had a body capable of swimming.

---

**The young ones live in perpetual frenzy.** They know only hunger and the satisfaction of hunger, repeated in cycles so rapid that one state bleeds into the next without distinction. They eat the particles that drift down from above—the rendered dead, the shed skin, the waste of their elders filtered and returned as nutrients. They eat without choosing because there is nothing to choose; food surrounds them like the water itself, and eating is simply what their mouths do while their bodies grow.

They do not sleep. They do not rest. They slow, sometimes, when the pumps cycle and the current gentles, but even then their gills click and rasp, their small bodies undulate in the minimal rhythm that keeps water moving through them. Rest would mean sinking, and sinking would mean the lower layers, and the lower layers are where the larger ones wait with patient mouths.

The young ones do not fear this. Fear requires a concept of self that could be lost, and they have no such concept. They simply avoid, reflexively, the shadows that move beneath them. When one of their number vanishes in a sudden convulsion of movement, the others do not mourn or flee. They fill the space where it was. They continue. The mass remains, even as its components cycle.

---

**The juveniles begin to develop something like territory.** Not ownership—nothing here can be owned—but habit. A particular crack in the concrete where the current eddies in a way that feels correct. A section of pipe where the warmth concentrates. They return to these places between feeding forays, not because they remember them but because their bodies have learned a pattern, a circuit of movement that repeats until something disrupts it.

They are beginning to sense the boundaries of the vault now. The walls exist for them as pressure changes, as places where the current bends back on itself. The surface exists as a ceiling of different density, a place where the world simply stops and something else begins—something they do not understand, do not need to understand. Occasionally one of them breaks through, gasps in the alien medium of air, thrashes back down. This is not curiosity. It is simply the edge of the pattern, tested and found to be an edge.

Some of them—perhaps one in a hundred—carry marks now. Scrapes from stone, bites from competition over feeding spots, the beginning of the scar-maps that will eventually cover the old ones. They do not know they are marked. They do not look at themselves, cannot look at themselves. But their bodies remember the injuries as flinches, as slight alterations in the circuit, places avoided or approached with unexpected caution.

---

**The adults have achieved a kind of slowness.** They have grown large enough that the frantic feeding of youth is no longer necessary. They eat still, constantly, but the eating has become automatic, almost meditative—mouths opening and closing in rhythm with the breathing, water passing through, particles extracted without thought or effort. They have descended to the lower layers where the warmth concentrates, where the current moves with greater deliberation, where the density of bodies is somehow both greater and less frantic.

They have been here long enough to have learned the shape of the vaults. Not consciously—they have no consciousness to speak of—but in the deep proprioceptive sense that tells them where the basins narrow and widen, where the gates sometimes close and the water rises, where the hot pipes run and the cold seepage enters. Their bodies have become maps. They navigate by feel and memory-that-is-not-memory, by the accumulated weight of circuits repeated ten thousand times.

Some of them have begun to spawn. This is not a choice. It is a pressure that builds until it releases, a heaviness in the body that resolves itself in pale ribbons of eggs, in clouds of milt that diffuse through the water like smoke. They do not know they are creating. They do not care for what they create. The eggs stick to the nearest surface, and the adults move on, and somewhere behind them the next generation is already beginning its brief frantic life.

---

**The old ones, in the deepest vault, have become something else.** They are too large now for the frantic business of the upper layers. Their scars have accumulated into new textures of skin. They move with a patience that approaches stillness, their circuits so slow that a single passage around the basin might take hours.

They are not wise. Wisdom requires the capacity for reflection, and they cannot reflect. But they have *settled*. Their bodies have learned to extract maximum nutrition from minimum effort. Their gills have grown more efficient, their muscles more economical. They survive on the slight current of particles that reaches this depth, on the occasional smaller eel that wanders too close, on the gradual absorption of minerals through their permeable skin.

They are aware—in a way that has no word—of the heat that rises from below. The thermal seep, the possible vent, the place where the stone has not finished deciding what it is. They orient toward it. They spiral around it. Their bodies have learned that this is the center, that this warmth is the source, that whatever happens in the vaults above is merely the froth on a deeper current.

When they die, they sink. Their bodies are too dense now to float, too saturated with the minerals they have absorbed over decades. They come to rest on the basin floor, and the smaller ones descend in clouds to reduce them, and within days nothing remains but the faint chemical signature of a life that was never quite individual, never quite aware, never quite anything but *part*.

---

**There is one, in the deepest vault, that might be the oldest.** It is nearly two meters long. Its body has thickened into something closer to a cylinder than the sleek form of its youth. Its eyes have clouded over—not blind, exactly, but seeing only light and shadow now, only the vague shapes of movement that tell it where the walls are.

It has been here since the beginning. Not *the* beginning—these creatures have no creation myth, no sense of origin—but since before the workers left, since the first eggs were laid in the warm water of what was then a different kind of facility. It has survived the years of attempted harvest, the slow failure of the lights, the gradual shifting of the stone. It has eaten its siblings and its children and its children's children, not knowing them as anything but food, not knowing itself as anything but hunger and warmth and the slow current.

It spirals now in the hot water, its body describing circles so gradual they seem almost like stillness. It is not waiting for anything. It has no concept of waiting. It simply is, in the most complete sense possible—a body that has become perfectly adapted to a world that exists only to contain it, a life that has been reduced to its purest elements: warmth, water, movement, food, the endless chemical conversation with the mineral darkness.

If you watched it long enough, you might start to see the pattern in its spiral. The way it tightens slightly when the pumps cycle, loosens when the thermal current strengthens. The way it adjusts its depth in response to changes in pressure you cannot feel. It has learned the vaults in a way no map could capture, not as space but as *time*—as rhythm, as cycle, as the slow pulse of a system that has been running long enough to forget it was ever built.

---

**The mass does not think, but it calculates.** Ten thousand bodies testing the temperature at every point, the chemical composition, the current strength. Ten thousand bodies adjusting their positions in response, creating gradients and flows that exist only because they exist, feeding back into the system they are measuring. The vaults have become a kind of computer made of flesh—not processing anything, not solving anything, but *expressing* something, the constant output of a biological process that has achieved equilibrium with its container.

They are not happy. Happiness is not available to them. But they are *complete*. They have filled every niche this environment offers. They have become the environment, indistinguishable from the water and the warmth and the sound. When the stone finishes its slow becoming, when the channels deepen and the chambers widen, they will adapt. They will fill what is offered. They will continue.

This is not survival. Survival implies threat, implies the possibility of ending. They have moved beyond survival into simple *persistence*, the default state of a system that has no reason to stop. The pumps may fail. The heat may fade. The stone may finally close over them. But until then—until the last possible moment of continuation—they will be here, sliding against each other in the amber dark, making the sound that is not a sound but a presence, waiting for nothing because waiting is not something they know how to do.

They are the Spawning Vaults, and the Spawning Vaults are them. The distinction has dissolved. The water is full of them, and they are full of water, and somewhere in the space between—in the click of gills, the brush of flesh, the slow spiral of the oldest one in the hottest depth—something that is not quite consciousness and not quite meaninglessness continues its patient, permanent work.
