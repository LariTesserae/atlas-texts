They call themselves nothing. Names require breath spent on distinction, and here breath is too precious, moisture too sacred to waste on the arbitrary. But if you could perceive the way they recognize each other—through the particular resonance each body makes against the salt-stone, a frequency as unique as a fingerprint—you would understand they have identities deeper than names.

---

**The Tenders** occupy the lower chambers, the ones with walls thick enough to hold the night's cold through the hottest hours. They are the pale crab-things you might glimpse, but *crab* captures nothing of their nature. Their shells are not chitin but a living mineral lattice they grow from the pillar itself, incorporating salt crystals into their exoskeletons until they become extensions of the architecture. When a Tender dies, its body does not decay. It calcifies in place, becoming a new surface for plants to colonize, a new cavity for eggs to occupy. The oldest parts of the lower city are built from Tender remains going back generations beyond counting.

They tend more than eggs. They tend *everything*. A Tender's life is a continuous slow patrol through its claimed territory—perhaps three chambers, perhaps seven, depending on the complexity of the growth there—during which it adjusts, prunes, balances. This plant is taking too much light from that one. This crystal formation is beginning to block a drainage channel. This clutch of eggs has been oriented wrongly and will not hatch. The Tenders make corrections measured in fractions of millimeters, in nudges delivered over the course of days. They do not communicate with each other in any way you could detect, and yet their work is coordinated. The lower city functions as a single organism because every Tender understands, without discussion, what the whole requires.

Their eyes—if eyes they are—face downward. They watch the ground, the growth, the endless small disasters requiring endless small interventions. They do not look up. The upper city is not their concern. Perhaps they do not know it exists.

---

**The Listeners** stretch between stems in the middle terraces, motionless for so long that vegetation grows across their bodies, that salt crystals form along their extended limbs. You mistook them for vines because they have become vines, in a sense—their skin photosynthesizes in a limited way, their bodies have become trellises for the climbing plants that thread through everything. But they are alive. They are waiting.

What they listen for, no one can say. Perhaps the footsteps of whatever species carved this city, returning at last. Perhaps seismic signals from the distant pillars, communications in a frequency that takes years to arrive. Perhaps nothing at all—perhaps listening has become its own purpose, a meditation so deep it has forgotten what it was meditating on. Their clustered eyes track the sun not because they need to see it but because it is the only thing that moves, and movement is what they are tuned to perceive. When something crosses their field of vision—a falling salt-bead, a Tender on patrol, you—they do not react. They simply *note*. Somewhere in their elongated bodies, in whatever passes for their memory, your presence has been recorded. Long after you leave, long after you die, a Listener will still hold the shape of your movement through the terrace it guards.

They feed, but feeding is almost incidental. Their mouthparts work at the waxy leaves the way your lungs work at air: autonomically, constantly, beneath the threshold of intention. They require so little. They have slowed their metabolisms to the pace of the pillar itself, burning through calories the way the salt-stone erodes—imperceptibly, geologically, over timespans that make a human lifetime seem like a flashbulb.

Once every few years, a Listener moves. It unthreads itself from the vegetation that has grown over it, tearing leaves and stems, leaving a wound in the garden that will take months to heal. It relocates to a different position—sometimes meters away, sometimes on an entirely different terrace—and settles into stillness again. Why? What prompts the relocation? The other Listeners do not react. The Tenders, if they notice the disturbance, simply begin their slow work of repair. No pattern has ever been detected in these movements. Perhaps there is none. Perhaps a Listener simply reaches some internal threshold, some limit on how long it can occupy a single position, and must shift or die.

---

In the upper lattice, inside the skull-sized spheres, **the Dreamers** glow.

No one has ever seen a Dreamer directly. The spheres have openings—small ones, large enough for air to circulate, large enough for the violet light to escape at dusk—but nothing has ever emerged from them, and nothing that enters has ever returned. Researchers (there have been researchers; the pillar has attracted attention over the centuries, brief flurries of study that always end in abandonment) have lowered recording devices into the spheres and retrieved only static, or silence, or sounds so low-frequency they were felt rather than heard, vibrations that induced unease without explanation.

What is known: the Dreamers produce light. The Dreamers produce heat—slightly, enough to keep their spheres a few degrees warmer than the surrounding stone. The Dreamers produce a smell, detectable only in the still hours of deepest night, something like ozone and something like honey and something like the memory of water. The Dreamers do not move, or if they move, they move so slowly that motion-triggered sensors have never captured it. The Dreamers may be one organism or many, a single distributed being occupying every sphere or a community of individuals in permanent isolation. The Dreamers may have been here before the city was carved, the spheres shaped around them. The Dreamers may have arrived afterward, colonizing the architecture the way the plants colonized it, the way the Tenders and Listeners colonized it, opportunists finding niches.

At dusk, when the violet light pulses—it does pulse, slowly, over the course of hours, brightening and dimming in rhythms that almost but never quite synchronize—the Listeners in the middle terraces orient toward it. Their clustered eyes turn upward for the only time. They watch the glow until full darkness falls and the glow becomes the only light on the pillar, brighter than stars, visible from kilometers away across the basin.

What passes between Dreamer and Listener in those hours? The Tenders do not look up. The Dreamers never descend. But something is exchanged, some recognition, some confirmation. The pillar knows itself through this nightly communion, each part acknowledging the others across the vertical distance that separates them.

---

There are **other things**. 

In the crevices where the ramps have crumbled, where even the Tenders cannot reach, something like a worm threads through the salt-stone itself, digesting minerals directly, leaving tunnels finer than hair. These tunnels eventually compromise the structure they pass through; sections of terrace collapse without warning, falling to the basin floor in explosions of white dust that take hours to settle. The worm-things are not malicious. They are not anything. They are simply a process, like erosion, like crystallization, like the slow closing of doorways.

On the north face of the pillar, where shade persists through most of the day, a different ecology has established itself. The plants here are paler, more delicate, their leaves thin enough to see through. Among them moves something that is definitely not a Tender, not a Listener, not anything else documented—a quick, darting presence that has only ever been glimpsed at the edge of vision. It may be a predator. It may be the only predator the pillar hosts, feeding on the slow and the still, pruning the system in its own way. No one has ever seen it feed. No one has ever found remains.

And in the deepest chamber, at the pillar's core, accessible only through a passage that narrows to the point of impossibility, there is rumored to be water. Not much. A seep, a trickle, moisture condensing from some underground source and collecting in a depression no larger than a cupped palm. The Tenders who live closest to this chamber are larger than their kin, their shells thicker, their movements even slower. They guard something, though whether it is the water or something else, something the water sustains, no one can say. No researcher has ever reached the chamber. The passage is too narrow, the guardians too patient, the pillar too unwilling to yield its deepest secret.

---

They have been here longer than the city. This is the secret the architecture almost reveals, if you look closely enough at the carvings, at the proportions of the doorways and stairs. The city was not built *for* these creatures—but it was built *around* them, shaped to accommodate presences that were already ancient when the carvers arrived. The stairs are too steep for Tenders, but there are ramps beside them, original ramps, part of the first design. The spheres in the upper lattice are too regular to be natural, but the Dreamers inside them—or whatever the Dreamers evolved from—were here first, and the spheres were shaped to honor them, to house them, to keep them.

The carvers are gone. Their purpose is forgotten. But the accommodation remains, built into the bones of the place, and the creatures who live here now live in a temple that was made for them by worshippers they will never know, could never have known, beings from an era so distant that even the salt-stone does not remember.

---

If you stay long enough—days, weeks, the time it takes for the pillar to begin accepting your presence—you will notice them noticing you.

Not reacting. Not responding. Just... including you in their awareness, the way they include the sun, the way they include the slow shifting of the basin floor, the way they include everything that enters their world and becomes, for a time, part of its system.

A Tender will adjust its patrol to account for your shadow. A Listener will incorporate your breathing into whatever frequency it monitors. The violet glow at dusk will seem, just for a moment, to pulse in rhythm with your heartbeat—and then the rhythm will diverge, and you will realize you were imagining it, or you will realize you were not imagining it and something has taken your measure and moved on.

You are not unwelcome. You are not welcome. You are simply *present*, another temporary variation, another brief perturbation in the long, slow, silent life of the pillar.

When you leave—and you will leave; the pillar does not hold, does not trap, does not need you—you will carry the silence with you. It will live behind your eyes for years. You will find yourself pausing in crowded rooms, straining to hear something beneath the noise, some frequency you almost learned to perceive. You will find yourself moving more slowly, speaking less, watching the interplay of light and shadow on ordinary walls as though it might resolve into meaning.

The pillar gave you nothing. The pillar took nothing. But you were inside it, for a time, and now some part of it is inside you, crystallizing slowly, filling in the spaces between your thoughts with patient mineral silence.

Somewhere far away, in a chamber you will never find again, something turns an egg one fraction of a degree.

And waits.
