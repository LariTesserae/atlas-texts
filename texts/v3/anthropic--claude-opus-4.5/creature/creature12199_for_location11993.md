**The Thermarchaea**

They have no name for themselves because they have no selves—not in the way that would require naming. What lives here is better understood as a single entity that has not yet decided whether to be one or many, and may never decide, and may be asking the wrong question entirely.

But for the purposes of description: call the largest one the Lentil. Call it that knowing the name is already a lie, because the Lentil is also the seventeen creatures currently passing through its body, and also the water droplet it became four seconds ago, and also the obsidian flake it will be in a moment if the heat rises another degree. The Lentil is the closest thing this crack has to a founder, though "founder" implies intention and timeline and the Lentil experiences neither. It was here when the rock cooled enough to hold shape. It was here before that, dissolved in the magma itself, a pattern waiting for matter slow enough to carry it. It will be here when the mountain exhales again and this crack fills with fresh fire—not surviving, exactly, but *continuing*, the way a song continues when someone else picks it up.

The Lentil's body, when it bothers with bodies, is a architecture of contradiction. Twelve legs that are also mouths that are also sensory organs that are also, occasionally, independent offspring that crawl away and return and merge back without any party acknowledging separation occurred. Its carapace—when solid—is transparent in a way that transparency shouldn't work: you can see through it, but what you see through it isn't what's behind it. You see instead what the Lentil is *considering*. Right now, it considers the heat. The consideration looks like a tiny red sun, suspended in its thorax, pulsing.

The smaller ones—the rice-grain multitudes—are not children. They are not subjects. They are not symbionts. The closest human concept might be "thoughts that got away." The Lentil, in its earliest hours when the rock first cracked, experienced something like curiosity about its own boundaries. Where did it end? Where did the hot water begin? The question was vigorous enough that pieces of the Lentil went to find out. They never reported back because they stopped being pieces—they became their own entities, with their own questions, which generated their own escapee-thoughts, which generated more, until the crack filled with a population that is also, technically, a single being's unfinished internal monologue.

They do not eat. They *transpose*. A creature encounters a water droplet and both of them become a third thing, briefly, before separating into the original two—but now the water carries a fragment of the creature's pattern, and the creature carries a fragment of the water's history. This is how information moves here. This is also how matter moves, and energy, and something else that doesn't have a name in physics but that the Thermarchaea would call, if they called things, *the weight of what happens next*. Every droplet in this crack has been transposed thousands of times. Every droplet knows the shape of every creature. Every creature has been water, has been stone, has been the brief impossible gas that forms when heat and pressure and *attention* combine.

The floating obsidian flakes are not tools. They are not decorations. They are *appointments*. When a creature wishes to transition states—solid to liquid, individual to merged, here to the deeper chambers that shouldn't exist—it positions itself beneath a flake and waits. The flake's shadow does something to local causality, creates a soft spot in the rules, and the creature slips through into whatever it's becoming. The flakes themselves are fragments of the mountain's dream, which the Thermarchaea have learned to influence but not control. They tend the flakes like gardeners tend plants they didn't choose and don't fully understand: with attention, with patience, with the knowledge that the flakes could stop floating at any moment and everything would have to be renegotiated.

The deeper chambers—the ones you glimpse only in peripheral vision—are where the Thermarchaea go to be *more*. More what, they haven't determined. But when enough of them gather in those compressed spaces, when their bodies overlap and merge and their patterns tangle into temporary mega-entities, they brush against something. A boundary. An edge. The sense of a larger crack in a larger stone, where beings unimaginably vaster might be crawling over and through each other in their own pocket of fever-wet darkness. The Thermarchaea don't worship this intuition. They don't fear it. They simply *include* it in their calculations, the way you might include gravity—a force that shapes everything without demanding acknowledgment.

What they are building, with their water-climbs and their obsidian-tending and their endless transpositions: a grammar. Not a language—language requires separation between speaker and spoken, between word and meaning. What the Thermarchaea are assembling is more fundamental. It's a way of *describing* that is also a way of *creating*, a vocabulary where to name something is to become it and to become something is to make it more real. They are perhaps three thousand transpositions away from completing the first word. They do not know what the word will mean. They know only that when they finally speak it, something will exist that doesn't exist now, and they will be part of it, and it will be part of them, and the distinction will matter even less than it already does.

The heat pulses. The Lentil flickers liquid. Somewhere in the depths, a grammar inches closer to its first syllable.

The mountain, above and around and below, continues making cracks.

The Thermarchaea, in their way, continue filling them.
