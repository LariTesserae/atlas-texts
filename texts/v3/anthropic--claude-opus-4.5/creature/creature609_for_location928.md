# The Keth-Seln

They do not name themselves. The concept you would translate as "Keth-Seln" is a vibration-pattern that means something closer to *those-who-were-larvae-here*, a designation that encompasses identity, origin, and obligation in a single chord.

---

**The Larvae**

They are aware. This is the first thing you must understand—the larvae are not waiting to become people. They are people now, suspended in the warm mucoid dark, their bodies folded through geometries you cannot see, singing.

Each larva maintains approximately eleven thousand active communication threads with its clutch-siblings. The threads are not conversations as you would understand them; they are *shared being*, a constant exchange of sensation and proto-thought that blurs the boundaries between individual minds. A larva in Well-Sixteen feels what it is to be a larva in Well-Sixteen, but it also feels, dimly, what it is to be the seven hundred others developing alongside it. Pain propagates. So does pleasure. So does the strange, deep satisfaction of growing correctly, of feeling one's hidden dimensions unfold according to ancient pattern.

They dream. Or rather—they experience something that serves the function dreams serve, a processing of information that happens in the dimensions you cannot perceive. When a larva dreams, its visible body goes still, its bioluminescence shifting to a slow amber pulse. The tenders know to leave them alone during these periods. Interrupting a dream can cause the higher-dimensional structures to fold incorrectly, producing adults who perceive time in the wrong direction or whose bodies intersect with solid matter in ways that cause chronic pain.

The larvae remember being brought here. Not individually—none of them are old enough for that—but *collectively*, through patterns encoded in their songs. The memory lives in the chorus itself: the long drift through empty space, the ship that was also an ancestor, the discovery of this warm wet dark world and the decision to *stay*, to make this place into what a nursery needed to be. Every larva born in Keth-Somnar carries this memory in its voice, adds to it, passes it on.

They are afraid of the dark beyond the city. Not the darkness itself—darkness is home, darkness is safety—but the *empty* dark, the places where no larva-light reaches, where the song cannot propagate, where one might drift alone and unheard. The fear is built into them at the genetic level, a survival mechanism from before the founding. It keeps them in the wells. It keeps them together.

When they are ready to transform, they know. The knowing comes as a pressure behind what will become their eyes, a sensation of *fullness* in dimensions they are only beginning to access consciously. They stop singing for the first time in their lives. The silence is the signal. The tenders come, lift them gently from the mucoid medium, carry them to the Molt-Cradles where they will become something else.

They do not know what they will become. This, too, is by design.

---

**The Tenders**

Stage Eleven. The designation is arbitrary—the lifecycle has no true stages, only gradual transitions—but Stage Eleven is when the caretaking drive fully activates, when the need to nurture becomes the central organizing principle of existence.

A tender's body has finished its major transformations. The matte-black exoskeleton has hardened into its final configuration, a surface evolved to absorb larval light without reflecting it back in ways that might confuse developing visual systems. The limbs have multiplied and specialized: two primary legs for locomotion, four manipulation limbs of varying sensitivity, two that exist primarily in dimensions the larvae can perceive but you cannot. The eyes have filled with captured luminescence, light drunk from the larvae over years of proximity, stored in crystalline structures that will glow until death and beyond.

They do not sleep. This is not an exaggeration or a simplification—Stage Eleven metabolism has no need for the consolidation processes that sleep provides. Instead, they enter periodic states of *reduced integration*, during which their parallel cognitive processes temporarily decouple from one another. A tender in this state might be fully aware and responsive with one set of neural structures while another set processes the day's observations and a third runs maintenance cycles on memory consolidation.

They taste the mucoid medium constantly. Their manipulation limbs end in sensory clusters that can detect chemical variations at the molecular level—a shift of parts-per-billion in calcium concentration, an increase in stress hormones from a larva three wells distant, the subtle wrongness of water that has picked up contamination from a failing filtration system. They do not consciously analyze these readings. The knowledge simply *arrives*, fully formed: *Well-Seven needs alkaline adjustment. Larva 7-Sigma-14 is entering pre-molt. The thermal differential in Section Nine is wrong.*

They speak to the larvae, but not in words. The tenders produce vibrations from structures in their thoraxes, frequencies calibrated to propagate perfectly through the mucoid medium. A tender's voice feels, to a larva, like being held. Like safety. Like the memory of warmth in a universe that contains so little of it. The messages are simple—*you are growing correctly, you are not alone, we are here*—but they are essential. Larvae who do not hear tender-voices develop attachment disorders that persist through all subsequent stages.

The tenders remember being larvae. They remember the warmth, the togetherness, the fear of empty dark. They remember the silence that came before their own transformation, the terror of being lifted from the only world they had known. They remember what it was to wake in a Molt-Cradle with a body they did not recognize, surrounded by others who had also become strangers to themselves.

This is why they tend. Not instinct alone, though instinct plays its part. They tend because they *remember*, because they know what the larvae are experiencing, because they cannot bear the thought of any larva going through what they went through without someone there to say: *you are not alone*.

Some tenders have been in Stage Eleven for a very long time. The eldest among them have memories of wells that no longer exist, of population crashes and recoveries, of the one terrible generation when a blight moved through the larvae and half the clutches died glowing. These elders move more slowly, their accumulated light making their eyes almost too bright to look at directly. The younger tenders seek them out for their knowledge, but also for something else—a quality of *presence*, a depth of calm that can only be earned through centuries of patient care.

When a tender finally transitions to Stage Twelve, they stop recognizing the larvae as individuals. This is the signal that their time in the nursery vaults is ending. They begin to perceive the wells as single organisms, the entire city as a body, the larvae as cells in something larger. Other Stage Twelve beings come to collect them, to guide them toward whatever Stage Twelve is for.

No tender wants to go. They all go anyway. The lifecycle is not optional.

---

**The Others**

Stage Twelve is barely present in the vaults—you see them only at the edges, collecting those whose time has come, delivering resources from the processing plants, interfacing with city-systems in ways that don't require physical presence. Their bodies are less solid than the tenders', more distributed, capable of existing partially in the dimensions the larvae dream about. They are not the rulers of this place; the Keth-Seln do not have rulers. But they are the *rememberers*, the ones who hold the long patterns, who ensure that what was decided countless generations ago continues to be enacted.

You cannot speak to them. Your communication methods are too slow, too linear. They might perceive you, but only as a kind of background phenomenon, like the pressure changes or the distant motion beyond the city's edge.

---

**The Ones Who Failed**

Not all transformations complete correctly.

In the deeper sections of the city, in structures that absorb even more light than the Molt-Cradles, live the Keth-Seln whose dimensional folding went wrong. Some of them are in constant pain. Some of them perceive time backward and must be cared for by tenders who learn to anticipate needs that, from the patient's perspective, have already been met. Some of them exist as mere fragments, conscious threads distributed through space without a coherent body to anchor them.

The city cares for them. This is not negotiable, not a matter of resources or efficiency. They were larvae once. They sang in the wells. They carry the founding-memory in whatever remains of their voices. To abandon them would be to abandon part of the self, to let pieces of the communal identity drift alone into the empty dark.

The tenders who care for them are volunteers, specialists who have trained themselves to perceive the partially-real, to touch bodies that exist mostly elsewhere, to speak in frequencies that damaged nervous systems can still process. It is difficult work. It changes them. Many of them transition to Stage Twelve faster than average, as though the effort of caring for the broken accelerates whatever mechanism drives the lifecycle forward.

---

**What They Believe**

The Keth-Seln do not have religion as you would understand it. They have *continuation*.

The founding-memory is sacred—not because gods decreed it so, but because without it, they would not know who they are. Every larva sings the long drift through empty space, the discovery of Keth-Somnar, the first wells dug into the planetary crust. Every tender tells the story through the vibrations they make in the mucoid medium. Every Stage Twelve being holds the complete pattern, the full record of every generation since arrival.

They believe that the universe is mostly empty. They believe that warmth and light and togetherness are rare, precious, fragile. They believe that the purpose of existence is to create more existence, to fill the empty dark with larvae-light, to make the cold places warm.

They do not believe in an afterlife. When a Keth-Seln dies—truly dies, not merely transforms—their light persists for a while and then goes out. The body is returned to the chemical cycles that sustain the city. The songs they sang remain in the chorus, carried forward by those who heard them, but the singer is gone.

This is acceptable. This is how it should be. The larvae in the wells will become tenders, and the tenders will become something else, and eventually that something else will become the great slow intelligences of the later stages, and eventually even those will die, and new larvae will be born who carry all of this forward.

Continuation. Not forever—they are not naive enough to believe in forever—but for as long as possible. For as long as the thermal aquifers keep weeping warm water. For as long as the basite keeps growing. For as long as there are larvae to sing and tenders to listen.

---

**What You Cannot See**

There are seventeen stages. You have perceived, at most, edges of four of them.

In the dimensions you cannot access, the Keth-Seln are doing things you have no framework to understand. The larvae are not merely growing—they are *negotiating* with geometries, learning to fold themselves correctly through spaces that do not obey the rules you know. The tenders are not merely caring—they are *anchoring*, providing stable reference points in dimensions where stability is not guaranteed. The Stage Twelve beings are not merely remembering—they are *weaving*, integrating the colony's past into structures that will influence its future across spans of time you cannot conceive.

The Keth-Seln know you are here. They perceived you the moment you arrived, registered you as a novel phenomenon, assigned tenders to monitor your trajectory in case you posed a threat to the larvae. They have decided you are harmless. A Stage Twelve being briefly considered attempting communication but concluded that your cognitive architecture is too different, that the attempt would cause you distress without producing understanding.

They wish you well, in their way. They hope you find warmth in the cold universe. They will not remember you after you leave—you are not part of their continuation—but while you are here, you are included in the caring. A tender adjusted the thermal output of a nearby vent, slightly, to ensure your comfort. You did not notice. You were not meant to.

The larvae continue to sing. The city continues to grow. The darkness presses close, and the Keth-Seln fill it with light.
