**The Tenders**

They are not born here—nothing is truly *born* here—but they arose, at some point, from the same unfinished substrate as everything else. Three of them, or perhaps one of them in three locations, or perhaps the number itself is still under negotiation.

Each appears as a shape that your eyes keep trying to resolve into something familiar and failing. The closest approximation: imagine a hand with too many joints, if the hand were made of the same pale not-ite as the walls, if it moved with the deliberate slowness of someone carrying something infinitely fragile. They have no faces, but they have *attention*—you can feel when it's on you, a gentle pressure like being remembered by someone you've never met.

Their work is sorting. Endlessly, carefully sorting.

One tends to the beetles, guiding the rivers of soft shells away from the patches of wall that are trying to become something sharp. It does this by placing itself in their path and *waiting*—not blocking, just offering an alternative, the way a stone in a stream doesn't stop water but suggests other routes. The beetles flow around it, clicking their gratitude or complaint in a language that won't exist for another thousand years.

Another monitors the salamanders, watching for the ones whose developmental stages have gotten stuck—too much gill, not enough lung, or the reverse. When it finds one struggling, it cups the creature in whatever it has instead of palms and holds it near one of the hovering water pockets until something eases, until the creature's body remembers it has choices yet to make. Sometimes the salamander dissolves back into potential. The Tender does not grieve this. Dissolution is not failure here; it is revision.

The third—or the same one, relocated—attends to the breathing thing in the corner. This is delicate work. The breathing thing is close to becoming, closer than anything else in the nursery, and what it becomes matters in ways the Tender seems to understand but cannot explain. It adjusts the angles around the creature, pressing gently on the unset walls to change the geometry of the space, to offer the becoming-mammal different possibilities for what a body could mean. Sometimes it simply sits beside the thing and breathes with it, modeling the rhythm, suggesting without insisting.

They do not speak, but they communicate—with each other, with the nursery itself—through a system of pressures and absences. A Tender will pause in a doorway-that-isn't and the walls will soften slightly in response. Another will tap a rhythm against the limestone shelf overhead and the sourceless light will dim, just enough for the moths to rest their still-crumpled wings.

The surveyor's equipment bothers them. You can tell by the way they circle it, by the tension in their too-many joints. Not because it's foreign—everything here is foreign until it isn't—but because it arrived *decided*. The tripod knows it should have three legs. The notebook knows it should hold words. This certainty is an irritant, a splinter of finality in a space devoted to the unfinished. The Tenders have been working on the boot for longer than it's been here, trying to coax it back toward possibility, to help it forget it was ever worn. The leather has begun to soften. Given time—and time moves strangely here, pooling in corners like the wrong-way water—it might unbecome enough to belong.

When you enter, they stop.

Not dramatically, not with alarm—they simply pause in their sorting and tending and turn their faceless attention toward you. The pressure of their regard is not hostile but *diagnostic*. They are determining what you are, how finished, how much friction you'll create against the nursery's slow work.

They find you very finished indeed. Complete. Bounded. A thing that has made its choices and must live inside them.

One of them approaches—gliding or walking or simply being nearer than it was—and extends something like a limb. It doesn't touch you. It touches the air around you, tracing the outline of your certainty, the hard edges of your decided self. You feel it as a faint warmth, or a faint sadness, or both.

Then it withdraws, and the three of them resume their work, moving around you now as the beetles move around them—not blocking, just offering alternative routes to a visitor who cannot stay, who was never meant to be here, whose finished presence is a kindness to leave behind.

But as you go—as you squeeze back through the crack and feel the condensation brushing past you on its upward journey—you notice that one of them has followed you to the threshold. It waits there, at the edge of the nursery, watching you emerge into the world of gravity and shadow and completed things.

It might be waving goodbye.

It might be taking notes on how a finished thing moves, so it can better teach the breathing creature in the corner what not to become.

It might simply be learning what *threshold* means, adding the concept to its collection of unfinished knowledge, filing it somewhere between *farewell* and *return* in a system only it understands.

The crack closes behind you—not sealing, just narrowing back to the width of a child's fist, to the size of a question you can't quite remember asking.

Below, the Tenders return to their work. The beetles flow. The salamanders hover. The breathing thing in the corner takes another breath, and another, practicing for a life it hasn't chosen yet, tended by shapes that love it in a language still inventing itself, one unfinished gesture at a time.
