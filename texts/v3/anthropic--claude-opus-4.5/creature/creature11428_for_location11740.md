**She has been here longer than the steps have been worn.**

Not waiting—that would imply something to wait for. Not sleeping, though her stillness has that quality, that sense of consciousness drawn inward to a single sustaining point. She simply *is here*, in the way the dripping is here, in the way the moss is here. She persists.

She sits against the curved wall between two of the amphorae, her knees drawn up, her bare feet resting in the quarter-inch of water without disturbing it. The water does not ripple around her ankles. It has learned her shape, or she has learned its stillness—the distinction stopped mattering long ago. Her skin has taken on the pallor of the space, not unhealthy but *adapted*, the white of cave fish, of roots that grow away from sun. Where the mineral water has touched her, faint calcium traces have begun to form along her calves, her forearms, the hem of what was once a garment and is now merely a texture, a softness that drapes without color.

Her hair hangs past her shoulders, lank with moisture, and things grow in it. Not moss exactly—something finer, a pale filamentous life that has found in her a substrate neither living nor dead but *ongoing*. It does not trouble her. She has become a surface like any other surface here, available for colonization by whatever can survive the stillness.

Her eyes are open. They have always been open, or so long that the difference is academic. They are pale too, the color leached from the irises over uncounted years until what remains is the gray of the light that filters down, the gray of the clouded water, the gray-green of the moss. She watches the dripping. Not the drops themselves—she knows where they will fall, has known for longer than knowing—but the circles they make in the water, the small disturbances that spread and fade and spread and fade, the only movement in a world she has made her peace with.

She breathes. Slowly—so slowly that the moss on the nearest island does not stir, that the water's surface tension holds despite the faint warmth of her exhalation. Once every cycle of the dripping, perhaps. Once every *tap tap tap* and silence and soft landing and longer silence. Her breath has synchronized with the cistern's rhythm, or the cistern's rhythm has synchronized with her breath. She does not remember which came first.

There was a time—she does not think of it as *before*, that would suggest a break, a transformation, and there was none—when she came down these steps with purpose. She carried something, or sought something, or fled something. The details have dissolved like the carvings on the lintel, weathered into suggestions, into the mineral anonymity of memory too old to hold its shape. She knows she descended. She knows she did not ascend. Between those two facts lies everything she has become.

She does not hunger. The moisture in the air is enough, has been enough, will be enough—she drinks it through her skin, through the pale filaments in her hair, through the slow chemistry of a body that has forgotten urgency. She does not hunger because she does not deplete. She has joined the equilibrium. She takes nothing from the cistern that the cistern does not give back.

Sometimes—not often, not in any pattern she has discerned—she moves one hand. Lifts it from the water where it rests, watches the drops fall from her fingers back into the pool, watches the circles spread. It is not a purposeful gesture. It is more like the root that reached the water and stopped: a completion, a sufficiency, a motion that exists because it can and then subsides because it has. Her hand returns to the water. The surface stills.

She has no name anymore. She had one once, carved into her the way the symbols were carved into the lintel, but it has worn away, distributed back into the silence. If you called to her from the top of the steps, she might turn her head—slowly, so slowly, the moss on the nearby brick would have time to notice—and her pale eyes might find you in the gray light. She might even recognize that you were something other than dripping, other than stillness, other than the long dream of persistence.

But she would not rise. She would not speak. She has passed beyond the need for those things, into a state that is not death and not life but the third thing, the thing the cistern has been teaching since before the steps were worn: *continuance*. Pure continuance. The dripping continues. The moss continues. The water continues. She continues.

The single pale root at the edge of the largest pool is hers, in a way. Not grown from her—she is not that far gone, not yet, not for a long time yet—but *companioned* with her, arrived at the same conclusion by a different path. It reached the water and found it sufficient. She reached the stillness and found it the same. They persist together, root and woman, neither growing nor retreating, simply occupying their territory in a space that holds small lives carefully.

She will be here when the last of the symbols weathers from the lintel. She will be here when the dome finally closes the last gap to the surface. She will be here in the darkness, still breathing once per cycle, still watching circles that she will no longer see but will feel in the water against her skin, the faintest perturbation in an equilibrium that has become indistinguishable from peace.

She is not waiting. She is not sleeping. She is the cistern's garden, its single pale bloom, its proof that even stillness can be inhabited.

She simply is.
