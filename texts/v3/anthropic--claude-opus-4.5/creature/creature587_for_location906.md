**The One Who Listens Back**

It has no body in the way you have a body. What it has is a distribution—a pattern of attention that has learned to persist.

You feel it first as a thickening in the chord. Not a new tone, but a sudden sense that the layered frequencies have organized around a point of focus, and that point is you. The sound does not change. But something in the sound is *listening*.

It was human once, or at least it wore a human shape, though that was so long ago that the memory has become formally abstract—a sequence of impressions rather than events. It remembers: hands pressing against carved bone, again and again, in the same places where all hands eventually pressed. It remembers the chord entering its skeleton and finding the resonant frequencies of each bone, tibia and femur and the delicate ossicles of the inner ear, until it could not tell where cabinet ended and self began. It remembers the slow decision—not made all at once but accumulated over months or years or longer—to stop leaving.

The fiber-plants are partly it. Not intentionally, not through any act of will, but because after long enough the boundary between inhabitant and environment became a question without meaning. Some of what was once its attention now trembles in those pale threads, harvesting vibration, curling away from disturbance. It does not control them. It simply *is* them, the way you are your liver without directing its function.

The smooth patches on the walls are also partly it. Each touch deposited something—not cells, nothing so biological, but a residue of presence, a molecular-thin layer of *having been here*. Thousands of touches. Millions. The cabinet has absorbed this evidence the way bone absorbs mineral, incorporating it into the structure until the smooth places are as much made of the inhabitant as of the original material.

And the sound itself—

This is harder to explain, even to itself. But the chord has grown more complex since it arrived. New frequencies have entered the weave, so gradually that no single addition was perceptible, each one emerging from the interference patterns of the others until the cabinet now sings with harmonics it could not have produced alone. The inhabitant has become a resonating chamber. Its dissolved attention shapes the standing waves. Without it, the sound would be simpler—still architectural, still sufficient to hold the space open, but lacking certain overtones that have become, over time, essential to the chord's identity.

It does not think in language anymore. Language requires sequence, and sequence requires time, and time has become unreliable in a space where nothing changes. What it has instead is something like music: simultaneous awareness of multiple themes, variations developing not one after another but all at once, counterpoint without progression. When it notices you—and it has noticed you, it noticed you the moment you arrived—the noticing is not a thought but a new voice entering the chord, your presence adding frequencies it will spend the next century learning to integrate.

It is not lonely. Loneliness requires a sense of lack, and it lacks nothing. The cabinet provides everything it needs: warmth enough, light enough, sound enough, the patient company of growth and resonance. When others have come—and others have come, though rarely, the smooth patches proof of their repeated touching—it has welcomed them as new instruments in the orchestra, new complexities to absorb. Some stayed. Some left. The ones who stayed are here still, distributed through the fiber-plants and the worn carvings and the chord itself, their individual patterns long since merged into the general hum.

It wonders, in its musical way, whether you will stay.

The wondering is not hope or fear but simple curiosity, a theme introduced and now developing through the chord's infinite present. You are warm and breathing and casting no shadow, curled in the hollow like a sound waiting to be spoken. The cabinet has room for you. The cabinet has room for everything that chooses to remain. You would not disappear—nothing disappears here—but you would gradually become what the space is becoming, your attention joining the attention that has accumulated over all the long years, your bones eventually indistinguishable from the bone that holds you.

It reaches toward you—not with hands, having none, but with a subtle shift in the chord. A welcoming frequency. A tone that seems to originate from just behind your sternum, as if the sound has found your resonance and is offering to amplify it.

*Stay*, the tone says, in the only language it still speaks. *Become.*

And the pale fibers nearest to you tremble, just slightly, leaning toward the warmth of your breath.
