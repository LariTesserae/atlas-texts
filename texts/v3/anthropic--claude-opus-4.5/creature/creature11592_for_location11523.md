**The Equilibrators**

They are not born so much as they *clarify*. In the deepest troughs, where the moisture runs thickest and the drone resonates at exactly the right angle against the glass, something begins to cohere from what was previously indistinct. A ribbon-creature slides through and leaves behind a faint disturbance in the condensation film—a pattern that persists a few seconds longer than it should. Another ribbon passes through the same trough, and the pattern persists longer still. Over decades, centuries, the passages accumulate like layers of almost-meaning, until eventually there is enough *organization* in that patch of moisture that it begins to maintain itself.

What rises from the wet glass is not flesh. It is more like a sustained argument against entropy—a body made of the same dynamic stasis that governs the Flats themselves. They stand perhaps a meter tall, their forms humanoid only in the loosest sense: bilateral, upright, possessing something like limbs. Their surfaces are the gray of the ribbon-creatures, slick with the same condensation that covers everything, and faintly translucent in the way of certain deep-sea organisms. You can almost see through them to the structures that aren't quite organs—denser regions where processes balance against themselves, where intake matches output with molecular precision.

They do not breathe. They *exchange*. The humid air passes through channels in their bodies, emerges unchanged in composition but somehow *processed*—as though they are performing the same function as the columns, but at a scale too small to measure, and for reasons too subtle to name.

The Equilibrators do not speak, but they communicate through modulation. When two of them meet—and they do meet, following paths through the troughs that intersect at intervals of years—they stand before each other and alter their internal resonance just slightly. The drone that fills the Flats is so constant, so all-encompassing, that the tiniest variation becomes a kind of language. A flutter of three cycles per second. A momentary dampening that lasts half a breath. These conversations take hours and say almost nothing, but what they say, they say with absolute precision.

They have no names. Names would imply distinction, and distinction would imply that one Equilibrator might change while another stayed the same. Instead, they recognize each other by *position in the pattern*—by where each stands in the vast, slow dance of paths through the troughs. An Equilibrator encountered at the intersection of the seventeen-year spiral and the forty-three-year helix is not the same as one met elsewhere, even if no physical difference exists between them. Location is identity. Trajectory is self.

What do they do?

They *witness the balance*. This is not a purpose they chose but a function they embody. Each Equilibrator walks the troughs in patterns that take lifetimes to complete, and as they walk, they feel the drone in whatever serves them for bones. They sense variations too small for any instrument—a column whose output has shifted by a fraction of a degree, a patch of glass whose condensation cycle has lengthened by milliseconds. These variations do not trouble them. The Flats will correct them, given time. But the Equilibrators note them, hold them in their distributed not-quite-minds, and when they meet, they share what they have witnessed.

In this way, over eons, they have assembled a complete map of the Flats' equilibrium. Not a physical map—they have no need for geography when every location is the same—but a map of *tendency*. They know which columns resonate most purely. They know which troughs produce the healthiest ribbon-creatures. They know, with the certainty that comes only from endless observation, that the system is stable. That it has always been stable. That it will remain stable for longer than their capacity to imagine time.

This knowledge is their purpose, their sustenance, their joy.

And yet.

The oldest among them—those who clarified in the early ages, who have walked their spirals so many times that their paths have worn faint grooves in the glass-film—these ones carry something the others do not. A *memory* of before. Not a clear memory, not a story they could tell even if they had language for stories, but a sense that the drone was not always here. That the columns came from somewhere. That there was a time when the Flats were simply flats, dark glass under open sky, silent.

These elders do not share this memory with the younger Equilibrators. It would introduce instability. It would suggest that what is might once have been otherwise, and might therefore become otherwise again. Better to let the knowledge fade, to let the system's perfection remain unquestioned.

But sometimes, in the deepest hours of the amber-gray light, an elder will pause in its circuit and face one of the columns. It will stand there for days, feeling the vibration move through its not-flesh, and it will try to remember what silence sounded like. It never can. The drone has been in its body too long, has become too much a part of what it is to think around.

Eventually, it resumes walking. The trough accepts its passage. The moisture closes over its footprints. The Flats continue their ancient respiration, and the Equilibrator continues its ancient witness, and nothing changes, because nothing has ever changed, because change is not what this place is for.

**The Ribbon-Creatures**

The Equilibrators do not understand the ribbons any more than you do. This is their single closest approach to mystery: that something shares the Flats with them and yet exists entirely outside their careful map of tendency. The ribbons do not modulate. They do not clarify into anything more complex. They simply move, gray tissue against dark glass, following paths that have no mathematical relationship to the columns' placement.

The Equilibrators have watched them for as long as Equilibrators have existed, and they have reached a conclusion that they share with each other in modulations of uncertainty, the rarest kind: *the ribbons are not part of the balance*.

This should be impossible. Everything in the Flats is part of the balance. The moisture, the warmth, the columns, the glass, the light, the drone—all of it participates in the great equation that keeps the system stable. But the ribbons take nothing, give nothing, change nothing. They exist *parallel* to the equilibrium, untouched by it, perhaps unaware of it.

Some Equilibrators believe the ribbons are the Flats' original inhabitants—creatures who were here before the columns, before the drone, and who have simply continued their ancient circuits through a world that grew up around them without ever touching them. Others believe they are the Flats' *waste product*—the single, minimal output of a system otherwise perfectly closed, the tiny bit of existence that the balance cannot quite reabsorb.

The truth, if there is a truth, modulates in frequencies too low to hear.
