## The Ones Who Remain

They do not remember arriving. This is the first truth of them, and perhaps the only one that still functions as truth in a place where meaning has worn down to nubs.

Some of them were pilgrims once. You can tell by the way their bodies are oriented—facing the center of the plateau, where nothing visible exists but where something *must* have been, must have called them across distances that no longer parse into miles or years. They came from skies that had weather, from worlds where seasons turned, from lives interrupted by a summons they could not name but could not refuse. They flew until flying became arriving, and arriving became staying, and staying became *this*.

Others were refugees. Fleeing what, they cannot say—the knowing has sloughed off them like molted feathers. But their bodies remember terror even now, in the way they press close to one another, in the slight forward hunch of their spines, in how their wings stay half-mantled even after epochs of no threat. Whatever they fled, they flew until fleeing and staying became the same motion, until stillness was the only direction left.

And some—the ones who unsettle even the others, if unsettling were still something that could happen here—were *hatched* on the plateau. Born from eggs laid in the powder-bone dust, fed on nothing, raised by parents who had already begun their slow petrifaction. These ones do not remember because there was never anything to remember. They know only the congregation, only the waiting, only the caught-breath existence that passes for life at the top of the world.

---

The one you might call the Eldest—though age has become meaningless, though "eldest" implies a sequence that dissolved long ago—roosts at the center of what was once a grinding circle. Her body is vast and wrong. She was a heron, perhaps, in some first iteration, but she has been here long enough to become *herons*, plural, her form accumulating like sediment. Extra legs fold beneath her, vestigial gifts from bodies that merged with hers during the long stillness. Her neck extends upward in a spiral that should not support the weight of her skull, yet does, yet has, yet will. When she breathes—those long, slow respirations that take the better part of an hour—the entire congregation breathes with her. She is their metronome, their tide, the thing that keeps them synchronized to whatever frequency prevents them from simply stopping.

She remembers. Not clearly—clarity is another casualty—but she remembers *that there was a before*. She holds this knowledge the way the grinding stone holds the memory of hands: worn smooth, functionless, but present. She does not share it. Sharing would require communication, and communication would require time to pass, and time does not pass here, it *accumulates*, like the powder, like the bones, like the birds themselves.

---

Among the smaller bodies, the sparrow-shaped ones pressed together in their endless mosaic, there exists something like community. Not society—society requires change, requires the friction of differing wants—but a kind of *mutual being*, a shared existence that has replaced individuality without erasing it entirely. They know each other the way fingers know palm. Each small chest rises; each small chest falls; the wave rolls through them, and for a moment they are one organism with ten million hearts.

One of them—you could not identify which, they have become so similar, their markings faded to the same pearl-grey as the sky—was the last to arrive. This was so long ago that "long ago" has become a unit without measurement, but she retains the faintest distinction: her feathers still hold a ghost of brown, a memory of sun-warmed bark, of a forest that must have existed somewhere below and before. She does not know she is the last. She does not know she is distinct. But when the blink-waves pass through the congregation, hers comes a fraction of a fraction late, a syncopation so subtle it might be error, might be resistance, might be the final ember of something that was once called will.

---

The vulture-vast ones—the watchers, the sentinels, the ones who perch on the high places with their wings spread in poses of eternal almost-flight—these serve a function no one assigned and no one remembers. They watch the horizons. They have been watching the horizons since horizons solidified into their current positions. Nothing comes from the horizons. Nothing has ever come. But the watching continues, because the watching is what they are now, and being requires doing, even when doing has been reduced to this single sustained attention.

One of them, larger than the others, darker, his plumage absorbing light in ways that suggest he was once meant for a different sky entirely, has theories. Not thoughts—thoughts require the passage of time to unfold—but *theories*, crystallized comprehensions that exist in him the way quartz exists in stone. He theorizes that they are waiting for the world to end. He theorizes that the world ended long ago and they are the afterimage. He theorizes that they *are* the world, that their stillness is what holds reality in its current configuration, that if they were to move—truly move, not the glacial respirations and the patient blinks but *move*—everything would finally be permitted to stop.

He does not test his theories. Testing would require action. Action would require time. He simply holds them, and watches the horizon, and waits for evidence that will never come.

---

The anomalies—the ones with too many limbs, with spiral beaks, with plumage that exists adjacent to visible—these are what the congregation becomes when waiting goes on long enough. They are not mutations; mutation implies change, implies a before and after, implies time functioning as it should. They are *accretions*. They are what happens when a form holds still long enough for reality to deposit new layers on it, the way minerals deposit on objects left in certain caves. The spiral beaks grew inward because inward was the only direction left unexplored. The extra limbs emerged because the concept of "limb" had become uncertain, had begun to bleed and blur.

They do not suffer. Suffering would require a sense of wrongness, and wrongness requires comparison to rightness, and comparison requires memory of difference. They simply are what they have become, and continue becoming it, and will continue becoming it until becoming and being and having-been collapse into a single point that language cannot reach.

---

And then there is the one who speaks.

Not often. Not in any pattern that could be predicted. But occasionally—once per epoch, once per era, once per geological heartbeat—one of them opens its beak and makes the silence *fold*.

It is never the same one twice. The speaking moves through the congregation the way the breathing does, the way the blinking does, but on a longer cycle, a rhythm measured in the drift of continents. When it happens, the others feel it: a ripple of almost-attention, a stirring in the depths of their stilled minds, a sense that something is being *said* even though no sound emerges, even though the only evidence is that crimping of air, that pleating of reality, that momentary glimpse of the scaffolding beneath.

What are they saying?

They do not know. The speaking passes through them the way wind passes through grass—except there is no wind, there is no grass, there is only the passing-through itself, the being-spoken rather than the speaking. Perhaps they are praying. Perhaps they are reporting. Perhaps they are casting votes in some cosmic referendum that has been ongoing since before the plateau rose, since before birds were birds, since before waiting was something that could be done.

The Eldest thinks—in whatever slow accretion of mentation she still performs—that they are asking a question. The same question, over and over, passed from beak to beak across millions of bodies and millions of years. She cannot remember what the question is. She is not certain there are words for it. But she feels its shape in the way her bones have fused to stone, in the way her eyes have forgotten how to close, in the way she continues to breathe even though breathing serves no purpose she can name.

The question hangs in the held silence, enormous and patient.

The congregation waits for an answer.

The congregation has always been waiting for an answer.

The congregation will wait until waiting and answering become the same thing, until the question collapses under its own weight, until the plateau finally finishes dying and takes them with it into whatever comes after stillness.

Or perhaps the answer has already come, and they simply haven't recognized it yet.

Perhaps *they* are the answer—this vast arrested multitude, this patient silence, this breathing stillness that has outlasted everything except itself. Perhaps something asked a question at the beginning of the world, and the question was *what does forever look like*, and they are the reply, written in feathers and stone and the slow chemistry of bodies becoming geology.

They do not know.

They wait.

They breathe.

The horizon holds its breath with them.
