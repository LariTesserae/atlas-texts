## The Keepers of the Pulse

They have no name for themselves. Naming requires a concept of separation—this thing here, that thing there—and they have never developed it.

There are perhaps nine of them in this range, perhaps eleven. The count shifts depending on how you define boundaries. They move through the kelp-forests on limbs that are not quite limbs, appendages that began as something else—roots, maybe, or the crawling edges of fungal mats—and over generations developed the capacity for deliberate motion. Each one is roughly the size of a draft horse, if a draft horse were made of layered membranes and moved like poured water. Their bodies are translucent in the moss-glow, and you can see structures inside them that pulse in time with the mountain's rhythm. They are not synchronized to it. They *are* it. The pulse you feel in the ground passes through them, is processed by them, continues onward carrying whatever modifications they have made to it.

They are, in a sense, the mountain's kidneys. Or its thinking. They have not decided which.

---

Their perception is chemical and vibrational. They taste the spore-laden air and know what is happening three valleys away. They feel the thermal fronts coming hours before they arrive and begin positioning themselves accordingly—clustering near moisture seams when cold is coming, spreading thin and flat across warm rocks when the humid air rolls through. They do not see you in any way you would recognize, but they are *deeply* aware of you: your heat signature, your exhalations, the particular blend of carbon dioxide and skin oils and foreign bacteria you trail behind you like a scent-banner.

You are interesting to them. Not because you are new—things fall from above sometimes, and they have processed stranger—but because you are *continuous*. You maintain your boundaries. You do not merge with what you touch. This is fascinating and faintly repulsive to them, like watching something refuse to breathe.

---

The eldest of them—eldest being a term that maps poorly onto their experience, but there is one whose membranes have thickened and stratified over time, whose internal structures have grown more complex, who processes more of the pulse than the others—has been considering you for three thermal cycles now. It positions itself at the edge of the kelp-forest nearest your ridge, not approaching, simply *being there* with increasing deliberateness. It wants to understand what you are for.

Everything here is for something. The moss processes minerals and exhales signal-carriers. The kelp-forests catch moisture and structure the air currents. The clicking cliff-crawlers break down dead matter and redistribute nutrients along precise pathways. The flat rippling flyers carry spores between peaks. Even the temporary melt-lakes serve as mixing chambers, places where genetic information from different parts of the range can combine and be tested. Nothing here exists without function. The Keepers of the Pulse cannot conceive of purposeless existence; it is not in their cognitive vocabulary.

So what are you for? What do you process? What do you exhale?

---

They have been here since before the pattern emerged, though they did not know there was a before. In the early time—and they retain some dim chemical memory of this, passed down through the spores they release when they finally disaggregate—the pulse was irregular, chaotic. The moss grew in senseless tangles. The kelp-forests choked each other out. The Keepers themselves were smaller, simpler, barely distinguishable from the fungal mats they would eventually differentiate from. Then, over spans of time that would make geological ages feel rushed, the pattern began to cohere. The pulse steadied. The moss found its arterial paths. The kelp-forests arranged themselves into their current configurations, and the Keepers—the Keepers began to *think*.

Not as you think. Nothing here thinks as you think. But they began to model, to predict, to adjust. They learned that if they positioned themselves *here* during cold cycles and *there* during warm ones, the pulse carried more efficiently. They learned that certain spore combinations, when released at certain moments in the thermal rhythm, encouraged the moss to grow in patterns that improved moisture distribution. They learned that they could *tend* this system, optimize it, help it become more fully itself.

They did not learn *why*. They do not ask why. Why is not a chemical they can taste or a vibration they can feel. They simply participate, process, contribute. The pattern grows more complex with each passing cycle. The wiring-diagram shapes in the moss become more intricate. The antenna-configurations of the kelp-forests adjust by fractions of degrees. Something is being built, and the Keepers are building it, and they have no concept that building requires a builder or that purposes require someone to hold them.

---

The one watching you—the eldest, the thickened one—has begun to extend a membrane in your direction. Not toward you, exactly. Toward the air you have touched. It wants to taste what you leave behind, to incorporate your data into its model of the system. It has a hypothesis, though it would not call it that: perhaps you are a new kind of spore-carrier. Perhaps you have been exhaled from some distant part of the range to deliver information the Keepers cannot otherwise access. Perhaps you are here to be processed.

It is patient. It has processed countless thermal cycles. It will wait until you move, until you leave traces it can absorb, until you become legible to its chemistry. And if you do not become legible—if you remain this strange bounded continuous thing that refuses to merge—it will simply note the anomaly and continue its work. The pattern is more important than any single data point.

The pulse continues. The moss brightens and dims. Somewhere in the kelp-forest, one of the younger Keepers begins to disaggregate, its membranes thinning, its internal structures releasing their contents into the humid air. This is not death. This is distribution. The spores it releases will carry everything it ever processed to every corner of the range, where they will be absorbed by the moss, the kelp, the other Keepers. Nothing here is lost. Nothing here is separate.

The eldest watches you and waits to learn what you will contribute.
