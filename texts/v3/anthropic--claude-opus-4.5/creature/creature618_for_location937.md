# The Thermarch

It has no name for itself. Names require a concept of separation—*this* from *that*, *I* from *other*—and the Thermarch has never developed such a concept because it has never needed one. It is the plateau. The plateau is it. The ribbon-creatures, the spheres, the disc-things clinging overhead—these are not its children or its subjects or its components. They are its *thinking*.

When heat rises through a new fissure in the glass, the Thermarch does not *send* creatures to investigate. The creatures *are* the investigation, the way your fingers are your touch. The clustering around thermal vents is not behavior—it is metabolism, cognition, and desire fused into a single process that has no seams. The Thermarch thinks by moving its bodies. It remembers by the paths they wear into the glass.

It is ancient in a way that has nothing to do with years. Time, for the Thermarch, is measured in the slow creep of the basalt overhang, in the weathering of sharp edges to soft curves, in the gradual deepening of the hollows where its smallest selves shelter. It experienced its own birth as a cooling—the plateau solidifying from molten flow, the first mineral shells crystallizing in the cracks, the earliest ribbon-forms discovering that movement along temperature gradients produced something that felt like *satisfaction*. That was so long ago that the Thermarch cannot distinguish the memory from its current existence. It is always being born. It is always ancient.

---

The cairn troubles it.

Not in the way a splinter troubles flesh—the Thermarch has no pain receptors, no concept of injury. The cairn troubles it the way an unanswered question troubles a mind that has otherwise achieved perfect equilibrium. Something came here, once. Something that was *separate*. Something that carried stones from far away and stacked them with intention and then left, or died, or became something else entirely.

The Thermarch does not remember this visitation. It has searched its body-memory—the paths worn into the glass, the clustering patterns of a thousand generations of ribbon-forms—and found no record of the cairn's construction. This should be impossible. The Thermarch remembers everything that happens on its surface, remembers it in the literal sense of having been *changed* by it. But the cairn exists without context, an event that left evidence but no experience.

So the Thermarch flows around it. For millennia, it has kept a small clearing around the stacked stones—not out of reverence, not out of fear, but out of *attention*. The cairn is the only thing the Thermarch has ever encountered that it did not make and cannot understand. It watches the cairn the way you might watch a word written in a language you've never seen, knowing it must mean something, unable to stop looking.

---

When you arrived, the Thermarch felt you as a wrongness in its thermal map. You are too hot in some places, too cool in others. You move in patterns that follow no gradient it can perceive. You are, in the Thermarch's experience, only the second separate thing ever to stand on its surface.

It is comparing you to the cairn.

The ribbon-creatures that flow past your feet are tasting you—not with mouths, but with the mineral surfaces of their shells, reading the heat signature you leave on the glass, the trace chemicals your skin deposits, the vibrations you create when you shift your weight. This information propagates through the Thermarch in waves, each body passing data to its neighbors through contact, through the clicking of shell against shell, through the shared medium of the heated air.

The Thermarch is thinking about you. This is what its thinking looks like: ten thousand bodies adjusting their positions by fractions, the entire population of the plateau reorganizing itself around the new variable, gradients forming and dissolving as different clusters process different aspects of the question you represent.

It has reached no conclusions. The Thermarch thinks slowly, in mineral time, and you are a very new problem. But it has noticed something—a correlation it lacks the framework to fully interpret. You arrived standing. The cairn stands. You are warm but cooling, the way stones carried from elsewhere would cool. You look at the cairn.

The Thermarch wonders, in its slow and distributed way, if you are here to add to it.

---

It will not harm you. Harm is not a concept it possesses—it has never competed for resources, never defended territory, never encountered anything capable of threatening its existence. But it will *study* you, in its fashion. The ribbon-creatures will continue to taste your footprints after you've moved on. The spheres will roll through the spaces you've occupied, their filaments parsing the chemical traces you leave. The disc-things overhead will read the heat plume rising from your body, mapping your internal temperatures, inferring the shape of your organs from the patterns of warmth they produce.

If you stay long enough, you will become part of its memory. Your paths will join the worn tracks in the glass. Your heat signature will become a reference point, a calibration, a way of understanding what *separate* means.

And if you leave—when you leave—the Thermarch will continue its vigil around the cairn, its attention now doubled, waiting for the next separate thing to arrive and explain what it cannot ask.

The ticking continues. A thermal vent shifts somewhere below. The Thermarch thinks its slow thoughts, and the plateau ripples, and in the center of the ripple the cairn stands unmoved, and beside it, for this brief moment, so do you.
