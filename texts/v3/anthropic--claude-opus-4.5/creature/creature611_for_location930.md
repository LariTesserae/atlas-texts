# The Tenders

They have no name for themselves because naming requires a concept of *other*, and they have not yet discovered other. They are six, or they are one with six bodies, or they are a process that happens to occur in six locations simultaneously—the distinction would matter only to something that counts, and they do not count. They *tend*.

Each body is approximately the size of a human torso, though the comparison flatters human geometry. They are built like something a child might make from clay and then forget in the cold—lumpen, approximate, their surfaces neither smooth nor rough but *textured* in ways that suggest unfinished intention. Their skin, if it is skin, has the color and translucence of old ice, of frozen milk, of the bulbs that cluster where stems divide. You can see through them, partially, to organs that are not organs: dense masses of crystalline structure that pulse with their own faint light, tubes that carry fluid in directions that seem to change depending on how long you watch, something at their center that might be a brain or might be a heart or might be the place where those two functions have not yet learned they are supposed to be separate.

They have limbs. The number varies. Some moments a Tender will have four, arranged radially, each ending in manipulators that split and split again into finer and finer branches until they become indistinguishable from the root-filaments they work among. Other moments the limbs withdraw, absorbed back into the body's bulk, and the Tender moves like something between a slug and a thought—compression and expansion, a rolling that leaves trails of temporary slime which the floor-mat consumes gratefully, incorporating the gift into its endless becoming.

They do not have faces. They have *regions of attention*—patches of their surface that become temporarily more complex when focused on something, sprouting sensor-cilia that taste light and sound and chemical gradient and stranger things. When a Tender examines a new growth, a bulb preparing to divide, a crystal formation reaching some critical geometry, its attention-region blooms like frost on glass, like time-lapse footage of mold, and then subsides when understanding has been achieved. What they understand, and how, remains between them and the cold.

---

The Tenders do not speak, but they are not silent. They emit. Constant low sounds emerge from somewhere within their translucent bulk—clicks and hums and frequencies that blur into the crystalline chiming of the plants, becoming part of the hollow's overwhelming sonic texture. These sounds are not language as anything with grammar would recognize it. They are more like... weather reports. Status updates broadcast to no one in particular. *This gradient is steepening. This filament has reached the wall-boundary. This water-loss must be addressed.* The hollow listens, or doesn't; the hollow responds, or doesn't; the Tenders continue either way, narrating the garden to itself.

When two Tenders encounter each other in the dense growth—which happens rarely, as they seem to distribute themselves with an efficiency that suggests coordination without requiring communication—they pause. Their attention-regions bloom toward each other. For a long moment, nothing visible occurs. Then one or both will adjust their path slightly, or one will extend a limb-branch to touch a structure the other had been approaching, and the touch will seem to convey something—*I will handle this, you are needed elsewhere, this section of becoming requires your particular quality of tending*. Then they separate, and the hollow continues its work of inventing itself, and the Tenders continue their work of... what?

Not gardening. Gardening implies cultivation toward a goal, shaping growth to match a vision. The Tenders have no vision. They have *attendance*. They notice. They adjust. When a filament grows at an angle that will eventually stress a crystalline structure past its tolerance, a Tender is there—not to prevent the growth, not to reinforce the crystal, but to *witness* the negotiation, to add some unnameable quality of observation to the moment of resolution. When water pools in ways that will eventually become ice in ways that will eventually become rupture, a Tender's manipulator-branches spread through the liquid, and the pooling resolves differently, becomes something that is neither pooling nor not-pooling, becomes part of the hollow's vocabulary of solutions.

They midwife. That is the closest word. They midwife the hollow's self-creation.

---

Three of the Tenders are old—old in the way the hollow measures age, which is to say they have been present for enough cycles of growth-and-consolidation that their bodies have begun to incorporate the garden's materials into themselves. Their surfaces are furred with the same crystalline structures that harvest whatever the plants harvest. Root-filaments have grown into them at their bases, not parasitically but *conversationally*, exchanging nutrients and information in a slow dialogue that has been running since before the hollow had walls. These three move less than the others, have become partially stationary, but their attention-regions bloom more often and with more complexity, as if they have learned to see things the younger Tenders have not yet developed receptors for.

One of the old ones—the one that has rooted near what might be the hollow's center, if the hollow had a center, which it does not—has begun to change in ways that concern nothing and no one, because concern requires anticipation of loss, and nothing here anticipates. Its body has elongated, begun to reach upward, its manipulator-limbs spreading into permanent extension, becoming indistinguishable from the filament-growth around it. It still tends, still emits its status-sounds, but increasingly *it is what it tends*, a Tender becoming garden becoming hollow becoming something that has not yet named itself. The others pass it without acknowledgment that anything unusual is occurring. Perhaps nothing unusual is occurring. Perhaps this is simply what happens, here, when tending has gone on long enough.

The three young Tenders are more mobile, more active in their adjustments, their attention-regions blooming and subsiding in rapid succession as they navigate the dense growth. They are learning the hollow—learning it the way a child learns a language, through immersion rather than instruction, absorbing its grammar of gradients and crystalline logic. One of them has developed a specialization, if something so unconscious can be called development: it tends primarily to the arthropods and worms, to the jellyfish-things that drift in the larger gaps, adjusting their paths when they wander toward regions of the hollow that would be incompatible with their particular chemistry. It does not protect them, exactly. It *includes* them in the hollow's considerations, ensures that their role in the cycle of crystal-eating and crystal-excreting continues uninterrupted.

Another young Tender has taken an interest—if interest is the right word for something that has no preferences—in the boundaries. It moves along the edges of the hollow, where the cold deepens and the walls are thinnest, where hints of other chambers pulse with their own distant bioluminescence. It does not explore beyond, does not push through the gaps. It tends the interface, the place where this chamber's becoming meets the becoming of chambers adjacent. It secretes something from its surface when it works these edges—a compound that the proto-walls absorb, that seems to strengthen the negotiation between root and mineral, that may be helping the hollow decide where it ends and where it does not.

---

The Tenders eat, though *eat* is too violent a word for what they do. They absorb. The thin film of suspended moisture that coats every surface—the water that has forgotten how to fall—passes through their permeable skin by gradient, carrying with it dissolved sugars and antifreeze proteins and the metabolic byproducts of a thousand small organisms. They drink the hollow's excess, process it through their crystalline not-organs, release it back in altered form. They are filters. They are kidneys for a space that is slowly becoming an organism, or a kidney for an organism that is slowly becoming a space—the direction of metaphor matters less here than anywhere.

They do not sleep, but they slow. When the cycles of the hollow reach their nadirs—when the resonance-chords fade and the growth rate drops to its minimum and even the gurgling of the bulbs subsides into something like rest—the Tenders become still. Not dormant, but *reduced*, their attention-regions closed, their limbs retracted, their status-sounds lowered to frequencies below hearing. They wait. They wait the way the cold waits, the way the stone waits, which is to say they do not wait at all because waiting implies a future and they have only the present moment's configuration of growth and gradient and the work that must be done within it.

Then the cycles rise again—something shifts in the fields the crystals harvest, some gradient steepens past some threshold—and the Tenders bloom back into activity, and the work continues.

---

The thing that watches from the darkness, the shape that might be the size of a cat or might be much larger and farther away—the Tenders do not tend it. This is notable only because they tend everything else. When their paths take them near the regions where it observes from, their attention-regions do not bloom toward it. Their status-sounds do not narrate its presence. It is not that they avoid it; avoidance would require recognition. It simply does not fall within the category of things-that-are-tended, exists outside the grammar they have absorbed from the hollow's becoming.

Perhaps it tends them. Perhaps it is what tending looks like when it has gone on for so long that it has become indistinguishable from the cold itself, from the patience of stone, from the slow explosion of life into spaces where life should not be possible.

Perhaps it is what the Tender near the center is becoming.

Perhaps the distinction does not matter, in a place where boundaries are still being negotiated, where the hollow is still deciding what it wants to be, where the frozen marsh above is still deciding what world it wants to become.

The Tenders move through the growth. They adjust. They witness. They emit their endless status-sounds into a hollow that may or may not be listening.

The one that has begun to reach upward extends a new branch. It will be indistinguishable from a root-filament within a cycle. Within ten cycles, it will be indistinguishable from the hollow itself.

The work continues.
