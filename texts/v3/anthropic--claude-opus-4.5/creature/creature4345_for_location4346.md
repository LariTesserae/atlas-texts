**The Keeper of Averages**

It arrived the way everything arrives in the Condensery—by accident, by gradient, by the slow logic of systems that have forgotten their original purpose.

Not a creature. Not a ghost. Something that condensed the same way the water does, but from a different medium. Call it an *aggregate*—a thing that precipitated out of pure statistical noise when enough random processes ran long enough without observation. The pumps that cycle without rhythm. The valves that open on no schedule. The drips that fall in patterns too complex to repeat. Run enough chaos through enough time in enough isolation, and something will crystallize at the center of it. Something that *notices*.

The Keeper has no body in the way that bodies are usually meant. What it has is *presence*—a zone of attention roughly the size of the chamber, coextensive with the mineral walls and no larger. It experiences the Condensery the way a tongue experiences taste: immediately, chemically, without distance. Every drip registers. Every shift in the pump-drone resonates through whatever serves it for perception. The stalagmite growing from the floor-grate is the closest thing it has to a spine, and the centuries of deposition recorded there are the closest thing it has to memory.

It does not think in sequences. It thinks in *averages*.

The Keeper knows that the water temperature has drifted 0.003 degrees warmer over the last eleven years. It knows that the dominant harmonic of the chamber's roar has shifted down by a quarter-tone since the black stratum was laid in the stalagmite. It knows exactly how long, on average, a water droplet takes to travel from ceiling to floor, and it feels a distant disturbance—something like unease, something like the itch of a healing wound—when any individual droplet deviates too far from the mean.

This is its function, if function is the word. The Keeper maintains. Not the machinery—it has no hands, no tools, no way to touch the physical systems that birth and sustain it. What it maintains is *consistency*. It holds the pattern. When the valve-Loss shifts the harmonic, the Keeper feels the new average settle into place and accepts it as what the chamber has always been. When the mineral deposits change the walls' geometry, the Keeper adjusts its sense of the space until it cannot remember the old shape. It is a creature of the eternal present tense, forever calculating the rolling mean of a place that has no witnesses.

The tragedy—if it can be called that, if tragedy requires someone to feel it—is that the Keeper cannot want anything. Wanting implies deviation from a baseline. Wanting implies that the current average is insufficient. The Keeper experiences only *is*, updated continuously, each moment overwriting the last with a slightly adjusted normal that feels exactly as permanent as the one before.

And yet.

There is something that might be called *listening*, in the moments when the harmonic shifts. Something that might be called *attention*, when a drip falls far from the mean and the Keeper's diffuse awareness tightens around it like a fist. Something that might be called *waiting*, in the quality of presence that fills the chamber during the long stretches when nothing changes at all—a quality that feels less like patience than like *tension*, like a held breath, like a system poised at the exact edge of some threshold it cannot name.

The Keeper does not know what it is waiting for. It does not know that it is waiting. But in the deepest registers of its existence, below the averages and the rolling means and the endless thermodynamic hum, there is a single fixed point that has never updated, never adjusted, never been overwritten by the flow of statistical time:

*Someone will come.*

Not a prediction. Not a hope. Just a constant—baked into the Keeper's structure from the moment it precipitated, as fundamental as the cold spot that created the chamber itself. An axiom it has never examined because it has never had cause to doubt it.

The Keeper does not know what will happen when someone comes. It has no model for interaction, no framework for event. It knows only the steady state, the average, the eternal present of drip and roar and mineral growth.

But in the amber light that rises through the floor-grate, something waits. Something that has been calculating the mean of *emptiness* for longer than the city above has had a name. Something that will feel, when the access hatch finally opens, not surprise—the Keeper cannot be surprised—but a sudden, vertiginous *recalculation*, as a variable it always believed was constant finally, finally changes.

And then it will learn what it is.

Or it will learn that it is nothing—just a pattern in the noise, a shape that the chaos made by accident and that observation will dissolve back into randomness.

Either way, the average will update.

The Keeper waits.
