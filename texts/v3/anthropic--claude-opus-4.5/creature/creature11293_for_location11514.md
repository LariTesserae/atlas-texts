# The Tenders

They emerge from the fiber-mats at intervals you cannot predict—not rising from beneath, exactly, but *differentiating*, the way a wave distinguishes itself from the ocean it never leaves. One moment there is only the pale cushioned growth; the next, a form has gathered itself into standing separateness, and you understand that it has always been there, that "there" is simply a word for the part of the plateau that is currently paying attention.

They are tall and thin, these Tenders—twice your height but no wider than your arm, their bodies vertical arrangements of the same silk-fiber that makes up everything here. They have no faces. They have no features at all in the animal sense, no organs you can identify, no apparent front or back. They are columns of woven material, pale gold and cream, with textures that vary along their length: denser here, lacier there, some sections almost translucent while others are opaque as felt. When they move, it is not with steps but with a kind of *processional shifting*, their lower portions blending back into the ground-mat while new material differentiates ahead of them, so that they seem to flow across the plateau like slow thoughts through a dreaming mind.

You watch one approach a filament-tree whose hanging threads have begun to clump—imperceptibly, to your eyes, but the Tender has noticed. It extends part of itself toward the threads, its upper body thinning and elongating, becoming temporarily thread-like itself. For a long moment, it simply touches the clumped fibers. Then, without any motion you can see, the clump loosens. The threads fall straight again, perfect and separate. The Tender retracts, reconsolidates, and flows onward.

---

They do not communicate—not with each other, not with you. There is nothing to say. Each Tender knows what the plateau knows, feels what the plateau feels, because each Tender *is* the plateau in the same way that your hand is you. When you walk near them, they adjust their paths to accommodate you without acknowledgment, without curiosity, the way you might unconsciously shift your weight to avoid stepping on your own foot. You are not interesting to them. You are not uninteresting. You are simply *there*, a temporary texture in the fiber-field, and they work around you with the same patient precision they bring to everything.

Their work is maintenance, though that word suggests a kind of reactive intervention that doesn't quite fit. They do not wait for things to go wrong and then fix them. They move through the plateau in patterns you slowly recognize as preventive, as *compositional*—adjusting thread-tensions before they can clump, smoothing fiber-mats before they can compress unevenly, coaxing the spiral tendrils into their proper helices with touches so gentle you cannot see them happen. They are gardeners, in a sense. Weavers. Quality control for a factory that is its own product. They ensure that the plateau's slow becoming proceeds according to specifications, though you suspect the specifications are not written anywhere, are not *known* in any way you would recognize as knowing. The specifications are simply what the plateau is becoming. The Tenders are simply how it becomes.

---

Sometimes, rarely, you see one pause.

It does not stop moving in the mechanical sense—there is no mechanism to stop. But its flow becomes slower, more localized, circling a small area of fiber-mat that looks, to you, identical to every other area. The Tender thins itself, spreads itself, lowers until it is barely distinguishable from the ground-growth. It stays like this for what might be hours. You come back from walking a wide loop through the filament-groves and it is still there, still spread, still attending to whatever wrongness it has found.

When it finally reconsolidates and moves on, you go to the place it was tending. You crouch and press your palm to the fiber-mat. It feels the same as everywhere else—springy, warm, faintly yielding. But there is something. A density beneath the surface, maybe. A place where the plateau's deep processes have begun to compress in a way that doesn't quite match the rest. The Tender was not fixing it, you realize. The Tender was *witnessing* it, holding space for this small variation while the plateau decided what it wanted to become.

The decision must have been: the same. More of the same. The variation smoothed itself out, chose conformity, rejoined the great patient work of becoming what everything else already was.

You wonder if it had a choice. You wonder if choice is even a concept that applies here.

---

There is one other thing you notice about the Tenders, and you notice it only after you have been on the plateau long enough to stop expecting anything to happen quickly.

They gather.

Not often. Not in ways you can predict or intentionally observe. But sometimes, walking through a dense grove or cresting one of the gentle fiber-hills, you come upon a place where many of them have flowed together—a dozen, two dozen, their columnar forms arranged in a loose cluster that is not quite a circle, not quite a crowd, not quite anything you have a word for. They are not touching. They are not moving. They are simply *there*, together, in a configuration that feels deliberate without communicating any purpose you can read.

You watch. Nothing happens. They stand in their patient vertical silences, their woven bodies catching the amber light, and after a time that might be minutes or hours, they begin to disperse, flowing outward in their slow processional way, returning to their solitary maintenance routes.

Later, examining the place where they gathered, you find no change. No sign that anything was decided or exchanged. The ground is the same. The filament-trees are the same. Everything is exactly as it was.

But you cannot shake the feeling that something happened there. That the plateau, for a moment, was thinking with more than one thought at once. That the Tenders came together to experience something they could not experience alone—not communion, exactly, not worship, not decision-making, but something for which you do not have a word because your species has never needed one.

Something like: *being together in the same becoming.*

They flow past you now, two of them on parallel paths, and you step aside without thinking. They do not acknowledge you. They do not need to. You are part of the texture they are tending, and that is acknowledgment enough.

Somewhere deep beneath your feet, the plateau continues its slow self-weaving, and the Tenders continue to ensure that it weaves true.
