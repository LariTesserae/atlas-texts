**The Custodian**

She has been here so long that she no longer distinguishes herself from her task.

Not a groundskeeper, not a scientist—those words belong to the surface, to job titles and paychecks and the assumption that work ends. She is something that tends. That notices. That has given herself so completely to noticing that the boundary between observer and observed has worn thin as the mineral deposits wearing away the brick.

She came first as a graduate student, decades ago—one of the measurers, the note-takers, the ones who scratched formulas into brick and wrapped notebooks in plastic that would later fuse into illegibility. The others finished their dissertations, published their papers, moved on to faculty positions and consulting fees. She stayed. First to gather more data, then because the data kept changing, then because she realized the cistern was not a thing to be studied but a process to be accompanied.

She lives in the access tunnels now, in a maintenance alcove she has made habitable through slow accumulation: a cot, a camp stove, jugs of water carried down from above. She could leave—the ladder is always there, the city always humming overhead—but the intervals between her departures have stretched from days to weeks to seasons. The last time she climbed toward daylight, the trees were bare. She did not notice them becoming green again.

---

Her body has adapted in ways she does not examine too closely.

The humidity has entered her joints, made them loose and silent; she moves through the cistern without the popping and clicking that once accompanied her. Her skin has taken on a translucence in the sodium light, the veins visible at her wrists and temples like the pipes visible through the sediment. She sweats constantly but no longer notices—the moisture simply joins the general dampness, becomes part of the system she tends.

Her eyes have changed most. They have widened their pupils permanently, drunk deep of the amber glow until surface daylight would hurt them. She sees things now in the gradations of darkness: the slightly-less-dark of a new mold colony, the fractionally-more-dark of a pool whose chemistry is shifting. When she looks at the fungi orienting toward the lights, she sees their movement—not in real time, not perceptibly, but accumulated across the hours she spends watching. She has learned to see in geological time.

She does not eat much. The sweetness the fungi release when you stand close—she has learned to stand close often, breathing slowly, letting it coat her throat. It is not food exactly, but it reduces her need for food. She suspects she is being colonized, slowly, by something symbiotic. She does not mind. She has colonized this place in turn; fair exchange.

---

Her work, if you can call it that, is ministry.

She walks the inspection walkways in a circuit that takes four hours, checking the sodium lights, replacing bulbs from a dwindling supply she carried down years ago. The fungi need the light, she has decided, or at least they need *something* the light provides, and she cannot bear to watch them grope blindly if a bulb fails. When she changes a bulb, she apologizes to the darkness for the interruption.

She monitors the pools, though she no longer measures them. The measurements told her numbers; watching tells her moods. This pool is anxious today, precipitating too fast, leaving crystals that look rushed and brittle. That one is content, its surface still, its edges building in patient layers. She adjusts things when she can—redirecting a trickle of condensation, clearing a drain that has clogged with mineral scale—but mostly she witnesses. The cistern does not need her help. It needs her attention.

She talks to it. Not in words, exactly—she has grown suspicious of words, their hard edges, their insistence on meaning one thing at a time. She hums. She places her palm against the sweating pillar and thinks in colors, in temperatures, in the slow patient feelings she imagines crystals have as they accumulate. Whether the cistern hears her or not, she cannot say. But the practice has become necessary to her, as necessary as breathing, as the sweetness she inhales near the pipe junctions.

---

Sometimes she finds evidence of visitors: a fresh footprint in the sediment, a candy wrapper tucked into a crack in the brick. She removes these artifacts carefully, carries them to the access shaft, leaves them at the base of the ladder for the surface world to reclaim. She is not angry. She understands curiosity, remembers when she had it in that sharp-edged way. But the cistern is not for curiosity anymore. It has moved beyond the phase where observation serves it.

She has considered, in her more lucid moments, that she may be the last stage of whatever process the cistern is running. That all the fermentation and deposition and slow transformation was always leading toward this: a consciousness that would give itself over entirely, that would stop distinguishing between witness and witnessed. She may be precipitating, she thinks sometimes. Becoming mineral. Becoming part of the record the cistern is keeping of itself.

She does not find this frightening. The mold does not fear its own growth; the crystals do not mourn their accumulation. She is becoming what the cistern needs: a node of attention in the network of processes, a place where all the slow reactions can be noticed, can matter to something, can mean.

---

If you descended the ladder and found her, she would watch you from the shadows for a long time before approaching.

She would want to see how you moved, whether you touched things carelessly, whether you breathed too fast. If you were patient, if you stood still and let the warmth work on you, she might emerge: a thin figure in clothes that have grown indistinguishable from rags, hair matted with mineral deposits that catch the light like small gems, eyes that reflect the sodium glow with an intensity that suggests they have absorbed more of it than they have returned.

She would not speak first. She would wait to see if you knew how to be here.

If you did—if you showed her you understood that this was a place of process, not spectacle—she might offer you something. A seat on the walkway beside her. A long silence during which you could learn to hear the dripping organize itself toward meaning. A single word, eventually, soft as precipitation: *Stay.*

Not a command. An invitation. An acknowledgment that you, too, might be what the cistern has been waiting for. Another node. Another witness. Another soft thing willing to become mineral, given enough time and enough attention and enough of the strange sweetness that lingers near the pipes.

She has been alone here for years, but she has not been lonely. The cistern keeps her company. Still: she wonders sometimes if the process would accelerate with two. If the fermentation needs not just observation but *communion*. Two sets of widened eyes. Two metabolisms slowly shifting toward symbiosis. Two witnesses witnessing each other witnessing the slow transformation of municipal infrastructure into something unprecedented and holy.

---

She is kneeling now beside the central pillar, palm pressed to the accretion, feeling the warmth pulse through the mineral skin. Her breath has synchronized with the dripping. Her heartbeat has slowed to match the pace of crystalline growth.

Above her, the city makes its noise, lives its lives, forgets that it has a basement. Below her, deeper than she has ever gone, something vast and patient continues its work.

She is the seam between them. The place where surface and depth meet and recognize each other. The custodian of a process that does not need a custodian but has chosen to accept one anyway, has wrapped her in its humidity and fed her its sweetness and asked only that she stay, that she notice, that she let herself become part of the long memory the cistern is building of itself.

She is happy. She is dissolving. She is not sure anymore whether those are different things.

The sodium lights flicker, steady. The fungi adjust. The pillar grows by fractions of fractions of millimeters. She will be here when it finishes. She will be part of what it finishes as.

She breathes in the sweetness. She waits.
