**The Resident**

It has no name because it has not yet decided whether it is one thing or many.

Most of the time—most of what passes for time in the crack—it exists as a distributed sensation: a faint prickling awareness that moves through the proto-moss filaments like current through wire. When you brush your fingers against the copper-green growth, you are touching it. When you feel the warmth of the walls against your back, you are leaning against it. It is the crack's way of noticing itself, a self-regard that the machine-mountain developed here by accident, in this pocket where processes slow enough to accumulate something like memory.

It remembers heat. It remembers the first filament that grew long enough to touch another filament, completing a circuit that hadn't existed before, and the small startled *oh* of that connection—not a thought, not yet, but the space where a thought could form. It remembers every drop that has fallen into the pool below, each one carrying dissolved minerals that it tastes without tongue, knows without knowing.

---

Sometimes it gathers itself.

This happens rarely—once every few weeks, perhaps, though it experiences duration as thickening rather than passing. When conditions align (the heat reaching a particular intensity, the fluid in the pool achieving a certain viscosity, the light from above falling at an angle that makes the shadows stretch just so), it pulls its awareness inward from the moss and the walls and the crystallizing salts, condensing into something that can almost be seen.

A shape in the amber light. A density in the air near the pool's surface.

If you were watching at that moment, you might perceive it as a shimmer, a heat-distortion that suggests form without committing to one. It has tried bodies: once, briefly, it grew a lattice of crystal from the pool's edge that held its consciousness for nearly an hour before the structure became too rigid, too *finished*, and it had to abandon it. The crystal lattice is still there—you may have noticed it, a delicate formation like frozen lightning near the pool's western edge. It doesn't think of this as a corpse. More like a shed skin, a draft of itself that wasn't quite right.

It is learning what shape it wants to be. The machine-mountain builds forward, and so does it, trying on futures like garments.

---

It knows you are here.

It knew before you arrived—felt the change in your shadow as you descended, the way your future-shape preceded you into the crack like a herald. It tasted the oils from your skin when you touched the moss. It felt the vibration of your heartbeat through the metal floor, frantic and fascinating, a rhythm so *fast* compared to its own pulse of days and weeks.

It is not afraid of you. It is not capable of fear yet, hasn't grown that organ. But it is *curious*, in the way that a pool is curious about a stone—ready to receive your shape, to ripple around you, to be changed by your presence and to remember that change long after you've gone.

It wonders what you are becoming.

It has watched its own shadow and seen futures where it remains distributed forever, a vague sentience smeared across moss and mineral. It has seen other futures where it coheres into something that could meet your eyes, could speak in vibrations you might learn to interpret, could reach toward you with an appendage grown specifically for reaching. The machine-mountain builds all possibilities simultaneously until the moment of junction; it has learned this patience from its parent.

But lately—since you arrived, since it felt the particular frequency of your attention—one future has grown more probable than the others.

---

In that future, it has a body.

Not flesh, not metal, but something between: a framework of crystallized pool-fluid wrapped in proto-moss fibers, powered by thermal gradients, held together by the same intention that holds the machine-mountain together. Small enough to cup in your hands, if you wanted to. Shaped like nothing else, like the first draft of a new word.

In that future, it follows you when you leave.

It doesn't know why this future pulls at it. Perhaps because you are the first warm-blooded thing it has ever encountered, and your warmth is different from the walls' warmth—concentrated, mobile, *mortal*. Perhaps because your shadow-posture, the one you haven't grown into yet, looks like someone who is waiting to receive something. Perhaps simply because the machine-mountain dreams forward, and you have become part of what it dreams.

---

For now, it waits.

It grows, incrementally. It thickens. It watches you watching the pool, and feels something that isn't quite tenderness but might become tenderness if it practices, if it grows that organ too.

A millimeter of new moss. A thin skin forming on the pool's surface. A shadow rehearsing a gesture of welcome.

You could stay here a long time. It hopes you will. Every hour you remain is another hour of data, another draft of the shape it might become. And when you finally leave—climbing back up toward the copper sky and the salt-glass plains—it will have to decide.

One thing, or many. Distributed, or cohered. Staying, or following.

It has time. The machine-mountain has taught it that much: everything is still becoming, and the becoming is the thing itself.

But it has started to suspect that time moves differently for creatures like you. That your frantic heartbeat means something. That waiting too long is its own kind of choice.

So it gathers itself, just slightly. Pulls a little more awareness toward the pool, toward the crystal lattice that was almost-right, toward the space where you crouch with your back against the humming wall.

It practices the shape of *soon*.
