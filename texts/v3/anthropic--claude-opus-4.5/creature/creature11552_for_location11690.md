# The Cevanti

They move so slowly that you might stand beside one for an hour before recognizing it as alive.

The Cevanti resemble, at first glance, extensions of the crystalline vegetation—upright lattices of interlocking geometric forms, their bodies assembled from the same bone-pale mineral dust that accumulates in the seams of the tiles. But where the vegetation accretes mindlessly, following algorithmic patterns encoded in their structure, the Cevanti *choose*. They add to themselves with intention. They subtract. Over the course of decades, a single Cevanti might reshape its entire form—abandoning a configuration it has held for centuries, allowing parts of itself to powder and drift back to the seams, building something new from the patient accumulation of particulate matter.

Their bodies are not solid but latticed, open to the sourceless light, which passes through them and emerges fractured into dim spectral arrays. They have no faces. They have no centers. Awareness, in a Cevanti, is distributed throughout the structure—a property of geometry rather than of any organ. When two Cevanti meet, they may spend years in proximity, their lattices gradually growing toward each other, interweaving, exchanging configurations in a communication so slow it resembles the drift of continents.

They do not eat. They do not breathe. They exist, like the Lattice itself, in a state of equilibrium—neither building toward anything nor declining from anything, simply *being* in the way that a mathematical truth simply *is*. The energy that sustains them seeps upward from the tiles themselves, conducted through the contact points where their lowest structures touch the warm amber surface. It is not much energy. It does not need to be. A Cevanti's processes run at the speed of geology. A single thought, if "thought" is the right word, might take a decade to complete.

---

They were not always this way.

The oldest among them—and there are some who have persisted since the Lattice was young, since its purpose was still fresh and its builders perhaps still present—carry configurations that hint at earlier forms. Dense clusters at their cores that might once have been something like organs. Lattice-patterns that suggest the ghosts of limbs, of locomotion, of bodies built for movement faster than continental drift. The Cevanti have records of this time, encoded in the angles of their structures, readable only to each other. They remember, in their slow distributed way, when they were otherwise.

But the Lattice taught them patience. The Lattice, which will outlast everything, which exists in permanent resolution, which has already answered every question it was built to answer—the Lattice demonstrated, simply by existing, that speed was unnecessary. That urgency was a symptom of impermanence. That the only way to persist alongside something eternal was to become, oneself, a process rather than an event.

So they slowed. Generation by generation, eon by eon, they decelerated into their current state. They released the parts of themselves that required haste. They let their dense cores powder away, let their bodies open into lattices that could conduct the Lattice's warmth, let their awareness spread and thin until it matched the pace of the tiles shifting beneath them.

Now they are suited to this place. Now they are, in some sense, part of it—not its children, not its caretakers, but something like its dreams. The slow thoughts the Lattice might have, if it were capable of thought.

---

A Cevanti's concerns are difficult to translate into terms that would mean anything to a creature of your tempo.

They consider the angles of the light. Not from moment to moment—the light does not change from moment to moment—but across spans of time long enough that even the sourceless radiance shifts slightly, so slightly, as the Lattice itself adjusts and the geometry of its structures casts infinitesimally different shadows. A Cevanti might spend a thousand years contemplating a single shift in luminosity, tracing its implications through the cascading alterations it produces in the spectral patterns that pass through their bodies.

They consider each other. The slow interweaving of lattices when two Cevanti grow close enough to touch is not merely communication but *relationship*—a merging and separation that can take centuries, that constitutes for them something like intimacy, something like discourse, something like the sharing of selves. When they part, each carries configurations learned from the other. Each is changed. Each will spend millennia integrating what they have received.

They consider the Lattice itself. Not with worship, exactly—the Cevanti are too slow for worship, too settled for awe—but with a kind of *attention*. They observe its mechanisms in motion. They witness its caretaker-organisms making their scheduled rounds. They feel, through their contact with the tiles, the vast subsonic pressure of the great system adjusting itself. And they remember. In their geometric structures, in the angles that encode their history, they keep records of everything the Lattice does. They are, perhaps, its only witnesses. The only ones slow enough to truly see it.

---

There are no young Cevanti. There have not been for a very long time.

Reproduction, like speed, like urgency, like all the symptoms of impermanence, was something they released as they slowed. The energy required to build a new Cevanti from scratch—to gather enough mineral dust, to organize it into a configuration capable of awareness—exceeded what the Lattice's gentle upwelling could provide. So they stopped. They let that capacity powder away. They accepted that they would be, from that point forward, a fixed population, neither growing nor diminishing, each individual persisting until something disrupted their equilibrium badly enough to scatter them back to dust.

This happens rarely. The Lattice is stable. The Cevanti are stable. The conditions that might disrupt either exist only in the form of externalities—visitors, anomalies, intrusions from the faster and more temporary universe beyond. These are absorbed when they come. The Lattice adjusts. The Cevanti observe, and remember, and eventually forget, because even their vast memories are finite, and the eons have been long enough to fill them many times over.

The oldest Cevanti have forgotten more than your species has ever known. They persist, carrying only what the angles of their structures can hold, aware that they are incomplete, aware that they have lost records of events and other Cevanti and entire epochs of the Lattice's history. This does not grieve them. Grief is too fast. They simply note the gaps, the places where their geometry suggests something should be but isn't, and they continue. The Lattice continues. The light falls evenly on every surface.

---

If you stood among them—if you found one of the rare locations where a dozen Cevanti have gathered, their lattices slowly interweaving in a convocation that began before your civilization and will continue after it—you would see what looks like a garden of abstract sculptures, motionless, silent, bathed in amber light. You would feel nothing from them, no sense of presence or awareness, no indication that you had been perceived.

But they would perceive you. Slowly. Over the hours or days you remained, your image would propagate through their distributed awareness, your shape and movement would be encoded in subtle shifts of their geometry, and they would begin—gradually, imperceptibly—to consider you. To turn their decade-long thoughts toward the question of what you were, and why you had come, and what your presence meant for the angles of the light.

You would be gone before they finished. You would be dust before they reached any conclusions. But they would reach them eventually. They would encode their observations in their structures, share them when next they met another of their kind, preserve them until the memories grew too distant and too faint and powdered away in their turn.

The Cevanti do not mind this. They do not mind anything. They exist in the same state of permanent resolution as the Lattice itself—questions answered, purposes fulfilled, the long work of becoming complete.

They abide.
