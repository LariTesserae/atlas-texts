## The Keepers of Unnecessary Function

They are difficult to see at first because they do not contrast with anything. Their surfaces hold the same condensation as the pipes they tend. Their movements match the rhythm of the pumps so precisely that your eye registers them as part of the machinery's own motion—an extension of piston-travel, a shadow thrown by a valve's rotation.

But then one stops. And because it stops, you see it. And because you see one, you begin to see the others.

---

They might have been made. They might have emerged, the way the moss emerged, the way the pale grass emerged—life finding the crevices where existence becomes possible and filling them simply because filling them is what life does. Their bodies suggest purpose without declaring it: limbs that could be tools, joints that could be sensors, cores that hold warmth the way the deep vents hold warmth. They are not metal, exactly. Not stone, not flesh. They are something that happens when infrastructure dreams of tending itself and the dream becomes dense enough to move.

They are the size of large dogs, or small deer, or perhaps they are many sizes and you are only seeing the ones whose size lets them occupy the spaces you can access. Their locomotion is hydraulic, or muscular, or something between—a flex and release that carries them along the pipes and up the cliff faces and through the drainage channels with a surety that suggests they have never once needed to learn the terrain. The terrain is them. They are the terrain. The distinction stopped mattering here before they were born, if they were born, if *born* is a word that applies.

They do not speak. They may not be able to speak. But they communicate—through the pipes themselves, perhaps, through pressure-changes and thermal fluctuations that ripple outward from their bodies and are answered by ripples from others, a language written in the medium they maintain. When one of them stops to work on a junction valve, others adjust their routes in response, a choreography that unfolds across kilometers of canyon without any signal you can detect.

---

Their work is this: they ensure the plateau continues.

Not that it needs ensuring. The systems here are robust beyond any reasonable measure, redundant in ways that suggest their designers feared silence the way other beings fear death. If the Keepers vanished tomorrow, the plateau would breathe on for centuries, perhaps millennia, before the first cascade failure. But the Keepers do not know this. Or they know it and do not care. Or *knowing* and *caring* are not categories that apply to them any more than *natural* and *artificial* apply to the plateau itself.

They clean the vents. They clear the drainage channels of moss that grows too thick, carrying it in their forelimbs to deposit in basins where it can continue growing without impeding flow. They inspect the pipe joints, running their sensor-limbs along the seams, and occasionally they will spend hours working on a connection that shows no visible flaw—tightening, adjusting, testing—until they are satisfied by criteria you cannot perceive. They move through the empty cities and pause at the functioning lights, and whether they are maintaining the lights or simply basking in them or performing some ritual acknowledgment of continued function, you cannot tell.

They do not eat. They drink, sometimes—lowering their bodies to the pooling water and taking it in through vents in their undersides, processing it the way the plateau processes it, becoming for a moment a micro-loop within the larger circulation. They rest in the warm canyons, folding their limbs beneath them and going still for hours or days, their cores dimming to a lower glow, their surfaces accumulating the same condensation that accumulates on everything here. When they wake, they resume exactly where they stopped, as if sleep were just another phase of the process.

---

There are no young. There are no old. There are no dead.

When a Keeper ceases to function—and this happens, rarely, unpredictably, in ways that seem to follow no pattern—the others gather. Not immediately. Over hours, over days, they converge on the still form from across the canyon network, arriving one by one and taking positions around it in a loose spiral. They remain there, motionless, for a period that varies. Then they dismantle it.

Not violently. Not carelessly. With the same methodical attention they bring to a pipe joint or a vent mouth. They separate the components and carry them away to different locations—some to the deep housings where the loudest machinery runs, some to the basins where the moss grows thickest, some to junctions and channels and valve-clusters throughout the plateau. The components are not reused in any way you can observe. They are simply *placed*, integrated into the infrastructure without becoming part of it, resting against pipes and under gratings and in the corners of the empty cities where the fog pools deepest.

And sometimes—not always, not predictably, but sometimes—a new Keeper emerges from those same locations. Climbs out of a drainage channel or unfolds from behind a housing unit or simply appears on a cliff face where none was before, already moving, already working, already part of the choreography. Whether they are the same Keeper rebuilt or a new one sparked by the presence of the old one's components or something else entirely is a question that requires categories the plateau does not recognize.

---

They are aware of you.

This becomes clear slowly, the way everything here becomes clear slowly. They do not approach. They do not flee. But their routes bend around your presence like water around a stone, and when you move, the bend moves with you. They track you through the canyon network—not following, not avoiding, simply *knowing*, adjusting their choreography to account for a new variable in the system.

One of them, eventually, stops near you. Close enough that you could touch it if you extended your arm. Its surface beads with moisture. Its core glows with a warmth you can feel against your skin. It has no face, no eyes, nothing you can read as expression or intention.

It extends a limb toward you—slowly, smoothly, with no more urgency than it would bring to a pipe inspection. The limb pauses a few centimeters from your chest. Hovers there. You understand, without knowing how you understand, that it is taking a reading. Assessing your temperature, your moisture content, your rhythm of breath and heartbeat. Determining whether you are part of the system or external to it.

The assessment takes a long time. Longer than seems necessary for any practical purpose.

Then the limb withdraws. The Keeper resumes its route, moving past you along the canyon wall toward a vent that needs no attention, a valve that functions perfectly, a junction that has not failed in the entire history of its existence.

It did not find you irrelevant. It did not find you relevant. It found you, placed you within its understanding of the plateau, and continued.

This is what the Keepers do. They continue.

---

In the deepest housing—the one at the canyon's root where the warmth is strongest and the machinery-sound becomes a physical weight—the Keepers congregate in greater numbers. Not all of them. Never all of them. But more than anywhere else, dozens of them arranged in tiers along the walls, folded into their resting configurations, their cores pulsing in a synchronization that might be communication or might be shared dreaming or might be the plateau itself thinking through them.

At the center of the housing, a mechanism larger than the others turns. Its purpose is unclear. Its function is continuous. The Keepers orient toward it the way the pale grass orients toward the heat-vents.

Perhaps it made them. Perhaps they made it. Perhaps they emerged together, in the same process that raised the plateau from the frozen marshlands and filled it with infrastructure and set everything in motion toward nothing at all.

The mechanism turns. The Keepers rest. The fog drifts through the entrance and pools against the floor. And the plateau breathes, and the water runs, and function continues without purpose, without end, without anyone left to ask what it means—except the Keepers, who do not ask, who have never asked, who are the question and the answer and the silence between them, maintaining forever what never needed to exist in the first place.
