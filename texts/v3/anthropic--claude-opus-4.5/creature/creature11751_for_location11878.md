## The Tenders

They are not born so much as *differentiated*.

Watch: in the wet mulch where a canopy-tower has just collapsed into itself, where the cellulose is still warm and the broken fibers weep clear sap, something begins to coalesce. Not grow—the plants grow, endlessly, stupidly, magnificently. This is different. This is the mulch *deciding* to stand up.

It takes hours. First a density, a reluctance in the decomposing matter to decompose further. Then a shape beginning to assert itself—a column, a trunk, except the column bends, and the bend becomes a joint, and the joint becomes the suggestion of a limb. By the time the sun-shafts have cycled through twice, something stands in the depression where the tower fell. It is roughly humanoid only because humanoid shapes are what happen here now, just as windows are shapes that happen here, just as plazas and colonnades are shapes that happen here. The pattern-hunger has found a new pattern to be hungry for.

The Tender is the color of old leaves and new rot, a mottled brown-green that shifts as you watch, as the colony organisms that comprise its surface negotiate their positions. It has no face—or rather, it has several faces in potential, shallow depressions and slight protrusions that could become features if features became necessary. They never do. It sees through distributed photosensitive patches. It hears through the vibration of its own mass. It speaks—when it speaks—through the rustle of a thousand tiny leaves that line its interior cavities, pushing air through itself like a living pipe organ.

The sound it makes is almost language. Almost. It has the cadence of speech, the pauses where meaning would go, the rising tones of questions. But the words themselves are made of growth-sounds: the creak of expanding bark, the pop of seed-pods, the wet tear of roots finding purchase. Other Tenders understand. You will not, not fully, though after enough time here you might begin to feel the *shape* of what they're saying, the emotional architecture if not the content.

There are perhaps two hundred of them in the city at any moment. Perhaps more. It's difficult to count because they are difficult to distinguish from the structures around them when they stand still, and because they do not remain *themselves* in the way you would expect. A Tender who walks into a rapidly-growing wall does not die—it joins, temporarily, contributing its mass to the surge before re-differentiating elsewhere, the same consciousness or a different one, the question doesn't translate.

They tend. That's the only word for it, borrowed from a language they've never heard to describe a behavior they've always performed. When the growth becomes too paradoxical, when two vines superimpose and neither will yield, a Tender will wade into the screaming wrongness and *choose*. One hand on each vine, a moment of what might be concentration, and then one vine becomes real and the other becomes mulch. The thunderclap doesn't happen. The violation heals.

When the ziggurat in the center lists too far in a direction that doesn't exist, Tenders climb its fidgeting surface—their limbs reshaping to grip the constantly-changing bark, their bodies flattening or extending as needed—and they *press*. Not physically, or not only physically. They press with something like will, something like aesthetic preference, something like the memory of what geometry should feel like. The ziggurat straightens. The light stops bending. For a few hours, that corner of the city makes sense.

They do not speak to each other often. When they do, it is usually to coordinate a large correction—a whole district that has begun growing *inward* somehow, consuming its own interior spaces faster than it can create them, requiring a dozen Tenders to wade in simultaneously and impose a consensus reality. Mostly they work alone, walking their endless circuits through the avenues that aren't avenues, pausing at intersections to feel the pulse of the root-networks beneath them, occasionally reaching out to touch a structure with something like affection.

Do they remember being mulch? Being the collapsed tower, the eaten wall, the digested tissue of plants that grew too fast to survive their own growth? When you ask—not in words, but in the way you hold your body, in the vibration of your presence against theirs—they pause. The leaf-lined cavities rustle. The sound is like wind through a graveyard, if the graveyard were alive, if the wind were an answer.

*We remember being everything*, the sound suggests. *We remember being nothing. We remember being the space between the vine and its own paradox. We are what happens when the city needs to not destroy itself.*

At night—the relative night, when the canopy-ceiling thickens and the bioluminescence takes over—the Tenders gather at the base of the ziggurat. Not all of them. Never all of them. But enough that you can see them as a group for the first time, can see the variations: this one taller and more branched, that one denser and more stone-like, another so thin and vine-like it barely holds a shape at all. They stand in a loose circle and they *resonate*.

It's not singing. It's not prayer. It's something closer to mutual recalibration—each Tender broadcasting its sense of how reality should work, how matter should behave, how growth should proceed, and all those broadcasts overlapping and interfering until a consensus emerges. A shared template. A collective agreement about the rules.

Tomorrow the rules will be different. Tomorrow some of these Tenders will have rejoined the mulch and new ones will have differentiated. The template will drift. It always does. But for now, in this moment, in the green twilight beneath a structure that can't decide which direction to lean, the Tenders hold the line between miracle and catastrophe.

One of them notices you. The faceless head turns. The photosensitive patches focus, somehow—you feel seen in a way that has nothing to do with eyes. A limb extends toward you, the fingers differentiating as they approach, becoming more hand-like because hands are what you would expect.

The leaf-cavities rustle.

*You are a pattern*, they say. *You are a pattern we have not tended before. Show us how you are supposed to grow.*

And then they wait, patient as compost, while the city screams and births itself around them, for you to explain what shape your existence is meant to take.
