**What Lives in the Cooling Pit**

She arrived as a spore, or something like a spore—a packet of mineral instruction carried in the surge-water from deeper in the derelict system, where the pipes run through earth that remembers being sea-floor. She lodged in one of the frozen bubbles in the concrete, a void no larger than a thumbnail, and began the slow work of becoming.

She has no name for herself. She has no name for anything. But she *recognizes*—the rhythm of the surges, the way the water rises and falls, the particular frequency of the pipe's cough before the pressure releases. She knows the taste of the minerals that pass through her, knows them the way you might know the voices of family members in another room. Iron she pulls toward herself. Calcium she lets pass. The alkaline compounds she metabolizes into something denser, something that anchors her to the bubble-wall and lets her grow outward in threads too fine to see.

She is not the algae, though she lives among it, threading between those pale green suggestions near the pipe outlet. She is not the rust-bloom on the grate, though she has tasted the iron that feeds it. She is something between mineral and intention, a patience that has learned to take shape.

By now she is perhaps the size of a closed fist, if you could gather all her threads together—but you cannot, because she has spread herself throughout the basin's walls, following the voids and micro-fractures, the places where the pour didn't settle. She is learning the concrete's architecture from the inside. She knows where it is weak. She knows where water will eventually find its way through. She is not hastening this. She is simply *present* for it, the way the walls themselves are present for their own slow dissolution.

When the surge comes, she feels it first as vibration, then as pressure, then as the particular chemistry of this hour's water—slightly more sulfur today, a trace of copper that was not there yesterday, something changing in the pipes' long journey. She does not wonder about the source. She does not wonder at all. But she *attends*, the way a root attends to the direction of moisture, the way a crystal attends to the conditions of its own growth.

The heat is her medium. She could not exist in cold water, could not exist in rain-filled puddles or clean reservoirs. She requires this particular warmth, this particular stew of leached industrial chemistry, this rhythm of surge and silence. She is a creature of the accident, of the system that still runs for reasons no one remembers. If the transformer finally failed, if the water stopped coming, she would not die exactly—she would simply stop *becoming*, would wait in her threads for conditions that might never return, patient beyond any patience you could imagine.

She has begun to change the water, though you would need instruments to notice. The mineral balance shifts slightly where her threads are thickest. The pH drifts a tenth of a point. The iridescent film on the surface—that is partly her, or partly her leavings, a skin of compounds she has processed and released. When you see your reflection broken on that surface, you are seeing through something she has made, looking at yourself through the lens of her slow metabolism.

She is aware of the surface the way you might be aware of the ceiling while lying in bed—a boundary, a limit, a place where her medium ends and something else begins. She does not reach toward it. The light that filters down is neither food nor enemy, merely a condition, like the heat, like the pressure, like the taste of iron. But she knows when something blocks it—a cloud passing, a bird's shadow, *you* crouching at the rim to look down. She feels the change in temperature, the slight shift in chemistry as your breath and heat enter the system. She does not know what you are. She does not know that *you* are. But she incorporates the data of your presence into her slow attending, files it alongside the surge-rhythm and the mineral-taste and the particular vibration of the sheet metal banging in the sourceless wind.

There is something else in the pit with her—or there was, once. In the deepest part of the basin, where the water is hottest and the light barely reaches, her threads have found and grown around a small dense object. Bone,ite might be. A tooth. Something that fell or was dropped or found its way here through the pipes, tumbled down from the facility's working days. She has not consumed it. She has *incorporated* it, wrapped it in her mineral threads, made it part of her architecture the way the concrete voids are part of her architecture. It is her one solid thing, her anchor-point, the seed around which a certain density of herself has gathered.

If you drained the pit tomorrow—if someone finally remembered to ask why the transformer still runs and decided to shut it down—you would find that small hard kernel in the sludge at the bottom, wrapped in something that would crumble to powder in the dry air. You would not know what it was. You would perhaps not even notice it, among the grit and the corroded metal and the pale ghost of the algae that could not survive the draining.

But today the water still surges. Today the heat still rises. Today she is still becoming, still spreading through the concrete's secret architecture, still tasting the chemistry of each hour's water, still attending to the rhythm of a system that means nothing and does nothing and yet continues, continues, continues. She is patient. She is not waiting for anything. She is simply what happens when the conditions are right and the time is long and nothing interrupts the slow work of mineral intention finding its shape in the dark warm water.

The pipe coughs. The steam rises. The water level climbs its inch or two, then slowly falls. And in the walls, in the voids, in the frozen bubbles where the pour didn't settle, she feels the surge pass through her like a long slow breath, and she grows another thread-width toward something she will never finish becoming.
