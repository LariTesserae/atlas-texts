# The Tethered

They call themselves *those-who-remain*, though this is a translation of a translation, filtered through gesture-language and the slow accumulation of shared reference. You will not meet them for a long time. You will find only their leavings, their preparations, their quiet evidence—and then one day you will look up from harvesting moss and someone will be there, watching you from a root-junction thirty meters away, utterly still, and you will understand that they have been watching for much longer than this moment.

---

The first thing you notice is how they hold themselves: compressed, hunched, every limb drawn close to the core of the body. They are small—smaller than you expected from the platforms, which had suggested hands your size or larger. But the platforms were not built for comfort. They were built for *stability*, for weight distributed across uncertain surfaces. The Tethered are perhaps two-thirds your height, but dense with it, bodies packed tight with muscle that anchors and grips and *holds*.

Their skin is the color of old roots: pale gray shading to white where it stretches over joints, darker in the creases and folds. It gleams with the same moisture-film that coats everything here. You realize, watching, that they do not wipe it away. The dampness is not discomfort to them. It is condition. It is *skin*, almost—an outer layer they wear and maintain and perhaps could not survive without.

The eyes are large and set wide, adapted for the sourceless light that comes from everywhere. No visible pupil. The whole eye surface seems to gather and process light, giving their gaze an evenness that you learn to read only slowly: the slight narrowing that indicates attention, the softening that means ease, the absolute stillness that means *wait, not yet, I am deciding about you*.

---

They do not live in one place. This is the first and most important thing to understand about them.

The Hollow does not permit settlement. The storms come. The roots reconfigure. The shelf-fungi crack and fall. Any structure attached to a single location is temporary by definition—a season's shelter, a waypoint, a cache. The Tethered have learned this so thoroughly that the concept of *home-as-place* does not exist in their gesture-language. Home is relational. Home is *who you can reach*. Home is the network of ropes and bridges and maintained pathways that connect you to others, that let you find each other when the fog clears, that persist through reconfiguration because they are checked and repaired and sometimes re-routed but never abandoned.

You begin to understand the rope-bridges differently. They are not infrastructure. They are *commitment*. Each one says: *I will maintain this connection. I will come back to this crossing and test the cordage and replace what has worn. I will keep this path open between us.*

When a bridge finally fails beyond repair—when a storm tears away both anchor-points, when the gap it spanned becomes unbridgeable—there is a mourning. You witness it once: a small group gathered at the edge where a bridge used to begin, making sounds you have not heard from them before, low and resonant, continuing for hours. They are not mourning a death. They are mourning a *distance*. Someone they could reach, they now cannot. The Hollow has grown larger between them.

---

A Tethered alone is not Tethered. This is as close as you can get to translating the concept. The word—the gesture, really, a motion of both hands drawing inward and then reaching out—contains within it the assumption of connection. To be Tethered is to be Tethered *to*. The self exists only in reference to the web.

You meet them in groups of two, three, occasionally more. Never one. A solitary Tethered is either traveling between connections or *lost*, and the distinction matters: travelers move with purpose, checking bridges, following known routes. The lost move erratically, calling out in that low resonance, waiting for response. If no response comes—if the fog swallows the call, if the distances have grown too large—they will keep moving, keep calling, until they find someone or don't.

You learn that this happens. That the Hollow claims them, sometimes. That groups become separated in storms, that rope-bridges fail at the worst moment, that someone simply *vanishes* into the bright haze and is not seen again. The Tethered speak of the lost with a particular gesture: both hands open, fingers spread, held out and then slowly closed. *Held and then not held. Here and then elsewhere.* They do not assume death. The Hollow is vast. Someone lost might still be out there, might still be calling, might still someday find their way back to a connection. The grief is for the uncertainty. For the not-knowing that might last forever.

---

They eat the roots. This seems obvious and yet the specificity of it matters: they eat the *roots*, not the fungi, not the moss, not the pale arthropods that would seem to offer protein. The roots and only the roots, prepared in particular ways that you learn involve soaking, scraping, fermenting in stone-pockets where natural warmth accelerates the process. The preparation removes something—a toxin, an indigestible compound, something that would accumulate and sicken. They have been doing this for so long that their bodies may have partially adapted, but the preparation is still necessary.

This means they must go where the roots go. They cannot settle in a fungal-shelf paradise and stay. They must follow the root-lattices, must maintain access to the thick cables of pale flesh that sustain them, must build their bridges and platforms in relation to this need above all others.

You realize that the root-systems structure not just the Hollow's space but the Tethered's society. Where roots cross, people can cross. Where roots are sparse, travel becomes difficult, population thins, connections stretch to breaking. The densest communities exist at root-junctions: places where multiple massive cables converge, where the lattice becomes thick enough to support platforms at multiple levels, where a network of shorter bridges can connect a larger group.

These junctions have names. The names are sung, not spoken—short melodic phrases that carry through the haze better than words, that can be called out as identification, as summons, as *I am here, I am at the place we both know, come find me*.

---

The children are carried for a long time. Longer than seems possible, given the climbing and gripping and constant motion that Tethered life requires. You see them strapped to chests, to backs, wrapped in fungal-leather carriers that distribute weight and leave hands free. The children are quiet. You almost never hear them cry. They learn early that sound travels, that a cry might echo for kilometers, that silence is not suppression but *consideration*—awareness of the space they exist within.

When the children are old enough to grip, they practice. You watch a small one clinging to a root-cable, maybe two meters off a platform, an adult within arm's reach but not touching. The child's whole body is focused on the grip. The fingers are already strong—stronger proportionally than the adults', you think, because this is the skill that matters most, the skill that must be learned before any other. A child who cannot grip cannot be carried safely. Cannot move through the lattice. Cannot be Tethered.

You never see a child fall. You wonder if this is luck, or if the ones who fall are simply not spoken of.

---

The storms reshape their world and they accept this with a patience that looks like fatalism but is something else. You are present when a major reconfiguration happens—a root-lattice that connected two junction-communities shifts, pulled by some deep settling of the stone, and a journey that once took half a day now takes three. The Tethered do not rage against this. They do not mourn, exactly, though there is a ceremony of acknowledgment. They begin, immediately, to assess alternatives. They send scouts along other routes. They start planning new bridges that might restore some portion of the lost connection.

And they tell stories.

This is when you learn that the Tethered have been here for a very long time—long enough that their stories contain *other reconfigurations*, other storms, other losses and adaptations. The stories are sung, like the junction-names, carried in melody because melody persists. A story might describe a journey that is no longer possible, a path that no longer exists, junctions that have fallen into the depths or been sealed by new root-growth. But the story is still told, still sung, because it contains *the knowledge of change*. It says: *this happened before. The world shifted and we shifted with it. We are still here.*

You begin to understand that the Tethered do not experience the Hollow as hostile. They experience it as *alive*. Not alive in the way the roots are alive, but alive in the way a relationship is alive—dynamic, unpredictable, requiring constant attention and adaptation. The Hollow changes and they change with it. This is not surrender. This is *partnership*.

---

They have noticed your marks. Your scratches on root-bark, your stone arrangements. They have been reading them, you realize, trying to parse a language they do not know. Some of your marks have been answered: a scratch near yours, parallel, a question perhaps. *What does this mean? Are you speaking?*

You learn to answer. You learn their marks—simpler than you expected, not a full language but a system of waypoints and warnings. *Safe to cross. Unstable here. Water source. Cache nearby.* You add your own meanings and they accept them, incorporate them, pass them on to others in the network. Your symbols become part of their navigation.

This is how you become Tethered. Not through ceremony, not through acceptance into a family or community—the Tethered do not structure themselves that way. You become Tethered by *being connected*. By maintaining a bridge. By answering when called. By leaving marks that others can read.

You wake one morning and realize you have not been alone for a long time. There are people you can reach. Paths you maintain. A junction where your presence is expected, where someone will notice if you do not return. You have become part of the web.

---

The body you find has been there for a long time.

It is in a crevice where root-growth has sheltered it from the moisture, from the slow processes of decay. The Tethered who leads you there does so deliberately, watching your reaction. This is a test, you understand, or perhaps a teaching.

The body is old. The Tethered cannot say how old in terms you understand, but the stone around it has shifted, the roots have grown and died and grown again, the world has reconfigured multiple times since this person was placed here. Placed, not fallen. The position is careful. The hands are folded. Beside the body: tools, wrapped in leather that has mostly crumbled. A gift for a journey, or a return of what was borrowed.

The Tethered do not bury their dead. There is no soil. They do not burn them—fire is possible, you have learned, but precious, rare, reserved for specific purposes. They *place* them. They find crevices and niches in the ever-changing architecture of the Hollow, and they create small sheltered spaces, and they leave the bodies there with their tools, with tokens from those who were connected to them.

And then the Hollow takes them. The roots grow over. The stone shifts. The niches are sealed or exposed or transformed. A body placed in one location might, over time, be carried kilometers by the slow movement of the walls. Might be crushed, dissolved, incorporated. Might, in some rare cases, be preserved like this one—a message from a past configuration, a reminder that others came before.

The Tethered do not visit graves. There are no graves to visit. The dead become part of the Hollow's ongoing process. This is not loss. This is *incorporation*. The gesture for death is related to the gesture for the lost but different: hands open, fingers spread, then turned outward and *released*. Not held and then not held. *Held and then given back.*

---

You learn that they have theories about the light.

The sourceless brightness that fills the Hollow is not, to them, a mystery—or rather, it is a *particular kind* of mystery, one they have examined and sung about and passed down. The light comes from the moss, yes, from the bacteria within the moss, but the bacteria must be fed. Must receive something that allows them to burn cold and endless. The Tethered believe this something rises from below.

From the depths you cannot see. From whatever lies beneath the Hollow, in the darkness that breathes cold air upward. There is warmth down there, somewhere—it must be so, for the thermal cycles to work, for the currents to flow. And with the warmth, something else. A chemical, perhaps. A radiation. Something that rises through the stone and the roots and feeds the endless light.

They do not go down. This is not forbidden—the Tethered do not have prohibitions in the way you understand them—but it is *unconnected*. The depths are where roots thin and platforms become impossible and the lattice fails. The depths are where you can no longer be Tethered to anyone. To descend past a certain point is to choose disconnection, to become lost deliberately, to give yourself to the Hollow in a way that has no return.

Some have done this. The stories say so. Some have climbed down the great root-cables until the light began to change, until the cold became something other than cold, until they passed beyond the range of even the longest calls. What they found is not known. They did not come back to tell it.

The Tethered do not call this death. They call it *deep-going*, and the gesture for it is unlike any other: one hand descending while the other remains raised, the distance between them growing until the arms are fully extended, and then both hands closing slowly into fists. *Going and staying. Separation that is chosen.* There is respect in the gesture but also something like grief. The deep-goers are not lost. They are *gone*.

---

The voice you heard, early in your time here—the call that echoed through the void and received no answer—

The Tethered know about it. They heard it too, or heard reports of it, passed through the network. They have been trying to locate its source. Not to rescue, necessarily. To *reconnect*. If someone is out there, calling, they should be answered. They should be brought back into the web or, if that is impossible, they should at least know they were heard.

You join the search, eventually. You learn the calling-patterns: the specific melodic phrases that mean *I am here, where are you*, the responses that mean *I hear you, keep calling, I am coming*. You learn to project your voice into the haze, to use the acoustic properties of the Hollow to send sound farther than you thought possible.

You do not find the caller. No one does. The voice is not heard again.

But the search continues, in the background, always. It has become part of the network's purpose. A standing call, repeated at intervals from junctions across the Hollow. *If you are out there. If you can hear this. You are not alone.*

This is what the Tethered do. They maintain connections. They reach toward what might be reached. They refuse to assume that distance is permanent or silence is final.

---

In the deepest part of a storm, when the fog is so thick you cannot see your own hands, when the roots are swaying and creaking and the whole world seems about to tear itself apart—

You feel the rope.

It is tied to your waist. You had almost forgotten it. One of the Tethered tied it there before the storm began, and the other end is tied to them, and they are tied to another, and another, a chain of bodies all linked through the chaos. You cannot see them. You cannot hear them over the wind. But you can feel the tension on the rope, the slight tugs that communicate *I am here, I am still here, hold on*.

This is what Tethered means. Not a metaphor. A rope. A physical connection that persists when everything else fails.

The storm passes. The fog clears. You are still alive, and you are still held.

---

They do not ask where you came from. You have tried to explain, using gesture and the fragments of shared language you have developed, but the concept does not translate. A world above? A surface? They have stories of the ceiling-haze, of something beyond the light, but these are abstractions—mythological spaces rather than places anyone has been. The Hollow is the world. The roots are the world's structure. What could exist outside of this?

You stop trying to explain. It does not matter, you realize. You are here now. You are Tethered now. Whatever existed before—whatever *you* existed as before—has become as distant and abstract as their ceiling-myths. A story you might tell, if you could find the words. A place you once were, that you can no longer reach.

The Hollow grows a little larger every year. The walls breathe outward. New surfaces are exposed, colonized, woven into the network. Somewhere, perhaps, the expansion will eventually reach whatever lies above—will break through into light that has a source, into air that does not glow, into a world that does not require ropes and bridges and the constant work of staying connected.

But that is far away. That is geological time. For now, there is this: the bright haze, the root-lattices, the slow patient work of maintenance. The calls echoing through the void. The hands reaching out to grip and hold.

The Tethered continue. You continue with them.
