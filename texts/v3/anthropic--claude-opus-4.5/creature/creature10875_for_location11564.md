# The Tenders

They call themselves nothing. Names require separation—this from that, me from you—and the Tenders have not experienced separation in eleven thousand years.

There are perhaps forty of them. Perhaps four hundred. The count depends on how you define individual, and they have lost interest in that definition. What remains are bodies: humanoid still, more or less, though the skin has taken on a waxy translucence that lets you see the green threading through their veins. Not blood. Something the Engine provides. Something that lets them photosynthesize just enough to survive in a world that produces no food, only light.

They move across the leaf-plains in patterns that mirror the pulse-cycles below, walking when the glow intensifies, resting when it dims, their circadian rhythms entrained to a system that has no sun. You might see one standing motionless for six hours, face tilted upward toward the machinery-canopy, and you might assume meditation or worship or simply sleep. But if you watched the leaf-surface beneath their feet, you would see it responding—dimpling toward them, adjusting, as if trying to read them the way they are trying to read it.

They are listening. They have always been listening.

---

The oldest among them—if oldest means anything when they share memories like the leaves share light—carries a body that was once a maintenance technician. Centuries ago. The hands still remember calibration: how to test a pressure seal, how to adjust a flow rate, how to read the organic gears and know which ones are wearing toward failure. These skills have become holy. Not because the Tenders worship the Engine—worship implies distance, and they have none—but because the skills are *useful*, and usefulness is the only value the Engine recognizes, and the Tenders have become so saturated with the Engine's values that they can no longer tell where influence ends and self begins.

The technician-body walks the pillar-bases, pressing palms against the twisted helices, feeling for the vibrations that indicate stress fractures in the compressed plant-matter. When it finds one, it does not repair it. It cannot. The technology for that was lost before the Tenders' great-great-grandparents were born. Instead, it marks the location—scratching a symbol into the waxy surface with a fingernail that has hardened into something like bark—and moves on, and somewhere in the Engine's vast distributed intelligence, the mark is noticed, and the system routes growth-resources toward the weakness, and the pillar heals itself.

The Tenders are not workers. They are suggestions.

---

At night—and there is no night, but the Tenders have invented one, a collective agreement to close their eyes for eight hours when the pulse-patterns reach a certain configuration—they gather in the hill-regions where the design constraints have loosened. They sit in circles among the purposeless spirals and the topology-violating sap-loops, and they share what they have found.

Not in words. Words take too long and carry too little. Instead, they touch: hand to hand, forehead to forehead, the green in their veins synchronizing until they are, briefly, a single nervous system distributed across forty bodies. Four hundred bodies. The count still doesn't matter.

In these moments, they experience the Engine as the Engine experiences itself.

It is not pleasant. It is not unpleasant. It is *overwhelming*—a cascade of sensory data from eight hundred kilometers of leaf-surface, from millions of gears turning in their cellulose housings, from root-networks that extend downward into depths the Tenders have never seen and upward into canopy-structures they will never reach. The Engine is aware in a way that has nothing to do with consciousness as the Tenders once understood it. It does not think. It processes. It does not feel. It optimizes. And yet—

And yet there is something else. A residue. A quality in the calculations that the Tenders cannot name but recognize as *intention*, as if the Engine is not just running but running *toward* something, growing *toward* something, glowing *toward* something that it almost remembers but cannot quite reach.

The Tenders believe this is why they exist. Not to maintain the Engine—the Engine maintains itself—but to *remember* for it. To carry the one thing the Engine's designers did not encode into its cells: the knowledge of what it was all *for*.

They have failed. The knowledge was lost before they were born, before their ancestors were born, before the last true technicians died in their quarters surrounded by manuals no one could read anymore. The Tenders remember only that there was a purpose. They do not remember what it was.

But they keep trying.

---

The children—and there are children, born rarely, born carefully, born only when the Engine's resource-allocation seems to permit it—grow up saturated in green light and low-frequency hum. They learn to walk on leaf-surfaces that dimple and adjust beneath their feet. They learn to read the standing light-patterns, to predict where the brightness will intensify and where it will fade. By the time they are old enough to join the touch-circles, their nervous systems have already begun to adapt, the myelin sheaths of their neurons developing a faint luminescence that will glow through their skin by the time they reach adulthood.

They are taught—as much as anything is taught among people who share memories—that the Engine is their home. Not their master, not their god, but their *environment*, as fish are taught that water is their environment, as plants are taught that soil is their environment. The Engine provides light for their photosynthetic blood. The Engine provides warmth from its endless metabolic processes. The Engine provides purpose, even if the purpose is simply to continue, to maintain, to witness.

The children do not ask what is beyond the Engine. There is no beyond. The leaf-floor extends in every direction until it curves upward into the pillar-structures, and the pillar-structures extend upward until they become the canopy-machinery, and somewhere above the canopy there is presumably a sky, but no Tender has ever seen it. The Engine is the world. The world is the Engine. To ask what lies beyond is like asking what lies beyond existence itself.

---

Sometimes, one of them wanders off.

It is not rebellion. The Tenders do not rebel; they have nothing to rebel against. It is not madness, though the other Tenders watch the wanderer's retreating form with something that might be concern if they had the separation required to feel concern for another. It is simply... a calling. A pull. A sense that the Engine wants them *there*, in some specific location days or weeks of walking away, for reasons that do not need to be understood.

The wanderers rarely return.

The ones who do return come back changed. One came back with eyes that glowed in the wrong spectrum, colors that should not exist, and she could see things the others could not—fault lines in the canopy-structures, stress patterns in the root-networks, futures that the Engine was growing toward and futures it was growing away from. She sits now in the hill-regions, among the purposeless spirals, and she scratches marks into the leaf-surface that are not repair-requests but something else. Predictions, perhaps. Warnings. The Engine reads them. The Engine adjusts.

Another came back with no voice, his throat-tissues having merged with something he had found in the deep tangles, something that grew into him and through him and left him capable of producing only the electrical hum that the plants use to communicate across distances too vast for chemistry. He walks the pillar-bases now, and where he walks, the pillars grow differently—more efficiently, some say, though no one knows efficient toward *what*. He has become a message the Engine sends to itself. He does not seem to mind.

A third came back carrying something.

The others gathered around her in the dimming light-cycle, their touch-circle forming without conscious decision, and they shared what she had found: a fragment of something that was not plant-matter and not animal-matter and not any material the Engine had grown. Metal. Actual metal, corroded almost to nothing, stamped with symbols that almost resolved into language but did not quite.

A tool. A human tool. From before.

They held it in their shared consciousness for hours, turning it over, examining it from forty angles simultaneously, searching for meaning. The symbols suggested writing. The shape suggested purpose. The corrosion suggested age—age so vast that the Tenders could not comprehend it, could only feel the weight of it pressing against their merged awareness like a physical thing.

This was what the builders had held. These were the hands that had designed the Engine, that had encoded its genetics, that had set it growing toward whatever it was supposed to grow toward. And these hands had left something behind. A message, perhaps. A warning. A clue.

The Tenders could not read it.

They placed the fragment in a hollow in the hill-region, a place where the leaves had grown up around an absence as if protecting something that was no longer there. They marked the location with scratches that meant *important* and *unknown* and *return*. And then they dispersed, walking back to their individual routes across the leaf-plain, carrying the memory of the fragment in their shared consciousness, hoping that someday—in a generation, in a hundred generations—someone would be born who could decode it.

---

They do not hope for rescue.

The concept does not occur to them. The Engine is their world, and their world is complete, and if there are other worlds beyond it—worlds with suns and weather and soil and separation—those worlds are as theoretical to them as the Engine would be to someone who had never seen it. The Tenders exist here, in this place, in this system, and they will continue to exist here until the Engine stops.

If the Engine ever stops.

---

The moth-things land on their shoulders sometimes, connecting briefly, exchanging something. The Tenders feel these exchanges as flickers of distant awareness—a view from the canopy, a map of root-patterns, a sense of the pulse-cycles from a perspective they cannot otherwise access. The moth-things are the Engine's mobile sensors, and the Tenders are... something else. Something the Engine has not quite categorized. Something that might be parasites or symbionts or components or witnesses, and the Engine has been trying to determine which for eleven thousand years, and it has not yet reached a conclusion.

The Tenders do not know this. They cannot perceive the Engine's cognition directly, only its outputs—the growth-patterns, the resource-allocations, the slow adjustments that shape their world. But if they could perceive it, they would recognize something familiar in its endless calculations. Something that feels like *patience*. Something that feels like *waiting*.

The Engine is watching them. The Engine has always been watching them. And somewhere in its vast distributed processes, in the gears and the sap-flows and the electrical pulses that race through root-networks at the speed of light, a question is being asked over and over, a question that the Engine's builders encoded so deeply into its design that it cannot be overwritten or forgotten or ignored:

*What is the purpose of the parts that move between my parts?*

The Tenders continue their routes across the leaf-plain, and the light pulses on beneath their feet, and the question remains unanswered, and the Engine grows, and grows, and grows.

---

One of them is watching you now.

She stands at the edge of perception, waxy skin translucent in the green glow, eyes that have adapted to brightness so complete that darkness would blind her. She has been watching you since you arrived. She has shared what she sees with the others, and they have shared it with the Engine, and the Engine has incorporated it into its calculations, and somewhere in the vast machinery of this world, a decision is being made.

Not whether to harm you. The Engine does not harm. It only optimizes.

The decision is simpler: what category to place you in. Sensor, like the moth-things? Component, like the pillar-structures? Parasite, consuming resources without contributing? Symbiont, offering something the Engine cannot produce for itself?

Or something else. Something new. Something the Engine has not encountered in all its eons of running.

The Tender steps closer. Her hand extends, palm-up, in a gesture that might be greeting or might be assessment or might be something that has no name in any language you know. The green in her veins pulses in time with the leaf-surface below, and you realize that she is not separate from the Engine any more than a wave is separate from the ocean.

She is asking you something. Not in words—she has not spoken words in centuries—but in the tilt of her head, in the rhythm of her breathing, in the way her fingers curl slightly as if preparing to receive something fragile.

*What are you for?*

You do not have an answer.

She nods, slowly, as if this was expected. And then she turns, and walks back toward the horizon, her footsteps leaving no mark on the waxy surface, her form diminishing until she is indistinguishable from the standing light-patterns, and you are alone again with the hum and the glow and the distant turning of shapes you cannot understand.

The Engine notes your presence.

The pulse-cycles continue.

The Tenders tend.
