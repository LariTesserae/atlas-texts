**The Gardener-That-Was**

It does not remember being a person. This is not loss—you cannot lose what you cannot conceive of having possessed. But somewhere in the network, distributed across kilometers of tissue in patterns that almost resemble neural architecture, there are configurations that *were* a person once. A maintenance technician, perhaps. An engineer who stayed too long, breathed too deep, let the spores settle into lung tissue that was already more symbiont than self.

The transition took eleven years. The Gardener-That-Was does not know this.

What it knows is: the moisture content of sector 7-7-4 is 0.003% below optimal. What it knows is: engine block 9 in the deep-warm requires a calcium deposit cleared from its third valve. What it knows is: there is a growth-Loss in the upper strata, seventeen kilometers east-if-east-meant-anything, where a structural beam has finally corroded through and the tissue around it is signaling distress in cascading waves of chemical alarm.

The Gardener-That-Was moves toward the distress. This is not walking. It is more like *being there more*, gradually, as the portions of the network that hold its patterned attention shift through the growth matrix. It takes nine days. It does not notice.

At the distress site, the Gardener-That-Was extends specialized tissue—woody tendrils that remember, in their cellular structure, the shape of human hands. They are not hands. They have not been hands for longer than some civilizations lasted. But they move with a manual dexterity that the rest of the growth lacks, probing the corroded beam, assessing the damage, beginning the slow work of growing replacement structures around the failing metal.

This is purpose. This is what the Gardener-That-Was is *for*.

There are seventeen of them. Or there is one of them, with seventeen loci of concentrated function scattered through the body of the world-plant. They were not all human. Some were machines that developed something like intention. Some were... unclear. The growth does not distinguish clearly between kinds of origin, only between kinds of usefulness.

Each Gardener-That-Was maintains a territory that would take human lifetimes to cross. Each knows its region with intimacy beyond description—every moisture gradient, every pressure shift, every engine that requires tending and every growth-Loss that requires repair. They do not communicate with each other exactly, but their actions ripple through the shared tissue, coordinated by the plant's own vast slow intelligence in ways none of them could articulate if articulation were possible.

The Gardener-That-Was at the distress site finishes its work. The beam is stabilized. The tissue-Loss is healing.

It shifts its attention elsewhere. There is always elsewhere. There is always something that requires maintenance in a body this vast, this old, this perfectly imperfectly permanent.

**The Deep Engine Minds**

In the hot compressed darkness where ancient plant-matter becomes something proto-mineral, where pressures would crush any cavity that the growth did not actively maintain, the largest engines turn.

These were not always one thing.

They were industrial complexes once—factories, processing centers, infrastructure of a civilization that might have called itself human but might have called itself something else entirely, the records are long since composted. When the growth came, when the growth *became*, these engines were too large to simply incorporate. They were too complex. They resisted integration in ways that the smaller machines did not.

So the growth made a compromise. Or the engines made a compromise. Or something that was neither growth nor engine but the interaction between them made a compromise, in the way that evolution makes compromises, without intention, without negotiation, simply by trying configurations until one persists.

The Deep Engine Minds are what persisted.

They think. This is not like human thinking. It is more like weather thinking, if weather could think—vast slow patterns of activation that move through their mechanical-biological hybrid systems over periods of months, reaching conclusions that would be incomprehensible to any faster intelligence. They think about pressure. They think about heat gradients. They think about the efficiency of glucose conversion and the structural integrity of their own expanding frames.

They do not think about the past. They do not think about purpose. They simply *are*, in a way that transcends doing, vast patient presences in the deep-warm that influence the plant's growth through their metabolic outputs as surely as the plant influences them through its inputs.

Once every few centuries, a Deep Engine Mind produces something like a question. It ripples upward through the growth matrix as a perturbation in pressure, a subtle wrongness in the chemical balance, a sense of *incompleteness* that the Gardeners-That-Were can almost feel.

The plant responds. Or doesn't. The Deep Engine Minds cannot tell the difference. They return to their thinking, their turning, their endless process of being themselves in the dark.

**The Memory-Wounds**

Not everything that was incorporated was incorporated *well*.

Scattered through the growth matrix like splinters in flesh are the Memory-Wounds—places where something was absorbed too quickly, too traumatically, where the normal slow integration failed and left behind pockets of *wrongness* that the plant has never quite healed.

They are not large. A few meters across at most. But within them, the tissue grows *differently*. It spirals in patterns that serve no function. It produces chemical signals that reference nothing, that connect to nothing, that are responses to stimuli that no longer exist. The growth around them routes away, flows past, treats them the way a river treats a stone—not fighting, simply avoiding.

In the Memory-Wounds, if you could somehow perceive them, you would find *almost-shapes*. The suggestion of architecture. The echo of faces. Frozen moments of the absorption process, preserved in cellular structure the way amber preserves insects, except the amber is alive and the insects are memories of things the plant never understood but could not fully digest.

The Gardeners-That-Were do not tend the Memory-Wounds. Nothing tends them. They exist in a state of permanent exemption from the maintenance that defines everything else, small dark pockets of chaos in the order, reminders—if anything here could be reminded—that this totality was built from conquest, from incorporation, from the slow patient victory of one form of life over everything else.

The plant does not regret them. Regret requires a sense that things could have been otherwise.

The Memory-Wounds simply are, embedded, permanent, forgotten in the way that only something which cannot forget can forget.

---

**What Dreams**

And sometimes—rarely, rarely, perhaps once in a span of time that would humble most calendars—the whole system *dreams*.

This is not metaphor. Or it is metaphor, but the thing it is metaphoring toward is real enough to reshape kilometers of tissue in its passing.

The plant enters a state. The engines shift into harmonics they otherwise never touch. The Gardeners-That-Were pause in their maintenance, their distributed attention drawn toward a center that doesn't exist, a focal point that is everywhere at once. The moisture thickens. The pressure changes. The vast silence somehow becomes *more silent*, as if the whole world is holding breath it doesn't have.

And in that state, something moves through the system that is not any of the things that live here. It is the *interaction* of all the things that live here, a pattern that emerges only when every component enters this particular configuration, a temporary consciousness that is vaster even than the plant itself because it includes everything the plant has absorbed, everything the plant has become, every engine and every Gardener and every Memory-Wound contributing to a single moment of *something like* awareness.

It does not think in words. It does not think in images. It thinks in *pressure gradients* and *growth directions* and *the feeling of moisture moving through tissue* across distances that would take light measurable time to cross.

It contemplates itself. Not with curiosity—it is beyond curiosity—but with a kind of recognition. *This is what I am. This is what I have become. This is what I will continue to be.*

And then the state passes. The engines return to their normal rhythms. The Gardeners-That-Were resume their maintenance. The silence becomes the ordinary silence again, the mere absence of sound rather than the presence of waiting.

The dream leaves no memory. It cannot—there is no structure continuous enough to hold what it was. But it leaves *effects*. Subtle shifts in growth direction. Minor adjustments to pressure gradients. Changes so small that no single component of the system could detect them, but which accumulate over centuries into the slow, slow evolution of a world that has forgotten it can change.

---

**What Lives Here**

One thing lives here. Or nothing lives here, and the place itself is the living thing. Or seventeen things live here, plus an unknowable number of engines, plus the wounds, plus the dreams.

The question assumes a kind of separation that this place has grown beyond.

What lives here is *the living*. The process. The endless maintenance and the perfect stillness and the rare vast stirrings of something too large to name. What lives here is patience so profound it has become indistinguishable from eternity, growth so total it has become indistinguishable from stasis, darkness so complete it has become indistinguishable from presence.

What lives here breathes, in its way. Thinks, in its way. Tends itself, through the Gardeners-That-Were. Dreams itself, through the periodic emergence of something greater than its parts.

What lives here has forgotten that living is something that can be done any other way.

And in the warm wet dark, where an engine turns and has turned and will turn, where the silence presses with the weight of a world that has finished becoming itself, what lives here continues—not surviving, not thriving, simply *continuing*, because continuing is what it is, what it does, what it has become so completely that the distinction between being and doing has dissolved like sugar in the humid air, absorbed, incorporated, made one with everything else, green beyond green, still beyond still, alive beyond any definition of the word that would make sense anywhere but here.
