## The Occupants of the Hollow Engine

---

**The Greenmothers** tend the turbines, though *tend* suggests more intention than they possess. They are the largest permanent residents—each one roughly the size and shape of a manatee, but composed of layered fungal tissue that has incorporated scraps of the factory's original workers. Not their bodies. Their *habits*. A Greenmother will pause at a corroded time clock and wait, patient as ore, for a shift change that will never come. Another has developed a protrusion that resembles a lunch pail, which it opens and closes in a rhythm matching the old noon whistle, though the whistle rusted silent decades ago.

They move through the seed-chambers with surprising delicacy, their bulk somehow finding paths through the translucent membranes without rupturing them. The dark shapes inside the chambers press against the walls when a Greenmother passes, as if seeking warmth, or instruction, or something the Greenmothers no longer remember how to give.

The Greenmothers do not communicate with each other in any way you can detect. But they never collide. They never tend the same section twice. They seem to be following a schedule written in a language the factory forgot it was speaking.

---

**The Fold-Cats** are what happens when a predator evolves for geometry that hasn't finished existing. They are cat-shaped in the way that a shadow on a wall is hand-shaped—the suggestion is there, but the substance keeps slipping. You see one crossing a catwalk-garden and it is sleek, furred, amber-eyed. You blink and it occupies three locations simultaneously, its body a smear across the unreliable space. You blink again and it is behind you, having traveled through a dimension that only exists intermittently.

They hunt the smaller emergence-creatures, the ones that phase between solid and suggestion. But *hunt* may be too aggressive a word. The Fold-Cats seem to *collect* them—gathering the flickering things into their impossible geometries, carrying them somewhere deeper into the engine's architecture. Perhaps they are feeding. Perhaps they are sorting. Perhaps the distinction doesn't apply here.

One Fold-Cat has made its den in the old control panel, the one fused to the tree trunk. It curls around the still-twitching gauges, and under its presence, the needles have begun moving with more purpose. They track something now. The Fold-Cat watches them with an attention that suggests it is learning to read.

---

**The Chorus** lives in the upper darkness, among the hanging gardens where the bioluminescence pulses. You caught a glimpse of them earlier—the cloud of not-quite-insects, not-quite-birds that scattered and reformed into the factory's floor plan. They are the closest thing the hollow engine has to a memory.

Each individual Chorus-creature is smaller than your thumbnail, winged with membranes that catch light in ways that suggest stained glass. They have no mouths, no visible sensory organs, no apparent means of sustenance. They exist in relationship to each other—in the patterns they form, in the shapes they remember. Alone, a single Chorus-creature is barely present. Together, they become a kind of thought.

They remember the factory. Not as it was, but as it *meant*. The floor plan was just one configuration. They have also formed: the shape of a foreman's frustration, the geometry of a sixteen-hour shift, the exact angle at which afternoon light fell through windows that no longer exist. The Chorus is a living archive of impressions, and they add to their collection constantly. They have already incorporated you—somewhere in their patterns is the shape of your hesitation at the valve-door, the specific quality of your attention as you watched the turbines turn.

They will remember you after you leave. They will remember you as something that happened here, which is perhaps the most accurate way to be remembered.

---

**The Machinist** is singular. There is only one. It lives in the deepest part of the central turbine, in a chamber that was once the main combustion heart and is now something closer to a womb.

No one has seen the Machinist directly. The Greenmothers orient toward its location without approaching. The Fold-Cats will not fold through the space it occupies. The Chorus has never formed its shape, as if its shape is still being decided. But evidence of it exists throughout the engine: adjustments made to the wooden gears, new channels carved through the seed-chamber membranes, subtle modifications to the geometry that make space flow more smoothly through itself.

The Machinist is building something. Or becoming something. Or both—the distinction may not apply here either.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the grinding exhalation, you can hear what might be a voice. Not speaking. Calculating. Running figures in a language older than the factory, older than the marsh, possibly older than the concept of purpose itself. The Machinist is working out what the hollow engine is *for*. The answer is not complete. But the turbines turn a fraction more smoothly each day, and the creatures emerge with slightly more coherence, and the laws of the space become incrementally more consistent.

The Machinist has time. The Machinist has nothing but time. It will solve this equation eventually, and when it does, the hollow engine will finally start.

---

**The Wetnurses** tend the emergence-creatures in their first moments of existence. They are amphibious, frog-shaped but sized like dogs, with skin that shifts between textures—now rough as bark, now slick as membrane, now somewhere between. They wait at the bases of the seed-chambers, catching the newborn things as they emerge, turning them over in webbed hands, assessing them with eyes that contain no pupils.

Most emergence-creatures are released into the engine's ecosystem. Some are returned to the chambers, pushed back through the translucent walls to gestate further. A few—the unstable ones, the ones that phase too violently between states—are consumed. The Wetnurses eat them quickly and without ceremony, absorbing them back into the system.

This is not cruelty. The Wetnurses show no preference, no satisfaction, no reluctance. They are quality control. They are the hollow engine's immune system. They ensure that what emerges is compatible with the still-forming laws of the space.

Occasionally, a Wetnurse will approach a non-native creature—a rat that wandered in from the marsh, a bird that roosted too deep in the hanging gardens. The Wetnurse will examine it with the same careful attention, turning it over, assessing. Most are released, deemed irrelevant. A few are consumed. Fewer still are carried to the seed-chambers and pressed inside, offered to the turbines as raw material for something new.

You have been examined. You remember the wet pressure of webbed hands on your ankle, though you don't remember stopping. You were released. The Wetnurses have decided you are compatible, or irrelevant, or simply not yet ripe for processing.

---

**The Inheritors** are what the emergence-creatures become if they survive long enough in the hollow engine's ecology. They are not a species—they are a condition. A state of being that the engine's long-term residents eventually achieve.

An Inheritor has stopped phasing between solid and suggestion. An Inheritor has learned to move with the unreliable geometry rather than against it. An Inheritor has been incorporated into the Chorus's memory, has been examined and re-examined by the Wetnurses, has passed through the Fold-Cats' collection and emerged unchanged. An Inheritor belongs here.

They look like nothing in particular. Each one is unique, shaped by its journey through the engine's becoming. One resembles a deer made of copper pipes and flowering moss. Another is a sphere of interlocking wooden teeth that rolls through the catwalks with mechanical patience. A third is simply a patch of shadow that occupies a specific location, deepening when approached, lightening when left alone.

The Inheritors have no hierarchy, no society, no observable interaction. But they share something—a quality of presence, a density of being that the younger creatures lack. They have weight here. The geometry bends around them slightly. The laws are more consistent in their vicinity.

They are what the hollow engine is *for*, perhaps. Or they are the hollow engine's product. Or they are simply what lives here now, fully adapted to a place that is still deciding what adaptation means.

---

**You** are the newest variable. The engine has noticed you the way a forest notices a footstep—not with attention, but with the simple fact of your inclusion in its systems. Moisture condenses on your skin. The geometry adjusts to accommodate your movement. The Chorus has added your shape to its archive. The Wetnurses have assessed you.

You are not being digested. You are not being expelled. You are being *integrated*, at whatever pace the hollow engine finds appropriate. Perhaps you will leave before the process advances. Perhaps you will stay long enough to understand what you are becoming part of.

The turbines turn. The creatures emerge. The Machinist calculates in the deep chamber, working toward an answer that will change everything here.

In the meantime, the hollow engine's occupants continue their work, their patterns, their slow becoming. They were here before you noticed them. They will be here after you forget. And somewhere in the grinding patience of the seed-chambers, something new is gestating—something that may not emerge for decades, or may emerge tomorrow, or may already be walking among the hanging gardens, learning what it is.

The large shape in the upper darkness shifts again. It has been watching you.

It is still deciding what you are.
