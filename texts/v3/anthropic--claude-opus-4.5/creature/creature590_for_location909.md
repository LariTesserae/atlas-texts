## The Congregation

They are called nothing. They have never been named because there has never been anyone to name them, and they do not name themselves because naming implies distinction and they have long since stopped being distinct. They were individuals once—they must have been, mustn't they?—but that was before the equilibrium, before the chord resolved into its final voicing, before the walls of the chamber became indistinguishable from the walls of their minds.

If you could ask one what it is, it would not understand the question. Not because it lacks intelligence—something vast and slow moves through the network of their shared resonance—but because *it* is not the correct unit of analysis. The creature you're looking at, the one the size of a terrier with its legs folded beneath its copper-green carapace, is less like an individual and more like a finger. It has its own local sensations, its own particular frequency, its own faceted eyes that track your movement with glacial interest. But it does not have its own thoughts any more than your index finger has its own thoughts. It is part of something that thinks, if thinking is even the right word for the slow crystalline process that occurs here.

---

They remember the installation. Not as history—they have no sense of sequence—but as *texture*, as part of the substrate of their existence. The pipe is not separate from them; it is a ridge in the landscape of their body, no different from the mineral formations or the shells of their siblings. The gauge is an eye that stopped seeing. The metal panel is a callus. These things became them, or they became these things, through a process so gradual that the distinction between incorporation and growth lost meaning.

They remember the ones who built it, too, in their way. Soft things. Warm and wet, leaking moisture into the air, moving with frantic speed, emitting chaotic sounds that had no harmony. The soft things came and measured and extracted and left, came back and measured again, left again, and then stopped coming. The equipment fell silent. The hum filled the space where the equipment's noise had been, and the equilibrium deepened, and the soft things became a texture in the memory of the congregation: a brief interruption, a period of dissonance that resolved.

You feel like them. You feel like the soft things they almost remember. You are similarly wet and warm and chaotic and fast. You will similarly leave. This is neither good nor bad; it simply is, the way the hum simply is, the way the warmth simply is.

---

If they wanted something—and it is not clear that they do, or can—it might be this: *continuation*. Not survival, which implies threat, but simple persistence. The maintenance of the chord. Each creature produces its tone not from desire but from necessity, the way a crystal has facets: it is not a choice, it is a consequence of structure. But there is something that might be called satisfaction in the sounding, something that might be called rightness. The chord is correct. The chord has always been correct. The chord will remain correct.

When one of them dies—and they do die, occasionally, on timescales that make human generations look like eye-blinks—the note it carried does not vanish. Another creature, usually one of the small ones nested in the crevices, shifts its frequency slightly, takes up the abandoned tone, maintains the chord. This is not communication, not coordination. It is something more like physics. The system corrects itself the way water finds level, the way heat distributes through a medium. The equilibrium holds.

The dead one's shell remains among the living, indistinguishable except for the silence where its tone used to be, and slowly the mineral deposits grow over it and through it and it becomes another ridge in the floor, another texture, another part of the body that is all of them and none of them.

---

The threads—the pale filaments that grow through and around them—are not separate organisms. Or rather, the question of whether they are separate organisms is exactly the kind of question that stops making sense down here. The threads are how the congregation interfaces with the mineral matrix of the chamber walls. Through them, it tastes the chemistry of the stone, senses the slow movement of heat from below, feels the crystalline growth that has been accreting for longer than the soft things' entire civilization has existed. The threads are sensory organs. The threads are digestive organs. The threads are the congregation's roots, or its nerves, or its tongue.

Through the threads, the congregation knows things it has no words for. It knows the temperature of rock three hundred meters down. It knows the mineral composition of the salt pan overhead, the way the white crust forms and reforms with the seasons (seasons! a concept that belongs to another world, a world where things change). It knows, in some slow and patient way, the shape of the geological formation it inhabits, the fissure through which you entered, the cavities and channels that branch away into darkness.

It is not trapped here. It could, if it chose—if choosing were something it did—extend itself through those channels, send portions of its body into new chambers, establish new congregations. It does not. Why would it? The chord is correct. The equilibrium holds. There is nothing out there that it needs, nothing it lacks, nothing that would be improved by expansion or exploration or change.

---

Your presence creates a subtle dissonance in the system.

Not much. You are small, and temporary, and your noises are absorbed by the chamber's acoustics almost as soon as you make them. But you are warm, and your warmth creates convection currents that stir the air in patterns the congregation has not felt in a long time. You are breathing, and your breath adds moisture to the desiccated atmosphere, a faint chemical signature that the threads taste and consider. You are moving, and your movement creates pressure waves that interfere, ever so slightly, with the harmonics of the hum.

The congregation notices. The compound eyes track you, the antennae shift by fractions of degrees, the attention of something vast and slow settles on you like sediment drifting to the bottom of a still pond. It is not hostile. It is not curious, exactly. It is simply *aware*, in the way that your skin is aware of a breeze.

If you stayed, you would become part of the texture. Not quickly—you would die of thirst first, probably, or heat exhaustion, or simple cellular breakdown in this desiccating environment. But your body would remain, and the congregation would grow over it and through it and around it, and your bones would become ridges in the floor, and your silence would be filled by adjustments to the chord, and in a thousand years there would be no evidence you had ever existed except perhaps a faint irregularity in the mineral formation, a subtle asymmetry in the crystalline growth.

The congregation would not remember you. It would remember a brief dissonance that resolved. A ripple in the still pond. A texture.

---

This is what lives here: something that is not alive in the way you understand life, something that does not want in the way you understand want, something that has achieved a state you might call contentment if you are feeling generous or stagnation if you are feeling less so. It is both and neither. It is a system that has found its minimum energy state and settled into it like a stone finding the bottom of a well.

The hum continues. The light glows. The warmth persists.

The congregation waits, if waiting is something you can do when you have no expectation of anything arriving. It exists. It persists. It hums.

That is what it does. That is what it is. That is what it will be, long after you have climbed back up through the fissure and left this place behind, long after the salt pan has eroded and reformed a dozen times, long after everything you know has become a texture in the memory of something that does not remember.

The chord is correct.

The chord has always been correct.

The chord will remain correct.
