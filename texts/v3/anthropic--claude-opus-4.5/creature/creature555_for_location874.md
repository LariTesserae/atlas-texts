# The Teem

They have no name for themselves as a whole. This is not because they lack language—they have sounds that mean things, passed between them in the dark—but because the concept of *themselves as a category* has not yet become necessary. There is the valley. There are the things in the valley that are not them. And there are *those-who-hold*, which is the closest translation for what they call each other, and that has been enough.

---

They are smaller than you expect, finding the tools. The sharpened bone, thrust into the earth at that angle, was wielded by something that stands perhaps to your hip, maybe less. Their bodies are dense and low-slung, built for moving through the crush of vegetation without disturbing it more than necessary—not because they fear detection, but because energy spent pushing through fronds is energy not spent on other things, and they have learned economy. They move with their weight centered, arms—four of them, the upper pair stronger, the lower pair more dexterous—held close to the torso until needed.

Their skin is not quite skin. It has the quality of the plants here, that membrane-like bark—slightly damp always, slightly yielding, capable of some photosynthesis from the foxfire glow, though not enough to sustain them. It marks them as native in a way that goes deeper than ancestry. The valley grew them the way it grew everything else, out of the mineral-rich soil and the wet dark, shaped by the same pressures that shaped the seething vegetation and the swarming insects. They are not apart from this place. They are a *fold* in it, a concentration, the way a whirlpool is a fold in water.

Their eyes are large and many-lensed, adapted not for the darkness—there is no true darkness here, only the unreliable pulse of foxfire—but for parsing movement in a world where everything moves. They see the valley not as a landscape but as a *pattern of changes*, a constant flow that they read the way you might read a face. When they go still, which is often, it is not stillness the way you understand it. It is *attention*, the whole body becoming a receptive surface.

---

They live in groups of shifting size—sometimes three or four, sometimes a dozen, rarely alone. The groups are not families in the way you understand family, though kinship matters. They are more like *task-clusters*, forming around needs and dissolving when the needs are met. A hunt might pull together eight of them from three different bloodlines, working in coordinated silence through the understory, then scattering back to their separate sleeping-hollows before the fungal light has cycled twice.

The sleeping-hollows are the closest thing they have to architecture: depressions in the soft ground, lined with shed fronds and the fibrous material you found twisted into cordage. They do not build upward, do not enclose themselves. The valley is already enclosed. The humidity is already warmth. They need only a place where the eternal drip of condensation is diverted, where the crawling insectoid multitudes pass around rather than over, where they can let the attention relax into something like rest.

They sleep lightly and often, in stretches measured in minutes, several times through what would be a day if days existed here. Their dreams, if they dream, are not separable from their waking perception of the valley's movement. They slip in and out of consciousness the way the foxfire slips in and out of brightness. It is not a boundary they mark.

---

Their tools tell you what they do: they hunt, they process, they carry.

The hunts are not for the largest creatures—those heavy pale things that push through the understory are too dangerous, too costly. The Teem—call them that, though they wouldn't—pursue the mid-sized swimmers of the current, the animals that navigate the density in predictable patterns. They have learned the patterns, or rather, they have learned how to *read* the patterns as the patterns change. A hunting party will position itself along a likely route, go still, become part of the movement-flow of the valley, and wait. When prey passes—felt more than seen, a disturbance in the pattern—they strike from multiple angles, not to kill immediately but to direct, to drive the animal toward the one who holds the sharpened bone.

The kills are processed quickly and communally. Everything is eaten or used; nothing rots unconsumed. They have not invented containers, not yet, so nothing is stored. The hunt feeds the group that hunted. Tomorrow—not that there is tomorrow—they will hunt again. The valley provides in this way: continuously, demandingly, without surplus. You eat what you kill when you kill it, or the valley takes it back.

The stones arranged in circles, stained dark—these are for breaking shells. The pools are full of hard-bodied things, and the Teem have learned that certain rocks, struck at certain angles, open them efficiently. The circles are not ritual. They are *stations*, places where the work is done, where the shells accumulate until the shell-pile itself becomes a landmark. Some of the older stations have shell-middens higher than the Teem themselves, years of cracking and eating compressed into small monuments of use.

---

They communicate, and this is where they become difficult to describe.

The sounds they make with their mouths are simple: clicks, grunts, a rising tone that indicates direction, a falling tone that indicates warning. Enough for hunting. Enough for gathering. But the *other* communication, the kind that passes between them when they go still together, when they all turn their attention to the same fold in the valley's pattern—this is not sound. It is not gesture. It is something closer to *shared reading*, a collective parsing of the movement-flow that becomes, in the parsing, a kind of language.

They know things about each other that they have not told. They know when one of them is sick by the way the sick one moves through the pattern, the way the valley responds. They know when one is gravid, when one is old, when one is lying about the location of a shell-station. The valley is a medium through which information passes, and they have learned to read it well enough that deception is difficult.

This is not telepathy. It is something simpler and stranger: a shared environment so dense with signal that individuals become *legible* to each other, the way a word becomes legible on a page. They cannot read each other's thoughts. They can read each other's *effects*.

And so they have not developed complex spoken language, not yet. They have not needed it. The valley speaks for them, through them, and they listen.

---

They know the makers-of-tools are watched. The valley watches everything.

This is not paranoia. It is simply true, simply the nature of existence in a place where ten thousand eyes need no light to see, where every movement is registered by something, where the insectoid multitudes crawl over their sleeping bodies and the larger things note their hunting-paths and the vegetation grows toward and away from their trails in response to some chemical signature they cannot control.

They do not experience this as threat. They experience it as *texture*, as the basic grain of existence. Being watched is not different from being wet or being warm. It is simply a property of the valley. They watch back, with their many-lensed eyes, and the watching is mutual, and the mutuality is the only equilibrium possible.

When they hunt, they know they are also hunted. When they eat, they know they will be eaten. When they breed—in brief, efficient couplings that happen in the sleeping-hollows, that produce offspring small and many, most of whom will feed the valley before they feed themselves—they know they are contributing to a system that does not distinguish between contributor and contribution.

They are not at peace with this. They are not in conflict with it. They simply *are* it, the way a current is the river, the way a breath is the breathing.

---

Their religion, if it can be called that, is attention.

They do not worship the valley. They do not propitiate it or ask it for favor. But they have developed practices—not rituals, not yet, but something that will become ritual—around the act of paying attention to it. The stillness they fall into, the collective reading of the pattern, the moments when a hunting party pauses before the kill and all of them orient toward the same point in the movement-flow: these are not strategic. They are *observances*, in the oldest sense of the word. They are moments when the Teem observe the valley, and in observing, acknowledge it.

The young are taught to go still. This is the first and most important teaching. Before they learn to hold a bone-tool, before they learn the shell-stations, before they learn the hunting-patterns, they learn to stop moving and *attend*. The ones who cannot learn this do not survive. Not because the others kill them—nothing so direct—but because a creature that cannot go still in the Spawning Dark is a creature that disrupts the pattern, that draws the wrong attention, that becomes visible in a way that the visible do not survive.

Some of the old ones, the ones who have lived through dozens of fungal-cycles, who have cracked shells at the same station until their lower hands are calloused smooth, who have watched their own offspring feed the valley more often than not—some of these old ones are said to have gone *deeper* into the attention. To have stopped distinguishing between themselves and what they attend to. They sit at the edges of the shell-middens, or in the hollows where the pooling water glows brightest, and they do not hunt, do not eat unless fed, do not respond to clicks or grunts or rising tones.

They are not dead. They are still breathing, still damp-skinned, still many-eyed. But they have become *porous* in some way, open to the valley's pattern in a way that closes them to everything else. The others bring them food, sometimes. The others watch them, sometimes, the way they watch everything. And sometimes the old ones move—a slow shift, an orientation toward something no one else has seen—and the others follow the gaze, and learn something about the pattern that they could not have learned otherwise.

This is not leadership. It is not prophecy. It is something the Teem have no word for yet, though they will need one, eventually, as the practice calcifies into tradition.

---

They are not individuals the way you are. But they are not a hive, not a colony, not a collective in the way you might imagine.

Each one has preferences, patterns of behavior that distinguish it from the others. One cracks shells with a particular rhythm. One sleeps in a particular hollow, returning to it across dozens of cycles, defending it not with violence but with presence—simply being there, simply having been there, until the others route around. One makes a sound no other makes, a kind of trill in the mid-range, and the others have learned that this sound means *this one is here*, and they orient by it in the darkness the way you might orient by a familiar voice.

But these preferences, these distinguishing patterns, do not coalesce into *selfhood* the way yours do. The Teem do not experience themselves as the centers of their own stories. They experience themselves as *locations*, as points in the pattern, as folds in the valley's continuous becoming. The one who trills is not *I who trill*. The one who trills is *the trilling*, is *where the trilling happens*, is a feature of the soundscape as much as a creature with a life.

This is difficult for you to understand. It will be difficult for them, too, eventually, when they develop the cognitive complexity to ask questions about it. For now, they simply live in the way they live, and the question does not arise.

---

Their future is not visible from here. The valley is young, and they are younger, and neither has stabilized into a form that will persist.

Perhaps the tools will become more complex. Perhaps the shell-stations will become settlements, the sleeping-hollows will become shelters, the attention-practices will become temples. Perhaps they will learn to name themselves, to speak of themselves as *we* and *they*, to experience the selfhood that will make them legible to you in ways they are not yet legible.

Perhaps the valley will change. The young rock will stop releasing minerals, the warmth will fade, the foxfire will dim. Perhaps the density will thin and the Teem will spread, become something rangier and more solitary, lose the pattern-reading in favor of something more portable.

Perhaps something else will climb out of the pools, or drop from the canopy, or push through the understory—something better adapted, something hungrier, something that reads the Teem the way the Teem read the valley and finds them *useful*.

For now, there is only this: the roar that is not a roar but a density of small sounds. The light that is not light but a glow that comes from life itself. The creatures that are not creatures but folds in a pattern, holding their bone-tools, cracking their shells, going still together at the edge of pools that churn with everything that is and is becoming.

The valley breathes. The Teem breathe with it. And in the dark that is not dark, in the silence that is not silence, they attend.
