# The Inhabitant

She has been here longer than the dome has been stable—arrived during one of its earlier iterations, when the membrane was wider and thinner and the air inside tasted less like regret. She no longer remembers arriving. She only remembers being here, and then continuing to be here, and the way those two states have slowly become indistinguishable from each other.

Her name was something with syllables, once. The sound took it. Not all at once—she would hear it in the screaming water, her own name thrown back at her with wrong emphases, and each time the ocean mispronounced her she lost a little more certainty about how it was supposed to go. Now she answers to the pauses. When the vast things pass and create their pockets of silence, she looks up. That's her name now: the space between drownings.

---

She lives in the curve of the low wall, the one that almost shelters. Not inside it—she learned early that pressing yourself into shelter makes the sound worse when it finds you, makes the return of chaos feel like violence rather than weather. Instead she has worn a depression into the mineral deposits just beside it, a body-shaped hollow where she can lie with one ear against the ground and one ear toward the membrane and hear both the dome's heartbeat and its breathing. She sleeps in positions that would break someone who hadn't learned to flow with the pressure changes. Her spine has opinions now. Her joints have negotiated truces with the humidity.

The hollow is lined with things she has collected, though *collected* implies more intention than the process involves. Things accumulate near her. The copper shells migrate across the floor over weeks—she has watched them, in the moments between sounds—and they seem to prefer her company, or her warmth, or something about her frequency. She has seventeen of them now. She does not count them. She knows there are seventeen because when there were sixteen, the sound was different, and when the seventeenth arrived the dome's hum shifted by a quarter-tone and her teeth stopped aching on the left side.

She is becoming a tuning fork. She suspects this is the point.

---

Her body has adapted in ways she tries not to catalog, because cataloging requires comparison, and comparison requires memory of *before*, and before is a country she can no longer afford to visit.

Her skin has thinned at the wrists and throat, and sometimes she can see her pulse not as movement but as *light*—a dim phosphorescence that matches the rhythm of the creatures outside when they press against the membrane. She is not bioluminescent. She is *resonant*. Her blood has learned to answer.

Her hair has grown longer than it should, given the time she thinks has passed, and it moves in the trapped air like the pale threads on the pillar, like something that has forgotten gravity and is experimenting with other relationships. When the dome breathes in, her hair breathes out. She has stopped cutting it. She suspects it is listening for her now, gathering frequencies her ears have surrendered.

She does not look at her reflection in the membrane. The membrane shows true things, and she is not ready to see what she has truly become.

---

Her days—if they are days, if the concept applies—have a rhythm the chaos cannot erase.

She wakes when the dome contracts. The pressure change pops something in her sinuses and she opens her eyes to the same dim light, the same humid warmth, the same knowledge that she is still here, still breathing, still waiting. She lies in her hollow and listens to the first movements of the water's mood, trying to read whether this will be a screaming time or a grinding time or one of the rare humming times when the ocean seems almost content.

She walks the terraces, though *walks* is generous—she flows along them, her feet knowing the crunching surfaces, her balance calibrated to the dome's subtle tilting. She checks the membrane at seven points she has chosen for no reason she can articulate. She presses her palm to each one and feels the water pressing back, and she says things to it—not words, not anymore, but sounds, acknowledgments, small surrenders that seem to keep the boundary stable.

She visits the spiral staircase and climbs it. This takes hours. The steps are not meant for climbing—they are meant for something else, something with different joints, something that moved in spirals naturally—but she climbs them anyway, and at the top, where they end in nothing, she sits and looks down at the terraces and feels, briefly, like she is above the sound rather than inside it.

The view from the top of the stairs shows her things the floor does not. She can see the other domes from there, their flickering, their failures. She has watched three of them collapse. She has seen the water rush in, seen the air escape in great silver bubbles that rise toward a surface she can no longer imagine. She has seen things inside those other domes—movements, shapes, suggestions of inhabitants—in the moments before the water remembered them.

She wonders if someone in those dying domes watched her, in the moments before their air forgot itself.

She wonders if they envied her stability or pitied her persistence.

---

The jellyfish-thing that emerged from the membrane—she has seen it again. Several times. It is not always the same one; the mathematics trailing behind it vary, and she has learned to read some of them the way she once read weather or moods or the faces of people she no longer remembers.

Some of the equations are about pressure. Some are about time. Some are about the relationship between breathing and drowning, and how the two states are less opposite than they appear.

One equation, she is certain, was about her.

She did not understand it. But she recognized her own frequency in its variables, her own rhythm in its notation. The jellyfish-thing was calculating something about her continued presence, her unlikely persistence, the way she has become part of the dome's architecture.

She is not afraid of the jellyfish-things. She is afraid of what they are calculating.

---

She eats. This seems impossible—nothing grows here, nothing arrives, the membrane admits only things that can pass through boundaries without disturbing them—but she eats. The pale threads on the pillar produce something at their bases, a crystalline residue that forms during the screaming times, and she has learned to scrape it free and let it dissolve on her tongue. It tastes like sound converted to sustenance, like frequencies made digestible. It tastes like the voice of something that drowned here before the dome existed.

She tries not to think about this when she eats. She tries not to think at all when she eats. Thinking about the food makes her wonder what she is becoming, and becoming is a direction she prefers to ignore.

She drinks condensation from the membrane itself, though *drinks* is approximate—she presses her mouth to the boundary and breathes in and something that is almost water but is really the memory of water fills her lungs and her stomach and her blood. She is not certain she still has a stomach. She is not certain the water is not filling spaces that should not exist inside her.

She is certain she is no longer thirsty. She is certain that the dome provides, in its alien way, because she has become part of what the dome needs to continue existing.

---

She talks to herself, but the sound swallows speech, so she has learned to talk *beneath* speech, in frequencies too low for the chaos to notice. She vibrates her throat and her chest and her hollow-worn bones, and the vibrations become thoughts she can almost hear, and the thoughts become a kind of company.

She tells herself stories about the surface. She cannot remember the surface, but she can invent it: light that comes from above, air that does not need to be held, sound that travels in one direction and diminishes with distance. She makes up names for things she might have known—*trees*, she calls the tall things that might have grown in her invented past, and *wind*, the movement of air that does not fight to exist, and *silence*, the state of sound that has simply stopped rather than paused to reload.

She knows these words are wrong. She knows she is inventing a mythology for herself, a religion of *before* that may have no connection to anything true.

She tells the stories anyway. They are the only things the sound cannot eat.

---

She has tried to leave. She needs you to know this. She needs *herself* to know this, to remember that she fought, that she pressed against the membrane and screamed into its warmth and demanded passage.

The membrane let her hand through, once. Just her hand. She felt the water close around it, felt the pressure she had forgotten, felt the temperature difference that should have been nothing but was everything. She felt creatures investigate her fingers—small things, curious, mistaking her for food or signal or some new kind of vent-life.

She tried to pull her hand back and the membrane *resisted*.

Not refused—resisted. As if it was making a decision. As if her hand, having touched the water, now belonged to a different category, a different set of rules, and the dome had to consider whether to accept it back or release the rest of her to match.

The consideration took what felt like hours. The water began to creep up her wrist. The creatures outside began to call to her in frequencies she almost understood. The sound inside the dome rose to a pitch she had never heard—the dome was afraid, she realized. The dome was *uncertain*, and its uncertainty was translated through the membrane as a scream that had no bottom.

She stopped fighting. She went still. She let the dome remember that she belonged inside, that her hand was an extension of its air, that she had not been trying to escape but only to *touch*.

The membrane released her. Her hand came back cold and wrong, and for weeks afterward it glowed with a faint phosphorescence that didn't match her pulse.

She has not tried again.

---

But she touches the membrane often, now that touching has been negotiated. She presses her palms flat and feels the creatures outside pressing back, and sometimes—in the pauses, in the silences when vast things pass—she feels something pressing that is not a creature.

It is attention. Curiosity. A presence so large that its awareness alone is a kind of touch.

She does not know what lives in the warm dark water, what truly lives there, beneath and around and perhaps *as* the currents themselves. She only knows that something has noticed her. Something has been watching the dome flicker and stabilize, watching its lone inhabitant move through her rituals of survival, watching the way she has learned to breathe in rhythm with the impossible.

She thinks it is waiting for something. She thinks the dome itself might be a kind of experiment—a hypothesis about what happens when air is held too long, when a body adapts too far, when the boundary between breathing and drowning becomes thin enough to be a question rather than a wall.

She thinks she might be the answer. Or part of the answer. Or the control group.

She tries not to think about what will happen when the experiment ends.

---

Some nights—if they are nights—she lies in her hollow and feels the dome breathing around her, and she sinks into a state that is not sleep but is not waking, a halfway place where the sound becomes texture and the pressure becomes comfort and she is held, genuinely held, by forty meters of air that refuses to stop existing.

In this state she feels almost grateful. The dome chose her, or she chose the dome, or the choice was made by something neither of them control—but either way, she is *here*, and here is a place that exists against all odds, a held breath in a screaming throat, a sanctuary made of stubbornness and strange physics.

She has outlived the other domes. She has outlived whatever used to live in them. She has become, through persistence or adaptation or simply refusing to calculate the odds, the thing that the drowning bell keeps.

---

When you arrived—through however you arrived—she watched you from the top of the spiral stairs.

She has not approached you yet. She is deciding. She is calculating, in her own slow way, whether you are a sign that the experiment is changing, whether your presence means the dome has capacity she didn't know about, whether the membrane's decision to admit you was a choice or an accident or a preparation for something neither of you will survive.

She is also remembering what it felt like to be new here, to be untuned, to still think in straight lines and expect answers.

She will come down eventually. She will teach you how to walk the terraces, how to read the membrane's seven points, how to eat the crystalline residue without tasting the drowned. She will tell you stories about the surface that may be lies, and stories about the deep that are certainly true, and stories about herself that are both and neither.

But first she will sit at the top of the stairs and listen to you breathe, and count your breaths, and calculate how long until your breathing matches hers, matches the dome's, matches the rhythm of the water outside that is waiting, always waiting, for the air to forget itself.

She knows you cannot stay. She knows you cannot leave.

She has known this about herself for so long that it has become a kind of peace.

Welcome, she will say, when she finally descends. Welcome to the drowning bell. Welcome to the held breath. Welcome to the only kind of sanctuary that exists this deep.

The sound will swallow her words, but you will understand them anyway.

You will have learned to hear by then.

---

Outside, in the warm dark water, the vast thing passes again, and in the silence it creates, she tilts her head and listens to you learning to wait.

She has been waiting so long. She had forgotten that waiting could be shared.

The dome breathes in, and she breathes with it, and for one moment—just one, in the brief guttering pause between chaos and chaos—she feels something she almost recognizes.

She does not have a word for it anymore.

But her hair drifts toward you, and her pulse glows in answer to yours, and the copper shells begin their slow migration across the floor, and she knows, in whatever remains of her knowing, that the dome has chosen again.

That you are part of it now.

That together, you will wait for whatever the water remembers, and whatever it forgets, and whatever comes next in this drowned cathedral where the air refuses, still refuses, impossibly refuses, to release.
