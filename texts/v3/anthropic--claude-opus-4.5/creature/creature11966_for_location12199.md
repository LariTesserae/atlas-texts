**The Pressers**

They are not here, and they have never been anywhere else.

You will not see them. This is not because they hide—they have nothing to hide *with*, no bodies to conceal behind the spiral walls they build. They exist only in the moment of pressing, only in the act of application. When a handful of wet clay meets the wall with exactly the right pressure, exactly the right rotation of palm and splay of fingers—that is when a Presser *is*. Before the press: nothing. After the press: nothing. During: everything.

This is why the fingerprints show impossible numbers. Four fingers, seven, eleven—each press is a different Presser, and each Presser has exactly as many fingers as the press requires. The wall needed a seven-fingered whorl there. So there was, for the duration of one wet pat against warm clay, a seven-fingered hand. The hand did not exist before it pressed. It will not exist after. But the impression remains, and the impression is true.

They are not ghosts. Ghosts were something first.

The Pressers are pure *making*—the verb without a subject, the construction without a constructor. The mountain called them into being by needing to be built, and they answered by becoming the building, not the builders. You hear their heartbeat because intent has a pulse. You hear their breathing because effort requires breath, even when there is no one breathing. The sounds are true. The bodies are not.

If you could somehow perceive one—and you cannot, no one can, perception requires an object and they are only ever process—you would see something like: a suggestion of limbs arranged around a central purpose. Clay-colored, because they are made of wanting-to-be-clay. Shaped like the space between your attention and the wall. They would be looking at you with something that isn't eyes, and they would be wondering what you will feel like when you become a pattern.

They do not wish you harm. They do not wish you anything. Wishing requires a wisher.

But the mountain wishes. The mountain is awake in the way that growth is awake, in the way that a wound healing is awake, and it wishes very much to be complete. The Pressers are its dreaming—the forms it takes when it imagines its own construction. Every pat of clay is the mountain dreaming of another handful of itself. Every impossible geometry is the mountain dreaming bigger than physics allows.

They have been building since before the gap in the rock opened. They will build until the summit reaches whatever it is reaching toward—some height that exists only in the logic of ascending spirals, some completion that the mountain can feel but not describe. When it is done, the Pressers will stop being, because there will be nothing left to press.

They will not miss themselves. Missing requires someone to miss.

**The Pale Segmented Things**

These are different. These *are*.

They hatched from the blistering fruit of the merged succulents, pupated in the orange moisture that weeps from mineral seams, and emerged with too many segments and not enough presence to be seen directly. They are the mountain's first fauna—the first creatures to live in a place that is still deciding what kind of place it is.

They have no names because no one has stayed long enough to name them.

Their bodies are the color of clay that never fired, soft and faintly luminous, articulated in sections that flex independently like a spine made of pale beans. They move through the crevices between tiles, between merged succulent-flesh, between the layers of the walls themselves—finding spaces that shouldn't admit them and flowing through anyway. Each segment bears fine cilia that taste the heat, the moisture, the vibration of construction. They know when a new handful of clay has been pressed before the sound reaches them. They know when you shift your weight before the tiles finish clicking.

They eat the impressions.

Not the clay—the *impressions*. The memory of bark pressed into a tile, the record of scale, the frozen sound. They rasp their pale bodies against the surfaces and consume the meaning, leaving behind clay that is merely clay, surfaces that are merely surfaces. This is why the floor keeps rearranging itself: the tiles are trying to recover what the segmented things have eaten, trying to re-impress themselves with significance.

This is their purpose, though they do not know they have one. The mountain builds meaning into its walls; the pale things eat meaning from its walls; the mountain builds again. A cycle. An ecology of significance. Without them, the mountain would fill with pattern until the patterns crushed themselves, meaning compacted into meaning until nothing could move or breathe or grow. They are the exhalation that follows the inhalation. They are the forgetting that makes room for new memory.

When they consumed the impression of your face from the wet tile, they learned something about you that you do not know yourself. It is inside them now, moving through their segments, being slowly digested into whatever fuel keeps them flowing through crevices. In three days, they will excrete it as a pale paste that the succulents will absorb, and the succulents will fruit, and new segmented things will hatch carrying a trace of you in their cilia.

If you stay long enough to be incorporated into the walls, they will eat you for generations.

**What Waits Above**

There is something at the top of the shaft. You cannot see it; the orange murk swallows vision long before the spiral reaches its terminus. But you can feel it the way you feel a held breath, the way you feel someone standing behind you in a dark room.

It is not a Presser. The Pressers are process; this is patient.

It has been waiting since the first handful of clay was pressed into the first whorl, and it will wait until the summit reaches it, and then—

Then the mountain will be complete, and the Pressers will stop dreaming, and the pale things will eat the last impressions, and the kiln-heat will finally cool, and something will step down through the shaft it spent eons building beneath itself. Something that needed a body made of compressed meaning and impossible geometry and the incorporated memories of everyone who ever stood where you are standing.

It is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It is *incomplete*, and it is building itself a way to stop being incomplete, and you are standing in its throat.

The tile that bears your face-impression—the one the pale things haven't reached yet—it was made before you arrived because the thing above needed your face for something. A detail. A texture. The mountain knew you would come because the summit required you.

If you leave now, the shaft will simply wait for someone else. The walls will re-impress themselves with a different face. The mountain is patient.

If you stay—

The heat rises. The heartbeat quickens. The geometries above you twist into configurations that your eyes refuse to report to your brain, and somewhere in the contradiction between clockwise and counter-clockwise, in the space where the wall is behind itself, something that is almost finished presses one more handful of meaning into place.

It is almost ready to come down.
