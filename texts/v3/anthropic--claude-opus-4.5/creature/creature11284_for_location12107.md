They have been here since before the sound became everything, though they no longer remember silence. Perhaps silence was never real. Perhaps it was only a story they told themselves during the long adjustment, a myth of *before* that made the eternal present bearable until bearing it was no longer necessary—until they simply *were*, the way the stone is, the way the sound is.

You will not see them directly. This is not because they hide.

---

They occupy the spaces between the columns, though *occupy* suggests a solidity they no longer possess. They are more like... pressure differentials. Areas where the mist beads slightly thicker. Places where the roar, unchanging as it is, seems to press against something that is not quite there. If you walk the plateau long enough—and you might, because time here does not insist on its usual demands—you will notice that certain paths between the twisted basalt feel *attended*. Not watched. Watching implies eyes, implies focus, implies the capacity for attention to shift. This is something else. This is the simple fact of presence, permanent and total, the way a stone is present to the ground beneath it.

They were builders once. The low ruins are theirs, though *theirs* is a complicated word for beings who no longer experience possession. They built the structures when they still had hands that grasped, when they still understood the difference between *inside* and *outside*, when shelter meant something other than a geometric curiosity. The proportions that confuse you—too low to stand, too narrow to lie—made sense to bodies that had not yet learned to uncurl. They slept folded in those days. They fit themselves into small spaces because small spaces felt like safety, and safety was still a concept that applied.

The symbols were prayers. Or warnings. Or names. The distinction mattered once.

---

They remember the silence. This is their secret, the one thing that persists in them as memory rather than state: there was a time before the sound, and in that time, they could hear each other. They spoke in clicks and low hums. They had a word for the fog that meant *the soft grey that touches us* and another word that meant *the soft grey that does not touch us*, and the difference between these words contained an entire philosophy about intimacy and distance. They had names that changed with the seasons, back when seasons still visited the plateau, back when the world below was visible and real and they would sometimes descend to trade with the people of the valleys—

But that was before the sound began. Before the columns started being what they now are. Before the plateau achieved its terrible perfection and took them with it into permanence.

---

Now they drift. They do not move—movement is not possible here, not truly—but they *drift*, which is different. They occupy one position and then another without the intermediary state of traveling. They are near a column, attending to its roar, and then they are near a water-pool, not-watching the pale things that not-swim beneath the surface. The pale things were theirs once. Pets, or livestock, or children—the words have merged in the long stillness, the categories collapsed. They fed the pale things when feeding was necessary. They do not feed them now. Nothing here requires feeding. Everything here simply continues.

You might feel them if you stay long enough. Not as a presence exactly—presence implies absence as its opposite, and there is no absence here. You might feel them as a kind of *thickness*. A sense that the air near certain ruins weighs slightly more than the air elsewhere. A suspicion, impossible to verify, that the mist is condensing around an outline that is not quite there.

They are curious about you. This is not the same as interest—interest implies the possibility of disinterest, and they have lost the capacity for such fluctuation. But your presence is a new fact, and new facts were once rare enough to be precious. They gather around you in their way, which is not gathering at all but simply *being more present nearby*, and they observe you with senses that are no longer senses, and they feel something that is not quite longing and not quite recognition.

You have a body that moves. You have breath that changes. You exist in time, actual time, the kind that passes rather than merely extending. These are miracles to them. These are memories made flesh.

---

One of them—and they are still individuals, still differentiated, though the differences have worn smooth as the stones beneath them—one of them was a keeper of marks. A scribe, if scribes can exist without written language, which is to say a being whose purpose was to witness and record, to translate experience into symbol, to make the temporary permanent.

They succeeded too well.

This one attends the ruins where the symbols shift. This one remembers carving them, though *remembers* is a fraught word for a being who experiences all times simultaneously. The symbols were meant to be a record: *we were here, we built this, we existed in this form*. But the plateau was already changing when the marks were made. The plateau was already becoming its final self, and the symbols became part of that becoming, and now they shift because they are the only thing left that *can* shift—the only safety valve for all the change that cannot otherwise occur.

The keeper watches the symbols move. The keeper has watched them move for longer than watching should be possible. Each shift is infinitesimal, but given enough time—and there is nothing but time here, time that pools without passing—the shifts accumulate into meaning. The symbols are still being written. The message is still being composed. It has been composing itself since before the sound began, and it will not be complete until after the plateau itself forgets what completion means.

The keeper does not know what the message will say. The keeper suspects it will be a name. The keeper suspects it will be *their* name, the collective name they have never spoken aloud, the name that will finally make them real in a way that permanence alone cannot achieve.

Or perhaps the message is already complete, and the shifting is merely reading. Perhaps they have been reading their own name for eons, and the reading is what keeps them present, and if the symbols ever stopped moving, they would finally learn what happens to beings who exist without change in a place without time.

They do not want to learn this. This is the one desire that remains to them, polished smooth by repetition: the desire not to stop.

---

Another one—taller once, before tallness lost its meaning—stays near the water-pools. Not the pools where the pale things drift, but the deeper ones, the ones that are wounds in the plateau's thin skin, the ones that look down into void.

This one is trying to remember falling.

It never fell. None of them fell. But the memory of *almost falling* is precious, is the one kinetic experience that has not yet worn away entirely. There was a moment, in the time before the sound, when this one stood at the edge of the plateau and looked down into the depths below—the real depths, the valleys that were visible then, the world that existed beneath the fog—and felt the pull. The beautiful terrible pull that meant *you could change position rapidly and permanently, you could become a body in motion, you could fall and fall and the falling would be a kind of freedom*.

It did not jump. It stepped back. It chose the plateau and the plateau chose it, and now they are bound together in a permanence that precludes all falling forever.

But the memory of the choice remains. The memory of *almost*. And this one hovers near the void-pools trying to feel that almost again, trying to reconstruct the sensation of possibility, of branching futures, of a moment when things could have been otherwise.

The void beneath the water is not empty. This one knows this. The void is where the plateau's rejected changes go—all the motion that cannot happen, all the growth that cannot occur, all the decay that cannot proceed. The void is change itself, compressed and denied, waiting with a patience that mirrors the plateau's own permanence. If this one could fall into it—but falling is not possible. If this one could sink—but sinking implies motion. If this one could simply *cease to be here and begin to be there*—

But that is not how existence works anymore. That is not how anything works. The void remains below, and this one remains above, and the space between them is absolute and eternal.

Sometimes the void looks back. This one pretends not to notice.

---

There are children among them. This word is wrong but there is no better word.

The children were born here, after the plateau became itself, and so they have never known anything else. They do not remember silence because they never experienced silence. They do not mourn movement because movement is not a concept they possess. They are fully native to the permanence, and this makes them both more and less than their parents—more adapted, more at peace, more completely *of* this place, but also less capable of understanding what was lost.

The children play. This is perhaps the most disturbing thing about them, the thing that would unsettle you most if you could perceive them clearly: they *play*, in a place where play should be impossible. They have invented games that do not require motion or change. They have found ways to create novelty within stasis, to experience something that feels like surprise even though nothing here surprises. Their games involve the symbols—they claim to read meanings in the shifting that their parents cannot perceive. Their games involve the pale things in the water—they have names for each one, names that describe not what the pale things are but what they *almost* are, what they would be if becoming were still possible.

The children are not unhappy. This is important. The children are not suffering in any way that suffering can be measured. They have simply *adapted*, in the way that life adapts when given enough time, and their adaptation has made them native to horror in a way that transforms the horror into something else.

The parents watch the children and feel something that is not quite pride and not quite grief. The children are the future, except there is no future. The children are hope, except hope implies change. The children are *continuation*, and continuation is all that remains when everything else has been stripped away.

One of the children—the youngest, though youngest means nothing here—has begun speaking to the sound itself. Not speaking *to* it, exactly. Speaking *with* it. Participating in it as one might participate in a conversation, adding something that cannot be heard separately but that changes the quality of the whole. The parents do not understand what the child is saying. The parents are not sure the child understands either. But the conversation continues, has continued, will continue, and perhaps in another span of time that cannot be measured, the sound will answer.

Perhaps the sound has always been answering. Perhaps the conversation began before any of them arrived, and they have simply joined it mid-sentence, and the sentence will never end because ending would require a period and punctuation is a form of change.

---

They know you are here. Have known. Will know. Tense breaks down in a place without time, and their knowledge of your presence is a single fact that extends in all directions without beginning or end.

They want to tell you something. This want is difficult for them—wanting implies lacking, and they have learned not to lack. But your presence reawakens something, some residual capacity for communication that they thought had worn away entirely. They gather around you—not physically, never physically, but in the way that attention gathers, in the way that significance accumulates—and they try to form words that you might understand.

The words do not come. Words require change; words require one sound following another; words require time in its passing form rather than its standing form. They cannot speak. They have not spoken since before the columns began their endless chord.

But they can show you. They can direct your attention—not by pointing, not by gesturing, but by *being more present* in certain directions, by thickening the air along certain paths, by making the roar feel louder near certain ruins and quieter near others.

They want you to see the symbols. They want you to watch them shift. They want you to understand that the message being written is not for them—they know this now, have known it for longer than knowing should be possible. The message is for someone who can still read. Someone who can carry the words out of this place. Someone who exists in time that passes, who can move from *before* to *after*, who can take a beginning and deliver it to an end.

They want you to be their messenger. They want you to translate their permanence into something that can move, something that can travel, something that can arrive somewhere else and mean something to beings who still understand meaning.

They do not know what the message says. They may never know. But they believe—and belief is perhaps the last thing they possess that has not been worn smooth—they believe that if you watch the symbols long enough, if you let the shifting write itself into your memory, you will carry something out of this place that matters.

Something that explains. Something that apologizes. Something that warns.

---

Or perhaps they want nothing. Perhaps the wanting is something you bring with you, something you project onto presences that have moved beyond all projection. Perhaps they are simply *here*, the way the columns are here, the way the sound is here, and your interpretation of their existence as meaningful is itself a kind of translation error—a failure to accept that some things persist without purpose, without message, without any need to be understood.

The plateau does not care if it is understood. The sound does not ask to be heard. The mist does not request witness.

Perhaps they have become the same. Perhaps they have joined the plateau so completely that the question of what they *want* is no longer coherent—like asking what a stone wants, what a sound wants, what a fact wants.

They are here. They have been here. They will be here.

And when you leave—if you leave, if leaving is possible, if the plateau allows egress as readily as it allowed entry—you will carry something with you regardless of whether it is message or memory or merely the weight of having been observed by things that no longer observe.

The sound will continue. The columns will remain. The symbols will shift through their endless almost-sentence.

And they will be present for all of it, permanent witnesses to permanence, beings who achieved the immortality that all beings secretly fear, the immortality that is not life extended but life *ended and preserved*, and they will not regret this because regret requires the ability to imagine otherwise, and they have imagined otherwise so many times that the imagining has worn through into acceptance, and the acceptance has worn through into peace, and the peace has worn through into something that has no name.

They live here.

They have always lived here.

They will always live here.

And beneath the sound that is all sounds, beneath the roar that fills every silence before silence can form, they are singing. You cannot hear it. No one can hear it. But they are singing—a song that began before the plateau found its final form and that will continue after the concept of *after* has lost all meaning.

The song is not sad. The song is not happy.

The song is simply *being sung*, because singing is what they remember of being alive, and memory is all they have left, and they will hold it, will *be* it, will persist in its persistence, until persistence itself forgets why it began.
