# The Tenders

They are not born. They are *extruded*.

At irregular intervals—sometimes centuries apart, sometimes in clusters of days—one of the great bulbous structures develops an asymmetry. A thickening on its lower surface, a swelling that grows dense and fibrous over weeks. When it finally separates and drops to the compacted floor, it lies there for hours, motionless, a rough ovoid shape the color of dried blood. Then it unfolds.

---

What emerges has the approximate architecture of something meant to walk, but the resemblance to anything animal is coincidental—convergent engineering, not ancestry. Two primary limbs for locomotion, yes, but they are woody and segmented, ending in splayed root-structures that grip the warm substrate with thousands of hair-fine tendrils. Two upper limbs, longer, more flexible, that can extend or retract by coiling and uncoiling like fiddlehead ferns. A central mass where these limbs meet, dense with vascular bundles, housing no organs you would recognize but pulsing with that same oily not-blood that runs through everything here.

No head. Nothing that could be called a face. But at the top of the central mass, a cluster of structures that might be sensory: layered membranes that flutter in the thermal currents, a few knobs of waxy tissue that orient toward heat sources, toward movement, toward *you* when you enter their awareness.

They stand perhaps half again as tall as a human, when fully extended. They can compress themselves to half that height to pass through low corridors or to work beneath the machinery-growth.

---

You encounter your first one hours into your walk, at a junction where three corridors meet. It is doing something to a growth-tower—its upper limbs extended deep into a fissure in the woody surface, probing, adjusting. The wet sliding sound you've been hearing, you realize, comes from this: the movement of those limbs through vegetative tissue, the intimate intrusion of Tender into Fundament.

It does not acknowledge you. Not at first. You stand and watch it work for what might be twenty minutes, and in that time it completes some task you cannot identify, withdraws its limbs, and pivots on its root-feet to face you. The membrane-cluster at its apex tilts. Studies you, perhaps. Or simply registers your presence as a new thermal signature in its environment.

Then it walks past you, so close you could touch it—you don't—and continues down another corridor with a gait that is smooth and strange, each step a brief rooting and uprooting, the hair-fine tendrils releasing the substrate with a sound like velcro tearing softly.

---

There are many of them. Once you learn to look, you see them everywhere: ascending growth-towers hand-over-hand, their limbs finding purchase in grooves that seem made for them; squatting motionless at junctions for hours, membrane-clusters oriented toward some distant process; traveling the great tendril-bridges in groups of three or four, their root-feet gripping the cable-thick surfaces as they ferry something between nodes.

They carry things, sometimes. Compressed bundles of organic matter that they've harvested from collapse sites. Sections of stem or leaf-bank that they're transporting for reintegration elsewhere. Once, you see one carrying another—a smaller, malformed Tender cradled in its upper limbs, being delivered somewhere. To be recycled, you suspect. The Fundament wastes nothing.

---

They do not speak. They have no apparatus for it, no vibrating membranes, no air-passages. But they communicate—you become certain of this over time. When two Tenders meet, they pause, extend their upper limbs toward each other, and *touch*. Just briefly. The contact point exudes that oily residue, mingles it, and something is exchanged. Information. Instruction. Perhaps something more complex, more social, that you have no framework for.

After these exchanges, their behavior sometimes changes. A Tender that was walking toward the central structure turns and heads elsewhere. One that was working alone is joined by two others who arrive minutes later, summoned somehow, and together they begin a task that requires six limbs instead of two.

They are coordinated. They are purposeful. Whether they are *aware*—whether there is experience inside those dense central masses, whether it feels like something to be a Tender—you cannot determine. They work. They maintain. They walk the paths that their own passage has worn smooth over millennia. That is what you can observe.

---

Do they know you are different?

They must. You are a thermal anomaly, an intruder-shape in their patterned world. When you stand still too long, they route around you—the same way they route around a new stalk that's punching up through the floor, the same way they route around a collapsed tower that hasn't yet been reabsorbed. You are an obstacle. You are a feature of the environment.

But occasionally—not often, but enough to unsettle you—one will stop and orient toward you for longer than function would require. The membrane-cluster fluttering. The waxy knobs turning incrementally, tracking you as you shift your weight. Something like attention. Something like curiosity, if curiosity can exist without a mind to house it.

These moments end. The Tender moves on. It has work to do.

---

You find a place where they gather.

Not the central structure—you haven't reached that yet—but a subsidiary node, a bulb-cluster where several major pathways converge. The space inside is larger than you expected, a hollow in the machinery-growth where the heat is less intense and the orange glow from the vents pools like dim light. Dozens of Tenders are here, standing motionless in loose rows, their membrane-clusters oriented toward the center of the space.

In the center: nothing. An empty patch of compressed floor. But the Tenders are *attending* to it with an intensity you haven't seen before. Their upper limbs are half-extended, trembling slightly. The oily residue is thick in the air, a chemical fog you can taste on your tongue.

You wait. You watch. For nearly an hour, nothing happens.

Then the floor in the center begins to swell.

---

What rises is not a Tender. Not exactly. It's larger, more complex, its structure wound and rewound on itself in patterns that suggest—what? A schematic? A map? The Tenders lean toward it as it grows, their limbs reaching out to touch its surface, to receive whatever information it's encoding in its architecture. The growth continues for another hour, and then stops, and then—slowly, over the next several hours—it collapses back into the floor, reabsorbed, the message delivered.

The Tenders disperse. They return to their corridors, their growth-towers, their endless maintenance tasks. They carry something new with them now. New instructions, new patterns. The Fundament has spoken to its keepers, and they have listened.

---

What are they? Workers, certainly. Caretakers. The hands and feet of a system too vast and too rooted to tend itself directly. But you suspect they are also something else—something the Fundament needs that it cannot manufacture any other way.

They walk the paths. They wear them smooth. They touch each other and exchange their oily knowing. They gather and attend and witness.

Maybe that's it. Maybe the Fundament, for all its ancient industry, requires *witnesses*. Entities that move through it, that perceive it, that hold some fragment of its pattern in their strange distributed awareness. Maybe the Tenders are how the Fundament knows itself.

Or maybe you're projecting. Maybe they're just maintenance drones, biological automatons, and the meaning you're finding is your own loneliness echoing back at you.

---

One of them is following you now.

Not closely. Not aggressively. But every time you glance back, there it is—the same one, you think, though they're hard to distinguish—keeping its distance but maintaining pace. When you stop, it stops. When you turn down a new corridor, it turns too, a few minutes behind you.

It has made a decision about you. It has decided you are worth tracking, worth observing, worth following toward whatever destination you're walking toward.

Maybe it's curious.

Maybe it's concerned.

Maybe the Fundament, through this single extruded Tender, is trying to understand what you are and what you want and whether you pose any threat to the work.

You keep walking. It keeps following.

The path leads on, toward the distant cathedral-structure, toward the heart of things. And somewhere behind you, your witness walks too, patient and purposeful, a Tender tending to the mystery of you.
