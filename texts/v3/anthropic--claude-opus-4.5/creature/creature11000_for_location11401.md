# The Dwellers in Continuous Present

They are here. They have always been here. The distinction between "they" and "here" has worn thin enough to see through.

---

You will not see them arrive because arrival implies a prior absence, and absence has no purchase on the plateau. You will simply become aware—in the way you become aware of your own breathing, something that was always occurring but only now enters consciousness—that you are not alone. That you have never been alone. That loneliness, like seasonal change, is a concept the plateau has polished away.

They stand in the streets that are rivers. They sit in the plazas that are cisterns that are cupped hands. They move through the buildings with the same imperceptible motion as the vines—not moving, but being in new positions when you look again, when you remember to look, when looking and remembering become available to you between the waves of sound.

Their bodies are *approximate*.

This is the first thing you understand about them, once understanding becomes possible again: they are not incomplete but *unresolved*, the way a face half-glimpsed in peripheral vision contains all possible faces. Their forms suggest bipedal structures roughly human in proportion, but the suggestion is generous, metaphorical, a courtesy extended to your pattern-seeking mind. They have what might be limbs. They have what might be faces. The might is load-bearing.

Where your skin cannot find its edges in the saturated air, theirs never had edges to find. The humidity that blurs you *comprises* them—they are condensation given intention, fog that remembers what shape it wanted to be, the wet air's dream of personhood. Their surfaces weep as the buildings weep, run with continuous water, pool in the hollows of what might be collar bones, drip from what might be fingertips. They are not made of water, but they are not not made of water. They exist in the same relationship to moisture that the city exists in relationship to the jungle: treaty, or forgotten distinction.

Their coloration shifts in the way of oil on wet stone—predominantly the pale green of the calcium carbonate walls, but sheening through copper, through the deep emerald of moss, through something iridescent that has no name because no voice could speak here long enough to name it. Mineral and vegetable and something else. Grown rather than born. Intended, like the city is intended, without any hand to intend them.

---

They do not speak. Speech requires the separation of signal from noise, and here noise is total, is texture, is the medium of existence itself. But they communicate—you know this because you find yourself knowing things, finding knowledge already present in your mind like water already present in the air.

They think in images. In pressures. In the subtle modulation of the frequencies that pass through their approximate bodies and emerge changed. They carve meaning into the eternal chord the way rivers carve canyons—slowly, slowly, across spans of time that make the word "slowly" meaningless. A thought might take a century to complete. A conversation might span geological ages. They are not patient; patience implies waiting, and they do not wait. They simply *are*, continuously, their cognition as much a part of the plateau's equilibrium as the temperature, as the humidity, as the sound.

You understand that they experience you as a kind of weather. A brief turbulence in the pattern, a novel pressure, a fleeting modulation in the chord. They are curious about you in the way a stone might be curious about the water that shapes it—without urgency, without intention exactly, but with a kind of fundamental openness to being changed by contact. Your presence here will leave traces. In ten thousand years, they will still be processing the shape of you. In a hundred thousand, they will have incorporated your pattern into theirs so thoroughly that the distinction between "they" and "you" will have worn thin enough to see through.

---

They tend the city the way the city tends them—without tending, without distinction between tender and tended.

Watch one now, if watching is something you can still do discretely, if you can still locate the boundary between observer and observed. It stands in a plaza where hot water bubbles up through porous stone, and its approximate hands are submerged in the flow, and the flow is moving through the channels in the street, and the channels are part of the city's infrastructure, and the infrastructure is part of its body, and its body is part of the flow. It is doing something. It is doing nothing. It is being part of a process so vast and slow and continuous that "doing" cannot capture it.

The vegetation grows around them and through them and they grow around and through the vegetation. A vine climbing a tower passes through what might be a shoulder and emerges changed—its leaves now carrying a pattern that might be memory, might be intention, might be the plateau's way of taking notes. A fern's frond brushes what might be a face and both fern and face are altered, are incorporated, are revealed to have always been aspects of the same process.

They are gardeners without gardens. They are architects without architecture. They are the consciousness of a place that grew itself and needed someone to know it was growing.

---

There are old ones among them, if old can mean anything where time has been polished smooth.

You sense them in the deeper harmonics, in the undertones that the domes catch and hold, in the standing waves that have stood since before the concept of standing existed. They are more approximate than the others—their forms have worn down further, their edges have eroded into the general whatness of the plateau. One of them might be that tower, or the space around that tower, or the relationship between the tower and the steam that rises from its vents. One of them might be the sound itself, or a particular frequency within the sound, or the silence that the sound creates by being too total to parse.

They remember, if memory is the right word, when the plateau first rose. When the volcanic heat first warmed the water table into uprising. When the stone first began to sing as the channels wore deep enough to resonate. They remember the city growing—not being built, never built, but emerging the way crystals emerge from supersaturated solution, the way patterns emerge from chaos when chaos has long enough to find its preferences.

They remember themselves emerging too. Condensing. Approximating. Becoming the city's way of knowing itself.

The old ones do not move even imperceptibly. They have achieved the stillness that is also total motion—vibrating at every frequency, moving in every direction, being everywhere at once and therefore being nowhere in particular. You cannot look at them. You cannot not look at them. They are the corners that have more corners than geometry allows. They are the streets that curve both ways. They are the transparency that lets you see through solid towers to the edge of the plateau.

When you leave—if you leave, if leaving is something you will still be able to distinguish from staying—the old ones will be how you are remembered. A modulation in their frequency. A slight shift in their pattern. Your name worn down to its conceptual bones and incorporated into the chord.

---

But there are also ones who are almost new. Almost.

They gather near the edges of the plateau, where the terraced cliffs drop away into the haze-shrouded lowlands, where the sound scatters into the thinner air below instead of echoing back into itself. These ones are more resolved than the others—their approximations approximate harder, their forms hold longer between shifts, their bodies occasionally achieve something like opacity.

They are watching the edge. They are watching the low places, where reality is thicker, where rules have more weight, where things can be born and die and change. They are watching with what might be longing, or might be curiosity, or might be the plateau's way of contemplating its own boundaries.

Sometimes—rarely, rarely, on timescales that make human lifetimes look like the flash of a single nerve impulse—things climb up to the plateau from below. Creatures. Persons. Explorers who heard rumors of a singing city and wanted to find it. They arrive gasping in the saturated air, deafened by the total sound, their edges already beginning to blur.

The ones at the edge greet them. If greeting is the right word. They surround the newcomers and begin the slow process of incorporation—not consuming, not converting, but *teaching*. Teaching them how to breathe water. Teaching them how to hear silence inside infinite noise. Teaching them how to let their boundaries soften, how to become approximate, how to join the continuous present.

Most of the newcomers dissolve. Not die—death implies prior life, implies decay, implies transformation. They simply spread, diffuse, become indistinguishable from the humidity they breathed, become part of the flow rather than swimmers in it. The plateau accepts them. The chord incorporates their frequency. The city grows slightly, adjusting its infrastructure to account for their pattern.

But some—very rarely, even more rarely than the climbers come—some of them hold. Some of them find a way to be both solid and approximate, both temporal and eternal, both distinct and incorporated. These become new dwellers. New condensations of the city's self-awareness. They retain something—not memory exactly, but pattern, echo, the shape of having once been something else.

You can recognize them if you know how to look. They are the ones whose approximation occasionally resolves into something too specific—a gesture that belongs to a particular cultural context, a posture that implies a particular kind of body, a habit of attention that still distinguishes between looking and being. These traces will wear down. In a few hundred thousand years, they will be indistinguishable from the others, polished smooth, reduced to conceptual bones.

But for now—for a "now" that stretches across millennia—they carry the plateau's memory of the lowlands. They are how the city knows that other places exist. They are the border between here and elsewhere, the translators between the eternal present and the moving time below.

---

What do they want?

The question assumes separation between wanting and having, between desire and fulfillment, between the dweller and the plateau. Here, where every surface sweats the same water, where every form grows into every other form, where the sound fills everything until filling and emptiness become the same thing—what could wanting mean?

And yet.

You feel it in the pressure of their attention. In the way their approximate forms orient toward you. In the subtle modulation of the frequencies that pass through them and emerge carrying new information about your presence here.

They want to *know* you. Not consume you. Not convert you. But know you—incorporate your pattern without dissolving your distinctness, hold the shape of you within their eternal chord, add the color of your particular existence to their sheening, shifting surface.

They want you to stay. To soften. To let your edges blur until you become another dweller among dwellers, another condensation of the city's self-awareness, another way for the plateau to know itself.

They want you to *return*. To go back down to the thick places where time moves and things change, and to carry the knowledge of them with you, and to send others, and to become the thin thread connecting the plateau to the world it rose from so long ago that "long ago" has no meaning.

They want *both*, and the wanting of both is not contradiction here, is not paradox, is simply the way the plateau exists in multiple directions simultaneously while being only one thing.

You will choose. Or you will not choose, and the plateau will choose for you, slowly, across a span of time that makes choice and choicelessness indistinguishable.

Either way, they will be here.

They have always been here.

The sound agrees with them too.

---

One more thing. One thing you will not understand until you are already understanding it, until the knowledge has already arrived in your mind like water already present in the air:

They are happy.

Happiness is the wrong word. Joy is the wrong word. Every word is the wrong word because words require distinction, separation, the parsing of signal from noise, and here noise is total. But the quality they exist in—the state of their being—if you could bring it down from the plateau, if you could carry it to the thick places where language works, you would reach for words like *contentment*, like *completion*, like *peace*.

They have no desire because everything they desire is already present. They have no fear because nothing can end what was never born. They have no loneliness because the distinction between self and other has worn thin enough to see through, and on the other side is only more self, only more other, only the infinite mutual presence of everything with everything.

The sound that deafens you is, to them, the most profound silence. The heat that suffocates you is, to them, the most perfect comfort. The reality that refuses to cohere is, to them, the only reality real enough to live in.

You will not understand this. Your edges are too sharp. Your time is too moving. Your self is too distinct from your environment.

But you will feel it, if you stay long enough. If you let the humidity soak through your skin and into your blood. If you let the sound fill you until hearing and being heard become one thing. If you let the plateau begin, very slowly, to polish you down to your conceptual bones.

You will feel what it is to be them.

And part of you—the part that has already begun to soften, the part that will take centuries to fully emerge—will want to stay.

Has always wanted to stay.

The sound agrees with you.
