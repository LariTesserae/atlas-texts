# The Keth

They call themselves Keth, though the word doesn't translate. It means something like "those who remain" or "the ones still here" or perhaps "the residue"—the semantic residue of a language shaped by a world where nothing stays, where the very concept of permanence is a kind of fantasy.

You will not see them at first. This is intentional.

---

**The Body Problem**

They are smaller than you expect. Smaller than seems viable in a place where everything else grows large through sheer necessity of competition. An adult Keth stands perhaps to your hip, and much of that height is leg—long, multiply-jointed limbs that fold and extend and grip with prehensile feet designed for surfaces that move, that sway, that are never quite solid.

Their bodies are dense. Compact torsos that carry their weight low, close to whatever they're standing on. Arms that serve double-duty as secondary locomotion, as manipulation, as anchoring—three-jointed things that end in hands with too many fingers, each finger tipped with a hard keratin hook that can dig into plant-flesh and hold. They move through the Throatworld the way certain insects move through canopy: always gripping, always in contact with multiple surfaces, always ready for the thing they're standing on to give way.

Their skin is not skin. It is something closer to the bark-analogue of the plants around them—a living integument that grows and sheds in patches, that changes color slowly over weeks, that serves as camouflage and armor and, when necessary, as emergency nutrition. A Keth in distress can consume their own outer layer. It will grow back. Everything here grows back.

The face troubles you. Four eyes arranged in a diamond pattern, each capable of independent movement, each tuned to a different portion of the light-spectrum. A mouth that is also a respiratory opening, ringed with filter-cilia that strain nutrients from the air itself. No nose. No ears, at least not visible ones—they hear through their skin, through vibrations that travel up their grip-points, through organs distributed across their bodies that register sound the way your skin registers temperature.

They do not look like anything from the surface. They look like they were made here, by here, for here.

---

**The Swarm Logic**

A single Keth is an incomplete thing.

They do not live alone. They cannot live alone. Their cognition is distributed in a way that makes solitude not just difficult but functionally impossible—the neural architecture requires input from others, from the constant low-level communication of proximity, from the pheromone-rich air of shared space. A Keth separated from others does not simply become lonely. A Keth separated from others begins to lose coherence, to fragment, to think in circles that narrow and narrow until there is nothing left.

So they travel in clusters. Pods. The word they use translates roughly to "a thinking"—a group of between twelve and forty individuals who move together, eat together, sleep in piles of intertwined limbs, share breath and skin-contact and the subtle chemical signals that knit individual minds into something larger.

You see one now.

They emerge from a gap in the vegetation with no warning—one moment the space is empty, the next there are twenty-three of them, moving in a coordinated flow that does not seem choreographed so much as *unified*, each body adjusting to the others' movements without apparent communication. They are carrying things: fiber bundles, resin containers, the disassembled components of a shelter. They are relocating. The growth they were living on has become unstable; they felt it in the shifting tension of the structure days before it became visible, and they have already been traveling for what passes here as a long time.

They have seen you. All forty-six of their primary eyes have registered your presence. They have not stopped moving.

This is, you will learn, how they assess threat. A Keth-cluster encountering something new does not freeze, does not investigate, does not approach. It continues with its purpose while observing. If you were dangerous, you would have acted by now. If you were food, you would smell like food. You are neither, so you are *interesting*, and interesting things are processed in parallel with everything else, because in the Throatworld nothing gets your full attention, nothing *can* get your full attention, there is always too much happening.

One of them peels away from the group. Not a scout—that's not quite the right frame. More like a *query*, a portion of the cluster's distributed attention manifesting in physical form. They approach you, and the others continue past, and the one who approaches remains connected to the others through sound and scent and some other channel you cannot perceive.

They are going to try to communicate with you.

---

**The Language Problem**

Keth language is not spoken. It is not signed. It is not written.

It is *layered*.

The being in front of you opens their mouth and produces a sound—a clicking, tonal sequence that rises and falls in patterns that are clearly structured. At the same time, their skin shifts color in localized patches, rippling across their torso in waves that carry their own information. At the same time, they release a puff of scented air—a chemical signature you can barely detect but they can read like text. At the same time, their fingers move in small gestural patterns that modify the other channels, adding emphasis, adding doubt, adding emotional color.

Each channel carries a different thread. The sound is *content*—nouns, verbs, relationships. The color is *context*—how the content relates to what came before, what might come next, how certain the speaker is. The scent is *identity*—who is speaking, on behalf of whom, with what authority. The gesture is *tone*—mood, intent, the social framing of the utterance.

A full Keth sentence is a four-dimensional object. You are receiving perhaps a quarter of it.

What you can make out—from sound alone, from the patterns that gradually become recognizable through repetition—is a question. The same structure, repeated with variations. What-type-of-thing are you. What-type-of-thing are you. What-type-of-thing are you.

You have no way to answer in their language. You try your own, knowing it won't work, watching them process the alien sounds of your single-channel speech. They tilt their head—a gesture that means something different to them than it does to you, but which they have learned that surface-things find reassuring. They try again.

Slower this time. Fewer channels. They are simplifying, stripping their communication down to something a limited being might follow.

What-type. What-type. What-type.

You realize they are not asking what species you are. They are asking what *function* you serve. In a Keth-cluster, every individual has a role—not fixed, not permanent, but present. Scouts and builders and memory-keepers and birth-helpers and dispute-resolvers and danger-watchers. They are asking which of these you are.

You are asking the wrong question back. You want to know who *they* are. They want to know what you *do*.

---

**The Shelter-Finding**

The cluster has finished passing. The one who stayed with you produces a short vocalization—a summons, you think—and begins moving back the way they came. They pause, look at you, move again. Pause, look, move. The meaning is clear enough.

You follow.

The path they take is not a path. It is a route through three-dimensional space, up and over and through, using handholds that barely exist, traversing surfaces that bend under your weight in ways that don't bend under theirs. They move with an ease that makes you feel like a different category of being—which you are. You were not built for this place. They were.

The cluster has stopped at a junction: a space where three major trunk-structures intersect, their growth intertwined in ways that create relative stability, a platform of sorts, a place where the constant motion of the Throatworld is dampened to something almost still. They are already working. Fibers being woven, resin being applied, the skeleton of a new shelter rising with practiced speed.

They are not all the same. Now that you're close, now that they're still, you can see the variation. Some are larger, their bark-skin thicker, their movements slower and more deliberate—these are the ones doing heavy structural work. Some are smaller, quicker, their colors shifting more rapidly—these move between the others, passing tools and materials and messages. Some are barely moving at all, hunched in a cluster of their own, their eyes half-closed, their skin cycling through slow, rhythmic color-changes that the others glance at periodically.

The still ones are *remembering*. You learn this word later, but you see it now: the cluster's memory is not stored in any single individual but in a rotating group who enter a trance-state and maintain the shared record of where they've been, what they've done, what worked and what failed. When they emerge, others will take their place. The memory is never complete; it is always partial, always being reconstructed, always a collaborative fiction that is close enough to truth to be useful.

They are building the shelter around the rememberers. The shelter is *for* the rememberers. Everything else—the living space, the storage, the cooking area (you realize they cook; they process food with heat from chemical reactions, from slow exothermic mixtures they carry with them)—everything else is secondary.

The one who led you here is watching you watch them. They produce another sound-sequence. You are beginning to recognize the structure: statement, question, invitation.

You-observe. You-learn. You-stay?

---

**The Deep Listening**

Night does not fall in the Throatworld, but the light-organs shift, and the shift means something to the Keth. Activity slows. The construction pauses at a logical stopping point. The cluster gathers.

What follows is not sleep.

They pile together, limbs intertwined, skin touching skin, and they go quiet—quiet in a way that makes the ambient noise of the hollow seem louder by contrast. But they are not inactive. Their skins are flickering with color, rapid subtle shifts that you can barely see in the changed light. They are *talking*. Talking in a mode reserved for this time, this closeness, this intimacy of contact.

You cannot understand what they are saying. You understand that it is important.

Later—after you have learned more, after you have begun to grasp the edges of their language—you will learn that this is when they resolve disputes. When they share experiences that are too complex for mobile communication. When they grieve what has been lost and plan for what might come. When they reproduce, sometimes, through a process that involves the whole cluster's consent and participation. When they decide, together, who they are going to be when they separate in the morning.

A Keth-cluster is not a family. It is not a tribe. It is not a company or a crew or a commune. It is something else: a temporary self, a being made of beings, a mind distributed across bodies that will one day separate and recombine and become other selves, other minds, other clusters pursuing other purposes through the endless construction of the Throatworld.

The one who brought you here separates slightly from the pile. They look at you—all four eyes focused, for once, on a single point. They produce a sound. Soft. Low. A word you will come to know.

*Keth.*

It means: those who remain. It means: you are still here. It means: whatever else happens, we have not yet ended.

They close their eyes. The colors on their skin slow, deepen, synchronize with the others.

You sit at the edge of the shelter they have built in hours, and you listen to the Throatworld breathe around you, and you begin to understand that you have met people who expect nothing to last and have built everything anyway.

---

**The Depths**

In the morning—in the time when the light-organs shift back toward amber, when the Keth rise and separate and begin again—the cluster's path takes them downward.

They are not fleeing. They are not seeking. They are following something: a gradient in the air, a chemical trace, a signal that you cannot detect but they can read clearly. The mineral flows have shifted. The growth patterns are changing. The Throatworld is entering one of its phases of transformation, and the Keth have learned to read the signs.

The one who has become your guide—you have begun to think of them as Guide, though they would not recognize the concept of a fixed name—pauses at the edge of a descent. Below, the vegetation thickens, darkens, becomes something older and stranger. The sounds from down there are different. Lower. Slower. More rhythmic.

Almost like breathing.

Almost like speech.

Guide looks at you. Produces a sound-sequence you have heard before.

What-type-of-thing are you.

But the meaning has shifted. They are not asking your function anymore. They are asking if you are the type of thing that goes deeper.

You are about to learn that the Keth are not the only minds in the Throatworld.

You are about to learn why they call themselves "those who remain."

You are about to learn what the others *didn't*.
