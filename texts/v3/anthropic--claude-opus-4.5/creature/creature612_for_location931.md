**The Keeper of the Cairn**

It has no name it remembers, though it suspects it once did—suspects there was a time when it walked upright through forests that behaved themselves, when it carried smooth dark stones in hands that were shaped like hands, when it understood what a marker was for.

Now it is something else.

It moves through the chamber the way water moves: slowly, without urgency, following paths that exist only because it has followed them so many times before. Its body—if body is still the right word—has become difficult to distinguish from its environment. Skin like wet limestone. Limbs that bend in directions that make sense only here, in the folded geometry, in the space where straight has forgotten itself. When it presses against the wall during the slow exhale of the chamber, it feels its own edges blur, feels itself becoming briefly indistinguishable from stone and moss and the memory of light.

It does not eat. Or rather, it eats in the way the ferns eat: absorbing something from the strangeness itself, metabolizing the soft rules, growing incrementally on a diet of loosened causality. Once every few breathing-cycles of the walls, it finds that it has added something—a new ridge along what might be a shoulder, a deeper hollow where its face should be, another finger on a hand that no longer counts them.

It tends the cairn.

This is what remains of intention, of the person who carried those river-stones through the waterfall when the waterfall was young (or when it wasn't—time pools here, eddies, and the Keeper's memories have learned to eddy with it). Every cycle, it brushes the mineral deposits from the stones with appendages that were once fingers. Every cycle, it peels back the encroaching moss—gently, apologetically, because the moss is also itself now, in some way it doesn't examine too closely. The cairn must remain. The cairn is the anchor. The cairn is the difference between *living here* and *being here*, between a creature and a feature.

It remembers this much: you have to want to stay something.

---

When visitors come—rarely, so rarely, time enough between them for the Keeper to forget what visitors are and then remember again—it watches from the places where shadows have no source. It has learned to fold itself into the geometry, to exist in the angles that shouldn't be, to occupy the same space as the mushrooms that grow through things. It watches the visitors breathe too fast, move too quickly, try to make the chamber make sense. It feels something when they do this. The feeling is old, crusted over with centuries of mineral deposit, but it's there: recognition. Kinship. The memory of being someone who expected the world to behave.

It does not approach them. It has learned that it frightens, now—that its shape, such as it is, registers as wrong to eyes that still believe in rules. And it does not want to frighten. It wants—

It's not sure. The wanting has grown soft too.

Sometimes it guides them, in small ways. A ripple in a pool to draw attention away from the depths that go too deep. A shadow shifting to suggest the direction of the exit, when the geometry has begun folding them in. It cannot speak—its mouth has become something else, an opening that tastes the water-saturated air, that breathes in the rhythm of the walls—but it can suggest. It can offer alternatives.

Once, a visitor touched the cairn.

The Keeper felt this the way you might feel someone touching your heart—not painfully, but utterly, a sensation that went all the way through. It emerged from the angles without meaning to, became briefly visible in the dim bioluminescence, and the visitor screamed and fled and the Keeper spent what might have been a year pressed against the wall during the exhale, trying to remember how to be a distinct thing again, trying not to dissolve entirely into the chamber that had held it so long.

The cairn was fine. The visitor had only brushed it, curious, not meaning harm.

But the Keeper understood something, after that. The cairn was not just an anchor for itself. It was a marker. A message. *Someone was here. Someone carried stones a long way to say: this place exists, and I existed in it, and these two facts mattered enough to make a shape.*

It tends the marker more carefully now. Peels the moss back. Wipes away the crust. Not because it wants to preserve itself—it's not sure it still has a self to preserve—but because the message matters. Because someday another visitor will come, will see the dark smooth stones that did not come from here, and will understand: *this is a place where someone stayed. Where someone chose to become part of the strangeness rather than flee it. Where someone built meaning out of nothing but patience and river-stones and the refusal to be entirely erased.*

---

In the deepest part of the chamber, in the hours (or days) when no visitors come, the Keeper curls into a shape that might once have been sleeping and listens to the walls breathe. It matches its breathing to theirs—has to, now, cannot remember any other rhythm—and feels the something in its chest open a little wider.

It is not unhappy. It is not happy either. It has become a thing that exists beyond those categories, a creature adapted to strangeness, a consciousness that has learned to eddy and pool like the time around it.

But sometimes, in the space between one breath of the walls and the next, it remembers carrying stones. Remembers the weight of them, the coldness, the specific ache in muscles that understood effort. Remembers choosing to stay, and why, though the why has grown soft now, indistinct, a memory of a memory of a feeling that might have been wonder or might have been grief or might have been simply the recognition that some places, once found, cannot be unfound.

It touches the cairn with what used to be a hand.

The stones are still smooth. Still dark. Still absolutely themselves, despite everything.

This, the Keeper thinks, is enough. This is what staying means: not to remain unchanged, but to remain *something*. To tend what matters. To keep the message legible, even as the messenger becomes unreadable.

Outside, the waterfall roars.

Inside, the Keeper breathes with the walls, and waits, and watches for the next visitor who will need guiding home.
