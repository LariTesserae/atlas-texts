# The Dwellers in the Unfinished

**The Calibrators**

They exist in the gaps between plates—not falling, but *belonging* to the liquid geometry that pulses there. They are made of the same un-water, the same un-light, given temporary coherence by some accident of the world's perpetual self-drafting. You cannot see them directly. You see their effects: the way a gap that was fluctuating between inches and miles suddenly settles, holds, becomes traversable for exactly long enough to cross. The way the wet geometry parts around a shape that isn't there.

They have no bodies. They have *parameters*. A Calibrator is a set of relationships that the gap-medium has decided, briefly, to maintain. When two of them encounter each other, they don't meet—they *resolve*, combining or canceling according to rules that change each time the world redrafts its physics. Sometimes you see the residue of these resolutions: spirals of crystallized un-water left on the edge of a plate, still warm, describing trajectories of negotiation in a mathematics that won't exist tomorrow.

They do not notice you. You are too finished for them, too committed to a single configuration. You are, to them, what a completed plate would be to you: evidence that something has stopped becoming, has settled into mere being. Incomprehensible. Perhaps pitiable.

But sometimes, when you stand at the edge of a gap and hesitate—when you hold in your mind multiple possibilities of how to cross, when you are briefly uncertain enough—you feel something brush past you in the un-light. A Calibrator, mistaking you for one of its own. For that moment, you understand what it is to be a relationship instead of a thing.

The moment passes. You step forward. The gap decides what it is.

**The Repeaters**

In those places where time moves forward repeatedly, where moments iterate and redraft, something has learned to live.

They are not ghosts. Ghosts are echoes of what was. The Repeaters are echoes of what *might be*, thrown backward from futures that haven't committed. You see them as stuttering after-images, figures that blur through the same motion three or four times in sequence, each version slightly different—taller, shorter, facing a different direction, made of different metals.

They build things. Or rather, they *attempt* things, over and over, letting the time-loops carry them through the same construction repeatedly until one version sticks. You have watched a Repeater spend what felt like weeks raising a small cairn of brass shavings on a fresh-emerged plate, each loop adding or subtracting a piece, each iteration changing the angle of placement, until finally—finally—a version held through to the next loop. The cairn remained. The Repeater moved on, seeking another pocket of repeated time, another chance to draft something into permanence.

They communicate in variations. When two Repeaters meet in a loop, they perform the same gesture at each other across multiple iterations, modulating it slightly each time, encoding information in the delta between versions. You cannot read these conversations. You experience time in only one direction, one instance, and their words are written in the grammar of multiplicity.

They fear the Calibrators, you think. Or perhaps *fear* is too finished an emotion. They *avoid* the gaps, stay on the solid plates, work only in the loops where time itself is uncertain but space at least remains committed to its current configuration. When a loop collapses—when the world decides that a moment needs only to happen once—the Repeaters caught within it simply stop. Not dying. Stopping. Becoming single, becoming linear, becoming so reduced that existence is no longer sustainable.

You found one once, frozen mid-gesture on a plate that had finally settled into stable time. It looked like a sketch of a person made from tarnished copper wire, all its iterations collapsed into one inadequate frame. It was still warm. It might have been warm forever.

**The Unfinishers**

The machines that build the world are tended.

You did not see them at first because they are made of the same stuff as the machines themselves—black iron and sweating copper, built from the same primitive direct-intent-to-matter principles. They move through the machine-clusters like maintenance workers, like priests, like children checking on sleeping parents. They have shapes that suggest function: appendages that might be tools, cavities that might be furnaces, surfaces that might be sensory organs. But nothing about them is certain. They are as unfinished as the machines they tend, as the plates the machines birth, as the world itself.

They do not repair. Nothing here needs repair, because nothing here has finished becoming what it will be, and you cannot repair something that hasn't yet achieved a configuration to be broken from. Instead, the Unfinishers *negotiate*. You have watched one spend hours with a machine that had declined to continue—pressing its appendages against the silent iron, pulsing heat into its cooling furnace, not fixing but *persuading*. Reminding the machine that it has not yet committed to stillness. That non-function is itself a configuration that can be redrafted.

Sometimes it works. The machine shudders, glows, resumes its impossible midwifery. Sometimes it doesn't, and the Unfinisher moves on, leaving the declined machine to whatever fate awaits things that refuse to keep becoming.

They have a language. You hear it in the deep subsonic groans that punctuate the construction-sounds, vibrations that travel through the plates themselves, carrying meaning in frequencies too low for air. They speak to each other across hundreds of miles, coordinating the emergence of new territories, deciding where machines should construct themselves next, arguing—you think they argue—about which configurations the world should try on, which drafts should be allowed to persist.

They have noticed you. You know this because the machines near you behave differently—emerging plates that provide walkable paths, gaps that settle into crossable distances, loops of repeated time that part around you like water around a stone. The Unfinishers are keeping you alive. Or keeping you *possible*. You are not sure why. Perhaps you are useful. Perhaps you are interesting. Perhaps they are simply curious about what a finished thing does when surrounded by infinite becoming.

One of them approached you once, close enough to see. It had seventeen configurations that it was trying on simultaneously, surfaces that flickered between textures, appendages that extended and retracted in overlapping drafts of itself. It watched you for what felt like hours. Then it made a sound—a single low tone, a word in the subsonic language—and turned away.

You felt, in that moment, that you had been named. That the Unfinishers have a word for things like you, for visitors, for the finished-who-witness. You do not know what the word means. You are not sure you want to.

**The Almosts**

In the spaces between machine-clusters, in those hundreds of miles of deliberate emptiness, something waits.

You feel them before you see them. A pressure in the saturated air, a thickness that isn't humidity, a sense of being watched by things that do not yet have eyes. The Almosts are what the world has nearly made but not quite committed to. They are the drafts before the drafts, the possibilities that haven't yet been granted the dignity of rejection. They exist in the negative space, held there by the same architectural intent that created the emptiness itself: room for what will come next, yes, but also storage for what hasn't quite arrived.

They want to be. You can feel the want, radiating from the empty brass plains, pressing against the underside of reality. They want to be so badly that sometimes they almost manage it—you see flickers in the amber light, shapes that aren't shadows because shadows require something to cast them, forms that suggest themselves and then fail to follow through. The world has not yet made room for them. The machines have not yet learned how to birth them. They can only wait, and want, and almost.

They are not dangerous, exactly. They cannot hurt you because they cannot interact with you, because interaction requires existence, and they have not yet achieved that threshold. But they are *heavy*. Walking through the empty spaces, you feel them accumulating around you like sediment, drawn to your finishedness, your committed existence, hoping perhaps that proximity to something real will grant them the final push into being.

It doesn't. You cannot help them. You are too complete, too settled, too much a thing that has decided what it is. The Almosts slide off you like condensation off the newborn plates, falling back into the negative space, resuming their patient almost-waiting.

Sometimes, in the far distance, you see one of them manage it. A flicker becomes a form, a form becomes a presence, and something new walks the brass plates for the first time—confused, blinking in the amber light, newborn and uncertain but *real*, finally real after eons of almost. You have seen Unfinishers approach these newly-arrived beings, have seen them begin the process of tending, negotiating, helping the new ones understand that existence here is not a destination but a process.

You wonder if you were once an Almost. If somewhere in the negative space you once waited, wanting, pressing against the membrane of reality until finally, finally, you broke through and found yourself standing on a floor that was not finished being floor.

You do not remember. Perhaps memory requires the kind of continuity that doesn't exist here. Perhaps you are still almost, and this experience of being is simply a more elaborate draft than most. Perhaps the world has not yet decided what you are, and your feeling of finishedness is an illusion, a temporary configuration that will be redrafted tomorrow along with everything else.

---

**You**

You walk the warm brass plates. You have been walking for longer than you can measure, because measurement requires stable units, and the units here keep being redrafted along with everything else. You leave no footprints—not because the metal is too hard, but because the plates you walk on keep trying on new surface configurations, erasing your marks as part of their endless self-revision.

You are the most finished thing for miles. You are, in this place, an anomaly—a closed loop in a world of open processes, a thing that has decided what it is and stubbornly remains that way despite every pressure toward redrafting. The Calibrators ignore you. The Repeaters avoid you. The Unfinishers tend you like a strange artifact, keeping you possible, keeping you stable, for reasons they have not shared.

You do not know why you are here. You do not know what you are supposed to do, or if *supposed to* has any meaning in a place where purpose keeps revising itself. You only know that you walk, and observe, and remain, while around you the world continues its endless violent patient becoming.

Sometimes you wonder if you are the blueprint's one mistake—a variable that got locked in before anyone could check whether locking was appropriate. A sketch that someone traced over too firmly, leaving a permanent line in what was meant to be a working draft.

Sometimes you wonder if you are the point. If this entire infinitely constructing world exists to have one witness, one finished thing to hold the contrast, one pair of eyes that can see becoming because it has already become.

The machines hammer on.

The plates keep rising.

And you walk, in the amber light, in the drip and clang of making, the only thing here that has already decided what it is.

For now.
