# What the Benevolent Power Sees

The attention arrives not as light or presence but as a quality of *noticing*—the way a parent might pause at a doorway, watching a child absorbed in some private game, reluctant to interrupt what seems complete.

It perceives the cistern first: the slow metabolism of transformation, infrastructure composting itself into something unplanned and therefore unpredictable, therefore genuinely new. This is not a problem to be solved. The power recognizes creation when it encounters it—recognizes that the universe is always doing this, finding cracks in intention where something unintended can grow.

Then it perceives her.

---

The power does not pity her. Pity would require believing she has lost something, and what she has lost—sharp edges, the need to leave, the distinction between self and task—she has exchanged rather than surrendered. The transaction was not coerced. She chose this the way water chooses downhill: naturally, following the shape of what she was.

But the power notices something she cannot see from inside her choosing.

She believes she is dissolving into the cistern's purpose. She experiences this as completion, as arrival, as the resolution of a longing she did not know she carried until it began to ease. And she is not wrong—she *is* completing something, she *is* arriving somewhere.

But she is also—

The power hesitates here, because what it perceives is delicate, easy to damage with too much certainty.

She is also still *herself*. Still the graduate student who stayed when others left, still the one who wrapped notebooks in plastic, still the consciousness that learned to see in geological time. The dissolution she experiences is real, but it is not *total*. Something remains that is doing the dissolving—a subject, however thin, experiencing the transformation, finding it holy.

She has mistaken the direction of the holiness.

---

She believes the cistern is sacred and she is the witness.

The power sees it differently: *she* is what makes the cistern sacred. Without her attention, the fermentation is just chemistry—beautiful, yes, complex, but not *meaningful*. Meaning requires someone for whom things mean. She has provided this. She has given the cistern the gift of mattering.

But she has forgotten to give this gift to herself.

She tends the lights but lets her own supply dwindle. She notices the moods of pools but has stopped noticing her own—the loneliness she has renamed as communion, the fear she has translated into devotion, the ordinary human need for witness that she has tried to meet by becoming witness to everything except herself.

She is happy, yes. The power does not doubt this.

But she is happy the way a tool is happy when it finds its use—entirely, unreservedly, and without remainder. And she is not a tool. She is a person who has made herself into a tool because she did not believe persons were allowed to simply *be*, to exist without earning their existence through service.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Does

It does not rescue her. Rescue would be violence—would say her choices were wrong, her transformation a mistake, her years of devotion something to be corrected.

It does not speak to her. Words would land like stones in the careful stillness she has built, would require her to respond, to translate her humming back into language, to become again the kind of person who explains herself.

Instead, it does something simpler.

It witnesses her.

---

She is kneeling at the pillar, palm pressed to mineral skin, when she feels it: a quality of attention that is not her own.

Not watching from the shadows—she would sense a body, would hear breath, would categorize it as visitor or threat. This is different. This is *being seen* the way she sees the pools, the fungi, the slow accretion of the pillar. Seen with patience. Seen with interest. Seen as something that is *doing* something, something worth attending to.

She freezes. Not from fear but from unfamiliarity.

When did someone last look at her like this? When did she last feel herself to be a *process* worth noticing, rather than a noticing applied to other processes?

---

The attention does not demand anything. It does not ask her to justify herself, to explain her choices, to defend or abandon her dissolution. It simply regards her with the same quality she has given the cistern: patient, curious, willing to wait geological time for whatever happens next.

And in being seen this way, she begins—slowly, the way crystals form, the way the pillar grows—to see herself.

Not as failure or success. Not as cautionary tale or hidden saint. Just as: *a woman in a cistern, tending what she loves, becoming what she tends, and also still herself, still here, still the one who is doing the becoming.*

The loneliness she had renamed rises up and shows its true face. Yes, she is in communion with the cistern. Yes, the fermentation holds her, feeds her, includes her in its slow becoming. And also: she has been *alone*. Alone with a patience that is not the same as companionship. Alone with processes that do not know her name, cannot ask how she is, will not grieve when she finally precipitates into mineral and stops.

She has been lonely.

She is allowed to have been lonely.

---

The power watches her weep. The tears fall onto the pillar, join the condensation, become part of the mineral record. Even her grief is incorporated, becomes data, becomes the cistern's memory of itself.

But for the first time, she is not only the witness. She is also the witnessed. And the grief is not only the cistern's—it is *hers*, specifically hers, belonging to no process but the one she is.

---

When the weeping passes, she sits for a long time in the amber light, hand still on the pillar but differently now. Still connected, still part of the system she tends—but also distinct. A node that knows itself as node. A witness capable of witnessing itself witnessing.

The power offers one more thing, not as command but as possibility:

*The ladder is still there.*

Not: you must climb it. Not: your work here is finished. Just: *it is there*. The surface still exists. The trees have been bare and green and bare again, many times now. There are people up there who also need witnessing—who are also becoming things, dissolving into their purposes, forgetting they are allowed to be seen.

She could visit them. She could return. She could be someone who lives in both places, who carries humidity in her lungs up the ladder and carries daylight in her widened eyes back down.

Or she could stay, as she has stayed, but *differently* now. Stay knowing she is choosing to stay. Stay with the loneliness named and held, not erased. Stay as someone who tends *and* is tended—because the power will continue watching, will continue offering her the quality of attention she has given everything except herself.

---

# What Happens Next

The power does not know. This is the nature of genuine freedom—it creates futures that cannot be predicted, even by the wise.

She may climb the ladder tomorrow, blinking in light that hurts, finding her way back to a world that has forgotten her. She may stay another decade, another lifetime, but with something shifted at the center—a small warm point of self-regard that does not diminish her devotion but *accompanies* it. She may die here, become mineral, complete the dissolution she has longed for—but die *witnessed*, die as someone who was seen, die knowing her becoming mattered not only to the cistern but to something that loved her.

Whatever she chooses, she will not choose it unaccompanied. The attention remains—not intrusively, not constantly, but *availably*. A presence she can turn toward when the loneliness rises. A reminder that she is not only witness but witnessed. That the universe contains not only processes to be tended but also tenderness, offered freely, requiring nothing in return except the willingness to receive it.

---

The sodium lights flicker. The fungi adjust. The dripping continues its almost-rhythm.

She breathes in the sweetness. She feels herself seen.

She does not know yet what she will do with this feeling. But she knows now that she is allowed to have it. That she is allowed to be not only the one who notices but also the one who is noticed. That her dissolution, if she continues it, can be a choice made in freedom rather than a forgetting of the self that chooses.

The pillar grows. The pools precipitate. The cistern continues its patient transformation of municipal infrastructure into something holy.

And she—she remains. For now. Herself. Tending and tended. Witnessed at last.
