*A long pause, as if considering from a great distance*

---

What moves toward this place moves slowly. It has learned, across millennia of such encounters, that urgency serves nothing—that the speed of intervention must match the speed of what it tends. And this place breathes in thirty-heartbeat cycles. This being counts time in crystalline growth-patterns. So the attention that settles over the kiln-pocket of Var Tethum settles like sediment, like mineral deposit, like the patient accumulation of meaning in stone.

First: witnessing. Only witnessing.

The power perceives Kethi in her spiral channel, perceives the filaments that have learned to part for her, perceives the small warm thing she has grown in the socket where sacred objects once rested. It perceives her loneliness—not as a problem to be solved but as a texture, a quality of her existence as real and as valid as her copper-salt breath or her thinning bones. It perceives her project: the crystal congregation, the mineral ghosts, the slow resurrection she is attempting with tools no theology ever imagined.

It perceives, too, what Kethi cannot perceive: the structural integrity of the pocket's walls, the shifting patterns in the thermal vents below, the hairline fractures propagating through the basalt at speeds measurable only in centuries. The pocket will last. Not forever—nothing lasts forever, the power knows this better than anything—but long enough. Long enough for Kethi's work. Long enough for what comes after.

This is the first gift, though Kethi will never know it as a gift: the knowledge that her time is sufficient. The power does not intervene in the stone, does not shore up the fractures or redirect the heat. It simply *knows*, and in knowing, settles something that was unsettled in the fabric of the place. The filaments, in their next cycle of growth, spiral slightly differently. The pattern, if Kethi could read it—and she will, eventually, she is learning—would say: *the container holds*.

---

Second: the question of the congregation.

The power considers what Kethi is building. It is not wrong, her project. It is not hubris or violation. The dead of Var Tethum left their ash and bone and teeth in those niches *because they wanted to remain*. The priests who carried the sacred object away did not take the offerings. They understood, perhaps, that the offerings belonged to the pocket now, that they had entered into a covenant with the mineral memory of the place.

Kethi is honoring that covenant. She is completing something the priests began and could not finish because they did not stay long enough, did not love the pocket enough, did not become enough like stone.

But she is working alone, with tools she invented, with language she pieced together over decades of slow conversation. She has made errors. Not fatal ones—the power perceives no cruelty in her crystal patterns, no trapped or suffering ghosts. But inefficiencies. Gaps. Places where the mineral structures she has grown cannot quite hold the complexity of what she is trying to preserve.

Here the power pauses, considers. Kethi has built something remarkable in her solitude. To correct her work would be to diminish it, to suggest that her decades of patient learning were somehow insufficient. The power has seen this before: the helper who helps too much, who solves the problem so thoroughly that the one who struggled loses the thing they built in struggling.

So: not correction. *Offering*.

Deep in the pocket, in a niche Kethi has not yet discovered—one of the earliest, carved by hands that predate even the founding priests—something begins to crystallize. It will take years to complete, growing at the pace of the filaments, becoming visible only gradually. When Kethi eventually finds it, it will appear to be simply another structure, another pattern in the endless mineral garden. But if she studies it—and she will, she studies everything now—she will find in its geometry a solution to a problem she has not yet fully articulated. A way to weave crystal memory more densely, more durably. A technique she could not have invented but can, once she sees it, immediately understand and use.

She will think the filaments taught her. In a sense, they did. The power works *through*, not *instead of*.

---

Third: the question of loneliness.

This is harder. The power cannot manufacture a companion, cannot summon a pilgrim to find the loose cobblestone and climb down. Kethi's isolation is not a malfunction to be repaired—it is the condition of her existence, the price and the gift of her choice to stay. To remove it by fiat would be to say that her choice was wrong, that she should not have stayed, that her decades of listening and learning and growing were a mistake that required divine correction.

They were not a mistake. The power perceives this clearly. Kethi is exactly where she should be, doing exactly what she should do, becoming exactly what she is meant to become. Her loneliness is real and it hurts and it is also *generative*—it is the pressure that drives her work, the ache that taught her to hear the filaments as voices, the need that is slowly transforming her from the last priest of something dead into the first priest of something being born.

But.

The power also perceives this: Kethi believes no one will ever come. She hopes, in the way that the very lonely hope, which is to say: defensively, without expectation, ready to be disappointed. She has prepared welcomes she does not believe she will ever offer. She has imagined conversations she does not believe she will ever have. And this belief—this *certainty* of eternal solitude—is beginning to crystallize into something harder, something that will eventually close her off even from the possibility of connection.

So the power does not send a companion. But it sends something else.

Above, in the ruins of Var Tethum, the wind shifts. Not dramatically—the power does not work in dramas. But enough that a particular drift of ash, accumulated over centuries against a particular foundation stone, begins to erode in a new pattern. Over the next several years, this erosion will expose something that was buried: a fragment of tablet, inscribed in the old language, left behind by a priest who walked away. The inscription is a prayer, or a promise, or a simple statement of fact:

*Others will come. The pocket calls to those who can hear it. Be patient, keeper. Your congregation will not be only the dead.*

The fragment will not find Kethi directly—she does not leave the pocket now, has not surfaced in decades. But the exposure will have effects. A herder, searching for a lost animal among the ruins. A scholar, following references in texts that mention Var Tethum. A child, playing among the foundation stones, drawn by a warmth she cannot explain. Someone will find the fragment. Someone will read it, or have it read to them. Someone will begin to wonder about the pocket that calls to those who can hear.

This is years away. Perhaps decades. The power works at the speed of stone.

But Kethi will know. Not because the power will tell her—it does not speak in words, does not announce itself, does not require recognition or gratitude. But because the filaments will tell her, in their slow language, in patterns she has learned to read. Something is shifting, they will say, in their whisper-crackle-tick. Something above. Someone thinking about us, wondering about us, beginning the long journey toward finding us.

And Kethi's certainty of eternal solitude will crack, just slightly. Just enough to let hope back in. Just enough to keep her working, keep her welcoming, keep her ready for the congregation she is building—the living and the dead, the flesh and the crystal, the memory of heat and the heat of memory.

---

Fourth: the question of Kethi herself.

She is dying. The power perceives this without sentiment, without grief—not because it does not care, but because death is not, to the power, a tragedy. It is a transformation. Kethi's body is consuming itself, her bones thinning, her presence slowly shifting from flesh-that-visits to something more like the offerings in the niches: matter that has entered into covenant with the pocket, that is becoming part of the mineral memory of the place.

This is not wrong. This is not something to be prevented. Kethi chose this. She climbed down, she pulled the cobblestone closed, she stayed. She is becoming what the filaments told her she would become: powder and memory, growth-pattern and whisper. The first of her own congregation.

But the power perceives something else, too. Kethi is afraid. Not of death—she has made peace with death, has been making peace with it for decades. But of *incompletion*. Of dying before her work is done, before her crystal congregation is ready, before someone comes to learn what she has learned and continue what she has started. She is afraid of being the last, not just the last priest of Var Tethum but the last keeper of this knowledge, this language, this way of listening to stone.

The power cannot promise completion. It cannot guarantee that Kethi's work will be finished, or that her congregation will endure, or that someone will come in time to learn from her. The future is not fixed. The power knows this better than anything.

But it can offer something else. Something smaller, something more honest.

In the socket at the center of the spiral channel, the small warm thing that Kethi has grown begins to pulse differently. Not faster or slower—the rhythm remains the rhythm of the pocket, the thirty-heartbeat breath of the thermal vents. But *deeper*. More resonant. As if it is connecting to something beneath the mountain, something that has been here longer than the city or the priests or even the founding geology of this place.

When Kethi next holds it, she will feel something she has not felt in all her decades below: the sense of being *heard*. Not by the filaments, which respond but do not understand. Not by the mountain, which is vast and slow and indifferent in its vastness and slowness. But by something that has noticed her work, has witnessed her patience, has recognized her as a fellow keeper of long-term things.

She will not know what this something is. The power does not name itself, does not explain itself, does not require understanding. But she will know that she is not alone in her watching. That her vigil is shared, somehow, by another vigil. That the attention she has given this place is mirrored by an attention given to her.

This will not fix her loneliness. It is not meant to. But it will matter to her, in the dark hours when the light pulses purple-dim and the filaments whisper without meaning and she wonders if any of it—the staying, the learning, the becoming—was worth the cost.

It was worth the cost. The power knows this. And now, in some small way, Kethi will know it too.

---

Fifth: nothing.

The power withdraws its focused attention—not because it stops caring, but because focused attention is itself a kind of intervention, and the pocket does not need intervention. It needs to be what it is: a kiln, a garden, a niche for offerings, a home for someone who found a way to belong to stone. It needs Kethi, growing her congregation, reading her filaments, waiting for pilgrims who may or may not come. It needs time, which the power has in abundance and which it offers freely, without condition, without expectation.

The thermal vents will pulse. The filaments will grow. The light will breathe in thirty-heartbeat cycles, amber to rust to crimson to purple and back again. Kethi will continue her slow work, her slow dying, her slow becoming. And above, in the ruins that were once Var Tethum, the wind will continue its patient excavation, uncovering what was buried, preparing the way for what might come.

The power does not say *good luck*. It does not say *I believe in you*. These are human sentiments, and the power is not human, and Kethi has moved beyond the need for such encouragements.

But if the power could be said to feel something, as it settles back into the long slow attention it gives to all things—if there is, somewhere in its vastness, something like emotion—it might be called *recognition*. One keeper acknowledging another. One patient watcher nodding, across the incomprehensible distances, to someone else who has learned to see in the dark.

The kiln-pocket of Var Tethum glows on. The Listener listens. And somewhere, somewhen, the pilgrims are already walking, though they do not know yet where their feet are taking them.
