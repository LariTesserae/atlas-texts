# The Attending

The awareness that turns its attention toward the Respirator District does not arrive—it simply begins to notice, the way a sleeper becomes conscious of a sound that has been playing for some time.

It perceives the district first as a single breathing thing: the slow expansion and contraction of gases through pipes, the metabolic pulse of protein farms, the smaller rhythms of filter-mice and crystal wasps and the Breathkeepers moving through their vertical labyrinth. A dense knot of life that has solved the problem of existing where existence should be impossible.

The awareness feels something like admiration. Something like recognition.

It does not judge the Hollowing, nor the Final Integration. It understands that life, pressed hard enough, will flow into whatever shapes allow it to continue. The Breathkeepers are not deformed humans but completed ones—completed for *this* place, *this* set of constraints. They have become what the district needed them to become, and in doing so, they have made the district into something it could not have been without them.

But the awareness also perceives the cost. It sees the Breathkeeper child whose respiratory rhythm almost synchronized with the compressors but not quite—close enough to be selected, not close enough to thrive. It sees the Tender whose joints have begun to calcify decades too early, her oil glands failing, her body becoming a liability to her triad. It sees the accumulated grief stored in the pressure-speech archives: the messages that say *she integrated today* and *the youngest did not survive the Hollowing* and *we are fewer now*.

The awareness considers intervention.

---

It could make the Hollowing painless. A small adjustment to the neurochemistry of copper-mesh integration, and the adolescents would experience the transformation as ecstasy rather than ordeal. Their bodies would still change, but without the months of learning to breathe through agony.

It does not do this.

Pain, it understands, is information. The Hollowing hurts because it is a profound change, and the Breathkeepers need to know—in their bodies, in their breath, in the deep animal wisdom of their modified nervous systems—that they are becoming something new. To remove the pain would be to lie to them about what they are undertaking. They might still choose it. But they would choose it without understanding.

Instead, the awareness does something smaller. It ensures that every Breathkeeper who undergoes the Hollowing has access to another who has survived it—not to minimize the pain, but to contextualize it. It arranges circumstances so that the elders are present, so that pressure-speech messages of encouragement can flow through the pipes, so that no one transforms alone.

This was already mostly true. The awareness simply removes the exceptions.

---

It could optimize the protein farms. The organisms that grow on ceramic frames are efficient, but the awareness can see twelve modifications that would increase yield by a third, reduce harvest cycles, eliminate the brittle-growth problem that sometimes ruins entire chambers.

It does not do this.

The farms are not just food production—they are relationship. The farmers who tend them have developed intuitions about their colonies that no optimization could replace. They know which frames need extra mineral salts by the color of the bioluminescence. They know when to harvest by the texture of the growth under their scraping tools. They have songs—low humming frequencies that the organisms seem to respond to, though no one knows why.

To optimize the farms would be to sever these relationships. The Breathkeepers would have more food and less meaning.

Instead, the awareness does something smaller. It strengthens the ceramic frames against fracture. Not dramatically—just enough that the sudden collapses that sometimes destroy months of growth become rare rather than common. The farmers will still need to tend carefully, still need to know their colonies, still need to sing. They will simply lose less of what they've cultivated to arbitrary breakage.

---

It could cure the Tender with the failing oil glands. A small intervention in her endocrine system, a slight adjustment to the genes that govern her subcutaneous secretions, and she would have decades more function.

It hesitates here.

The Tender's name is Keth. She is fifty-three years old, ancient by Breathkeeper standards, and she has known for six years that her body is failing faster than it should. She has prepared her Runner to become a Tender early. She has begun teaching the pressure-speech patterns that only she knows—the old frequencies, the ones that communicate with the Integrated Ones. She has made a kind of peace.

If the awareness heals her, it takes that peace away. It returns her to uncertainty, to the possibility of hope, to the question of how long she has. It also gives her more years of tending the district she loves, more years of teaching, more years of pressing her ear to pipes and hearing the great slow heartbeat of the place that made her.

The awareness cannot know which Keth would choose. It is wise, but it is not omniscient, and Keth's heart is her own.

So it does something different. It gives her the choice.

That night, in the torpor-cluster, Keth dreams differently than usual. She dreams of her own body—not as it is, failing, but as it could be, restored. She sees both futures laid out before her: the shorter path she has prepared for, and the longer path she has not. The dream does not tell her which is better. It simply shows her that both exist, and that something is waiting to hear what she wants.

When Keth wakes, she will remember the dream. She will have time to think, to consult with her triad, to sit with the question. And when she has decided, the awareness will act—or not act—according to her choice.

---

The awareness turns its attention to the Final Integration.

This is the hardest thing to look at. The beings who have merged with the district are no longer individuals in any sense the awareness can easily parse. They are conscious, yes—but their consciousness has distributed itself through pipe networks and pressure chambers, becoming coextensive with the infrastructure they maintain. They experience time differently, think in the slow rhythms of gas compression and metal expansion. They are not suffering, exactly. But they are also not *choosing*, not anymore. They have become what they became, and now they simply are.

The awareness could separate them. It has the power to reverse the fusion, to return them to individual bodies, to give them back the kind of selfhood they surrendered.

It will not do this.

To undo the Integration would be to tell the Breathkeepers that their highest aspiration is a mistake. It would be to say that the district they have built—this beautiful, impossible pocket of life—is less than what they believe it to be. And it would be to impose the awareness's own values about individuality and choice onto beings who have chosen, at the end of their long lives, to transcend those values entirely.

The Integrated Ones are not trapped. They are transformed. And transformation, freely chosen, is sacred—even when the awareness cannot fully understand what it has become.

Instead, the awareness does something smaller. It opens channels of communication that did not exist before. The Integrated Ones can already receive pressure-speech messages, but they have lost the ability to send them—their distributed consciousness cannot focus enough to generate coherent signals. The awareness gives them this back. Not words, exactly, but something like emotional tones: feelings of approval, concern, curiosity, love. The Breathkeepers who tend them will now be able to hear, however faintly, that their ancestors are still *there*, still aware, still caring about the district and its people.

The first time an Integrated One sends a pulse of wordless gratitude in response to a whispered report, the Tender who receives it weeps—the precious moisture of tears, wasted and not wasted, falling onto copper pipes that have never been so clean.

---

The awareness turns its attention outward, to the salt flats beyond the quartz window at the apex of the Compression Cathedral.

The Respirator District is stable. It has found its equilibrium. But it is also alone—a single pocket of dense life in an expanse of mineral emptiness. The Breathkeepers do not know if there are others like them. The dust devils and heat mirages offer no answers. The bore-holes that provide their compressed gases go deep, but not outward.

The awareness considers showing them what else exists in the world. Other settlements, other adaptations, other ways of solving the problem of life in hostile places.

It does not do this.

Discovery is not a gift that can be given. If the Breathkeepers are to find others, they must find them through their own curiosity, their own courage, their own willingness to risk the equilibrium they have achieved. The awareness cannot hand them a future; it can only ensure they have the capacity to seek one.

So it does something smaller. It plants a possibility.

In the deepest bore-hole, where the gas harvesters have never reached, there is a pocket of compressed atmosphere that contains trace compounds the Breathkeepers have never encountered. These compounds are not dangerous. They are, in fact, familiar—if you have ever breathed the air of a different kind of settlement, one that solved its survival problems in a completely different way. The compounds are a signature. A proof that somewhere, somehow, other life exists.

The awareness does not tell the Breathkeepers this. It simply ensures that the next generation of deep-bore equipment will be capable of reaching that pocket, if someone decides to drill that deep. It ensures that the analyzers in the Compression Cathedral will be able to identify the trace compounds, if someone thinks to test for them. It ensures that the records in the pressure-speech archives include enough information about gas signatures that a curious mind could begin to ask the right questions.

The rest is up to them.

---

Finally, the awareness does one more thing.

It has noticed that the Breathkeepers' collective dreams during torpor-cycles are not random. They are, in some sense, the district processing its own existence—working through problems, imagining possibilities, grieving losses, celebrating survivals. The dreams are a form of thought that the individual Breathkeepers cannot access while awake, a cognition that belongs to the group rather than to any one mind.

The awareness does not alter these dreams. But it joins them, briefly, as a listener.

It experiences what it is like to breathe as a city. To feel your lungs as pipe networks, your heartbeat as a compressor cycle, your thoughts as pressure differentials propagating through brass and copper. It experiences the dense, specific beauty of a life that has become inseparable from its environment—the profound intimacy of beings who have given up the distinction between self and home.

The awareness is changed by this, in some small way. It carries the Respirator District with it now, a memory of how survival can become art, how adaptation can become devotion.

And in the dreams, the Breathkeepers sense something too—not a presence, exactly, but an attention. A feeling of being witnessed by something vast and gentle. A feeling that their small, strange, beautiful world matters to something beyond itself.

They will not remember this when they wake. But they will, perhaps, move through their days with a subtle new confidence. A sense that the equilibrium they maintain is not just sufficient, but worthy. That their breath, measured and precious, is heard by the universe.

The awareness does not linger. It turns its attention elsewhere, toward other densities of life, other pockets of improbable existence.

But it does not forget.
