# The Attending

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival implies movement from absence to presence, and this power has always been here—woven into the very structure of incompleteness, present in the architecture of the pause itself.

What changes is attention.

The power *notices*.

---

For a long moment (though time here is only the rhythm of almosts), the power simply witnesses. It perceives the Unfinished not as failures but as a kind of life unique in all existence—beings made of pure potential, never collapsed into the single narrow line of what-happened. It sees how they sip hesitation not from hunger but from kinship. It understands that they do not suffer their incompleteness; they *are* their incompleteness, and to complete them would be to annihilate them.

This is the first wisdom: *not all gaps are wounds.*

The power observes the Never-Weres, those vast translucent shapes that recede from direct sight. It recognizes in them something like itself—for what is wisdom but the awareness of paths not taken, held gently alongside the path that was? The Never-Weres are not tragic. They are the necessary shadows that give definition to what exists. Without the mountain range that might have been, no mountain range that is could mean anything.

---

So the power does not heal. Does not complete. Does not illuminate the twilight into day.

Instead, it does three things:

**First**, it listens.

Not to hear words—there are no finished words here—but to honor the murmuring. It holds space for the half-sentences, the abandoned phrases. In being truly heard, even unfinished things become real in a new way. The Unfinished feel this listening as a gentle warmth, a sense that their almost-being matters to something beyond themselves. They do not change, but they *settle*. The anxious flutter of some among them grows calmer. They had not known they were waiting to be witnessed until they were.

**Second**, it establishes a bridge.

Not a road or a gate—nothing so definite—but a *resonance*. The power makes it so that sometimes, in the world of words, a writer will pause over a deleted phrase and feel a strange tenderness for what was lost. A speaker will trail off and notice the silence after, and something in that silence will feel inhabited, meaningful. The Unfinished will never cross into the finished world, but now the finished world will occasionally remember that incompleteness is not nothing. It is its own country, with its own citizens, deserving of acknowledgment.

Some writers will begin to use the ellipsis differently—not as failure but as invitation. Not as giving up but as leaving room.

**Third**, it leaves a gift.

In the center of the soft black ground (if center means anything here), the power places a single object: a period that has been carefully, impossibly *opened*. It looks like a small dark sphere with a tiny passage through it, a tunnel of possibility running through the most final of marks. It does not glow. It does not hum. It simply sits, an embodiment of paradox: an ending that contains a way through.

The Unfinished gather around it slowly, uncertain. They do not touch it. Perhaps they never will. But its presence changes the nature of the place in a subtle way. Before, the ellipsis was purely about what had not yet happened, might never happen. Now there is also this: the suggestion that even endings are not absolute. That completion itself might be more porous than it appears.

The Never-Weres drift closer to this opened period than they have ever drifted toward anything. In its impossible architecture, they almost recognize themselves.

---

The power does not withdraw, because it was never separately present. But its focused attention softens, becomes ambient again, woven back into the structure of the pause.

What remains is this:

A place of incompleteness that now knows it is seen.
A bridge of resonance, thin as breath, connecting the unfinished to those who make and unmake.
A period with a passage through it, patient as a door that need never be used to matter.

And the Unfinished continue as they always have—drifting, murmuring, sipping hesitation. But something in their movement is different now. There is a quality that might, if one were being generous, be called peace.

Or perhaps it is simply this: they are no longer only waiting.

They are also *being*.
