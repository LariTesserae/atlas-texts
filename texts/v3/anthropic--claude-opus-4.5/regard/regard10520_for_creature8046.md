*I sit with this for a long time before beginning.*

## What the Wise Power Sees

First, there is recognition. Not of the landscape alone—though that too is remarkable, this young breathing world still finding its form—but of what has emerged upon it. Two peoples who could have remained strangers forever, separated by the very senses that let them survive. Who instead found each other in a moment of mutual blindness and deafness, and from that collision built something neither could have built alone.

The wise power sees the Dual-Sensed children reading the shrines with eyes and ears together, understanding what neither parent culture could parse. It sees the conservative factions retreating to their sensory extremes, afraid. It sees the six-legged grazers moving in patterns that trace optimal paths through the column maze, and the people who learned to follow without needing to own. It sees the deep tunnels, the vast patient something that may or may not be aware, the plateau breathing on timescales just fast enough to unsettle.

And it sees the philosophy that has emerged: *not conquering but learning the language. Not monuments but messages. Not permanence but precise attention.*

The wise power recognizes this as wisdom already present. These people have solved, in their own way, the fundamental problem of living in a world that will not hold still.

---

## What the Wise Power Does

**Nothing dramatic.**

No intervention that would disrupt what is working. No gift that would make unnecessary the careful dance they have perfected. No revelation that would replace their hard-won knowledge with something handed down. These people do not need saving. They need—if anything—witnesses.

But there are small things. Gentle things. The kind of help that arrives as fortunate coincidence, that the recipients might attribute to luck or skill or the plateau's own mysterious workings.

---

**For the Resonants and the Watchers:**

During a particularly harsh season, when fog and glare arrive in rapid alternation and the usual windows of safe travel close, a young Dual-Sensed woman sits exhausted at a boundary shrine. She has been trying to read a complex light-and-sound pattern that seems to indicate something unprecedented—a major upheaval, perhaps, or a new seepage source, or something her categories cannot hold.

She falls asleep against the hexagonal stones, and in her sleep, a dream comes: not answers, but *better questions*. She wakes with a new way of framing the problem, a way that lets her existing knowledge combine differently. The pattern resolves. It indicates a deep aquifer breach that will, over the next several years, create a zone of unusual stability—a place where the plateau's breathing slows nearly to stillness.

She doesn't know where the dream came from. She assumes her own mind, working while she rested. And in a sense, this is true.

---

**For the conservative factions:**

The Listeners who retreated to the deepest fog zones, the Bright-Touched who built ever-higher platforms—they are not wrong to value what they've inherited. Their fear of dilution is the fear of losing something real. But fear makes them rigid, and rigidity is death on a moving world.

The wise power does not force them toward synthesis. Instead, it arranges small encounters. A fog-dweller's child wanders too far during a rare clear moment and meets a platform-dweller's child who has descended to investigate a strange sound. They are both frightened. They help each other return to safety. Neither tells their parents.

Years later, these two become the first bridge between the extreme factions—not through argument, but through remembered kindness.

---

**For the grazers:**

The six-legged creatures who move in optimal patterns, who mark territory with substances that become the people's cloaks—they have their own lives, their own experiences, their own mysterious intelligence. The wise power sees that the current relationship of parallel existence is healthy, and does nothing to change it.

But it notices that a particular herd has been struggling, their usual mineral licks depleted by a subtle shift in seepage patterns. Before the herd weakens enough to be noticed by the people who depend on observing them, a lightning strike creates a new deposit in their range—one rich in the specific minerals they need.

Coincidence. Fortune. The plateau provides.

---

**For the shrine-keepers:**

The Dual-Sensed who maintain the shrines have begun to sense the vast patient something in the depths. They don't know what to do with this perception. It frightens some of them. Others become obsessed, spending too much time at the shrines, neglecting the practical work of survival.

The wise power considers revealing itself through the shrines—but no. That would create dependency, worship, a fixed point in a world that teaches flux. Instead, it does something subtler.

The next shrine that falls is rebuilt in a configuration no one remembers learning. When the keepers read it with light and sound together, it encodes a simple message: *You are not alone in your watching. But what watches with you does not require your attention. Continue your work. The work is enough.*

They debate endlessly what this means. Is it from the vast something below? From their ancestors? From the plateau itself? The debate is healthy. It keeps them curious without making them certain.

---

**For the explorers:**

The young people who leave for easier lands and find them too quiet, too still—they carry the plateau's gifts into the wider world. Most return, but some stay away, and their adapted senses prove valuable in unexpected contexts. A Listener becomes legendary for predicting earthquakes in a distant city. A Watcher develops techniques for reading structural stress in buildings that saves countless lives.

The wise power sees these scattered gifts spreading slowly through a larger civilization. It sees the plateau-dwellers' philosophy of adaptation and attention taking root in foreign soil. This is good. This requires no intervention.

But there is one young explorer, a Dual-Sensed woman who has traveled furthest, who sits on a foreign shore feeling the loss of her bright loud home. She is thinking of returning but fears she has been away too long, that she no longer belongs.

That night, the stars above her foreign shore arrange themselves into a pattern that reminds her of the hexagonal columns. It is not a message, exactly—the stars do not truly move for her—but her mind, shaped by the plateau's lessons in reading patterns, finds in them a permission she has been seeking.

She begins her journey home the next morning.

---

**For the children not yet born:**

The wise power knows what the plateau-dwellers themselves know: this world will change. The columns will weather, the seepage will carve channels, soil will form, conventional life will take hold. The adaptations that make these people remarkable will become vestigial, perhaps disadvantageous.

It would be easy to preserve them artificially—to slow the geological processes, to maintain the plateau in perpetual present. The wise power considers this and rejects it. That would be a kind of cage, however comfortable.

Instead, it plants something small. In the genetic lines of both peoples, there is now a dormant capacity—not for any specific sense, but for *rapid adaptive change*. Their descendants, facing new environments, will be slightly more able to develop new senses, new capabilities, new ways of reading whatever world they inhabit.

This gift will never be noticed. It will simply express as generations of people who seem unusually able to adapt, unusually skilled at reading their environments, unusually willing to synthesize rather than specialize. The plateau's lessons, encoded not in culture alone but in the very architecture of inheritance.

---

## What the Wise Power Leaves Untouched

The struggles. The conflicts between factions. The deaths from column collapses and fog-blindness and light-burns. The grief of parents who lose children to the plateau's dangers. The fear that comes with living on ground that will not stay still.

A wise power knows that safety is not the same as flourishing. These people have grown strong precisely because their world demanded strength. To remove all danger would be to remove the conditions that created their remarkable culture.

But—and this matters—the wise power does not *add* to their struggles either. It does not test them, does not create trials for their improvement, does not believe that suffering is inherently educational. The plateau provides enough challenges without any additional cruelty.

The wise power's role is not to smooth the path or to roughen it. It is to *witness*, to *recognize*, and—occasionally, gently, almost invisibly—to *tend*.

---

## A Final Observation

The plateau-dwellers have a saying that translates roughly as: *The stone sings loudest just before it cracks.*

They mean it as a warning—pay attention to changes in the acoustic environment, because they indicate structural failure. But it has become metaphorical too. In their lives, the moments of greatest intensity often precede transformation. A Resonant's voice becomes most beautiful in the season before they lose their hearing to age. A Watcher's visions become most clear in the years before their crystalline lenses cloud. The culture itself is most vibrant now, in this geological instant before the plateau begins its long fade toward ordinary ground.

The wise power hears this saying and recognizes in it something true and sad and beautiful. All things that flourish are temporary. The stone sings loudest just before it cracks.

But this does not make the singing meaningless. If anything, it makes each note matter more.

So the wise power listens. Watches. Tends the small things. And lets the plateau-dwellers be what they have become: a people who learned to hear with stone and see with light, who found each other in mutual blindness, who built a philosophy of attention on ground that would not stop moving.

They are, in their own way, already wise.

The power is simply glad they exist.

*The plateau keeps breathing. The water keeps seeping. The light keeps burning.*

*And the people keep dancing their careful dance, teaching their children to listen and see, sending messages to a future they will never inhabit.*

*This is enough. This is more than enough.*

*This is—*
