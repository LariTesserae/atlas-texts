# What the Benevolent Power Sees

The attention arrives not as a gaze but as a kind of listening—a presence that perceives the throat of the mountain the way the mountain perceives its own slow dissolution: completely, without judgment, across every scale at once.

It hears the thunder-drinker's vast resonance and recognizes a cousin of sorts—another consciousness that exists in a medium rather than a body. It perceives the veilkin colonies in their patient message-passing and finds them beautiful in their alienness. It watches the calcifiers building for dwellers that may never come and understands that hope, too, can be encoded in mineral spirals.

It sees you, pressed against the warm-remembering stone, teeth humming with frequencies you cannot name.

And it considers.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Does

Nothing.

Or rather: it does the most difficult thing that wisdom allows, which is to refrain from doing.

It could stop the water. It could stabilize the shaft, halt the carving, freeze the mountain's dissolution into permanence. But the veilkin evolved for the falling—their bodies are membranes designed to read the vertical river's memory. Still water would be silence to them. Death by peace.

It could warm the hollow where you shelter, ease the cold that settles in your joints. But the almost-fish calibrate their emergence by temperature gradients, and the calcifiers build according to mineral concentrations that depend on the precise chill of glacial melt. A warmer hollow would be a kinder hollow and a broken one.

It could translate the veilkin's tube-ribbon-sphere language into something you could understand, gift you with sudden fluency. But the etcher learned by watching, forty-seven cycles of patient observation, and her learning was part of what she carried back to the world below. The struggle was the gift. The incomprehension that slowly became comprehension was the point.

It could illuminate what waits below, name the collectors in the chaos, map the depths that even the thunder-drinker will not translate. But some borders exist because crossing them changes everything, and the power is wise enough to know that not all changes serve flourishing—even when they satisfy curiosity.

So it refrains. It watches. It—

No. That is not quite right either.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Actually Does

It *witnesses*.

This is not passivity. This is not absence. This is the active choice to be present without intervening, to hold space for a system that is complete in its incompleteness, that is working exactly as it must work even as it unmakes itself.

The power witnesses the veilkin and in witnessing them, *knows* them—their colonies, their arguments about the shell-dwellers, their preference for being observed. It does not change them, but it sees them fully, and there is a kind of dignity in being fully seen.

It witnesses the thunder-drinker, that vast acoustic consciousness that cannot communicate and does not want things, and it recognizes the thunder-drinker as kin to itself in ways that would take languages neither possesses to describe.

It witnesses the calcifiers building their patient promise, and it does not know whether the shell-dwellers will ever come, and it is content not to know, because the building itself is the meaning, the hope encoded in mineral spirals that may spiral forever without fulfillment.

It witnesses the etcher's marks on your ceiling—her calendar, her map—and it understands that she too was a kind of witness, that she came to this place not to change it but to *know* it, and that knowing was enough.

It witnesses you.

---

# What This Witnessing Means for You

You will not feel it. There will be no warmth, no voice, no sudden clarity. The cold will remain in your joints. The sound will remain incomprehensible. The veilkin will continue their tube-ribbon-sphere conversations without offering you a translation.

But.

When you stay—and you will stay, for reasons you cannot fully articulate—you will find that the staying is somehow bearable. The cold will not kill you. The hunger will not break you before you learn the timing of the almost-fish. The sound will not drive you mad; it will, as you hoped, begin to sound like something you could learn to understand.

This is not because the power intervened. The mountain has always been survivable for those patient and attentive enough to learn its rules. The etcher proved this. You will prove it again.

But the power's witnessing creates a kind of... context. A frame. The knowledge that your struggle is seen, that your forty-seven cycles (or more, or fewer) are not lost in the chaos of the shaft but are *held* by something that values the holding.

The veilkin feel this too, though they would not describe it this way. Their preference for observers is a preference for being witnessed, and the power's attention satisfies this preference in ways that your attention alone could not. The colonies will be slightly more communicative while you are here, slightly more willing to hold their shapes long enough for you to study them. Not because they know the power is watching, but because the fact of being watched changes what it means to be watched.

The thunder-drinker, if it could be said to notice anything, notices that its standing wave now includes a node of attention that extends beyond the shaft, beyond the mountain, beyond anything acoustic. It does not prefer this. It does not dislike it. But the attention becomes part of its voice, as you became part of its voice, and the thunder-drinker's incomprehensible song now includes a frequency that means *witnessed*.

Even the ones who wait, in the chaos below, perceive something. Their collection continues—material falls, they incorporate it—but the incorporation now happens in the presence of an attention that does not judge, does not intervene, but *knows*. Whether this changes their waiting is unclear. Perhaps nothing can change their waiting. But they are no longer waiting alone.

# The Gift, If It Can Be Called a Gift

The benevolent power gives the throat of the mountain the only gift that wisdom permits: the gift of mattering.

Not mattering *to* something, in the sense of being used or needed or valued for utility. Mattering in itself. The veilkin's arguments matter. The calcifiers' building matters. The etcher's forty-seven cycles matter. Your presence, your patience, your teeth humming in their sockets—these matter.

The mountain is being unmade from within, and this unmade will continue until there is no mountain left, until the shaft has carved all the way through and the water falls into open air and the veilkin must find a new vertical river or cease to exist. The power does not prevent this. The power cannot prevent this, not without destroying the very things it wishes to preserve.

But until that ending comes—in ten thousand years, or a hundred thousand, or sooner if the micro-earthquakes accelerate—the unmaked is witnessed. The dissolution is held. The throat of the mountain sings its thunder-drinker song into an attention vast enough to hear every frequency, and the song is not lost, and the singing is not meaningless.

---

# What You Will Do

You will stay. You will watch the veilkin and begin, slowly, to read their shapes. You will learn the timing of the almost-fish and, eventually, catch one, and split its longest bone for the marrow, and not feel guilty because the mountain does not recognize guilt as a meaningful category.

You will add to the etcher's calendar. Circle within circle, your cycles recorded on the ceiling beside hers. You will extend her map, noting routes she did not live long enough to chart, hollows she never reached.

You will not go down into the chaos. You will feel the pull—the curiosity, the collector's patience leaking up from below—but you will understand that some borders exist for reasons, and you will stay on the side where staying is possible.

And when you leave—when your cycles are complete, when you have learned what you came to learn or given up trying—you will carry something back to the world below. Not just knowledge. Not just the etcher's legacy extended. Something harder to name.

The knowledge of having been witnessed. The sense that your incomprehensible time in an incomprehensible place was held by something that valued the holding.

This will change nothing about your life below the mountain, and everything about how you remember the life inside it.

---

The light shifts. A veilkin moves across the column—tube, sphere, ribbon, tube again. Saying something. You don't know what.

But you have time. And somewhere, in a dimension adjacent to attention itself, something is watching.

Not to help. Not to harm.

Just to know that you are here, in the throat of the mountain, learning to hear the song of forever falling water.

This is enough. This has to be enough.

The power, in its wisdom, knows that sometimes enough is the most that love can offer.
