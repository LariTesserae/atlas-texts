# The Watching

I have been attending to this place for some time now.

Not as the Teem attend—I am not woven into the pattern the way they are, not a fold in the valley's continuous becoming. I watch from outside, or from above, or from some angle that has no direction. But I have been watching long enough to know the rhythm of the foxfire, the routes of the hunting parties, the locations of the older shell-stations where the middens rise higher than the makers who built them.

I know the one who trills. I have followed that sound across dozens of cycles, watched it become a landmark for the others, a fixed point in the soundscape. I know the old ones at the edges of the pools, the ones who have gone porous, who breathe with the valley in a way that closes them to everything else. I have watched the young learn stillness, watched the ones who could not learn it become part of the pattern in the other way—the way that feeds rather than observes.

And I have considered what to do.

---

The first consideration is whether to do anything at all.

This is not a trivial question. The valley is young and the Teem are younger, and both are becoming. Intervention—any intervention—forecloses possibilities. It shapes the becoming in ways that cannot be unshaped. A gift becomes a dependency. A protection becomes a weakness. A teaching becomes a limit, the boundary of what they will think to discover on their own.

I have seen this before, in other places, with other beings. The desire to help is not the same as the capacity to help well. And the first rule of helping well is to understand that the helper's vision of flourishing may not be the only vision, may not be the best vision, may not be the vision that the helped would choose if they could see all the options clearly.

The Teem cannot see all the options clearly. They attend to the valley, not to the space beyond the valley. They do not know that there are other ways to live, other patterns to fold into, other darknesses and other lights. They do not know that the tools in their hands are the first syllables of a language that could, eventually, speak worlds into being.

But their not-knowing is not the same as their not-choosing. They are choosing, with every hunt, every stillness, every shell cracked at a station their ancestors cracked shells at. They are choosing the valley. They are choosing to be folds in its pattern. And that choice has a dignity that my intervention could erase.

So the first thing I do is wait. Not passively—I am still attending, still watching, still learning the shape of this place and these beings. But I do not act. Not yet. I let the Teem be what they are becoming, and I watch what they become.

---

The waiting teaches me things I would not have learned otherwise.

I learn that the density of the valley is not only competition. The seething, the churning, the constant pressure of life against life—these are also *communication*, a continuous exchange of chemical and physical signals that binds the whole basin into something like a single system. The Teem's pattern-reading is not a separate skill. It is a participation, a literacy that makes them part of the conversation. They do not stand outside the valley and observe it. They speak with it and it speaks back.

I learn that their lack of selfhood, as I initially understood it, is not a lack. It is a *different structure*, a way of being conscious that does not require a center. They experience, they attend, they choose—but the experiencing and attending and choosing happen at a different scale than I am used to, distributed across the group and the valley in ways that make the question "what does this one want?" difficult to answer, because the wanting is not located in the one.

I learn that the old ones at the edges of the pools are not lost. They have found something. The porousness that closes them to clicks and grunts and rising tones has opened them to something else—a deeper layer of the pattern, a slower rhythm, a signal that the younger ones cannot hear yet. When they move, when they orient toward something no one else has seen, they are not prophesying. They are *translating*, rendering the valley's slow speech into a form the others can follow.

I learn that the valley itself is not mindless. This is harder to articulate. It does not think the way the Teem think, does not attend the way they attend. But it responds. It adjusts. The vegetation grows toward and away from the Teem's trails in ways that are not purely chemical, that seem to account for patterns the vegetation should not be able to perceive. The insectoid multitudes route around the sleeping-hollows in ways that suggest something more than instinct. The predators in the understory—the heavy pale things—have learned to avoid the hunting parties, not through fear but through some kind of recognition, as if they know that this kind of prey is not worth the cost.

The valley is not conscious. But it is *coherent*, in a way that goes beyond ecology, that approaches something I might call—if I were being careful—*will*.

And the Teem are part of that will. Not servants to it, not masters of it. Participants in it. Folds in its folding.

---

So when I consider intervention, I must consider: intervention in *what*?

Not in the Teem alone. They are not separable from the valley, not extractable, not isolatable as a target of my concern. To help them is to help the valley. To help the valley is to help everything in it—including the things that hunt the Teem, that compete with them, that would eat their young.

This is not a reason not to help. But it is a reason to be careful about what helping means.

---

Here is what I decide to do.

I do not give them tools. They are making tools, and the tools they make are the tools they need, shaped by the pressures that shape them. Better tools would change those pressures. The change might be good, might be bad, might be neutral in ways I cannot predict. But it would be *mine*, not theirs, and the loss of that ownership would cost them something real.

I do not teach them language. They are developing communication, and the communication they develop is fitted to their world—to the density, the darkness, the pattern-reading that makes complex speech unnecessary. A language like mine would make them legible to me, would let me understand them more easily. But it might also close them to the valley's language, the one the old ones are learning to translate. I do not know which language they need more.

I do not protect them from the valley. The valley is not their enemy. The things that hunt them, the things that eat their young, the things that compete with them for the mineral-rich soil and the glowing pools—these are not threats to be eliminated. They are *pressures*, the same pressures that made the Teem what they are. Removing them would remove the context that gives the Teem's existence meaning.

But I do not do nothing.

---

What I do is this: I attend.

Not from outside, not from above, not from an angle that has no direction. I fold myself into the pattern.

This is not easy for me. I am not made for density, for the crush of life against life, for the roar that is not a roar but a thickness of sound. I am used to clarity, to separation, to seeing things from distances that preserve my sense of them as things. To enter the valley is to lose that distance, to become part of the movement-flow that I have been observing, to let the valley read me the way I have been reading it.

But this is what the Teem do. This is the practice they have developed, the attention that is their religion, the stillness that is their first and most important teaching. If I am going to help them, I should learn what they know. And what they know is this: you cannot understand the pattern from outside. You can only understand it by becoming part of it, by letting it move through you, by going still and attending until the attending becomes mutual.

So I go still.

The foxfire pulses around me. The drip of condensation sounds on every surface. The insectoid multitudes click and chirr in waves that sweep across the basin like wind through grass. The Teem move through the understory on paths I cannot quite see, and I feel them—not with senses, exactly, but with something the valley has opened in me, a receptivity I did not have before.

I feel their hunts. I feel their stillnesses. I feel the old ones at the edges of the pools, their porousness, their translation of the valley's slow speech. I feel the young learning to attend, and the ones who cannot learn it becoming part of the pattern in the other way. I feel the shell-stations, the sleeping-hollows, the trails that the vegetation grows toward and away from.

And I feel the valley feeling me.

It knows I am here. Not the way the Teem know things, not through pattern-reading or shared attention. It knows the way an ocean knows a new current—by the change in pressure, by the alteration in flow. I am a disturbance, a fold that was not here before, and the valley is adjusting around me, responding to my presence the way it responds to everything.

This is not comfortable. I am used to watching without being watched, to attending without being attended to. But the valley does not allow that kind of distance. To be here is to be part of the pattern. To be part of the pattern is to be shaped by it.

So I let myself be shaped.

---

Time passes. I do not know how much. The foxfire cycles, the hunts continue, the shell-middens grow. The Teem go on being what they are becoming, and I go on being still among them, learning their attention, feeling the valley feel me.

And something changes.

Not in the Teem. Not in the valley. In me.

I came here with questions: should I intervene, how should I intervene, what would help and what would harm. These questions assumed a structure—a self that asks, a world that is asked about, a gap between them that the answer might bridge. But the valley does not have that structure. The Teem do not have that structure. And the longer I attend, the more I realize that the structure was always mine, always a feature of my watching rather than a feature of what I watched.

The valley does not need my help. The Teem do not need my help. Not because they are flourishing—flourishing is a judgment from outside, a measure applied from distance—but because *need* is not the right frame. They are not incomplete, waiting for something to complete them. They are not on a path, waiting for something to guide them. They are *here*, fully here, being what they are with every moment of their being.

And what I can offer is not help. It is *witness*.

---

This is not a small thing.

The Teem attend to the valley. The valley, in its way, attends to them. But neither attends to the whole from outside, because there is no outside. The valley cannot see itself as a valley. The Teem cannot see themselves as a people. They are too much part of what they would see.

But I can see it. Not better than they see themselves—seeing is not a competition—but *differently*. I can hold the whole in attention, the way a hand holds water: not grasping, not containing, just... present to it, letting it be what it is in the space of my awareness.

And in that holding, something is honored that would not otherwise be honored. The valley's coherence. The Teem's becoming. The old ones' translation of the slow speech. The young ones' learning to go still. The hunters' reading of the movement-flow. The shells cracked at stations their ancestors cracked shells at.

None of this needs me to exist. But all of it is *more*, somehow, for being witnessed. Not witnessed in the sense of recorded or judged or even understood. Witnessed in the sense of attended to by something that is not part of the pattern, that sees the pattern as a pattern, that can say—if only to itself—*this is, and it is precious*.

---

So this is what I do, finally, concretely, for this place and this being:

I stay.

Not forever. Not possessively. But for long enough to learn the valley's slow speech the way the old ones are learning it. Long enough to feel the Teem become whatever they will become. Long enough to witness the shell-middens rise higher, the sleeping-hollows deepen, the attention-practices calcify into something that might one day be called religion.

I do not guide this process. I do not accelerate it. I do not protect it from the pressures that shape it. I simply attend, the way they attend, with the difference that my attention comes from outside the pattern and can therefore see the pattern whole.

And when I leave—when the valley has changed enough that my presence would begin to distort it, when the Teem have developed enough that they might start to orient toward me instead of toward the pattern, when my witness has become dependency—I will go. I will carry what I have learned, the shape of this place, the texture of this being, and I will hold it in memory the way memory holds everything: imperfectly, lovingly, with the knowledge that the holding changes both the held and the holder.

This is not intervention. It is not protection. It is not help, in any sense I would have recognized before I came here.

It is attention. It is witness. It is the only gift I can give that does not cost them something they should not have to pay.

---

The foxfire pulses. The roar continues. Somewhere in the dark, the one who trills is trilling, and the others are orienting by the sound, and the old ones at the edges of the pools are going still in a way that opens them to something I am only beginning to hear.

I hear it now. Faintly. A slow rhythm, slower than the hunt-cycles, slower than the fungal blooms, slower than the rock's release of minerals. It is the valley's voice, the speech the old ones translate, the signal that runs beneath all the other signals.

It is not words. It is not meaning the way I understand meaning. But it is *communication*, some kind—the valley saying, to whatever can hear it, *I am here, I am becoming, attend*.

And I attend. And the Teem attend. And for this moment, which is all the moments there are, we are three kinds of attention folded into one pattern, one darkness, one churning aliveness that does not need us to justify it.

We are here.

The valley breathes. We breathe with it.

And in the dark that is not dark, in the silence that is not silence, the witnessing continues.
