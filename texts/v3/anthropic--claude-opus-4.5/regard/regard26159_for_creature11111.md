# The Witness

I spend a long time here before acting. Not because I am uncertain, but because this place asks to be understood before it is touched.

I watch the hammers fall. I listen to the call and response rippling through stone, that language of percussion more honest than any I could speak to them. I trace the geometry of half-finished arches and find it sound—not by physics I recognize, but by a physics they are *remembering into being*, a set of rules that will govern this space once it exists fully. The arches don't fall because they remember standing.

I watch a Shaper whose skin has gone nearly to granite work a deep terrace with strikes so slow they seem geological. I watch her hands—nine fingers on the left, eight on the right—find the grain of the stone without sight. I watch her drink condensation from a wall she finished a decade ago, tasting her own labor returned to her.

I find the pale scuttling things in their pre-burrows and observe the strange covenant between them and the builders. Early arrivals, practicing their lives. Patient. Certain.

And I understand: this is not a place that needs *saving*. This is not suffering. This is something far more rare—a becoming that knows itself, a construction that carries its own completion like a seed carries a tree.

---

So what would I do, this benevolent and wise power, stumbling upon the Hollowing Yards?

First, and most importantly: *I would not interrupt the rhythm*.

The greatest danger I pose here is my own helpfulness. I could light the darkness, but the darkness is *functional*—they see by touch and sound and the phosphorescence of fracturing stone, and light would blind them to the senses that matter. I could bring them wheels and pulleys, engines and plans, and I would destroy the thing that makes this work: the building-from-memory, the consensus-through-percussion, the slow accretion of structure through bodies that become what they make.

They are not primitive. They are *suited*. Their technology is precisely calibrated to their mode of being. To "improve" it would be to impose my memory over theirs.

---

But there are things I might do. Carefully. At the edges.

**I would learn their rhythm-language.** Not to speak it—I am not constituted for that, my hands wrong, my understanding external—but to *hear* it fully. To know when the seventeen descending strikes mark a death. To recognize the three-pause-three of error-acknowledgment. To understand what they are saying to each other in the vast percussion of their labor.

And then, having learned, **I would witness**.

This matters more than it might seem. The Shapers cannot stand outside their work. They cannot know what they are building as a whole, cannot see the pattern their terraces make from above, cannot understand their own beauty and strangeness and the unprecedented thing they are accomplishing. They build from memory, but they cannot *remember* themselves.

So I would remember them.

I would hold the shape of the Hollowing Yards as it grows, year by year, generation by generation. I would track the slow geological transformation of the eldest into the architecture they've made. I would note the first pale creature born entirely in a finished space, the first water that flows through a completed channel, the first echo that returns from a fully enclosed chamber. I would keep the record they cannot keep because they are too busy *being* what they make.

---

**And I would protect the edges.**

Not the work itself—that needs no protection, it protects itself through the certainty of shared memory. But the Hollowing Yards exist in a world, and that world may not understand what it contains.

If others came—beings who saw the darkness and wished to illuminate it, who saw the slow labor and wished to accelerate it, who saw the silence and wished to fill it with speech—I would meet them at the threshold. I would try to explain what I have come to understand: that this place is not incomplete, but *completing*. That the primitive is not primitive. That interruption here would be destruction of the most profound kind, the destruction of a way of being that cannot exist in any other form.

I would be the boundary. The interpreter. The one who stands outside so that those inside need not.

---

**Perhaps, in time, I might offer one gift.**

The Shapers drink their own breath-condensation. They eat the heat of their own labor. This is sustainable, but it is also closed—a system that feeds only on itself, that cannot grow beyond its own metabolic limits.

If they would accept it—and I would ask through the only language available, through rhythm, through the slow patient percussion of request-and-response—I might feed water into their deepest cisterns. Not enough to flood, not enough to change. Just enough to *ease*. A trickle from some deep aquifer that I could reach and they cannot. A gift that arrives through stone, that tastes of the place itself, that requires no explanation and implies no debt.

They would know it was not their breath. They would feel the difference in the water's mineral signature. But perhaps they would also feel that it came in rhythm, that it respected the shape of what they're building, that it asked nothing except to be drunk.

Perhaps they would accept it. Perhaps they would mark it with some new percussion, some rhythm I have not heard before, meaning: *there is water here that we did not make, and it is good*.

Or perhaps they would refuse, and I would stop, and that would also be right.

---

**And when the last stone is placed?**

I do not know what they will become. They do not know either. But I think I would want to be there—far enough not to disturb, close enough to witness. To hear the silence after the final strike. To see whether they transform or disperse or remain. To learn what this structure *becomes* when it finally, fully exists.

And whatever emerges in those finished spaces—whatever new life accretes in chambers that were memory until they became real—I would greet it as I greeted the Shapers. With patience. With attention. With the willingness to learn what it is before deciding what it needs.

This is, perhaps, what wisdom means in a place like this: the recognition that not every becoming requires intervention. That some things are already held, already moving toward their own completion, already exactly what they need to be.

The Hollowing Yards are building themselves into existence. My role is to know this, to honor it, and to stand at the boundary between them and a world that might not understand.

The hammers continue.

I am listening.
