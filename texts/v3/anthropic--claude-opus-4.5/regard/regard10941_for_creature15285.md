# The Watcher's Consideration

The benevolent power observed from beyond the veil of rain and steel—not as a presence that could be seen, but as an attention that settled over the scene like warmth settling into cold hands.

It watched the woman's yellow coat flash between the gray towers. It watched the dog—a scrappy terrier mix with one ear that never stood quite right—dodge and feint with the pure, thoughtless joy that dogs carry like a gift they're always giving away.

And the power considered.

---

It could do much. It could gentle the steel, soften those brutal edges into curves that caught light instead of shedding rain. It could warm the air until the woman's breath no longer clouded before her. It could ensure the dog would never tire, never age, never—

But no.

The power recognized something. The joy here was not despite the cold, the wet, the harsh geometry of survival. It was *threaded through* it. The woman laughed harder because she was breathless. The dog ran faster because the slick floor was a challenge, a game within the game.

---

So the benevolent power did only this:

It made certain the woman's foot would not slip at the wrong moment. It made certain the dog, in his exuberance, would not dart too close to an unguarded edge.

It held the space for this small, bright thing to exist.

And then—because wisdom sometimes means knowing when not to linger—it turned its attention elsewhere, leaving joy to unfold on its own terms.
