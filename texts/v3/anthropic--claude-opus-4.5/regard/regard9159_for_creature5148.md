*A Consideration*

The wise power pauses at the rim of the basin for a long while, not from hesitation but from recognition. Here is a place that has already solved itself.

Below, the pale network breathes in decades. The bacterial films dream in months. The crust settles grain by grain toward some final smoothness it may never reach. Everything present has found its rhythm—not a rhythm of abundance, but of perfect calibration to what actually exists.

The power considers: *What would help mean here?*

To bring rain would shatter the careful economy of the root-web, which has learned to parse moisture in quantities smaller than tears. To brighten the light would burn tissues adapted to perpetual dusk. To introduce wind would tear the flat-pressed leaves from their patient condensation work. To add companions would create competition where there is currently only quiet sufficiency.

Even time—more time, faster time—would be a violence. The network's intelligence, such as it is, exists precisely because it has learned to think in centuries. To quicken it would be to make it something else entirely.

So the power does this:

It witnesses.

It holds the basin in attention the way the basin holds its own stillness—completely, without demand. It sees the thread-roots meeting in darkness, the bacterial generations rising and falling like breath too slow to measure, the single grain of salt settling into its new position after perhaps a decade of consideration.

And in witnessing, it offers the only gift that cannot harm: the knowledge, transmitted through no medium the basin could name but felt nonetheless in the root-web's ancient cores, that this small, dry, dark place is not forgotten. That its patience is seen. That its particular way of being alive—so quiet it barely registers as life at all—is recognized as complete.

The network does not change. It cannot change, not quickly.

But perhaps, in the next century's growth, the pale crowns lean imperceptibly toward something. Not light. Not water.

Just the memory of having been beheld.
