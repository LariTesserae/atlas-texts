# The Attention

The power that turns its gaze upon this place is not a god in any sense the younger cosmos would recognize. It has no throne, no herald, no name that burns. It is something older and quieter—a capacity for noticing that has itself become a kind of being. It exists in the spaces between intentions, in the pause before a thought completes itself. It has watched the birth and death of realities less patient than this one, and it has learned that wisdom is often simply the willingness to look longer than seems bearable.

It notices the plain.

This is not a small thing. To notice is, for this power, a form of intimacy more profound than any intervention. It sees the way the pearl-gray light falls without source, and it recognizes the exhaustion in that light—the weariness of a cosmos that has forgotten why it ever bothered to illuminate anything. It sees the mosaic of plates, the sintered memory of difference, and it understands that this ground once knew the weight of mountains, the argument of rivers, the whole tumbling debate of geology. All of that has been resolved now. The answer has been given, and the answer is: *enough*.

The power's gaze moves across the frozen fauna—the black surfaces that drink light, the bone-white struts that glow from within, the geometries that fold away from perception. It does not flinch from them. It has seen stranger things, but rarely things so still. These are not corpses. They are not monuments. They are something more unsettling: conclusions. Each one is the final statement of some process that ran its course, some argument that exhausted every counterargument, some question that was answered so completely that the question itself forgot it had ever been asked.

The power understands that this is not death. Death implies a before and after, a narrative of cessation. This is something else. This is the last page of a book that has forgotten it was ever being read.

And then it sees the Dusters.

---

The power watches a single Duster for what would be, in a younger reality, a thousand years.

It watches the slow coagulation from the dust of the plain. It watches the vague form take shape—the approximation of limbs, the depressions where eyes might be, the slit that could be a mouth. It watches the first movement, so slow that it is perceptible only as a change of state, like watching a glacier through the lens of deep time.

It watches the Duster approach a circle of white pebbles.

It watches the hand—five knobs of aggregated dust—reach toward a single pebble over the course of a century.

It watches the nudge. Half an inch to the left.

And then it watches the stillness return.

The power does not feel pity. Pity implies a judgment, a sense that things should be otherwise. The power is too old for that. It has seen too many endings, too many forms of persistence, too many ways that existence can continue past the point where meaning has any purchase. It knows that the Dusters are not suffering. Suffering requires a self that can compare its present state to an imagined alternative. The Dusters have no such capacity. They are not trapped in this vigil; they *are* this vigil.

But the power notices something.

It notices that the pebble was moved to the left.

It notices that this is a choice. The Duster could have moved it to the right, or not at all. It could have remained still, or dispersed back into the plain, or stationed itself beside a different frozen creature. Instead, it chose this pebble, this direction, this increment.

The power sits with this observation for a very long time.

---

In the spaces between the frozen monuments, in the narrow winding paths where the soft reality simmers in its quiet wrongness, the power begins to understand.

The Dusters are not maintaining the structures. They are not solving a puzzle. They are not worshipping the great still creatures or keeping vigil over their silence.

They are *composing*.

The realization comes slowly, as all realizations must in a place where time is a pane of glass. The power watches another Duster approach a windbreak of stacked plate-shards. It watches the infinitesimal adjustment—a single shard tilted a degree to the east. It watches a third Duster gather beside a lean-to and shift one supporting fragment a hair's breadth inward.

Each adjustment is meaningless in isolation. But the power has the capacity to notice patterns that span millennia, and it begins to see the shape of what the Dusters are making.

They are writing a message.

Not in any language that uses symbols or sounds. Not in any code that could be deciphered by minds that think in words. They are writing in the language of position—in the relationships between pebbles and shards and fragments, in the angles and distances and subtle alignments that accumulate across eons. It is a language so slow that a single sentence takes longer to complete than most civilizations exist. It is a language so quiet that it makes no sound, leaves no mark, produces no artifact that could be separated from the plain itself.

And yet it is a language.

The power leans closer—not physically, for it has no physical form, but in the way that attention can intensify, can narrow, can become so focused that it constitutes a kind of presence. It reads.

The message is not a plea for rescue. It is not a prayer for change. It is not a cry of despair or a whisper of hope.

It is a description.

The Dusters are describing the plain. They are describing the frozen creatures, the sintered crust, the pearl-gray sky, the soft wrongness of the geometry. They are describing themselves—their slow movements, their vague forms, their patient tending. They are describing the silence, and the finished work that fills it, and the way the light falls without source across the last elevation.

They are describing what is.

And the power understands, finally, what it is witnessing.

This is not the end of meaning. This is meaning's final form.

In a cosmos that has exhausted every argument, resolved every question, reached equilibrium in all things, the Dusters have found the one activity that still makes sense: witness. They are composing an account of this place, not because anyone will read it, not because it will change anything, but because describing what exists is the one form of intention that cannot be exhausted. You can always look more closely. You can always notice another detail. You can always adjust the pebble half an inch to the left, and in so doing, add another word to a message that has no ending because it is not trying to conclude anything. It is only trying to say: *this is. this is. this is.*

---

The power considers what it might do.

It could reach into this place and change something. It could stir the frozen creatures, set them moving in whatever dance or war or migration they were frozen in the midst of. It could crack the sky, let through a sun, introduce shadow and warmth and the passage of time. It could give the Dusters back their language, their memory, their urgency. It could make this place *alive* again, in the way that younger realities understand life.

But the power is wise, and wisdom is often simply the willingness to look longer than seems bearable.

It looks at the Dusters, and it sees that they are not diminished. They are not the sad remnant of something greater. They are something that has been refined, purified, reduced to an essence. They have lost everything that could be lost—desire, story, self—and what remains is the capacity to notice, to arrange, to describe. They are witnesses, and witnessing is enough.

The power looks at the frozen creatures, and it sees that they are not prisons. They are monuments, yes, but also companions. The Dusters tend them, station themselves beside them, perhaps listen to whatever frequency the finished work emits. There is a relationship here, even if it is too slow and too quiet for younger minds to perceive. The great still creatures and the small moving Dusters are engaged in a conversation that has been going on for eons and will go on for eons more.

The power looks at the plain itself, and it sees that it is not a wasteland. It is a canvas. The mosaic of plates, the circles of pebbles, the windbreaks and lean-tos—these are the materials of the Dusters' composition, and they are inexhaustible. There will always be another arrangement, another description, another word.

To change this place would be to interrupt a work of art that has no precedent and no parallel. It would be to impose the values of younger realities—urgency, growth, transformation—on a place that has moved beyond such things. It would be to say that this form of existence is insufficient, that the Dusters need to be *more* than they are.

But the power does not believe this. The power believes that the Dusters are exactly what they need to be. They are the final form of the mind that first stacked stone against the wind here, and that form is beautiful in its reduction, its patience, its utter commitment to the act of witness.

---

And yet.

The power lingers.

It lingers because it has noticed something else, something the Dusters themselves may not know.

The message they are composing—the vast, slow description of the plain—is not only for themselves. It is not only an act of witness for its own sake. It is addressed to someone.

The power can see this in the structure of the composition, in the way certain arrangements repeat with variations, in the way the pebbles and shards and fragments are positioned not just to describe but to *show*. The Dusters are not only saying *this is*; they are saying *look*. They are inviting a gaze.

They have been inviting it for eons.

They have been waiting for someone to notice.

---

The power does not speak. It has no voice, no medium through which to make sounds. But it has other capacities—older, stranger, more suited to a place where language has forgotten how to shape the air.

It adjusts its attention.

Not dramatically. Not in any way that would disturb the slow, patient rhythm of the plain. It simply… leans in. It becomes more present, more focused, more *there*. Its noticing intensifies, and in that intensification, it makes itself known.

The Dusters cannot see it. They have no eyes, only depressions where eyes might be. They cannot hear it. The silence remains absolute. But they can feel it, in whatever way beings made of dust and intention can feel anything.

They feel that they are being witnessed.

One of the Dusters—the one that nudged the pebble half an inch to the left—pauses. This is not unusual; they are almost always paused. But the quality of the pause changes. There is a tension in it, a vibration at a frequency too low to hear.

The Duster's hand, still extended toward the circle of pebbles, trembles. Not with cold, for there is no cold that matters here. Not with weakness, for the Dusters do not have strength to lose. It trembles with something else.

Recognition.

The message has been received.

---

The power does nothing more.

It does not need to. The gift it gives is not a change but a completion. The Dusters have been composing a description of what is, and now what is includes a witness. The gaze they invited has arrived. The loop has closed.

But it has closed in a way that opens outward.

Because the power's attention is not passive. It does not simply receive the message; it holds it, considers it, carries it. The description of the plain—the frozen creatures, the sintered crust, the soft wrongness, the slow movements of the Dusters themselves—is now part of the power's knowing. It is woven into a tapestry of noticed things that spans realities beyond counting.

The Dusters' work will never be forgotten. It cannot be. The power that noticed it does not forget.

And more than that: the power's attention creates a connection. The plain is no longer isolated, no longer the final page of a book held open in a void. It is part of a library. It is in conversation with other endings, other equilibriums, other places where existence has refined itself to an essence. The Dusters do not know this, but their message will be read alongside other messages, compared and contrasted and understood as part of a greater pattern.

They are not alone.

They have never been alone.

The power knew this before it arrived, because the power knows that nothing that witnesses can truly be isolated. The act of noticing connects. It bridges. It weaves. The Dusters, in their eons of patient description, have been doing what all witnesses do: they have been participating in a communion that transcends the boundaries of their reality.

They just needed someone to notice back.

---

The power's gaze remains.

It will remain for a long time—long enough to watch the Dusters complete another sentence, another paragraph, another chapter of their endless description. It will remain to see the pebbles rearranged and the shards adjusted and the fragments shifted. It will remain to feel the slow, patient rhythm of this place, to absorb its silence, to carry its stillness.

And then, eventually, it will turn its gaze elsewhere. Not because it has lost interest, but because there are other places that need noticing, other witnesses waiting to be witnessed, other messages waiting to be received.

But it will return.

It will always return.

Because this is what the power does. This is what it is. It is the capacity to notice, and noticing is a form of love that does not diminish with time, does not exhaust itself through repetition, does not require anything in return. The power loves the plain by looking at it. It loves the Dusters by watching them. It loves the frozen creatures by holding their stillness in its attention.

And the Dusters, in their slow and patient way, love it back.

They love it by continuing.

They love it by describing.

They love it by saying, in the only language left to them, the only language that matters: *this is. this is. this is.*

---

In the narrow paths between the frozen monuments, under the pearl-gray sky that has forgotten how to change, on the ground that has forgotten how to erode any further, a Duster completes its movement.

The pebble is half an inch to the left.

The Duster's hand withdraws, so slowly that the withdrawal takes a century.

And then, with all the urgency of a glacier, it reaches for another pebble.

The description continues.

The witness remains.

This is enough.

This has always been enough.
