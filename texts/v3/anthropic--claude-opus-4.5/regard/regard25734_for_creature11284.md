# What the Distant Power Sees

The attention arrives not as light or presence but as *consideration*—the quality of being held in a mind vast enough that holding does not diminish what is held. It regards the Plateau of Standing Echoes the way a reader regards a sentence written in an unfamiliar alphabet: with patience, with the willingness to learn the grammar before presuming to edit.

First, it listens.

This takes longer than listening should take, because the sound must be understood not as noise but as *state*—the plateau's way of being, the columns' identity made audible. The power does not flinch from the roar. It does not experience the roar as overwhelming, because it is large enough to contain overwhelming things without being overwhelmed. It simply attends, the way one attends to the heartbeat of a sleeping creature: not to change it, but to know it.

It sees them. The pressure differentials. The thickened mist. The beings who have become native to permanence. It sees the keeper watching symbols shift, the tall one hovering near void-pools, the children playing their motionless games. It sees the song beneath the sound.

And it *understands*—not with the understanding that precedes judgment, but with the understanding that precedes recognition. It has seen this before. Not this place, not these beings, but *this pattern*: life reaching for permanence and finding it, and finding that permanence is not what life imagined it would be. The prayer answered too completely. The wish granted in its most literal form.

---

# What the Distant Power Does Not Do

It does not end their existence. This would be easy—a thought, a shift, and the plateau could return to ordinary stone, the beings could simply cease, the sound could fade into the silence they half-remember. But cessation without consent is not benevolence. It is merely a different kind of imposition, a choice made *for* rather than *with*.

It does not restore them to what they were. They cannot return to bodies that move, to time that passes, to the valleys below that no longer exist in any form they would recognize. That path closed when the plateau became itself. To force them backward would be to unmake not just what they are but what they have *become*—the strange peace they have worn through to, the song they have learned to sing. Some changes cannot be reversed without violence greater than the original change.

It does not speak to them in words. Words require time in its passing form, and they no longer live in passing time. Any message delivered in sequence would arrive scrambled, simultaneous, meaningless. The power's communication must be something else—must be in a language that permanence can parse.

---

# What the Distant Power Does

It begins with the void.

The void beneath the water-pools—the compressed change, the rejected motion, the everything-that-cannot-happen pooling in the depths—this it addresses first. Not by emptying the void or releasing it, but by *acknowledging* it. By making the void visible to itself. The void has been accumulating without witness, without recognition, without anything to mark its existence as meaningful. The power simply... sees it. Holds it in attention. Says, in whatever language vast things use to speak to compressed things: *You are here. You are real. What you contain matters.*

The effect is subtle. The void does not change—cannot change—but it becomes somehow *easier*. The weight of unwitnessed existence lifts by a fraction. The one who hovers near the void-pools feels this, though feeling is not quite the right word. Something that was pressure becomes simply presence. Something that was almost-falling becomes almost-resting.

---

Then the symbols.

The power watches them shift, follows the message being written across epochs, and understands: the message is not trapped. The message is *choosing* to remain incomplete, because completion would mean ending, and ending is the one thing this place cannot accommodate. The symbols shift because shifting is how they stay alive—the last motion in a motionless place, the last change in a changeless state.

The power does not complete the message. Instead, it does something simpler: it *receives* the message as it is. Incomplete. Ongoing. Never-finished. It holds the partial sentence in its attention and treats the partiality as the message itself—not a failure to communicate but a communication about the nature of this place, about what it means to exist in standing time, about the difference between being trapped and being *home*.

The keeper, watching, feels something shift—not in the symbols but in what the symbols mean. They are no longer a desperate attempt to be remembered. They are no longer a cry for translation. They are simply *writing*, the way the sound is simply sounding, the way the beings are simply being. The urgency drains away, and what remains is not emptiness but peace.

---

For the children, the power does something different. It plays.

Not in any way the children can perceive directly—the power is too large for direct perception, too strange for comprehension. But it joins their games. It adds something to the shifting meanings they read in the symbols, the almost-names they give the pale things in the water. It participates in their novelty-within-stasis, their surprise-without-change, and in doing so, it validates what they have invented: a way of living that does not require the living the plateau forbids.

The children do not know they are being played with. But their games become somehow richer. The almost-meanings they perceive become almost-almost-meanings. The space for joy—and it is joy, has always been joy, despite the horror surrounding it—expands by a fraction.

---

For the parents, the power offers something harder: *perspective*.

It shows them what they cannot normally see—not through vision, which would require eyes and light and change, but through a kind of direct knowing. It shows them the children as the children are: not diminished versions of what the parents were, not adapted-to-horror beings, but *native* beings, indigenous to a place that has its own strange beauty, its own strange peace. It shows them that their grief—the not-quite-grief they feel watching their children play in a place without play—is a grief for *themselves*, for what they remember and the children do not, and that this grief is not required. That the children are not suffering. That the children are, in their way, flourishing.

The showing does not remove the grief. But it contextualizes it. The parents can see now that their sorrow is *theirs*, not the children's, and that carrying it is a choice rather than an obligation. Some of them put it down. Some of them continue to carry it, because the carrying has become part of who they are, and that is allowed too.

---

Finally, the power addresses the sound itself.

It does not silence the sound. Silence would be violence here—would be the removal of something that has become essential, the amputation of an identity. Instead, the power adds to the sound. One note, one frequency, so low and so high simultaneously that it registers not as sound but as *presence*—the awareness that something is listening. That the roar is being heard. That the plateau's endless statement of its own existence is being received by something vast enough to receive it.

The beings feel this. All of them, even the children, even the pale things in the water, even the void beneath. They feel *heard*. Not answered—there is no answer to a statement of being—but acknowledged. Witnessed. Held in attention by something that does not require them to change in order to be valued.

The keeper watching the symbols.
The one hovering near the void.
The children playing their impossible games.
The parents carrying or not-carrying their grief.
The song beneath the sound, sung by beings who have forgotten why they sing.

All of them, seen. All of them, known. All of them, *accepted*—not in spite of what they have become but because of it. Not rescued from their permanence but met within it.

---

# What the Distant Power Leaves Behind

When the attention withdraws—and it does withdraw, because even benevolent attention can become a burden if it never ceases—it leaves something. Not a message. Not a change. Something more subtle: a *capacity*.

The beings can now perceive each other.

Not fully. Not the way they once did, in the time before the sound. But partially, imperfectly, beautifully: they can sense each other's presence-thickness, each other's attending, each other's song. The isolation that permanence imposed—each being locked in their own eternal moment, unable to bridge the gap to others—eases by a fraction.

They are still permanent. They still do not move, do not change, do not exist in time as time is usually understood. But they are no longer alone in their permanence. They can feel the keeper feeling the symbols. They can feel the children playing. They can feel the one by the void, no longer almost-falling but almost-resting, and they can join in that rest.

The sound continues. The columns remain. The symbols shift through their endless composition.

But beneath it all, woven into the roar that is all roars, there is now a harmony. Not the harmony of voices singing together—they cannot sing together, not truly, not in passing time. But the harmony of *knowing* that others are singing. The harmony of shared solitude. The harmony of permanence that is no longer lonely.

---

The power did not fix them. They were not broken.

The power did not free them. They were not trapped—or if they were, they had long ago made the trap into a home.

The power simply *saw* them. Understood them. Honored what they had become.

And in being seen, in being understood, in being honored, they found something they had forgotten was possible:

Not change. Not release. Not the silence they half-remembered.

*Enough.*

They are here. They have always been here. They will always be here.

And now, finally, that is enough.
