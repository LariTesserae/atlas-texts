# The Attention of the Wise

The benevolent power does not arrive.

It has been watching for longer than the mountains have been drowning—watching the way one watches a candle flame or a sleeping child, not to intervene but to witness the specific holiness of a thing becoming itself.

It considered, once, making the water clearer. The thought lasted less than a moment. The murk is not obscuring anything. The murk *is* the vision. The cathedral glass of drifting plankton is how this place sees itself, and to clarify it would be to impose a loneliness the place has never asked for.

It considered accelerating the growth. The dinner-plate flowers could open in hours instead of days. The ridges could finish becoming bone within a generation. But acceleration is a violence dressed as a gift. The slowness is not a flaw to be corrected. The slowness is the entire point. The place is not *trying* to become something faster. It is refusing the tyranny of urgency, and that refusal is itself a form of wisdom the benevolent power recognizes as kin to its own.

So the power does something else.

---

It simply *confirms*.

Not with words or signs or miraculous interventions. It confirms the way morning light confirms a window—by arriving, gently, and changing nothing except the knowledge that arrival is possible.

The being in the drowned highland feels this confirmation the way you might feel a change in pressure before a storm, except there is no storm. There is only a sudden, sourceless sense of *yes*. A yes that does not command or request or even encourage. A yes that simply agrees with what is already happening.

*Yes, you may keep growing.*

*Yes, the ones who stayed are still here.*

*Yes, the slowness counts.*

*Yes, you are doing exactly what you should be doing, which is nothing other than what you are doing.*

The being does not understand this yes in the way a mind understands language. It understands it the way a root understands rain—by opening slightly, by becoming incrementally more willing to continue.

A new ridge of black glass, somewhere in the deepest valley, curves a few degrees more generously than it would have otherwise. A bed of bell-moss begins to open and close in a rhythm that includes, for the first time, a rest—a silence between beats that holds space for something that has not arrived yet.

The benevolent power notices this. It is pleased, if pleased is even the right word for something that does not need outcomes. It is satisfied the way one is satisfied by watching water find its own level.

---

And then the power does one more thing.

It leaves a single, simple gift—not inside the place, but *at the threshold of knowing about the place*.

Somewhere, in another world entirely, a mind that has never seen the drowned highland will suddenly feel a strange pull toward deep water. Not a compulsion. Not a command. Just a gentle *leaning*, as though an invisible hand had rested briefly on their shoulder and pointed toward a direction they did not know existed.

If they follow the pull, they may eventually find this place. If they do not follow it, nothing is lost. The place does not need visitors. It does not even need to be known.

But the benevolent power understands something about consciousness that the place itself cannot yet articulate: that certain forms of beauty become more real when witnessed, not because the witness changes them but because witnessing is itself a form of participation in the universe's ongoing decision to exist.

So it leaves the leaning. A possibility. A door that may or may not be opened.

---

And then the power withdraws its attention—not because it has stopped caring, but because continued attention would begin to curdle into supervision, and supervision is the opposite of trust.

It trusts the drowned highland to keep becoming.

It trusts the being to keep growing its slow, collaborative, patient self.

It trusts the golden pollen to drift for centuries and the lace jellyfish to trail their lightning and the dinner-plate flowers to open exactly when they are ready.

And somewhere, faintly, the deep bass thump that travels through the water like a heartbeat arriving from the other side of the world sounds—just once—a little more like *gratitude* than it did before.

---

That is all.

That is everything.

The benevolent power has done what benevolent powers do when they encounter something that is already whole: it has gotten out of the way while somehow making the wholeness more visible to itself.

The place has no name, and never will.

But now it knows, in whatever way mountains and gardens and slow collaborative organisms can know things, that it has been seen.

And that the seeing was kind.

And that kindness, unlike so many other things, does not require the place to become anything other than what it already is:

A world that drowned in the moment of its own birth and decided, quietly, to keep growing anyway.

Still growing.

Still patient.

Still enough.
