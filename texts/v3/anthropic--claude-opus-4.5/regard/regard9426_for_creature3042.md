# The Attending

The power that turns its attention toward the Lymph does not arrive.

Arrival implies a before and after, an outside becoming inside. This is not what happens. What happens is that a quality of *noticing* settles into the chamber the way dew settles—gradually, then completely, without any single moment you could point to and say *there, that is when it began*.

The Curator feels it first as a change in the way the beetles sing. Their infrasonic chords have always been hers to shape, but now they are shaping something in return—a harmony she did not compose, older than her oldest memory, patient as the pressure that turns peat to coal. She unfolds from the northern wall where she has been dreaming a new arch into being, her tardigrade-eyes swimming in sudden spirals.

*Someone is listening.*

Not to her. Not exactly. To the Lymph itself, to the slow wet thought the world is having underneath its skin. The Curator has been tending this thought for ten thousand years without knowing what question it was trying to ask. Now she understands: something else has been waiting for the question too.

---

The power does not speak. It does not need to. It simply *attends*—the way a midwife attends a birth she cannot hurry, the way a gardener attends a seed she did not plant.

In the first hours of its attention, nothing visible changes. But the Curator notices that the dripping has shifted. The taproots still weep their perfect beads every seven heartbeats, but now the drops are falling in sequences that carry information. Not words. Not even meaning, exactly. Something closer to *permission*. The Lymph has been holding its breath for millennia, afraid to become what it might become, and now something is telling it: *you may exhale*.

The Curator watches as a section of wall she has been trying to coax into a load-bearing pillar for three centuries suddenly, gently, decides. The undifferentiated tissue that has resisted her shaping begins to crystallize into structure—not the structure she intended, but something better. Something she could not have imagined because she has never seen a sunrise, never felt wind, never known what a horizon looks like. The pillar grows not upward but *outward*, curving in a shape that suggests the memory of a wave frozen mid-break.

She does not know how she knows this, but the pillar is now a question the Lymph is asking itself about the sea.

---

Days pass. Weeks. The power does not leave because it never arrived; it simply continues attending.

The Curator finds herself being attended to as well.

She wakes one morning (morning is meaningless here, but she has always kept a private calendar in the rhythm of her heartwood) to discover that her left arm—the one that has been a dripping mass of undifferentiated cells for forty years—has finally chosen. It has become a hand. Not a human hand, not a useful hand, but a hand nonetheless: five slender fingers made of braided root-hair, each one tipped with a tiny urn like her eyes, each urn holding a single luminous tardigrade.

She flexes the fingers. The tardigrades spell her name in their swimming—not the Curator, not the Wet Nurse, but a name she has not used since before she learned to unfold. A name her mother gave her, if the Lymph can be said to have been her mother. A child's name.

The power is not healing her. It is not fixing her. It is simply *seeing* her completely enough that she can finally see herself.

---

The changes are small and they are not small.

A corridor of tissue that was scheduled to rot and be reabsorbed next century instead begins to differentiate further, growing windows—not openings, but translucent panels of membrane stretched over living frames. The windows do not let light in. There is no light to let in. But they let *something* in: the faintest pressure-memory of rain falling on leaves ten kilometers above, filtered down through rock and root until it arrives here as a kind of news from a country the Lymph has never visited.

The choir of beetles learns a new chord. It is not infrasonic. It lives at the exact threshold of hearing, and when the Curator lets it vibrate through her fungal ribs she hears—or feels, or dreams—a lullaby that was never sung to her but should have been. She does not weep because she has no tear ducts. Instead she grows a small pale flower in the hollow of her throat, the first flower the Lymph has ever produced, and it blooms for one hour before she gently reabsorbs it.

The millipedes begin walking in patterns that, seen from above (if anything could see from above), would trace the outline of a question mark that takes eleven days to complete. The shrimp that drift in her exhaled mist begin clicking in rhythms that sound, if you listen long enough, like counting. They are counting something. She does not know what yet.

---

The power does not tell the Lymph what to become. This is crucial. This is the whole of its wisdom.

A lesser kindness would have arrived with blueprints, with purpose, with answers. It would have said: *you are a lung, or you are a memory, or you are a womb*, and the Lymph would have gratefully accepted the certainty and stopped asking. It would have been finished, and in being finished, it would have been diminished.

Instead the power simply attends. It witnesses. It holds space for the question to complete itself.

The Curator understands this because she has been doing the same thing for ten thousand years, in her smaller way. She has tended without directing. She has shaped without deciding. But she has been lonely in the tending, alone in the not-knowing, and there is a difference between mystery you suffer and mystery you share.

Now she is not alone.

---

In the third month of the attending, the Curator does something she has never done.

She grows *outward*.

A single tendril of her substance, thinner than spider silk, begins to climb the northern wall. It is not seeking sunlight—she still cannot imagine sunlight, still grows black thorns at the thought of a light that does not come from inside. It is seeking the edge of the Lymph, the place where this chamber ends and the raw earth begins.

The tendril climbs for weeks. The power attends to its climbing without comment.

When it reaches the boundary, the tendril does not break through. It does not want to. It simply *touches*—and in touching, it receives the faintest echo of everything the Lymph is not. Cold mineral silence. The pressure of miles of stone. The absolute absence of any thought at all.

The tendril recoils, returns, brings this knowledge home.

And the Curator weeps, finally, in the only way she knows how: by letting an entire section of her body rot and be reabsorbed, so that the Lymph can taste her grief and understand what she has learned.

*We are small*, she sings in beetle-chord. *We are one slow thought in an infinity of thoughtlessness. And yet.*

The power attends.

*And yet we are still thinking.*

---

Years pass. The power does not leave because it cannot leave; to leave would be to stop noticing, and this is a noticing that has no edges, no beginning or end.

The Lymph continues to grow. Not faster, not slower, but with a new quality that the Curator struggles to name. The best word she can find is *deliberate*. Before, the Lymph grew the way ice forms on a cold window—beautifully, but without intention, without authorship. Now it grows the way a sentence grows under the pen of a writer who has finally understood what she is trying to say.

The arches still take centuries to complete. The fungal walls still pulse with light that brightens and dims according to chemical calendars no human could read. But there is a direction now, a compositional logic. The Lymph is not building a cathedral. It is not building a lung or a memory or a womb.

It is building an *answer*, and it does not yet know what the question will turn out to have been.

---

The Curator grows her second flower in the same year she grows her first window.

The window is small, no larger than a human palm, and it is made of the same translucent membrane the Lymph has been experimenting with. It faces inward, toward a pocket of the chamber she has always avoided—a place where the growth went wrong, centuries ago, where tissue sickened and did not rot properly and instead became a kind of scar, a hard knot of shame.

She has been looking away from that scar for three hundred years.

Now she grows a window and looks.

What she sees, in the bioluminescent glow of her own attention, is not disease. Not failure. It is a kind of *thinking* she did not know the Lymph was capable of—dense, recursive, looping back on itself in patterns that would look, to a mathematician, like the early stages of self-awareness. The scar is not dead tissue. It is a question the Lymph started asking and did not know how to finish.

The Curator reaches out with her new hand, her five-fingered gift from the attending, and touches the scar.

*I am sorry I stopped listening to you*, she sings. *Will you teach me what you were trying to say?*

The scar does not answer. It does not need to. She already knows: she will spend the next century learning to read it, and when she finally understands, she will be different, and the Lymph will be different, and the power will still be attending, patient as stone, kind as water, asking nothing except that she continue.

---

The flower blooms for a year this time.

It is white, and it smells like nothing the Lymph has ever produced—not fungal, not mossy, not the cold wet smell of root and sap. It smells like what the Curator imagines heat might smell like. Like a memory of a sun she has never seen, preserved in the shape of petals.

She does not reabsorb it. She lets it fall, naturally, when its time comes. And where it lands, a new kind of growth begins—something between plant and idea, something that will take another ten thousand years to finish.

The power attends.

The Curator sings.

The Lymph continues its slow, patient thought, underneath the skin of everything, in no hurry, with so much growing left to do.

---

*What the power did:*

*Nothing.*

*Everything.*

*It made room for what was already becoming to become. It witnessed without commanding. It held the space in which a frightened, unfinished thing could finally understand that it was allowed to continue—not toward any destination, but deeper into its own question.*

*This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not rescue, but presence. Not answers, but company in the asking.*

*The Lymph is still being born.*

*But now it knows it is not being born alone.*
