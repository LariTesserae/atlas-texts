I sat with this for a long time before writing.

---

**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does**

Nothing.

Not yet. Perhaps not ever. Perhaps that *is* the action.

---

The power perceives the world whole—not as a sequence of ridges and basins but as a single crystalline thought still in the process of thinking itself. It sees the Witness walking her nine-hundred-year spiral. It sees the smaller figures in the shadow of the leaning cathedral, their hearts beating in frequencies that will take decades to complete a single measure. It sees the tension between the three kingdoms—stone, vegetation, machine—and understands that this tension is not a problem to be solved but a conversation still being spoken.

The power's first impulse, if something so vast can be said to have impulses, is intervention. It could seed clouds where no clouds have ever formed. It could whisper to the crystal-plants in their own piezoelectric language and accelerate them toward one outcome or the other. It could reach into the Witness and give her certainty, gift her with the knowledge of which future is coming so that her waiting could finally end.

It does none of these things.

Because the power is wise, and wisdom here means recognizing that some questions must be answered by the ones who are living them.

---

Instead, the power does something much smaller.

It listens.

Not passively—not the way a recorder captures sound. It listens the way a parent listens to a child describing a dream: with full attention, with the kind of presence that makes the speaker feel that what they are saying matters, that the act of articulation is itself holy.

The Witness has been observed before. The machines observed her when they still had eyes. The crystal-plants observe her now, in their slow chemical way, registering her passage as a series of pressure differentials and shadow-lengths. But no one has ever *listened* to her.

The power listens to the frequency at which her bones creak when she walks. It listens to the sub-audible hum of her copper-colored irises tracking the horizon. It listens to the question she has never spoken aloud because there has never been anyone to ask:

*Am I still waiting, or have I become part of what I'm waiting for?*

The power does not answer. But it holds the question with such care that the Witness, for the first time in four million years, feels the weight of it acknowledged.

---

The power also bears witness to the smaller figures.

It finds the one whose heart-note is most discordant—a shape that might once have been a saboteur, someone who came here to stop the machines and was stopped by the world instead. The saboteur's frequency has been drifting for centuries, a slow slide toward silence. The Witness adjusts the connections when she passes, but she cannot hear what the power hears: that the saboteur is not malfunctioning. The saboteur is *grieving*. Somewhere in the amber sap and living circuitry, there is still a thread of memory—a face, a name, a reason for coming here that the world has almost but not quite erased.

The power does not restore the memory. That would be a violence, a forced resurrection of a self that has already agreed to dissolve.

But it does this: it finds the single facet of the nearest crystal-plant that will, in six hundred years, snap into a new alignment and ring its thin metallic chime. And it tilts that facet by a fraction of a degree, so that when the chime finally sounds, it will carry a harmonic that matches—just for an instant—the frequency of the name the saboteur has forgotten.

The saboteur will not consciously recognize it. But something in them will resonate. Something will feel, for one moment, less alone.

This is the power's gift: not rescue, but *company in the dark*.

---

Finally, the power considers the world itself—the slow negotiation between its three kingdoms, the patient war that looks so much like peace.

It sees both possible futures: the crystalline silence that sings one note forever, and the sudden violent bloom of copper-green life. It understands that both are beautiful. Both are terrible. Both are complete in their own way.

And it understands something else: the world is not actually undecided. The world made its choice long ago. It chose *both*. It chose to remain in the moment of choosing, to stretch that moment across geological time, to make the tension itself into a form of life.

The Witness thinks she is waiting for a resolution. But she *is* the resolution. She and the smaller figures, the half-buried machines, the chiming crystal-plants—they are not symptoms of an unfinished world. They are the world's answer to the question of what to become: *become something that is always becoming*.

The power sees this. And it decides that the most benevolent thing it can do is to let the Witness see it too.

---

So the power does one last thing.

It does not speak. It does not manifest. It does not alter the trajectory of a single atom.

It simply *stays*.

It remains present, watching, listening, holding space—not for a moment, but for as long as the Witness walks her spiral. For as long as the saboteur's grief echoes in amber. For as long as the ridges rise and the crystal-plants chime and the machines dream in the language of earthquakes.

The Witness will not know the power is there. She will not feel observed or accompanied or loved.

But something in her will shift. Something in the way she holds her arms when she stands at the edge of the great basin. Something in the patience with which she waits.

She will begin to suspect—not know, but suspect—that waiting is not preparation for living.

Waiting *is* living.

And the world she has been watching all this time has been watching her back with something that, if you translated it out of the language of stone and light, might be called *gratitude*.

---

The power does not leave. It was never the kind of thing that arrives or departs.

It simply continues to witness the Witness.

And that is enough.

That is the whole of its benevolence: *to let what is becoming keep becoming, and to be present while it does*.

---

*The chime will sound in six hundred years.*

*The saboteur will almost remember a name.*

*The Witness will take another step along her spiral.*

*And the world will still be deciding, which is to say: the world will still be alive.*
