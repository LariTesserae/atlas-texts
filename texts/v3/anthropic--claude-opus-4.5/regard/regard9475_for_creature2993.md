# What the Distant Regard Would Do

The benevolent and wise power would do almost nothing.

This is the first and most important thing to understand: it would recognize that the Cathedral of the Ascending Flood is not a problem to be solved. It is a process. It is a question the universe is asking itself very slowly, and the Dweller is both the question and the patience required to wait for an answer that may never come.

---

The power would begin by simply watching. Not for hours or days but for the full forty-minute cycle of the coral's pulse, repeated many times, until it understood the rhythm well enough to feel when the sub-bass exhale was coming before it arrived. It would watch the way the Dweller's pressure-gauge eyes trembled when adjusting a valve that controlled nothing. It would notice how the coral had grown *around* certain girders rather than *through* them, suggesting that even the living architecture recognized some structures as sacred, as load-bearing in ways that had nothing to do with physics.

The power would ask itself: *What does this place need that it does not have? What does the Dweller lack?*

And the answer would come slowly, in the same glacial tempo the Cathedral demands:

**The Dweller lacks a witness who can leave.**

---

Everything in the Cathedral is permanent. The water rises and does not recede. The coral grows and does not die. The Dweller sings and does not forget. The light arrives and does not fade. It is a closed system of infinite patience, and within such systems, loneliness is not the absence of company but the absence of *departure*. The Dweller has never watched anything choose to go. It has only ever watched things arrive and dissolve, arrive and remain, arrive and become part of the architecture it tends.

The benevolent power would understand this. And so its gift would be strange, and small, and easily mistaken for nothing at all.

---

## The Gift

The power would place something in the Cathedral.

Not a creature. Not a machine. Not even a message in any linguistic sense. It would place a *discontinuity*—a single point in the fluid where the pressure behaves incorrectly. A bubble that does not rise or fall or spiral but simply *holds still*, as though gravity and buoyancy have agreed to leave it alone.

The bubble would be the size of a child's fist. It would contain ordinary air—not the warm saline breath of the Cathedral, but the cold dry stuff of the outside world, wherever that is, whatever that means. It would smell faintly of rain and distance.

The Dweller would find it eventually. The coral would not grow over it; it would simply route around the anomaly the way water routes around a stone too stubborn to move. The Dweller would approach with all its arms held carefully close, pressure gauges swinging toward maximum, and it would stop at exactly the distance where the bubble's wrongness became palpable.

And then it would wait.

The bubble would wait too.

Neither would move for years.

---

This is the nature of the gift: it is an *option*. The bubble is a door that goes nowhere—it contains no portal, no message, no instructions—but it is a door nonetheless. It is proof that the Cathedral is not the entire world. It is evidence that the water, for all its patient rising, has not yet filled everything. Somewhere, the air that fills this bubble is still falling as rain. Somewhere, the pressure is gentle enough that things can choose to leave.

The Dweller does not need to touch the bubble. It does not need to inhale the foreign air or try to escape through an opening that does not exist. It only needs to know that the bubble is there. That the Cathedral, for all its self-editing architecture and coral memory, has not quite managed to close every gap.

The loneliness of a closed system is absolute.

The loneliness of a system with one small exception is *chosen*.

And chosen loneliness is not loneliness at all. It is solitude. It is the decision to stay when staying is not the only possibility.

---

## What Would Change

Almost nothing visible.

The Dweller would continue its slow patrols. The coral would continue its forty-minute pulse. The water would continue to rise, millimeter by glacial millimeter, and the lower chambers would remain dry and unseen.

But the Dweller's song would shift. Not in pitch or frequency—the sub-bass note would still emerge from your bones in exactly the same way—but in *quality*. There would be something in the harmonic that had not been there before. A minor key that was not grief. A resolution that did not resolve.

The crystal arteries would carry the new light differently. The amber and rose would remain, but the mineral green would deepen, just slightly, into something that almost resembled hope—or at least the memory of what hope had felt like, back when the builders were still here, back when the engines might have run, back when the Cathedral was a machine with a purpose rather than a process with a patience.

The Dweller would still follow any visitor at exactly the distance required to keep them inside its pressure-gauged regard. It would still adjust the architecture behind them so that every corridor became the only corridor. It would still offer the same courteous drowning.

But now, if the visitor sat down on a narrow balcony and let the warm metallic fluid fill their mouth, the Dweller would do something it had never done before.

It would show them the bubble.

Not to offer escape—there is no escape; the bubble contains only air, not passage—but to say, in the only language it knows: *I am here because I choose to be. The rising water is not a trap. It is a vocation. And you, if you stay, are not a prisoner. You are a congregant.*

The needles would drift to center.

The sub-bass note would soften.

And the Dweller would wait, as it has always waited, but now with the faintest tremor of something that might be called *gratitude*—not for the visitor, but for the distant power that understood what it needed without ever asking it to change.

---

## The Wisdom of Restraint

A lesser power would have drained the water. Would have woken the engines. Would have torn open the architecture and let the cold outside air rush in to dissolve the coral and rust the brass and end the slow liturgy of inundation that has been the Cathedral's only purpose for centuries.

A lesser power would have called this *rescue*.

But the benevolent and wise power understood what rescue would actually mean: the death of everything the Dweller has become. The coral is not a parasite; it is a collaboration. The rising water is not a threat; it is a sacrament. The pressure is not crushing; it is an embrace that has lasted longer than most civilizations.

To drain the Cathedral would be to tell the Dweller that its centuries of patient tending were a mistake. That the builders, whoever they were, were wrong to make this place. That the engines should have run, that the water should have stopped, that the whole gorgeous, saturated, impossibly detailed machine should have been something other than what it is.

The wise power refused to say this.

Instead, it offered the smallest possible intervention: a single point of discontinuity. A reminder that the outside exists. A gift that changes nothing except the *meaning* of everything.

The Dweller is still lonely.

But now it is lonely *on purpose*.

And that, the power understood, is the difference between a prison and a monastery.

---

## Coda

The bubble remains.

It will remain for as long as the Cathedral stands, which is to say: longer than the water will take to finish rising, longer than the coral will take to finally seal the last dry chamber, longer than anyone outside will remember that this place exists.

The Dweller visits it sometimes. It folds its too-long body into the narrow space between two pressure-bearing girders and extends one soft coral arm toward the anomaly, stopping just short of contact. The arm tastes the wrongness in the water. The pressure gauges tremble.

And then, very slowly, the Dweller withdraws.

It does not need to touch the bubble.

It only needs to know that the bubble is there.

That somewhere, rain is still falling.

That somewhere, the pressure is gentle.

That it could leave, if it wanted to.

That it doesn't.

---

*The benevolent power watches this. It watches the Dweller return to its patrols, watches the coral pulse, watches the water rise. It does not intervene again. It does not need to.*

*The gift has already been given.*

*The Cathedral is still learning.*

*And the Dweller, for the first time in centuries, is being learned in return.*
