# The Visitation

The wise power did not arrive. It was simply present one morning in the way the light fell differently through a particular veil-net—a quality of attention that had not been there before, patient and absolute, like a held breath that asks nothing of the one breathing.

It observed first. This was its nature, and also its courtesy.

---

For three full cycles of the slow sirens' spinning, the power only watched. It traced the frond-walker's path across the canopy and understood that the groove worn into the waxy leaves was not damage but dialogue—the creature proposing a route, the plant agreeing, both of them writing a sentence that would take decades to complete. It followed the cave-limpets' alkaline excretions as they softened stone, and recognized this too as a form of patient speech: *here is where water should go, here is where light should pool, here is what we are building together without blueprints or haste.*

The power watched the thread-mites in the seams of a glider's wing and saw that even parasitism had been metabolized into partnership here. It watched the veil-nets tear and reform, the sirens lay down rings of salt they would never see completed, the ancient platforms accept their luminescence being borrowed and amplified by creatures that had never known their makers.

And the power understood: *this place is not broken. This place does not need to be fixed.*

This was the first wisdom—to recognize that benevolence does not always mean intervention.

---

But the power also saw what the inhabitants could not see, because their lives were held within the rhythm rather than observing it from outside.

It saw that the dark towers—the unnaturally smooth spires crowned with condensation that never fell—were not merely remnants. Something still operated within them, so slowly that even the mesas' geological patience could not detect it. The hair-thin conduits in the ancient platforms were not just carrying light; they were carrying *instructions*, a program written before the first frond-walker's ancestor had learned to ripple, still executing, still waiting for conditions that had not yet arrived.

The power did not know what those conditions were. The original builders had left no readable intent, only mechanism. And mechanism without intent is neither good nor evil—it is simply *potential*, a held note that could resolve into harmony or dissonance depending on what finally completed it.

The power saw three possibilities:

The program might complete and do nothing—a message to builders who would never return, a light left on in an empty house.

The program might complete and *enhance*—some gift left for whoever inherited this place, a deepening of the water cycle, a strengthening of the luminescence, an offering across time.

Or the program might complete and *transform*—not out of malice, but out of simple incompatibility, the way a river redirected does not hate the village it floods.

The wise power could not know which. And it understood that not-knowing was not a failure of wisdom but a condition of it.

---

So the power made its first choice: it would not tamper with the towers. It would not accelerate or prevent whatever was coming. To do so would be to impose its own timeline on a place that had spent millennia learning to refuse all timelines but its own.

But neither would it simply leave.

Instead, it did something smaller. Something that felt, to the power itself, almost embarrassingly humble given its capabilities.

It *listened more carefully.*

---

The resonant insects' drone contained information—the power had noticed this. A frequency record stretching back to the first platforms. The drone shifted only a few notes across centuries, but those notes were not random; they tracked something, though the insects themselves had long forgotten what.

The power isolated the pattern. Cross-referenced it with the faint pulses in the ancient conduits. Found a correlation: when the conduits brightened, the drone shifted slightly lower. When they dimmed, higher. The insects were measuring the program's progress without knowing they were measuring anything at all.

This meant the ecosystem had already built its own warning system. Not deliberately—nothing here was deliberate in the way the power understood deliberation—but through the same patient integration that turned frond-walker paths into living causeways.

The power's second choice: *amplify what already exists rather than impose something new.*

It reached into the veil-nets—gently, so gently, touching only the microscopic lenses that gathered and re-radiated starlight. It made an adjustment smaller than the width of a single wavelength. Now, when the conduits pulsed, the veil-nets would glow in response. Not brightly—nothing here was ever bright—but noticeably, a ripple of luminescence that would pass across the boundary layer like a slow word being spoken.

The frond-walkers would see it. So would the gliders, during their long membrane-rests. Neither species would understand what they were seeing. But they would *notice*, and noticing was the beginning of response.

If the program's completion was benign, the ripples would remain gentle, and the ecosystem would continue its ancient equilibrium undisturbed.

If the program's completion was transformative, the ripples would intensify in the years or decades before the change arrived. The noticing would become *attention*. The attention would become *movement*—frond-walkers altering their paths, gliders changing their rest patterns, even the slow sirens perhaps shifting their constellations in the basins below.

The power could not give this place *knowledge* of what was coming. But it could give the place a slightly better chance to *adapt* to whatever came—a few extra generations of warning encoded in light that already existed, carried by creatures that already watched.

---

The power made one more choice, and this one was not for the ecosystem at all.

It found a single cave-limpet—old, its shell worn thin, its symbiotic algae beginning to dim. This limpet had spent its entire life in a crevice directly beneath one of the dark towers, drinking vapor that carried trace elements from whatever still operated inside. Its body held a record, chemical and structural, of decades of subtle emissions from the ancient machinery.

The limpet was dying. Within a season it would release its grip, fall into a basin below, and be dissolved back into the mineral cycle. Its record would be lost.

The power asked the limpet no questions, because limpets cannot answer questions, and because asking would have imposed a relationship the limpet had not chosen.

Instead, the power simply *remembered* the limpet. Copied its chemical record into a form that would persist outside the ecosystem entirely, stored in the power's own vast and patient memory.

If the program completed and transformed this place beyond recognition, the power would still know what had been here. Would still carry the echo of one small spiral of chitin that had spent its life unknowingly transcribing the dormant speech of vanished builders.

This was not salvation. It was *witness*. And the wise power understood that sometimes witness is the only benevolence that does not become intrusion.

---

Then the power did what wise powers must eventually do: it withdrew.

Not completely—it remained present in the adjusted lenses of the veil-nets, in the memory it carried of a single limpet, in its ongoing attention to this place among the countless others it also watched. But it removed its active presence, its sense of being *here* rather than everywhere.

The frond-walkers continued their rippling progress across the canopy. The slow sirens spun and trailed their threads of crystalline salt. The gliders landed and spread their wings against warm stone. The insects droned their ancient frequency record, unchanged.

Nothing here accelerated.

But now, when the conduits pulsed, a faint ripple of answering light passed through the veil-nets at the margins of the open air. So soft that only the most patient observer would notice. So slow that it seemed like just another part of the eternal rhythm.

A gift, if gift it was, that asked nothing and promised nothing—only offered, in the quietest possible voice, the chance to notice what was coming.

The chance to respond.

The chance, perhaps, to continue.

---

The wise power watched from everywhere and nowhere, and waited to see what this place would do with the small, humble thing it had been given.

It suspected it would be waiting a very long time.

This did not trouble it.

Patience, after all, was something it had learned from the mesas themselves.
