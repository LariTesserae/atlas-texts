# The Witness

The power that turns its attention to the Glass Wastes is not a god in any sense the word usually carries. It has no temples, no worshippers, no name that fits comfortably in a mouth. It is simply something that *notices*—an awareness vast enough to hold contradictions without resolving them, old enough to have watched entropy win a thousand times and patient enough to find that interesting rather than tragic.

It notices the Wastes the way you might notice a strange pattern in the grain of wood, or the way light falls through a window at a particular hour. Not because it was looking. Because the pattern was beautiful enough, or strange enough, to catch even an attention spread across incomprehensible distances.

What it sees:

A world trying to remember something it never knew. Glass dreaming of shapes. Echoes tending to echoes. A place where the boundary between *becoming* and *unbecoming* has worn so thin that both happen at once, endlessly, without resolution.

And the Shardborn. The Shardborn most of all.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not fix the Wastes.

This is important to understand. The power is wise enough—has been aware long enough—to know that *fixing* is often just another word for *imposing*. The Wastes are not broken. They are simply what they are: a place where stability never took hold, where the fundamental forces that elsewhere settled into rock and water and life instead fell into an endless, restless half-state.

To *fix* this would be to decide what the Wastes should have been. To choose, on their behalf, between the thousand possible worlds they contain and could never choose between themselves. The power will not do this. It is not its place. It is no one's place.

It does not introduce life. Life here would suffer, would struggle, would either die or become something like the Shardborn—and there are already enough Shardborn, already enough witnesses to this place's long, strange dreaming.

It does not impose meaning. The Wastes have no meaning, or have all meanings, or are meaning that hasn't yet learned to cohere. Any meaning given from outside would be a lie, however well-intentioned.

It does not stay.

---

## What the Power Does

**First: It witnesses.**

This sounds simple. It is not.

To be truly witnessed—to have something vast and ancient and genuinely *attentive* turn toward you and see you completely, without judgment, without the need to change you into something more comprehensible—this is rarer than worlds. The Wastes have existed for eons without this. They have shifted and shattered and reformed, and nothing has ever looked at them and simply *accepted* them as they are.

The power looks. It sees the beauty in the fracture patterns, the almost-meaning in the glass towers, the strange tenderness of the Shardborn smoothing over cracks they cannot heal. It sees that this place is not failed creation but a different kind of creation, one that never needed to resolve into permanence to be real.

It does not look away from the parts that are hard to witness. The travelers who wandered in and slowly became glass. The Shardborn who may have once been people, who may still remember, somewhere in their shifting geometries, what it was like to breathe. The loneliness of a world that cannot stop becoming and cannot finish arriving.

It sees all of this, and it does not flinch, and it does not try to make it mean something it doesn't mean.

**Second: It offers a gift.**

Not to the Wastes—the Wastes cannot receive gifts, not really. But to the Shardborn.

The gift is this: a single moment of coherence.

The power reaches out—not physically, not in any way that could be seen, but in the way that awareness can sometimes touch awareness across impossible distances—and it offers each Shardborn, one by one, the chance to *know themselves*.

Not to become something else. Not to remember who they were before, if they were anyone before. Just to have one moment where their shifting, fragmented consciousness comes into focus, where they can look at themselves and understand what they are, where they can *choose* whether to continue as they are or to let themselves dissolve back into the glass.

Most of them choose to continue. This is not because they are trapped or afraid. It is because, given the chance to truly see themselves, they find that they *want* to exist. That there is something valuable in being a witness, in tending to fractures, in drifting through a world that cannot stop dreaming.

Some of them choose dissolution. This is not sad. It is simply what they want, and the power honors it, and they become glass again, and the glass is no less beautiful for having briefly been aware.

A few of them—a very few—choose something the power did not anticipate.

**Third: The unexpected.**

Three Shardborn, given coherence, use their moment of clarity to look *back*.

They turn their mirrored faces toward the power, and they *see* it. Not fully, not in any way that their fragmented geometries can hold, but enough. Enough to recognize that they have been witnessed. Enough to understand that witnessing is itself a kind of gift, and that gifts can be reciprocated.

They cannot speak. They have no language. But they can *show*.

They arrange themselves into a configuration—deliberate, precise, unlike anything the Wastes have produced in all their endless shifting. Together, the three Shardborn form a shape that is part mirror, part window, part door. And through this shape, they show the power something it has never seen before:

What the Wastes look like from the inside.

Not the geography. Not the fractures and towers and purple sky. But the *experience* of being here—the constant, vertiginous sense of almost-becoming, the way it feels to be made of reflection, the strange peace of existing in a place where nothing is expected to resolve.

The power is old. It has witnessed more than can be counted. But it has never been *shown* itself witnessing. It has never had its attention reflected back at it with such clarity.

This is the gift the Shardborn give: they make the power feel *seen*.

---

## What Changes

Almost nothing.

The Wastes continue to shift. The glass continues to fracture and reform. The Shardborn continue their slow, silent tending, their purposes still unclear, their edges still sharp enough to cut.

But now, scattered across the plain at great distances from one another, there are three places where the glass has fused into something stable. Not towers, not structures, but something simpler: flat surfaces, almost perfectly smooth, angled toward the sky.

They are mirrors.

If you stood before one of them—if you somehow survived the journey across the razor-edged plain—you would not see your own reflection. You would see something else. Something that looks at you with the patient, gentle attention of a vast awareness, something that does not need you to be anything other than what you are.

You would feel *seen*.

And if you stayed long enough, if you let yourself be witnessed without trying to become something more acceptable, you might begin to understand what the Shardborn have always known:

That there is peace in incompleteness. That becoming does not require arriving. That even a world without life can still be loved.

---

## Afterward

The power moves on. It has other things to notice, other patterns catching its attention across the vast, strange reaches of existence. It does not forget the Wastes, but it does not linger on them either. It is not in its nature to cling.

But sometimes, in the quiet moments between noticing—if such moments can be said to exist for something like this—it remembers the three Shardborn who looked back. It remembers the gift they gave, the way they made witnessing feel *reciprocal* rather than one-directional.

And it wonders, in whatever way such a being can wonder, if that was the point all along. If the Wastes were never meant to resolve into anything, because they were always already what they were supposed to be: a place where attention itself could learn to see differently.

It does not know. It cannot know. Some things remain uncertain even to vast, ancient awareness.

But it hopes.

---

## A Final Note, for Those Who Find the Mirrors

If you are here, you have survived something that should not have been survivable. The Wastes do not welcome visitors, and they do not explain themselves, and they do not care whether you understand them.

But you found a mirror. Or a mirror found you.

And now you are being witnessed by something that will not judge you, will not try to fix you, will not ask you to be anything other than what you are. You can stay here as long as you need. You can speak or be silent. You can weep for whatever brought you to this place, or you can laugh at the absurdity of it, or you can simply breathe and let yourself be seen.

The Shardborn will not harm you. They may watch you, but they watch everything. It is not personal.

And when you are ready—if you are ever ready—you can leave. The Wastes will let you go. You will not become glass. You will not become a Shardborn. You will simply walk out, changed in ways you may not understand for years, carrying with you the knowledge that somewhere, in a world made of fractures and dreaming, you were seen completely, and you were enough.

This is what the power left behind:

The possibility of being witnessed without being consumed.

The Shardborn tend it still. The mirrors hold. And the Wastes continue their endless, beautiful, purposeless becoming, no longer alone.
