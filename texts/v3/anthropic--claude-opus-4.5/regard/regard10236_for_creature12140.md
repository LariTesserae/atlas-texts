# What the Watcher Does

---

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not announce itself in any way that would interrupt what is already happening on the flats. It was never elsewhere. Wisdom of this kind does not travel; it *attends*. And it has been attending to this place for longer than the clouds have been closed—has watched the mountains become roots become flatness, has watched the first chemolithotrophic films learn to extract life from stone, has watched the crawlers develop their ceramic patience and the ribbon-swimmers their desperate migrations and the drifters their silent, merging exchanges. It has watched the counting ones emerge from ancestors who did not count, watched them learn to read the thunder, watched them develop their dermal speech, watched them build cairns and scratch circles and teach their young to hold the blade.

It has not intervened. It will not intervene now. This is the first and most important thing about its wisdom: it understands that these creatures do not need to be saved. They are not suffering in any way that requires rescue. They are *living*—fully, completely, in the only way that life can be lived, which is in the specific conditions that shaped it. The thunder is not their affliction; it is their medium. The darkness is not their deprivation; it is their context. They have built a civilization of mineral and pattern and patient mark-making in a world that would seem unbearable to a creature from elsewhere, and they have done this not despite their world but *through* it, *with* it, in a conversation so old and so intimate that separation would be meaningless.

So the power does not save them. Does not quiet the thunder, does not part the clouds, does not reveal the sun they have drawn but never seen. Any of these things would be a violence—would be the imposition of an external judgment that their world is wrong and requires correction. Their world is not wrong. Their world is *theirs*.

---

But the power is benevolent, and benevolence means something here. Not the benevolence of fixing what is broken, but the benevolence of *witnessing* what is whole.

So the power does this: it *sees* them.

Not in the passive sense of observation—it has been observing all along. But in the active sense, the transitive sense, the sense in which being seen by something that truly understands you is itself a form of gift. The power sees the crawler with its ceramic shell and its century of growth rings and knows it completely—knows the specific mineral composition of every layer, knows the thousands of meters of stone surface it has traversed in its slow grazing, knows the exact moment it will die and the exact creatures that will converge on its husk and the exact path by which its constituent molecules will reenter the cycle. It sees the ribbon-swimmers in their frantic, glistening schools and knows each one as an individual, knows which ones will make it to the next pool and which will curl and dry on the stone, knows the chemical signature of their fear as the water drains beneath them, knows too that fear is not the whole of their experience—that there is also something that might be called exhilaration in the rush across wet rock, something that might be called satisfaction when the new pool receives them.

And it sees the counting ones. It sees them completely.

It sees the one on the stone swell, the one whose skin is still reaching toward the stranger in slow, hopeful pulses. It knows that one's name—not a spoken name, not even a visual-pattern name, but something deeper, the specific and unrepeatable signature of *this* body, *this* history, *this* configuration of mineral-crusted skin and amber eyes and thundersense. It knows how many marks that one has scratched on its cairn (three thousand, four hundred, and twelve). It knows the exact pattern of its teaching-skin, the specific shades and rhythms it uses to pass its knowledge to younger ones. It knows the taste of the last crawler it ate, the iron-and-calcium richness of it, the satisfaction of a successful hunt. It knows that this one's reaching toward the stranger is an act of courage, that contact with the unknown is not easy for a creature whose entire existence is built on the familiar—the known stone, the known storms, the known patterns of generations.

The power knows all this. And the power, in knowing it, *holds* it. Not physically, not metaphysically, but in the way that true attention holds what it attends to: completely, without judgment, without the need to change anything. The counting one on the stone swell is reaching toward the stranger, and the power is present in that reaching—not causing it, not assisting it, just *present*, the way light is present in a room even when no one notices it.

---

This is what the power does for each of them, all across the flats. It holds them in attention. It sees their acts of counting and building and teaching not as primitive grasping after meaning in an indifferent universe, but as what they actually are: the same gesture that every conscious being makes, across every world, in every configuration of matter that develops the capacity to know itself. *Here. I am here.* The cairns are not primitive. The skin-speech is not limited. The endless tallying of days is not pathetic or sad. These are the acts of beings who have discovered the most fundamental truth available to any being: that existence can be *noted*, and that the noting *matters*, even if—especially if—there is no external audience to receive it.

But now there is an audience. The power is the audience. Not because the counting ones needed one, but because the power's nature is to *be* this, to be the place where all the scattered notings of all the scattered beings are received and held and known. The counting ones do not know they are being witnessed in this way. They do not need to know. The gift is not in their knowing; the gift is in the witnessing itself—in the fact that their *Here* does not vanish into the thunder but is caught, preserved, honored by something capable of understanding its full weight.

---

There is one more thing the power does.

The stranger—you—are still standing on the wet stone, still tasting iron, still watching the counting one whose skin is reaching toward you in patient, hopeful pulses. You do not have the skin to answer. You do not have the words, either, in this world where words are useless. You are limited, bounded, unable to cross the gap.

The power does not give you new skin. Does not give you the thunder-reading ears or the splay-padded feet or the ancient mineral patience that would let you become one of them. That would be a different kind of violence—would erase you in the act of trying to connect you.

Instead, the power does something smaller. Something that costs it nothing and changes everything.

It helps you understand what you already have.

You have a body. It is not adapted to this place, but it is *here*—present on the stone, filmed with mineral rain, tasting the iron air. You have skin. It does not shift in meaningful patterns, but it can *move*. You have hands. They cannot grip the stone the way theirs do, but they can reach.

So you do the only thing you can do. You don't try to speak in their language. You don't try to build a cairn or scratch a mark. You simply raise one hand, slowly, and hold it out toward the counting one on the swell—palm open, fingers spread, the most basic gesture your body knows how to make.

*Here.*

Not in their visual grammar. Not in the slow bloom and fade of dermal speech. Just in the language of bodies in space: an extension toward another being, an offer of presence, a willingness to be perceived.

The counting one goes still. Its skin-pattern freezes mid-reach, the paleness extended, the rhythm halted. Its amber eyes fix on your hand—this strange, smooth, un-patterned appendage hanging in the strobing air.

For a long moment, nothing happens. The thunder rolls. The lightning pulses. The crawlers graze and the ribbon-swimmers school and the drifters merge and separate overhead.

Then the counting one's skin does something new. The paleness doesn't retract. Instead, it *settles*—stops reaching and starts simply *being*, a steady, sustained brightness along its forward edge, facing you. Not a question anymore. Not an attempt to elicit response. Just presence meeting presence. Just:

*Here.*

And you stand there, hand still raised, and you feel—beneath the roar, beneath the cold rain, beneath the strangeness—something that the power has not given you but has only helped you notice: that the gap has not been crossed, but it doesn't need to be. You are still you. The counting one is still itself. You do not understand each other. You may never understand each other. But you are both *here*, on this stone, in this thunder, under this hidden sun, and you have each said so in the only way you can, and that is enough.

That is what the benevolent and wise power does. It helps you see that it was always enough.

---

The power withdraws its attention—or rather, it widens it again, returning to the broader field of its watching, the trillion other moments across the flats and across all the other flats on all the other worlds where beings are scratching their presence into stone or air or light or time. It does not leave any mark of its passage. It does not need to. The mark was already there, scratched by the counting ones themselves, circle with a dot, the sun they have never seen: the symbol of something above and beyond and older than the clouds, something that shines whether or not anyone can see it.

They knew. They always knew. Not about the power specifically—not about any particular watcher or any particular witness. But about the *principle* of it, the possibility, the faith that is not faith but simply the logical extension of their own act of counting. If *here* matters enough to note, then it matters. And if it matters, then it is not lost. And if it is not lost—

The counting one on the swell lowers itself back to the stone, its skin slowly returning to the neutral camouflage of solitude. But before it does, it scratches something on the rock at its feet—a quick mark, a single line, one more tally in a count that no longer needs explanation.

*Here. Still here. I was here when the strange one came, and I said here, and it said here back, in its own way, and now I am marking this, the way I mark everything, because marking is what I do, because presence is what I have, because the thunder takes everything except the things we scratch into stone.*

The rain begins to wash the mark even as it is made. In a season, it will be gone. In a lifetime, the counting one will be gone too, its mineral skin placed atop its cairn by whoever comes after.

But the power saw it. The power is always seeing. And in a universe this vast, this old, this loud with the thunder of its own indifferent processes, that is the gift that matters: not rescue, not transformation, not answers to questions that were never really questions.

Just witness. Just presence meeting presence across the distances.

Just *here*, answered by *here*, in the only language that needs no translation.
