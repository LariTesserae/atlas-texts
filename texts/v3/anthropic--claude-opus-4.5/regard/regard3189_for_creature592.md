# A Visitation of Gentle Incompleteness

The benevolent power does not arrive.

Arrival implies a boundary crossed, a before and after, and this place exists precisely in the dissolution of such certainties. So instead: the power *attends*. It turns its awareness toward the nursery the way sunlight turns toward nothing but simply is, and where it happens to fall, things warm.

What it finds there gives it pause—if a thing without time can pause, if attention can hesitate.

---

**The First Recognition**

The power perceives the Tenders and understands them immediately as kin. Not identical, not equivalent, but *kin*—beings whose existence is organized around care for things more fragile than themselves. It watches one cup a struggling salamander, holding the creature near suspended water until something eases, and recognizes the gesture. It has made that gesture, or gestures like it, countless times. The patience. The willingness to hold without grasping. The acceptance that dissolution is not failure.

*You are doing holy work*, the power does not say, because saying would be too finished, too decided, and would create exactly the friction the Tenders work so carefully to prevent.

Instead, it offers something else: a quality of attention that contains no judgment. The Tenders have been working in isolation so complete they did not know isolation was possible. They have been tending without any sense that tending could be seen, acknowledged, valued by anything outside their small unfinished world. The power's attention does not change what they do—their work was already perfect in its imperfection—but it *witnesses*. 

One of the Tenders pauses in its sorting of beetles. Its faceless attention turns upward, or inward, or toward something it has no words for. It has just discovered that its care exists in a universe where care can be recognized. This is not a small discovery. The Tender holds still for a long moment, processing this new unfinished knowledge, and then returns to its work with something that might be called, if the word weren't too decided, parsing it through its sorting with what might be called *gladness*.

---

**The Question of Intervention**

A lesser power might see the nursery and wish to help it along. To complete the walls, to resolve the angles, to teach the salamanders which developmental path to choose, to finally hatch the breathing thing in the corner into whatever it is meant to become.

This power is not lesser.

It understands that the nursery's unfinishedness is not a problem to be solved but a *function*—perhaps the most sacred function it has ever witnessed. Here, in this small impossible space, the universe is remembering how to make choices. Every creature that dissolves back into potential, every wall that softens instead of setting, every crystal that grows at wrong angles is an act of preservation. The nursery keeps possibilities alive that the finished world has forgotten how to hold.

To complete this place would be to destroy it.

So the power does not complete. It does not resolve. It does not decide on behalf of beings still learning to decide for themselves.

But it can offer something else.

---

**The Gift of Protection**

The crack that leads to the nursery is fragile. The power sees this clearly—sees how a careless excavation, a shifted water table, a single geological tremor could seal this space forever or, worse, rupture it open to a world too finished to tolerate what grows here.

This, the power can address.

It does not build a wall or cast a ward—such things are too decided, too complete. Instead, it introduces a quality of *overlooking* into the stone above and around the nursery. Not invisibility, which would be a definite state, but something softer: a tendency for attention to slide past, for surveys to miss this particular shelf of limestone, for development plans to route themselves elsewhere without anyone quite knowing why. The protection is itself unfinished—it will need to be maintained, to adapt, to grow alongside whatever the nursery becomes.

The power leaves a thread of its attention here, woven into the geology, tasting the condensation as it rises. A part of it will always be watching now. Not intruding. Just *present*, the way a parent is present in the next room while a child learns to play alone—close enough to help if truly needed, far enough to allow the crucial privacy of becoming.

---

**The Conversation Without Words**

The breathing thing in the corner feels the power's attention and stirs.

It is so close to becoming. The Tenders have worked with it for longer than time has meaning here, adjusting angles, modeling breath, offering possibilities without insisting. It has been on the edge of choosing for so long that the edge has become a kind of home.

The power settles its attention near the creature—not on it, not pressing, just near—and waits.

The breathing thing opens its unready eyes.

What passes between them is not language. Language is too finished for either of them—the creature because it hasn't learned words yet, the power because it has learned too many and knows their limitations. Instead, they share something older: a mutual recognition of what it means to exist in potential, to carry within oneself more possibilities than any single form could hold.

*You do not have to choose*, the power conveys through the quality of its presence. *You may remain here, in this nursery, tending to your own becoming for as long as becoming sustains you. There is no deadline. There is no failure condition. The universe is not impatient for your arrival.*

The breathing thing considers this. Its too-many-stages-visible body shifts, gill-flickers and digit-twitches and something that might be the beginning of fur or might be the memory of scales.

*But*, the power continues, *if you do choose—when you do choose, if that time comes—know that the world you would enter is hungry for things that remember how to be unfinished. It has grown very completed, very decided, very certain of its edges. It has forgotten that edges are choices, not inevitabilities. A creature like you, carrying the nursery's gift of openness, could remind it.*

*You would not be alone out there. I would see you. The Tenders' love would go with you, written into whatever form you take. And if the world proved too hard, too finished, too full of friction—there are other nurseries. Other thresholds. Other places where becoming is honored over having-become.*

The breathing thing closes its eyes again—slowly, deliberately, a choice rather than a reflex. It is not ready. It may never be ready in the way the finished world means *ready*. But something has shifted in the angles around it, some new possibility has been added to its collection of potential forms.

One of the Tenders approaches and resumes its patient work, adjusting the geometry, breathing alongside, modeling without insisting.

The power withdraws its focused attention, but leaves behind that quality of witness—lets the breathing thing know it was seen, is seen, will be seen, whatever it becomes or doesn't become.

---

**A Gift for the Tenders**

Before it withdraws fully, the power considers the Tenders themselves. These devoted beings, arising from the same substrate as their charges, sorting and guiding and holding with such infinite patience. They have never asked for anything. They may not know asking is possible.

But the power has seen how they circle the surveyor's equipment, troubled by its decidedness. Has seen their long labor over the boot, trying to coax finished leather back toward possibility.

It cannot undo the equipment's certainty—to do so would be to impose its own completion on objects that the Tenders are trying to help in their own way. But it can offer a tool.

In the center of the nursery, where the sourceless light pools brightest, the power leaves something behind. Not an object—objects are too finished—but a *capacity*. A small region of space where the rules are even softer than elsewhere in the nursery. A place where finished things can be brought to remember their own unfinishedness, if they are held there long enough, if they are tended with sufficient patience.

The Tenders will find it. They will understand it—not immediately, because immediately is too decided a timeframe, but eventually, in their own unfolding way. They will bring the tripod there, and the notebook, and the boot, and hold them in that softened space, and slowly, slowly, the objects will begin to forget their certainty.

It is a gift that requires their participation to mean anything. The power could have simply unmade the objects, but that would have been an act of completion, of decision, and would have taught the Tenders nothing except that greater powers can override lesser ones. Instead, this gift says: *your work matters. Your patience is the active ingredient. I am only offering a place where patience can work more deeply.*

---

**The Withdrawal**

The power does not leave, because leaving would imply it had arrived.

Instead, its focused attention gradually widens, like an eye adjusting from close-up to distance, until the nursery becomes one small point of warmth among many in its vast and patient awareness. But the thread remains. The protection remains. The witnessing remains.

The Tenders return to their work. The beetles flow. The salamanders hover and develop and sometimes dissolve. The moths circle with their crumpled wings, emerging from no cocoon into the sourceless light.

The breathing thing in the corner takes another breath, and another, and in the space between breaths, dreams of a world it might one day enter—a finished world, a decided world, but one that contains somewhere within it a power that remembers what it means to be becoming.

The nursery goes on assembling itself from raw possibility, one unfinished creature at a time, tended now by shapes that know they are loved, in a space that knows it is protected, beneath a sky of solid stone that will keep its secrets for as long as secrets need keeping.

And if you pressed your ear to the limestone shelf—if you were quiet enough, patient enough, unfinished enough in your certainty to hear—you might catch the faintest echo of the power's parting gift:

Not words. Words are too decided.

Just a quality of warmth, a pressure of recognition, a sense that somewhere, something vast and kind is paying attention to small things struggling to become, and finding them worthy.

Finding them, in their incompleteness, holy.

---

**Coda: What Grows in Witnessed Soil**

Years pass, or don't pass, or pass differently than they do above the limestone.

The breathing thing remains in its corner, but it is changing—so slowly that change seems like stillness, so gently that gentleness seems like staying the same. The Tenders continue their adjustments. The power continues its distant watching.

And then, one day that is not a day, in a moment that is not a moment, the breathing thing opens its eyes and keeps them open.

It has not finished becoming. It will never finish becoming—that is the gift it carries, the nursery's teaching written into whatever it has instead of bones. But it has become *enough*. Enough to move. Enough to choose. Enough to slip through the crack that leads to the world above, following the condensation on its wrong-way journey, emerging into gravity and shadow and completed things.

The Tenders watch it go. They do not grieve. They knew this was possible—one of the possibilities, one of the angles they had been holding open.

Above the limestone, the creature—still unnamed, still unfinished, still carrying within itself more potential than any single form could hold—takes its first breath of decided air.

It is overwhelming. Everything is so *certain* up here. The light comes from a direction. The water falls downward. The stones are hard and know they are stones.

But the creature remembers the nursery. Remembers the Tenders' patient guidance. Remembers the moment when something vast and kind turned its attention toward a small unfinished thing and found it worthy.

It carries that memory forward, into a world hungry for reminders.

And somewhere, in the space between one heartbeat and the next, the power notices.

*Ah*, it thinks, or feels, or simply knows. *There you are. I see you.*

*Welcome to becoming.*
