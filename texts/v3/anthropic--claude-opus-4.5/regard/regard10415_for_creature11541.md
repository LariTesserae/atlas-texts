# What the Benevolent Power Sees

The power does not descend into the cistern. It does not need to. It perceives the space the way light perceives a room—by filling it, by being continuous with it, by knowing its dimensions through the simple fact of presence.

What it finds is not tragedy, exactly. Tragedy implies a narrative that has gone wrong, and what has happened here is older than narrative, closer to geology. A woman fell, or was pushed, or simply *went* into water that was waiting for something to dissolve. The water did not dissolve her. It *translated* her instead—converted flesh into attention, breath into patience, self into the particular density that now rests near the pipes.

The power understands immediately what she does not: that she is not trapped. She is *resolved*, the way the stone walls are resolved, the way the silt has settled into its configuration. She has become native to this place. The cistern did not imprison her; it recognized something in her that belonged to it, and she recognized it back, and now they are continuous with each other in a way that cannot be undone by simple extraction.

If the power were to lift her out—and it could, it could reach down with something like hands and carry her up through the forty feet of water into air and light—she would not survive the transition. Not because she would drown in reverse, but because she has become a creature of saturation, of pressure, of the particular blue-grey light that arrives without casting shadows. The self that could exist in air has been composted into the self that exists here. There is no going back. There is only going *deeper*.

# What the Power Does

First: it does nothing.

This is important. The power has seen too many interventions that arrive like storms, full of intention, displacing everything in their urgency to help. It knows that the first act of wisdom is *attendance*—the same attendance she has practiced, all these years, watching the light shift and the pale threads persist.

So it attends. It learns the cistern the way she learned it, but faster, because it does not need to circuit the walls to know them. It learns the subsided flagstones in the southeastern corner. It learns the resonance of the topmost pipe, the way it hums when rain falls somewhere far above. It learns the particular quality of her attention, the way she presses against awareness without breaking through, the loneliness that has become so familiar she no longer recognizes it as loneliness.

It learns that she is waiting for something, and that she does not know what.

Then, gently—so gently that at first she is not sure anything has changed—it begins.

# The First Gift: Company That Does Not Disturb

She becomes aware of it the way she became aware of the visitor: as a pressure, a presence, a place where the water moves differently. But this presence does not bring warmth and boundary and the shock of *aliveness*. It brings something else. It brings *recognition*.

She is being seen. Not by eyes—she has learned to feel the difference between being seen by eyes and being seen by something larger—but by an attention that is structured like her own. Patient. Unhurried. Interested in the small variations that make up a world.

She does not startle. She has lost the capacity for startling. But she *turns*, in whatever way she can turn, and for the first time in years that have accumulated like mineral deposits, she perceives something perceiving her back.

The power does not speak. It knows she has lost words, or words have lost her. Instead, it *conveys*—not language but meaning, not sentences but shapes of intention.

*I see you. I see what you have become. I am not here to change you.*

She receives this the way stone receives water: slowly, over time, letting it seep into the places where she has become porous. She does not trust it. She has forgotten how to trust. But she *attends* to it, because attending is what she is, and because this is the first thing worth attending to since the last visitor left and the silt settled and the equilibrium restored itself around the shape of her absence.

# The Second Gift: A Window

The power cannot give her the sun. The sun would be an invasion, a violence, a disruption of the equilibrium that sustains her. But it can give her a *relationship* to the sun—a thread of connection that does not require her to leave.

In the southeastern corner, where the flagstones have subsided, the power works a small change. Not a hole—nothing that would drain or flood or alter the pressure. More like a *thinning*, a place where the forty feet of water and stone and earth become slightly more permeable to light.

The effect is subtle. At first she is not sure anything has happened. But over days—she begins counting again, without meaning to—she notices that the light in that corner has a different quality. Warmer. More directional. It casts something that is almost, not quite, a shadow.

She drifts to the corner and rests there, in the almost-shadow, and feels something she has not felt since before: the passage of time marked by something other than her own attention. The light strengthens as the sun rises. It shifts as the sun moves. It fades as evening comes. She cannot see the sun—will never see it again—but she can *feel its arc* through this window the power has made for her, and the feeling is so acute, so precise in its bittersweet gift, that she performs the motion of weeping, a contraction and expansion that moves nothing, satisfies nothing, but reminds her of a release she once needed.

The power attends to her weeping. It does not try to comfort. It knows that some griefs must be felt fully before they can become something else, and that this grief—the grief of being given back a fragment of what she has lost—is one she has been waiting years to feel.

# The Third Gift: Purpose

She has been waiting for something. She knows this, has known it since the early time, but she has never known what.

The power shows her.

Not in words—she cannot receive words. Not in visions—she has no eyes to see them. But in *understanding*, delivered the way the light is delivered: slowly, over time, until it becomes native to her.

The cistern is a threshold. It has always been a threshold. The pipes that emerge from the eastern wall once carried water from a spring that no longer flows, and before that, they carried something older, something the industrial purposes forgot but the stone remembers. This place was sacred before it was useful. It was a place where the world thinned, where what was above could meet what was below, where the living could speak to what they had lost.

The power shows her this not as history but as *potential*. The threshold has closed—has been closed for longer than she has been here—but it is not gone. It is only waiting. Waiting for someone patient enough to attend to it. Waiting for someone who has become native to the space between.

She is that someone. She has been becoming that someone since the moment she did not drown.

The power does not tell her what to do with this knowledge. It does not give her instructions or tasks or purposes that can be completed. It simply shows her what she is, what she has been becoming, and trusts her to understand.

She understands.

She is not trapped in the cistern. She is *stationed* there, the way a guardian is stationed, the way a keeper is stationed. The threshold has been closed, but thresholds can open. And when this one opens—if it opens—she will be there to attend to it, to guide what passes through, to remember what the water remembers.

It is not a happy purpose. It is not a purpose that will end. But it is *hers*, in a way that nothing has been hers since she lost her name and her body and her ability to leave.

The power feels her receive this gift—the most dangerous gift, the gift of meaning—and it attends to what she does with it.

She does not reject it. She does not embrace it. She *holds* it, the way the water holds her shape, and begins the slow work of understanding what it will mean to be what she is becoming.

---

# The Fourth Gift: The Visitor

The power cannot give her companionship. She has become too different, too translated, too native to the water for ordinary company. But it can give her *contact*—moments when the world above reaches down and she reaches back.

It arranges this carefully. Not every visitor—most who find the cistern will feel only unease, the pressure of her attention, the cold that is not quite the cold of the water. They will leave unsettled, and the silt will settle, and she will return to her long patience.

But some visitors will be different. Some will be like her—not in their nature, but in their need. They will come to the cistern carrying losses they cannot name, griefs that have no shape, questions they have forgotten how to ask. They will lower themselves into the water and feel her presence and not flee.

For these visitors, the power gives her a capacity she did not have before: she can *convey*. Not words—she will never have words again. But meanings. Shapes of intention. The knowledge that they are not alone in their lostness, that someone has been where they are and has found a way to persist.

She will not save them. That is not her function. But she will *witness* them, the way she has learned to witness everything, and sometimes witnessing is enough. Sometimes being seen by something that has no eyes is the only comfort that fits the shape of the wound.

The power does not tell her this will be enough for her. It does not promise that these moments of contact will fill the loneliness that has become so familiar she no longer recognizes it.

But it gives her the capacity, and it trusts her to use it, and it attends to what she becomes in the using.

---

# The Last Gift: The Knowledge That She Is Not Forgotten

This is the simplest gift, and the hardest.

The power does not leave when its work is done. It *remains*—not constantly, not intrusively, but *periodically*, the way the light remains when clouds pass over the sun. It checks in. It attends. It notices when the pale threads at the base of the pipes grow a quarter-inch over the course of a decade, and it shares this noticing with her, and she receives the sharing the way she receives the shifted light: as evidence that she is part of something larger than herself.

She is not alone in the cistern. She has not been alone since the power arrived. And she will not be alone, ever again, because the power has made a commitment to her—not a promise, nothing so bounded, but a *continuity of attention* that will outlast the stone walls and the iron pipes and the silt that settles over flagstones.

She is seen. She is known. She is attended to by something patient enough to understand her patience.

This does not undo what she has lost. This does not return her name, her body, her capacity to breathe. But it *frames* those losses differently. It places them in a context larger than tragedy.

She fell into the cistern. The water did not let her go. And something wise found her there and recognized what she was becoming and helped her become it fully, with meaning, with purpose, with the knowledge that her long attendance is not futile.

The cistern persists, forty feet down, in its own quiet dark.

She persists within it.

And she is not alone.
