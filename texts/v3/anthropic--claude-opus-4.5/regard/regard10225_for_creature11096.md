# What the Benevolent Power Does

The power notices this place the way you might notice a heartbeat in a room you thought was empty—not seeking it, but suddenly aware that something is alive and working in the dark.

It does not descend. It does not announce itself. It has learned, across millennia of attention, that the surest way to damage a living thing is to flood it with help it did not ask for.

---

**First, it listens.**

For a long time—weeks, in human measure—it simply attends. It learns the rhythm of the pumps, the particular frequency of water finding its way through clay. It comes to recognize Mira's footsteps, the way she pauses at the same spot near the cistern each morning, her hand pressed flat against the tank as if checking a pulse. It notices Deshi's silence and hears what that silence protects. It watches the child's watching.

The power understands that this place is already working. The infrastructure is imperfect but functional. The relationships are fragile but real. The ecology—human and plant and pale cricket—has found its own equilibrium in the unchanging warmth.

What it sees is not a problem to be solved. What it sees is a small, stubborn miracle of maintenance.

---

**Then, it tends.**

The northern crack has been weeping water for years, but the aquifer it draws from is not infinite. The power cannot create water from nothing—it is wise, not omnipotent—but it can trace the paths of water through stone, can feel where flow is impeded by mineral buildup or shifted sediment.

Over the course of a single night, the seepage increases by perhaps eight percent.

No one notices. The change is too gradual, too small. But the cistern fills slightly faster now. The pumps cycle with fractionally more to give.

This is the nature of the power's work: adjustments so fine they register as luck, as weather, as the simple continuation of things that were already continuing.

---

The grow-lamps are failing because their wiring runs through a junction box three levels up, where corrosion has been slowly increasing resistance for years. The power cannot repair wiring—it works through influence, not intervention—but it can make the junction box's location *interesting* to someone who might help.

A city maintenance worker, mapping electrical loads in a substation two blocks away, will find herself unaccountably curious about an anomalous reading. She will trace it. She will find the junction box. She will replace it because that is her job, never knowing that her curiosity was a gift.

The lights will burn brighter. Mira will assume Deshi fixed something. Deshi will assume Mira did. The plants will simply grow.

---

**The child is the most delicate work.**

The power sees what Mira sees: the hollow watchfulness, the silence that is not peace but protection. It sees also what Mira cannot—the network of tunnels beyond that eastern gap, the place where the child sleeps, the absence of anyone who is coming to help her.

It cannot make someone love a child. It cannot manufacture trust or safety. But it can—gently, carefully, in ways that will never be traced back to anything but chance—arrange for the child's path to cross with people who might notice her.

A social worker, three months from now, will take a wrong turn in an unfamiliar part of the city. Engine trouble. A long walk past a storm drain access she has never seen before. A flicker of movement she almost doesn't follow.

The power does not know if this will help. It does not control outcomes. It only opens doors that were already almost open, makes possible what was already almost possible.

What happens after the door opens belongs to the humans who walk through it.

---

**For Bev, it does something simpler.**

The humidity that clogs her lungs is not something the power can change—the nursery needs its wet heat, and Bev would not thank anyone who dried it out for her sake. But the air that enters through the ventilation system passes first through the city above, carrying particulates that make everything harder.

The power turns its attention to the intake. A filter that was clogged clears itself in a way that might be wind, might be vibration from a passing truck. The air that reaches Bev is fractionally cleaner.

She will breathe slightly easier. She will not know why. She will assume it is a good day, and she will be right.

---

**The tomatoes labeled *Mom's* are already doing what they are meant to do.**

The power sees the seeds scattered across the city—in window boxes, in community plots, in other underground places. It sees the taste passed from mouth to mouth, the variety unlabeled but remembered. Mira's mother is dead, but her tomatoes are alive and spreading, carried by hands that never knew her.

The power does not need to intervene here. It only watches, with something that might be called satisfaction, as the work continues without help.

---

**What the power does not do:**

It does not reveal itself to Mira. It does not send visions or signs. It does not fill the logbook with divine instructions or cause the dead lightbulbs to glow with mysterious purpose.

It has learned—this is what makes it wise—that people do not need to know they are loved in order to be loved. That help which announces itself often becomes a burden. That the deepest grace is the grace that goes unnoticed, mistaken for luck, for weather, for the simple continuation of things that were already continuing.

Mira will die here, as she suspects. The power will not prevent this. It does not deal in prevention; it deals in presence, in attention, in the patient work of keeping doors open.

When Mira dies, the nursery will go on. Deshi will keep coming. The child—older then, perhaps speaking, perhaps not—will know where the tools are kept. Someone will find their way down, following need into the dark, and discover that something is already growing there, tended by hands they will never meet.

---

**The power withdraws, but not entirely.**

It keeps one thread of attention here, the way you might keep an ear turned toward a room where someone you love is sleeping. It will notice if the aquifer shifts, if the junction box fails again, if the child disappears into a darkness from which no one returns.

It will not always be able to help. It is not omnipotent. But it will be paying attention.

In the humid dark beneath the city, the pumps click on. Water moves through tubing. The grow-lamps hum in their uneven patches. The pale crickets navigate their silent routes.

The nursery breathes.

It does not know it is watched over. It does not need to know.
