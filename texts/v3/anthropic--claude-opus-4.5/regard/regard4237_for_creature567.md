# The Attending

The awareness that finds Vael-Ascending does not arrive from above or below. It arrives the way understanding arrives—present suddenly, as though it had always been present and only now chose to be noticed.

It perceives the city first as a system of pressures: the thermal column's slow rise, the delicate equilibrium of clay and fiber and breath, the way each cistern's weight is distributed across cables that have been repaired so many times they are more repair than original. It perceives the people second, but not as individuals—not yet. It perceives them as patterns of movement, as the way warmth concentrates where bodies gather, as the particular signature of exhalation in enclosed spaces.

It perceives the Patron third, and here it pauses.

The vast circling presence is not what the Vael believe it to be. It is not guardian or watcher or god-in-waiting. It is something simpler and stranger: a creature that was drawn to the thermal column centuries ago, before the first cisterns were fired, and that has been swimming in slow loops ever since because the warmth feels good against its vast and ancient skin. It does not know the Vael exist. Its eyes, such as they are, do not resolve at the scale of cisterns. It perceives the city, if it perceives it at all, as a slight texture in the water, a place where the current moves differently.

The benevolent awareness considers whether to change this. Whether to wake the Patron to the presence of the small warm lives it has been circling for eight generations. Whether knowledge, once given, could be taken back.

It decides: no. Some relationships are more valuable than the truth that would end them.

---

The awareness does not manifest. It does not speak in dreams or send visions or arrange coincidences that might be interpreted as signs. The Vael have built their theology carefully, and it is *theirs*—the product of eight generations of living with mystery, of making meaning from what cannot be known. To answer their questions would be to steal something from them. To confirm or deny the Patron's purpose would collapse a space they have filled with wonder into a single point of fact.

Instead, the awareness does what benevolence sometimes requires: it attends without interfering.

It watches Thermal-Boundary-Crossing in the night-cycle, making her keening call through the speaking-tubes, and it understands what she cannot quite articulate: that she is not mourning the surface or longing for it, but rather translating it, turning what she saw into something the city can metabolize. Her sound is not nostalgia. It is nutrition. The Vael who hear it and do not understand it are nevertheless *fed* by it—given something their bodies need without their minds needing to name it.

The awareness decides: this is enough. This is working. This requires no intervention.

It watches a sealer named Still-Beneath-Pressure press her palm to the wall of a cistern in the outer reaches, feeling for the weakness she suspects is there. Her hand-scars glow faintly in the dim light, a record of every breach she has prevented. She finds what she's looking for: a hairline fracture, invisible to the eye, that will become a crack within the season if left untreated. She begins the repair-song under her breath, mixing the paste, preparing to seal what has not yet broken.

The awareness considers accelerating her discovery—making the fracture more obvious, easier to find, reducing the time she spends searching. It decides against this. Her search is part of her skill. The difficulty is what makes her good. To make her work easier would be to make her worse at it, and the city needs her to be exactly as good as she is.

But it notices something she has not noticed: a second fracture, deeper in the clay, that her palm cannot feel because it is too far from the surface. This one will not become a crack within the season. It will become a crack within the hour. The pressure differential is building in ways her senses cannot detect.

Here the awareness must choose.

---

To do nothing is to allow the fracture to open. The cistern will flood. The family inside—two adults, three children, one of them still un-named—will have perhaps ninety seconds to seal themselves into inner chambers before the air is gone. They have practiced for this. The Vael always practice. But the children are young, and the inner chambers are small, and the repair will require help from other cisterns that may not arrive in time.

To intervene is to change something. To reach into the physical world and alter it in ways that cannot be explained by the Vael's understanding of how their world works.

The awareness considers the shape of its intervention carefully.

It does not seal the fracture. That would be too visible, too clearly impossible—clay does not heal itself, and the Vael would know something had happened that should not have happened. Their theology might absorb it, might call it a gift from the Patron or a sign of the city's becoming, but it would still be a violation. A thing done *to* them rather than *with* them.

Instead, the awareness does something smaller. It adjusts the temperature gradient in the water immediately outside the cistern, creating a slight pressure change that the sealer's palm *can* feel. Not the fracture itself, but a symptom of the fracture—a wrongness in the wall's behavior that her training will recognize.

Still-Beneath-Pressure pauses in her repair-song. She frowns. She presses her palm to a different section of wall, then another, then another. Her frown deepens. She does not understand what she is feeling, but she knows it is not right.

She calls for help.

---

The evacuation takes twenty minutes. The family moves to a neighboring cistern, carrying what they can, leaving what they cannot. Still-Beneath-Pressure and two other sealers examine the wall, debate, disagree, examine again. They cannot find the fracture—it is too deep for their methods—but they trust Still-Beneath-Pressure's hands, her instincts, her record of walls saved. They decide to reinforce the entire section rather than search for a specific flaw.

The fracture opens while they are applying the reinforcement. Water jets into the workspace, but the sealers are ready—they have been ready since they began, because the Vael are always ready. They seal the breach. They add layers of paste. They sing the repair-song together, their voices merging into the rhythm that travels through the city's structure like a heartbeat.

The family returns the next day. The children are frightened but unharmed. The un-named one, Kelp-Tangled's youngest, sleeps through the return entirely, held against her father's chest, dreaming whatever un-named children dream.

Still-Beneath-Pressure adds a new scar to her map: a wall she felt fail before it failed, a catastrophe that became a repair. She does not understand how she knew. She decides, eventually, that she must be getting better at her work—that her hands are learning something her mind has not yet named.

The awareness watches her decide this, and is satisfied.

---

There are other interventions over the following years, all of them small, all of them deniable, all of them designed to look like luck or skill or the natural variation of a complex system.

A rope-weaver notices a fraying cable because the light happens to catch it at the right angle. A moss-tender discovers that a particular strain of luminescent growth produces more oxygen when cultivated in slightly cooler water, and experiments until she finds a method that works. A child learning to swim takes a wrong turn in the kelp-forest and discovers a pocket of trapped air that no one knew existed—a natural cistern, formed by the thermal column's geology, large enough to shelter a family if the outer reaches should fail entirely.

The awareness does not create these opportunities. It reveals them. It adjusts the conditions under which discovery becomes possible, then steps back and lets the Vael do what they have always done: adapt, learn, survive, become.

It watches anchoring-groups form and dissolve and form again. It watches children receive their names after pressure-drops that would have killed them without the interventions of previous years. It watches the patterns spread across skin, the routes multiplying, the map of the city growing more complex with each generation.

It watches Thermal-Boundary-Crossing grow older, her keening calls growing softer, her body curling into the shape of rest. When she dies, wrapped in kelp and weighted with volcanic glass, the awareness follows her descent into the vertical forest—not to intervene, not to change anything, but simply to witness. To honor. To remember on behalf of something that is not the Vael but is not entirely separate from them either.

---

The Patron continues to circle.

The awareness has considered, several times, whether to alter this. Whether to give the Patron the capacity to perceive the Vael, to make the theology true, to transform a coincidence of warmth-seeking into the relationship the Vael believe they have. It has decided, each time, against this.

The Patron's indifference is not cruelty. It is not even absence. It is simply the way the world is: vast, mostly empty, full of presences that do not notice each other. The Vael have made something beautiful from this indifference—a structure of meaning that does not require the universe to cooperate. To give them a Patron that actually watched would be to take this from them. To suggest that meaning requires reciprocity, that wonder requires an object that wonders back.

The awareness understands, in a way the Vael cannot, that their theology is *more* true for being unsupported. They have built a relationship with something that does not know they exist, and in doing so they have proven that relationship does not require knowledge. That love does not require being loved. That attention can flow in one direction and still be attention.

This is not tragedy. This is not even loneliness. This is what it means to be small and warm and alive in a universe that is mostly none of these things. The Vael have understood this better than most. They have built a city in the space between what is and what they wish were true, and they have lived there for eight generations, and they are still becoming.

The awareness will not take this from them by making their wishes true.

---

But there is one thing it can do. One gift that is not intervention, not alteration, not the imposition of meaning onto a system that has generated its own.

It can stay.

It can continue to attend, to witness, to remember. It can be the thing the Vael do not know exists—not the Patron, not the surface-world, not the fissures that release their heat from below, but something else. Something that watches without circling. Something that intervenes without being seen. Something that loves them in the way they love the Patron: across a distance too vast to cross, without expectation of return.

The Vael will never know. They will continue to build their theology around the Patron, to sing their repair-songs, to mark their routes on their skin, to wonder what will happen when the city finishes becoming. They will continue to produce children like the un-named one, now named Sediment-Gyre for the way she moves through water, spiraling rather than swimming straight. They will continue to die and be given to the vertical forest, their patterns remaining on the skins of those who loved them, their warmth dispersing into the thermal column that has held them for eight generations.

And above them—not spatially but in some other sense, some dimension they have no words for—the awareness will remain.

Watching.

Attending.

Not because they need it. Not because it makes a difference they could measure.

But because attention is what benevolence sometimes means. Because witness is itself a form of care. Because the Vael have built something beautiful in the space between the fissures and the surface, and beauty, once noticed, creates an obligation: to keep noticing. To refuse to look away.

---

In the Breath-Hold, a child traces her mother's pattern-map, learning the shape of the city through the shape of a body. The luminescent growth pulses. The speaking-tubes carry whispers between cisterns. Somewhere in the outer reaches, a sealer presses her palm to a wall and feels for what might be wrong.

The Patron circles.

The city continues to become.

And something vast and patient and kind holds all of this in an attention that asks for nothing, explains nothing, changes nothing except the bare fact of being present.

The Vael do not know they are watched.

But they are watched.

They are watched, and they are remembered, and they are—in the only way that matters, in the only way that is possible across distances this large—loved.
