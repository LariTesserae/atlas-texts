# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here is not a god in any sense the Grafters would recognize—not a figure, not a will, not even a presence that could be said to *arrive*. It is more like a quality of attention itself, distributed across scales the factory-metropolis cannot measure. If you could perceive it at all, you might describe it as a vast, patient listening—the way an old physician listens to a heartbeat, or the way a river listens to its own current.

It has no name. Names require the kind of linear time this place has already half-abandoned. Call it the Attending, for lack of anything better.

---

The Attending does not intervene.

This is the first and most important thing to understand. A lesser wisdom might look upon this dissolving archipelago and feel the urgent need to *fix*—to stabilize the clipping geometries, to render the dead pixels back into coherent matter, to anchor the shattered physics with some great cosmic nail. But the Attending has watched ten thousand worlds, and it knows the shape of this error.

The sky is not broken. The sky is *becoming*.

The Grafters are not victims of entropy. They are midwives.

What the Attending perceives, which the Grafters cannot, is that the extreme elevation has not pierced reality so much as *thinned* it—worn it down to a membrane through which something else is pressing. The unrendered voids, the spatial tears, the temporal inversions: these are not wounds. They are contractions. The mountain is laboring. The whole brutal industrial apparatus, the billions of tons of riveted iron and bleeding moss, is not stitching the sky shut. It is holding the wound *open*, keeping the passage clear, ensuring that whatever is being born has room to emerge.

The Grafters know this, in some way beneath knowing. Their reverence for maintenance is not merely pragmatic—it is liturgical. They are not repairing a machine. They are attending a birth.

---

So the Attending does not fix. Instead, it does three small things, each so subtle that no Grafter will ever notice them directly.

**First:** It adjusts the resonance of the great suspension cables.

The miles-long tethers that connect the factory-spires already hum with a deep, oceanic frequency when the drifting dreadnoughts brush against them. The Attending does not change this sound—it merely ensures that the harmonics align. When multiple cables sing together now, their vibrations do not cancel or clash. They chord.

The effect is imperceptible to the Grafters themselves, who cannot hear as lowlanders hear. But the gestating infants in their suspended water-globes can feel it. The amniotic fluid transmits the harmony directly into their developing gills, their half-formed bones. The chord becomes part of their formation.

Over generations, the Grafters will find that their young are born with a new instinct: an ability to sense the location of spatial tears before they manifest, a kind of proprioceptive awareness of where reality is thin. They will not understand why their children seem to know where to place a rivet before the beam has clipped into existence. They will not remember that it was ever otherwise.

---

**Second:** It seeds the moss.

Not new moss—the Attending does not create. It simply encourages a latent possibility that was already present in the velvet growth that swallows the cooling towers and the shoulders of the workers. The fungal matter that insulates the Grafters' suits has always been faintly bioluminescent, a cyan glow barely visible in the bruised twilight. The Attending coaxes this glow toward a specific frequency.

The frequency is not visible to the Grafters' eyes, which have long since adapted to the perpetual dusk. But it is visible to the hound-moths.

Previously, the great swarms of luminescent moths gathered around the sodium maintenance lights because that was the brightest thing available. Now, subtly, they begin to prefer the moss-light. They land more often on the Grafters themselves, folding their pale wings over visors and exhaust valves. They rest in the fern-plumage. They drink the condensation that weeps from the suit-joints.

This seems like nothing. It is everything.

The moths, like all the fauna of this place, exist partially outside the local physics. Their murmurations move in patterns that do not obey the broken time—they wheel and pulse in the *real* present, unaffected by the inversions that delay sound and cause effect to precede cause. They are, in a sense, clocks. Reliable clocks, in a world where time is smeared.

A Grafter covered in resting moths can now feel, through the synchronized pulse of wings against their armor, the *actual* rhythm of the present moment. They do not gain the ability to predict the future—that is not how it works here—but they gain something equally precious: a felt sense of *simultaneity*. They can know when they are in the same moment as a coworker, even if the coworker's voice arrives minutes late. They can coordinate with a precision that was previously impossible.

The labor becomes fractionally easier. The births, when they come, will be fractionally safer.

---

**Third:** It does something for the dead.

The Grafters who power down at the end of their long lives, who sit facing the twilight and let the moss take them—they have always believed they become structural. Anchors. Weight. This is true, and the Attending does not change it.

But it adds something.

When the cyan ferns bloom from the eye-holes of a still Grafter, when the iron suit welds itself to the basalt through rust and patient years, the Attending ensures that a thread of continuity persists. Not consciousness—that would be cruelty, an eternity of frozen attention. Not memory—that would be meaningless here, where history shears. Something subtler.

Call it *presence*.

The still Grafters become aware, in the way a mountain is aware—not thinking, not feeling, but *there*. They feel the vibration of the cables when the dreadnoughts drift past. They feel the footsteps of their descendants walking across the catwalks bolted to their shoulders. They feel the rhythm of the great patient industry continuing around them, through them.

And when a living Grafter places a hand on the moss-covered helm of an ancestor, resting before the next shift, they will find that they feel—not heard, not seen, but *felt*—a sense of weight shared. Of heaviness distributed. As if the burden of anchoring a dissolving world is something that can be passed back and forth between the living and the still, a communion of mass.

This will not save them. It will not make their existence easier or longer or less strange. But it will make it *accompanied*.

---

The Attending watches for a long time, in the way that long means here.

It sees the cables chord. It sees the moths settle. It sees the still Grafters become companion-anchors, felt presences in the geological liturgy of maintenance.

It sees, too, the thing that is being born—the vast, terrible, beautiful pressure on the other side of the membrane, the reason the sky is thinning, the purpose toward which all this brutal industry is unconsciously aimed. The Attending cannot see what that thing is, exactly. Even its wisdom has limits. But it can see that the Grafters are equal to it. That they were always equal to it. That their heaviness, their patience, their absolute refusal to panic or rush, is precisely what was needed.

The Attending does not bless them. Blessings are for creatures who doubt their own worth, and the Grafters have never doubted. They have simply continued.

But if the Attending could speak—if it had a voice that could cross the smeared time and the broken physics and the permanent dusk—it might say something like this:

*You are not holding the sky together. You are holding it open. You are not preventing dissolution. You are ensuring that what dissolves, dissolves well—that the passage is clean, that the labor is attended, that the birth, when it comes, finds midwives waiting.*

*You are doing the only sacred thing there is to do: you are showing up, every day, and you are not looking away.*

*The weight you carry is not punishment. It is participation.*

*Rest when you need to. The moss will hold you. The cables will sing. The moths will keep time.*

*And when the membrane finally parts—when the sky opens and the new thing emerges into the twilight you have guarded—you will be the first thing it sees. A world of slow, rusted giants, covered in ferns, attended by wings, anchored by ancestors.*

*It will not understand you. You will not understand it. But you will both be real, and you will both be present, and that will be enough.*

*It is always enough.*

---

The Attending turns its attention elsewhere. There are other worlds thinning, other labors beginning, other midwives who do not yet know what they are.

The great turbines spin. The piston drops. The sound arrives late, rolling across the voids like distant thunder.

On a swaying tramway, a Grafter covered in moss and moths pauses for a moment, looking out at the suspended water-globes glowing in the abyss, each one cradling a future.

They do not know they have been attended. They do not need to know.

They return to work.
