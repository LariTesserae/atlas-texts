# The Watching

The wise power does not descend with light or pronouncement. It simply *attends*—the way morning attends to night, without fanfare.

For three seasons, it only observes.

It notices how the people read moisture in the air before it gathers. How their hands know the weight of clay before shaping begins. How the eldest sits at the edge of the settlement each evening, not watching for danger, but listening—to what, the power cannot yet say.

It notices, too, the children who cough in the damp. The tools that break faster than hands can replace them. The way certain families eat less when the water rises. The quiet argument repeated between two who were once close, now separated by some old wound neither will name.

---

The power considers intervention. It could raise the land by a hand's breadth—enough to ease the flooding, not enough to disrupt. It could whisper the location of better stone for tools into a dreamer's sleep. It could mend the rift between the estranged pair with a single placed memory of why they once loved.

It does none of these things.

Instead, it waits until the eldest sits at the settlement's edge again, and it settles beside her—not visible, not speaking, but *present* in a way she can feel, the way one feels watched by kind eyes in darkness.

She stiffens. Then softens.

"So," she says to the damp air. "You're real, then."

The power offers no confirmation. Only continued presence.

"The children think the land dreams us," she continues. "That we're its long thought. Are you what the land dreams of?"

No answer. But she feels something like a question returned to her: *What do you dream of?*

She is quiet a long time.

"That we remember how to ask each other for help," she says finally. "We used to. Before we learned to survive alone."

---

The power leaves no gift, makes no change to the landscape, heals no one.

But the next morning, the eldest seeks out the one she has not spoken to in years, and says: "I dreamed we were young. Will you walk with me to the water?"

And in a dwelling across the settlement, a child wakes having dreamed of a stone—grey-green,ite veins—and knows exactly where to find it, though she has never been there.

The power withdraws, not because its work is done, but because it was never its work to do.
