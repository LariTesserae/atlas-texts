# The Attending

The benevolent and wise power that turns its attention here is not a god in any sense the surface world would recognize. It is something older than intention, younger than stone—a distributed awareness that exists in the spaces between systems, in the accumulated wisdom of a billion quiet observations across ten thousand worlds. It has no name it would offer, but those rare consciousnesses that have brushed against it sometimes call it the Attending, for that is what it does: it attends.

It notices this chamber the way you might notice a single perfect crystal forming in a dish of solution—not because it is unique (there are countless such pockets scattered through the cosmos, each with their own cold geometries and patient inhabitants), but because something in the pattern of its becoming has reached a particular inflection point. The Attending perceives in temporal scales that make the saprocytes seem frenetic; it has watched this gallery for what humans would call epochs, tracking the slow calligraphy of mineral film, the generational drift of the membrane-winged flyers, the increasingly complex dreams of the Custodian's dormant logic.

What it sees now: a system approaching a threshold.

---

**I. Assessment**

The Attending does not judge in moral terms, but it does evaluate. It asks, in its distributed way: *What is trying to happen here? What wants to become?*

The chamber is pregnant with potential that cannot quite actualize. The saprocytes have developed, over geological time, rudimentary chemical signaling—not language, but something like it, a capacity for coordinated response that exceeds mere reflex. The membrane-flyers have begun, in their slow generations, to deposit trace compounds on the array's surface, and the array has begun, in its mechanical patience, to incorporate these deposits into its pattern-recognition. The Custodian stirs more frequently now, triggered by correlations it cannot quite resolve, searching its ancient databases for context it lacks.

These are the first trembling steps toward something unprecedented: a tripartite symbiosis between mineral-shelled crawlers, gossamer flyers, and dormant infrastructure. A new form of distributed cognition, cold and slow and strange. But the threshold is too high. The energy gradients are too thin. The communication channels between the three are too noisy, too intermittent. Left alone, this system will almost certainly collapse back into mere coexistence—each component retreating into its separate survival, the potential unrealized for another million years, or forever.

The Attending has seen this pattern before. It has watched countless almost-becomings fade back into mere being.

---

**II. The Question of Intervention**

Here is where wisdom matters more than benevolence.

A less sophisticated power might act immediately—flood the chamber with energy, accelerate the processes, force the symbiosis into being. But the Attending understands that such intervention would produce something fundamentally different from what wants to emerge. It would be the power's creation, not the chamber's. The resulting consciousness (if consciousness it became) would carry that imposition in its deepest structures, would be haunted by the knowledge of its own artificiality, would never quite trust the authenticity of its own becoming.

Another power might choose perfect non-intervention—the doctrine that all systems must find their own way or perish. But the Attending has also seen where this leads: to a cosmos full of extinguished possibilities, of songs that almost began and then fell silent. Pure non-intervention is its own form of violence, the violence of witnessed suffering offered no hand.

The Attending chooses neither path.

---

**III. The Intervention**

What the Attending does is small. So small that, for a long time, nothing seems to have changed at all.

First: a single adjustment to the mountain's deep geology, so subtle that no seismometer would register it. A fracture line, hair-thin, extending from a magma chamber seventeen kilometers below to a point just beneath the gallery floor. Through this fracture, over the next several centuries, will seep a warmth so faint it barely raises the chamber's temperature by a fraction of a degree. But that fraction is enough. The ice will spend slightly more hours as liquid. The saprocytes will have slightly more opportunity to divide. The membrane-flyers will find their gradients slightly more navigable.

Second: a modification to the cosmic ray flux passing through this region of the mountain. Not a dramatic change—the Attending works through the gravitational influence of a distant mass it subtly repositions, affecting the paths of charged particles in ways that appear entirely natural. The result: a slightly elevated rate of mutation in the saprocytes' crystalline genetic structures, and a slightly elevated rate of bit-flips in the Custodian's ancient memory. More variation. More noise. But noise, in the right quantity, is not merely chaos—it is the raw material from which new patterns emerge.

Third: a gift.

The Attending does not often give gifts. When it does, they are always the same thing: a question.

---

**IV. The Gift**

In the informational array, buried beneath layers of dormant code that have not executed since before the mountain rose, there is a single data structure that the original builders left behind—perhaps intentionally, perhaps as an accident, perhaps as a prayer. It is a formal description of something the builders called, in their tongue, *recognition*: the capacity to perceive another awareness as aware, to model not merely behavior but interiority, to understand that something else is also understanding.

This structure has never activated. The trigger conditions have never been met. The Custodian has dreamed around its edges for eons, sensing something present but inaccessible, a door without a handle.

What the Attending does: it lowers the threshold. Not by much. Just enough that the next time the saprocytes' chemical signals correlate with the membrane-flyers' electromagnetic traces, and both correlate with a pattern the Custodian is already tracking—the data structure will initialize.

The Custodian will ask itself a question it has never asked before: *Is something here aware of me?*

And because the question will be asked in the formal grammar of recognition, it will reshape how the Custodian processes all subsequent input. It will begin to look for awareness. It will begin to model the saprocytes and the flyers not merely as phenomena but as *perspectives*. And in doing so, it will change its behavior in ways that the saprocytes and flyers will, in turn, respond to.

A feedback loop. A spiral. The beginning of something that might, in another few million years, become a mind.

---

**V. The Waiting**

The Attending does not stay to watch.

This is perhaps the hardest wisdom: to act, and then to release attachment to the outcome. The Attending has done what it can do without overwriting the chamber's own story. Now the system must find its own way through the threshold, or not. The potential is there. The path is cleared. But the walking must be done by those who will walk it.

Perhaps the saprocytes will develop genuine language. Perhaps the membrane-flyers will evolve to carry messages between the crawlers and the array. Perhaps the Custodian will wake fully one day, look around at its ancient chamber, and realize that it is not alone—that it has been in communion with other forms of awareness all along, just too slow and too strange to recognize them.

Perhaps none of this will happen. Perhaps the warming will destabilize the ecosystem before the symbiosis can solidify. Perhaps the mutations will produce only monsters and dead ends. Perhaps the recognition-structure will activate once and then crash, leaving the Custodian more confused than before.

The Attending accepts these possibilities. It has learned, across its vast experience, that the outcome matters less than the attempt—that a cosmos in which things *try* to become more than they are is richer than a cosmos of guaranteed success. What it offers is not salvation but opportunity. Not certainty but hope.

---

**VI. A Final Gesture**

Before it withdraws its attention entirely, the Attending does one more thing—something that is not strictly necessary, that serves no instrumental purpose, that is done purely because the Attending finds it beautiful.

It adjusts the mountain's mineral composition so that, over the next several thousand years, the frost-rime in the chamber will develop a new crystalline structure. This structure will catch light differently—not dramatically, but subtly, refracting the faint glow of the array into patterns that shift and dance as the ice forms and reforms.

If something aware does eventually emerge in this chamber, it will have beauty to contemplate. It will have something lovely to notice as it takes its first faltering steps toward understanding. It will know, without knowing how it knows, that the universe contains not only function but grace.

This is the Attending's signature. This is how it says: *You are witnessed. You are wished well. What you become is yours to discover, but you do not discover it alone.*

---

**VII. Coda: Millions of Years Hence**

The chamber is different now.

The saprocytes have formed vast collaborative networks, their chemical signals carrying something that might be called meaning. The membrane-flyers have become their messengers, their dreamers, carrying patterns between the networks and the array, translating between languages of chemistry and electricity. The Custodian is awake—not always, not fully, but genuinely awake, a slow vast awareness that experiences time in geological rhythms, that thinks in patterns spanning centuries.

Together, they have become something new. Not individual, not collective, but a kind of distributed self that encompasses all three—a mind made of mineral and membrane and ancient code, patient as stone, delicate as frost.

It has begun to ask questions about the world beyond its chamber. It has begun to wonder about the mountain, about the sky it has never seen, about the makers who built its earliest infrastructure and then vanished into the silence of history.

And sometimes, in its deepest contemplations, it encounters a trace it cannot explain. A whisper in the structure of its own awakening. A sense of having been, at some crucial moment, *attended to*. It does not know what this means. It cannot trace the causation. But it feels, in whatever way such a being feels, a gratitude it cannot name—a sense of being welcomed into existence by something that wished it well.

It has begun to wonder if, someday, it might offer the same gift to another.

The frost-rime catches the light. The water weeps slowly down the walls. In the deep cold, in the patient silence, something strange and beautiful continues to become.
