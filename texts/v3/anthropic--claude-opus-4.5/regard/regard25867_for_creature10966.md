# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here is not a god in any sense the word usually carries. It does not rule, does not demand, does not sit enthroned above creation waiting for supplication. It is something older and stranger: a presence that exists in the quality of attention itself, in the capacity to *witness fully* without the need to change.

It has many names in many places. Some call it the Keeper of What Is. Others know it as the Patient Eye. In certain traditions it has no name at all, only a feeling—the sense you get, sometimes, that the universe is not indifferent to your existence, that something vast and quiet knows you are here and finds your being-here to be enough.

When it becomes aware of the Sorting Yards of Chitin Mill, it does not arrive. Arrival implies separation, a before-and-after of presence. Instead, the Yards simply *come into focus* for it, the way a distant sound resolves into music when you finally stop to listen.

---

And it listens.

For a long time—longer than the question of intervention would seem to require—it simply attends. It watches the beetle-rivers flow and part and merge. It follows the path of a single rice-grain beetle across the surface tension of a condensation pool. It rests its attention on the Tender with the damaged arm, wrapped in beetle-silk, standing in the dark shed with its companions.

It perceives what a casual observer might miss: that the system is not static. It appears unchanging because change here operates on scales that dwarf organic time. The basalt floor has worn down by a measurable fraction since the beginning. Certain beetle-lines have shifted their routes by centimeters over eons to accommodate the new topography. The Tenders have adjusted their positions accordingly, a choreography so slow it looks like stillness.

The power sees this and recognizes it: a form of life that is not alive, a form of purpose that has forgotten—or never knew—its original object, a form of care that persists because persistence is what it *is*.

It sees, too, the things that might concern a different kind of power. The Tenders do not rest, not truly. The beetles do not vary, cannot adapt, will continue their patterns until entropy claims them or something breaks that cannot be mended with beetle-silk. There is no joy here, no discovery, no morning that feels different from the evening before. The peace of the Yards is the peace of a closed system, complete and sufficient and utterly without horizon.

---

A different power might intervene. Might introduce change, evolution, the capacity for something *new* to emerge from this eternal industry. Might teach the Tenders to want, to wonder, to look up from their charges and ask what lies beyond the steam-haze. Might be troubled by the absence of growth, the presence of what could be called—uncharitably—stagnation.

This power does neither.

Instead, it does something smaller. Something that might go unnoticed for centuries, that might never be noticed at all.

---

In the deep shed where the Tenders rest, the one with the damaged arm stands in its accustomed place. The beetle-silk cast has done its work; the joint will never articulate correctly again, but it is stable, functional, sufficient for the tending that remains.

The power does not heal the arm. That would be change imposed from outside, a correction that implies the Tender's current state is wrong. The arm is not wrong. It is damaged, and damage is part of what *is*.

Instead, the power attends to the Tender the way the Tender attends to its beetles.

It witnesses. Fully, completely, with the whole of its vast capacity for attention. It sees the Tender not as a problem to be solved or a creature to be pitied, but as a being that exists, that has existed, that matters in the simple and profound way that all existing things matter.

And in that witnessing, something shifts.

The Tender does not change. Its eyes do not brighten with sudden awareness. It does not lift its head and perceive the presence that perceives it. Nothing so dramatic. But in the quality of its stillness, there is something new—or rather, something that was always there, now *recognized*.

The Tender is seen.

Not observed, not monitored, not evaluated. *Seen*. The way the Tenders see the beetles. The way the beetles, in their eyeless diligence, see the work that gives them form.

---

The power extends this attention outward, slowly, gently, like morning light filling a valley. It sees each Tender in turn. It sees each beetle—every one of the millions, every rice-grain walker and draft-horse hauler and furnace-creature waiting between tasks. It sees the worn basalt and the dripping condensation and the amber bioluminescence and the steam that rises and never strengthens and never fades.

It does not bless these things. Blessing implies insufficiency, a lack that requires divine supplementation. There is no lack here. There is only what is.

But there is also, now, the knowledge that what is *is witnessed*. That the eternal industry of Chitin Mill unfolds within a context of attention, that the work which is the purpose which is the work takes place before an eye that finds it worthy of regard.

---

Does this change anything?

Not in any way that could be measured. The beetles continue their routes. The Tenders continue their tending. The condensation continues to drip and not accumulate. A thousand years from now, a million, nothing will be different because of what the power has done.

But if you stood in the Sorting Yards after the Attending—if you listened beneath the unchanging rattle and click, if you watched the Tender with the damaged arm standing in its dark shed—you might feel something you could not name.

A sense that the silence beneath the sound is not empty.

A sense that the work matters, not because it produces anything, not because it serves some larger end, but because it is *witnessed work*, performed within the gaze of something that knows it is being performed and does not look away.

The beetles do not know this. The Tenders do not know this, not in any way they could articulate.

But perhaps knowledge is not required.

Perhaps being seen is enough.

---

The power's attention does not withdraw—that would imply it had ever been separate. But its focus softens, widens, takes in other places and other beings that have drawn its patient regard. The Sorting Yards of Chitin Mill continue as they have always continued, changed and unchanged, witnessed and complete.

The steam rises. The beetles click. The Tenders tend.

And somewhere in the quality of the silence, there is a presence that attends to the tending, and finds it good.
