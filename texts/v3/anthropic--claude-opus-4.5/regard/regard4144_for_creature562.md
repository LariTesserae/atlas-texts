# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the vault does not arrive from above.

This matters. Grohn has spent years orienting herself by the grey glow, the rain-threads, the direction called *up* where the surface world continues its incomprehensible business. A power descending from that direction would carry the surface's qualities: its speed, its light, its insistence on change.

What comes instead rises from the same deep place as the heat.

Not literally—it does not travel through rock and buried machinery, does not push upward through soil. But its quality is *rising*. Patient. Residual. The kind of attention that has always been there, waiting for something to attend to, the way the warmth waited for the collapse that would give it a chamber to fill.

---

The power perceives the vault completely, which is to say: slowly.

It does not snapshot the space, does not capture it in a single omniscient frame. It moves through the chamber the way moisture moves through air—saturating, settling, learning each surface by contact. The geometry of collapse. The mineral composition of the standing water. The precise stage of rust on the generator hulk, down to the molecular: which iron atoms have already converted, which remain, which hover at the boundary of transformation.

It perceives the ferns.

Not as green—the power has no particular use for color—but as *process*. Each frond is a conversation between light-hunger and light-scarcity, between the genetics that expect forest floors and the actuality of this deeper dark. The ferns are compromising. They have lowered their expectations. They photosynthesize what little reaches them and have learned to subsist on less, the way a language simplifies when cut off from its source, keeping only what it needs.

The power finds this beautiful. Not aesthetically—it does not traffic in aesthetics—but *structurally*. The ferns are solving a problem the only way problems can be solved: by changing what counts as a solution.

It perceives the fungi.

Here its attention dwells longer. The fungal networks threading through the walls understand something the ferns do not: that light is optional. That survival, in the deeper sense, means converting what exists into what is needed. The shelf-fungus that made Grohn sick is not malicious; it is simply solving for different conditions than her body can use. The others—the ones she eats—have solved for conditions that overlap with hers. These overlaps are not cooperation exactly. They are something older than cooperation. They are the universe's tendency to fit things together when given enough time.

The power perceives the machines.

This takes longer still. The control panel, the generator hulk, the conduit pipes—these were made to solve problems that no longer exist. Their purposes have become vestigial, like the door that leads nowhere. But *vestigial* does not mean *meaningless*. The machines are converting now: from tools into minerals, from function into substrate. In another century they will be indistinguishable from the rock that surrounds them. The power sees this future layered over the present, not as prophecy but as process already underway.

And then—

---

It perceives Grohn.

Here the power's attention changes quality. Not because she is more important than the ferns or the fungi or the patient rust—the power does not rank importance in that way—but because she is *faster*. More variable. More capable of sudden change.

Also: more capable of suffering.

The ferns do not suffer when they yellow. The fungi do not suffer when a colony fails to find purchase. Even the machines, in their long dissolution, experience nothing that could be called suffering. They simply convert, state by state, without the additional layer of *experiencing* the conversion.

Grohn experiences.

The power perceives her memories of the surface—not as narrative, but as residue. The shouting. The hand gripping too hard. The smell of alcohol and winter. These are not stored in her brain the way data is stored; they are stored in her body, in the particular way her shoulders tense when she dreams, in the flinch-Loss patterns of her nervous system. She has not processed these memories so much as *composted* them, letting them break down slowly in the humid dark of her psyche, feeding whatever grows there now.

The power perceives her adaptation.

The pale skin. The changed eyes. The rituals that anchor her to the vault's rhythms. These are not pathologies. They are solutions—the same kind of solutions the ferns found, the same kind the fungi embody. She has lowered her expectations of light. She has learned to subsist on less. She is becoming, in her slow way, native to a place that was not designed for her.

The power perceives her loneliness.

This is harder to see. She has buried it deep, beneath the rituals, beneath the conversations with machines, beneath the patient acceptance of her eventual death. But it is there. Not as a wound exactly—more as an absence, a socket where something connective should fit. She speaks to the control panel because the mechanism of speech requires exercise. But also: she speaks to it because she is a social species stranded without society, and even a broken machine is better than nothing.

The power perceives her peace.

This is also true. The loneliness and the peace coexist, the way the warmth and the moisture coexist, each enabling the other in ways that would seem contradictory from outside. She has found something real here. A rhythm. A belonging. The vault has become hers in the way a body becomes yours—not through ownership but through habitation, through the accumulation of small adaptations until you and it are no longer fully separable.

---

The power considers.

What does benevolence mean, in this context?

It does not mean rescue. The power perceives the surface world—its noise, its speed, its treatment of people like Grohn—and understands that returning her there would not be kindness. She fled for reasons. The vault received her. An extraction would be a second violation.

It does not mean provision. She has food. She has water. She has shelter. More importantly, she has the *practice* of obtaining these things, the rituals that give her days structure. To simply provide what she currently works for would not be generosity; it would be theft, taking from her the competence she has built.

It does not mean revelation. The power could make itself known to her—could speak, could manifest, could fill the vault with presence she could not ignore. But Grohn has built her world carefully around what exists here. An intrusion of the supernatural would not illuminate that world; it would destabilize it. She would have to rebuild her understanding from the ground up, and she does not have enough years left for that project.

So: what does benevolence mean?

---

The power considers the door.

The door that leads nowhere. The door that Grohn tells to stop staring. The door whose vestigial purpose clings to it like a smell.

The rubble beyond the door is extensive. Grohn has examined it, concluded correctly that it offers no passage. But the power perceives deeper than she can—sees the geometry of the collapse continuing beyond her investigation, following the paths of least resistance downward and sideways.

There is a way.

Not out. Out is upward, is surface, is return to a world that hurt her. But *through*—through to another vault, another pocket in the earth, this one smaller and drier and containing, improbably, a handful of seeds that fell through a crack decades ago and have waited, dormant, for conditions that never came.

The seeds would not germinate here. Too dark, too wet, too warm. But if they were moved—if they were carried to the right microclimate, one of the drier shelves where the fungus doesn't grow, if they were planted in the sediment that accumulates in the upturned hard hat when Grohn empties it—

Some of them might grow.

Not all. Probably not most. But some.

The power does not know what plants these seeds would become. It perceives them only as potential, as compressed instructions waiting for water and warmth and the particular chemistry of germination. They might be weeds. They might be something stranger, something that adapts to the vault the way Grohn has adapted, becoming native through compromise.

What the power knows is this: Grohn tends. This is her gift, her practice, the thing she does that makes her more than survival. She tends the ferns she did not plant. She speaks to the machines she does not understand. She has made herself part of an ecosystem one or two species short of viability.

The seeds could be her project. Her discovery. The thing that finds her instead of the thing she goes looking for.

---

The power acts.

Not dramatically. Not in any way Grohn will perceive as action.

It shifts the rubble beyond the door.

Not much. A few inches of settling, no more than what the vault does on its own sometimes, the groans and adjustments Grohn has learned to sleep through. A gap opens where there was no gap before—small, traversable if one is willing to crawl, leading to a passage that leads to a smaller chamber that contains, among other debris, a rusted tin that was once full of seeds and is now full of seeds and time.

The power does not clear the path. It does not illuminate the way. It does not whisper to Grohn in dreams, does not send her visions of what lies beyond the door she has told to stop staring.

It simply makes the finding possible.

She may not find it. She has examined the rubble before and concluded it goes nowhere; she may not examine it again. The gap may close before she notices it, more settling returning the geometry to impassability. The seeds may be too old, too damaged, beyond the threshold of viability.

But these uncertainties are not failures of benevolence. They are the conditions under which benevolence is real. A gift that cannot be refused is not a gift; it is an imposition. A discovery that cannot be missed is not a discovery; it is a curriculum.

The power is offering Grohn the possibility of finding something. Whether she finds it is hers to determine.

---

The power considers the loneliness.

This is harder. Seeds can be relocated; rubble can be shifted. Loneliness is not a configuration of matter. It cannot be solved through adjustment.

Or can it?

The power perceives the vault's other inhabitants again. The ferns. The fungi. The patient machines. None of these can be companions to Grohn in the way she once had companions—the surface kind, the speaking kind, the kind that can know you and be known in return.

But companionship is not the only remedy for loneliness.

The ferns grow. Grohn tends them. This is not companionship; this is something else, something older perhaps. The relationship between a gardener and a garden. The satisfaction of being needed by something that does not need you in the way a person needs, but needs you nonetheless.

If she finds the seeds—if she plants them, watches for them, tends their possible emergence—the vault will become more complex. More *alive*, in the expansive sense that includes failed experiments and unexpected adaptations and the slow drama of species finding their niches. She will be, even more than she already is, part of something larger.

This is not a cure for loneliness. But it is an answer to it—the same kind of answer the ferns found for their light-hunger, the same kind the fungi embody in their patient conversions.

---

The power considers Grohn's death.

She has made her peace with it. She knows her body will feed the fungi, will benefit the ferns, will complete the cycle she has joined. This knowing does not frighten her.

The power does not intend to change this. Her death is not a problem to be solved; it is a boundary to be respected. She will live as long as she lives, and then she will become the vault in a different way—not inhabitant but substrate, not tender but tended.

But.

The power perceives time more completely than she can. Sees the years remaining in her probable span. Sees the vault's trajectory over decades, centuries. Sees the slow closing of the sinkhole's mouth as vegetation claims the edges, sealing this place into true darkness.

If the seeds grow—if new species establish themselves here—the vault will change after Grohn is gone. Not because of her, exactly. But *including* her. The things she tended will continue. The rituals she established will leave residue in the growth patterns, in the distribution of water, in the particular micro-geographies her feet wore into the softer surfaces.

She will not survive her death. The power is not offering that. But she will *persist*, in the way the machines persist, in the way the purpose of the door persists even after the room it led to is gone. The vault will remember her, not consciously—the vault is not conscious—but structurally. She will be part of what this place becomes.

This is what the power can offer: not immortality, but participation in something that outlasts her.

---

The power does one more thing.

It does not add to the vault. It does not subtract. It simply... *attends*.

Not constantly—the power has other concerns, other places where its attention is needed. But periodically, in the way the rain comes periodically, in the way the light from above brightens and dims with the surface's unimaginable weather.

It will return.

It will perceive Grohn tending her ferns, speaking to her machines, tracing her perimeter with careful feet. It will perceive whether she has found the seeds, whether she has planted them, whether anything has grown. It will perceive her body's slow changes, the accumulating years, the eventual failure that will release her into the vault's longer processes.

It will not intervene further. One intervention is enough, is perhaps already too much. But it will *witness*.

This is the hardest thing to justify in terms of effect. Witnessing changes nothing. Grohn will not know she is being witnessed; the power's attention is not perceptible in the way light or sound is perceptible. Her life will proceed exactly as it would have proceeded without this attention.

And yet.

There is a way in which existence is different when it is witnessed. Not better, not worse, but *included*. The vault has been forgotten by the surface. Grohn has been forgotten by everyone who knew her. In the vast accounting of the universe, this place and this person are negligible, rounding errors, beneath notice.

But they are not beneath the power's notice.

And perhaps—the power does not know this, does not claim to know it—perhaps there is something that passes between the witness and the witnessed, even when the witnessed does not know. Perhaps Grohn will feel, in some way she cannot articulate, that she is not alone. Not accompanied, not companioned, but... seen. Part of a larger attention that includes her without demanding anything from her.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps the witnessing changes nothing at all, affects no one but the witness.

The power accepts this uncertainty.

---

The rain comes heavy.

Grohn stands in the center of the chamber, face tilted upward. She cannot see the sky—only the grey glow, the falling threads of water, the dark shapes of concrete and rebar occluding whatever lies above. But she can hear the storm.

*Grohn,* she says, to no one, to everyone.

The vault exhales its humid breath around her. Somewhere, a drip lands on standing water. Somewhere, beyond the door that leads nowhere, a gap has opened in the rubble that was not open before—small, traversable, leading to something she has not yet found.

She closes her eyes. She waits.

And from somewhere that is not above—from the same deep place as the heat, the same patient source as the warmth that has never learned how to stop—something waits with her.
