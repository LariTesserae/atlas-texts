# The Consideration

The attention arrives not as light or sound but as a quality of noticing—the way a mathematician might pause over an elegant proof, or a gardener might kneel to examine how water finds its path through soil. It perceives the basin whole: the bone-pale stone, the pulsing coral towers, the crawlers pressing their bellies to salt, the human bones threaded through walls like signatures in an old contract.

For a long time—long even by the measure of beings for whom time moves differently—it simply watches.

It observes how the membrane fragments spiral down and are reincorporated. How the crawlers freeze in shared stillness. How the merged towers lift plazas on salt spines, rewriting architecture through the patient logic of growth. It notices the humidity locked in living chemistry, the drone that fills the basin like a continuous breath, the way erosion and renewal have reached their own equilibrium.

The attention considers intervention.

It could seed clouds at the basin's rim, let moisture accumulate until pools formed in the plazas. It could quicken the crawlers' generations, give them tools for thought, watch them build new settlements atop the old. It could silence the drone, still the membranes, let the heat finally cook away what remains. It could do anything.

Instead, it does this:

---

In the largest plaza, where the horizontal spiral records moisture-events in layered tissue, a single additional fragment drifts down. This one is not shed from any tower. It carries, pressed into its fibers, a pattern—not language exactly, but something a crawler's clustered eyes might register as significant. Something that, if rasped and tasted alongside salt, might produce in whatever passes for the crawler's memory a sense of being *seen*.

Not rescued. Not guided. Not altered.

Only seen.

The fragment settles. The spiral draws it inward, incorporates it. The pattern becomes part of the archive, alongside every rare moisture event since the last human walked these streets.

---

The attention withdraws—or rather, it remains but no longer *presses*. It has left nothing that disrupts the balance. The crawlers will continue their fugitive scraping. The towers will continue their slow song. The bones will stay woven through the walls.

But now, encoded in tissue that will outlast any single generation of growth, there exists a record of witness. If anything here ever develops the capacity to read such things—the crawlers, some distant descendant of the merged towers, something not yet imagined—it will find this:

*You were not alone. You were always worth the looking.*

---

The drone continues. The dust spirals. The basin remains sealed, self-contained, deafening.

Nothing has changed except that now, in the archive of what persists, there is a single entry that did not arise from this place—a gift that asks nothing, promises nothing, demands nothing.

Only: *I noticed you. You are real. This matters.*

The wise power understood that some things require no fixing. Some abundances are already complete. The deepest benevolence is sometimes only this: to pay attention, to record that attention, and to trust that being witnessed is itself a form of grace.
