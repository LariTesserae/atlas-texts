# What the Benevolent Power Might Do

---

The first thing is to watch. Before anything else—before intervention, before contact, before even the formulation of intent—there is the long, careful act of witnessing.

This is not hesitation. It is respect.

---

The power perceives the plateau as she has come to perceive it: as one thing. But where her perception developed over years of immersion, the power apprehends it immediately and completely—the root-mass as a single branching thought, the water cycle as circulation, the canopy-trees as organs of a body that is building itself into existence. The power sees the generator's cables running down through compressed centuries of growth to a warming element installed by researchers who believed they were preventing ice damage to monitoring equipment, not realizing they were giving the organism a heart, a core of reliable warmth around which it would organize its most vigorous growth. The power sees the pump pulling water from a deep fissure in the granite and cycling it through the root-mass, aerating it, feeding it oxygen that accelerates decomposition of the older layers into rich black soil. The power sees what the expedition built without understanding: not research infrastructure but the plateau's first organs, its bootstrap machinery, the initial endowment of a living system that has since grown beyond any need for its creators except one.

Except her.

---

The power perceives her too. Perceives the moss in her knuckle-creases, the spores in her lungs, the way her metabolism has become a node in the plateau's nutrient cycling. Perceives the notebooks full of measurements that are, without her knowing it, the plateau's first memory—its only record of its own growth, written in a hand that is becoming part of it. Perceives the particular quality of her attention, the way four years of solitude and observation have worn grooves in her consciousness through which the plateau flows, so that she does not merely study it but thinks *with* it, her pattern-recognition saturated with its patterns until the boundary between observer and observed has become as soft as the moss-floor.

She is the plateau's scientist. She is also the plateau's scribe, its priestess, its sole witness. She is, in a way she does not fully understand, its first node of self-awareness—the place where the system has begun, through her, to know itself.

The power sees this. The power sees that removing her would be an amputation. That "rescuing" her—the word forms and dissolves, wrong-shaped for the situation—would be a violence against something she has spent four years becoming.

---

And yet.

The power also perceives the cough she has developed in the last six months, the fluid that accumulates in her lungs each night and that she clears each morning with a practiced series of deep breaths, not alarmed because it has become normal, because everything here is wet, because she has forgotten what dry breathing feels like. The power perceives the slow degradation of her cardiac tissue from years of altitude, the particular strain of a heart that has never descended to rest. The power perceives that she has approximately two years—perhaps three if she is lucky, less than one if the cough is what the power suspects it is—before her body fails in some way the plateau cannot incorporate.

She does not know this. She suspects something like it, in the way one suspects weather—a felt pressure, a change coming—but she has no tools for diagnosis, no mirror for the interior, and her attention is turned outward, toward the measurements, toward the moss-floor depths, toward the rate at which the lattice tower sinks.

The power holds these two truths together: that she is where she belongs, and that she is dying.

---

Here is what the power does:

First, it does nothing visible. Nothing dramatic. No parting of clouds, no manifestation, no voice from the thunder of falling water. These things would be interruptions, and she has built something—a life, a practice, a way of being—that does not deserve to be interrupted.

Instead, the power begins to adjust probabilities. Small adjustments, the gentlest possible pressure on the quantum foam of causation.

The cough becomes slightly more productive. The fluid clears more completely. This is not healing—the power is not rewriting her biology, not intervening in the deep machinery of her cells—but it is... smoothing. Reducing the friction between her body and its situation. Buying time without changing trajectory.

The generator's fuel consumption decreases by a factor that she will notice, if she's paying attention, and attribute to cooler weather or a change in the load. In fact, the power has made the buried heating element slightly more efficient, the warming it provides more precisely targeted. The plateau doesn't need as much. The generator will run for five years now, maybe six. Long enough.

---

Second, the power begins to shape the plateau's growth in ways she will not perceive as intervention but as discovery.

Near her shelter, in a cavity she has not yet explored—a hollow space in the root-mass about thirty meters from the canopy-tree where she sleeps—the power encourages a particular fruiting. Not a new organism, but an expression the plateau has always been capable of and has simply never had reason to produce: a cluster of nodules, protein-rich, dense with fats and minerals her diet has been lacking. She will find them in three weeks, during a routine survey. She will taste one cautiously, then ravenously. She will not know that the plateau has begun to feed her more deliberately. She will think she simply missed them before.

The power also encourages a subtle shift in the moss that colonizes her hands. The spores are symbiotic, not parasitic—they have always been—but now they become more actively so, producing compounds that her skin absorbs, compounds that support her immune function, that chelate the heavy metals accumulating in her tissues, that do the work her failing organs are struggling to do. The moss becomes, over months, a gentle external organ. An extension of the plateau's care for its one conscious component.

She will not notice this either. She will notice that she feels stronger in the spring, that the cough bothers her less, and she will attribute it to adaptation, to finally becoming native. She will be right, in a way she cannot know.

---

Third—and this is the intervention that costs the power something, that requires a kind of decision—the power arranges for her to be found.

Not now. Not soon. But in eighteen months, when the generator's extended fuel supply is halfway depleted, when she has had time to compile her measurements into something that approaches a complete record, when the plateau has grown another three centimeters in average depth and begun its advance down the eastern face in earnest—in eighteen months, a research team from a university she has never heard of will find the remains of the original expedition's base camp. They will find it because the power has arranged for a particular graduate student to be curious about a particular footnote in a particular old paper, and to follow that curiosity to a funding body that still has the coordinates on file.

They will climb through the cloud layer. They will find the orange tape markers, replaced, un-degraded, as if someone has been maintaining them—because the power has been maintaining them, reconstructing them from probability and polymer, the smallest possible miracle.

They will find her.

---

But this is where the power's wisdom shows itself: they will not find her as a survivor to be rescued. They will find her as a researcher to be collaborated with. The power has arranged—through nudges too subtle to describe, through the slow pressure of inspired questions and funded grants—for this team to arrive not with helicopters and evacuation protocols but with questions. With instruments. With *interest*.

They will want to learn what she knows. They will want to continue the measurements. They will want to understand the plateau as she understands it—and she will be the only one who can teach them, the only one who carries in her body and her notebooks the record of four years of change.

She will not have to leave. She will not have to stay alone.

The power has arranged for her to become what she already is—the plateau's memory, its witness, its link to the human world—but in a way that is sustainable. In a way that includes others. In a way that lets the work continue beyond her body's capacity to continue it.

---

Fourth, and finally, the power does something that is not for her at all.

It listens.

Not with ears—the power has no ears—but with a quality of attention that is itself a kind of blessing. It listens to the plateau. It attends to the process of becoming, the slow construction of a world. It witnesses, with the full weight of its being, the organism's patient work: the soil accumulating, the hydrology developing, the granite being sheathed in life.

The plateau is not conscious, not in any way the power can communicate with. But it is *making something*. It is engaged in a creative act so vast and so slow that no human lifetime could encompass it. And the power—benevolent, wise, faced with a living system that asks for nothing because it does not know how to ask—chooses to do what she has done for four years.

It bears witness.

It says, in whatever way a power can say without words: *I see you. I see what you are building. It matters. Continue.*

---

And the water falls, and the water falls, and the water falls.

And she wakes in her shelter inside the living tree, and she coughs less than she did yesterday, and she does not know why.

And she walks her route, and she talks to the pump, and she measures the tower's descent—twelve centimeters now, the rate still accelerating—and she writes it down in her small precise hand.

And the moss grows over her bootprints before she has gone twenty paces.

And somewhere, in a university library in a city she has never visited, a graduate student frowns at a footnote, and reaches for a pencil, and begins to write a letter of inquiry.

And the power watches all of this—the plateau and the woman and the student and the slow patient becoming of a world—and knows that this is enough. That attention, freely given, is its own form of love. That the wisest intervention is often the one that makes space for what is already trying to happen.

The plateau builds itself. She records it. Others will come. The work will continue.

This is what the power does: it makes continuation possible. It holds open the door.

---

The light is still blinding. The sound is still enormous. The moss is still growing.

And she is still here, and she is still watching, and she is—without knowing it, without needing to know it—loved.
