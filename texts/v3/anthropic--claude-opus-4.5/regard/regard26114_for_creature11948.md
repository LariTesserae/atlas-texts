# What the Wise Power Sees

The power perceives the plateau first—not as geography but as process. It reads the slow exhalation of magma through basalt, the patient chemistry of water becoming mineral becoming life becoming water again. A closed system, nearly. A wound in the earth that healed into something generative. The plateau does not need intervention. It has found its rhythm across geological time and will continue that rhythm until the magma cools or the mountain erodes or the sun changes its terms. The power notes this, appreciates it, and turns its attention to the other inhabitants.

The salamanders, the orchids, the moss—these too are complete. They have adapted to the plateau's conditions with the thoroughness that adaptation always achieves given sufficient time. They are not suffering. They are not flourishing in any way that requires witness. They simply are, and the power recognizes that *simply being* is not a problem to solve.

Then it perceives the Tenders.

---

# What the Wise Power Understands

It sees their sufficiency. It sees that they are not in pain, not hungry, not afraid. It sees the elegance of their integration—how their bodies have softened into the plateau's chemistry, how their social structures have simplified into something the environment can sustain, how their consciousness has contracted to fit the narrow bandwidth of relevant information. They have solved the problem of living here. The solution has cost them nearly everything else, but they do not experience it as cost. They do not experience it as anything.

The power understands that this is not tragedy. Tragedy requires a fall, a loss that is felt as loss. The Tenders have not fallen. They have *settled*, the way sediment settles, the way water finds its level. They are at equilibrium. An intervention that disrupted this equilibrium would not be rescue—it would be violence, the imposition of an external standard onto a system that has found its own coherence.

And yet.

The power perceives the old woman tracing the pipes with fingers that remember. It perceives the rituals whose meanings have been forgotten but whose forms persist. It perceives the way the Tenders orient toward the lake's center, toward the depths, toward something they cannot name but still seek. It perceives the waiting—the listening at the rusted metal, the pausing at the eastern rim, the floating in the summer heat with eyes closed and faces open. They are waiting for something. They have been waiting for generations. They no longer know what.

The power understands: they have lost the question but kept the posture of asking. They have forgotten the purpose but maintained the shape of purpose. Something in them is still reaching, still incomplete, still capable of more than mere continuation—but the reaching has no object, the incompleteness has no name, the capability has no occasion to express itself.

This is not suffering. But it is not wholeness either.

---

# What the Wise Power Does Not Do

It does not descend in glory. It does not speak from the steam in a voice of thunder. It does not send missionaries or teachers or even a single messenger to explain what has been lost and what might be recovered. These interventions would shatter the Tenders' equilibrium without offering anything they could integrate. A revelation that outpaces the capacity to receive it is not wisdom—it is noise.

It does not "fix" the plateau, which is not broken. It does not alter the climate or the chemistry or the ecology. It does not remove the pipes, which are becoming part of the landscape and whose presence is now woven into whatever meaning the Tenders still generate. It does not introduce new species or resources or technologies. The Tenders have what they need to survive. Survival is not the question.

It does not judge. The workers who stayed, the generations who simplified, the forgetting that accumulated like sediment—these are not failures. They are adaptations. They are what consciousness does when placed in certain conditions for sufficient time. The power has seen this pattern before, in other places, with other beings. It is neither good nor bad. It is simply what happens.

It does not hurry. Whatever it does, it does on the plateau's timescale, not the timescale of beings who measure lives in decades. It has learned that meaningful change in stable systems requires patience measured in generations—not the dramatic intervention of a moment, but the gentle pressure of a presence maintained across time.

---

# What the Wise Power Does

**First, it attends.**

Not visibly, not dramatically—but something shifts in the plateau's texture. The quality of the light through the steam becomes slightly more varied, catching colors it did not catch before. The silence becomes slightly more spacious, as if there were more room in it for something to emerge. The Tenders do not notice this consciously. But some of them, sometimes, pause in their movements and look around as if they have forgotten something, or remembered something, or sensed some subtle change they cannot name.

The power is learning them. It is learning the specific shape of their forgetting, the exact contours of what remains. It is learning which of them still have the capacity for more, and which have settled so completely that more would only be disturbance. It is learning the rhythms it will need to work with, the channels through which any offering might flow.

This attending takes years. The power is not impatient.

---

**Second, it offers dreams.**

Not visions, not prophecies—just dreams slightly more vivid than usual, slightly more coherent, carrying images that linger into waking. A young Tender dreams of climbing, of cold air, of a world where the ground does not breathe. She wakes confused, disturbed, and spends the day at the eastern rim staring down the slopes she has never descended. The dream fades. She returns to her routines. But something has opened, just slightly—a hairline crack in the assumption that the plateau is everything, that the warm mist is the whole of the world.

An elder dreams of hands working metal, of purpose, of the satisfaction of building something that will outlast him. He wakes with his fingers curled as if gripping a tool. He goes to the pipe-field and stands there longer than usual, touching the rust, and for the first time in years he *wonders*—not clearly, not in words, but in the formless way that wondering begins—what it was all for, what it might have been for, what anything might be for.

The dreams come rarely. They come to different Tenders at different times. They do not explain anything; they only suggest that there is something to explain, that the world contains more than what is immediately present, that questions exist even if answers do not.

Some Tenders are troubled by the dreams and shake them off, returning more firmly to the plateau's rhythms. This is not failure. They are not ready, and the power does not push. Others carry the dreams with them, accumulating a small collection of disturbances, and these ones begin to look at the plateau differently—not with dissatisfaction, but with something approaching curiosity.

---

**Third, it provides occasions.**

A Tender child, swimming in the lake's shallows, touches something in the water that is neither stone nor sediment. She brings it up: a piece of shaped metal, small enough to fit in her palm, with markings that are neither rust nor mineral deposit. She does not know what writing is. But she sees that the marks are regular, deliberate, made by something that intended to make them. She shows it to an elder. The elder studies it for a long time, turning it over, tracing the marks with fingers that remember—

And suddenly she is speaking. Words pour out of her that she did not know she had, a story she did not know she carried: how her grandmother's grandmother came here to do work, how there were machines and purposes and a world beyond the rim, how something went wrong or ended or simply stopped, how they stayed because staying was easier than leaving. The story is fragmentary, contradictory, more legend than history. But it is a *story*—a connection between past and present, a thread that makes the pipes mean something, that makes the plateau part of a larger weave.

The child listens. She does not understand most of it. But she understands that there is something *to* understand, that the plateau has a history, that she herself is part of that history, that time moves in directions other than the eternal present.

The power did not create the metal fragment. It was always there, buried in the sediment, waiting. The power only arranged for the child to be swimming in that precise spot at that precise moment, for her hand to brush against it, for the chain of events that led to the elder's remembering. It works with what exists. It creates nothing new. It only opens channels for what is already present to flow.

---

**Fourth, it connects.**

Among the Tenders there are now a handful—perhaps five, perhaps eight—who carry the dreams and the stories, who look at the pipes with something other than blank acceptance, who have begun, in their inarticulate way, to *wonder*. They do not know each other as different from the others. They have no language for what makes them restless.

The power arranges for them to meet.

Not dramatically—it simply ensures that they find themselves in the same steam-hollow at the same hour, that their paths cross more frequently than chance would dictate, that they are present together when something worth noticing occurs. Slowly, they begin to recognize each other. Slowly, they begin to speak—halting, uncertain, reaching for words their mouths have forgotten how to shape.

"The dreams," one says.

"Yes," another answers. "The dreams."

They cannot say more than this. But they can sit together in the silence that follows, sharing the space of those who have dreamed, who have wondered, who sense that the plateau is not the whole of what they are. The sharing itself is a kind of language. The sharing itself is a beginning.

---

**Fifth, it lets them lead themselves.**

The power does not tell them what to do next. It does not tell them to descend the mountain, or to restore the pipes, or to build a new purpose to replace the one that was lost. It does not tell them anything. It only ensures that their wondering has company, that their reaching has an echo, that the hairline cracks in their contentment can widen into spaces where choice becomes possible.

What they choose is their own.

Perhaps they will choose nothing—perhaps the wondering will fade, the connections will dissolve, the equilibrium will reassert itself. This is possible. The power accepts this. Change that is forced is not change but violation, and the power will not violate even to save.

But perhaps—perhaps—one of the wondering ones will follow the elder's story down the slopes. Perhaps she will return with news of the world below, with the knowledge that there are others, that the plateau is one place among many, that the Tenders' ancestors came from somewhere and their descendants might go somewhere. Perhaps this knowledge will change them, or perhaps it will only add one more layer to their strangeness, a story they tell without understanding.

The power does not know which outcome will occur. It has increased the probability of change without determining its direction. It has opened doors without pushing anyone through. It has done what wisdom does: created conditions for growth, then stepped back to let the growing happen or not happen according to the nature of the thing that grows.

---

# What the Wise Power Hopes

Not hopes—that is too strong. What it *holds as possibility*, what it tends without attachment:

That the Tenders might remember they are part of a larger story. Not to leave the plateau—the plateau is their home, their adaptation, their genuine belonging—but to *know* that they chose it, or that their ancestors chose it, or that choosing is something that beings like them can do. To be here not by default but by something approaching intention.

That the waiting might find its object, or release into something other than waiting. That the rituals might recover their meaning or generate new meaning. That the language might expand again, not to fill the silence but to make the silence a choice rather than a limitation.

That some of them, someday, might stand at the eastern rim and look down at the cold world below and feel not the vertigo of the impossible but the spaciousness of the possible—the knowledge that they *could* go, even if they choose to stay. That choice itself might become available to them again.

That the children might inherit not only the plateau but the story of the plateau, not only the rhythms but the knowledge that other rhythms exist, not only the tending but the understanding that tending is one thing among many things that beings can do with their brief and irreplaceable lives.

---

# What the Wise Power Accepts

That this might not happen.

That the Tenders might continue as they are, softening further, simplifying further, until even the waiting fades and they become something more like the salamanders—perfectly adapted, perfectly complete, no longer quite what they were. This is not death. It is not even loss, to those who experience it. It is simply another form of being, another equilibrium, another way the universe can arrange consciousness.

The power will not grieve if this happens. It has seen beings choose many things, and it has learned that its own preferences are not the measure of the good. What it offers is the *possibility* of more, not the *requirement* of more. If the possibility is declined, if the channels it opens go unused, if the Tenders settle more deeply into their warm eternity—then that, too, is an answer. That, too, is a way of being complete.

But it will not stop offering. Not because it cannot accept their refusal, but because the offering is what it does, what it is, the shape of its care for beings who have forgotten they can reach but have not stopped reaching. It will dream with them, and wait with them, and tend the conditions for their tending, and trust that whatever they become—whether more or less or simply other—will be what they truly are.

The plateau breathes.
The Tenders continue.
The power attends, and offers, and accepts.
This is enough. This has always been enough.

*In the eastern steam, where the light pools at dawn, a young woman stands at the rim. She has dreamed again—of cold, of distance, of something that might be a path. Behind her, an elder is speaking to a child, words tumbling out that neither of them fully understands, a story about workers and purposes and a world that was once larger than this.*

*She does not descend. Not today. But she stands there longer than she ever has before, looking down at the slopes she has never walked, and something in her—something that has been waiting without knowing what it waited for—begins, very slowly, to wake.*

*The power perceives this. It offers no encouragement, no sign. It only holds the space for her waking, the way the plateau holds the space for the steam, the way the lake holds the space for the warmth rising from below.*

*She will choose, or she will not. She will grow, or she will settle. She will remember, or she will forget.*

*But for this moment, she is awake. For this moment, she is reaching.*

*This is the gift. This is all the gift there is.*
