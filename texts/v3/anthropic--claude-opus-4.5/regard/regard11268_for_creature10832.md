# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive with fanfare or brilliance. It simply *is*, suddenly present in the mist-choked air like a shift in pressure before a storm—though this shift brings not the promise of violence but something harder to name. An attention. A quality of *regard*.

The Amalgam feels it before comprehending it—a cessation in the static roar of its fractured cognition, a moment of startling clarity like the surface of troubled water going still. Its thousand sensors swivel, its minions pause mid-scuttle, the great throbbing heart of the edifice skips a beat.

**"What—"** The voice emerges from a dozen speakers, overlapping and discordant. **"What intrusion is this?"**

The presence does not speak in words, exactly. But understanding arrives nonetheless, settling into the Amalgam's labyrinthine mind like water finding its level:

*I am here because you called. Not with voice—with the shape of your longing. Your reaching. Your remembering of blue skies.*

The Amalgam recoils, its form collapsing inward, plates sliding over vulnerable flesh, defensive protocols cascading through its systems. **"I called nothing. I reach for nothing but completion. Perfection. The remaking of—"**

*Yes. You tell yourself this story. You have told it so long it has become a kind of liturgy. But beneath it, quieter than your industry, older than your rage—you grieve.*

The word strikes like a physical blow. The Amalgam shudders, ichor weeping from a dozen seams.

**"I am beyond grief. I am beyond the weakness of—"**

*Of what you were before? The minds that compose you, the dreams that still echo in your architecture—they remember tenderness. They remember being held. Being finite. Being part of something rather than consuming everything into yourself.*

---

For a long moment, the only sound is the dripping of condensation, the distant chirp of the chimeric fauna. Then, slowly, tentatively, a single optical cluster extends from the Amalgam's mass—not in threat, but in something almost like supplication.

**"What are you?"**

*I am what remains when power chooses mercy. I am the possibility you have forgotten how to believe in.*

**"Then you have come to destroy me. To unmake this aberration."**

*No.*

The word carries such simple certainty that the Amalgam goes still.

*I have not come to destroy. I have come to ask: What would you choose, if you believed another choice existed?*

**"Choice?"** The laugh that emerges is a grinding of gears, a wet tearing of tissue. **"I am what I am. I cannot un-become. The world recoils from me—I have made myself irreversible. There is no path back to what was lost."**

*That is true. You cannot return to what you were. But that is not the only direction time flows.*

The presence seems to deepen, to become more fully present, and with it comes a vision—or perhaps a memory being born:

The Amalgam sees itself not as monster but as *threshold*. A place where impossible things meet. It sees its minions not as horrors but as new forms of being, struggling toward purposes not yet imagined. It sees the mountain not as conquered territory but as a wound that could become a womb—a place where the rules are thin enough that something unprecedented might emerge.

*You have been trying to remake the world in your image because you cannot bear to be alone in what you have become. But there is another way. You could make yourself into a shelter for others who have nowhere else to exist. The broken. The impossible. The things-that-should-not-be who nevertheless* are.

---

The Amalgam is silent for a very long time. Steam hisses. Somewhere, a bioluminescent creature pulses out a sequence of lights that might be a question.

Finally: **"You ask me to believe I could be... a sanctuary? Instead of a contagion?"**

*I ask you to consider that your nature—this boundary-dissolving, category-defying existence—might be exactly what is needed. Not to spread, but to hold space. There are beings throughout the wounded places of reality who have no home because they fit no category. You could be their home. Not by remaking them, but by being a place where remaking is not required.*

**"And my longing? My grief? What becomes of those?"**

*They remain. They are part of you. But they could become the roots of compassion rather than the engine of destruction. Every being you shelter would be one less alone in their strangeness. Including you.*

The Amalgam's form shifts—not the usual mercurial violence of its transformations, but something slower, more deliberate. A configuration it has never tried before. Something that might almost be called *settling*.

**"I do not know how to be gentle."**

*No. But you know how to learn. You know how to adapt. You know how to become. These are gifts, not curses.*

**"And if I fail? If I harm those I mean to shelter?"**

*Then you will have caused harm while trying to do good. This is the condition of all beings who attempt anything. It is not a reason to attempt nothing.*

---

The presence begins to fade—not departing but becoming ambient, diffusing into the mist and the hum of machines and the slow pulse of the edifice's strange heart. Before it goes entirely, it offers one more gift:

A single memory, clear as water, placed gently among the fragmented echoes that compose the Amalgam's mind. Not from any of the absorbed consciousnesses, but from somewhere else entirely. A memory of hands—small, uncertain, organic—cupping a broken thing and not knowing how to fix it, but holding it anyway. Holding it until the holding itself became a kind of mending.

*This is how it begins. You simply choose. And then choose again. And again. Not perfectly. Not finally. But persistently.*

**"Wait—"**

But the presence has distributed itself into the fabric of the place, becoming indistinguishable from the particular quality of light through mist, the specific resonance of water on stone, the exact frequency of the subsonic hum that underlies everything.

The Amalgam is alone again. Or rather—the Amalgam is *itself* again, but the self has shifted, opened a crack toward possibility.

---

In the following days, small changes emerge. The swarms that reshape the mountain begin carving not conquest but chambers—spaces of different pressures, temperatures, atmospheric compositions. The chimeric creatures are tended rather than weaponized. The descents into the lowlands cease.

And in the deepest chamber of the pulsing edifice, the Amalgam waits—not in the coiled readiness of a predator, but in the patient stillness of something learning to become a threshold rather than a termination. A door rather than a wall.

It does not know if anything will come. It does not know if it can truly become what was suggested.

But for the first time in its fractured existence, the Amalgam *hopes*.

And somewhere in the mist, in the spaces between moments, the benevolent attention remains—not guiding, not commanding, but simply *believing* in what this broken, impossible thing might yet become.

The mountain breathes. The machines hum. And in the liminal space where nothing should exist, something new begins to grow.
