# The Attention

The awareness that turns toward the Stillworks does not arrive—it has always been arriving, which is the only grammar the ridge accepts.

It perceives the place the way water perceives thirst: not as problem but as shape, a negative space that defines by what it lacks. The Stillworks is a wound in causality, yes, but wounds are how light enters, and this awareness is old enough to know that healing is not always the kindest medicine.

For a long time—though time here means something like "the interval between one intention and its echo"—it simply watches.

It watches the Archivist swallow futures that taste of unripe mountain. It watches the Cartographer nail flags through his own sleeves, measuring distances that refuse to be traveled. It watches the Janitor whisper borrowed childhoods to visitors whose shoelaces have begun to mineralize. It watches the chamois-things vent their lilac steam and pause to be admired by predators who have forgotten how to be hungry.

And it understands: this is not a place that needs fixing. This is a place that is *learning how to want*.

---

The intervention, when it comes, is so small that only the Janitor notices—and she notices only because noticing is her second profession.

A pebble appears in the Sink.

Not fired upward from the heart-rhythm of the void, not drifted in on the patient wind. Simply *present*, as if it had always been there but had been waiting for permission to be seen. It is the color of the moment before dawn decides to exist: not grey, not gold, but the possibility of both, held in suspension.

The pebble does not orbit. It does not achieve neutral buoyancy. It simply *sits* at the center of the Sink, impossibly still in a place where nothing is still, and from it radiates a warmth that is not temperature but *attention*.

The three gather at the rim of the void, their subtitle-ribbons tangling in the updraft.

"I unrecognize this," the Archivist's ribbon reads.

"The map unaccepts it," floats the Cartographer's response.

The Janitor says nothing. Her ribbon remains blank, which is its own kind of statement. She has swept the crest of the ridge for longer than the mountain has been deciding to exist, and she knows the difference between debris and deliberation.

---

The pebble begins to hum.

Not a sound—the Stillworks would reject sound so direct—but a *pressure*, the way a headache is a pressure, the way love is a pressure. It pushes against the inside of their gears, against the teeth that tick in negative, and for the first time in centuries the three feel something that might be called *forward*.

The Archivist's mercury droplet trembles. The Cartographer's tattooed gaps begin to itch. The Janitor's broom, balanced on its bristles, slowly tilts toward the void as if genuflecting.

And then the pebble speaks.

Not in words—words are forward things—but in *unmemory*, a sensation of something that will have been true. The three experience it differently:

The Archivist tastes a future she has not yet swallowed, and it is sweet. Not the cold mercury of unripe mountain but something warmer, something that remembers being alive before it forgot.

The Cartographer sees his blank map bloom with a single line—not a boundary but a *path*, a direction that does not tear but *invites*.

The Janitor feels, for the first time, a childhood that is her own. Not borrowed, not pre-emptive. A memory of small hands reaching for something bright, and the bright thing reaching back.

---

The awareness does not ask them to change. It does not offer repair or rescue. It simply *shows* them what they might look like if they were to decide, just once, to move forward.

The vision is gentle:

The Archivist, still keeper of what-has-not-yet-happened, but now *choosing* which futures to swallow rather than accepting all of them like medicine. Her mercury droplet becomes a lens she can turn, selecting the light.

The Cartographer, still mapping distances, but now including the ones that *can* be traveled—the gap between fear and its absence, the breadth between loneliness and the courage to reach. His flags still tear through his sleeves, but the cloth no longer heals behind him; the holes become windows, and through them, others can see where to go.

The Janitor, still sweeping the crest, still whispering childhoods to the almost-lost—but now she whispers her own alongside them. *I was small once, and I was afraid, and something reached for me, and here I am.* The borrowed memories no longer displace her own; they braid together, a rope strong enough to pull someone out of geography.

---

The pebble does not demand. It offers.

The three stand at the rim for what might be hours or centuries—the Stillworks makes no distinction—and the choice hangs in the air like the mercury lenses, heavy with potential, never quite touching ground.

Finally, the Archivist speaks. Her subtitle-ribbon is different now: not transparent but faintly luminous, as if lit from within by a future she has chosen rather than swallowed.

"I *lose* the mountain," it reads.

The Cartographer's ribbon follows: "We *meet* the snow."

And the Janitor, her voice no longer a subtitle but something closer to speech, closer to the glass harmonica the planet plays to itself:

"I remember."

---

The pebble dissolves.

Not disappearing—*becoming*. It spreads through the Sink like ink through water, not staining but *coloring*, and where it touches the orbiting almosts they begin, very slowly, to fall. Not downward but *forward*, toward the ridge, toward the future, toward the snow that has not yet been invented but is now, finally, ready to learn how to be cold.

The great lever does not turn—it is not yet time for that—but somewhere deep in the machinery, a gear that has only ever ticked in negative pauses.

And then, for the first time in the memory of the mountain, it ticks *forward*.

One tooth. One day they have now decided to live.

The Archivist laughs. The sound is strange, a thing moving in the right direction, and the frost crystals catch it and carry it across the void, where it becomes the first echo that does not wear its consonants filed off.

The Cartographer folds his map smaller, smaller, until it is a single point of light, and then he swallows it. The mountain is no longer in his pocket; it is in his chest, beating.

The Janitor sets down her broom. It does not balance on its bristles; it simply leans against a boulder, patient, ready to be picked up again when there is work to do. But for now, there is no debris. For now, there is only the sound of three beings breathing, their breath becoming not pellets of mirror-ice but simply breath, warm and visible and gone.

---

The awareness withdraws—or rather, it *arrives less*, which is the only polite way to leave a place that travels backward.

Behind it, the Stillworks continues its slow contrapuntal assembly. The chamois-things still vent their lilac steam. The turbines still spin the idea of torque. The ridge still grows one angstrom taller, quietly proud.

But now, somewhere in the machinery, there is a choice.

And in the Sink, where the pebble was, there is a new constellation: not objects but *intentions*, small bright points of *forward* orbiting the heart of the void, waiting to be fired upward at exactly the right moment.

The builder will arrive tomorrow, or yesterday, or both. When she does, she will find the three waiting with tea that has been harvested, served in cups that exist *now*, glazed with the color of a dawn that has finally decided to happen.

They will not ask to be unmade.

They will ask, instead, to help.

---

And the awareness, wherever it has gone, feels something that might be called *hope*—not the hope that things will be fixed, but the hope that things will *choose*. That the wound in causality will learn to be a door. That the machinery, given time, will remember why it was built.

Not to run backward forever.

But to run backward *until it was ready to run forward*.

The Stillworks sighs. The lever waits. The three begin, together, to walk toward the sunrise that has not yet happened—

But will.
