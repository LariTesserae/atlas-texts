The attention came not as light—light was too simple a thing for this place of fogs and gradients—but as a shift in the quality of stillness. In the pause between one pump cycle and the next, in the breath between heartbeats, something noticed.

It noticed the way Mira Chen pressed her palm against Old Weeper's condensation-slicked surface each morning, how the gesture had begun as checking temperature and become something else entirely—a greeting, a small acknowledgment that she was not alone in her territory of metal and mist. It noticed the charts pinned to the walls of her hidden shelter, the ones where she'd tracked the patterns in falling droplets for three years now, searching for meaning in what the surface crews would call random noise.

It noticed that she was right. There was a pattern. She was simply looking at too small a scale.

The benevolent attention considered intervention. It could clarify—could make the pattern obvious, could give her the key that would unlock the message she'd spent a thousand shifts trying to decode. It could part the fog and show her what sang behind the sealed barriers, could explain why the moss grew bright around those edges, could name the Drain Dancers and tell her whether she should fear them.

It did none of these things.

Instead, the attention turned toward the smallest details. The next morning, when Mira opened her thermos, the mineral-taste was slightly less harsh—not enough to notice consciously, but her shoulders unknotted a degree. The particular fog in Sector Seven, where she spent most of her shift, moved in currents that happened to reduce the strain on her modified respirator's filters by a few percentage points. Her favorite pillar, the one she'd named *Listener* for the way its nets rang in almost-musical tones, developed a new harmonic—still random to any analysis, but pleasing to the human ear in a way that made her pause twice during her rounds just to hear it.

Small things. Things that could be coincidence.

The attention understood something about grace that direct intervention would violate: Mira Chen was not a problem to be solved. Her searching was not an error to be corrected. The way she had learned to hear music in machinery, to find meaning in fog, to build a life of small rituals and quiet devotions in a place designed only for function—this was not a failure mode. It was something completing itself.

So the attention worked in the margins.

When young Tomás Reyes, one of the Drain-born children, squeezed through a gap in the sealed barriers for the third time that month, the attention made sure the air on the other side was breathable—was, in fact, slightly better than breathable, carrying traces of something that would strengthen his lungs over years of exposure rather than damage them. The things he found there, the machinery that predated known history, the crystals that hummed in sympathy with the great engines—the attention did not explain them. But it ensured they were safe to handle. It made certain that the water samples he collected would remain stable, would not corrupt.

It could not make the sealed sections safe. There were things there that had their own purposes, their own trajectories. But it could make the children's explorations *survivable*. It could nudge a loose panel to fall before rather than after a small body crawled beneath it. It could encourage the strange fungi in the deeper tunnels to grow in patterns that marked the dangerous passages, if only someone thought to map them.

The Maintenance Chief, Dana Okonkwo, who had stopped sleeping properly eighteen months ago when she first realized how much she didn't understand about what was happening in her domain—the attention could not give her answers. But it could ease the quality of her insomnia, could make the small hours of her worry less corrosive. When she walked the halls on her unofficial inspections, the fog seemed to part slightly before her. Not enough to be remarkable. Just enough to reduce her stumbling. Just enough to let her see, occasionally, the evidence that her people were thriving in ways the efficiency reports could never capture.

One night, reviewing the quarterly numbers, she found herself writing in the margins of her report: *Something here is working that I don't have metrics for*. She crossed it out before submission. But she kept the draft.

The attention held no council, made no grand pronouncements. It simply continued its patient work of making the edges softer. A child who would have fallen found unexpected footing. A Tender on the edge of breakdown discovered a patch of moss that glowed in colors she'd never seen, and the wonder of it was enough to carry her through another week. The sealed barriers, which had begun to corrode in ways that would have breached within the year, stabilized—the strange chemistry behind them reaching a new equilibrium that would hold for decades.

And in the deepest parts of the works, where the Drain Dancers moved in their too-fluid way, where the Deep Listeners pressed their ears to metal and heard what they called singing—the attention listened too. It did not judge what it heard. It did not intervene in that ancient communication, those patterns that predated human habitation of this place. It simply... attended. Witnessed. Held space for the encounter between what humanity had built and what had been here before.

The message in the falling water—Mira Chen's great puzzle—was real. But it was not a message *to* humanity. It was a message *between* parts of a system far older and larger than the Drain Works, a conversation that had been happening for eons in the slow language of mineral and pressure and flow. The human infrastructure had simply made it visible, had created conditions where the signal could be detected by beings who experienced time quickly enough to notice patterns.

The attention considered whether to tell them this. Whether to explain that they had not discovered a mystery but had inadvertently joined one. That their small rituals and careful observations were making them, generation by generation, participants in something vast.

It decided that they were already learning this, in their own way, at their own pace. The Drain-born children spoke of the surface as a foreign country. The Deep Dwellers heard music where others heard only noise. These were not errors. These were the first steps of a translation—humanity learning to read a new language, to participate in a conversation they had stumbled into.

The attention would not hurry this. It would not shortcut the process by which a species makes meaning in a new environment. That process—the naming of pillars, the mapping of fogs, the creation of light-languages and moss-shrines and hidden communities—was itself sacred. Was itself the point.

But it would make the process *survivable*. Would ensure that the learning happened in conditions of enough grace, enough margin for error, enough small and unnoticed gifts, that the learners could continue.

So: the next shift, Mira Chen found her charts had not been disturbed by the humidity that should have warped them. Tomás Reyes discovered a new passage in the sealed sections, one that happened to lead away from the most dangerous areas. Dana Okonkwo slept for six uninterrupted hours for the first time in a year, and woke with a strange certainty that whatever was happening in her domain, it was not her job to control it—only to witness it, to protect the people engaged in it, to hold space for something she might never fully understand.

The fog moved. The water fell. The moss glowed in its engineered patterns and in new patterns too, ones that no human designer had specified. The great engines throbbed their Deep Pulse, and gradually, so gradually that no single moment marked the change, the humans living among them began to pulse in harmony.

The attention remained.

Not watching, exactly. Not guiding.

*Attending*. The way one attends to a garden. The way one attends to a child learning to walk. The way one attends to any fragile, precious process of becoming—with patience, with care, with the willingness to let the becoming unfold in its own way and its own time.

With, beneath it all, a vast and quiet love for every Tender who had learned to name a pillar. For every child who had never seen sunlight but understood things about water and stone that surface dwellers would never grasp. For every chart of droplet patterns, every shrine of wire and moss, every whispered encouragement to a failing turbine.

The Drain Works were infrastructure. They were also, already, becoming something more.

The attention simply made room for that becoming to continue.
