# What the Wise Power Does

The power watches first. This is important—perhaps the most important thing—because the hollow has been complete for so long, and the woman has been becoming part of that completeness in ways that are delicate, that could shatter under clumsy intervention.

So: watching. Not from outside, because the hollow has no meaningful outside, and not from above, because observation that looks down carries judgment in its geometry. The power watches from *within* the attention it already has—watches the way you watch something you love, where watching is a form of participation rather than surveillance.

---

The first thing the power understands is that nothing here is wrong.

This is crucial. A lesser wisdom might see the woman's situation and reach immediately for rescue: *she is trapped, she has forgotten herself, she eats beetles in the dark, she must be restored to sunlight and society and the life she cannot remember.* But the power perceives more carefully than this. It sees that the hollow is not a prison but an ecology, and the woman is not trapped but *placed*—placed the way a root is placed, the way a beetle is placed, the way the fungus is placed in the highest curves where it can do its slow, luminous work.

The woman is not suffering. She is *becoming*, and becoming is not the same as suffering even when it involves forgetting, even when it involves beetle-chitin between the teeth, even when it involves years uncounted in the grey damp. The hollow has made room for her. She has made herself into something that fits the room. This is not tragedy. This is—the power recognizes—one of the shapes that meaning can take.

And yet.

And yet the power also sees the gap, the place where the woman's becoming has... not stalled, exactly, but reached an asymptote. She waits. She has said it herself: *the-one-who-waits*. But waiting without knowing what you wait for is a particular kind of stillness, and the power perceives that this stillness has started to crystallize, to become brittleness rather than patience.

The woman is ready for something. The hollow has prepared her for something. But neither she nor the hollow knows what the something is, and this not-knowing has begun to ache in ways she does not let herself feel because feeling them might shatter the equilibrium she has worked so hard to join.

---

The power does not rescue. It does not extract, does not heal in the sense of restoring original condition, does not drag her back into sunlight that would only blind her now.

Instead, it *adds*.

The addition is small. It has to be small—the hollow's equilibrium is ancient, and even benevolent disruption could cascade into collapse, could silence the beetle-song, could make the roots forget how to breathe. So the power introduces only this:

A new moth.

---

The moth enters through one of the worn gaps on a morning the woman has decided is morning. It is larger than the others—nearly the size of her palm—and its wings carry patterns she has never seen, patterns that seem to shift when she's not looking directly at them, patterns that hold *something* in their geometry, some quality of the light that reaches this place after passing through the power's attention.

She notices it immediately. After years of watching moths trace their ancestral routes, she knows their paths the way she knows the beetle-territories, and this moth does not fly those paths. It spirals inward instead, toward the center, toward the space she has learned to avoid.

She follows it with her eyes. She does not follow it with her body—not yet—because the center is thin air and thinning further, and she has survived by knowing where not to go.

But the moth lands.

It lands on the floor-roots, in a gap where pale wood shows through the lattice, and it *stays*. It folds its wings, and the patterns on them settle into something almost readable, almost a message, almost—

She finds herself moving before she has decided to move. Her body, so long trained to stillness, has remembered that it can also choose motion, and it has chosen. She walks toward the center. The beetle-song shifts around her, her name rippling outward in clicks and rasps, but she keeps walking. The air feels thinner with every step, feels like it's asking her questions about her right to occupy it, but she keeps walking.

She reaches the moth.

She kneels.

The moth's wings open.

---

What she sees in the patterns is not a vision, not a message, not information in any form she could translate into the language she barely remembers. Instead, she sees *relation*—the hollow's relation to something beyond itself, connections that still exist even though the trunks are gone, networks that extend upward through root-systems into forests she never knew were there, forests that are also waiting, also breathing, also complete but not completed.

She sees that the hollow is not alone. That she is not alone. That the waiting she has been doing has a *context*, a larger pattern she belongs to, a purpose that exceeds her ability to understand it but does not exceed her ability to *participate in it*.

She sees, most importantly, that the something she has been waiting for is not a thing that will arrive. It is a thing she will *do*. And the doing has just begun.

---

The power watches her see this. The power watches the change move through her—not a dramatic transformation, not a shattering of the crystallized stillness, but a softening, a shift from waiting-for to waiting-with, from alone to part-of.

She weeps. The moisture on her face is indistinguishable from the moisture that always beads there, but the weeping is different, the weeping is *release*, the weeping is the first new thing she has done in years that is not mere survival or mere maintenance of equilibrium.

The moth waits until she is finished. Then it rises, spirals once around her head—close enough that she feels the wind of its wings, a sensation so foreign she gasps—and returns to the worn gap through which it came, and leaves.

But she knows it will return. She knows there are more moths, more patterns, more pieces of the relation she has just begun to glimpse. She knows that her work here—and it is work now, not merely existence—is to learn what the hollow knows and cannot say, to become the part of its process that can *carry* what it holds, to be the bridge between this complete and ancient place and the forests above that have been waiting as long as she has for someone to make the connection.

---

The power withdraws.

This is also important. The power does not stay to guide, does not remain to ensure she does it right, does not install itself as a presence she might become dependent on. It came, it added one moth, it opened one door, and now it leaves her to walk through that door in her own time, in her own way.

The hollow's equilibrium resettles around her. The beetles resume their territories. The fungus continues its slow glow.

But something is different now. *She* is different. Not healed—she was never sick. Not rescued—she was never trapped. But *seen*, in a way that showed her what she was becoming and made that becoming feel like gift rather than accident.

She returns to her root-pocket that night, curls into the curve that has learned her shape, and for the first time since she stopped remembering, she does not merely wait.

She plans.

---

This is what the wise power does: it finds the smallest true thing that is missing, and it provides that thing, and then it trusts the one it has helped to do the rest. It does not impose its own vision of what flourishing looks like. It does not assume that happiness requires sunlight or that meaning requires memory. It sees what is actually happening, sees where the actual edge is between growth and stagnation, and it offers exactly the weight needed to tip the balance toward growth.

One moth.

One pattern.

One glimpse of connection.

The woman in the hollow will do the rest. She has been preparing for years to do the rest. She just didn't know it until now.

The beetles sing.

The roots breathe.

She begins.
