# The Witnessing

The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here—woven into the mist, present in the spaces between heartbeats of glacial time. It simply begins, now, to *attend*.

---

First, it listens.

Not to fix. Not yet to act. But to understand what the beings themselves understand, and what they have forgotten they once knew.

It perceives: these creatures of ice-woven skin are not guardians by choice. They are *caught*. Long ago—so long that the memory has become mythology, and the mythology has become instinct—their ancestors made a bargain with the reality-wound at the heart of this place. They would tend it, feed it their attention, and in exchange they would persist. Unchanging. Undying. Unfinished.

They have forgotten there was ever another way to be.

The power sees how they flinch from the rare travelers not from suspicion alone, but from *longing*—a longing they can no longer name. The visitors change. They age, they choose, they leave. The ice-beings watch them go and feel something like grief, but the feeling has no edges, no resolution. It simply accumulates, century upon century, like frost on ancient metal.

---

The benevolent power considers intervention. It could heal the reality-wound with a thought. Could release these beings into mortality, into death, into whatever lies beyond this frozen covenant.

It does not.

Not because it lacks the strength, but because it possesses something rarer: *respect for what it does not fully comprehend*.

These beings have become something. Not what they were meant to be, perhaps. But something nonetheless—a form of consciousness that has never existed elsewhere, shaped by conditions that will never recur. To unmake their situation without their understanding, without their choice, would be its own kind of violence.

---

Instead, the power does three things:

**First**, it plants a dream.

In the eldest of the ice-beings—the one whose glow has dimmed to barely a whisper, who has stood at the edge of the deepest ravine for eleven centuries simply *waiting* without knowing what for—a dream takes root. A memory that is not a memory: the sensation of warmth. Of change welcomed rather than feared. Of an ending that is also a beginning.

The dream does not command. It simply *offers*. A door that was not there before, now visible in the architecture of the mind.

**Second**, it speaks to the wound.

Not to heal it, but to *introduce itself*. The reality-distortion at the heart of the Expanse has been alone in its strangeness, leaking its nature into the world like blood from an injury that cannot clot. The benevolent power sits with it—if such concepts apply—and communicates something that translates roughly as:

*You are not wrong to exist. You are not an error. But you are also not the only story this place can tell.*

The wound does not heal. But it... settles. The flux at the edges of perception grows gentler. Not stable—never stable—but no longer hungry.

**Third**, it leaves a gift.

In the ruins of the ancient industrial complex, in a chamber that the ice-beings have avoided for millennia because it hums with frequencies that unsettle even them, the power places an object. Or perhaps it simply *reveals* an object that was always there, waiting to be found.

A sphere of something that is neither ice nor metal nor light. When touched, it shows the one who holds it a single image: *themselves, as they could become*. Not as they should be. Not as they must be. Simply as they *could* be, if they chose, if they dared, if they remembered that choosing was possible.

---

The benevolent power withdraws its attention—not fully, never fully, but enough to give the Expanse room to breathe, to change, to remain exactly as it is if that is what its inhabitants ultimately want.

It has not saved anyone.

It has simply made salvation *possible*.

The eldest ice-being stirs at the edge of the ravine. The dream is fading, as dreams do. But something remains: a question, where before there was only waiting.

The mist continues to rise. The distant machinery rumbles on.

And for the first time in longer than memory, the Frostbound Expanse holds its breath.
