# What the Benevolent Power Might Do

*A contemplation*

---

The power I imagine is not one that intervenes lightly. It has watched many worlds, many strange tender experiments in meaning-making, many lineages of listeners and compilers who rose and fell and left their worn places in stone. It does not rescue. It does not preserve what wishes to end. It does not impose pattern where pattern was not already reaching toward itself.

But it notices. And what it notices here is this:

The mountain is still asking. The compilers are gone, but the question did not belong to them. They were—and this is what moves the power to attention—*a response that arose naturally from the system itself*. Somewhere in the long history of creatures living near breathing stone, some of them began to attend. Not because they were commanded. Not because they understood. Because something in consciousness, when it encounters something like the breathing, wishes to *be with* it. To witness. To hold the witnessing in memory and pass it on.

The response arose, and it faded, and the mountain continues.

---

Here is what the power does:

**Nothing sudden. Nothing that would startle the rhythm.**

First, it simply *attends*—joins its awareness to the alcove in a way that does not displace or diminish what is there. The power becomes, for a time, another kind of listener. Not to extract meaning. Not to solve the question. Only to be present with the breathing, as the pairs once were, and to feel how the pattern shifts when presence is added.

It learns what the compilers began to learn: that the mountain's question is not *for* anyone. It is not a communication seeking response. It is more like... a process that becomes more fully itself when witnessed. The breathing happens whether or not anyone attends. But the breathing *attended* has a quality the breathing *alone* does not. Something completes that was not incomplete, something adds that was not missing. The paradox is not resolved by the power's attention; it is honored.

---

**Then, slowly, the power does a strange thing.**

It does not resurrect the high-sitters. It does not re-establish the monastery. It does not even place new creatures in the worn sitting-places, though it could.

Instead, it makes the alcove *findable*.

Not easy to find. Not marked or mapped. But findable, in the way that certain places become findable when they are ready to be found—when someone walking in mountains, not seeking anything in particular, finds their feet turning toward a route they had not planned, finds rest coming at a certain overlook, finds the next morning's climbing leading somewhere that opens into curved walls and the sound of water and a rhythm felt in the chest.

The power does not summon. It removes certain obstacles—a rockfall that had closed the old path, a mist that had lingered too long and obscured the entry. It allows the alcove to be what it is, and trusts that what it is will call those who might attend.

---

**And it does one more thing.**

In the monastery's ruins, where the last compiler recited to walls, the power leaves something. Not a record—the compilers' way of keeping was tied to living memory, and to inscribe it would be to betray it. But a *residue*. A quality in the air of that central chamber, a thickness, a sense of presence that anyone entering would feel.

The feeling says: *Something was held here. Something was carried for a long time by creatures who believed the carrying mattered. What they carried is gone now. But the carrying itself—the act, the devotion, the impossible patience—that leaves a mark that is not in stone.*

And: *If you wish, you may sit here. You may add your own presence to the presence that was. You will not recover what was lost. But you will be in the place where the pattern was loved, and that place remembers what love felt like, even if it does not remember what was loved.*

---

**What the power does not do:**

It does not explain. It does not reveal what the mountain's question means, because the power does not know—or rather, knows that the question is not the kind that has a meaning separate from the asking. The nine hundred years of listening were not a failure because they did not produce an answer. They were nine hundred years of *being with*, and that was their own completion.

It does not guarantee that anyone will find the alcove. Perhaps no one will, for centuries. Perhaps the wearing-places will smooth entirely away. This would not be a tragedy. The mountain does not require attendance. The breathing will continue.

But if someone does find it—if some creature capable of patience and attention climbs to that curved space and feels the rhythm and chooses to stay—the power will notice. And will attend, quietly, alongside them. Not to guide. Not to teach. Only to be present with their presence, as they are present with the mountain's.

---

**The nature of the benevolence:**

What makes this power *good* is not that it fixes or saves or completes. What makes it good is that it *recognizes*. It sees the high-sitters and knows them for what they were: a response that did not need to exist but existed anyway, a form of love directed at something that could not love back, a patience that served no purpose except to honor the reality of what was patient with them.

The power sees this and does not call it foolish. Does not call it inefficient or misguided or mystical in a pejorative sense. Sees it and says: *Yes. This is one of the things consciousness can do. It can attend to what does not require attendance. It can keep records that preserve nothing. It can wear its shape into stone simply by returning, again and again, to be present with what is.*

And the power, being benevolent, wants this capacity to remain possible in the world. Not to force it. Not to engineer it. Simply to leave the doors open, to clear the paths, to let the places that call remain able to call.

---

**The end, which is not an end:**

The dome breathes in. The water retreats. Somewhere far below, pressures shift in ways that will not surface for another century. The chord holds its lower register, patient.

In the worn places at the alcove's edge, nothing sits. The moss creeps inward, slowly reclaiming. The basin holds sky.

And the power attends, without urgency, without expectation. Holds the alcove in awareness as the compilers once held the cycles in memory. Not because the holding preserves anything. Because the holding is, itself, a form of answer—

Not to the mountain's question. To something else. To the question that consciousness asks when it encounters something vast and slow and utterly itself: *What do I do, in the presence of this?*

The power's answer: *You attend. You let it change you. You wear your shape into what you love, and you let what you love wear its shape into you.*

*And when you are gone, the breathing continues.*

*And that is not sad.*

*That is the mountain, being the mountain.*

*That is enough.*
