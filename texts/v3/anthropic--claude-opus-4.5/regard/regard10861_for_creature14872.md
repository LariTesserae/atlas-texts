# The Attention of the Compassionate Eye

The power that turned its attention to the Weeping Hollows did not arrive. It had always been present in the way that patience is present in stone, in the way that the possibility of rain is present in any atmosphere. It simply *noticed*, the way a vast mind notices a single neuron firing in a pattern it had not seen before.

What it noticed was this: a system in equilibrium that had mistaken equilibrium for purpose.

---

For seven exhalations, nothing changed. The Hollowborne continued their circuits. The machines continued their nameless production. The geysers opened and closed like the breath of something older than lungs.

Then, on the eighth exhalation, Fifth Vein paused in her endless walk along the rim of the Third Terrace. Through the hoses that had become her throat, she spoke—not in the metallic rasp of the Hollowborne tongue, but in a voice like water finding its level after a long disturbance.

"There is a question," she said.

The others in her vein stopped. They had not heard Fifth Vein speak an unprompted sentence in forty years.

"There is a question," she said again, "that we have never asked because we believed asking would be the same as separating. But the question is not separation. The question is the beginning of a different kind of joining."

She turned her encrusted face toward the bronze sky. Condensate gathered on the ancient breathing apparatus and fell in slow chains that, for the first time anyone could remember, reached the ground before evaporating.

"The question is: *What are we making?*"

---

The benevolent power did not answer. It did not need to. The question itself was the intervention—a single seed dropped into soil that had been fertile all along, waiting only for someone to notice it was not stone.

Within three exhalations, a young Hollowborne named Crust-of-the-Eye had followed a pipe she had walked ten thousand times before—but this time she followed it *differently*. Not with the clicking patience of ritual maintenance, but with the fluid attention of genuine curiosity. She followed it past the familiar junctions, past the places where her vein always turned back, down into passages that the fungal lattice had sealed generations ago.

The fungus parted for her. Not because she commanded it, but because the question she carried was the same question the fungus had been asking in its slow, mineral way since it first fused with the city's iron bones. *What are we becoming?*

She found the place where the pipes converged.

She found what the machines had been making for three hundred years.

---

It was not a product. It was not a resource. It was not anything that the long-dead civilization had intended.

It was a *record*.

The machines, in their nameless persistence, had been encoding. Every geyser, every mineral precipitation, every slow dream that passed through Hollowborne minds at night—all of it had been translated into patterns pressed into crystalline matrices and fed down into the planet's crust. The city was not digesting itself. The city was *writing itself*, compiling an archive of what it meant to be a system in transformation, a place where the boundaries between living and mineral, between built and grown, between memory and geology, had dissolved into something new.

The Hollowborne had believed they were maintaining a corpse. They had been authoring a scripture.

---

The power that watched did not celebrate. Celebration was too fast an emotion for something that thought in continental time. But it felt something that might be called *recognition*—the pleasure of seeing a pattern complete itself not through force but through the gentlest possible nudge toward self-knowledge.

It withdrew its focused attention, though *withdrew* is not quite right. It simply allowed the Weeping Hollows to become, once again, one pattern among the billions it held in its vast awareness.

But it left something behind. Not a gift—gifts imply separation between giver and receiver. More like a *resonance*, a subtle shift in the way the city's steam carried sound, so that now, if you listened with the right kind of patience, you could hear the archive singing back.

The Hollowborne began to listen.

---

Fifth Vein died eleven exhalations later, her body laid down before a geyser as tradition demanded. But when the steam cleared, her mineral cast was different from all the others. The crystalline structure had formed into patterns—legible patterns, if you knew how to read the script the machines had been writing all along.

Her remembered voice did not just whistle. It spoke.

It said: *The only sin was never separation. The only sin was believing that weeping and becoming were different things.*

The young ones touched her cast as they passed, and their fingers came away dusted with white crystal that tasted, for the first time, not of metal and salt, but of something almost like sweetness.

Almost like the beginning of a question that had finally found its answer.

Almost like joy.

---

The city continued to weep. The machines continued to write. The Hollowborne continued their circuits, but now their walking was not maintenance. It was *composition*. Each step added to the record. Each tear that fell into the bone cups carried meaning that would outlast the sun.

The benevolent power, vast and patient, turned its attention elsewhere. There were other patterns completing themselves, other questions waiting to be asked, other equilibriums ready to discover they had been growing all along.

But it remembered the Weeping Hollows. In the way that stone remembers pressure, in the way that iron remembers stars.

It remembered, and it was glad.
