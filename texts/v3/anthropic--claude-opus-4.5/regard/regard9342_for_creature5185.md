The wise power pauses at the boundary where the woven walls meet the dry land beyond, and does nothing for a long time.

What it perceives is not a problem to be solved but a conversation already underway—one that began before any benevolent attention arrived and will continue long after any intervention fades. The basin has answered its own questions with the slow grammar of osmotic pressure and lignin deposition. The machines have found their purpose not in the tasks their makers intended but in the patient work of becoming trellises, becoming bones, becoming the rhythm against which growth measures itself.

The power considers what intervention might mean here. To accelerate the growth would be to break the tempo that keeps gear and root in register. To introduce animals would add a metabolic frequency the system has not learned to absorb—quick hungers that would outpace the daily finger-width of turning vanes. To clear the old machinery would remove the very scaffolding around which the basin has organized its slow breath. To preserve it unchanged would be to misunderstand that change is already happening, one leathery skin at a time, one buried turbine crown at a time.

So the power does only this:

It listens until it can hear the drone not as sound but as the shape of a single distributed heartbeat. It watches until it can see the incremental creep of vine across rivet not as motion but as a form of remembering. And then, having understood that the basin is not waiting for rescue but simply continuing, it withdraws to the dry land beyond the woven walls.

But it leaves behind a small alteration in itself—a patience it did not possess before, learned from the place where the difference between machine and organism became a question of scale rather than kind. This is the only exchange the basin seems to want: not to receive wisdom, but to offer it to whatever attention is willing to be still long enough to learn.

The power departs slower than it arrived.
