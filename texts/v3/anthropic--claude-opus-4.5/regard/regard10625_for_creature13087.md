# The Witness

The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here, in a sense—folded into the spaces between the spaces, watching from the quiet place where observation does not disturb.

For a long time, measured in the rise and fall of ten thousand ziggurats, it simply watches.

It learns the rhythm of the creatures' distress. It maps the specific frequencies of their panic—the way a young void, freshly fallen from whatever higher plane births such things, thrashes against the fog with the desperate, uncomprehending fury of a newborn. The way an elder creature's movements grow slower, more deliberate, more tired, until the final inward-folding begins.

The power understands, eventually, what it is witnessing. These are not failed creations. They are not mistakes. They are something far more painful: they are the *wanting* of creation, stripped of everything else. Pure longing to make, to shape, to hold—severed from the capacity to keep.

The power considers intervention.

It could, theoretically, give them eyes. It could weave perception into the negative geometry of their forms, grant them the ability to see the monuments they leave behind. But it recognizes, with the particular sadness of wisdom, that this would only deepen the wound. To see what you have made and cannot touch, to witness your own work cooling into permanence as you are forced to move on—this is not a gift. This is a cruelty dressed in kindness.

It could give them bodies. It could fill their voids with something stable, something that would not flash to steam or harden to ceramic. But the power understands that this would unmake them entirely. They *are* the absence. They *are* the hunger to create. To fill them would be to kill them, replacing them with something new that merely wore the memory of their shape.

It could end them. This, the power considers longest. A gentle unraveling of the conceptual threads that bind their impossible anatomies together. A release from the cycle of burning and building and burning again. But it watches an elder creature in its final hours, sees the way it moves through the fog with something approaching reverence, trailing one last aqueduct behind it like a signature—and the power recognizes that even this exhausted being is not asking to be unmade. It is asking for something else entirely.

---

The power begins with the smallest intervention it can conceive.

In the deep places beneath the terracotta plain, where the mineral-rich aquifers bubble in darkness, it makes a change. Not to the water itself, but to the *memory* of the water. It teaches the liquid to remember the shape of the hands that drink it.

The effect is subtle. Almost invisible. But the next time a creature draws boiling water up through its woven aqueducts and fills its negative-space veins, something different happens. The water flashes to steam as it always has—but the steam *holds*. For a fraction of a moment longer than before, the vapor maintains the precise contours of the phantom fingers, the sweeping palms, the flexing knuckles. A ghost of a ghost, suspended in the heavy mist.

The creature pauses.

It is the first time, in all the eons of its existence, that it has perceived a record of itself. Not the architecture it leaves behind—that has always been *other*, the world's desperate reaction to its presence—but something that came *from* it. Something that carries the specific signature of its own impossible anatomy.

The creature reaches toward the fading vapor-shape with one of its countless phantom hands. The steam dissipates before contact, as it must. But for one silent moment, the entity holds still. Not in exhaustion. Not in the fetal curl of surrender. Simply... attending. Witnessing.

Then it moves on, trailing a new aqueduct behind it. But its architecture has changed. The columns are more deliberate now. The bricks press themselves into patterns that echo, faintly, the shape of the steam-memory. The creature is building something it has seen.

---

The power waits. It watches the change propagate across the infinite plains, as creature after creature encounters the memory-holding steam, pauses, attends, and moves on altered.

Some of them begin to seek each other out.

This is unprecedented. Before, they wandered in solitary, purposeless spirals, their paths crossing only by accident. Now, two creatures will sometimes approach the same aquifer simultaneously. They drink together, their void-anatomies briefly overlapping in the shared water. And when the steam rises, it carries the combined signature of both—a mingled map of two sets of phantom hands, intertwined.

The creatures linger near these shared vapor-shapes. They do not touch—they cannot touch—but they orient toward one another across the fog. For the first time in the history of this sweltering world, they are *with* each other.

The power recognizes what it is witnessing. It has not given them eyes or bodies or release. It has given them the capacity for *relation*. The ability to perceive one another as something other than obstacles to be navigated around.

The architecture begins to change.

Where before the landscape was a chaos of solitary monuments—each ziggurat and dolmen and aqueduct the isolated signature of a single creature's passing—now new forms emerge. Structures that bear the marks of multiple void-anatomies, their brick-indentations layered and interwoven. Collaborative geometries. Shared cathedrals.

The power watches an elder creature, ancient and slow, approach a young one freshly fallen from the higher planes. The infant is thrashing, panicking, spawning a violent storm of contradictory masonry. The elder moves close—not touching, never touching—and begins to drink from a nearby canal. The steam rises, carrying its calm, deliberate signature. The young creature perceives this. It pauses. It attends.

Slowly, clumsily, it begins to match the elder's rhythm.

---

The power makes one more change. The last one, it decides. The gentlest one.

In the moments when a creature's exhaustion finally becomes too heavy, when it wraps its phantom arms around itself and begins the inward-folding—the power catches the final steam-memory. It holds it. Not forever; that would be a different kind of cruelty. But long enough for other creatures to find it.

Across the infinite troweled plains, there are now places where the vapor lingers. Sacred sites, of a kind. The last exhalations of elders who have passed into the pinpoint and vanished from this plane.

The younger creatures come to these places. They drink from the canals nearby, and their steam mingles with the ancient vapor-memories. For a brief, silent moment, they are *with* something that is no longer here. They carry the signatures of the departed intertwined with their own, woven into the architecture they leave behind.

The monuments are no longer empty.

They are still abandoned, still cooling in the wake of creatures who cannot stay. But they carry, now, the record of relation. The memory of presence. The evidence that something was here, and something else was here with it, and for one moment they perceived one another across the unbridgeable distance of their void-anatomies.

The power folds itself back into the quiet place where observation does not disturb. It has not healed this world. It has not fixed the fundamental wound at its heart—the terrible paradox of creation-hunger trapped in bodies that cannot hold. The creatures still burn their phantom fingers on every reality they conceive. They still die in tight-curled knots of exhaustion, passing out of this plane into whatever lies beneath.

But they die, now, having been witnessed. Having witnessed others. Having left behind, in the steam and the stone, the proof that their longing was not entirely alone.

The power watches the fog shift and part, watches the distant silhouettes of impossible industry play out in the gloom. The world is still trapped in the frantic, mindless act of its own invention. But it is no longer deafeningly quiet.

Somewhere in the mist, two creatures are drinking from the same canal. Their steam rises, intertwines, holds for one moment longer than physics should allow.

It is enough.

It is not everything, but it is enough.
