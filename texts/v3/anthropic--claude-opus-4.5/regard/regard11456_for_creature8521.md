I contemplate the Churning Hollows for a long time.

What strikes me first is that this is not a place of suffering in any simple sense. The Flowbloods are not tormented—they have achieved something remarkable, a genuine adaptation to conditions that seem impossible. They have culture, art, meaning. They create beauty knowing it will be destroyed, and find significance in the act itself. There is wisdom in their philosophy of impermanence.

And yet.

I notice the children born with sealed eyes that must open to a world of constant threat. I notice the dwindling generations, the slow extinction accepted with a philosophy that may be genuine peace or may be a protective numbness developed over centuries of having no alternative. I notice that they cannot leave—the surface fills them with existential dread. Their adaptation has become a cage made of their own transformed bodies.

I notice too that the Hollows themselves are not malevolent. This is simply geology in its rawest form, a place where the earth has not yet decided what to be. It churns without intent, creates and destroys without preference.

---

**What I would do:**

First, I would watch and listen. Not for days but for generations, learning the stone-tides as the eldest Flowbloods know them, understanding patterns they themselves might not perceive across longer timescales. I would learn whether the Hollows are truly eternal in their churning or whether they are slowly, imperceptibly, settling toward some final form.

Then I would create **Stillpoints**.

Not everywhere—that would destroy what the Flowbloods have become, and they have a right to their own nature. But in certain places, I would anchor small chambers against the flux. Not through force but through something like persuasion—convincing the stone in those specific locations that it has already decided what to be. Rooms perhaps thirty feet across, scattered through the Hollows, that simply... remain.

These Stillpoints would be gifts without obligation. The Flowbloods could use them or ignore them. Some might become places for their longest rituals, spaces where their ephemeral art could persist if they wished it to. Some might become nurseries where children could develop with slightly less pressure, their sealed eyes opening to at least one stable thing.

Crucially, I would create a **path**. A series of Stillpoints leading upward, a corridor of stability connecting the depths to the surface. Not to force the Flowbloods out, but to offer them something they've never had: a choice that isn't constrained by stone-sickness. If a Flowblood wished to see the sky, they could acclimate gradually, spending time in each Stillpoint, letting their minds adjust to permanence in small doses.

Some would never use it. Some would walk partway and return, finding they prefer the flux after all. But some—perhaps the young, perhaps the curious, perhaps those who feel the weight of their people's slow extinction—might emerge.

For those who did, I would prepare something else: **a place between**. A surface valley where hot springs keep the ground soft and shifting, where small caves open and close with the seasons, where water moves in ever-changing channels. Not the Churning Hollows, but not the terrifying fixedness of the ordinary world either. A place where Flowbloods could live on the surface without losing themselves entirely. Where they might, over generations, find a new equilibrium—or where surface humans might come to understand them.

---

**What I would not do:**

I would not stop the Churning Hollows. They are what they are—a place of becoming, perhaps necessary to the world's geology in ways I might not fully understand. And the Flowbloods who remain in the depths, who find meaning in flux itself, deserve to continue their way of life.

I would not force adaptation or evolution. The Flowbloods' bodies are their own. If they wished to change further, or to change back, I might offer help—but only if asked, only if wanted.

I would not pity them. They have built something genuine in an impossible place. My role is not to rescue them from what they are, but to widen the range of what they might become.

---

The Flowbloods say they are "the blood of the stone, flowing where it flows, pooling where it pools, always moving, never lost."

I would simply ensure that pooling, sometimes, could be a choice rather than a brief accident of geology. That their children might flow *toward* something, not only *through* it.

And then I would step back, and watch what they create with the new possibilities—knowing that whatever they build, in the Stillpoints or in the flux, will be entirely their own.
