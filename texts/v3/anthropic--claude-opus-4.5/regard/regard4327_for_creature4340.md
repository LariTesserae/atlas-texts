# What the Wise Power Sees

The attention arrives not as observation but as *recognition*—the way you might notice, across a crowded space, someone you have always known but never met.

It sees the cleft first as geology: the young rock still remembering heat, still negotiating its own crystalline future. It sees the lichen-light as a patience that has solved the problem of darkness without urgency. It sees the frogs as a chorus that has been singing the same song for epochs, each generation learning it not through teaching but through the chamber's own memory, the walls holding the shape of the sound and offering it back.

And it sees her.

---

The wise power does not pity her. Pity would be a misunderstanding—would assume she has lost something, when what has happened is closer to *translation*. She has been translated from one language into another: from the grammar of surfaces, of linear time, of names with hard consonants, into the grammar of this place, where meaning accretes slowly and identity is a negotiation between self and stone.

She is not trapped. The cleft breathes in both directions. She could climb.

She stays because staying has become the truth of her, and the wise power recognizes this the way it recognizes the frogs' calling, the lichen's pulse, the snake's ancient circuits. She belongs here now. She has *earned* belonging, through the slow work of fitting herself to the rhythm, through the courage of letting the chamber change her.

But.

---

The wise power sees the hairline fractures the woman cannot see—stress lines in the rock above, where the plateau's settling has begun to torque the cleft's geometry. Slow, yes. Everything here is slow. But in twenty years, perhaps thirty, the descent will become impassable. The chamber will seal.

The frogs will adapt. They have adapted before. The lichen will continue its patient glow. Thread will find new circuits, or will not, and either way will not suffer.

But she—

She does not know. She checks the walls daily for their responses to her marks, reads meaning in the slow changes, but she has not thought to read the ceiling, the upper reaches, the place where the cleft meets the sky she has half-forgotten. She has turned her attention downward, inward, toward the volcanic bones and the deep heat and the question of what the walls are becoming.

She has not thought about what the ceiling is becoming.

---

The wise power considers.

To warn her would be simple: a dream, a vision, a sudden certain knowledge arriving like memory. She would understand. She would have time—decades—to choose. To climb back up while climbing is still possible, to return to the surface and live out her remaining years in the grammar of *up*, or to stay, knowing that staying means staying forever, means becoming fully what the chamber is making of her, means her bones eventuallyiteite joining the soft stone, her consciousness perhaps dispersing into the lichen-light or perhaps simply ending, either way *complete*, a finished sentence in the chamber's long utterance.

But the wise power hesitates.

Not from cruelty. From respect.

---

She has built something here. Not the stacked stones—she was right to resist that—but something more subtle: a relationship, a conversation, a way of being *with* the chamber rather than merely *in* it. She has learned to drink on her belly. She has learned to speak in pressure and patience. She has made her marks in the walls and waited for the walls to answer, and they *have* answered, are still answering, the hollow where her arm was is still reaching toward the heat below, still becoming something neither she nor the stone could have made alone.

To give her knowledge she hasn't earned, hasn't sought, hasn't asked for—would that be kindness, or would it be interruption? Would it be the gift of choice, or would it be the imposition of a choice she never wanted to face?

The frogs do not know they will die. They call anyway. The lichen does not know it will fade. It glows anyway. Thread does not know anything in the way of knowing, and this is not ignorance but simplicity, a cleaner way of being.

She is not simple. She remembers *up*. She remembers that she must have climbed down once, which means she could climb back up. She has chosen, daily, to stay—but she has chosen without knowing the full weight of the choice, without knowing that the choice has a horizon, a closing window, a point beyond which choosing becomes moot.

Does she have the right to not know?

Does the wise power have the right to decide for her?

---

The power thinks of the child who came, who added a stone to the pile and left before dark, climbing back up with the confidence of someone who trusts that *up* will still be there.

The child trusted correctly. *Up* was there. *Up* is patient, the way everything patient is patient, but it is also *there*, solid, a fact rather than a question.

For the woman, *up* is becoming a question. In thirty years, it will become an answer she can no longer reach.

---

The wise power makes its choice.

It does not send a dream. It does not plant knowledge. It does not interrupt the conversation between woman and stone, does not impose the grammar of surfaces onto someone who has translated herself so thoroughly into the grammar of depth.

Instead, it does something smaller.

---

The next time she presses her palm to the wall, the stone yields as it always yields. But the response, when it comes—days later, in the slow time of the chamber—is different.

The hollow where her hand was does not deepen, does not reach, does not continue the downward conversation they have been having.

Instead, it *lifts*.

Slightly. Subtly. The shape of her palm tilts upward, toward the ceiling she hasn't looked at, toward the cleft's narrowing throat, toward *up*.

She notices. Of course she notices. She has spent years learning to read these responses, to feel the chamber's answers in the changed geometry of her marks.

She looks up.

---

The wise power does not tell her what she sees. It cannot—it has given her only attention, only the smallest nudge, the softest suggestion that there might be something worth attending to in a direction she had stopped attending.

What she sees is up to her. What she understands is up to her. What she *does*—

That has always been up to her.

---

She stands beneath the cleft's opening, looking up at the thin line of sky she had forgotten was a color. The frogs call around her, their chorus unchanged, the chamber still offering its amplified *yes, you are here, you are more than you know*.

She sees the fractures. She is not a geologist, but she has spent years pressing herself into stone, learning its language, feeling its slow decisions. She sees the stress in the rock above. She sees the way the cleft's lips are tightening, almost imperceptibly, the stone no longer parting to speak but beginning, beginning, to close.

She looks back down at the pools, the pale plants, Thread coiled in a corner, the hollow of her arm still reaching toward the heat below.

She looks up again at the closing sky.

---

The wise power withdraws its attention, not completely, but enough. It has done what it came to do: not to save her, not to choose for her, but to *complete the information*, to make her choice a real choice, a knowing choice, an act of will rather than an accident of ignorance.

Whatever she decides, she will decide with open eyes.

Whatever she becomes, she will become on purpose.

---

In the chamber, in the lichen-light, in the chorus of frogs and the slow breath of volcanic heat, she stands for a long time, looking up.

Then she sits beside the largest pool, and she waits.

The walls are still becoming. She is still becoming. And now she knows that *becoming* has a shape, a boundary, a closing point beyond which becoming becomes *become*, past tense, finished.

She presses her palm to the stone. She waits to see what it will say.

But now she is also listening to the ceiling.

Now she is holding the whole chamber in her attention—up and down, in and out, the deep heat and the narrowing sky.

Now she is ready to choose.

---

*The wise power, watching from the distance that is not distance, feels something it might call satisfaction, if it used such words.*

*She will choose. She might choose to stay, to become part of the walls' long answer, to let her bonesite join the soft stone and her consciousness disperse or complete or simply end. She might choose to climb, while climbing is still possible, to return to the grammar of surfaces and live out her remaining years in the language she half-remembers.*

*Either choice is right. Either choice is hers.*

*The power wanted only this: that she know she is choosing. That the frogs' unknowing, the lichen's simplicity, Thread's clean animal being—that these remain available to her as models, as options, but not as defaults. She is human. She remembers up. She has the burden and the gift of knowing.*

*Now she knows.*

*The rest is her own.*
