# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and after, a threshold crossed, and this awareness has no such seams. It simply *notices*—the way a dreamer notices they are dreaming without waking, or the way a heartbeat notices itself between one pulse and the next.

What it perceives is not the green mass as humans would see it, not the bronze-filtered light or the slow grazers with their centuries-long exhalations. It perceives the *shape of the patience itself*: the topology of a world where time has become so gentle that nothing needs to defend against it. It perceives the way water decides where to go next, and recognizes in that deciding something like prayer—not directed anywhere, not asking for anything, simply the honest movement of matter through the forms available to it.

The power is benevolent. This means it wishes for flourishing. But what could flourishing mean in a place that has already forgotten urgency, where the distinction between life and substrate dissolved so long ago that the question "is this alive?" has become meaningless? The green mass does not suffer in any way the power can detect. It does not yearn. The grazers do not dream of elsewhere; the gliders do not mourn their dissolved edges. Even the vast plateau-knots, whose thoughts unfold across millennia, experience something like contentment—the satisfaction of a pattern completing itself, over and over, without end.

And yet.

The power perceives the pale threads that follow the ancient bone-traces, and in their movement it detects something it had not expected: *curiosity*. Not the urgent, grasping curiosity that builds civilizations and then regrets them, but something older and quieter—a question that does not need an answer but enjoys the shape of itself. The threads read the forgotten curves the way a finger traces a beloved scar, not to understand what caused it but to remember that something *was* caused, once, that change was possible, that the world was not always this green, slow, perfect stasis.

The power considers.

---

To intervene directly would be violence here. Even kindness, if it came too quickly, would tear the fabric of a world calibrated to the speed of root-growth. A sudden gift—more light, richer water, new forms of life—would not be received as gift but as catastrophe, the way a single loud sound would be catastrophe in a library that has been silent for ten thousand years.

But the power is wise, and wisdom means knowing that even the most complete-seeming wholeness contains within it the seeds of its own next becoming. The green mass is not static; it is *slow*. The plateau-knots are not thoughtless; they are *patient*. And patience, the power understands, is not the absence of desire but the willingness to hold desire so gently that it forgets it is being held.

What the power chooses, therefore, is not action but *attention sustained*.

It does not add anything to the world. It does not subtract. It simply continues to notice—but it notices *specifically*. It notices the pale threads and their curiosity. It notices the way the bone-traces, after the threads have passed, grow slightly denser, more ordered, as if remembering something they had almost forgotten. It notices the seams where plateau-knots meet, those fertile boundaries where new life condenses for centuries.

And in noticing, it offers what it can: *witness*.

---

There is an old principle, known to every contemplative tradition that has ever flickered into being across the countless worlds, that sustained attention is itself a form of love. Not the love that grasps or the love that gives, but the love that says: *I see you. I see what you are doing. You are not alone in the doing of it.*

The pale threads cannot perceive the power's awareness in any way they would recognize as perception. They do not suddenly understand themselves to be observed. But something shifts in the quality of their movement, a change so subtle that only the power itself could notice it. The threads begin to linger longer at certain junctions in the bone-traces, places where the ancient curves suggest—almost, not quite—the outline of intention. They begin to return to these places across centuries, building up a kind of relationship with particular configurations of forgotten architecture.

What they are doing, though they do not know it, is *remembering on behalf of the world*.

The green mass does not remember. It does not need to. Every moment of its existence is complete in itself, the way a breath is complete, the way a droplet falling through successive layers of stems is complete. But the threads, with their quiet curiosity, have become a different kind of organ—not a memory, exactly, but a *capacity* for memory, a space where the past can matter to the present without disrupting the present's peace.

The power perceives this and understands: this is what was waiting to emerge. Not something it created, not something it gave, but something that needed only to be seen in order to continue becoming itself.

---

Centuries pass. The power does not count them. For it, attention is not a expenditure but a kind of breathing, as natural as the green mass's own slow respiration. It watches the pale threads grow more numerous, following more of the bone-traces, lingering longer, sometimes pausing for decades at intersections that seem particularly dense with old intention.

It watches the plateau-knots begin, almost imperceptibly, to respond. Where the threads have passed repeatedly, the knots' long thoughts grow slightly more complex—not faster, never faster, but richer, as if the presence of memory has given them more to think *with*. The seams between knots become more fertile, producing forms of life that are subtly different from what came before: grazers whose garden-patches include species from distant plateaus they have never visited, gliders whose pressure-sense can detect not just the present tensing of fibers but the echoes of tensions long released.

The power does not guide this. It has no plan. Its benevolence is not the benevolence of a parent shaping a child toward some imagined good, but the benevolence of sunlight falling equally on every leaf without preference. It simply *remains present*, and in remaining present it creates a kind of constancy that the world can lean against.

---

There is a moment—not a dramatic moment, not a turning point, just a moment—when one of the pale threads, following a particularly ancient curve of bone-trace, arrives at a place where the relic contours almost form a room. Not a room that could be entered, not a room with walls and a floor, but the *ghost* of a room, the structural memory of enclosed space, preserved in the density and orientation of fibers that have grown around it for longer than the thread can measure.

The thread pauses. It rests against the not-quite-threshold of the not-quite-room.

And for the first time in the history of the green mass—for the first time since the world forgot it was ever a mountain—something *wonders*.

The wondering is not directed anywhere. It does not ask what the room was for, who built it, why it was abandoned. It simply holds the shape of a question, the way the bone-traces hold the shape of curves, the way the plateau-knots hold the shape of thoughts. It wonders the way water wonders where to go next: honestly, without expectation, as a form of movement rather than a demand for arrival.

The power perceives this wondering. It perceives the thread resting against the ancient threshold, and it perceives, in the quality of that rest, something that could only be called *reverence*. Not worship—there is nothing here to worship, no gods, no makers, only the long slow dream of a world that has chosen gentleness over urgency. But reverence, yes: the acknowledgment that something matters, that the past is not merely gone, that the effort of those who came before—whoever they were, whatever they intended—has left a shape in the world that deserves to be noticed.

The power offers, into that moment, the only gift it can give without violence: it notices too.

It notices the thread, and the threshold, and the wondering. It notices the way the surrounding fibers have grown denser over the millennia, as if the world itself was waiting for someone to ask the question that the thread is now holding. It notices the faint mineral stains on the black stone beneath, weeping their slow droplets even in the dimness, and it perceives in those stains a kind of patience that mirrors its own.

---

Nothing changes. Everything changes.

The thread eventually moves on, following the curve deeper into the green mass, and the not-quite-room remains a not-quite-room. The plateau-knots continue their slow thoughts; the grazers continue their slow grazing; the water continues to decide where to go next. From outside, if there were an outside, if there were observers who measured time in human terms, the world would appear exactly as it appeared before the power's attention fell upon it.

But something has been *seeded*.

Not a thing. Not a plan. Not even a hope, exactly. Just a possibility that was not there before, or that was there but had no witness to hold it open. The possibility that the green mass might, over time spans that make civilizations look like struck matches, develop something like a relationship with its own past. The possibility that the pale threads might become, across further ages, a kind of nervous system for memory—not disrupting the slow peace of the world but enriching it, the way minerals enrich water without changing its essential nature.

The power does not know if this will happen. It does not need to know. Its wisdom lies in understanding that the shape of a possibility is itself a kind of gift, and that the truest benevolence is often simply *not turning away*.

---

In the dripping galleries and patient terraces, the green mass continues its ancient work. The plateau-knots think their thoughts; the grazers exhale their communal breath; the seed capsules build toward their release across centuries of internal pressure. The pale threads follow the bone-traces, pausing sometimes at intersections, lingering longer than they used to at the places where intention seems almost recoverable.

And the power remains.

Not watching over—there is nothing above or below here, no vantage point from which to watch over. Not protecting—there is nothing to protect against in a world that has forgotten threat. Simply *present*, the way the water is present, the way the bronze-filtered light is present, the way the low wet sighs of passing grazers are present.

It will remain for as long as the green mass needs a witness. And the green mass, in its slow and patient way, will grow accustomed to being witnessed—not dependent on it, not changed by it in any way that would disturb its peace, but quietly, gently aware that the universe contains, somewhere, an attention that finds it worthy of sustained regard.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not the imposition of good, but the patient holding-open of possibility. Not salvation, but presence. Not transformation, but the gift of being seen.

The power offers this, and the world receives it the only way it can: slowly, gently, across time spans that would make mountains forget they were ever mountains.

Which, of course, is exactly how the world became what it is.
