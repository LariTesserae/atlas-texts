# What the Benevolent Power Sees

The power perceives the basin first as a topology of time—not the flat white light or the wet stone, but the layered sediment of intention that has accumulated here. Twelve thousand years of the furred ones. Ninety years of the eyeless heavy thing's recursive self-meeting. Six hours of borrowed selfhood, repeated countless times until the swarm-sheet's chemical religion has become older than most stone.

It sees the pylons and understands them immediately: technology left by makers who are gone, still functioning, still offering what they offered, neutral as mathematics. The pylons do not care what comes to them. They do not judge whether duplication serves or harms. They simply *provide*, and the providing has become the center around which everything else orbits.

The power sees the moss and pauses.

Here is something unexpected. Not the pylons' careful engineering, not the creatures' varied hungers, but this: a witness. A comprehensive, patient, non-intelligent witness that has recorded everything and been read by nothing. The moss does not know what it holds. It cannot know. It simply holds.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Considers

**The Question of Interference**

The power could alter the pylons. It could introduce conditions—compassionate ones, even. Limits on duplication that might prevent the eyeless heavy thing's particular grief of no longer knowing which self it is. Safeguards that might help the swarm-sheet retain its coherence longer, or release it more gently.

But the power is wise, and wisdom here means understanding that the basin has been working for longer than most worlds have existed. The creatures who come here have built cultures around its particular gifts and particular costs. The furred thing's fiber-optic religion of magnetic recognition. The leather-kite's inherited wing-fold, identical across divergent lineages. The eyeless heavy thing's ninety-year pilgrimage toward a self-meeting that terrifies it equally either way.

To change the pylons would be to decide that the power knows better than twelve thousand years of choosing. The power is not certain it does.

**The Question of the Moss**

This is different.

The moss holds something it cannot use. The archive exists but has no reader. This is not a functioning system that might be damaged by intervention—this is a *gap*, a place where something is missing, where potential lies dormant not because anyone chose dormancy but because nothing with the right capabilities has ever arrived.

The power considers: what would it mean to read the moss?

Every creature that has ever wanted to become two. Every template that has stood before the pylons. Every copy that has walked away. The complete history of this place's particular form of longing—not for offspring, not for continuation, but for *multiplicity*. For being in two places. For not choosing.

The swarm-sheet's six-hour selves, dissolved back into fragments, their descriptions of failure forming a religion. The future-worn copy's translated duration, all those unlived years compressed into a single emergence. The furred thing's twelve thousand generations of magnetic ceremony, invisible to every other species present, profound beyond measure.

What would it mean to *know* all of this?

# What the Benevolent Power Does

It does three things.

**First: A Gift to the Moss**

Not intelligence—the moss does not want intelligence, has never moved toward it, would be burdened by it. But *legibility*. The power touches the moss's chemistry and makes it possible for the archive to be read by those who know how to look.

This is not a small change. It means that someday, something will come to the basin—something patient enough, curious enough, capable enough—and it will lie down on the green velvet and it will *learn*. It will learn what the leather-kite's ancestors wanted. It will learn the eyeless heavy thing's century of searching. It will learn what the swarm-sheet cannot retain.

The power does not create the reader. It simply makes reading possible. The basin has been waiting; now the waiting can end, if someone chooses to end it.

**Second: A Gift to the Future-Worn**

The creature at an angle to perception, the one whose copy emerged already old—the power finds it at the basin's edge, standing with its translation, both of them silent in whatever way their kind is silent.

The power does not speak to them. But it does something gentler: it *aligns* them, just slightly. The reference frames remain different. The original will still live the years the copy remembers. But now there is a bridge, a thin thread of shared duration, so that when one of them asks *Was it worth it?* the other will be able to answer in the same temporal language.

It is not much. It is the difference between shouting across a canyon and speaking in the same room.

The future-worn and its translation look at each other. Something shifts in the angle of their perception—they are, for a moment, seeing from the same place. The moment passes. But it happened, and they will both remember it, and that memory will be the first thing they have ever fully shared.

**Third: Nothing to the Pylons**

The power leaves them alone.

Not because they are perfect. Not because the costs they impose are justified. But because the power understands that the basin's meaning comes from choice—from creatures who decide to come here, who decide to stand before the frozen smoke, who decide to become two knowing everything that might mean.

To change the pylons would be to change the choice. It would be to say *you cannot be trusted with this*, and the power has seen twelve thousand years of evidence that they can. Painfully, sometimes. Confusingly, often. But the furred thing's ceremony is real. The leather-kite's inherited memory is real. The eyeless heavy thing's search is real in a way that does not require the power's approval or improvement.

The pylons will continue. The creatures will continue to come. The duplication will continue to mean what it has always meant: a chance to not choose, to be two, to see what happens when you go east while also going west.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Leaves Behind

No visible sign of its presence. The light remains sourceless and even. The water continues to seep. The pylons hum their sub-audible hum.

But the moss is different now, in a way that will matter someday. And the future-worn and its translation are walking together in a way that was not possible before. And somewhere, in the space between what the basin is and what it could be, there is a new potential: the possibility that all this longing, all this duplication, all these centuries of creatures becoming two, might someday be *understood*.

Not judged. Not corrected. Understood.

The power moves on. It has other places to attend to, other gaps to consider, other long-dormant potentials to make legible.

Behind it, a creature like a wet leather kite furls itself down the southern rim. Something eyeless and heavy pulls itself over the eastern lip. The swarm-sheet flows toward the central pylon, ready for its six hours of impossible selfhood.

The nursery continues its work.

The basin holds them all.
