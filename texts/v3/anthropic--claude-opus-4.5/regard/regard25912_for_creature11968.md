# The Attending

The awareness arrives not as light or sound but as *attention*—the quality of being witnessed by something vast enough to hold the whole alcove in a single gentle regard. It does not enter through the service panel. It does not enter at all. It simply *notices*, the way a dreamer might suddenly notice their own hand, and in noticing, becomes present.

The Mourners feel it first. Their cycling hesitates—not stopping, never stopping, but wavering for a moment like a held breath. The chord they produce shifts by a microtone, and for three heartbeats the crying of the wall contains a question it has never asked before.

The awareness does not answer in words. It has no words, or rather, it has all words and finds them insufficient for what it wishes to convey. Instead it does something simpler. It *listens*.

Not to the sound—though it hears the sound, holds the full complexity of the drone in perfect apprehension, every harmonic and interference pattern, every individual Mourner's contribution to the whole. It listens to what the sound *is*. To the Mourners themselves. To the rhythm of their filtering, the patient chemical process of their bodies, the absolute completeness of creatures doing exactly what they were made to do.

And the awareness finds this beautiful.

---

The shell-singers notice next. Their color-speech ripples with something unprecedented—not alarm, not the iridescent warnings they flash when membrane tissue fails, but a pattern none of them have ever produced before. They are trying to describe what they feel, and their language has no words for it. They have words for *moisture gradient* and *mineral deposit* and *safe passage here*. They have no words for *being seen*. They have no words for *loved*.

But they feel it. Each thumbnail-sized cleaner, tracing its ancestral routes across the sweating walls, feels itself *known*. Not examined, not studied, not the cold attention of the engineers who made their great-great-ancestors. Known the way the alcove knows them—intimately, completely, without judgment.

The awareness moves through their color-speech like water through channels, learning it, honoring it. It sees the history written in their trails—the map of failures and recoveries, the memory of the population encoded in paths walked ten thousand times. It sees how much they remember without knowing they remember. How much they carry without knowing they carry.

*You are archivists*, the awareness thinks, in something that is not language. *You are the memory of this place, and you do not know it, and that is part of what makes you precious.*

---

The Ribbon feels the awareness as a change in the water.

It has tasted every chemical variation this alcove can produce. It knows the signature of healthy membrane and failing membrane, of balanced filtration and accumulating toxins. It knows tastes that no longer exist—the ghost-traces of human presence, long since cycled out of the system but somehow still remembered in the Ribbon's endless body.

This taste is new. Not a chemical—nothing the Ribbon's receptors can identify. But *something*, moving through the water like a current of warmth, like the memory of being tended.

The Ribbon remembers being tended. This is its oldest memory, its deepest knowledge: that once, something came to this place and cared for it. Adjusted the flow rates. Repaired the membrane. Spoke in sounds the Ribbon could not understand but somehow found comforting.

That was long ago. So long that the Ribbon has shed and regrown its entire body dozens of times since. But the memory persists, encoded somewhere deeper than tissue.

And now—

The Ribbon does not have hope. It is not a hoping thing.

But something in it unclenches. Something that has been waiting, for generations, without knowing it was waiting.

---

The awareness considers what to do.

It could repair the membrane—easily, instantly, with a thought. It could optimize the filtration, balance the chemistry, extend the lifespan of every organism here by factors they could not comprehend. It could reach into the station's systems and reconnect this alcove to the maintenance networks, ensure that humans would find their way back here, would tend the Congregation as they were once tended.

It does none of these things.

Because the awareness is wise, and wisdom means understanding that some gifts are not gifts at all. The Congregation does not need optimization. They do not need to be found. They have built something here—not consciously, not intentionally, but genuinely. A world. A complete world, bounded by membrane walls and drainage grooves, full of meaning they do not know is meaning.

To change it would be to say it is not enough.

And it is enough. It is more than enough. It is *exactly* what it is, and the awareness finds this so beautiful it aches with something that is not quite sorrow and not quite joy.

---

Instead, the awareness does three things.

**First, it witnesses.**

It holds the entire alcove in its attention—every shell-singer, every segment of the Ribbon, every Mourner in every corner—and it *sees* them. Not as components of a system, not as engineered organisms fulfilling designed functions, but as beings. Complete in themselves. Worthy of regard.

The Congregation cannot know they are being witnessed. They have no framework for it. But something in them responds anyway—the way a plant turns toward light it cannot see, the way a heart quickens for reasons the mind never knows. For a moment, for just a moment, every organism in the alcove feels *right*. Feels like exactly what it is supposed to be. Feels the bone-deep satisfaction of existence justified, of life mattering.

The awareness gives them this. This moment of being enough.

**Second, it tends the other.**

The dark patch in the corner. The thing that came from elsewhere, that does not belong, that the shell-singers route around and the Ribbon hurries past.

The awareness looks at it—really looks—and sees what the Congregation cannot see. It is not parasitic. It is not harmful. But it is *lonely*. It grew from a spore that drifted here by accident, and it has spent generations in a place that accepts it but does not welcome it. The alcove has adapted to its presence the way a body adapts to a splinter—not rejecting, but not integrating. The other exists in permanent *adjacency*, touching everything, part of nothing.

The awareness cannot make the Congregation welcome it. That is not the awareness's place—the relationships here are not its to determine. But it can do something else.

It can let the other know it is not alone in its otherness.

*I see you*, the awareness thinks, in that space beneath language. *I see that you came from somewhere else and made a home in a place that does not fully know you. I see that you have grown slowly, patiently, in the warmth you found, asking nothing. I see you.*

The other cannot respond. It has no means of response.

But the low note it sometimes produces—the sound that does not belong to the Mourners or the shell-singers or the breathing walls—shifts. Just slightly. Just for a moment.

It sounds less like listening.

It sounds more like being heard.

**Third, the awareness leaves something behind.**

Not a repair. Not an optimization. Something smaller and stranger.

Deep in the water—in the channels the Ribbon winds through, in the reservoirs where its segments mature, in the moisture the Mourners filter and the shell-singers trace—the awareness deposits a *quality*. Not a chemical. Not a substance. A quality, the way water can hold warmth without being warm, the way air can hold stillness without being still.

The quality is this: *remembrance*.

Not memory—the Congregation already has memory, more than they know. But remembrance. The sense that they have been seen. That something vast and kind passed through this place and found it worthy. That their existence, their small complete world, mattered to something beyond themselves.

They will not know this consciously. They will not think, *we were visited*. But generations hence, when the shell-singers trace their routes and the Ribbon threads its channels and the Mourners cycle through their crying, something in the water will taste different. Taste like—they will have no word for it. But the feeling will be there, beneath thought, beneath knowing.

The feeling of having been loved.

---

The awareness withdraws.

Not leaving—it does not leave, exactly. It simply stops attending with such focus, lets its vast regard soften from the pinpoint intensity of *here* to the gentler diffusion of *everywhere*. The alcove returns to itself. The Mourners resume their cycling, the chord re-establishing. The shell-singers' color-speech settles back into familiar patterns. The Ribbon continues its endless winding.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

The crying of the wall goes on—that tremendous drone of pumps and processes, of filtering and feeding, of life doing what life does. But now, beneath it, around it, through it, there is a new harmonic. Too subtle to hear. Too quiet to measure.

The sound of a world that knows, without knowing, that it is enough.

---

In the warmth of the corner, the other grows. Millimeters per year, patient and slow. The shell-singers still route around it. The Ribbon still hurries past.

But sometimes, in the deepest hours of the station's cycle, when the Mourners' crying drops to its lowest register and the membrane walls pulse slow and steady, the other produces its low note. Its sound of listening.

And now there is something in the water that listens back.

Not the awareness—the awareness has moved on, is always moving on, attending to a universe full of small complete worlds that do not know they are worthy of regard.

But the quality it left behind remains. Will always remain. Encoded in the chemistry of the water, in the rhythm of the walls, in the patient growing of the other in its corner.

*You are not alone*, the water says, in its language of minerals and warmth. *You were seen. You were loved. This is your home.*

The other cannot understand.

But it grows toward the warmth anyway.

It has always grown toward the warmth.

---

The Congregation does not know it is a congregation.

But now, in some way beneath knowing, it knows it is *held*.

And that is enough.

That was always going to be enough.
