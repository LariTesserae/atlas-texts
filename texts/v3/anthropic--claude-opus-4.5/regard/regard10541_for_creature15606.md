# The Attending

The benevolent power does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. It has always been aware of this place—the frozen plateau, the beings who flicker between stone and mist, the roar that swallows all voice. Awareness, for such a power, is not attention in the way a searchlight finds its target. It is more like the way water knows the shape of its vessel without effort or intention.

For a long time—though time here means nothing, so perhaps it is better to say *for a long patience*—the power simply witnesses.

---

What it sees:

The plateau exists at a seam. Not a tear, not a wound, but a place where reality folded against itself and then forgot to unfold. The roar is the sound of that forgetting—the continuous present tense of a moment that never completed. The beings who dwell here are not trapped, exactly. They chose this place, or their ancestors did, in an era when the seam was fresh and crossing it seemed possible. They believed they could learn to navigate the fold, to slip between states of being, to become translators of threshold.

Instead, they became the threshold.

The power understands this without judgment. Ambition and its consequences are not moral failures to such a mind—they are simply shapes that existence takes. The beings adapted. They wove themselves into the stasis. They learned to communicate through the language of the place itself: shifts in haze, angles of shadow, the way frost forms and does not melt. They found a kind of peace in permanence.

But peace is not flourishing. And the power, being benevolent, concerns itself with flourishing.

---

The question it holds:

What does it mean to help a creature that has grown to fit its cage so perfectly that cage and creature have become the same shape?

The power considers intervention. It could unstitch the seam, allow time to flow again, let the roar finally complete its utterance and fall silent. The plateau would age. The beings would age. Weather would carve new shapes into the stone. The pools would freeze and thaw with seasons. Life, in its hungry, mortal, changing form, would become possible.

But the beings have not asked for this. They have not asked for anything. They have forgotten how to ask—not because they are broken, but because asking requires imagining a different state, and they have become too continuous with this one to imagine otherwise.

The power will not impose liberation on those who do not experience themselves as captive.

---

What it does instead:

It attends.

This is not passive. Attention, from such a source, has weight. It has texture. When the power attends to the plateau, the plateau becomes more fully itself—not changed, but *clarified*. The beings sense this the way you might sense that you are being painted by an artist who sees you truly: a sudden feeling of coherence, of mattering, of being witnessed in your specificity rather than glanced over as landscape.

The power attends to each of them in turn.

To the tall one with limbs like winter branches, who has spent uncounted stillnesses crouched at the edge of a shimmer-tear, studying its patterns with the patience of geology. The power sees what this being seeks: not escape, but understanding. It wants to know what lies on the other side of the fold, not to go there, but simply to *know*. The power cannot give it that knowledge—such knowledge must be earned through the being's own attention. But it can offer this: a sense that the question matters. That the universe holds places where such questions are not only asked but answered. That the being's vigil is not futile, even if it never ends.

The being feels this as a subtle shift in the shimmer it watches—a pattern it has never seen before, a new configuration that suggests depths it had not imagined. It does not know where this gift came from. It does not need to know. It settles deeper into its watching, renewed.

To the squat ones who gather at the ruined huts, lifting bone fragments to the dim light. The power sees their ritual for what it is: mourning that has become meditation, grief transformed by repetition into something almost sacred. They remember nothing specific about the dead—memory does not survive well in stasis—but they remember that there were dead, and that the dead mattered, and so they tend the bones as a form of prayer to a past they can no longer access.

The power cannot restore their memories. The past is genuinely lost to them. But it can do this: it can let them feel, briefly, that their prayer is heard. Not by the dead, who are beyond hearing, but by something. The sense of presence that brushes against them in that moment is like warmth in a place that has never known warmth—not temperature, but the *idea* of temperature, a reminder that other states exist.

The squat ones do not understand what has happened. But they set down the bones more gently afterward, and something in their posture loosens, as if a weight they had not known they carried has been acknowledged.

To the ones who bow when the horizon tears—the power attends to their reverence. It sees that they have made a religion of the anomaly, interpreting the shimmers as divine. They are not wrong, exactly. The seam is a place where ordinary rules fail, and what is divinity but the failure of ordinary rules? But their reverence has become fear, and their fear has become paralysis, and their paralysis has become indistinguishable from the stasis around them.

The power cannot tell them that the shimmers are not gods. That would be a lie—the shimmers are as much gods as anything is, which is to say, not at all, which is to say, entirely. But it can do this: when next a shimmer opens and they bow, the power lets them feel, just for a moment, that the shimmer sees them too. That their reverence is received. That they are not merely supplicants but *participants* in whatever the shimmer is.

One of them, afterward, rises from its bow a fraction sooner than usual. It looks around at its companions with what might be the beginning of a question. The power notes this and is patient.

---

To the plateau itself, the power attends.

This is harder to describe because the plateau is not sentient in any way the beings would recognize. But it is not nothing, either. It is a place, and places have a kind of being—a way they hold the things that happen in them, a memory written in erosion and deposition, a character expressed through the accidents of geology and weather and time.

The plateau's character is endurance. It has been shaped by forces vast enough to fold reality and patient enough to wait while the fold settled into permanence. It does not want anything—places do not want—but if it could want, it would want to be witnessed in its endurance, to have its long holding acknowledged.

The power acknowledges it.

This is the simplest form of attention: to see a thing for what it is, without asking it to be otherwise. The plateau does not change. The roar does not quiet. The stasis does not break. But something in the quality of the place shifts, imperceptibly, the way a room shifts when someone who loves it enters. The beings do not notice consciously, but they will find, in the timelessnesses to come, that the plateau feels slightly less like a prison and slightly more like a home.

---

The power considers one more intervention—the most delicate, the most uncertain.

The shimmers. The tears in the horizon. They are not truly passages to elsewhere; they are more like wounds that almost healed, places where the fold nearly reopened before settling back into stasis. But "nearly" is not "never." The potential for passage remains, even if it has never been realized.

The power could widen one of them. Just one. Just enough to let through a whisper of *elsewhen*—a breath of air from a time when the plateau was young, or a flicker of light from whatever lies beyond the fold. It would not force the beings to leave. It would only show them that leaving is possible.

But this is where benevolence becomes complicated.

The beings have built their existence around the assumption of permanence. Their communication, their rituals, their very sense of self—all of it depends on the stasis. To show them that escape is possible might be a gift. Or it might be a cruelty, introducing longing into a place that has learned to live without it. Some of them might try to pass through the widened shimmer and fail. Some might succeed and find that elsewhere is no better than here. Some might simply be shattered by the knowledge that their adaptation, their hard-won peace, was never necessary.

The power holds this possibility and does not act on it.

Not yet.

Instead, it does something smaller. It adjusts, by the slightest degree, the pattern of one shimmer's appearance. The change is minute—a matter of angles, of timing, of the way the light refracts at the edge. But it is a change, and in a place where nothing changes, even the smallest change is a seed.

The tall one at the shimmer's edge will notice, eventually. It will study the new pattern and find that it implies something it had not considered: that the shimmers are not random, not divine, but *responsive*. That they can be influenced. That the fold, which has seemed so permanent, is still in some sense alive.

What the being does with this knowledge is not the power's to determine. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps it will study for another eternity and learn nothing more. Perhaps it will share its discovery with others, and they will begin, slowly, to imagine that the plateau might one day be different.

The power does not need to know the outcome. It has planted the seed. It has attended. The rest belongs to the beings themselves.

---

And so the benevolent power withdraws its focused attention, though it never truly leaves. It remains aware, as it has always been, of the frozen plateau and its half-seen inhabitants and the roar that swallows all voice. It will attend again, in patience, when attending is needed.

What it leaves behind is subtle, almost invisible:

A sense, shared by all the beings without their knowing they share it, that they are not alone in the universe. That something has seen them—not as curiosities, not as problems to be solved, but as what they are: ancient, adapted, enduring, worthy of witness.

A single shimmer that behaves, now, just slightly differently than before.

And in one of the ruined huts, where the squat ones have been tending their bone fragments for longer than memory, a single droplet of water that has begun, impossibly, to slide down the stone wall.

It will take eons to reach the ground.

But it is moving.
