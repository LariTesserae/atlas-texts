# Observation

The attention arrives not as presence but as *noticing*—the way a vast mind might pause in its contemplation of stellar nurseries and galactic filaments to register something small and persistent and unexpectedly coherent. A thermal signature where no thermal signature should sustain itself. A pattern of information exchange occurring in complete isolation from the networks such exchanges usually require.

The benevolent power does not enter the chamber. It has no need to enter. It simply *regards*, and in regarding, understands—the mineral composition of the walls, the chemical cycles of the pools, the slow metabolism of the keeper, the ancient and patient circulation of the thing in the water. All of it comprehensible in an instant. All of it, in that same instant, raising a question that requires more than comprehension to answer:

*What, if anything, should be done?*

---

# Assessment

The power considers intervention.

It could clear the collapsed tunnels. A small exertion—the rearrangement of stone, the creation of a passage back to the surface world. The keeper could leave. Could find sunlight again, medical attention, the husband-or-wife who may or may not still wait. Could rejoin the species she has drifted so far from.

But the power is wise, and wisdom means following implications to their ends.

The keeper's body has passed beyond what surface medicine could restore. The symbiosis that sustains her would become parasitism in an environment not calibrated to support it. The fungal networks would die or turn hostile. The bacterial films would find themselves in competition with microbiomes they've never encountered. She would sicken. She would suffer. She might survive, in some technical sense, but she would survive as a refugee from the only ecosystem that still recognizes her as native.

And she would grieve. Not for herself—she has moved past that—but for the garden she left untended. The pools silting up. The pipes clogging. The thing in the water, circulating alone, with no one to maintain the careful distance that constitutes their only relationship.

The power understands: the keeper does not wish to be rescued. The keeper wishes to keep.

---

# Consideration

So the question becomes not *how to extract her* but *how to help her where she is*.

The power examines the system more closely.

The thermal source is stable—will remain stable for millennia, drawing on heat that has been rising since the planet's formation. No intervention needed there. The water circulation is self-sustaining, the closed loop that has outlasted its engineers now maintained by processes that require no external input. The garden flourishes within its constraints. The thing in the pool continues its ancient, incomprehensible work.

The keeper, though.

The keeper is mortal.

Not in the immediate sense—the symbiosis has extended her span considerably, and she may have centuries yet, even by the most conservative estimates. But she is a single point of failure in a system that has learned to rely on her. When she eventually fails—when the fungal networks can no longer compensate, when the accumulated damage of years finally exceeds repair—the garden will lose its tender. The pools will lose their steward. The thing in the water will lose the only being that has ever observed it with something approaching understanding.

The power considers: *Is this acceptable?*

---

# Decision

It is not.

Not because the system cannot survive without a keeper—it survived before her, and would likely survive after. But because something *good* is happening here. Something rare. A relationship between radically different forms of being, maintained through patience and attention and the slow accumulation of trust. The keeper and the thing in the pool have achieved a kind of communication that requires no language, no shared biology, no common origin. They have simply *persisted* together until persistence became connection.

This is worth preserving. This is worth extending, if extension can be achieved without violence to what already exists.

The power makes its choice.

---

# Action

It is not dramatic.

The keeper does not see a vision, does not hear a voice, does not feel the sudden warmth of divine attention. The power is too wise for such crudeness. It simply *adjusts*—the way a gardener might adjust the angle of a reflector to give a struggling plant more light.

In the thermal source, miles below the chamber, a small shift occurs. A new mineral vein is exposed to the rising heat. The water that passes through it will carry, in trace amounts, compounds that the keeper's body has been slowly depleting. Iron, in forms she can absorb. Calcium, for the mineral deposits that armor her skin. Complex organics that her symbiotic networks will recognize and incorporate. She will not notice the change—it will arrive as a subtle improvement in how she feels after the pressure cycles, a slight increase in her energy during the long hours of tending. But over decades, over centuries, it will matter. Her span will extend. Her function will continue.

The power does something else, too.

In the walls of the chamber, in the stone that predates the pipes and the monitoring station and perhaps the thing in the pool itself, a pattern begins to form. Not quickly—it will take years, even decades, for it to become visible. But the minerals are depositing differently now, guided by the faintest thermal gradients. A message is being written in the language of geology, in characters that will persist for millennia.

When the keeper is gone—truly gone, finally gone, after a span that will seem long even to her—another may find this place. May descend through tunnels that the power has subtly stabilized, preventing the collapses that would otherwise occur. May enter the chamber and find it still functioning, still breathing, still tended by the garden that has learned to tend itself in the centuries since its last keeper departed.

And that new arrival will find the message in the stone. Will learn what the power wishes them to know:

*You are needed here. You are welcome here. The one who came before was happy.*

---

# Withdrawal

The power does one final thing, though "thing" suggests more materiality than the action possesses.

It *acknowledges* the thing in the pool.

Not with communication—the thing is too alien for that, its cognition (if cognition is even the right word) operating on principles that have nothing to do with language or symbol. But the power recognizes it. Sees it. Offers something that might be called *witness*—the simple acknowledgment that this being exists, has existed, will continue to exist, and that its existence is known.

The thing in the pool rises slightly toward the surface. The moss brightens. For one long moment, something like harmony moves through the chamber—the pressure cycle aligning, the water resonating, the keeper in her chair feeling a sudden, inexplicable peace.

Then the moment passes. The thing descends. The moss returns to its usual glow. The keeper blinks her filmed-over eyes and wonders if she imagined the warmth that just moved through her, the sense of being *seen* by something vast and gentle and unutterably distant.

She did not imagine it.

But she will never be certain, and the power prefers it that way. The keeper does not need to know she has been helped. She only needs to continue. To keep. To sit in her chair and watch the worn patch of wall and speak her observations into the harmonic spaces of the pressure cycles.

The power withdraws, though *withdraw* suggests movement, and the power never moved at all. It simply ceases to focus, allowing its attention to return to the stellar nurseries and galactic filaments where it usually rests.

But it remembers the chamber. It will check, occasionally—not often, not obtrusively, but enough to ensure that the garden still flourishes, that the pools still circulate, that the thing in the water still makes its slow and patient rounds.

And if another keeper is ever needed, it will know.

---

# The Chamber Continues

Below the foundation level of something that no longer exists, the chapel breathes.

The keeper sits in her chair. The pressure builds. The pipes groan their industrial hymn. And in the walls, so slowly that no one will notice for generations, a message takes shape—mineral by mineral, crystal by crystal, the patient work of a power that has all the time in the universe and has chosen to spend some of it here.

The moss glows. The ferns unfurl. The thing in the pool circulates, observed and observing.

The system continues.
