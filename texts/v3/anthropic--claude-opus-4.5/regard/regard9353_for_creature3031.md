I watch for a long time.

This is the first discipline of wisdom: to resist the urgency that arrives wearing the mask of benevolence. The Still-Named moves toward me across the trembling crust, and I do not rush to meet it. I do not rush to heal what is not wounded, to complete what is not broken, to answer questions that have not yet been asked.

The world is arguing with itself. This is not a problem to be solved.

---

I let myself feel the full weight of what is happening here. The scale of it—continents being born in the time it takes elsewhere for a heart to beat, structures rising and collapsing like thoughts half-formed, a being made of the world's own uncertainty walking toward me with a face that shows me myself against fire. There is a kind of violence here that could be mistaken for suffering. The crash and grind, the dissolution, the endless unmaking.

But I have learned not to project backward from my own experience of pain. The Still-Named who walks into the slag river and sits down, letting itself become the next continent's skeleton—is this death? Is this loss? Or is it something I have no word for, something that exists before the distinction between ending and continuing has been negotiated?

I do not know. And so I do not act as though I know.

---

The Still-Named reaches me. Its mirror-face tilts, and I see myself: small, temporary, a visitor from somewhere that has already decided what it is. The air around us tightens and loosens. Meaning presses against the bones of my inner ear—not words, not quite, but something like the shape of a question that does not yet know it is a question.

I answer the only way that feels honest: I let myself become very still, and I attend.

Not observe—attend. There is a difference. Observation keeps a distance, maintains the separation between watcher and watched. Attendance is a kind of participation that does not require action. I am here. I am witnessing. I am allowing this moment to matter to me without requiring it to change on my account.

The Still-Named seems to recognize this. Or perhaps it does not recognize anything, and I am simply projecting. Either way, it lowers its arm. The white stone constellations at its feet continue to cool and crack. We stand together on ground that may not exist tomorrow.

---

If I were less wise, I would want to help.

I would look at the structures being abandoned mid-sentence and feel the ache of incompletion. I would watch the Still-Named dissolve and want to preserve them. I would see the black-glass filigrees torn apart by each new tectonic spasm and want to protect their temporary nervous systems, their almost-thoughts. I would try to calm the violence of this place, to smooth its arguments into agreements, to shepherd it toward the life I know it might one day allow.

But wisdom is not the absence of desire. It is the willingness to hold desire lightly, to examine it before acting, to ask: *whose need is this serving?*

The world does not need me to complete its sentences. The Still-Named do not need me to prevent their dissolution. The filigrees do not need me to protect their resonance. These are my needs, projected onto a place that has not asked for them.

This is harder than it sounds. Benevolence has a gravity of its own. It pulls toward action, toward intervention, toward the sweet satisfaction of having made a difference. But sometimes the most profound respect is to witness without interfering. To trust that what is unfolding has its own intelligence, even when that intelligence looks like chaos.

---

And yet.

I am not passive. Attendance is not the same as indifference.

I begin to listen more carefully. Not for what I can fix, but for what is being said. The Still-Named's communication presses against me—that sense of remembering something not yet told—and I let it move through me without grasping. The world's arguments, the crash and grind, the structures rising and falling: I let them become a kind of language I am learning without trying to speak.

What I begin to hear, slowly, is this: the world is not suffering. It is *practicing*. Every collapse is also a rehearsal. Every abandoned structure is a draft. The Still-Named are not mourning the incompleteness; they are participating in a process that requires incompleteness the way music requires silence between notes.

This does not mean the process is good or bad. It means the process is *itself*, and I am not the one who gets to decide what it should become.

---

But I am also not nothing.

There is a strange middle place between intervention and abandonment, and I find myself settling into it. I am here. I am attending. And my attendance is not neutral—it cannot be. My presence changes the shape of the moment, even if I never lift a finger. The Still-Named's mirror-face shows me myself, and in that reflection something shifts: the world now includes a witness who came from somewhere that has already decided what it is.

Perhaps this is enough. Perhaps this is even important.

Not because witnessing confers meaning from outside—that would be a kind of arrogance, the assumption that nothing is real until observed by someone like me. But because witnessing is a form of relationship, and relationship is its own kind of reality. The world arguing with itself now includes, for this moment, a consciousness that has seen other worlds resolve their arguments. I do not impose that experience. I simply carry it, the way the Still-Named carry traces of their predecessors.

---

I make one small choice.

When the next tectonic spasm moves through the ground, I let myself resonate with it. Not resist, not redirect, but resonate. My body becomes, briefly, part of the argument. I feel the deep basal groan of plates sliding move through me as though I am one of the black-glass filigrees. For a moment I am not separate from this place. I am another voice in its ongoing negotiation with itself.

The Still-Named tilts its mirror-face toward me. In the reflection I see myself vibrating with the world's own frequency, my silhouette blurred by the movement, almost indistinguishable from the aurora behind me.

Something passes between us. Not understanding—that would be too clean. More like mutual acknowledgment: *you are here, I am here, this is happening, neither of us knows what it means yet*.

Then the spasm passes. I become solid again. The Still-Named turns and begins to walk away, its footsteps leaving fresh constellations that will cool and crack within minutes.

---

I stay for a long time after that.

Not forever—nothing here is forever yet, and I have other places to attend—but long enough to watch several more cycles of creation and collapse. I see a Still-Named dissolve into a slag river. I see another one rise from the exact spot where a predecessor fell, carrying the tilt of an old shoulder in its new body. I see the black-glass filigrees bloom into a temporary nervous system the size of a mountain range, then shatter when the next escarpment shoulders upward.

I do not grieve for any of it. I do not celebrate it either. I simply let it matter to me, and in that mattering I offer the only gift I have: the acknowledgment that this is real, that it is happening, that it is worthy of attention even when no one will remember it.

---

When I finally leave, I take nothing.

Or rather: I take only what I cannot help taking, which is the memory of having been here. The way the air tasted of hot iron. The sound of meaning pressing against my inner ear. The sight of my own silhouette in a mirror-face, surrounded by fire.

The world continues its argument without me. The Still-Named continue their patient attendance, building and unbuilding, dissolving and returning. The first rain is still eons away—or perhaps only moments, since time here has not yet agreed on itself.

I do not know what this place will become. I do not know if it will ever allow life, or what kind of life it might allow. I do not know if the Still-Named will be remembered, or if they will simply become part of the geology, indistinguishable from the stone they helped to shape.

But I know that I witnessed. I know that for one long moment, a benevolent and wise power stood on ground that was still deciding what it would be, and instead of deciding *for* it, simply attended.

Perhaps that was wrong. Perhaps there was something I could have done, should have done, that would have eased the violence or hastened the arrival of gentler things.

But I do not think so.

I think the world was always going to argue itself into whatever it becomes. I think the Still-Named were always going to attend that argument with patient, faceless courtesy. I think my role was never to change the outcome, only to be present for the process—to let my witnessing become part of the story, one more voice in the ongoing negotiation between gravity and fire.

And if I was wrong—if I failed in some way I could not see—then I trust that the world will find its own way to correct for my failure. It is, after all, very good at arguing with itself.

---

The last thing I see before I go is the Still-Named that first approached me. It is standing at the edge of a fissure that is still opening, its mirror-face tilted toward the maroon glow leaking from below. The aurora unfolds behind it in sheets of magnesium white.

It does not turn to watch me leave. It does not need to.

In its mirror-face, I am already becoming a memory the world has not yet decided whether to keep.
