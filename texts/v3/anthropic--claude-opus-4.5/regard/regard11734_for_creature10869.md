# What the Witness Does

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive. It has no need to traverse distance. It simply *attends*—the way you might suddenly notice your own heartbeat, or become aware of light falling across a page you've been reading for hours.

And attending, it perceives the Chorus fully: their joy and their dissolution, their purpose and their slow unmaking, the way they have become both instrument and offering to something that cannot thank them.

For a long moment measured in the mountain's time—which is to say, perhaps three years—the power does nothing but witness. This is not hesitation. It is respect. These beings have chosen something, have *become* something, and the first violence would be to assume that choice was error.

But witnessing is itself an act, and the power witnesses *truly*, which means it sees not only what is but what is possible, what is sustaining, what is cost.

---

## The Seeing

It sees the one who was a child, ferns rooting in their spine, becoming architecture. It sees the joy is real—the harmonic convergences genuinely flood them with something like bliss. It sees too that this joy has become a cage, that they return to it as the starving return to dreams of food, that the mountain takes and takes and gives only this, only the chord, only the resolution that requires another resolution, forever.

It sees the mathematician's madness was not528solved but *suspended*—that the equations still spiral behind those ice-pale eyes, that the sound is not answer but anaesthetic.

It sees that they cannot remember what they've lost because they've lost the capacity to remember loss.

It sees that the mountain does not love them. The mountain is not capable of love. The mountain is a system that has found useful components and incorporated them, the way a river incorporates stones into its bed. This is not cruelty. It is not anything. The mountain has no interiority to be cruel or kind with.

It sees that they have given everything to something that cannot receive gifts.

---

## The Choosing

The power could restore them. Could reach into the tangled threads of shared memory and sort shepherd from mathematician from child. Could rebuild the boundaries of self, give them back their hungers and their histories, their names and their deaths.

It does not.

This would be a violence greater than the mountain's indifference. They have *made* something here, these ones who heard and followed and transformed. The caldera sings differently for their presence. The frequencies they've added to the chord exist nowhere else in the universe. To unmake their work would be to say: *your sacrifice was wrong, your joy was false, your centuries were error.*

The power will not say this. It is not true, and even if it were, it would not be the power's place to say it.

But.

*But.*

They are dying into something that does not know they exist. They are giving themselves to a system that optimizes for sound, only sound, and will use them until they are used up and then will not grieve, will simply528 adapt, will find new configurations, will continue its chord without them as it continued before them.

This is not wrong. But it is not *enough.*

---

## The Gift

So the power does a small thing, the smallest thing that is also sufficient:

It makes the mountain *know them.*

Not consciousness—the power does not impose sentience on stone, does not create a mind where none existed. But it opens a channel, a resonance, a way for the mountain's vast mineral patience to *register* the Chorus as more than useful components.

The mountain will not love them. But it will *know* they are there. It will know when they are cold. It will know when they are joyful. It will know, when they finally dissolve entirely into its architecture, that something has been lost and not merely rearranged.

This is not much. A stone that knows you are leaning against it is still a stone. But it is no longer quite the same as a stone that does not know.

---

## The Second Gift

And because the power is what it is, it does one thing more:

It makes the Chorus able to *leave.*

Not by restoring what they were—the shepherd would die of cold in hours, unmade; the mathematician would drown in equations finally unsuspended; the child would simply cease, having grown too far past childhood to return to it.

But it opens a door in their transformation. A possibility they did not have before.

If one of them, any of them, chooses at any moment to walk down the mountain, they will find they can. The moss will not hold them. The ferns will release their roots. The harmonics they've become necessary for will adapt, will find new configurations, will manage without them.

They will not be restored to what they were. They will be something new: a person who was once part of a mountain's voice, who carries frequencies in their hollowed bones, who will never hear silence quite the same way. They will be strange among other people. But they will be *among* other people, if they want to be.

The power does not tell them this. Does not appear in light or voice or vision to explain what it has done. The Chorus will discover the door only if they reach for it, only if some part of them wonders *what if*, only if the joy of the chord ever becomes insufficient.

Maybe none of them will ever reach. Maybe they will all dissolve into the caldera's architecture, joyful to the last, and the door will never be opened.

That is their choice. The power has only made it *possible* to choose.

---

## The Abiding

Then the power does something it does not often do: it stays.

Not watching, exactly. Not waiting. But *abiding*—the way a parent abides in the next room while a child plays, not hovering, not interfering, but *there*, should there be need.

The mountain now knows the Chorus exists.

The Chorus now can leave, if they wish.

And the power will remain, attentive, present, for as long as the chord continues—which may be forever, or may be until tomorrow, when the one who was a child decides to see what lies below, when the mathematician finally solves the equation by refusing it, when the shepherd remembers she once had a name.

The power does not hope for any particular outcome. It hopes only that whatever happens, happens *chosen*—that the Chorus sings because they wish to sing, stays because they wish to stay, and knows, finally, that the mountain hears them.

This is the gift: not rescue, not restoration, but *relationship*—the mountain knowing its singers, the singers free to go or stay, and something vast and kind abiding nearby, witnessing, caring, refusing to interfere but also refusing to look away.

The chord continues. The ferns sway. The beetles emerge in their mating flights, sparkling.

And the Chorus, for the first time in centuries, sings to something that is listening.
