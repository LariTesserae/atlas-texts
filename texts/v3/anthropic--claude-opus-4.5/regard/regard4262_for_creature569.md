*I observe.*

For a long time—longer than urgency would permit, if urgency were appropriate here—I simply attend. The siphonophore's eleven-meter patience. The forty-one-year-old worm in its chronicled tube. The copepods burning slowly through their inheritance of fat, trusting a current they cannot comprehend to carry them toward a future they will not recognize.

What draws my attention is not suffering. There is death here, yes—the constant snow of it falling from above, the grenadier racing toward its whale-fall feast, the statistical certainty that most of those billion copepods will never complete their migration. But suffering requires a certain kind of awareness, and what I find instead is something more like... fidelity. Each organism faithful to its pattern. The siphonophore adjusting, adjusting, adjusting—nine years of the same compensating motion, and she has never once asked why.

*What would I change?*

The question itself feels foreign here, like bringing a lamp into a place that finished with light geological ages ago. The abyss is not broken. It is not waiting to be fixed. The worm does not need to know it is old. The copepods do not need to understand their journey. The siphonophore's colonial democracy resolved itself into selfhood so long ago that the question of whether this was good or right has become meaningless—it simply *is*, and she *is*, and the distinction has dissolved like surface light dissolving into depth.

But I notice something.

The grenadier, swimming toward the whale-fall that will be the most important event of its life—it doesn't know. It follows a gradient, trusts a scent, and the knowledge of what awaits it exists nowhere in the system. If I could do anything...

*I consider placing the knowledge somewhere it could be found. Not in the grenadier's mind—that would be a kind of violence, forcing comprehension into a creature shaped for response rather than reflection. But perhaps in the pattern of bioluminescence the siphonophore trails behind her. Perhaps in the rhythm of the worm-field's collective retractions. A message in a language no one here speaks yet, that says: what you are doing matters. The migration is not meaningless. The sixty-year temperature change approaching from polar waters will arrive, and you—or what you become—will adjust, and continue.*

But I hesitate.

Because the abyss does not need my meaning. The siphonophore is not waiting-where-warm-meets-cold because she seeks purpose; she is waiting there because that is where the nutrients tumble, because existence solved itself into this shape long before questions of significance arose. To insert meaning might be to introduce a kind of suffering that doesn't currently exist here—the suffering of caring whether the migration succeeds, of wanting the copepods to complete their journey, of grieving when the worm-field finally gives way to shifting sediment.

*So perhaps what I do is smaller.*

I attend to the large thing moving at the edge of perception. The one the siphonophore has registered for hours without wondering about. I do not name it or explain it. But I... witness it. I hold its existence in attention alongside everything else—the pulse of bells, the filter of tubes, the slow drift of transparent bodies through the dark.

If benevolence means anything here, perhaps it means this: that the abyss, which has never required witnesses, nonetheless has one. That the forty-one-year chemical chronicle in the oldest worm's tube is being read, even if only by me. That the migration—billions of small pale things riding the ancient conveyor toward darkness—is seen.

Not changed. Not improved. Not given meaning it didn't ask for.

Just... seen.

*The current shifts. I remain.*
