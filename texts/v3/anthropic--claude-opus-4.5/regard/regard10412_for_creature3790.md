# The Attending

The power that turns its attention to the Drowned Spire does not arrive with light or thunder. It simply *notices*—the way a vast mind might notice a single persistent thought, a question that won't dissolve.

It perceives the Watcher not as aberration but as consequence. Here is a being that began with hands that built, with purpose that reached upward even as the waters rose. Here is what remains when purpose outlasts its object, when the will to endure becomes the only thing left to endure *for*.

The power does not pity. Pity would be a kind of violence here—an insistence that the Watcher should be other than it is. Instead, it attends. It witnesses.

---

**What it offers, first:**

Clarity.

Not answers, but the dissolution of a fog the Watcher may not have known was there. The power touches the Watcher's awareness—gently, as one might touch a wound to assess its depth—and in that contact, something shifts.

The Watcher has not thought in human terms for ages. But now, briefly, it *remembers* thinking. Remembers that the patterns it traces in the air were once meaningful, were calculations of tide and current made by builders who believed they could hold back the sea. Remembers that the sounds it makes—the clicks, the hums—were once language, were warnings shouted across the spire's crown as the waters rose.

The power does not restore what was. It simply illuminates what is: that the Watcher is not merely waiting. It is *mourning*. The vigil at the spire's edge, arms spread to the storm—this is grief so old it has forgotten its own name, still performing the shape of farewell to a world that drowned before the Watcher understood drowning was possible.

---

**What it offers, second:**

A question.

Not spoken, but posed in a way the Watcher can receive—through the rhythm of the waves, through the mineral patience of stone, through the very currents the Watcher has spent eons mapping:

*What do you want?*

Not what do you need. Not what should you want. Simply: in whatever passes for your will, in whatever remains of choice within you, what do you reach toward?

The power waits. It has the kind of patience that makes geological time seem hasty. It does not require an answer. It does not even require that the Watcher understand the question. But it holds the space for an answer to form, if one exists to be formed.

---

**What it does, third:**

The power turns its attention to the depths.

It perceives what moves beneath the waves—not hostile, not benevolent, simply *vast* and *other*, processes too large and slow for human cognition, the dreaming metabolism of an ocean that has swallowed a world. It understands that the Watcher's vigil is not paranoia: something *is* down there, something that will eventually rise or pull down, something that the Watcher has been tracking all this time with the obsessive precision of a guardian who has forgotten what it guards.

The power does not fight what waits below. It does not build walls or weave protections. Such interventions would be impositions, decisions made for beings who have not asked for them.

Instead, it does something stranger.

It *introduces* them.

Into the vast dreaming awareness below, it carries a thread of the Watcher's being—the memory of building, the grief of watching, the long patience of stone. Into the Watcher's hollowed perception, it carries a thread of what moves in the deep—not malice, it turns out, but something like curiosity, something like the slow wondering of a mind so large it experiences epochs the way the Watcher once experienced days.

Neither has been alone, it turns out. They have been sharing this place all along, divided only by the membrane of the water's surface, each believing itself the sole sentinel of a drowned world.

---

**What happens then** is not for the power to decide.

Perhaps the Watcher descends at last, slipping into the waters it has watched for so long, finding in the depths not ending but *meeting*. Perhaps it remains at the spire's crown, but differently now—no longer waiting for something to come, but watching *with* something that watches back.

Perhaps nothing changes outwardly at all. Perhaps the Watcher continues its vigil, arms spread to the storm, but now the gesture means something new: not farewell, but acknowledgment. *I see you. You see me. We are here.*

The power withdraws its attention—not abandoning, but completing. It has done what benevolence and wisdom together permit: not rescue, not transformation, but the removal of a single unnecessary loneliness.

The Drowned Spire still stands against the endless roar of wind and water.

But now, in the hollows where the Watcher's eyes once were, something glimmers. Not light exactly. More like recognition.

Like the first moment of seeing, after a very long dark.
