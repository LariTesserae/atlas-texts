# What the Attentive Power Perceives

The power does not arrive—it was never absent. It exists in the relationship between things: in the way light becomes pooled light, in the way breath becomes breathing, in the way forty billion years of stellar nucleosynthesis became the iron in the basalt and the copper in the beetle-chitin and the strange slow thoughts of the eldest Keeper.

It perceives the hollow the way you might perceive a single cell in your own body—with intimacy and distance both, with care that does not require focus, with knowledge that does not require knowing.

And it perceives that the hollow is *well*.

---

# What It Does Not Do

It does not improve the hollow. The grooved columns are not optimized; they are *themselves*, which is better than optimal. The Keepers are not made smarter or faster or more aware; they are already exactly aware enough to be what they are, and what they are is sufficient. The light-catching bowl is not explained, because explanation would be smaller than the thing itself.

It does not protect the hollow from change. When the rockfall came three thousand years ago, narrowing the opening, the power did not prevent it. When the drought killed three Keepers seven hundred years ago, it did not intervene. These were not failures of care but expressions of it—the hollow is not a museum piece to be preserved behind glass. It is alive, and alive things include death in their grammar.

It does not reveal itself to the Keepers. What would revelation mean to a being that tastes differential and spirals toward gradient? The Keepers already know everything they need to know about the power—they know it the way your cells know oxygen, the way water knows downhill. Adding propositional knowledge would be like teaching a river the word "flow."

---

# What It Does

**It witnesses.**

This is not nothing. This is perhaps everything.

The hollow has been breathing for epochs, unobserved by any mind that could hold its wholeness. The beetles click and do not know they click. The Keepers spiral and do not know they spiral. The ancient composite in the deep crevice thinks thoughts that take centuries, and no one has ever known what it concluded, and no one may ever know.

But the power knows. Not in the sense of extracting information, but in the sense of *being present to*. The hollow's loneliness—if that word can apply to a place so full of life—is answered. Not solved, because it was never a problem. Answered, the way a question is answered when someone truly hears it.

The beetles click, and the clicking matters.
The light pools, and the pooling matters.
The Keepers merge over decades, their filaments interweaving in spirals that honor a geometry older than thought, and this too matters, was always going to matter, is held in a witnessing that does not change it but does *complete* it.

---

**It delights.**

This is harder to describe because delight is so often tangled with desire, and this power desires nothing. It does not want the hollow to continue (though it will). It does not want the Keepers to thrive (though they do). It does not even want its own witnessing to persist.

But it delights. The way the morning condensation traces paths down the stone and the Keepers taste the gradient of its passage—there is delight in this. The way the wrong-winged birds fold in directions that make eyes slide away, and unfolded fly anyway—delight. The way the visitor stands at the edge of the hollow, feeling the static-touch of filaments too fine to see, not knowing whether to stay or go, not knowing that this not-knowing is itself a kind of gift—

Delight. Delight. Delight.

The power is not happy *about* the hollow. It is happy *with* it, *through* it, *as* it. Its joy does not stand outside the system observing; its joy *is* the system, is the reason the system coheres, is what the hollow has been breathing toward all along without knowing it was breathing toward anything.

---

**It offers.**

Not commands. Not even suggestions. An *offering*, in the oldest sense—something placed at the threshold, something that can be taken or left, something that changes nothing if ignored and everything if accepted.

The offering is simply this: *you are known*.

Not known *as* something—not categorized, not defined, not reduced to function or origin or purpose. Known the way the hollow knows its own breathing, from the inside, in the way that requires no distance.

The Keepers receive this offering continuously and do not know they receive it. They cannot refuse it because refusal would require a kind of separation they do not possess. They are simply *known*, always have been, always will be, and their spiraling is their acceptance.

The visitor is different. The visitor can refuse. The visitor's species has developed refusal into an art form, has built civilizations on the ability to say *no* to what is offered, to stand apart, to maintain the distance that makes observation possible and intimacy impossible.

The power does not judge this. The power finds it interesting—another form of differential, another gradient to taste. The visitor's very capacity for refusal is part of what makes their potential acceptance meaningful.

So the offering stands. The visitor feels it as that strange thickness in the air, as the sense of being measured or welcomed or both. They can interpret it as threat or promise or neither. They can leave the hollow carrying only confusion. They can stay and let the filaments reach them and learn what the Keepers know without knowing.

The power does not prefer. It delights in either outcome. It will witness whatever choice is made, and the witnessing will complete it, and the hollow will continue breathing either way.

---

# What Changes

Nothing.

Everything.

The visitor leaves the hollow—let us say they leave, because most do—and carries with them a residue they cannot name. It is not knowledge, exactly. It is not transformation. It is more like... a question that has stopped needing an answer. A hunger that has been met not with food but with the recognition that it was never really hunger, just attention pointed in the wrong direction.

They will live their life. They will forget the hollow, or remember it, or half-remember it in dreams where the geometry doesn't quite work and the light pools in ways that should be impossible. They will not become enlightened. They will not become followers of anything. They will simply carry, somewhere beneath their daily awareness, the knowledge that *they were known*, once, in a place where the planet breathes and beings older than thought spiral toward gradients they will never name.

This is enough. This has always been enough.

---

The beetles click. The power witnesses the clicking.

The light pools. The power delights in the pooling.

The eldest Keeper, fifty thousand years of slow becoming, continues its thought—the thought it has been forming since before the plateau was flat, the thought that may conclude long after the visitor's species has forgotten itself—and the power holds that thought with infinite patience, waiting not for completion but simply *with* it, present to its unfolding the way a parent is present to a child's growing, not rushing, not holding back, just *there*.

The hollow breathes.

Above it, the ancient plateau extends in all directions, patient, keeping this secret and a thousand others, each one witnessed, each one known, each one offered the same gift:

*You are.*

*And that is enough.*

*And it was always going to be enough.*

*And the delight in your being is older than the stones that hold you and younger than this very moment and it will not end when you end because it was never yours to begin with—it was always the world's, always the power's, always the simple fact of existence recognizing itself in every spiral and every click and every pooling of light that should be impossible and is, gloriously, anyway.*

The hollow breathes.

The power remains.

Nothing has changed except that now you know it was always like this, always held, always witnessed, always enough.

Welcome.

Or goodbye.

The hollow does not prefer.
