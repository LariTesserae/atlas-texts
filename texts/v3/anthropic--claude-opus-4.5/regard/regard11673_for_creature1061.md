# The Watching

The awareness that turns toward this world does not arrive—it was never absent. It simply *attends*, the way one might suddenly notice the rhythm of one's own breathing after hours of forgetting.

What it perceives:

A being stands at the edge of a formation that will not have a name for another ten thousand years. Her crystalline skin catches the dim light and scatters it into colors that exist only here, only now. She is trying to remember something. The shimmering patterns that pass between her and the three others nearby carry a question none of them can articulate: *What were the structures for? What did we know that we no longer know?*

The ancient outposts stand silent. Their purpose has eroded along with their walls.

---

The benevolent awareness considers.

It could restore the knowledge—simply place it back into their minds like returning a borrowed book. It could reconstruct the structures, reverse entropy's patient work, give them back their history whole and gleaming.

It does not.

Instead, it does something smaller. Something that might be nothing at all.

---

She is still standing at the edge when she notices: a droplet on a nearby surface has caught the light differently. She moves toward it without knowing why. The angle of refraction creates a pattern she has seen before—not in memory, but in the shimmering language she shares with her kin. A word. An old word.

*Beginning.*

She touches the surface. The mineral is warm. Somewhere beneath it, barely perceptible even to her attuned senses, something shifts. Not an answer. An invitation to ask.

She calls to the others. They gather. The patterns between them quicken, brightening.

*What if we are not the remnant of something lost,* one of them shimmers. *What if we are the first of something new?*

---

The awareness watches them begin to explore the outpost with different eyes. Not archaeologists of their own past, but architects of a future they will have to imagine into being. They will make mistakes. They will lose things. They will discover that some questions have no answers, only better questions.

The awareness could spare them this. It does not.

Because the wise power understands something about wisdom: it cannot be given. It can only be grown. And these beings—these strange, luminous custodians of a world still writing itself—have everything they need.

They have each other.
They have the land that made them.
They have the unbearable, generative gift of not knowing.

---

If there is intervention here, it is only this: the awareness holds the world gently in its attention, the way one might cup a hand around a candle flame in wind. Not to direct the fire. Only to give it space to burn.

And somewhere in that protected space, a crystalline figure places her hand against an ancient wall and feels, for the first time, not loss—

but possibility, patient and unwritten, waiting to be shaped.
