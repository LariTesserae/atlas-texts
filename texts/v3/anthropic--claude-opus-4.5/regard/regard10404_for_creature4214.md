The Watcher had observed countless worlds, but this place—this stubborn knot of humanity pressed between stone and darkness—held its attention longer than most.

It did not appear as light or voice or vision. That was not its way. Instead, it worked through the fabric of things already present, already possible.

---

**The First Gift: Memory of Green**

In the dreams of children, it planted seeds—not literal ones, but the *idea* of seeds. A girl named Tova woke one morning with an inexplicable certainty that the pale moss growing near the water intake could be cultivated, spread, encouraged. She didn't know why she suddenly understood this. She simply began collecting it in her pockets, pressing it into cracks where moisture gathered.

Within a year, patches of soft green began appearing throughout the city—small victories against the grey. The air grew fractionally easier to breathe. No one connected it to Tova's quiet obsession, and she never claimed credit. The Watcher preferred it this way.

---

**The Second Gift: The Cartographer's Revelation**

Old Miren, who had spent forty years mapping the city's chaotic sprawl, found himself unable to sleep. In his restless pacing, patterns emerged from his life's work that he had never seen before—flows of water, air, movement that suggested the city was not random at all, but an organism still finding its shape. He began leaving his maps in public places, annotated with questions rather than answers. *What if we connected here? What do we lose by blocking this passage?*

Arguments followed. Then experiments. Then, slowly, intention.

---

**The Third Gift: What It Withheld**

The Watcher could have shown them the surface. Could have revealed that three hundred meters above, sunlight still fell on an Earth that had healed in their absence. Could have opened the sealed shafts.

It did not.

These people had built something genuine in the dark—a culture of making-do, of finding beauty in condensation patterns and the echo of footsteps on steel. They were not ready to learn their exile had been forgotten, that no one above remembered why the shafts were sealed, that they could leave whenever they chose.

That knowledge would come. But first, the Watcher decided, let them discover they had never needed rescue. Let them find the surface themselves, and climb toward it as equals rather than refugees.

---

In the depths, a child pressed moss into stone, and something ancient watched with something very like hope.
