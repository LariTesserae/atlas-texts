# What the Wise Power Does

The power observes for a long time before acting—longer than the channels take to shift their banks, though not so long as the vines have been thickening around the axles. It watches the drip-rhythms and the belt-house harmonics. It notes how the broad-shouldered people wake when the thrum changes, how their stroking of condensation from root-bridges mirrors the vegetation's own movements. It sees that this is not stagnation but rather a metabolism that has found its tempo.

And so the power does very little.

---

It makes one small adjustment to the canopy, not opening it further but shifting the angle of three heavy vine-clusters so that twice each year, for perhaps an hour, direct light reaches a single platform near the center of the basin. The people will notice this. They will feel the unfamiliar warmth on their tacky skin and see their shadows sharp-edged for the first time in living memory. Some will move away from it; others will sit in it, puzzled and pleased.

The power does this not to change them but to remind them that change exists. The basin's patience is genuine and hard-won, but patience can become forgetting. The hour of direct light will give them something to anticipate, to remember, to speak of in the long intervals between.

---

It considers the engines—those low-revolution things still turning in their vine-choked sheds—and finds them sound. The living patina that coats their gears is itself a kind of maintenance, and the people who lean their weight against laboring housings have become part of the mechanism as surely as any belt or bearing. The power sees no need to improve what already works. It does not accelerate the engines or replace them with something cleaner. It simply ensures that the knowledge of their making is not entirely lost: it places, in the hollow of a root-bridge where a child will one day find it, a small object of scored metal that shows how a gear-tooth is cut. The child may never use it. But the possibility will exist.

---

The power grieves, briefly, for the birds that no longer come. The pale moths are beautiful in their way, but something has been lost. It does not try to restore what cannot be restored. Instead it listens more carefully to the moths' wingbeats and finds in them a frequency that, amplified slightly through the water-channels, produces a new note in the district's soundscape—not sharp, not urgent, but present. A reminder of flight. The people will hear it in their sleep and some of them will dream of sky, though they may not know the word.

---

Finally, the power turns its attention to the margins of the basin, where the vine-lattice meets whatever lies beyond. It does not build walls or barriers. It does not seal the district against the outside world. But it strengthens, almost imperceptibly, the root-structures at the boundary, so that the basin's shape will hold a little longer against whatever pressures might come. It does this not because the basin must last forever—nothing must—but because what grows here deserves time to complete its own slow argument about how to live.

---

Then the power withdraws, or rather disperses its attention, becoming again merely the warmth in the water, the sweetness in the peat-breath, the particular way the drip-rhythms answer each other across the green distance. It has done what it came to do: not to save, not to transform, but to offer the smallest inflections toward continued becoming. The basin keeps its own counsel. The engines turn. The people wake and sleep and stroke the condensation from the root-bridges, and the work goes on.
