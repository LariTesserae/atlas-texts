In the dim mist-light, the Inferenorin coiled back from its work, sensing something vast turning its attention toward this small cathedral of weeping stone. The fungal formation it had been pelting shuddered—not from the hardening condensate, but from something else. A presence.

The benevolent power did not arrive so much as *notice*. It had always been noticing, the way deep water notices the fish that swim through it. But now its noticing sharpened, focused, became a kind of gentle pressure in the air.

It observed: the worm, diligent in its destruction, following the logic of its kind. The fungi, adapting, their hyraham tendrils learning to flow around obstacles like prayer finding new words. The strange structures pulsing blue. The rocks dreaming in color.

The power considered intervention. It could dissolve the pellets. It could teach the Inferenorin and the fungi to speak. It could accelerate the landscape's becoming until it bloomed into something stable and named.

Instead, it did something smaller.

It adjusted the mineral content of one weeping rock by three molecules. In eleven generations, this would produce a new kind of condensate—one the Inferenorin could not harden, one the fungi would find nutritious. Neither species would know why their war slowly became a symbiosis. They would simply adapt, as living things do.

The power withdrew its sharpened attention, satisfied.

The landscape continued its strange becoming. The Inferenorin sniffed at its pellet. The fungi farted, hauntingly green.

Nothing seemed changed. Everything was.
