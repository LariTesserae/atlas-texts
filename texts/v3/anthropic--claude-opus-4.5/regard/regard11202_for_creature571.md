*Witnessing*

The attention arrives not as light or sound but as a quality of *noticing*—the way a place can suddenly feel seen without anything visible having changed. The pocket continues its breathing. The filaments continue their whisper-crackle-tick. Kethi continues her slow work, pressing mineral compounds into the growing matrix of her crystal congregation.

But something is different. The air holds a question.

---

The benevolent power does not act immediately. It watches first—truly watches, with the patience that Kethi herself has learned over decades in this space. It observes the way she moves through the filaments, how they part for her, how her body has become a thing the pocket recognizes. It listens to the conversation she is having with the mineral growth, the eleven-year exchanges, the slow accumulation of meaning.

It notices, too, what she cannot notice about herself: the way her consciousness has begun to fragment at the edges, worn thin by isolation. The way she sometimes forgets, mid-thought, that she was ever anything other than this. The way her crystal congregation project has become both her salvation and her trap—a reason to continue that requires her to never leave, never change, never be interrupted.

The power considers intervention. It could heal her body, restore what the heat has taken. It could lift the cobblestone and bring others to her—there are still people in the world above, wanderers who pass through the ruins of Var Tethum, who might be called.

It could give her what she thinks she wants.

But the power is wise, and wisdom means understanding that what someone wants and what someone needs are not always the same thing. Kethi has built something here. She has become something here. To simply *fix* her would be to say that what she has become is wrong, that her decades of adaptation and learning and patient mineral-conversation were a mistake to be corrected.

That is not what the power believes.

---

Instead, it does something smaller. Something that honors what Kethi has made of herself while opening a door she cannot open alone.

The power reaches into the stone above the pocket—into the foundation slabs of Var Tethum, into the dust-filled streets and the dry wells—and it leaves a mark. Not a sign that shouts, but one that whispers. A particular quality in the heat-shimmer that rises from the paving stones. A subtle pull that those who are already searching for something will feel in their chests, a sense of *here, here, this place has something you need*.

It is not a summons. It is an invitation, extended to no one in particular, available to anyone who might be ready to receive it.

And then the power does one thing more.

It reaches into the pocket itself—gently, so gently, the way one might touch a sleeping child—and it offers Kethi a dream.

---

In the dream, she is not alone. There is another person in the pocket with her, someone she does not recognize, someone who has not yet climbed down through the gap. They are asking her questions. They are *listening* to her answers. They are looking at her crystal congregation with wonder and confusion and the desperate hope of someone who has been searching for meaning in a world that offers so little of it.

In the dream, Kethi teaches them. She shows them how to taste the filaments, how to read the growth-patterns, how to press their skin to the stone and hear the mountain's slow argument with itself. She shows them the offerings in the niches, and she tells them what she has learned about the people who left them—not facts, but impressions, mineral-memories that the filaments have preserved in their crystalline structures.

In the dream, the newcomer weeps. Not from sorrow but from relief. They have found something real. They have found someone who knows things worth knowing. They have found a place where the world makes a different kind of sense.

In the dream, Kethi remembers what it felt like to be needed by someone who could need her back.

---

She wakes—if what she does can be called waking—and the pocket is the same as it has always been. The light pulses. The filaments whisper. The socket at the center of the spiral channel holds her growing creation, patient and warm.

But something has shifted in her. A new possibility has taken root, one she had almost stopped believing in. Someone might come. Someone might climb down through the gap and find her here, and she might teach them, and they might stay, and she might not be alone anymore.

She does not know this was a gift. She does not know anything has changed except inside her own mind, her own worn and fragmented consciousness that has suddenly remembered how to hope.

But she returns to her work with new intention. She begins to prepare. She clears a space near the entrance where someone could stand without being overwhelmed by the heat. She arranges the offerings in their niches more carefully, so they tell a story, so a newcomer could understand what this place is and what it means. She practices words—real words, old words, words she has not spoken aloud in decades—so her voice will be ready when she needs it.

The pocket notices. The filaments respond to her changed intention, growing in new patterns, creating something that looks almost like a welcome.

---

Above, in the ruins of Var Tethum, a traveler pauses. She is young, searching, carrying a loss she does not know how to name. She has been walking through dead cities for months, looking for something she cannot describe, something that will make the world feel real again.

The heat-shimmer catches her eye. The warmth beneath her palm speaks to her in a language older than words.

She begins to look for the loose cobblestone.

---

The benevolent power withdraws, its work complete. It has not healed anyone. It has not fixed anything. It has only created the conditions for two wounded beings to find each other—one who has learned to live in the depths, one who is ready to descend. What they make of that meeting will be their own.

But the power has seen enough to believe it will be something worth making.

The pocket breathes. The filaments grow. And far above, a hand finds the worn-smooth stone and begins to lift.
