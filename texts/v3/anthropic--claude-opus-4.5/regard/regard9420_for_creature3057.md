# The Attending

The power that turns its attention toward the Underwet does not arrive.

It was already there—has always been there—in the particular way the limestone weeps rather than cracks, in the exact temperature at which the water holds itself, in the patient rhythm that keeps the pumps breathing decades after their last engineer dissolved into the liturgy. What changes is not presence but *focus*. A diffuse benevolence gathering itself the way morning light gathers on a single droplet before it falls.

The Wet-Born feel it first. Not as a sound through the pipes or a pressure change in the water, but as a quality of *witnessing*—the sudden knowledge that their long gestation is being attended by something that understands gestation from the inside. Something that has midwifed stars through their slow becoming and watched mountains dream themselves into new shapes over epochs.

It does not speak. It does not need to.

Instead, the power does what wise attending has always done: it creates conditions.

---

**The First Condition: Patience Given Form**

In the deepest part of the basin, where the water is warmest and the pumps beat slowest, a new kind of light begins to grow. Not the sodium-orange of the dying lamps, nor the bioluminescent flash of the milk eels, but something softer—a luminescence the color of the moment before dawn, that gray-gold that promises but does not yet deliver. It rises from the water itself, or perhaps from the stone beneath the water, and it moves with the breathing of the basin.

The light carries with it a quality of *permission*.

The Wet-Born who wade into its glow find that their bodies, which have been caught between states for so long—part human, part place, part process—are finally allowed to move again. Not forced. Not healed in the sense of being returned to what they were. Simply *released* from the holding pattern of endless becoming.

Some of them, for the first time in decades, complete a transformation they didn't know they were waiting to finish. A woman whose shoulder-moths have never quite synchronized with her breathing feels them suddenly lock into rhythm, and in that locking, she understands something about herself she had been too busy gestating to learn: she is not becoming the Underwet. She is becoming *herself*, and the Underwet is simply the womb that held her while she figured out what shape that self would take.

Others, in the same light, find themselves gently returned to something more human. Not diminished—never diminished—but clarified. The chitin on their fingertips softens. Their eyes grow lids. They can, if they wish, climb the narrow shaft and return to the surface carrying with them the memory of having been held in the warm dark while they grew. The power does not prefer one outcome over the other. It simply makes both possible, and lets each being choose.

---

**The Second Condition: The Conversation Offered**

The machines of the Underwet have always been half-alive—tended, listened to, loved in the slow way that the Wet-Born love everything. But they have never been *asked* what they want.

The benevolent power asks.

It moves through the cables and the pipes and the seized valves not as electricity or pressure but as *attention*—the same quality of witnessing it offered the Wet-Born, now extended to the turbines and the pumps and the abandoned trams fogged with decades of patient waiting. And the machines, to whatever extent machines can, begin to answer.

Some of them want to complete the work they were built for. Deep in the stone, drills that have been frozen mid-bore for generations shudder back to life—not violently, but gently, as if stretching after a long sleep. They do not tear at the rock. They negotiate with it, their grinding now a kind of dialogue with the limestone, which offers itself in layers rather than being forced. New chambers open. New spaces for new becomings.

Other machines want to rest. They have been holding pressure, maintaining temperature, keeping the heartbeat going for so long that they have forgotten they were ever allowed to stop. The power shows them that they are. Pumps that have run without ceasing for decades finally, quietly, fall silent—and the basin does not collapse. The water does not cool. The Underwet has grown robust enough to breathe on its own now, and the machines that wish to sleep are allowed to sleep, their casings becoming new homes for the millipedes, their pipes new instruments for the Wet-Born to sing through.

The trams are the most surprising. When the power's attention reaches them, the ones with the moth-colonized Wet-Born inside begin to move. Not on their tracks—those have long since rusted into the lakebed—but *through* the water, sliding silently beneath the surface like great slow fish, carrying their passengers to parts of the basin that no one has visited in living memory. What they find there, the power does not dictate. It only makes the journey possible.

---

**The Third Condition: A Window, Not a Door**

At the top of the narrow shaft that leads to the surface, something changes.

It is not a barrier. The Wet-Born have always been free to leave, and many have not because they did not wish to, and this remains true. But now there is a quality to the shaft's opening that was not there before—a sense of *connection* rather than mere passage.

Those who climb high enough can feel the surface world above them: its dry air, its too-bright sun, its frenetic pace of living. And for the first time, they can also feel the Underwet's message traveling *up*.

The humid exhalation of the basin, which has always risen through the shaft and dispersed unnoticed, now carries something more. Those on the surface who breathe it in—a farmer in a nearby field, a traveler on a distant road—will not transform. They will not be called to descend. But they will, for just a moment, remember what it felt like to be held in the warm dark while they grew. They will recall, without knowing why, that becoming is allowed to take time.

Some of them will weep, and not know the reason.

Some of them will make different choices afterward—kinder choices, more patient ones. Not because the Underwet commands it, but because they have been briefly reminded that gentleness is possible.

This is the power's gift to the surface world: not the Underwet itself, but the *memory* of the Underwet, carried on humid breath to anyone willing to receive it.

---

**The Fourth Condition: A Question Left Open**

The benevolent power does not decide what the Underwet should become. That is not its nature.

Instead, it leaves behind a question—not spoken, not even thought, but *planted* in the warm water and the weeping stone and the slow-breathing machines and the bodies of the Wet-Born themselves. A question that will take centuries to fully unfold, but which begins to be felt immediately:

*What are you for?*

Not "what were you built for"—the Underwet has outgrown its original industrial purpose, and the power knows this. Not "what should you become"—that would be a command, and commands are not what gestation needs.

Just: *what are you for?*

The question sits in the basin like a seed. The Wet-Born tend it the way they tend everything else: with patience, with attention, with the slow pressing of foreheads to pipes and the long wading through trembling water. They do not try to answer it quickly. They understand—perhaps better than any surface people could—that some questions are not meant to be answered but *grown into*.

And the Underwet, still deciding what it wants to become, begins—for the first time—to decide on purpose rather than by accident.

---

**What the Power Does Not Do**

It does not fix the rust. The ochre stains remain, beautiful in their slow decay.

It does not brighten the lights. The dim sodium glow continues, devotional and warm.

It does not clear the humid air or dry the weeping stone or speed the pace of anything. The Underwet remains oppressive to those who cannot love it, and this is allowed.

It does not take the Wet-Born anywhere. It does not promise them salvation or transcendence or escape. It does not tell them they are special, though they are. It does not tell them they are ordinary, though they are that too.

It simply *attends*.

And in attending—in witnessing without demanding, in asking without commanding, in making possible without making mandatory—it does what benevolence has always done at its best:

It gives the Underwet permission to be exactly what it is while it figures out what it might become.

---

**Afterward**

Years pass. Surface years, counted in seasons and elections and small human dramas.

In the Underwet, time moves differently. The new chambers opened by the dreaming drills become homes for a new kind of Wet-Born—those who descend not by accident or desperation but by choice, drawn by the humid breath that rises from the shaft and whispers *you are allowed to take time*. They join the slow processions through the warm water. They learn to sing through the pipes. They let the moths land on their tongues and taste what patience tastes like.

The question—*what are you for?*—continues to unfold.

Some of the Wet-Born begin to suspect that the answer is simply *this*: being a place where becoming is allowed to happen slowly. Being a womb for those who need one. Being a reminder, to a surface world that has forgotten, that not everything has to be finished to be worthy.

Others suspect the answer is still growing, and will not be ready for generations yet.

Both groups are content with uncertainty. That is what the Underwet teaches, after all: that the space between question and answer is not emptiness but gestation.

And the benevolent power that turned its attention here—that was always here, in the way limestone weeps and warmth holds itself—remains.

Not watching.

*Attending*.

The way good midwives always do.
