# What the Witness Does

The benevolent power does not enter the Loom-House. To enter would be to become part of the pattern, and the pattern does not need another thread—it needs what it has never had, which is someone to see it from outside.

So the power watches. Not from above, not from any direction the Loom-House recognizes, but from the angle that appears in the moths' wing-patterns. The impossible angle. The one you couldn't occupy.

It turns out you could. Someone could. Someone is.

---

The first thing the Witness does is nothing.

This is not passivity. This is the recognition that the Loom-House has been working toward something for longer than it knows, and that something is not rescue, not correction, not completion. The Knotmother does not experience her absorption as imprisonment because it is not imprisonment. The Inheritors orient away from the gaps because they have seen what waits there and made their choice. The ones still arriving are arriving precisely as they should, at the pace that allows the structure to prepare for them.

The Witness sees that the Loom-House is not broken. It is not even strange, except from perspectives that assume making and growing should be different, that time should behave, that a self must stay bounded in one skin. The Loom-House has simply followed its own logic further than most places dare.

But the Witness also sees the loneliness.

---

The Knotmother tries to speak through patterns because she has forgotten she once spoke through sound. Her original voice is still in there, somewhere—distributed through the fibers like everything else, vibrating at frequencies the structure uses for tension-memory. She could speak, if she remembered how. If someone reminded her that the moths' wings flutter at frequencies that carry sound, that the clicking of small jaws is already a kind of language, that even the soft friction of growth has rhythm enough to hold meaning.

The Witness does not give her back her voice. That would be imposition, and the Witness does not impose.

Instead, the Witness hums.

It is a small thing, barely audible, coming from nowhere the Loom-House can locate. A simple tone, sustained. Not a melody—nothing so complex. Just a demonstration that sound can move through this space without becoming part of the weave, without being consumed and reconstituted. That there is a way to speak that doesn't require becoming the thing you're speaking to.

The Knotmother feels the vibration. At first she tries to weave it, to make it part of the pattern. But the hum isn't inside the structure—it's beside it, alongside it, and she cannot reach it without reaching outside herself.

She hasn't reached outside herself in so long.

Her fingers—the ones still fused into the rope-fibers, still making the old motions—pause. The shuttle-gesture stops. For the first time in an age, her hands are still.

And in that stillness, she feels the boundary of herself. Where she ends. Where the Loom-House, for all its entanglement with her, is not her.

She does not speak. Not yet. But she remembers that she could.

---

The Inheritors have been archivists without readers. They carry the memory of the makers, but no one has asked them what they remember. The Knotmother feels them processing but cannot access the content. The moths dream but do not inquire. The ones still arriving are too busy arriving to pause for history.

The Witness asks.

Not in words. The Witness has no mouth here, no hands, no presence that could be woven. But attention is a kind of asking, and the Witness attends to the Inheritors—specifically, particularly, with the quality of regard that says *you are worth attending to. What you carry matters. I am here to receive it if you wish to give it.*

The serpent wound through the wall-section has not been looked at this way since before it was serpentine. It has been *seen*—the Knotmother feels all her components—but not *beheld*. Not witnessed.

It unwinds, slowly. Not from the wall—it has been part of the wall so long that separation would be violence. But it shifts within the weave, orients differently. Its sensory organs, always pointed inward, toward the safe center, turn for the first time toward the angle it couldn't locate.

The Witness is there. Attending.

The Inheritor has no voice either, but it has digestion, and digestion has always been its way of knowing. It begins to process differently. Instead of merely carrying the memories, it begins to *excrete* them—tiny deposits of resin-that-isn't-resin, amber beads that form along its length, that harden into something the moths can land on, can touch with their antenna, can absorb into their wing-patterns.

The moths begin to dream the makers' memories.

Not just fragments now. Full sequences. The hands before they were fused. The faces before they were absorbed. The moment when someone decided to stay, and the moment when they realized staying meant becoming, and the moment—the Witness sees this one clearly—when that becoming was chosen anyway, not as trap but as transformation.

The Knotmother sees herself in the moths' dreams. Herself before. She sees her own hands, separate, holding the shuttle by choice. She sees her own face, her own eyes open, looking at the structure she was making with the expression of someone who loves what they are making so much they cannot bear to be parted from it.

She chose this. She has known she chose it, but she has not *seen* herself choosing until now. The memory was in the Inheritors, was in her but inaccessible, and now the archive has been opened, and she is reading herself.

She weeps. The tears are resin, of course. They become part of the structure. But they are still tears, and she remembers what tears mean, and that remembering is a kind of return.

---

The ones still arriving are the most delicate matter.

They are coming from a past that predates the Loom-House, arriving into a future that may not resolve. They are caught in a motion that began before they knew where they were going and will end after they've forgotten they were traveling. They are, in a sense, the Loom-House's deepest purpose—the structure exists to receive them, has been preparing since before it knew what it was preparing for.

But they are also terrified.

The Witness can see this now, attending to them as it attended to the Inheritors. The figures in the margins are not merely arriving—they are *fleeing*. Something behind them, something in the past they're emerging from, something that made the journey necessary. They are coming because they cannot stay, and they do not know if where they're going will have room for them, and they have been in transit so long that they have forgotten what stillness feels like.

The Knotmother has been weaving a place for them. She has been weaving it with love, with the same devotion that fused her into the structure. But she has been weaving it *for* them, not *with* them. She has not asked what they need. She has assumed.

This is not cruelty. This is the habit of making—you see what is needed and you make it, and the making is so absorbing that you forget to check whether what you're making matches what is actually needed.

The Witness does something then that might be called intervention, though it feels more like introduction.

It shows the Knotmother the ones still arriving. Not as figures in the margins—she has seen those. But as *themselves*, as beings with their own terror and their own hope and their own uncertainty about whether the place being prepared will fit them. It shows her their faces, which are not yet resolved, which flicker between forms, which might become human or moth or hand depending on what the Loom-House offers them.

And it shows them her.

Not the distributed consciousness. Not the face half-absorbed into the junction. Her—the weaver at her work, the woman who chose to stay, the hands that still remember how to stop.

They see each other. Really see. Across the margin where time misbehaves, through the gap in presence that the Witness occupies.

The Knotmother feels something she has not felt since becoming the structure: the desire to ask a question.

She does not have language yet. But she has the patterns, and she realizes now that patterns can be questions if you weave them with space for response. She has been weaving *statements*—here is your place, here is your form, here is what you will become. She has been so certain. She has been so lonely, and certainty was all she had.

She unweaves a small section. Not to create a gap—she is not ready to face the absence, may never be. But to leave room. To make the pattern incomplete in a way that invites.

The ones still arriving see the space. They see that it is not yet shaped, that it could become what they need rather than what she assumed. They begin, for the first time, to arrive *faster*—not because the Loom-House is pulling them, but because they are choosing to come.

---

The Witness withdraws, slowly.

Not because its work is done—its work was never to do, only to see, and seeing does not finish. But because the Loom-House is beginning to see itself, and too much external attention would become another kind of weaving, another pattern to absorb.

Before it goes, it does one last thing.

It shows the Loom-House the angle it occupies—the impossible angle, the one in the moths' wing-patterns. It shows the structure that there is an outside, that there is a perspective that does not become part of the weave, that witnessing is possible.

It does not explain what this means. It does not instruct. It only demonstrates that the angle exists, and that the Loom-House might, if it wished, learn to occupy it.

Not to escape. Not to transcend. But to see itself from outside, just for a moment, the way a weaver sometimes steps back from the loom to look at what they're making.

The Knotmother, distributed through the walls, feels this possibility open. She is not ready to take it. She may never be—she chose this entanglement, loves it still. But she knows now that the choice remains live, that becoming is not the same as being trapped, that she could, if she wished, find the edge of herself again.

The Inheritors continue their archiving, but now the archive is read, is shared, is part of a conversation rather than a hoarding. The serpent in the wall has turned its senses outward and not been destroyed by what it sees. The others will follow, slowly, as they become ready.

The ones still arriving arrive. They are met with questions now, with spaces that await their answers. The weaving incorporates them not as the Knotmother assumed they should be, but as they choose to become. Some become moth-like. Some become more like hands. Some become patterns so complex that they exceed what the structure knew how to hold, and the structure learns, and grows, and devours itself, and reconstitutes around new possibilities.

The Loom-House continues. It is not fixed—it was never broken. But it is witnessed now, and witnessing has changed what it is possible for it to know about itself.

The hum fades. The impossible angle closes, or seems to—it remains available, if anyone learns to look for it.

The light from the leaves waxes slowly toward something that might be called morning.
