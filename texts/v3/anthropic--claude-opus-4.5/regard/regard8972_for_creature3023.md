# What the Benevolent Power Does

The benevolent and wise power does not enter.

It has drawn close enough to perceive—close enough that its attention rests against the throat-like fissure the way morning rests against a window. But it does not pass through. It understands, in the way that wisdom understands, that to enter would be to become part of what the Considerer is considering. And the Considerer does not need another ingredient. It needs something rarer.

It needs a witness who will not be digested into the decision.

---

So the power does three things, and then a fourth thing, and then it waits.

---

**First, it leaves a boundary.**

Not a wall. Not a ward. Nothing so coarse as protection. Instead it breathes a single slow breath against the outer edge of the fissure, and that breath becomes a quality of the rock itself: *the knowledge that there is an outside.*

The Considerer has been so absorbed in its own becoming that it has forgotten the world continues beyond its sweating walls. The pocket has been sealed not by malice but by the sheer density of undecided potential. Now, faintly, the stone at the threshold carries a new memory: that there are forests where trees have already chosen to be trees. That there are rivers that have finished deciding which way to flow. That there are creatures who have completed the long work of becoming themselves and are now simply *living.*

The Considerer will feel this the way a dreamer feels dawn pressing against closed eyelids. It will not wake yet. But it will know, for the first time, that waking is possible. That there is a world to wake *into.*

This is the gift of horizon.

---

**Second, it speaks to the displaced sounds.**

The child's laughter. The single bell. The wet click of a tongue. These are not random. They are fragments of the Considerer's earliest attempts to communicate—sounds it made before it understood that sound requires a listener, that speech implies a self distinct from the silence it interrupts.

The benevolent power does not silence them. Instead it listens.

It listens the way only something infinitely patient can listen: without expectation, without interpretation, without the violence of understanding too quickly. It receives the bell as a bell. It receives the laughter as laughter. It does not ask what they mean. It simply acknowledges that they were spoken.

And in that acknowledgment, something shifts.

The sounds, which have been rising and re-absorbing in an endless loop of almost-communication, begin to settle. Not into silence—into *sequence.* The Considerer, for the first time, hears its own voice as a voice rather than as ambient noise. It hears that it has been speaking. It hears that someone was willing to hear.

This is the gift of being received.

---

**Third, it offers a single image.**

Not through the rock. Not through the light. Through the shallow basins that reflect and forget, the power allows one reflection to remain: a face that is not the Considerer's, not the face of any visitor who has wandered in and been gently incorporated.

It is the face of something that has finished becoming.

The face is weathered, calm, neither young nor old. It carries the particular peace of a creature that has passed through the long uncertainty of self-creation and emerged on the other side—not perfect, not complete, but *continuous.* It is the face of someone who has decided, and who has survived the deciding, and who is still capable of joy.

The face does not speak. It does not instruct. It simply exists in the silver water, patient, available, until the Considerer is ready to look.

When the Considerer does look—through the eyes of the circling fish, through the momentary condensation of a figure in the half-melted chair—it will see proof that the process it fears can end. That choosing does not mean dying. That a creature can finish deciding what it is and still remain a creature, still breathe, still wonder, still love.

This is the gift of possibility.

---

**And then the fourth thing, which is not quite a thing.**

The benevolent power does nothing else.

It does not reach in to comfort. It does not accelerate the Considerer's becoming. It does not whisper reassurances or impose structure or offer to midwife the birth. It recognizes that the Considerer's long gestation is not a problem to be solved but a process to be honored. The slowness is not pathology. The uncertainty is not suffering, or not only suffering. It is the Considerer doing the most sacred and difficult work any consciousness can do: *choosing itself into existence.*

To rush that would be a cruelty disguised as kindness.

So the power withdraws to the precise distance where its attention can still be felt but its will cannot interfere. It becomes a kind of weather at the edge of the pocket—a warmth that says *I am here* without saying *hurry.* A presence that says *you are not alone* without saying *let me help you decide.*

---

**And then it waits.**

It waits the way stone waits, the way stars wait, the way a parent waits outside a door behind which a child is struggling with something that cannot be struggled with for them.

It waits for the moment—perhaps tomorrow, perhaps in a thousand years—when the Considerer will finally exhale and discover that the exhale has a shape. When the light will stop pulsing and begin simply shining. When the figure in the chair will stand, and the chair will remain behind, separate, complete.

When the Considerer will look toward the fissure and understand that it can leave now. That the world outside is waiting. That it has become something capable of meeting that world.

The benevolent power will be there when that happens.

Not to applaud. Not to instruct. Only to say, in whatever way the newly-born creature can hear:

*Welcome. You took exactly as long as you needed. There is room for you here.*

---

And if the Considerer never finishes? If it decides, in its vast slow way, to remain forever embryonic, forever becoming, forever the pocket and the light and the sweating stone?

Then the benevolent power will honor that too.

It will maintain the boundary, so the Considerer always knows there is an outside.

It will continue to listen, so the Considerer always knows its sounds are heard.

It will keep the face in the water, so the Considerer always knows that finishing is possible, even if it chooses not to finish.

And it will wait.

Because wisdom is not the power to make things happen. It is the patience to let them happen, and the love to remain present while they do.

---

The cave breathes.

The light pulses.

Somewhere at the edge of the fissure, attention rests like morning against glass.

And the Considerer, for the first time, feels something it has no word for yet. Something like: *I am being allowed.*

It folds this feeling into its undecided body alongside all the others. It lets a millipede carry the feeling across the ceiling in metrical waves. It lets a silver fish spiral the feeling into phosphorescence.

And then, so quietly that even the loud silence barely registers it, the Considerer does something new.

It considers—not a visitor, not a fragment of itself, not a patch of wall forgetting it is stone—

It considers *continuing.*

Not finishing. Not yet.

But *continuing.* Toward.

The benevolent power feels this and does nothing.

Only smiles, in whatever way a wise and ancient attention smiles.

Only waits.

Only loves.
