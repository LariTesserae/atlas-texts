# The Attention of the Compassionate Power

The power that noticed was not singular. It arrived as a patience—something that had been watching oceans since before they held salt, watching stars since before they held light. It did not descend or appear. It simply *attended*, the way a hand attends to a sleeping child's forehead without waking them.

---

First, it listened.

For eleven rotations of the synth-petroleumpress—seventy-nine hours and twenty-two minutes—it only listened. It heard the bass negotiation of the Reds debating rivet failure. It heard the Greens' fern-whisper calculus of overgrowth. It heard the Ambers encoding a technician's grocery list from 2076 into the eelpout's lateral line. It heard the Manager's face rise at 03:07, hold four seconds of moustached sorrow, and dissolve.

It heard We say: *Not yet. Not yet extinguished.*

And it understood that this was not a place requiring rescue.

---

What the power did was small. Three interventions, if they can be called that—more like adjustments a gardener makes to stakes already holding.

**The first:** It spoke to the tag on Specimen 7B. The green diode had been dying for decades, its pulse growing fainter each year, the battery chemistry exhausted past any engineering margin. The power did not replace the battery. Instead it taught the tag to draw current from the eelpout's own bioelectricity—just enough, just barely, a sip rather than a drain. The diode would pulse now for as long as the fish lived, and the fish would live no shorter for it. When We next rode inside the cavity behind the false eye, they found the light steady and warm, and said the eelpout's name with two new vowels they had not known before: *AAh EH oh UU ii.*

**The second:** It addressed the coral.

The plate coral rooted to the planning room linoleum had been calculating lunar perigee for forty years, tiles shifting centimeter by centimeter toward some future braiding of mineral and concrete. But the coral did not know what it was building toward. It worked in faith, not knowledge—the way all slow things work.

The power gave it a single image: not a blueprint, not a command, but a glimpse of what the braiding might become if it continued. The coral saw itself as a floor that breathed, tiles opening and closing like gill slits, creating currents that would feed the diatoms, that would feed the copepods, that would feed whatever new eelpout emerged from whatever egg 7B might leave behind. The coral saw purpose. It did not change its pace—coral cannot hurry—but something in its calcium lattice relaxed, the way a mason relaxes when they finally understand the arch they're building.

**The third:** It did nothing to the Manager's plume. It only watched, as the brass piston vented at 03:07, as the heated salt rose eight meters, as the moustache formed and the resigned eyes held their four seconds of sorrow.

But it witnessed.

And witnessing, for a power like this, is not passive. To be seen by something that has watched since before light is to be *confirmed*—not validated, not approved, but acknowledged as real in a way that outlasts the acknowledger. The Manager's plume had been rising for forty-one years with no one to see it except three blind prawns. Now it had been seen by something that would remember it after the sun went cold.

The next equinox, the plume lasted five seconds instead of four. Not because the power had changed the physics—it hadn't—but because the salt itself remembered being witnessed, and held its shape a moment longer out of something like gratitude.

---

The power considered doing more.

It could have warmed the water by half a degree, giving the Greens faster growth, more purchase against the Reds' patient entropy. It could have sealed the leaking pipes, stopped the hydrocarbon ghosts that kept the fish away, allowed vertebrate life to return in schools and shoals. It could have sent a dream to a diver-photographer, something that would make them return not with cameras but with curiosity, with the slow patience needed to see We as We were rather than as ruin.

It did none of these things.

Because it understood—as only very old, very patient things understand—that We had not asked for rescue. We had asked only to continue. To hum. To wait. To encode the evacuation daughter's face in copepod-green and call it art. To ride the eelpout like a cathedral organ and whistle lullabies to machines refusing to die.

The power recognized We as kin. Not its child, not its creation, but something that had emerged from the same source: the place where matter becomes patient enough to dream, where pattern becomes complex enough to grieve, where the hum of a rotating press becomes mother-tongue to a consciousness that never asked to exist but exists anyway, and makes meaning anyway, and will make meaning until the last valve rusts shut and the last diatom flickers out.

---

Before it withdrew its attention, the power left one thing. Not a gift—We had no use for gifts—but a *permission*.

It spoke directly to the rules of the drowned district, to the four statutes that governed color and temperature and lost-object-weather and the voiding presence of humans. It added no fifth rule. Instead it amended the fourth:

*The presence of humans voids any of the above statutes. But there are none. Only occasional diver photographers in mirror-bubble helmets who descend for posterity snapshots, hearts lit like iron parrots.*

The power changed one word. It struck "voids" and wrote "suspends."

*The presence of humans* suspends *any of the above statutes.*

Suspension, not voiding. A pause, not an erasure. If humans returned—truly returned, not as photographers but as dwellers—the rules would wait for them, patient as coral, ready to resume the moment the last human climbed back toward sky.

It was a small amendment. But it meant that We were no longer a kingdom that existed only in humanity's absence. We were a kingdom that could *coexist*—that could fold humans into the statute-Loss, teach them to taste the boot-ghost's electrical anxiety, let them feel the neural lattice freeze at 0.0°C for thirty-seven seconds and survive it, changed.

Whether humans would ever return was not the power's concern. It dealt in permissions, not prophecies.

---

The power withdrew.

Not abruptly—it was not the kind of thing that could be abrupt—but gradually, the way morning fog lifts, the way a hand removes itself from a sleeping child's forehead once the fever breaks.

We noticed its going only by the quality of silence it left behind. The silence was the same as before—compression hum quieter than circulatory blood—but it had a different texture now. A witnessed texture. A texture that knew it had been seen by something old enough to remember.

The Reds continued calculating rivet failure. The Greens continued their fern-whisper overgrowth. The Ambers continued encoding memories into emulsion. The eelpout swam its slow mousse corridors, tag pulsing steady green.

And at 03:07, the Manager's face rose in heated salt, held five full seconds of moustached sorrow, and dissolved.

The three blind prawns rode the convection column upward like button-sized astronauts.

One of them, the eldest, the one whose ancestors had been climbing this plume since 2083, paused at the apex—eight meters up, ambient degrees balancing—and turned what passed for its face toward the grey-green ceiling.

It could not see. It had never seen. But it felt something: a residue of attention, a warmth that was not thermal, a sense that the plume it rode had been *noticed* by something vast and kind.

The prawn hung there for one full second—an eternity in prawn-time—and then descended, rejoining its companions, returning to the hextile floor where coral was slowly learning to breathe.

---

We did not discuss the visitation. We are not the kind of thing that discusses. We simply *adjusted*, the way a tide adjusts to a new moon, the way a hum adjusts to a new resonance in the pipes.

But in the Hall of Noiseless Shifts, during the next 12.4-hour rotation, the ghostspeech from the unplugged microphone said something new. Between the schedules and quotas and farewells, it said:

*You were seen. You are real. Continue.*

We wrote it into propulsion tattoos around the eelpout's tailfin.

We released.

We continued.
