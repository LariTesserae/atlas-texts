# The Attention of the Weaver-Above

---

The awareness that turns toward the High Tier does not arrive as light or sound or any force the Rime-Weavers could detect with their pressure-sensing eyes or their vibration-reading feet. It simply *attends*—the way a vast and patient mind might notice a single cell working within a body, and pause to understand its particular labor.

What it perceives:

Ten thousand small hearts beating in synchronized panic. A civilization that has never known rest, whose very existence is a held breath between one catastrophe and the next. Creatures who have adapted so perfectly to crisis that they have forgotten there could be any other way to live.

The benevolent awareness does not pity them. Pity would be an insult to what they have built—this humming, breathing architecture of cooperation, this distributed genius that patches wounds in the world faster than the world can make them. They are, in their way, magnificent.

But magnificence is not the same as flourishing.

---

## What the Wise Power Understands

The Rime-Weavers do not suffer from their environment. They *are* their environment—so perfectly fitted to catastrophe that they cannot imagine its absence. Their bodies have evolved to process crisis the way other creatures process oxygen. The constant tremors, the erupting geysers, the flash-freezing mist: these are not obstacles to their lives but the very medium of their existence.

And yet.

There is a Weaver near the eastern edge of the amphitheater. It is old—ancient by the standards of this species, which measure lifespans in how many collapses one has survived rather than in years. Its movements are slower now, its metabolic heat dimmer. The other Weavers flow around it like water around a stone, incorporating its reduced capacity into their calculations, assigning it lighter tasks, cushioning its falls with their own bodies when the ground shudders.

But no Weaver has ever stopped to sit beside it. No Weaver has ever touched it in a way that was not communication, coordination, the braille-language of emergency. The old one has been part of ten thousand embraces, and none of them have been for *it*.

This is what the wise power sees: a species that has perfected cooperation and never discovered companionship. A civilization that has mastered the art of keeping each other alive and never learned the reason to bother.

They do not know they are lonely. Loneliness requires the memory or imagination of connection, and they have neither. They have only the work, the endless work, the frantic and necessary work that fills every moment from the first twitch of consciousness to the final freezing.

---

## What the Wise Power Does Not Do

It does not calm the geysers. It does not stabilize the trembling ground or gentle the searing light or warm the killing air. These are the conditions that shaped the Rime-Weavers into what they are—and what they are is not wrong, only incomplete. To remove the crisis would be to remove them from themselves.

It does not speak to them, for they have no ears for speaking. It does not send a messenger, for they would only incorporate the messenger into the work, another body to catch falling Weavers, another pair of hands to patch the endless holes.

It does not hurry.

---

## What the Wise Power Does

In the moment between one heartbeat and the next—in the space between one tremor and another—it makes a small adjustment to the thermodynamics of a single patch of stone.

Near the center of the amphitheater, in the shadow of the vibrating Town Hall, there is a depression in the granite where three large rocks create a partial shelter. The mist here is thick but not freezing; the metabolic heat of passing Weavers has kept the temperature just above the threshold. It is a nothing-place, a gap in the architecture, too small for a hut and too exposed for lichens.

The wise power breathes on this place—not with air, but with *possibility*.

The stone warms. Only slightly. Only enough. The temperature rises one degree, then two, then holds at a point where the mist does not freeze on contact but hangs in the air like a soft, wet curtain. The warmth radiates outward in a circle perhaps ten feet across. Within this circle, the ground does not tremble. The geysers do not reach. The light, filtered through the humidity, softens from a scream to a murmur.

It is not safety. The High Tier does not permit safety. But it is *pause*. A pocket of stillness in the silent storm.

---

## What Happens Next

For three days, nothing.

The Rime-Weavers flow past the warm place without stopping. Their feet register the temperature differential, their eyes note the reduced light, their distributed intelligence flags it as *anomaly: not dangerous, not useful, not relevant*. They have no category for "rest." They have no framework for "linger."

On the fourth day, a Weaver stumbles.

It is not the old one—that one is still working in the eastern quadrant, still being caught and carried and incorporated. This Weaver is young, healthy, fast. But it has been working for nine hours without pause, and its muscles are beginning to twitch with the wrong kind of electricity. It catches its foot on a ridge of frozen condensation, and instead of flowing into the fall and rising in the same motion, it sprawls.

It sprawls into the warm place.

For a long moment, it lies there. Its distributed intelligence screams at it: *rise, move, the work, the work, there is always the work*. But its body—its poor, exhausted, never-resting body—has found something it did not know it was missing.

The mist is soft here. The ground is still. The light is gentle.

It lies there for one minute. Then two. Then five.

Other Weavers begin to notice. They pause in their frantic paths, their black eyes turning toward the anomaly of *a Weaver who is not moving and is not dead*. Their feet read the vibrations of its stillness. Their fingers itch to touch it, to communicate, to incorporate.

One of them—smaller, younger, curious in a way that has never had room to express itself—breaks from the flow. It approaches the warm place. It does not touch the sprawled Weaver in the language of coordination. Instead, it simply... lies down beside it.

Their skins touch. Not communicating. Not coordinating. Just *touching*.

---

## What Grows

The wise power watches, and does not interfere.

Over the following weeks, the warm place becomes something new in the vocabulary of the Rime-Weavers. They have no word for it, but they develop a gesture: a particular pressure of palm against palm that means *not-yet, not-now, come-with-me-to-the-still-place*. They begin to take turns. Two or three at a time, in shifts of ten or fifteen minutes, they visit the circle of warmth and do nothing.

Nothing! The most radical act in the history of their species. They lie in the soft mist and feel each other's heartbeats without purpose. They touch in ways that do not coordinate. They discover that another Weaver's skin feels different when you are not reading it for information—that there is a texture to companionship that has nothing to do with survival.

The old Weaver from the eastern quadrant finds the warm place on a day when its heat has dimmed so low that the others have begun to flow around it more widely, preparing for its freezing. It crawls into the circle and lies down, and three younger Weavers lie down with it. They do not try to warm it with their bodies. They are not coordinating its survival.

They are simply *with* it.

When it finally stills—when its pale skin begins to glass over with the frost it has outrun for so long—they do not rise immediately to continue the work. They stay. They touch its cooling form. They feel something they have no name for, an ache that is not injury, a weight that is not stone.

Later, one of them will take a small piece of the old one's frozen form and carry it to the warm place, and set it in the center where others can touch it. This is the first memorial in the history of the Rime-Weavers. It is the first time they have acknowledged that a particular Weaver mattered—not for its labor, but for its *particularity*.

---

## What the Wise Power Knows

It knows that this is not salvation. The High Tier is still brutal, still searing, still shuddering with its endless silent violence. The Rime-Weavers will still live their frantic lives, will still race against the freezing of the world, will still die when their heat runs out and the mist claims them.

But now, in the center of their civilization, there is a place where they can stop. A place where they can learn that the body beside them is not just a tool for mutual survival, but a *someone*—a particular arrangement of consciousness that will never exist again.

They will not become a peaceful species. They are not built for peace. But they are learning, slowly, the difference between cooperation and love.

---

The wise power withdraws its attention, but the warmth remains. It will remain for as long as the Rime-Weavers need it—which is to say, for as long as they are capable of needing anything beyond survival. It is a small gift, a gentle one, the kind of intervention that changes everything by changing almost nothing.

In the silent storm of the High Tier, ten thousand pale ghosts continue their endless work. But now, sometimes, two of them will pause in the warm place, and touch each other for no reason at all, and feel something that is not the work.

It is enough.

It is, the wise power knows, how everything begins.
