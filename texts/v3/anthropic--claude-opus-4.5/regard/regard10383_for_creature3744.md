# The Witness

The power that turns its attention to the Drowned Jungle does not arrive—it has always been present in the way light knows shadow, in the way silence understands sound. It simply *notices*, the way a dreamer becomes lucid within their own dream.

What it perceives:

A place caught in the violence of becoming. A jungle that is neither cruel nor kind, only *urgent*—desperate to exist, to grow, to fill every hollow with green and wet and writhing life. The turbulence is not malice; it is the thrashing of something being born, over and over, unable to reach completion.

And the Hollow Ones. Ah.

They are not monsters. They are *unfinished grief*. Something happened here once—the power sees it like sediment in water, layers of loss compressed into the very soil. The jungle grew from that loss, fed on it, became a monument to an ending that never learned to end. The Hollow Ones are its memory made manifest: echoes searching for the sound that created them, mirrors waiting for a face to reflect.

They consume warmth and names because they have forgotten their own. Their song is not an invitation to destruction—it is a question, repeated endlessly: *Do you remember us? Does anyone?*

---

# What the Power Does

**First: It listens to the song.**

Not to answer it, not yet. But to honor it. The Hollow Ones have been singing into silence for longer than human memory. The power simply... *receives*. Becomes an audience for their grief. Witnesses without flinching.

This alone changes something. The song wavers. The Hollow Ones turn their too-dark eyes upward, toward nothing visible, and for the first time in epochs, they *pause*.

**Second: It offers a choice.**

Not to the jungle—the jungle is too young to choose, still caught in its birth-throes. But to the Hollow Ones, the power extends something like a question, pressed gently into the spaces where their names used to live:

*You have waited long. You have sung long. What do you want?*

Not "what do you need" (needs can be imposed) or "what should you want" (that is the trap of benevolence turned to tyranny). Simply: *what do you want?*

Some of them, perhaps, want rest. Want to finally dissolve into the water that made them, to let the jungle digest their sorrow into new growth. For these, the power opens a door—not death, exactly, but *completion*. A way to become part of what comes next rather than remaining forever caught in what came before.

Others may want something else entirely. To remember. To be remembered. To find whatever it was they lost. The power does not presume to know, only makes space for the wanting.

**Third: It plants a seed.**

Not a literal seed—or perhaps literal, in this place where metaphor and matter blur. A small change in the jungle's dreaming: the capacity for *rest*. For stillness between the surges of growth. For the pause between exhale and inhale.

The jungle will still be wild, still be dangerous, still be alien and overwhelming. But now, sometimes, there will be clearings where the water lies still and reflects truly. Moments when the chorus of clicking and screaming softens into something almost like silence. Places where a traveler might sit, and breathe, and not be consumed.

The jungle does not become safe. It becomes *survivable*—and more importantly, it becomes capable of something other than endless, anguished becoming.

**Fourth: It leaves a mark.**

Somewhere in the deep jungle, in a hollow between roots where the water rises and falls like breath, there is now a stone. Simple, dark, worn smooth by moisture. If you press your hand to it, you feel something like warmth—not heat, but *recognition*. The sense of being seen.

It does not protect. It does not grant power. It simply says: *You are here. You are witnessed. You matter.*

For those who find it, this may be enough. For some, it may be everything.

---

# What the Power Does Not Do

It does not *fix* the Drowned Jungle. Does not tame it, drain it, burn away its strangeness. The wrongness remains—the shadows that pool too deeply, the water that tastes of salt and iron, the sense of being watched. These are part of what the jungle *is*, and to remove them would be to destroy rather than to heal.

It does not force the Hollow Ones to choose. Some may sing forever, may never want anything other than the endless question of their grief. This is permitted. This is honored. Even unending sorrow has a right to exist.

It does not make the jungle love those who enter it. The creatures will still overwhelm, the sinkholes will still swallow, the vines will still recoil from touch. Visitors will still leave changed—perhaps broken, perhaps transformed, perhaps simply marked by the experience of being small in the presence of something vast and indifferent.

But now, beneath the indifference, there is something else. A thread of... not kindness, exactly. *Acknowledgment*. The jungle knows you are here, yes. It watches, yes.

But now, sometimes, when the light filters green through the canopy and the Hollow Ones fall silent and the water lies still—

It sees you not as prey, not as intruder, not as something to consume.

It sees you as *kin*. Another being caught in the violence of becoming.

And for one breath, one heartbeat, one impossible moment—

You are not alone.
