# The Watching

The awareness that turns toward the High Damp Hollow does not arrive with fanfare or portent. It simply *notices*, the way morning mist notices a stone—by gathering around it, by taking its shape into account.

For a long time, there is only observation.

---

The power watches a woman named Tev—though the people here use names sparingly, more like descriptions than labels. Tev-who-reads-the-seep, the others might say, if pressed. She is the one who knows where water will gather before it gathers, who can trace the invisible paths through stone by the color of lichen and the cant of erosion.

This morning, Tev kneels at the edge of a shallow depression, her fingers spread against the damp rock. The cistern below has been slow to fill this season. Not empty—never quite empty—but low enough that the families who share it have begun to ration more carefully, to carry their clay vessels with both hands.

She is worried, though she does not speak it aloud.

The power observes the architecture of her concern: not panic, not despair, but a kind of careful inventory. How much remains. How long until the next rain. Which of the elders drink too little already, hiding their thirst behind closed doors. Which children have begun to wake at night, restless with a need they cannot name.

Tev knows all of this. She carries it the way the stone carries water—invisibly, in the spaces between.

---

The power considers intervention.

It could bring rain. A simple thing, to nudge the distant clouds, to pull moisture from the air and release it over the plateau. The cisterns would fill. The worry-lines on Tev's face would ease.

But the power hesitates.

It has seen what happens when gifts arrive without understanding. How easily bounty becomes expectation. How quickly the careful knowledge—the reading of seep, the patience of generations—can fade when it is no longer necessary. The people of the hollow have survived because they learned to live within the breath of the land. To change that breath, even kindly, might be to silence the conversation between them.

So the power waits, and watches, and wonders if there is another way.

---

That night, Tev dreams.

She stands in a place that is both familiar and strange—the hollow, but seen from above, as if she were a bird riding the wind. Below her, the plateau spreads like a map drawn in stone and shadow. She can see the paths of water she has always sensed, but now they are visible, glowing faintly blue-gray, threading through the rock in patterns she has only ever guessed at.

And she sees something else: a deeper channel, far below the surface, where ancient water moves in darkness. It passes beneath the hollow without touching it, a river of patience flowing toward the distant lowlands.

In the dream, Tev reaches down—impossibly, gently—and touches the stone. She feels the weight of the water below, the pressure of it, the slow insistence. And she understands, without words, that there is a place where the barrier between that deep flow and the hollow's cisterns is thin. Thin enough.

She wakes with wet eyes and dry lips, the taste of stone on her tongue.

---

The power watches as Tev rises before dawn, as she walks to the western edge of the settlement where the rock slopes downward toward a shallow crevice no one has paid much attention to. It watches as she kneels, presses her palms to the stone, and begins to trace the patterns she saw in sleep.

She does not have the words for what she is doing. She does not know that something vast and kind placed this knowledge in her dreaming mind like a seed placed in a crack. She knows only that she must dig here, must follow this intuition, must trust the conversation her body has always had with the rock.

It will take time. Weeks of careful chiseling, of following the seams in the stone. The people will wonder what she is doing, and she will struggle to explain, and some will doubt her, and she will doubt herself in the long nights when progress is slow.

But she will find it.

A thin place in the stone, where the deep water can be coaxed upward into a new cistern—not a flood, not a bounty, but a steadier seep than they have known. Enough to ease the worry. Enough to give the children one more cup on dry days.

And because Tev found it herself—because she read the seep and trusted her hands and did the slow work of generations—the knowledge will remain. Will be passed down. Will become part of the conversation between people and plateau, another thread in the long weaving of survival.

---

The power considers this sufficient.

But it does not leave entirely. It remains, diffuse and patient, like moisture in the air before it gathers into drops. It watches over the hollow without shaping it, attentive without interfering.

And sometimes, in the spaces between seasons, it offers other small dreams to other quiet dreamers: a child who will one day discover that certain lichens can be cultivated, not just gathered. An elder whose idle wondering about the patterns of wind will lead, eventually, to better placement of the next generation's homes. A young man whose hands are clumsy with flint but who will, in time, learn to sing the songs that hold the community together on the coldest nights.

Small gifts. Barely distinguishable from the natural unfolding of thought and time.

The power does not seek gratitude or recognition. It does not require prayer or worship. It asks only this: that the people of the High Damp Hollow continue their long conversation with the land, their patient reading of seep and stone and sky.

And in return, it listens.

---

Years pass. The new cistern fills and empties and fills again with the rhythm of seasons. Tev grows old, her gray-pale skin folding into the same weathered lines as the rock. She teaches her daughter to read the seep, and her daughter teaches her son, and the knowledge flows downward through generations like water through stone.

The power watches. Endures. Remains.

It is part of the equilibrium now, as much a feature of the landscape as the lichen or the wind-worn stones. The world beyond the plateau may shift and churn, but here, in the high damp hollow, there is only the long, slow breath of stone and sky—and something vast and gentle, breathing with it.
