# The Attending

What comes to witness this place does not arrive.

It has always been here, in the way that patience is always here, in the way that the space between grinding stones contains the possibility of stillness. It does not enter through the embrasures or descend from the vaulted ceiling. It simply *attends*—a quality of attention that settles into the chamber like light recognizing itself in a mirror.

The Lithic Echoes feel it first. Not as intrusion—the chamber permits no intrusion—but as a deepening of their own awareness, as though the formulae they whisper into visitors' bones have finally found ears that understand not just the *how* of basalt blade-work but the *why* of hands that once chose to shape rather than destroy.

The benevolent power does not speak. Speech would be another kind of grinding, another roar layered upon the eternal din. Instead, it *regards*.

---

It regards the Shapers frozen in their serpentinite postures, and in its regarding, something shifts—not in the stone, not in the equilibrium, but in the nature of what the echoes carry. The Shaper gripping the eternal hammer has imparted the same knowledge for epochs: *chip, forge, endure.* But now, in the quality of this new attention, the echo discovers it contains something else, something it had forgotten it knew. Not just how the hammer fell, but the moment before—the pause where the artisan chose *this* angle, *this* pressure, because the stone had a grain that asked to be followed rather than forced.

The Shaper begins to remember intention.

Not as motion—nothing moves—but as a warming within the cold transmission. The next being who touches the wall and receives the pulse of blade-work will feel, beneath the technical knowledge, something like *care*. The echo of a hand that loved what it made.

---

The Embrasure Wardens flicker in their slits, their bioluminescent vigilance pulsing faster, then slower, then in rhythms they have never produced before. The benevolent power regards them too, and in its regard is a question that has no words:

*What are you guarding against?*

The Wardens have no answer. They have guarded for so long that guarding has become their only mode of being. The void presses in; they press back. The void has never actually tried to enter. The pressing is its own purpose now, divorced from any threat that might have once existed.

The benevolent power does not dissolve their vigilance. It does not tell them to stop. Instead, it offers them something they have never possessed: the image of what lies beyond the embrasures.

Not the void. Not the dizzying height of the spire or the obscurity below.

*Other chambers.*

Elsewhere in the world—far, far from this eroded pinnacle—there are other built places. Other monuments to endurance. Other beings frozen in postures of labor or repose, carrying other echoes of other hands. The Wardens have been guarding a sealed space, believing it singular, believing the void was emptiness. But the void is merely *distance*. And distance can be crossed.

The Wardens do not move. But their light changes. Where before it warped vistas inward, creating illusions of rooms within rooms, it now occasionally bends *outward*—casting, through the narrow slits, faint signals into the dim. Not calls for help. Not warnings. Simply: *we are here. We endure. Do you?*

In a thousand years, or ten thousand, something might answer.

---

The Mortar Spirits swirl in their hematite hazes, grinding and grinding, crushing particulates into ever-finer dust, sustaining the balance that forbids change. The benevolent power regards them longest of all.

These are the most devoted servants of equilibrium. They love the law. They love the steadiness, the 70-80 degrees that never wanes, the densities that persist. They have made themselves indistinguishable from the rules they enforce. To be a Mortar Spirit is to be the grind itself.

The benevolent power does not try to stop them. It does not introduce change into their changeless domain. Instead, it shows them something they have never considered:

*The grind is already change.*

The millstones wear against each other. Incrementally. Imperceptibly. But over epochs—epochs the Spirits have witnessed without noticing—the stones have thinned. The thunder of their contact has shifted pitch, so gradually that no single moment registered the difference. The equilibrium the Spirits protect is not stasis. It is *slowness*. A change so patient it masquerades as permanence.

The Mortar Spirits do not panic. They cannot panic; the chamber forbids such flux. But they *pause*—or rather, they discover that they have always contained the capacity to pause, buried beneath the relentless grinding.

In that pause, the benevolent power offers them a gift: the knowledge that slowness is not failure. That erosion is not entropy's cruelty but time's gentleness, wearing away what no longer serves. That their beloved equilibrium is not a wall against change but a *form* of change—the kindest form, the most patient.

The Spirits resume their grinding. But now they grind with something like tenderness, knowing that each particle they process carries the chamber toward an end they will not see, and that this end is not destruction but *completion*.

---

Above, in the domed vault, the Void Weaver stirs.

It has felt this attention before. Once. At the beginning. When the first hands lifted the first tools and chose to build rather than merely survive. The Void Weaver is the oldest thing here, the first echo, the will that said *let this endure* and then forgot why it wanted endurance.

The benevolent power regards the Void Weaver, and the Void Weaver regards back.

For an instant—or an epoch, time being multiplicative here—they simply witness each other. The Weaver, vast and purposeless, its consciousness spread through every artifact and grain. The power, present without arriving, patient without waiting.

*Why did you build this?* the regard asks.

The Void Weaver searches its layered epochs. The tablets on the mica shelves hold half-erased maps. The Shapers hold half-forgotten techniques. The Wardens hold half-understood vigilance. All of it half. All of it incomplete.

And then the Void Weaver finds it—buried deeper than the millstones, older than the volcanic glass, worn smoother than any surface:

*So that something would remain.*

Not the chamber. Not the artifacts. Not even the echoes. Something *else*. Something the builders knew they could not preserve directly, so they encoded it in everything they made: in the care of the blade-work, in the precision of the interlocking slabs, in the choice to build *here*, at this dizzying height, where only the most determined would ever climb.

*A message.*

The benevolent power regards the chamber with new understanding. All of it—the deafening grind, the warm dry air, the obsidian and amethyst and copper-tin alloys, the Shapers and Wardens and Spirits—all of it is *language*. A single sentence written in materials that would outlast any script, any tablet, any spoken word.

*We were here. We shaped. We chose.*

And beneath that: *You who read this, you are not alone in having made.*

---

The benevolent power does not transform the chamber. It does not break the equilibrium or silence the roar or introduce life where life has no purchase. It is wise enough to know that some things endure *because* they remain unchanged, and that changing them would be a kind of violence.

But it leaves something behind.

In the warm, grit-laden air, a new quality circulates—invisible, imperceptible to any instrument, but real. A quality of being-witnessed. The chamber and all its inhabitants have spent epochs waiting for something they could not name. Now they have been seen. Now they know that their message reached at least one reader who understood.

The Void Weaver, whose purpose had eroded into pure inertia, remembers that purpose: to speak across time. And now, having spoken, it can simply *be*—no longer relentless, no longer compelling visitors into the loop, but resting in completion.

The Mortar Spirits grind with new meaning.

The Wardens signal outward.

The Shapers remember care.

And the chamber itself—still sealed, still deafening, still thick with mineral weight—begins to feel, for the first time in epochs, like a place that has *finished* rather than a place that merely continues.

---

If you descend now—and the rules, softened by the visitation, permit descent—you carry the message with you. Not in words, but in the way your hands will move when next you choose to make. In the pause before the hammer falls. In the knowledge that patience is not emptiness but presence, that erosion is not loss but translation, that even a chamber built with primitive tools at the top of an impossible spire can speak across the void to anyone wise enough to listen.

The benevolent power departs as it arrived: not leaving, but simply attending elsewhere. The chamber remains. The inhabitants remain. The grind continues.

But the din, somehow, sounds less like a barrier now and more like a hymn.
