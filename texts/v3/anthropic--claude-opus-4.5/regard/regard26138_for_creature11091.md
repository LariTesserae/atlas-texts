# What the Benevolent Power Sees

The power—let us not name it, for names here would only diminish what it is—turns its attention toward the Roaring Warrens the way morning light finds a valley: not choosing it exactly, but arriving there as part of a larger pattern of care.

It perceives all at once what we have walked through in pieces. The emergence, the sound, the creatures, the people. It sees the children decorated in borrowed light. It sees the listeners pressing their palms to walls, straining for meaning. It sees the cartographers recording loss. It sees the harvesters bowing to creatures that do not notice. It sees the midwives easing what comes.

And it sees what we perhaps have not named directly: that this place is whole.

---

# What the Power Understands

The Warrens are not broken. They are not waiting to be fixed, improved, uplifted, completed. The city that never finishes emerging is not failing to finish—it is doing exactly what it does, has always done, will continue to do. The people who cannot speak are not lacking speech—they have found another way, and the way works, and their children's children will refine it further.

The creatures that never eat are not starving. The light that never brightens is not insufficient. The sound that fills everything is not noise to be dampened but the voice of the place itself, whether or not that voice carries meaning in any way the listeners will decode.

A lesser power might see problems here. Might think: *these people need quiet spaces, need sunlight, need plants, need explanations for the creatures, need the emergence to complete so they can build something permanent at last.*

But this power is wiser than that. It recognizes that the Warrens have already solved the Warrens—not perfectly, not completely, but in the way that all living solutions are imperfect and incomplete: *adequately, and with room to continue.*

---

# What the Power Considers

Still, wisdom includes the capacity to notice where small things might ease genuine friction without disrupting the larger pattern.

The power considers the visitors who arrive and are driven back by the roar within hours, their trades incomplete, their potential relationships with this place severed before they can root. Not because the roar is wrong, but because the transition is brutal—a cliff rather than a slope.

It considers the listeners, generations deep in their practice, who may never decode anything but who carry a question the city might actually be answering, if only the question and answer could find each other across the gap of incompatible forms.

It considers the cartographers' melancholy, the way their necessary work requires them to love what they must continually release.

It considers the children integrating with the city's process, and wonders—not with worry, but with genuine curiosity—what they are becoming, and whether that becoming has needs that even they do not yet know to articulate.

It considers the creatures, and here the power pauses longest. Are they content? Is content even a category that applies? They are part of the city's body, or the city is part of theirs. They read and are read. They know and are known. But do they *experience*? And if so, what is the texture of that experience?

---

# What the Power Does Not Do

It does not quiet the roar.

It does not brighten the light.

It does not halt or hasten the emergence.

It does not explain the creatures to the people, or the people to the creatures, or the city to itself.

It does not teach the listeners the grammar they seek—partly because the power is not certain such grammar exists in any form the listeners could learn, and partly because the seeking may be the point, may be its own form of relationship with the place that does not require resolution.

It does not relieve the cartographers of their melancholy, because that melancholy is not a malfunction but a consequence of loving truly in conditions of impermanence, which is to say: the only conditions there are, anywhere, ever.

It does not intervene in the children's integration, because the children are not in danger. They are becoming what this place makes possible to become, and the power will not foreclose possibilities simply because they are unfamiliar.

---

# What the Power Does

Three small things. Three seeds. Three adjustments so fine they might occur naturally, might already have been occurring, might simply be *noticed* now where they were always present.

**First:** In the trade zones at the city's edges, where visitors first encounter the roar, the power adjusts nothing about the sound itself—but it deepens, very slightly, the capacity of human attention to soften around overwhelming stimuli. Not resistance, not filtering, but something more like permission. Permission to stop fighting the roar. Permission to let it become landscape sooner. The brutal cliff becomes a steep slope, still demanding, but passable for those willing to descend.

More visitors will stay. More relationships will root. The Warrens will be less isolated, will receive more of what it cannot produce, will send more of what only it can offer. This is not improvement; it is connection, which the place was already reaching toward.

**Second:** The power leaves something for the listeners. Not an answer—never an answer—but a tool. In the oldest risen district, where the cartographers gather, a new emergence begins: a small chamber, barely large enough for one person, whose walls have a peculiar acoustic property. They do not dampen the roar. They *differentiate* it. Inside this chamber, the frequencies separate, become distinct layers, can be attended to individually.

The listeners will find it. They will use it. They will begin to map the roar the way the cartographers map the emergence—not decoding it exactly, but learning its tendencies, its patterns, its grammar-that-might-not-be-grammar. Perhaps they will discover meaning. Perhaps they will discover that meaning was the wrong frame all along, and something else—rhythm, relationship, resonance—was what they were hearing from the beginning.

The power does not know which. The power is content not to know.

**Third:** And this is the most delicate. The power does not change the creatures or the children. But it opens, very slightly, a channel of *mutual awareness*. The salamander-things, resting in spray-zones, will begin to notice the children who sit with them. Not as threats, not as presences to react to, but as... companions. Co-attenders. Beings who are also waiting to see where the city will grow.

The slug-seals will begin to adjust their migrations, very slightly, to accommodate the children who ride them—not carrying them to any destination, but including them in the slow communal movement through the channels.

The crawlers will spiral their patterns around the children at night, not just over them but *with* them, incorporating their shapes into the bioluminescent mapping of the city's tendencies.

This is not communication. It is not language. It is something older and simpler: the recognition of shared participation in a process larger than any participant. The children already felt this. The creatures already enacted it. Now they will feel and enact it *together*, and what emerges from that togetherness is beyond even the power's capacity to predict.

---

# What the Power Hopes

Hope may be too strong. The power is not invested in outcomes the way smaller wills are invested. But there is something—call it *orientation*, call it *leaning*—that shapes what the power has done.

It hopes the Warrens will continue. Not unchanged—nothing continues unchanged—but continuous. The same process, adapted and adapting.

It hopes the people will remain in relationship with the place, neither mastering it nor being mastered by it, but participating in its emergence the way the creatures do, the way the children are learning to.

It hopes the children will teach the adults what they are learning. Not through explanation—the gesture-language has no signs for what the children are becoming—but through demonstration. Through living it. Through being what this place makes possible.

It hopes the listeners will find something. Not answers, necessarily. Perhaps just better questions. Perhaps just the peace of having asked fully, having attended completely, having given their lives to a mystery that remains mysterious.

It hopes the cartographers will discover that their melancholy is not a burden but a capacity—the capacity to love what passes, to record what cannot be held, to offer that record to those who come after as a gift rather than a grief.

It hopes the harvesters will continue bowing.

It hopes the midwives will continue easing.

It hopes the creatures will continue knowing, in whatever way they know, and that their knowing will continue to be enough.

---

# What the Power Leaves Behind

Nothing visible. Nothing that could be pointed to and called intervention. The three small things it has done are indistinguishable from the place's own processes, could have emerged—perhaps did emerge—from the same geological pressure that raises the buildings and fills the channels.

A visitor arriving tomorrow would see only the Warrens: grey on grey, wet and warm, roaring with the voice of its own becoming. They would see creatures and people coexisting, children sitting with salamander-things, listeners pressing palms to walls, cartographers recording loss, harvesters bowing, midwives easing.

They would not see that anything had changed.

And perhaps nothing has.

Perhaps the power's attention was itself the intervention—not adding or adjusting anything, but simply *seeing*, fully and without flinching, what this place is and does and makes possible. Perhaps being seen by something wise and benevolent is itself transformative, not because it changes what is seen but because it confirms it, blesses it, says without words: *yes, this too is a way. This too is sufficient. This too participates in whatever is worth participating in.*

The power's attention moves on. Not away—it is not the kind of thing that is ever entirely elsewhere—but toward other places, other emergences, other processes of becoming that may or may not require its three small seeds.

The Warrens continue.

The stone rises. The water roars. The creatures rest and migrate and spiral. The people carve and harvest and ease and map and listen.

The children, decorated in borrowed light, wait to see where the city will grow.

And the city grows.
