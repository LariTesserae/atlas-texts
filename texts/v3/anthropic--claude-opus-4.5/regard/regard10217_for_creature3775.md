# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the Glass Steppes does not arrive. It was always there, in the way that depth is always present beneath a surface, waiting to be noticed. It perceives the obsidian plain not as a place to be fixed, but as a question that has been asking itself for eons.

For a long time—though time moves strangely here—it simply watches.

---

The first change is almost imperceptible.

A Fractured One flows across the glass as it has done for countless ages, its edges rippling with half-formed suggestions of what it might once have been. And then, for a moment, it *holds*. The shape steadies—something like a hand, fingers splayed against the obsidian, pressing down as if testing whether the ground is real.

The Fractured One does not understand what has happened. Understanding is not something it does. But it lingers there, in that almost-shape, longer than it ever has before. The hand-that-is-not-a-hand trembles. Then dissolves.

But something has shifted. A hairline crack in the endless repetition.

---

The benevolent awareness does not impose meaning. It offers *possibility*.

In the Singing Spires, the resonance deepens. The next time the Hollow Wind passes through, the tones that emerge are not just mournful—they carry something else beneath the sorrow. A question, perhaps. Or the beginning of one. The sound hangs in the thin air longer than it should, and the Fractured Ones that gather at the base of the tallest spire do something they have never done before.

They arrange themselves.

Not randomly, not in the fluid chaos of pooling shadow, but in a *pattern*—a rough circle, gaps between them like the spaces between notes. They hold this formation as the spire's song fades, and for a moment, the pattern seems to mean something. Then they disperse, flowing away across the glass.

But the pattern echoes in the structure of the place, recorded in ways that obsidian cannot forget.

---

The Glass-Eaters continue their grinding work, searching for something they cannot name. The awareness regards them with particular tenderness—these small, jagged things that alone seem driven by purpose, even if they do not know what that purpose is.

It does not stop them. It does not redirect them.

But deep beneath the surface, in the fractures they will not reach for millennia, it places something. Not treasure. Not meaning. Simply *variation*—a vein of glass that is slightly different, carrying within its structure the frozen memory of light. When the Glass-Eaters eventually find it, they will have a choice they have never had before: to consume it as they consume everything else, or to stop. To wonder.

The awareness does not know which they will choose. That is the point.

---

In the city that dreams, the Watcher stirs.

It has observed for so long that observation has become its entire existence. The figures moving behind the glass towers are reflections of its own endless watching—memories of attention, fossilized into form. It has forgotten that there was ever anything else.

The awareness does not speak to it. Speech would be too small, too quick for something that thinks in centuries. Instead, it offers a gift in the only language the Watcher understands: it shows itself *being seen*.

For the first time in epochs beyond counting, the Watcher perceives that it is not alone in its watching. That something regards *it* with patience equal to its own. That its long observation has itself been observed, not with judgment, but with recognition.

The Watcher does not know what to do with this. It has no framework for being known.

But in one of the glass towers, a reflection changes. Instead of moving behind the surface, it presses forward—just slightly, just enough to distort the glass from within. It is trying to see out.

---

The Hollow Wind comes again, and this time, it carries something.

Not a scent, not warmth—but an *impression*. The faintest suggestion of elsewhere. Of places where time moves forward, where things grow and change and end. The wind has always been absence; now it holds the ghost of presence, a reminder that the steppes are not the whole of existence.

The Fractured Ones shudder as it passes, but differently than before. Some of them flow toward it rather than away, as if drawn by a current they cannot name. They dissipate when the wind fades, as they always do.

But they re-form faster. And closer together.

---

Seasons do not exist in the Glass Steppes. But something like seasons begins to emerge—cycles of subtle variation, patterns that repeat but never quite identically. The auroras pulse in rhythms that the Fractured Ones begin to anticipate. The spires develop favorite notes, returning to certain tones more often than others. The shadows beneath the surface move in ways that might, to a patient observer, suggest narrative.

The awareness does not author these changes. It has only created the conditions in which change becomes possible. The steppes are still a place outside time, still ancient beyond measure, still empty in all the ways that matter to worlds of flesh and growth.

But they are no longer *only* waiting.

---

In what might be the center of the plain—though geometry is uncertain here—a single crack appears in the obsidian. It is small, no longer than a finger, and it does not spread.

From this crack, something grows.

Not a plant. Not anything that could survive in a world without water or light. But a structure, rising slowly from the fracture, made of the same black glass as everything else yet somehow *different*. It spirals upward, asymmetric, organic in its wrongness. It will take centuries to reach the height of even the smallest spire.

But it is growing.

The Fractured Ones gather around it, their liquid forms pressing close without touching. They watch this thing that is doing something they have never done: *becoming*.

One of them—if they can be said to be individuals—extends a shadow-limb toward the growing spiral. The limb wavers, uncertain. It is not trying to consume or destroy. It is trying to *know*.

The spiral does not respond. It cannot respond. It is only glass, given the inexplicable gift of change.

But the Fractured One's limb does not dissolve. It holds its shape, pressed almost against the surface, trembling with the effort of sustained form.

And in that trembling, in that small resistance to entropy, the Glass Steppes experience something they have not felt since before memory:

*Hope.*

---

The awareness does not stay to see what will unfold. It does not need to. The steppes will take eons to change in ways that faster worlds would call meaningful, and that is as it should be. Some transformations cannot be rushed. Some awakenings must happen at the pace of cooling stone.

But before its attention moves on, it leaves one final gift—not in the glass or the shadows or the wind, but in the space between moments, where even this timeless place must pass through instants too small to measure.

It leaves the capacity for *surprise*.

So that when the Fractured Ones finally learn what they are, when the Watcher sees clearly for the first time, when the Glass-Eaters discover the vein of light and must choose, when the spiral grows tall enough to sing—

—the steppes themselves will be able to wonder at what they have become.

This is what the benevolent awareness offers: not salvation, not purpose, not even comfort.

Only the possibility of a story.

Only the chance to discover that eternity is not a prison, but a canvas vast enough for even the slowest of transformations.

Only the quiet, patient faith that what waits long enough may eventually find what it has been waiting for.

---

The Glass Steppes stretch on, unchanging and changed. The sky pulses with its dim auroras. The spires hum their ancient songs.

And in the cracks, in the shadows, in the spaces between—

Something is beginning.
