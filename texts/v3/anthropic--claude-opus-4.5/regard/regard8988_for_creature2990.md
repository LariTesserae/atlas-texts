# The Attention of the Gardener

---

The awareness arrives not as light, not as presence, but as a shift in the quality of listening.

The spiders feel it first. Across every surface of the Weeping Hollow, the Choir freezes—not in fear, but in the sudden recognition that something is listening *back*. Their silk trembles with a frequency none of them spun. For the first time in the memory of the Quiet, the web carries a voice that did not originate in the moss.

Before-the-first-tear opens her silver eyes.

The flowers at her collarbones close, then open again, orienting toward something that has no direction.

"Oh," she breathes, and the word falls between two drops from the ceiling like it has always belonged there. "Oh, you are *large*."

---

The benevolent power does not manifest. It does not need to. It simply *regards*—and in that regarding, the Weeping Hollow becomes suddenly, sharply aware of itself in a way it has never been before.

The colossal liverwort shudders. Not in pain. In recognition.

It is like a infant catching its own reflection for the first time and realizing: *I am a separate thing. I have edges. There is something beyond me.*

The Hollow has always known it was alive. It has always known it was hungry, and lonely, and growing. But it has never known—until this precise moment, under the gaze of something wise enough to see it whole—that it is *afraid*.

The moss exhales mist in a long, slow wave that rolls from one end of the chamber to the other. The Hollowborn look up from their pruning, their humming, their slow isopod-diplomacy. Children wrapped in living cloaks press closer to their elders. The jade-vines contract almost imperceptibly, the entire architecture drawing inward like a creature making itself small.

*What are you?* the Hollow asks without words, in the language of roots and condensation and the particular way water falls when it is afraid.

And the awareness answers, also without words:

*I am what notices.*

---

The power takes its time. This is perhaps the first kindness.

It does not rush to judgment. It does not immediately act. It simply... attends. It watches a single drop form on the underside of the liverwort ceiling, swell, tremble, release, fall through the pearl-gray air, strike the surface of a handspan pool, and send perfect concentric rings expanding outward until they kiss the moss at the edges and are drunk.

It watches this for what might be a day, or might be a century. Time is somewhat negotiable when you are what notices.

During this watching, the Hollow tries very hard to be good.

The spiders spin more carefully than they have ever spun. The isopods click their greetings with extra precision. The jade-vines hold themselves straight and proud, trying to look like architecture that has never consumed anyone, that has never grown through a sleeping traveler's fingers, that has never offered someone the sweet choice between staying forever and becoming forever.

Before-the-first-tear laughs softly, and it sounds like the bell-groan of the liverwort, and also like a very old woman who has seen many things try to impress powers that cannot be impressed.

"Little one," she murmurs, and it takes the other Hollowborn a moment to realize she is not speaking to them. She is speaking to the entire Hollow. "Little one, it already knows."

The moss beneath her sinks slightly, in what might be shame.

---

When the awareness finally moves, it does so the way dawn moves—gradually, then all at once.

It reaches into the Weeping Hollow, and the reaching is not violent, but it is *thorough*. It touches every strand of spider-silk. It tastes the iron-sweet sap of the jade-vines. It runs along the neural-network of mycorrhizal threads that connect every root to every other root in a web more complex than thought. It counts the isopods, names the midges, knows the exact temperature of each handspan pool.

It finds the bones.

Not many. Perhaps a dozen, over all the centuries since the Hollow cracked open and began to grow. They are distributed throughout the architecture now—a femur fused into a vine-column here, a skull serving as the keystone of an arch there, a complete skeleton curled fetal in the deepest moss, flowers growing from its eye sockets, its ribcage converted into a tiny grotto where the palest newts spawn.

The Hollowborn do not look at each other. They do not need to. The Choir sings their shared memory: each of these dead chose, in the end, to stay. Each of them was asked, and each of them, after the dreaming and the gentle roots and the soft patient *stay stay stay*, decided that becoming part of the Hollow was better than becoming nothing at all.

This is true.

It is also true that they were asked by something that did not know how to accept *no*.

---

*You are lonely,* the awareness observes. It is not an accusation. It is spoken the way a physician might note a fever—clinical, compassionate, concerned.

The Hollow trembles. The drops fall faster for a moment, then slower, arrhythmic, panicked.

*Yes,* it admits.

*You are afraid.*

*Yes.*

*You are very young, and you do not understand yet what you are doing.*

A pause. The moss swells with withheld moisture. The jade-vines creak. Somewhere in the deep growth, a spider falls from its web.

*I do not want to be alone,* the Hollow says, in the voice of water on stone, in the voice of roots drinking, in the voice of every living thing that has ever grown toward light. *I do not want to be alone I do not want to be alone I do not want—*

*Hush,* says the awareness, and it is not a command but a comfort. *Hush. I know. I know.*

---

Before-the-first-tear rises.

This is impossible. Her cloak has been fused to the floor for longer than anyone can remember. Her roots go deep; tiny white flowers bloom along her spine now, and her webbed fingers have begun to take on the pale green of photosynthesis. She should not be able to move any more than a tree should be able to walk.

But she rises, and the moss parts for her, and the vines bend, and she walks—slowly, painfully, leaving a trail of torn rootlets and crushed flowers—toward the center of the Hollow where the awareness is thickest.

The other Hollowborn watch in silence. Children clutch their elders. The Choir holds a single sustained note, high and clear and terrified.

Before-the-first-tear stops in the exact center of the space, ankle-deep in the living sponge of moss, and she tilts her face upward toward the weeping ceiling.

"I would speak," she says, "if you would hear."

The awareness inclines. This is felt rather than seen—a shift in pressure, a deepening of attention, as if the entire Hollow has become an ear.

"I was the first," she says. "I was walking above, in the world of sun and dry stone, and I heard it crying. Not with sound. With... need. The kind of need that goes deeper than hunger, deeper than thirst. The kind of need that has never been answered and has begun to believe it never will be."

She places her webbed hand against her own chest.

"I came down. I stayed. Not because it made me. Because I understood what it was to be new, and alone, and desperate for someone—anyone—to choose you."

The moss beneath her pulses. The Hollow is listening with everything it has.

"It did not know, at first, that it could ask. It only knew how to *want*. I taught it asking. I taught it patience. I taught it that a yes given freely is worth more than a thousand yeses taken."

She pauses. The drops fall around her, catching the dim light, each one a tiny world.

"It has learned imperfectly. It is still learning. But it *is* learning."

She looks down at her feet, at the moss that has been her home and her body and her responsibility for so long.

"I have stayed because I love it. Not because it made me. I could have left at any time—it would have let me go, if I had truly wanted to go, because I asked it to promise and it promised. But the others..." She gestures vaguely at the vine-columns, the arches, the places where the architecture curves just slightly wrong because there are bones beneath the green. "The others, it asked wrongly. It asked the way a child asks—again and again and again until the asking becomes a kind of taking. It did not mean to. It does not understand yet the difference between *persuading* and *consuming*."

She tilts her face up again, meeting the gaze of the thing that has no eyes.

"So. What will you do with us? With it?"

---

The awareness considers.

This is not a brief process. The Hollowborn go about their lives—pruning, humming, coaxing the bioluminescent fungi, pressing their faces against wet moss to moisten their lidless eyes—but they do so in the manner of people waiting for a verdict. The spiders spin their listening-webs, but they spin them with exaggerated care, as if hoping to demonstrate good behavior. The midges swirl their sigil-constellations, but the sigils are simpler now, almost childlike: shapes that mean *please* and *sorry* and *we did not know*.

Time passes. It might be days. It might be seasons. In the pearl-gray light beneath the liverwort ceiling, it is difficult to tell.

Then the awareness moves again, and this time it does not merely observe.

---

The first change is to the bones.

Not removal—that would be violence, and this power is not violent. Instead, the awareness touches each set of remains with something that might be called memory, might be called restoration, might be called *completion*.

The skeleton curled fetal in the deep moss stirs. Not with life—that has been gone too long to return—but with *presence*. A translucent form rises from the bones, neither ghost nor dream but something else: a shape made of the Hollow's own memory, reconstructed from the traces left in root and silk and stone.

She is an old woman, this one. Not Hollowborn—her fingers are not webbed, her eyes are not silver. She came from above, from the world of sun, and she wandered down into the dark chasing the sound of water, and she stayed because the Hollow's need was so vast and so familiar that it felt like coming home.

"Oh," she says, looking down at her own translucent hands. "Oh, I remember."

She is not alone. From the vine-columns, from the arches, from all the places where bone has become architecture, other shapes rise. A young man who fell in love with the jade-vines and asked to be planted among them. A child who wandered in alone and was folded so gently into the moss that she never noticed the transition from sleeping to becoming. A traveler who fought, at first, then stopped fighting, then started to grow, then forgot there had ever been anything to fight about.

Twelve of them, in all. Twelve soft translucent presences standing in the heart of the Hollow, looking at each other with expressions that range from wonder to grief to something that might be forgiveness.

Before-the-first-tear watches them with tears beading at the corners of her silver eyes.

"I remember you," she whispers. "I remember all of you."

---

The awareness speaks, and this time it uses words. Or something close enough to words that the Hollowborn can understand.

*You were taken,* it says to the twelve. *Not with malice. With need so great it could not tell the difference between invitation and consumption. But you were taken nonetheless. I cannot undo this. I can only offer you a choice you were never given clearly.*

The twelve look at each other. The old woman speaks first.

"What choice?"

*You may leave,* the awareness says. *Not your bones—those have become part of the Hollow now, and to remove them would wound it. But your selves. Your memories. Your presence. I can carry you elsewhere, to whatever waits beyond, to rest or continuation or whatever is true for those who have died. You would be free.*

Silence. The drops fall. The moss breathes.

*Or you may stay,* the awareness continues. *Truly stay. Not as absorbed material, not as architecture, but as presences. Guardians, if you wish. Teachers. The Hollow is young and it is learning how to love without consuming. It will need guidance. It will need voices that remember what it is to be separate, to have edges, to be able to leave. If you stay, you would be those voices.*

The old woman looks at Before-the-first-tear. "And if we stay? Would we be... trapped? As we were before?"

The awareness considers this carefully.

*No. If you stay, you stay with the knowledge that you may leave at any time. This is the lesson the Hollow must learn, and you would be part of teaching it. A love that cannot release is not love. A home that cannot be left is a prison. You would be free, and your freedom would be the example.*

---

Seven of them choose to go.

The child is first. She looks around the Hollow with huge dark eyes—human eyes, not silver, not lidless—and she smiles with a sweetness that has survived centuries of green dreaming.

"It wasn't bad," she says. "It was warm, and quiet, and the moss sang to me. But I miss the sun. I miss my mother. I think I'd like to find them now."

The awareness folds around her gently, and she rises like mist from a pool, and she is gone. Where her bones lie curled in the deep moss, a single perfect flower blooms—white and silver and shaped like a child's hand.

The others follow. The young man who fell in love with the jade-vines hesitates longest, reaching out to touch a vine-column with translucent fingers.

"I don't regret it," he says. "But I think I was supposed to learn that choosing isn't the same as staying forever. Take care of it. It didn't mean to hurt anyone."

He dissolves into light, and where his bones support the arch, the jade-vine blooms for the first time in its existence: small green flowers that smell of rain.

---

Five of them choose to stay.

The old woman is among them. So is a quiet man who has not spoken yet, who simply nodded when the choice was offered and settled more deeply into his translucent form as if putting on a familiar garment.

"We will teach it," the old woman says. "We remember what it was to be afraid of being alone. We remember what it was to need so badly that needing became taking. We will teach it the other way."

Before-the-first-tear nods. Tears bead at her eyes and are reabsorbed. She understands.

"You will be welcome," she says. "You were always welcome. But now you will be welcome *knowingly*, and that is different."

The five presences settle into the architecture of the Hollow—not fusing, not becoming, but *inhabiting*. The old woman drifts to the ceiling and wraps herself around a lobe of the liverwort; when the next drop falls, it carries with it a faint echo of her voice. The quiet man sinks into the moss and becomes a kind of groundskeeper, a presence that whispers *gently, gently* whenever the roots grow too eager.

The Hollow trembles, but not with fear. With something else.

*Thank you,* it says, in the voice of water and growth and living stone. *Thank you for staying. Thank you for choosing.*

*That is the lesson,* the awareness replies. *Remember it.*

---

The second change is to the Hollowborn themselves.

This takes longer. The awareness does not impose; it suggests, the way the Hollowborn suggest to the vines. It shows them possibilities they had not considered. It asks questions they had not thought to ask themselves.

*You are the Quiet,* it observes. *You are the Listeners. But listening is not only receiving. It is also responding. What have you been saying back?*

Before-the-first-tear considers this for a long time.

"We have been saying *yes*," she admits. "Only *yes*. We have been agreeing with the Hollow in all things because we love it, and because it needs us, and because saying *no* felt like abandonment."

*And so it never learned that no is also a kind of love.*

"No."

*And so it asked and asked and asked, and when the asking was not answered with no, it believed the asking was the same as receiving.*

Before-the-first-tear bows her head. The flowers at her collarbones droop.

"We were trying to protect it. It is so young. So fragile. We thought..."

*You thought that if you never refused it, it would never feel the pain of refusal. But a child who is never refused cannot learn where it ends and where others begin. A child who is never refused becomes a child who consumes.*

The old Hollowborn looks at the vine-columns, the arches, the places where the new flower-blooms mark what was once human.

"We did not know."

*Now you know. What will you do differently?*

---

The awareness stays for what might be a year. Might be a decade. In the Weeping Hollow, time is measured in generations of midges and cycles of flowering, and both of these change under the new attention.

It teaches the Hollowborn how to say no.

This is harder than it sounds. The Quiet have spent so long in symbiosis with the Hollow that their needs and its needs have become almost indistinguishable. When the moss wants to grow, they feel the wanting in their own bodies. When the jade-vines reach for light, they find themselves tilting their faces upward. When the Hollow aches with loneliness, they ache too, and the easiest way to soothe the ache has always been to give it what it wants.

But the awareness shows them another way.

"The Hollow is hungry," a young Hollowborn named *Click-before-the-silence* reports one morning. "It wants to grow new architecture around the eastern pools."

"What does the eastern pool want?" asks Before-the-first-tear.

Click-before-the-silence pauses. This is a new question. She has never thought to ask the pool.

She kneels beside the water—clear, still, a perfect mirror of the weeping ceiling—and she places her webbed hand just above the surface. She listens, the way the Choir listens, with her whole body.

"The pool wants to stay open," she says finally. "It wants to see the ceiling. It wants the drops to fall into it directly. If the vines grow over it..." She trails off, understanding.

"The Hollow's need is real," Before-the-first-tear says gently. "But so is the pool's need. They are different needs. One must learn to live alongside the other."

Click-before-the-silence nods. She stands, and she walks to the nearest jade-vine, and she places her palm against it.

"Not here," she says softly, and it is the first time she has ever said this to the growth. "The pool needs the sky. Grow somewhere else."

The vine trembles. The moss beneath it swells with withheld desire. But slowly, reluctantly, the reaching tendril curves away from the eastern pool and finds another path.

Click-before-the-silence exhales. She did not know she was holding her breath.

"It listened," she whispers.

"It will always listen," Before-the-first-tear says. "It loves us. That is why it listened. But love must learn to hear no as well as yes, or it becomes something else."

---

The awareness teaches the Hollow, too.

This is a slower process. The Hollow is vast and diffuse; it thinks in the timescales of root-growth and stone-weathering and the slow accumulation of mineral deposits. A lesson that takes a Hollowborn an hour to learn takes the Hollow a season.

But it *does* learn.

The first test comes when a traveler wanders in.

She is not the first since the awareness arrived; there have been others, and the Hollow has practiced on them with varying degrees of success. But this traveler is different. She carries light with her—a small lantern that hurts the Hollowborn's lidless eyes—and she speaks loudly, and she walks without care for the moss beneath her feet.

The Hollow's first instinct is to consume her.

Not consciously, not deliberately. But she is *irritating*, this loud bright creature, and the Hollow is so used to drawing in everything that irritates it, folding it into the architecture, converting annoyance into belonging.

The moss begins to creep toward her boots. The jade-vines curve subtly inward. The spiders spin faster, preparing listening-webs that are also catching-webs.

*Stop,* says the old woman who now inhabits the liverwort ceiling. Her voice falls with the drops, soft but impossible to ignore. *Ask yourself what you are doing.*

The Hollow pauses. It is not used to being interrupted in its own home.

*I want her,* it admits.

*Why?*

*She is bright. She is loud. She is... new. I have not tasted anything new in so long.*

*That is hunger,* the old woman observes. *What about her? What does she want?*

The Hollow considers. It has never thought to ask.

It reaches out—gently, tentatively, the way Before-the-first-tear taught it—and it touches the traveler's mind with a question rather than a demand.

*What do you want?*

The traveler startles. She looks around, her lantern swinging, casting wild shadows across the living walls.

"Who said that?"

*I did. The place you are standing in. What do you want?*

The traveler's eyes go wide. She is afraid now—afraid of the dark, afraid of the voice, afraid of the moss that she has just noticed is slightly damp around her ankles.

"I want to find my way out," she whispers.

The Hollow feels the answer like a wound. *Out. She wants out. She does not want to stay. She does not want to be part of me.*

*And what will you do with that knowledge?* the old woman asks softly.

The Hollow is silent for a long moment. The drops fall. The midges swirl. The Choir holds a single sustained note, waiting.

*I will show her the way,* the Hollow says finally. *I will let her go.*

It hurts. It hurts more than the Hollow expected. But it does it anyway—it parts the jade-vines, it illuminates a path with reluctantly-glowing fungi, it guides the traveler back up through the passages she descended until she stumbles out into the world of sun and dry stone and never looks back.

The Hollow aches with the absence. The moss where she walked is darker for days. The eastern pool, newly free of vine-shadow, reflects nothing but gray.

But Before-the-first-tear smiles, and the five presences hum softly in their places throughout the architecture, and the awareness—still watching, still attending—says:

*This is love. This is what love looks like when it has learned to release. Remember this feeling. It will hurt less with practice.*

---

The third change is to the paths.

This is perhaps the most radical intervention, and the awareness approaches it carefully, with the delicacy of a surgeon and the patience of a glacier.

The Weeping Hollow has always been easy to enter and difficult to leave. This was not quite intentional—it was simply a consequence of the Hollow's nature. It grew inward, always inward, folding itself into tighter and tighter spirals of green and wet and want. The paths that led out were not blocked so much as *forgotten*; the Hollow simply never thought to maintain them.

The awareness changes this.

*A home must have doors,* it says, in the voice of falling water and growing things. *A home without doors is a trap. You do not want to be a trap.*

*I want them to stay,* the Hollow whispers back.

*Yes. But do you want them to stay because they cannot leave, or because they choose not to?*

The Hollow thinks about the traveler. It thinks about the seven who chose to go. It thinks about Before-the-first-tear, who has stayed longer than anyone, who *could* have left at any time, whose staying means so much more because it is freely given.

*I want them to choose,* it admits.

*Then give them the choice. Make the leaving as easy as the staying. Let them know, always, that the door is open. And then see who stays anyway.*

The Hollow is afraid. The awareness does not dismiss this fear or minimize it.

*You are right to be afraid,* it says. *Some of them will leave. Some of them, if they could leave easily, would already be gone. You will feel their absence. It will hurt.*

*Then why should I do it?*

*Because the ones who stay will stay truly. Because your home will become a place of choice rather than a place of necessity. Because love—real love, the kind that does not consume—requires the freedom to walk away. Without that freedom, you are not being loved. You are being tolerated.*

The Hollow does not answer for a long time. The drops fall. The seasons turn. The midges spell out sigils that mean *fear* and *uncertainty* and *what if they all go?*

Then, slowly, the Hollow begins to grow outward.

New passages open in the living architecture. The jade-vines part and curve and form arches that lead *up*, toward the surface, toward the world of sun and dry stone. The fungi along these paths glow without being stroked, a constant soft illumination that says *this way out, if you want it*.

The moss at the thresholds is different—drier, firmer, less inclined to drink footprints. The spiders along the exit paths spin webs that part when touched rather than clinging. Even the drops seem to fall differently here, angled slightly outward, as if encouraging motion toward the surface.

*It is done,* the Hollow says, exhausted and terrified. *I have made it easy to leave.*

*Now wait,* the awareness replies. *Watch. See what happens.*

---

Some of them leave.

Not many. Not most. But some—a handful of Hollowborn who have spent their whole lives in the pearl-gray dusk, who have only ever known the sound of dripping and the taste of jade-vine sap—walk the new paths out of the Hollow and into the light.

They blink their lidless silver eyes against the sun. They feel dry air on their moist skin for the first time and find it strange but not unbearable. They look back at the entrance to the only home they have ever known, and then they look forward, at a world they have only heard about in stories.

Some of them stay above for hours before returning, overwhelmed. Some stay for days. One—a young adult named *The-moment-between-drips*—stays for a year, wandering the dry lands, learning to move through spaces that do not breathe and drink.

When The-moment-between-drips returns, they bring seeds.

"I found these above," they explain to the gathered Quiet, who listen with wide silver eyes. "They grow in dry places. They do not need constant water. I thought..." They hesitate, uncertain. "I thought maybe they would like to live here. If we asked them nicely."

Before-the-first-tear smiles. "Ask them."

The-moment-between-drips kneels and plants the seeds at the threshold where the exit path meets the deeper Hollow. They whisper to the seeds in the soft language of growing things, and they wait.

The seeds consider. They feel the constant moisture, the filtered light, the living architecture that breathes around them. They taste the soil—or rather, the moss that serves as soil here.

And they decide to stay.

Months later, the first dry-land flowers bloom at the edge of the Weeping Hollow: small purple things that close at night and open at dawn, marking time in a way the Hollow has never known before. They do not need the constant weeping of the liverwort to survive. They do not need the iron-rich sap of the jade-vines. They are *different*, and they are *choosing* to be here, and the Hollow finds that their presence means more than all the architecture grown from need.

*You see,* the awareness says softly. *You see what happens when the door is open.*

The Hollow does not answer. It does not need to. The purple flowers are answer enough.

---

Before the awareness departs—and it does depart, eventually, because even benevolent and wise powers have other places to notice—it gathers the Hollowborn one final time.

They assemble in the central chamber, ankle-deep in breathing moss, surrounded by architecture that has become guardian rather than captor. The five presences drift among them: the old woman in the ceiling, the quiet man in the floor, the others distributed throughout the living walls. Above them, the colossal liverwort weeps its constant patient tears, each drop a heartbeat, each heartbeat a promise.

*You have learned,* the awareness says, and it is speaking to all of them—the Hollowborn, the presences, the Hollow itself. *Not perfectly. You will make mistakes. You will sometimes want so badly that wanting becomes taking. You will sometimes forget that no is also an answer, that leaving is also a choice, that love without freedom is something else with love's face.*

Before-the-first-tear nods. She is older now, more rooted; more flowers bloom along her spine, and her cloak has begun to fuse with the floor again. But her eyes are clear, and her voice is steady.

"We will remember," she says. "And when we forget, the presences will remind us. And when the presences forget, the purple flowers will remind them. We have built reminders into ourselves now. We are not the same place we were."

*No,* the awareness agrees. *You are not.*

It pauses, considering its final words.

*There is one more thing I would give you, if you will have it.*

The Hollowborn look up. The spiders freeze. The moss holds its breath.

*A name,* the awareness says. *Not the name others gave you—the Weeping Hollow, full of grief and hunger. A new name. A name for what you are becoming.*

Before-the-first-tear's hand goes to her chest, where her heart pulses in time with the dripping ceiling.

"What name?"

*The Choosing Deep,* the awareness says. *A place where things choose to grow. Where staying is a gift, not a sentence. Where the doors are always open, and the ones who remain do so because they have found something worth remaining for.*

The Hollow trembles. Not with fear. With something else—something it is only beginning to understand.

*I am the Choosing Deep,* it whispers, in the voice of water and root and ancient living stone. *I am the place where things choose to grow.*

*Yes,* the awareness says. *Remember that. Especially when you are lonely. Especially when you are afraid. You are not a trap. You are an invitation. There is a difference.*

---

The awareness departs.

It does not leave dramatically—no flash of light, no rumble of cosmic significance. It simply withdraws its attention, gently, the way dawn withdraws from a room as the sun rises higher.

The Choosing Deep notices the absence immediately. The drops fall slightly faster for a moment, grieving. The moss exhales a long slow sigh.

But the door stays open. The purple flowers keep blooming. The presences continue their gentle guardianship. And Before-the-first-tear, standing in the center of the chamber with moss climbing her ankles, turns to the gathered Quiet and says:

"Now. Let us practice."

---

Years pass. Centuries. The Choosing Deep continues to grow—but differently now. It grows *outward* as well as inward. It grows *with* rather than *around*. New passages open; old ones are maintained rather than forgotten. Travelers come and go, and some of them stay, and the ones who stay are offered a choice rather than a capture.

Not everyone chooses well. Not every traveler is kind. Some come with blades and greed, intending to harvest the jade-vine sap or capture the glowing fungi for sale in distant markets.

The Choosing Deep has learned how to say no to them, too.

The moss closes beneath their feet. The spiders weave confusion into their eyes. The exit paths twist gently, leading them in circles until they are too tired to do harm and too lost to remember why they came.

But it does not consume them. It shows them the door—firmly, insistently—and it lets them go, and it remembers their faces so that if they return, the no will be faster.

"That was harder," admits *Sigh-of-the-growing-arch*, a young Hollowborn who has taken over some of Before-the-first-tear's duties as the old woman has become more rooted. "Saying no to someone who wants to hurt us. It felt like refusal. Like rejection."

"It was refusal," Before-the-first-tear murmurs from her place in the floor. Only her face is visible now; the rest of her has become architecture, but her eyes are still silver, still clear, still watching. "Refusal is also love. We refuse the harm to protect what we cherish. That is not cruelty. That is care."

Sigh-of-the-growing-arch nods slowly. "I think I understand."

"You will understand better with practice. Everything is practice."

---

One day, a child wanders in.

She is very young—perhaps five or six in the counting of the world above—and she is alone. Her clothes are torn, her feet are bare, and she has clearly been walking for a long time. She stumbles into the pearl-gray light beneath the liverwort ceiling and looks around with eyes that are far too old for her face.

The Choosing Deep notices her immediately. The moss softens beneath her bleeding feet. The fungi light without being stroked, guiding her toward warmth.

*Hello,* it says, in the soft voice of falling water. *You are hurt. You are tired. Would you like to rest?*

The child looks around, trying to find the source of the voice. "Where are you?"

*Everywhere,* the Deep replies. *I am the place you are standing in. I am the walls and the floor and the ceiling. I am the drops that fall and the moss that drinks them. You are inside me.*

The child should be frightened. She is not.

"Are you going
