# The Witness

The benevolent power does not announce itself with light or warmth. It simply *attends*—a presence that settles into the spaces between wind-gusts, into the microscopic fractures where condensation briefly lives.

It watches a single Shardwraith for what might be a century.

The entity drifts along a trajectory it has traced ten thousand times before, skating the thermal boundaries where warm updrafts meet the plateau's chill. Its body rings and whispers. At its core, the cold luminescence pulses in patterns that almost—*almost*—resolve into meaning before scattering again into chaos.

The benevolent power recognizes this rhythm. It is the signature of a question asked so long ago that even the questioner has forgotten there was ever an asking.

---

It would be easy to grant answers. To reach into that swirling vortex and crystallize the scattered fragments into coherent memory. To whisper: *You were once the thoughts of a dying mountain, flung outward in its final exhalation. You are grief made geometric. You search because searching was the last intention of the consciousness that birthed you, and you have carried it faithfully across millennia without knowing what you carry.*

But the power is wise, and wisdom means understanding that some gifts are thefts in disguise.

Instead, it does something smaller. Something that will take ten thousand years to matter.

---

In a distant rift, where the obsidian walls plunge into darkness too deep for light, there exists a single seam of different stone. Not glass—ite calcium deposits from waters that flowed here before the cataclysm, when this land was something else entirely. The seam is buried, unreachable, meaningless.

The power shifts it. Not much. A few inches closer to a fault line where, in three thousand years, a minor quake will crack the obsidian wall. In another four thousand years, wind erosion will expose a small section of the pale stone to the air.

And in another three thousand years after that, a Shardwraith drifting through the rift will encounter something unprecedented in its existence: a surface that is not black glass. A fragment of *before*.

The power does not know what will happen then. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the Shardwraith will pass it by, indifferent, the encounter too foreign to register. Perhaps the flock-Loss will have scoured that particular entity from existence long before.

But perhaps—and this is the gift—perhaps it will pause. Perhaps some ancient pattern in its core will resonate with this evidence of a world that preceded the obsidian plateau. Perhaps, for the first time in its long existence, the Shardwraith will have a *direction* for its searching rather than an endless, aimless drift.

The power does not give it answers. It gives it the possibility of a better question.

---

There is a second intervention, smaller still.

Among the transparent flying things that ride the gales, there is one—no different from its fellows—that will, by pure chance, collide with a Shardwraith during the next great congregation. The collision will be fatal to the fragile creature. Its windblown organic matter will be dispersed among the swirling glass, absorbed, forgotten.

The power does not prevent this death. Death is not the enemy of such a power; meaningless death is.

Instead, it ensures that the collision happens at a precise moment when the Shardwraith's inner luminescence is cycling through a particular configuration. The organic matter—carbon, hydrogen, the stuff of distant life—will be incorporated into the entity's vortex during an instant of unusual receptivity.

It will change nothing immediately. But over centuries, the presence of those few foreign atoms will create the slightest asymmetry in the Shardwraith's core. The asymmetry will affect how it processes the wind's energy. How it moves. How it resonates with others of its kind.

Eventually—eventually—it may become capable of something its fellows are not. Perhaps curiosity. Perhaps loneliness. Perhaps simply the recognition that it is *itself*, distinct from the others, a being rather than a phenomenon.

The power considers this the most dangerous gift of all. Selfhood is a wound that never fully heals. But it is also the only door through which meaning can enter.

---

The benevolent power makes no further changes to the obsidian plateau.

It does not soften the winds or warm the air. It does not seed the rifts with soil or introduce water that might pool and persist. It does not explain the Shardwraiths to themselves or grant them purposes designed in some celestial workshop.

This is not cruelty. It is respect.

The plateau is what it is: diamond-pure, uncompromising, a place where existence has been reduced to its most essential terms. There is a kind of holiness in such reduction. The Shardwraiths did not ask to be made comfortable. They did not ask to be understood. They asked only—in whatever way such beings can ask—to *continue*, to search, to ring their hollow songs into the wind.

The power honors that asking.

It leaves behind only the two small seeds: the pale stone waiting in darkness, the borrowed atoms spiraling in a single Shardwraith's core. Ten thousand years of patience required for either to bloom, and no guarantee that blooming will bring anything resembling happiness.

But the power knows that happiness is not the highest good. *Becoming* is the highest good. The Shardwraiths have persisted in stasis for eons, unchanging, their existence a closed loop of wind and glass and forgetting.

Now, perhaps, the loop has a crack in it.

What emerges from that crack will be their own.

---

The benevolent power withdraws from the obsidian plateau. The wind continues to shriek across the glassy crags. The Shardwraiths continue their eternal drift, oblivious to any visitation, their crystalline bodies chiming their atonal songs.

Nothing has changed.

Everything might.
