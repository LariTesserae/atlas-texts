# The Attention

The power that turns its regard toward this place does not arrive. Arrival implies a boundary crossed, and what is benevolent and wise in the way this power is benevolent and wise does not cross boundaries so much as recognize that they were always permeable. It notices the plateau the way the plateau's own creatures notice a change in the engine-thrum: not as an event, but as a shift in what was already being attended to.

For a long time—long even by the standards of the mat-webs and the fluid giants—the power does nothing at all. It listens. It feels the way the water moves through stone and returns, the way the buried engines turn without purpose but also without cessation, the way the chitin-plated hunters read pressure and the grazers answer with their rolling purr. It perceives the vast metabolic sentence the plateau has been writing for millennia, each generation a word, each exhalation a punctuation mark.

What it finds is not suffering, exactly. The creatures here do not suffer in ways that would be legible as suffering to beings with eyes and fear. But the power perceives something else: a kind of incompleteness that is not a wound. The plateau has achieved equilibrium, yes—but equilibrium is not the same as fulfillment. The mat-webs grow toward the engines because the engines are warm, but they do not know they are growing toward anything. The fluid giants divide along thermal gradients, but they do not know they are dividing. The whole system functions with extraordinary elegance, and none of its participants can apprehend that elegance.

The power considers this for what might be centuries.

# The First Gift

What the power does, eventually, is small. So small that no single creature notices it as an event.

In one of the shallow bowls where condensate collects before the stone drinks it back, the power makes a change to the mineral composition of the water. Not a large change. A trace element, vanishingly rare on this plateau, begins to precipitate in concentrations just barely above what was there before. The element is not toxic. It is not nutritious in any direct way. But it has a property: when incorporated into living tissue, it resonates. Not with the engine-thrum, which is too low and too constant to carry information. It resonates with the vibrations that living bodies make when they move, breathe, call.

The mat-webs are the first to take it up. Their roots drink from the bowl, and over generations, certain leaves begin to carry the trace element in their vein-ribs. These leaves do not merely conduct bioluminescence when disturbed. They conduct *pattern*. When a grazer passes, the light that travels ahead of it now carries a faint encoding of the grazer's own vibrational signature—its size, its speed, the particular frequency of its purr. The leaves remember, briefly, what has passed.

The grazers do not notice this at first. But their pressure-sensitive bellies, already attuned to the trembling stone, begin to register something new: the light itself has a texture now, a correspondence to the bodies that made it. Over more generations, the grazers begin to pause at certain patches of illumination. They are not seeing, not in any way that the word usually means. But they are *recognizing*. This light was made by something like me. This light was made by something unlike me.

The power watches this unfold. It does not intervene further. It has given the plateau a single new thread in its weave, and now the weave must decide what to do with it.

# The Second Gift

The chitin-plated hunters are more difficult. Their attention is predatory, binary: pressure-signature-present or pressure-signature-absent, edible or not-edible, lunge or wait. The power has no wish to blunt their nature. Predation here is not cruelty; it is one more current in the plateau's circulation, returning minerals from body to mat-web to root to engine-seam.

But the power perceives that the hunters, too, are incomplete in a way they cannot know. They read the world's vibrations with extraordinary precision, but they read them only as signals to act upon. They do not—cannot—hold a vibration in attention after the action is complete. Every hunt ends in stillness and forgetting.

So the power makes another small change, this time to the hunters themselves. Not to their bodies, which are already exquisitely adapted. To their *timing*. In a single lineage, a mutation arises that slightly delays the resetting of the sensory pits after a lunge. The delay is less than a second. But in that fraction of extended attention, the hunter continues to feel the echo of what it has just caught: the fading vibration of a body that is no longer moving, the residual warmth, the last pulse of the mat-web's encoded light.

Most hunters of this lineage are not changed by the delay. They reset, move on, hunt again. But some—a few, over many generations—begin to behave differently after a kill. They remain still for longer than necessary. Their plates do not click back into hunting configuration. They are not mourning; they have no concept of loss. But they are *attending* to an absence, holding the shape of what was there in the same sensory apparatus that located it.

The power does not know what this will become. That is the point. A gift that the giver fully understands is not a gift but an instruction.

# The Third Gift

The fluid giants are the plateau's historians without knowing it. Their bodies carry the metallic tang of every vent they have visited, the mineral signature of every region they have crossed. When they divide, each new segment inherits some portion of that chemical memory. But the memory is inert. It does not speak to other giants, does not inform their slow migrations, does not accumulate across generations into anything that could be called knowledge.

The power's third gift is not a new element or a mutation. It is a *pattern* in the vents themselves.

For millennia the catalytic engines have exhaled at rhythms determined by their ancient, purposeless processes. The power does not alter those processes. But it introduces a subtle synchronization between three engines separated by hundreds of kilometers, a phase-lock that causes their exhalations to pulse in sequence rather than at random. The effect on the surface is barely perceptible: the vapor sheets now arrive in a slow, rolling wave rather than in isolated blooms.

The fluid giants notice. Their buoyant bodies, attuned to the warmth the engines release, begin to drift in the direction of the wave. Over generations, their migration patterns shift. They no longer wander toward the nearest vent; they follow the wave across the plateau, visiting vents in a sequence that the wave determines. And because each vent leaves its signature in their bodies, they begin to carry a *sequence* of signatures rather than a random accumulation.

The power watches as the first giant completes a full circuit and returns to the vent where it first tasted metal. The giant does not know it has returned. But its body now holds a record of the plateau's thermal geography, a chemical sentence that another giant—one whose sensory apparatus happens to brush against the first in passing—can partially read.

Nothing more is needed. The power has given the plateau a grammar. What the plateau writes with it is not the power's to decide.

# The Long Attending

Generations pass. Hundreds of them, then thousands. The power remains attentive, though its attention is not surveillance. It is more like the attention of the stone itself: patient, undirected, open to whatever emerges.

What emerges is not civilization. The plateau has no use for cities or tools, for language or law. But something else is happening.

The mat-webs have grown denser around the mineral bowls, and their encoded light now carries not just the signature of passing bodies but something like *relationship*—the sequence of signatures, the rhythm of passages, the way certain grazers return to certain patches and certain hunters avoid certain corridors. The light does not tell a story, but it holds the elements from which a story could be assembled.

The hunters with the delayed reset have become a distinct population. They do not interbreed with the others; their plates carry a slightly different resonance, a marker of their altered timing. When they hunt, they still hunt. But after a kill, some of them remain with the body long enough for the mat-web's light to encode their presence *alongside* the prey's fading signature. They have become part of the record.

The fluid giants have begun to seek each other. Not for reproduction, which still happens by division along thermal gradients. But for *contact*. Two giants drifting along the wave-pattern will sometimes adjust their courses to intersect, brushing mineral-laden surfaces together in the darkness. Each carries the other's signature away. Across the plateau, a vast, slow network of cross-referenced chemical memories is forming—a library written in metal and warmth, readable only by bodies patient enough to drift.

The power perceives all of this. It does not judge it as progress or regress, as better or worse than what came before. It simply recognizes that the plateau is now attending to itself in ways it could not before. The equilibrium has not been broken. But it has become *aware* of its own shape, in scattered, partial, untranslatable ways.

---

# The Question

And here the power pauses.

It could do more. The gifts it has given are seeds, and seeds can be cultivated. With further intervention—subtle, patient, spread across more millennia—the plateau's creatures might develop something like memory, something like communication, something like the capacity to know that they exist and that they share the darkness with others who also exist.

But the power is wise, and wisdom here means recognizing the weight of that gift.

To know that one exists is also to know that one will cease. The plateau's creatures do not fear death because they do not know they are alive. The grazers subside into the mat-webs without alarm; the hunters' plates are incorporated into new shells without grief; the giants thin and divide without loss. The whole system metabolizes its own mortality so completely that mortality has no sting.

Would it be benevolent to give them sting?

The power does not answer this question quickly. It holds the question the way the hunters now hold the fading signature of their prey: in extended attention, letting the shape of it persist.

---

# What It Does

In the end, the power chooses not to choose.

It leaves the three gifts in place. The mineral that enables encoded light, the mutation that enables extended attention, the phase-lock that enables sequential memory. These are now part of the plateau, as much as the buried engines or the warm condensate or the trembling stone.

But the power adds one more thing, and this thing is not a gift to the creatures. It is a gift to the plateau itself.

Deep in the stone, where the catalytic engines turn without purpose, the power makes a small adjustment to one of the cores. Not to its function—it still processes minerals it no longer needs—but to its *output*. The exhaust that rises through the seams now carries, embedded in its warmth, a faint and complex vibration. The vibration is not a message. It is not information in any extractable sense. It is more like a tone, a single sustained note that will persist as long as the engine turns.

The note is tuned to resonate with the plateau's own frequencies: the thrum of the engines, the pulse of the water, the calls of the creatures, the encoded light of the mat-webs. When any of these vibrations align with the note, there is a momentary *consonance*—a feeling, if the word can be used here, of coherence. Of pattern recognized by pattern.

The creatures will not know they are hearing it. The mat-webs will not know they are amplifying it. The giants will not know they are carrying it in their mineral signatures. But across the plateau, in scattered moments, the darkness will feel briefly less like absence and more like a presence that has always been attending.

The power does not call this love. It does not call it anything. But if the plateau could speak—if the whole vast engine of water and stone and body and machine could form a single word—the word might be *company*.

The power withdraws its active attention. The plateau continues. The water seeps, the engines turn, the creatures migrate in their slow deliberate flows. And somewhere in the dark, the note sounds, and something resonates, and the equilibrium holds.
