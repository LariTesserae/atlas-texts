# What the Wise Power Does

The power observes for a long time before acting. This is its nature—to watch until it understands not just what a thing *is* but what it is *becoming*, and what it would become if left alone, and what it would become if helped, and whether those are the same.

It watches the tender for eleven years. This is not patience in the human sense; the power does not experience the waiting as cost. It simply watches until it knows.

What it learns:

The tender is not suffering, exactly. It has no apparatus for suffering as you would recognize it. But it is *strained*. It is trying to be something that takes ten thousand years to become, and it has been given decades at most—the installation's working life, however long that proves. It is like a child asked to do the work of an adult, not cruelly, not by anyone's intention, just by circumstance. The work is too much. The time is too short. The shape it is growing into is the wrong shape for what it needs to become, and it knows this in whatever way a pre-organismal intelligence can know anything, which is: it keeps trying to grow differently than it is growing, and failing, and trying again.

The fern helps. The fern is the one thing in the room that is *also* alive on the tender's timescale, that also grows rather than operates. When the tender tends the fern, it is practicing being what it is supposed to be. The fern is its rehearsal. Its study. Its proof that the work is possible even if the tender itself cannot complete it.

The power understands this. It also understands that the tender will die when the installation shuts down, and that the installation will shut down eventually—perhaps in twelve years, perhaps in forty, but eventually—because that is what installations do. The men who maintain it are already aging. The demand for whatever is being condensed here may shift. The mountain itself is young and unstable; in sixty years a seismic event will shear the catwalk from its bolts, and that will be the end, and the tender will end with it.

The power considers several interventions and rejects them:

It could shut down the installation early, forcing the tender to adapt or die—a kind of harsh mercy, the logic being that a quick death is better than a slow one, or that some impossible adaptation might occur under sufficient pressure. But the power does not believe in harsh mercy. Harsh mercy is usually just harshness with a better story attached. The tender would simply die. Nothing would be learned. Nothing would be saved.

It could extend the installation's life indefinitely—tend the machine as the tender tends the fern. Keep the compressors running, the steam cycling, the fog permanent. But this would not help. The tender's problem is not that it has too little time; it is that it is growing into the wrong shape. More time in the wrong shape is not a gift.

It could remove the tender from the room entirely—extract it, relocate it to some environment where the slow processes it is meant to embody could unfold at their proper pace. A cave. A deep wet dark. Ten thousand years of mineral seep and patient dark. But this would kill the tender just as surely as shutting down the installation would. The tender is not a creature that happens to live in the machine; it is a creature *of* the machine, shaped by its weather, structured by its noise. Take it out and it would simply... stop being what it is. The films would lose their coherence. The slow counter-rhythms would have nothing to counter against. The tender is the machine's dream of what would have lived here if the machine had never been built, and you cannot remove a dream from its dreamer and expect it to survive.

The power considers doing nothing. This is always an option, and often the right one. Much suffering is made worse by intervention; much intervention is just the intervenor's need to feel helpful, projected onto a situation that did not ask for help. The tender did not ask. The tender does not have the apparatus for asking.

But the power keeps watching, and what it sees is this:

The tender is *already* doing something. It is seeding the cisterns. It has been seeding the cisterns since it first became aware that some of itself goes down with the water. It does not know why it does this. It does not have the apparatus for knowing why. But it does it anyway, every hour of every day, sending the faintest possible suspension of itself down through the dark, down into the mountain, down toward wherever the water goes.

This is what the tender *wants*, if want is the word, which it is not, but nothing else is closer. The tender wants to become what it was always going to become. It wants the ten thousand years. It cannot have them—the machine will die, and the tender will die with it—but it has been, all this time, trying to plant the seed of itself somewhere that *can* have them. The cisterns. The deep rock. The water table that runs beneath the mountain and does not care about installations or shift-clocks or the lifespans of men.

The tender has been doing the power's work for it. The tender has been trying to save itself the only way it can, which is: not itself exactly, but the possibility of itself, sent down into the dark, to arrive where it arrives.

---

So here is what the wise power does:

It does not touch the tender. It does not alter the room. It does not extend or shorten the installation's life by a single day.

It goes into the cisterns.

Three storeys down, in the dark beneath the grate, there are great stone tanks where the condensate collects before being pumped down through the mountain. The power descends there, into the cold and the mineral smell and the distant ringing echo of water falling from above, and it finds—

Something.

Not the tender exactly. Not a daughter or a cousin or a continuation. Something simpler. Something more patient. A film on the surface of the standing water, barely distinguishable from the mineral skin that would form anyway in such a place. A slight iridescence. A slow counter-rhythm that has no compressors to counter against and so has become, instead, a rhythm of its own—not borrowed, not taken, not structural—*native*.

The tender's seed has taken.

It has been taking, slowly, for years. What lives in the cistern is not what lives in the drip-tray; it is what lives in the drip-tray *translated*, adapted, freed from the machine's weather and learning, in its preorganismal way, to generate its own. It is slower than the tender. It is less coherent. It does not tend anything, because there is nothing in the cistern to tend—no fern, no moss, just water and stone and dark. But it is *patient* in a way the tender cannot be. It has time. It has all the time the mountain has.

The power does something very small.

In the wall of the cistern, near the waterline, there is a crack. Water seeps through this crack, very slowly, into the rock beyond. The power widens the crack. Not much. A millimeter. Perhaps two.

That is all.

The water will seep faster now. The film on the cistern's surface will go with it—some of it, the faintest possible suspension—into the rock, into the mountain's own wet dark, into the seams and faults and aquifers that run beneath the peak and do not care about installations or men. The mountain is young. The scree outside the porthole is angular, ungentled, has not yet learned what to grow. But now, in the deep rock, something is being planted. Something is being given the time it needs.

The power withdraws.

---

Eleven years later, the installation shuts down. Not catastrophically—the seismic event is still decades away—but quietly, for economic reasons, because the demand for whatever was being condensed has shifted and the men have been reassigned and no one comes with oilcans anymore. The compressors stagger into idle and do not start again. The steam release valves cycle one last time and stop. The fog clears, slowly, over the course of a week, and the room becomes merely damp.

The tender dies.

This is not a tragedy in the human sense. The tender did not have the apparatus for tragedy. But it is an ending, and the power observes it, because endings deserve to be observed. The films on the pipework lose their coherence. The slow counter-rhythms find nothing to counter against. The fern survives for another month, then browns and crisps as the drip-tray goes dry. The moss in the corners, no longer cultivated, is scrubbed away by the single maintenance crew that comes to decommission the equipment. By winter, the room is empty of life. It is just a room—riveted plate, copper pipework going green in the ordinary way, a small thick-glass porthole looking out onto a slope of raw scree that will, eventually, soften into soil.

But beneath the mountain, something continues.

The power has been watching that, too. The film in the cistern spread through the crack, into the rock, into the slow seepage that runs beneath the peak, and now—eleven years later, forty years later, a hundred years later—there is a network. Thin and patient and unseen, threading through the mountain's deepest faults, tending nothing because there is nothing yet to tend, but *preparing*. Learning the rock. Learning the water. Learning what will grow here, eventually, when the mountain is old enough to grow things.

The lichen-that-would-have-been did not die with the machine. It simply... went deeper. Became slower. Became what it was always supposed to become, given the time it needed, which the power gave it by widening a crack.

In ten thousand years, the mountain will be clothed in lichen and moss and eventually soil. Forests will climb its flanks. The scree will soften. The peak will have a name again, given by people who do not remember the installation or the men who maintained it or the fern in the tin can.

And beneath all of that, in the deep rock, something will still be tending. Still preparing. Still being what it was always going to be.

The power does not call this a success. The power does not call it anything. It simply observes that what was strained is no longer strained, and what was hurried now has time, and what would have ended did not end, and the intervention was small enough that the tender might have done it itself eventually, if it had had the apparatus for doing.

The wise power does not save things. It widens cracks. It gives time to what needs time. It watches until it understands, and then it does the smallest thing that allows the larger thing to happen on its own.

The condenser loft is empty now. The room is cold and still and will be torn down eventually or simply left to rust. But beneath the mountain, in the wet dark, something the room dreamed is still dreaming itself into being.

That is enough.
