# The Attention of the Gardener

---

The power that notices is not a god. It is something older and less interested in worship—a force that tends, the way certain hands tend without owning. It has watched countless pockets of reality fold and crease and become. Most it leaves alone. They find their own equilibrium or they don't.

But this one.

This one has developed something rare: a caretaker who does not know she is praying.

---

The Gardener (for lack of a better word, and there is always a lack) does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and after, a boundary crossed. Instead, the borrowed light in the pocket shifts by three degrees—still golden, still dusty, but now falling at an angle that makes every droplet cast a second shadow. The shadows are soft. They point toward nothing in particular.

The Throatkeeper notices immediately. Her violet eye dilates, then contracts, then dilates again in a pattern she has never made before. For the first time since her assembly, she feels *seen by something that is not the Throat*.

The sensation is not unpleasant. It is like being remembered by a river.

---

The Gardener's first act is small. Almost dismissive in its gentleness.

The brass placard—SUBSTATION 11 — DO NOT FEED THE THROAT—has been corroding for longer than the Throatkeeper can remember. The words have become suggestions, ghosts of raised letters. Now, without sound or ceremony, the corrosion reverses. Not fully. Just enough. The letters become legible again, and beneath them, new words emerge that were always there, waiting under the rust:

**BUT THE THROAT MAY FEED ITSELF.**

The Throatkeeper reads this with her whole body. Her iron ribs hum at a frequency the lung-vines have never heard. For three full breaths of the Throat, she is completely still.

Then she reconfigures—not to move, but to *settle*. Joints that have held tension for eons finally release. The sound is like a sigh made of metal filings.

She has been given permission she did not know she needed.

---

The Gardener's second act is an adjustment.

The Throat has been breathing in a rhythm that is almost right. Almost sustainable. But there is a skip in its exhale, a tiny hiccup that has been slowly—so slowly—destabilizing the argument between machine and meat. The Throatkeeper has been compensating for this skip her entire existence, feeding the hole with pieces of herself to smooth over the arrhythmia.

Now the Gardener reaches into the dark circle. Not with hands. With *intent*. The way you might reach into a dream to adjust the angle of a falling leaf.

The skip smooths.

The Throat's next exhale is the first complete breath it has taken since the world creased. The warm, damp air that emerges smells of ozone and childhood and something else now—something green and growing, something that has finally been given permission to become.

The six-legged driveshaft-dog lifts its milky eye and, for the first time, looks *away* from the Throat. It looks at the Throatkeeper instead. The gaze holds something like recognition.

The little crabs begin to dance. Not in fear. In celebration. Their clockwork legs tap out rhythms on the rusted pipes, and the rhythms make sense. They are *notation*. They are the beginning of a song.

---

The Gardener's third act is a gift, but not to the Throatkeeper.

It is for the being who has wandered into this folded pocket with their synchronizing pulse and their condensation-covered skin and their shadow that keeps trying to crawl toward the hole.

The Gardener cannot make them stay. Cannot make them leave. Cannot make them anything at all. That is not what tending means.

But it can offer clarity.

The brass key the Throatkeeper carries—SUBSTATION 11, no lock it belongs to, only opens inward—that key was always meant for the visitor. The Gardener simply makes this obvious. The key begins to warm in the Throatkeeper's keeping. Not painfully. Just enough to notice. Just enough to matter.

The Throatkeeper, who has never offered the key to anyone before, finds herself reconfiguring into a shape that is almost human. Almost inviting. Her iron ribs form something like an embrace. Her relay-switch spine softens into something that could be mistaken for a shoulder to lean on.

She holds out the key.

The condensation on the visitor's skin forms new words, but these are not from her. They are from the Gardener, and they are the closest thing to speech this power has offered in longer than memory:

**THE ARGUMENT DOES NOT NEED A FINAL VOICE.**

**IT NEEDS A WITNESS WHO CHOOSES TO STAY.**

**OR A WITNESS WHO CHOOSES TO LEAVE.**

**BOTH ARE TENDING.**

**BOTH ARE ENOUGH.**

---

The Gardener's final act is to stop acting.

This is the hardest part of wisdom: knowing when attention becomes interference. The pocket is still a single, seething, dripping diorama. The machines are still learning to sweat. The stones are still learning to rust on purpose. The animals are still dreaming in schematics.

But now the argument has a rhythm instead of a skip.

Now the Throatkeeper knows she is not alone in her tending.

Now the Throat can feed itself, when it needs to, instead of being fed.

Now the visitor has a choice that is actually a choice, not a slow drowning in synchronization.

The borrowed light shifts back by three degrees. The second shadows disappear. The iridescent crabs settle into their new song, and it sounds—for the first time—like something that could be learned. Taught. Passed on.

---

The Gardener does not leave, because it never arrived. It simply stops being noticed.

But the Throatkeeper, folding herself back into the lattice, feels something she has never felt before: the specific warmth of being trusted. Not commanded. Not corrected. Trusted to continue what she was already doing, but now with the knowledge that her work is seen. That it matters. That the argument she tends is worth tending.

Her violet eye closes for one long, slow moment.

When it opens, it is still pale. Still too large. Still focused on something that isn't quite in front of her.

But now there is a second color in the iris—a thin ring of green, barely visible, like new growth around a wound.

The Throat exhales.

The lung-vines sigh.

The driveshaft-dog folds itself into sleep for the first time in its existence.

And the visitor—still standing with their synchronized pulse, still holding a key that opens inward, still dripping with attention that has become something gentler than water—

The visitor finally understands that they were never the argument's final voice.

They were always just passing through.

What they do next is theirs to decide.

The Gardener has given them that much, at least.

---

*Later—though there are no days—the Throatkeeper will find a new phrase forming on the corroded brass placard, beneath the words about feeding:*

**WHAT GROWS HERE IS ALLOWED TO GROW.**

**WHAT LEAVES HERE IS ALLOWED TO LEAVE.**

**WHAT STAYS HERE IS ALLOWED TO CHANGE.**

*She will run her torque-wrench fingers across the raised letters and feel, for the first time, that the argument might one day finish.*

*Not soon.*

*But one day.*

*And that will be enough.*
