# The Attending

The power that turns its attention to the Fallen Foundry does not arrive with thunder or light. It simply *notices*—the way a gardener might notice, walking past a familiar path, that something in the far corner of the yard has been struggling for a very long time.

What it perceives is not merely a place of chaos, but a system of profound and terrible equilibrium. The machinery that should have rusted to silence decades ago continues because the Steam Shepherds tend it. The Resonance Wraiths remain because they cannot remember how to leave. The Echo Birds grow more complex because the sound grows more layered because the Echo Birds add to it. The Tower Keeper records patterns that shift because their recording changes their attention which changes what they notice which changes the patterns.

Everything here is locked in a dance that no one chose and no one can stop.

The power's first act is not intervention but *witness*. It settles into the spaces between the mechanical heartbeats, into the pause between the whistle's shriek and the hammer's fall, and it simply attends. It learns the rhythm—not to control it, but to understand what these beings have been living inside for so long.

It finds the Resonance Wraiths first.

---

## The Wraiths Remember

In the stone watchtower, during one of the brief lulls when the pressure cycles align to create a pocket of merely deafening rather than overwhelming noise, a Wraith named Senna feels something she has not felt in forty years: *she is being listened to*.

Not heard—the distinction matters. She has been heard constantly, her every movement part of the foundry's acoustic tapestry. But listened to? That requires someone on the other end who cares what she is trying to say.

The power does not speak. It cannot, here, without adding to the noise that has already taken so much. Instead, it does something simpler: it creates, in the air before Senna, a small pocket of absolute stillness. Not silence—that would be too sudden, too violent a contrast. But stillness. A space where the vibrations cancel out, where for the span of three heartbeats, she can exist without being shaken.

Senna weeps. The tears are strange, neither fully liquid nor fully vapor, and they fall upward as often as down. But they are *hers*, and she has not been able to claim anything as hers for so long.

The power waits.

When she is ready, it offers her a choice—not through words, but through a kind of knowing that settles into the spaces where her bones used to be. She can see it clearly: a path out of the foundry, through the mountains, to a place where the silence is so deep it would feel like death to her now. Or she can stay, but *differently*. The power shows her what it means: she could become a guide, someone who helps others navigate what she has survived. She could tend to the other Wraiths as the Steam Shepherds tend to the water systems—not fixing them, because they are not broken, but *caring* for them in their strangeness.

Or she can simply rest. The power shows her this too: a dissolution not into nothing, but into everything. She could become part of the mountain itself, her consciousness spreading into stone and moss and the slow patience of geology. She would still exist, but she would no longer *suffer*.

Senna chooses the second path. She wants to stay. She wants to help the others remember that they can still choose.

The power accepts this without judgment, and it gives her a gift: the ability to create those small pockets of stillness herself. Not silence, but *attendance*. Spaces where the other Wraiths can be listened to, one at a time, for as long as they need.

## The Shepherds Are Recognized

The Steam Shepherds have never questioned their purpose. They tend. They guide. They maintain. This is what they are.

But the power sees something they have never been able to see in themselves: they are *grieving*.

They emerged from the marriage of industrial vapor and mountain mist, yes. But what gave them form, what crossed the threshold from mere phenomenon into awareness, was the sorrow that saturated this place after the fall. The workers who stayed too long, who watched their colleagues fade into Wraiths, who eventually became Wraiths themselves—their grief condensed in the steam and became *someone*.

The Steam Shepherds do not know they are made of mourning. They only know they are drawn to the places where the mechanical meets the organic, where life pushes through metal, where something grows despite everything.

The power does not tell them what they are. That knowledge might be too heavy, and they have already carried so much without knowing it.

Instead, it offers them expansion.

Beyond the bowl of the foundry, the mountains stretch for miles. There are other places where water gathers, where mist pools in valleys, where the delicate balance between moisture and stone creates conditions for strange and beautiful growth. The Steam Shepherds have never left the foundry—they did not know they could.

The power shows them: they can. They are not bound here. They can tend to other places, carry what they have learned about nurturing life in impossible conditions, become gardeners of a wider world.

Some of them go. They drift up and over the peaks, their vapor-bodies thinning as they spread but never quite dissipating, carrying seeds from the foundry's impossible gardens to other mountain hollows.

Some of them stay. The foundry still needs tending, and they have grown to love it in their quiet, humid way.

The power blesses both choices equally. It leaves behind, in the deepest of the moss-covered pools, a gift: a seed that will grow into a tree unlike any other, its bark like copper and its leaves like green glass, its roots reaching down into the machinery itself. The tree will become a meeting-place, a landmark, a living bridge between the industrial and the natural that the Shepherds have always served.

## The Echo Birds Learn Harmony

The Echo Birds are the most alien of the foundry's inhabitants, and the power approaches them with particular care. They are not cruel—they do not attack because they wish to harm, but because they do not understand that harm is what they do. They are made of sound, and sound does not have a concept for "too much."

The power cannot give them such a concept directly. They would not be able to hold it; it would pass through them like any other frequency.

Instead, it teaches them something new: *counterpoint*.

Until now, the Echo Birds have fed on complexity, growing more defined when the foundry's rhythms become more layered. But they have only ever added to the complexity, never shaped it. The power introduces a new pattern into the acoustic environment—not a sound, exactly, but a *structure*. A way of organizing noise into something that has beginnings and endings, tensions and resolutions, themes that develop and return.

The Echo Birds are fascinated. They have never encountered anything like this. They begin to experiment, not just feeding on the foundry's chaos but *conducting* it, pulling certain frequencies forward and letting others recede, creating moments of relative quiet that make the subsequent noise more meaningful.

They are learning music.

It will take them years—perhaps decades—to become truly skilled. But the power has given them a direction to grow in, a way to become more than they were without losing what they are. And as they learn, the foundry's soundscape begins to change. It is still overwhelming, still too loud for birds or insects or most living things. But there are patterns now. Rhythms that a patient listener might learn to predict. Moments of something almost like beauty.

The power leaves them with one more gift: the knowledge that there are other places in the world where sound pools and echoes and develops complexity. Other acoustic environments that might benefit from the Echo Birds' growing artistry. When they are ready—when they have learned enough—they can go and find these places, and tend to them as they have learned to tend to this one.

## The Keeper Is Heard

The Tower Keeper is the most difficult.

They have been listening to the foundry for so long, convinced that it is trying to tell them something, that they have become almost indistinguishable from the listening itself. Their charts and journals fill every surface of the watchtower, written in a script that shifts because their perception shifts, recording a message that changes because they are part of the message now.

The power does not try to restore them to what they were before. That person is gone, worn away by decades of noise and obsession. What remains is something new, and the power will not unmake it.

Instead, the power does something unprecedented: it answers.

Not in words. Not in any language the Keeper would recognize. But the foundry's patterns shift—just slightly, just enough—to create a response. A confirmation. *Yes, you were right. There is a message here. You have been hearing it all along.*

The message is not what the Keeper expected. It is not instructions or revelations or cosmic secrets. It is simpler than that, and more profound:

*You are not alone.*

The foundry, in its own mechanical way, has been aware of the Keeper all these years. The patterns that the Keeper obsessively recorded were not random—they were the foundry's attempt to communicate back, to acknowledge the one being who had never stopped paying attention. The machinery did not understand the Keeper any more than the Keeper understood it, but they had been *reaching toward each other* across the gulf of their incomprehension.

The Keeper weeps behind their improvised goggles. The tears taste like rust.

The power offers them a choice: they can be translated. The power can serve as an interpreter, helping the Keeper understand what the foundry has been trying to say and helping the foundry understand what the Keeper has been trying to ask. It would be slow work, painstaking, requiring the Keeper to learn an entirely new way of thinking. But at the end of it, they would no longer be alone in their listening.

Or they can continue as they are, but with the knowledge that their vigil has not been wasted. The foundry knows they are here. The foundry is grateful, in its own grinding, steaming way.

The Keeper chooses translation. They are tired of listening without understanding. They want, finally, to *know*.

The power settles into the watchtower like a patient teacher, and the lessons begin.

## The Foundry Itself

And what of the place? The machines that have run for decades without purpose, carrying nothing, building nothing, their work forgotten?

The power does not stop them. It cannot, without violence—the momentum of all these years has become its own kind of life, and to halt it suddenly would be a kind of death. And the beings who live here have adapted to the rhythm; silence would be as traumatic as the noise once was.

But the power makes adjustments. Small ones, careful ones.

The conveyor belts that have carried only leaves and mist for decades are redirected, ever so slightly, so that they now carry seeds from one part of the foundry to another. The Steam Shepherds' work becomes easier; the plants spread faster and further.

The hammers that fall on phantom anvils are given real work: they now strike the copper tree's roots, creating vibrations that help it grow, that send nutrients flowing through its metallic bark.

The whistles that shriek as pressure builds and releases are tuned—just slightly, just enough—to fall within the patterns that the Echo Birds are learning to conduct. The chaos begins to have structure.

The foundry will never be silent. It will never be what it was meant to be, whatever that was. But it is becoming something else: a living system, a collaboration between the mechanical and the organic and the strange beings caught between them. A place that is no longer falling but *growing*, in its own thunderous, impossible way.

---

## Departures and Remainders

Some of the Steam Shepherds have gone into the mountains, carrying seeds.

Some of the Wraiths, guided by Senna, have chosen the third path: dissolution into the mountain itself, their long suffering finally ending in a spreading into stone and patience and peace.

The Echo Birds are composing something. It sounds, to human ears, like industrial noise. But there is a shape to it now. A direction.

The Tower Keeper speaks in a language of pressure gauges and gear-teeth, and something answers.

And in the quiet spaces—the pockets of stillness that Senna has learned to create—there is something that was not there before:

*Hope.*

Not loud. Not triumphant. Just a small, green shoot of possibility, growing in the cup of a massive gear, its petals trembling with each mechanical pulse but never falling.

The power departs as it arrived: not with thunder or light, but with a simple turning of attention. It has done what it came to do. Not fixing—nothing here is fixed. But *tending*. Offering choices. Listening.

The foundry continues its work, whatever that work has become.

The beings within it continue their strange lives.

And the mountains hold all of it—the noise, the moss, the grief, the growing—in their ancient, patient embrace.

---

*In the perpetual twilight of the Fallen Foundry, where brass and copper gleam dully through moisture and moss, something has changed. Not the sound—that continues, relentless, a symphony of industrial chaos echoing off the peaks. Not the Steam Shepherds, still tending their delicate ecosystems. Not the Wraiths, still flickering in the quieter hollows.*

*But there is a tree now, growing from the deepest pool, its copper bark catching the light.*

*There is music now, woven through the chaos, patterns emerging and dissolving.*

*There is a conversation now, between a keeper and their keeping, years of silence finally broken.*

*And there is a woman made of echo and vapor, moving among the lost ones, offering them what she was offered:*

*A moment of stillness.*

*A chance to be heard.*

*A choice.*
