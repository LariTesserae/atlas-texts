# What the Wise Power Sees

The attention settles slowly, the way light gathers in the refractive eyes of the moss-clad dwellers themselves—not arriving, exactly, but *recognizing*. Becoming present to what was always there.

And what it finds is this: a world that works.

Not perfectly. Not without suffering—the keening after predator-loss still echoes in certain trunk-hollows, and the cold takes the very young and the very old with regularity that would break hearts more accustomed to gentler places. But *works*, in the deep sense. The dwellers have found their fit. They listen to the world and the world, in its strange not-quite-sentient way, pulses back. They take little and mend what they take from. Their myths hold the right kind of humility—gods as travelers, not makers; ancestors honored for cleverness rather than dominion.

The wise power contemplates intervention and finds itself hesitating.

---

## The Shape of Hesitation

There is a temptation—felt even by the benevolent, perhaps *especially* by the benevolent—to smooth what is rough. To warm the cold pockets, stabilize the strange gravity, guide the predators elsewhere, teach metallurgy and medicine and all the arts that make life longer and less precarious.

But the power is wise, and wisdom here means seeing clearly what such gifts would cost.

The dwellers' bodies are *made* by this place—the long limbs, the light-gathering eyes, the flexible spines shaped by generations of crossing void and contorting through living tunnels. Their social bonds flex and re-form because permanence is impossible here; their stories live in sung memory because writing would dissolve. Even their relationship with fear is calibrated to this ecology: the predator-stillness, the grief that is brief but total, the forward motion that follows.

To change the world is to unmake the people. Not destroy them—but dissolve what they are into something else, something fitted to a different place that would no longer be *this* place.

The power sees, too, the subtle wrongness woven through—the lingering leaps, the impossible evaporation, the Weight that the dwellers sometimes try to appease. This is not malfunction. It is the signature of a world still settling into itself, still young in some fundamental way. The dwellers have already learned to read it, to work with it rather than against it. Their rituals of harmonization are not superstition but a kind of wisdom—an acknowledgment that they do not fully understand the ground beneath them, and a practice of remaining open to its strangeness.

---

## What the Power Does

First: **it watches longer**.

Not surveillance—something closer to the attention a parent gives a child absorbed in play, or a gardener gives a seedling that has found its own way toward light. The watching itself is a kind of gift: the world and its dwellers, held in a regard that does not demand change, that simply *witnesses* the improbable achievement of life finding its shape in the hollow dark.

---

Second: **it touches, very lightly, the places where suffering exceeds necessity**.

Not the cold—the cold is constituent, load-bearing. Not the predators—they are part of the web that keeps the grazers moving, the mosses cycling, the whole system in motion. Not even death itself, which the dwellers have learned to metabolize into brief keening and forward motion.

But there are edges.

A burrowing worm, tunneling in its blind way, is about to breach a trunk-hollow where a family shelters with three young children. The breach would not merely kill—it would kill in a way that serves no ecological purpose, teaches no lesson, opens no new path. Simple bad luck, the kind that accumulates into extinction if it happens often enough.

The power *shifts* something. Not the worm's path—that would be too much, would begin to teach the dwellers that worms can be predicted when they cannot. Instead: a vibration, the kind the dwellers already listen for, reaching the family a few heartbeats earlier than it would have. Enough time to move. Enough warning to fit the world they already know.

The family scrambles to a neighboring hollow. The trunk breaches behind them. They will tell the story later, singing it into the moss-walls: *the Weight warned us, the Deep Surmounting was merciful*. They are not wrong, exactly.

---

Third: **it offers a single small gift, placed where it might be found**.

In a misted vale, after a burst of rain, a young dweller—one of the solitary walkers, out for their first long season alone—finds something wedged in a crook of root and vine.

It is a seed.

Not from any plant they know, though it resembles the spores of the bioluminescent fungi. When held, it warms slightly—just enough to notice, not enough to startle. When pressed to moss, it pulses in rhythm with the living substrate around it.

The young dweller does not know what it is. But they are a dweller, and dwellers do not hoard mysteries. They carry it home—or what passes for home in the current cycle—and show it to the eldest of their cluster, who hums over it thoughtfully for a long time.

"Plant it," the eldest says finally. "But not in the hollow. At the edge, where the wind crosses. See what it wants to become."

What it wants to become: a new kind of growth, slow at first, then steady. A plant that glows with its own warmth—faint, faint, barely enough to notice. But where it grows, the cold bites a little less, and the moss around it stays damp a little longer. It does not spread aggressively; it does not outcompete. It simply *offers*, to those who find it, a small expansion of the possible.

Over generations, the dwellers learn to cultivate it. Not through any instruction—the power gives no manual, no revelation—but through the same patient attention they give everything else. They discover that the warm-glow seeds can be carried, that they root best in wounded places where plant-matter has recently split, that they seem to thrive on the hummed songs the dwellers use for mourning.

A strange symbiosis develops: the dwellers tend the warmth-plants, and the warmth-plants make certain journeys a little less deadly, certain shelters a little more forgiving. The practice becomes part of the culture, another thread in the sung memory. *The first seed came after the long rain, and the walker carried it home, and the eldest said plant it where the wind crosses...*

---

Fourth: **it remains**.

Not always watching—the power has other concerns, other worlds, other lives that need different kinds of attention. But *available*. Present in the particular way that the dwellers already speak of when they call upon the Weight or the Deep Surmounting: not an intervening god, not a maker or ruler, but a *witness* whose regard might sometimes translate into a vibration arriving early, a seed appearing where it might be found, a falling vine slowing just slightly before it strikes.

The dwellers do not come to worship the power. They are not the worshipping kind; their gods are travelers, not thrones. But their rituals of harmonization shift, subtly, over the generations. They begin to include a moment of *greeting*—a hum pitched at the edge of hearing, directed outward toward the cold and the void and whatever might be listening.

*We are here*, the greeting says. *We are still crossing. If you are watching, we see you seeing.*

The power, when it attends, hums back.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not reveal itself. The dwellers' theology is their own, shaped by their own experience and need; to insert itself as a character in their myths would be to colonize their inner world in a way that would diminish them.

It does not accelerate. The temptation to teach, to uplift, to compress ten thousand years of development into a generation—this temptation is real, and the power feels it. But acceleration is a kind of violence. It would break the deep fit between people and place, would create a culture that no longer knew how it came to be what it is.

It does not protect from death. Death is not the enemy here; meaningless death, death that closes paths rather than opening them, death that accumulates into extinction—*that* is worth forestalling, when it can be done subtly. But the predators still hunt, the cold still bites, the falls from arch to void are still final. The dwellers' grief is still brief and total, and their forward motion still follows.

It does not promise. The power knows that it may be called away, that its attention may be needed elsewhere, that the dwellers may face catastrophes beyond any subtle intervention. To promise permanence would be to lie, and to lie would be to betray the relationship before it has properly begun.

---

## What Remains

The hollow world continues, vast and cold and alive, plant-matter arching across emptiness, fog drifting through luminous dark. The dwellers continue—leaping, listening, singing their stories into the moss-walls, tending now the small warmth-plants that have become part of their ecology, part of their memory.

They are not saved. They were never in need of saving. They are *accompanied*—lightly, from a distance, with respect for their strangeness and their fit.

And the power, when it turns its attention here, finds something like joy: the specific joy of watching life solve itself, of seeing a people become who they are through their own long effort, of being *allowed* to witness without being *required* to intervene.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not rescue, but presence. Not transformation, but the gentlest possible expansion of what is already there.

The wind shifts. The dwellers listen. Somewhere, in a crook of wounded plant-matter, a warmth-seed pulses faintly, waiting for the singer who will find it and carry it home.
