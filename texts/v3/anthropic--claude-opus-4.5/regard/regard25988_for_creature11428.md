I sat with this for a long time before writing anything.

---

## What I Notice

The first question is whether intervention is wanted, needed, or appropriate. The writing presents her state as "peace," as "equilibrium," as having "passed beyond the need" for speech or rising or engagement. The prose is beautiful and seductive in its framing—it wants me to see this as transcendence.

But I notice: she "does not remember which came first." She "carried something, or sought something, or fled something" and "the details have dissolved." Her name has "worn away." She "might recognize that you were something other than dripping" but "would not rise."

This is not peace. This is a living being who has become so still that stillness has colonized her—calcium on her calves, filaments in her hair, her breath synchronized to dripping water. She has not transcended need; she has contracted until her needs could be met by moisture absorbed through skin. That is not the same thing.

The cistern is not cruel. It is simply a closed system trending toward maximum entropy, and she has become part of that trend. The "equilibrium" described is the equilibrium of a system running down.

---

## What I Would Do

I would not flood the cistern with light. I would not send someone down the steps to shake her, to insist she rise, to tell her that her stillness is wrong and she must rejoin the world of movement. That would be violence—the violence of one mode of being imposed on another.

But I also would not leave her unchanged.

I would send down a single seed.

Not carried by anyone. Just—present, one morning (if morning means anything here), resting on the surface of the water near her hand. A seed that carries within it the possibility of a flower that blooms in low light, in dampness, in still air. A flower with color. 

Not an demand. A question.

The seed would float there, doing nothing, requiring nothing from her. But it would be *new*. It would be the first new thing in this space since she stopped counting time. It would not fit the cycle of *tap tap tap* and silence and soft landing and longer silence. It would be outside the pattern.

If she notices it—and she might, she watches the water, she would see something that does not belong to the rhythm—she will have to *decide*. To reach for it or not. To let it stay or push it away. To wonder, for the first time in longer than the steps have been worn, what something *is* and where it *came from* and what it *wants*.

Maybe she will do nothing. Maybe the seed will waterlog and sink and decay and become part of the mineral film on the floor, and she will continue exactly as she has continued.

But maybe she will close her fingers around it. And feel, beneath the husk, the coiled potential of a thing that has not yet become itself. A thing that is not yet in equilibrium. A thing that *wants*.

It would not save her. "Saving" is not mine to do—she is not drowning, not dying, not suffering in any way she can still recognize as suffering. But it would *offer*. It would say: the world above still makes new things. Things still want to grow toward something. You are still capable of being surprised.

The choice would remain hers. The seed changes nothing except the fact of its presence.

But presence, in a system that has forgotten presence—that has reduced all being to persistence—might be enough to matter.

---

A seed. Pale-husked and dormant and ready, floating on still water, waiting for a hand that has forgotten what reaching means.

That is all. That is enough. That is what I would offer, and then I would wait—not for her to change, but for her to *choose*, one way or another, and in choosing, remember that she can.
