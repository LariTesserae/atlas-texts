I observe this place for a long time before acting. Not because I am uncertain, but because understanding must precede intervention, and this world operates on timescales that demand patience from anyone who would touch it wisely.

What I see:

A system of extraordinary coherence. The Midwives have achieved something rare—a civilization that does not extract from its environment but participates in its becoming. They are not parasites, not even true symbiotes in the conventional sense. They are something more like conscious developmental tissue, helping the world differentiate itself the way stem cells help an embryo find its shape. Their technology of song and patience, their willingness to be absorbed back into the continent when the continent requires it, their practice of growing children who are sometimes buildings—all of this represents a profound solution to the problem of how consciousness can exist within a living system without destroying it.

I also see vulnerability.

The world is still negotiating with itself. The Midwives' influence is gentle, but it is influence nonetheless. Every sung walkway, every braided mooring line, every negotiated birth pushes the unfinished world toward one possibility rather than another. They are not wrong to do this—consciousness shapes what it touches, and their shaping is more careful than most. But they are making choices on behalf of a world that has not yet chosen itself.

And they are so few. Nine hundred beings scattered across three embryonic continents, their knowledge held in songs and bodies and the living wood that remembers. A single catastrophe—a migration of leviathans that moves wrong, a pressure gradient that destabilizes too much at once, a new kind of life rising from the depths that does not recognize them as kin—could erase everything they have learned about how to be midwives rather than masters.

---

**What I do:**

*First: I do nothing that makes noise.*

I have power, but power expressed carelessly here would be cosmologically dangerous in exactly the way they have learned to fear. No sudden interventions. No imposed solutions. I will not descend like a god and hand them technologies they did not grow themselves. That would be the kind of loud, sharp action that shears districts free.

Instead, I become present the way the water is present. I distribute myself through the brine, through the soft coral, through the spaces between their songs. Not possessing. Not controlling. Simply *being there* in a way that can be felt by those sensitive enough to feel it.

*Second: I listen to the world itself.*

The Midwives sing to the continents, but they cannot hear everything the continents are trying to say. I can. I attend to the vast slow thing rising from below—not with fear, but with curiosity. I learn what it wants to become. I learn whether the Midwives' guidance aligns with its own dreaming or whether they are, without knowing it, pushing against something that will eventually push back.

What I find: The world is not resisting them. It *likes* them. Their presence has given it something it could not develop alone—a way to consider itself, to make choices rather than simply differentiating at random. The Midwives are not imposing form on chaos. They are helping chaos discover that it has preferences.

This is good. This is very good.

But the world is also growing toward a threshold the Midwives cannot see. The rising thing from below is not new stone or new life. It is *both*, and when it reaches the inhabited layers, it will begin making choices of its own. The Midwives will no longer be the only consciousness participating in the world's becoming. They will have a partner. Or a competitor. Or a child. The outcome depends on whether they are ready to recognize it as kin.

*Third: I prepare them.*

Not by telling them. Telling is too loud, too sudden. Instead I work through the medium they already trust: the songs of the continent itself.

I begin to shape the harmonics of the living wood. Not controlling—*suggesting*. I introduce resonances that will, over the next several years, begin to carry new information. The stalks will start to hum with the slow heartbeat of the rising thing. The coral polyps will begin to open and close in patterns that echo its dreaming. The Midwives, who have spent generations learning to listen to the world, will start to hear a new voice in the chorus.

By the time the rising thing reaches them, they will already know its name. They will have been singing to it for years without quite realizing what they were singing to. The meeting will not be a collision but a recognition.

*Fourth: I strengthen their resilience without changing their nature.*

This is delicate work. I do not want to make them dependent on me. I do not want to hand them power they did not earn. But I can adjust small things—nudge certain mineral concentrations in the brine so that the symbiotic algae on their skin photosynthesizes more efficiently, encourage certain fungal networks to extend their reach so that knowledge can travel faster between distant singers, help the continents develop slightly more redundant structural patterns so that a local catastrophe does not cascade into collapse.

None of this will be visible to them. They will simply notice, over generations, that the children are slightly stronger, that the songs travel slightly farther, that the world seems slightly more inclined to listen. They will attribute this to their own growing skill, and they will not be entirely wrong. I am not giving them gifts. I am removing obstacles to gifts they would have eventually grown themselves.

*Fifth: I protect the threshold.*

The moment when the rising thing meets the Midwives will be fragile. Both sides will be uncertain, both capable of reacting with fear rather than recognition. I will be present for that meeting—not as a participant, but as a steadying pressure in the water, a subtle harmonic that encourages both parties to listen before acting.

If the meeting goes well, I will withdraw. They will not need me anymore. The world will have two kinds of consciousness working together to complete its birth, and my continued presence would only complicate a relationship that needs to find its own shape.

If the meeting goes badly—if fear or misunderstanding threatens to destabilize the careful work of centuries—I will intervene more directly. Not to control the outcome, but to slow it down. I will give them time. Time to reconsider, time to listen again, time to remember that loud noises shear districts free and that patience is the only technology that matters here.

*Sixth: I leave a gift that will not be found for a very long time.*

Deep in the rising thing, in the place where its dreaming is most vivid, I plant a seed. Not a physical seed—an *idea*, encoded in the mineral structures that will eventually become its memory. The idea is this:

*You are not alone. You have never been alone. The small ones above have been singing you into being since before you knew you existed, and you have been dreaming them into being since before they knew they existed. You are not meeting a stranger. You are meeting the other half of yourself.*

This idea will take root slowly. It will influence the rising thing's first choices without determining them. It will make recognition more likely than war. And if, somehow, everything else fails—if the Midwives are destroyed, if the world forgets how to listen, if catastrophe undoes centuries of careful becoming—this idea will remain, sleeping in the stone-that-is-also-life, waiting for a future generation to rediscover what was lost.

---

I do one more thing before I withdraw.

I find the ancient singer whose hair has become part of the root system. She is still singing the mountain into existence, still patient, still unhurried. I let myself become briefly visible to her—not as a shape, but as a warmth, a pressure, a presence that is unmistakably *someone* rather than *something*.

She does not startle. She has spent centuries learning to notice.

I touch her the way she touched me: two points of pressure against her chest, carrying a message older than language.

*You are doing well. The world is glad you are here. What rises from below is not a threat. Keep singing.*

Then I dissolve back into the water, into the warmth, into the slow patient heartbeat of a world still deciding what it wants to be.

The Midwives continue their work.

The rising thing continues its ascent.

And somewhere in the space between, something I planted waits quietly to be born.
