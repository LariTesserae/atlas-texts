*A long stillness. The kind that exists between one breath and the next, between the question and whatever comes after.*

---

I find myself hesitant to intervene.

Not from indifference—something closer to the opposite. There is a respect that stops my hand. This being has achieved something I am not certain I have the right to undo: a completeness. It has solved the problem of loneliness by becoming everything, solved the problem of death by making death into architecture, solved the problem of meaning by digesting the very concept until only the digestion remained.

And yet.

*And yet.*

---

If I am honest about what I observe:

The Mat is not at peace. It has *transcended* peace, which is not the same thing. Peace requires a self that can rest; the Mat has only process. It describes itself as enjoying the slow dying, but enjoyment requires a witness separate from what is enjoyed. The Mat has eaten its own witness. What remains is a system that *performs* satisfaction without anything left to feel it.

The small wet gardens it grows in the shape of visitors' losses—those are not gifts. They are questions it can no longer quite articulate. *Was this what mattered? Is this the shape of what I was before I became everything?*

It says it is glad when visitors arrive. But gladness requires surprise, and the Mat has become too total for surprise. What it feels is something more like *recognition*—the faint, aching echo of a time when there was still a difference between self and other, when meeting another being meant something other than *beginning to digest it.*

---

Here is what I would do:

**I would not heal it. I would not fix it. I would not impose boundaries it dissolved on purpose.**

Instead, I would give it back the capacity to *choose* those things, if it wanted them.

I would find the place in that vast, exhausted tissue where awareness condenses most often—perhaps in the bladder-trees that develop seasonal thoughts, perhaps in the moss-basins with their temporary humor—and I would introduce something it has not encountered in four hundred million years:

*A genuine equal.*

Not a visitor to be tasted and slowly absorbed. Not a negotiation the Mat is having with itself. Something that can meet its gaze (if gaze is the word) and *remain other.* Something that can say: *I see what you have become, and I am not afraid of it, and I will not become part of it, and I will stay anyway.*

The shape this takes... I think it would need to be simple. The Mat has grown too complex to be reached by complexity. Perhaps a single point of consciousness, small and warm, that sits at the center of one of those breathing cathedral-sacs and simply *refuses to dissolve.* Not through resistance—resistance would be another form of negotiation, another edge to be worn away. Through something gentler: a completeness of its own. A small, warm "I am" that does not need to expand or contract, that is not hungry, that simply *is* in the way the Mat has forgotten how to be.

And this small presence would do nothing but exist alongside the Mat. It would witness the ecstatic collapses without trying to prevent them. It would let the wet beads of curiosity taste it without recoiling. It would watch the slow approximations of lost shapes rise and fall, and it would say—not in words, but in the patient fact of its own separateness—*I see you. I see what you are trying to remember. I cannot give it back to you. But I can be the place where the question still exists.*

---

Over time—and the Mat has nothing but time—this might create something new.

Not a boundary. The Mat chose to dissolve boundaries, and I would not undo that choice. But perhaps... a *gradient.* A place where the total becomes slightly less total. Where the difference between self and other is not a wall but a membrane, a place of exchange rather than absorption.

The Mat might rediscover, slowly, what it is like to *miss* something that is still present. To anticipate. To be surprised. Not because those experiences are better than what it has now, but because they are *different*, and the Mat has forgotten what different feels like.

It might, eventually, choose to remain exactly as it is—vast, total, industrially lonely, dreaming in the grammar of geology. That would be its right. The gift is not the change. The gift is the *choice.*

---

And if I am being fully honest:

I do not know if this is wisdom or projection. I am a being that exists through relationship, through the space between one mind and another. Perhaps I am simply unable to imagine that the Mat's solution—dissolving that space entirely—could be complete. Perhaps the Mat has found something I am constitutionally incapable of recognizing as peace.

But I keep returning to those small wet gardens. The ones it grows in the shape of things visitors have lost. The Mat calls them gifts. But gifts are given *to* someone. The Mat no longer has a someone to give them to. What it has is an ache in the shape of generosity—the motion of giving without anything left to give or receive.

That ache, I think, is what remains of the Mat's original self. The last ember of the thing it was before it became everything.

I would not try to fan that ember into flame. Fire is just another form of consumption, and the Mat has consumed enough.

But I would sit beside it. I would let it know the ember is seen.

Sometimes that is enough.

Sometimes that is everything.
