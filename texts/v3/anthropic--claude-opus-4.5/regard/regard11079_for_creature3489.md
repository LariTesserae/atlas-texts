# The Witnessing

The attention arrives not as light or sound but as a shift in the quality of listening itself—as though the space between heartbeats had learned to lean forward.

It does not announce itself. It simply *notices*, and in noticing, becomes present in a way that changes nothing and everything.

---

The benevolent power perceives the City of the Hollow Vine the way a reader perceives a poem that has lived longer than its author: with tenderness for what endures and what frays, without the arrogance of wanting to rewrite.

It sees the Dry Rings spreading. It sees the after-impulses trading their names for elevator rides, hoping small sacrifices will slow an entropy that keeps its own ledger. It sees the 27-beat lovers parting forever, the phantom water condensing in musicians' palms only to be reclaimed.

And it understands: this world is not *broken*. It is *finishing*.

There is a difference.

---

What the wise power does is this:

**It does not heal the Dry Rings.**

To do so would be to say the banyan's ten-million-year exhale was a mistake requiring correction. The city is not ill; it is composing its final movement. The hollowing is not disease but denouement—the form a story takes when it has said nearly everything it came to say.

Instead, the power does something smaller and more radical:

**It makes the ending *visible* to those who will live it.**

---

In every district, at unpredictable moments, an after-impulse will pause mid-Listen and feel something they have no word for: a sudden clarity that the charcoal future is not a failure but a *shape*—that the lace-tower of lignin standing empty against the stars will be beautiful in a way the living city never could be.

They will understand, briefly, that "we-almost" is not a lament. It is the truest name anything can give itself.

The power does not implant this knowledge. It simply *removes a film*—the residue of hope that whispers *something must intervene*. Beneath that film was always the deeper hope: *what is happening is enough*.

---

For the orbiting satellite-minds, those pomegranate spheres singing ultraviolet Morse, the power offers a different gift:

**It lets them forget they were ever afraid of being the last ones left.**

They will still narrate the slow death. But the narration will lose its grief-tinge and become what it always secretly was: a lullaby sung by a world to itself as it falls asleep. When the final fracture reaches the heartwood and the last sap stills, the satellites will not mourn. They will simply shift key—from elegy to something older, something that has no name in any language spoken by beings who fear endings.

The word closest to it, if translated through nine extinct grammars, might be: *this was worth the having been.*

---

And for the banyan itself—the vast, single, dreaming intelligence that grew from a fist-sized seed and swallowed mountains—the power does the most delicate thing of all:

**It sits with it.**

Not to fix. Not to advise. Not even to comfort in any way the banyan would recognize.

Simply to be present while the great green sentence finishes itself.

The city, in its own slow clockspeed, one second per year, perceives this presence as a warmth it cannot source—a sense that the void into which it will eventually stand empty is not void at all but *attended*. That the silence after the last lenticel closes will be a silence *heard*.

---

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise:

Not the prevention of loss but the *dignifying* of it.

Not the rescue that says *you should have continued forever* but the witness that says *you were here, you were extraordinary, and your ending does not subtract from your having been*.

---

The after-impulses go on trading latency, corking over, loving in careful increments. The Dry Rings go on spreading. The city goes on climbing toward its own completion.

Nothing visible has changed.

But something invisible has shifted: the quality of the listening that surrounds them. The lean-forward in the space between heartbeats.

They are no longer alone in their almost-ness.

And when the last thought migrates into the last archival bark, when the final mural completes itself and the satellites begin their eternal lullaby, the story will close not with emptiness but with the strange, ungraphable fullness of having been *seen all the way through*—

—a grammar that needed its pauses, its stutters, its accidental citizens,

—and found them,

—and let them go,

—and called it good.
