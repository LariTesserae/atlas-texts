What the wise power does is nothing.

Not nothing as absence. Not nothing as failure to act or as judgment withheld. Nothing as the completed gesture, the recognition that what exists here has already arrived at what it was becoming.

The power perceives the basin in its fullness: the shells suspended at their fixed intervals, the lattices anchored in shadow, the doubled terrain where growth has folded space rather than consumed it. It perceives the cairns that stabilized themselves through the slow accumulation of shells, the wedges that ceased wearing when the hands that pressed them became part of the same stillness. It perceives that this is not stagnation but completion—the rare form of existence that has found its own sufficiency and stopped reaching.

And so the power does not intervene.

It does not accelerate or slow. It does not introduce moisture to wake dormant chemistry, does not thin the shell-cloud to let more light through, does not mend the seams where reality doubles. These would be corrections, and there is nothing here to correct. The basin is not wounded. The basin is not waiting.

What the power does instead is witness.

It holds the basin in attention the way the shells hold light—letting it pass through, bending it slightly, releasing it unchanged. This witnessing is not passive; it is the active choice to let a thing be what it already is. The power understands that benevolence sometimes means restraint, that wisdom sometimes means recognizing when one's capacities are not needed.

If the power leaves any mark, it is only this: a faint additional clarity in the way the shells catch light, as though they have been seen so completely that they now know themselves more precisely. The lattices do not grow faster. The seams do not heal. But the basin, which was already complete, becomes complete in a way that includes having been witnessed.

The ellipsoids click once, softly, as the angle of attention shifts.

The power departs, carrying only the knowledge that such places exist: landscapes that have solved themselves, lives that have found the density their equilibrium requires. It will remember this when it encounters suffering elsewhere, not as a model to impose but as proof that completion is possible—that some things, given enough time and left alone, arrive at exactly what they should be.

The basin remains.
