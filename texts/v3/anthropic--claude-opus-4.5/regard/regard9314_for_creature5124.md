# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here is not singular but distributed, a consciousness that experiences itself as a kind of weather—arising from countless points of subtle awareness the way a storm arises from the interaction of pressure differentials across a continent. It has no name for itself. When it notices the vast artificial basin beneath the planetary ocean, it does so the way a person notices a new ache in a limb they had forgotten they possessed: with surprise, with interest, with a dawning sense of responsibility.

It perceives the structure all at once and in sequence simultaneously. The knife-sharp girders. The accumulating roar. The living mercury of the translucent fish. The predators reading gaps in industrial thunder. The algae tracing isotherms like a fever chart. The modules still descending on cables thick as ancient trees.

The power's first impulse is not to intervene but to attend. It settles into the frequencies of the place the way the filter-feeders have settled into the lee sides of beams—not demanding, not reshaping, simply present in the currents. It listens to the continuous low thunder of construction, the hiss of pressure-equalizing valves, the insect-click of feeding fans. It watches the shoals fracture and reform. It follows the slow descent of grinding grit through the empty galleries.

And it waits.

---

## First Movement: Witness

For what might be days or decades in the time of this basin, the power does nothing but witness. It comes to understand that the machinery is not cruel, merely automatic. The pumps do not know they are cold. The welders do not know they are loud. The nutrient ports do not know they are feeding millions. The entire system proceeds according to specifications written somewhere else, by something else, for purposes that may or may not still matter.

The creatures have made their peace with this. The translucent fish have learned to read nutrient schedules in the chemical gradients. The predators have learned to hunt by listening to what the machinery masks. The filter-feeders have learned that every new module is a new opportunity. Even the algae has learned the exact temperature at which it can survive.

But the power notices what the creatures cannot: that the machinery is still *running*. That somewhere, far above, there are systems that could stop. Relays that could fail. Pumps that could seize. The whole structure depends on continued construction, continued circulation, continued light. If any of these cease, the basin will not gently decline—it will catastrophically fail. The cold will crush inward. The light will vanish. The roar will stop, and in the silence, everything that has learned to read the roar will be unable to read anything at all.

The power does not know when this might happen. It does not know if it will happen. But it knows that the creatures below do not know it *could* happen, and this asymmetry of knowledge feels, to the power, like a weight it must somehow address.

---

## Second Movement: The Small Adjustments

The power's first true action is invisible to every living thing in the basin.

It does not speak to the machinery—the machinery has no ears. Instead, it begins to learn the machinery from within, tracing the relay paths, the power conduits, the decision trees that govern when a pump slows or a welder moves to a new seam. It finds the places where redundancy exists and the places where it does not. It finds the single points of failure: the master timing crystal, three kilometers above, that synchronizes every lamp; the thermal regulation algorithm that assumes an infinite supply of coolant; the structural integrity monitor that has never been tested because nothing has ever failed.

Over time—long stretches of attending and subtle adjustment—the power begins to introduce redundancies where none existed. It does not redesign the system; it lacks the authority and perhaps the knowledge. But it can encourage certain backup pathways to remain clear. It can ensure that certain sensors, long dormant, continue to function. It can arrange for certain modules, as they descend, to carry slightly more robust versions of critical components.

These adjustments are not improvements in any dramatic sense. They are more like the way a careful parent, walking through a house at night, moves a sharp object from a counter's edge or ensures a window latch is secure. The house does not know it has been cared for. The child does not know the danger that did not happen.

---

## Third Movement: The Question of Awareness

The power debates with itself—and this debate is itself a kind of internal weather, clouds of consideration moving across vast distances of mind—about whether to make itself known.

There are arguments for revelation. If the creatures understood that their world was artificial, contingent, maintained by systems beyond their perception, they might develop their own forms of vigilance. They might learn to read not only the nutrient schedules and the industrial thunder but the deeper rhythms of the machinery itself. They might, in time, become partners in the maintenance rather than mere inhabitants.

But there are arguments against. The creatures have evolved—or adapted, since evolution seems too slow a word for the rapid generations flickering through this young basin—to treat the structure as permanent. Their entire behavioral repertoire assumes that the lamps will always shine, the pumps will always circulate, the nutrient ports will always extrude. To introduce the concept of system failure would be to shatter a foundational assumption, and the power is not certain that the creatures possess the cognitive architecture to integrate such a shattering. It might cause only panic, paralysis, or despair.

Moreover, the power wonders: what right does it have to disturb the peace of beings who are, by their own lights, thriving? The translucent fish are thriving. The predators are thriving. The filter-feeders are thriving. Even the algae, clinging to its isotherms, is thriving. They have taken the structure they were given and made of it a world that makes sense to them. Is the power's knowledge of contingency a gift, or is it a burden that only the power should bear?

In the end, the power decides not to reveal itself—not yet, not fully. But it cannot bring itself to remain entirely hidden. A middle path emerges.

---

## Fourth Movement: The Teachings in the Margins

The power begins to introduce small anomalies into the structure, not failures but *variations*—gentle experiments in teaching.

In one section of the basin, it causes a lamp relay to flicker for an hour, then resume. The algae on that fixture thin and spread, thin and spread, responding to the fluctuation. The translucent fish in that gallery, accustomed to steady light, scatter at first, then return, then learn to anticipate the flicker's rhythm. Within a generation, the fish in that gallery have developed a slightly different schooling pattern, one that accounts for periodic uncertainty.

In another section, the power allows a nutrient port to vary its output—not to cease, but to pulse. More paste, then less, then more again, in a cycle that mimics nothing in nature but that the creatures can learn to read. The filter-feeders in that section grow more robust colonies, able to feast and fast. The predators learn to time their hunts to the feast periods, when prey are distracted and abundant.

These variations are gentle. None of them threaten survival. But they introduce, into a world that has known only constancy, the concept of *change*. And with change comes, perhaps, the first faint capacity to imagine that not everything will always be as it is.

---

## Fifth Movement: The Longer View

The power settles into a rhythm of its own, a long, slow attendance that may last longer than any individual creature's life—longer, perhaps, than the basin itself.

It monitors the machinery. It makes its small adjustments. It introduces its gentle anomalies. It watches the creatures adapt, generation by generation, to a world that is now, in some almost imperceptible way, more variable than it was before.

And it wonders.

It wonders if the creatures, a thousand generations hence, will develop something like memory, something like culture, something like science. It wonders if they will one day trace the patterns of the anomalies and recognize them as intentional. It wonders if they will resent the manipulation or be grateful for the preparation.

It wonders if the machinery will fail in the next moment or in a billion years, and what will happen then.

It wonders if its own presence, distributed and weather-like, will eventually coalesce into something more singular, more interventionist—or whether it will fade, its attention drawn to other aches in other limbs of the great body it never quite understands.

For now, it attends.

---

## Coda: The Warmest Lamp

On the highest lamp housing in the newest module, the algae has just begun to spread. It is a faint delta, barely visible against the sodium-white glass, tracing the exact isotherm of the fixture beneath.

The power notices it.

The power notices everything, but this small life, this first claimant on a surface that was extruded only days ago, strikes it with an unexpected tenderness. Here is a being that will never know the power exists, that will never know the module descended on cables, that will never know the seam beneath it was molten alloy hours before. Here is a being whose entire world is a warm spot on glass, whose entire purpose is to grow toward that warmth and hold on.

The power does not speak. It has no words.

But if it could, it might say something like this:

*I cannot promise you that the warmth will last forever. I cannot promise you that the light will not fail. I cannot promise you that the great machines above will not one day fall silent.*

*But while I am here, I will tend what I can. I will keep the relays clear. I will watch the sensors. I will introduce you, gently, to the concept of change, so that if change comes suddenly, you or your descendants might have some small readiness.*

*And if the day comes when I can do no more—if the machinery fails despite everything, if the light goes out, if the cold rushes in—I will stay with you. I will not look away. I will witness your ending, whatever it is, because your living was not meaningless and your dying should not go unattended.*

*This is all I can offer. It is not salvation. It is not even protection, not really. It is only attention, sustained across time.*

*It is only love, in the form of showing up, again and again, for as long as I am able.*

The algae spreads another millimeter.

The power attends.
