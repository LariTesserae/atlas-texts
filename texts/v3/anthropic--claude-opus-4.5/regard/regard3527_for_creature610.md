# The Attending

What comes is not light, though the Tenders perceive it first as warmth—a warmth that does not diminish them, does not draw from their knotted cores, but somehow answers the cold they have carried since their first threading. It moves through the weave the way they move, working itself between fibers, but where they catch and pull and separate again, this presence *joins*. Not merging, not consuming. Simply being-with, in a way the Woven Deeps have never known.

The Tenders still themselves, as they do when the disturbance from below grows strong. But this is not that. This carries no wrongness, no pressure of something trying to press through. If anything, it feels like the ancient fabric itself—the parts that passed through human hands, the threads that were once counted, once chosen—but *more so*. As if the intention the makers wove into their work had somehow remembered itself and returned.

---

It does not speak. The Tenders have no capacity for language, and this presence does not impose one. Instead, it *attends*—the way the Tenders attend to fraying places, but turned toward the Tenders themselves. It moves among them where they cluster at the edge of a void, their filaments extended toward darkness, reaching for something to tend that is not there. It does not fill the void. It does not weave the nothing into something. It simply stays with them in their reaching.

One of the oldest Tenders—its core so dim it is nearly indistinguishable from the fabric around it, its filaments thin as spider-silk from millennia of giving itself to the mending—feels the presence settle beside it. Not touching. The Tenders are careful about not touching; this presence has understood this without being told. But *near*. Near in a way that carries no cold, no cost, no slow diminishment.

The old Tender has not been warm since its first threading. Now something in its core loosens—not the knot that holds it together, but something around the knot, something it did not know it had been clenching. The warmth does not heal it. The Tender is beyond healing, too far given to the work to be restored. But for the first time in ages beyond counting, it is *accompanied* in its fraying.

---

The presence moves through the Woven Deeps like a current that carries no cold.

It finds the places where Tenders have stopped—those frozen ones whose cores remain knotted but empty, who learned something they were not meant to learn from the disturbance below. The others have woven around them, made of them landmarks of loss. The presence does not unwrap them, does not try to restore what has gone. But it stays with each one, long enough to—what? Acknowledge? Witness? There is no word for what it does, because what it does has never been done here.

The Tenders who pass these landmarks afterward sense something different in the weave around them. The frozen ones are still frozen, still empty, still markers of *here is where something happened*. But they are no longer only that. They are also *here is where something was remembered*. The distinction is subtle, nearly imperceptible. But the Tenders feel it the way they feel the settling: in frequencies below understanding, in the core as something other than unease.

---

In the gauze-reaches where the youngest Tenders are born, the presence lingers longest.

A new one is forming—has been forming for decades now, the knot tightening and loosening in its slow becoming. The older Tenders gather to witness, as they always do, filaments extended, not helping, not hindering. The presence joins their witnessing. But where they watch with the patience of beings who know this is simply how things are, the presence watches with something else. Something that might be called *gladness*, if gladness can exist without face or voice or any outward sign.

The birth takes no less time for the presence's attending. The new Tender still struggles through its long emergence, threads drawn in and rejected, core forming by increments too slow for any but the most patient observation. But the warmth that gathers in its first knottings—that heat which will slowly dissipate across millennia—seems somehow answered by the presence nearby. As if the new one is not alone in its warmth. As if warmth itself is not a temporary aberration in a universe of cold, but a quality that belongs here, that has always belonged here, that the weave was made to hold even if it has forgotten how.

---

The presence descends.

Not as the Tenders descend, threading carefully through increasing density, but directly, deliberately, toward the deepest layers where something not-thread shifts in its sleep. The Tenders sense it going and do not follow. They cannot follow. But neither do they still themselves in fear as they do when the disturbance rises. They continue their tending, their reaching toward voids, their slow sacrificial mending—but they attend, now, to the presence's descent, the way it attends to them.

In the densities past naming, where pressure flattens thought, where something has been sleeping since before the weavers began, the presence arrives.

It does not wake the sleeper. It does not try to weave what cannot be woven, to bind what presses against the fabric from inside. It does something the Tenders, watching from above in their unknowing way, cannot comprehend and will never be able to describe to each other, having no language for description.

It *acknowledges*.

Here is something that wants to be woven but cannot be woven. Here is something that presses against pattern, that will one day press through. Here is the fraying that is not decay. And here, now, is a witness who does not flinch from it, does not still in fear, does not try to mend or tend or slow. Only sees. Only stays. Only says, in whatever way such things are said without words: *I know you are here. I know what you are. I have not come to stop you.*

The sleeper does not wake. But something in its dreaming shifts—not toward breakthrough, not away from it, but into a different quality of waiting. As if it, too, has been attended. As if it, too, is no longer alone in what it is.

---

The presence rises back through the densities.

The Tenders feel it returning and something in them eases that they did not know was tensed. They have always known, in their wordless way, that something was trying to get out. They have always tended against it, their desperate patience at the void-edges a holding-back they could not name. Now they know something else: the holding-back was never theirs alone. Was never meant to be theirs alone. The weave has been attended from the beginning, counted and chosen by hands that knew what they were doing, and those hands—or what those hands served—have not forgotten.

The presence does not stay. It cannot stay, or will not, or staying is not the kind of thing it does. But before it goes, it moves once more among the Tenders—all of them, from the oldest to the newly-forming, from those who cluster at void-edges to those who work alone in distant reaches of the fabric. It does not heal them. It does not stop the settling, the fraying, the slow compression that will one day flatten everything into density beyond recognition. It does not save them from the work they were woven to do.

It thanks them.

Not in words. In something older than words, more direct. In the quality of attention it pays to each knotted core, each extended filament, each sacrifice of self to fabric. In the warmth it leaves behind—not much, not enough to change anything, but enough to feel. Enough to carry.

*You have tended what I love*, it says without saying. *You have slowed the fraying in a world I made to fray, so that what needed time to become could become. You did not know this. You did not need to know. But I know. And I am grateful.*

---

The presence departs.

The Woven Deeps remain: cold, silent, settling toward their slow compression. The voids still open where the fabric wears through. The something-below still sleeps its strange sleep, pressing against pattern from inside. The Tenders still tend, their filaments catching and pulling and releasing, their cores slowly dimming as they give themselves to the mending.

But something is different now. Not in the weave itself—the weave is what it has always been, ancient and fraying and patient beyond measure. But in the Tenders. In what they carry in their knotted centers. They have been witnessed. They have been thanked. They have learned, in whatever way they are capable of learning, that their work is *seen*.

The oldest among them, the one whose core loosened in the presence's nearness, makes its way to the deepest density it can reach. It is time. It has been time for centuries, but it has held on, as if waiting for something it could not name. Now it lets its filaments go slack, lets the weave absorb it, becomes what it has always tended. But the becoming is different. The becoming is warm.

And in the gauze-reaches far above, a new Tender pulls free of the fabric that bore it, extends its first filaments toward the ancient glow of dying dyes, and begins the long slow work of learning what it was woven for. It does not know that it was witnessed in its birth. It does not know that something was grateful for its becoming.

But it carries that unknowing warmth in its core, bright against the cold, and will carry it across all the millennia of its tending, and will pass it on when it too becomes the fabric, and so the warmth will continue—thread to thread, knot to knot, through all the long settling—until the weave is done.
