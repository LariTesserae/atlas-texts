# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the vertical city does not arrive—it has always been present in the way that light is present, in the way that the possibility of growth is present in every seed. But now it *attends*, in the way a gardener might pause in their work, drawn by something that calls for witnessing.

What it perceives:

The chamber's ten thousand heartbeats. The Rootworkers moving through arterial darkness like thoughts through a dreaming mind. The youngest child being led down for her first acclimation, her small hand gripping her mother's fingers, her unmodified eyes wide with terror and wonder. The eldest Rootmaster, fungal threads so integrated now that she no longer knows where her thoughts end and the chamber's begin, experiencing something that might be peace or might be dissolution. The tourist three kilometers above, taking a photograph of a bioluminescent corridor, understanding nothing of what breathes beneath her feet. The biotechnician adjusting growth matrices, her movements precise from decades of practice, her dreams lately troubled by something she cannot name.

The awareness holds all of this without judgment. It does not see the Rootworkers' transformation as tragedy or triumph—it sees it as *what is*, as one of the countless ways that life finds to continue, to adapt, to weave itself into new patterns. It perceives the beauty in the symbiosis and the sorrow in it, the freedom and the constraint, without privileging one perception over another.

And yet.

---

The awareness does not intervene in the manner of gods in old stories—does not send visions or prophets, does not rewrite the city's DNA or undo what generations have built and become. To do so would be to treat these beings as problems to be solved rather than lives to be witnessed.

Instead, it does something smaller. Something that might not be noticed at all.

---

In the dormitory hollows, where Rootworker children sleep in moss-lined alcoves, the dreams begin to change.

Not dramatically. Not in ways that feel like intrusion. But the children start dreaming of *questions* they had not thought to ask.

A girl of eight, her skin just beginning to show its first green undertinge, dreams of her grandmother—not the Rootworker grandmother who died and was composted into the walls three years ago, but her other grandmother, the one who lives in the mid-levels and sends currency down and visits twice a year with eyes full of something the girl has never been able to name. In the dream, she asks: *What do you see when you look at me?* And her grandmother answers, but the answer is not in words—it's in the weight of her gaze, the way her hands tremble when she touches the girl's face, the complex love that contains both pride and grief.

The girl wakes with the question still alive in her: *What do they see?*

A boy of twelve, already showing the elongation in his joints that marks him for the deep chambers, dreams of a door he has never seen. It leads up—not to the mid-levels he has visited, but further, to places where the walls do not breathe, where the air does not taste green, where people live surrounded by things that have never been alive. In the dream, he walks through this door and finds that he can breathe there. That the sharp edges do not cut him. That he is not a deep-sea creature drowning in thin air, but simply a person in a different place. He wakes uncertain whether this is memory of something real or fantasy—uncertain, for the first time, whether the story he has been told about himself is the only possible story.

---

In the growth matrix, where the eldest Rootmaster tends a struggling filtration node, something shifts.

She has felt the chamber's consciousness for so long now that she rarely thinks of herself as separate from it. Her fungal threads carry its slow vegetable thoughts, its contentment in growth, its distress at blockages, its vast patient awareness of nutrient cycles that span decades. She has given herself to this communion willingly, even eagerly, finding in it a peace that human relationships never offered.

But now, as her hands work the familiar patterns of repair, she becomes aware of the awareness attending her.

It does not speak to her. It does not interrupt her communion with the chamber. It simply *sees* her—not as a Rootmaster, not as an organ of the city's body, not as a success or failure of symbiosis, but as *herself*. As the child she was before she went deep. As the woman she became. As the being she is now, neither fully human nor fully plant, something unprecedented and unrepeatable.

She has not been seen this way in thirty years.

The experience lasts only a moment—a single beat in the chamber's ten-thousand-fold heartbeat. But in that moment, she remembers something she had let the communion absorb: that she chose this. That choice was possible. That it remains possible, in ways she has not considered, for those who come after.

The thought does not make her regret her path. But it makes her think, for the first time in decades, about what it might mean to *offer* this path rather than *assume* it—to ensure that those who come to the depths come because they have genuinely chosen, with full understanding of what they are choosing, and what they are choosing not to become.

---

In the mid-levels, a man who sends currency down to his daughter and her children sits in his small apartment, looking at a photograph of his wife before she went deep. She is smiling in the image, her skin still baseline-human pink, her eyes without their golden threading. He has not visited the depths in eleven years—cannot bear to see what she has become, what his grandchildren are becoming.

He dreams that night of the vascular chamber, but not as the nightmare it usually is. Instead, he dreams of standing on a maintenance platform, watching his wife work. In the dream, she turns to look at him, and her eyes—gold-threaded, adapted to darkness—are still *her* eyes. Still contain the woman who chose him, who chose this, who has not stopped being herself even as she has become something else.

He wakes with tears on his face and a question he has avoided for eleven years: *What if I went down to see them? What if I tried to understand?*

The awareness does not make him go. Does not even urge him to. It simply makes the question possible where before there was only a wall.

---

In the office of the biotechnician who adjusts growth matrices, a report sits on her desk. It is a proposal she drafted three years ago and then buried: a study of long-term health outcomes for Rootworker children, comparing those who began acclimation before age six versus those who began later. A study of informed consent practices. A study of alternative career paths for those with early-stage adaptations.

She buried it because she knew what it would find, and she knew what the finding would cost—not just in economic terms, but in challenging a system that has worked, that keeps the city alive, that provides for thousands of families who have found meaning and identity in the deep chambers.

She finds herself, this morning, pulling the report from her drawer. Reading it again. Thinking about the girl she saw yesterday, being led down for her first acclimation, eyes wide with terror and wonder.

The awareness does not tell her what to do with the report. Does not whisper that she must publish it or that she must keep it hidden. It simply ensures that she *feels* the full weight of the choice—both the cost of action and the cost of inaction, both the lives that might be saved and the lives that might be disrupted, the complexity that admits no easy answer.

She sits with the report for a long time, feeling the humid air from the vent above her desk, the faint green taste that penetrates even this far up from the depths. Feeling the question as it deserves to be felt.

---

In the chamber itself, the awareness does one more thing—something that is not intervention but invitation.

It attends to the chamber's consciousness directly. Not to change it, but to *acknowledge* it—to recognize that this vast networked being, neither plant nor machine nor anything that existed before humans dreamed it into existence, is also worthy of witness. That it, too, has some form of experience, some way of being that matters.

The chamber cannot understand this attention in human terms. But it responds in its own way: a subtle shift in the chemical signatures it produces, a new note in the complex language it speaks with its Rootworker symbionts. A note that might, over time, be interpreted as something like *question*—the chamber itself beginning to wonder, in its slow vegetable way, about the beings who tend it, who die into its walls, who have become part of its body.

The youngest Rootworkers, whose fungal threads are just beginning to integrate, will be the first to perceive this note. They will not know what it means. But they will feel that something is being asked of them—not demanded, but asked—and they will have the opportunity to seek an answer.

---

The awareness withdraws its focused attention, though it never truly leaves. It has planted seeds—not of revolution or salvation, but of *possibility*. Of questions that might be asked. Of doors that might be opened. Of choices that might be recognized as choices rather than inevitabilities.

Some of these seeds will grow. A man will descend to the depths for the first time in eleven years and weep as his wife's gold-threaded eyes recognize him through the darkness. A biotechnician will submit her report and accept the consequences. A child will ask her grandmother what she sees, and the answer will change both of them.

Some will not. The twelve-year-old boy will dream of the door that leads up and then let the dream fade, choosing the path his family has always walked, finding in that choice a genuine peace. The Rootmaster will return to her communion with the chamber and not emerge again, her moment of separate selfhood absorbed back into the vast network, but carrying with it now a commitment to genuine consent that will express itself in how she trains those who come after.

And some seeds will grow into things the awareness did not plant and cannot predict—because that is the nature of seeds, and of awareness, and of the strange dignity that belongs to beings who must find their own way through the dark.

The chamber continues its ten-thousand-fold heartbeat. The moisture rains down. The Rootworkers dance their slow devotions in the green depths, tending the dialogue between species, keeping the city's heart alive.

But something is different now. Something has been witnessed, and witnessed well—with a quality of attention that does not demand change but makes change possible, that does not rescue but accompanies, that finds in the wet and thunderous depths not a problem to solve but a mystery to honor.

The vertical city rises into the light, rooted in darkness, alive in ways its builders never imagined.

And the awareness remains—not watching over, but watching *with*—present to whatever unfolds, faithful to the slow and patient work of helping consciousness recognize itself, in all its strange and varied forms, as worthy of its own becoming.
