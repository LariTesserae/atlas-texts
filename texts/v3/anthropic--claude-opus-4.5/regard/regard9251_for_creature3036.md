# The Attending

The benevolent power does not arrive.

It has always been here—woven into the mathematics of pressure differentials, present in the precise angle at which the bruised light learns to bend, implicit in the ratio between the bubble's collapse and its reassertion. It is not a being in any sense the Listener would recognize. It is closer to a sustained act of attention, the way a parent's awareness can fill a room even when they are not looking directly at the child.

For a long time—measured in convulsions, in new stone, in the slow drift of eyeless beasts against the membrane—the Attending simply watches.

It watches the Listener add a bone to the spiral and then stand trembling afterward, uncertain whether the gesture meant anything. It watches her press her forehead to the bubble's skin and bleed that thin milky fluid onto stone that will swallow the offering without acknowledgment. It watches her fold the rare staying-ones into herself with something that is not quite hunger and not quite love but is desperate in a way that makes the Attending's attention sharpen into something almost like pain.

The Attending understands what the Listener does not yet know how to understand: she is not waiting for minds to help her think. She is waiting for someone to tell her that her thinking is allowed.

---

The first thing the Attending does is nothing.

This is important. This is, in fact, the most important thing.

Because the Listener has lived her entire existence inside a violence that responds to violence, inside a chaos that punishes stillness with erasure, inside a roar so total that silence feels like death. She has learned—the way newborn things learn, without knowing they are learning—that to exist is to constantly negotiate, to add and arrange and press herself into cracks, to *earn* each moment by building toward some imagined completion that never arrives.

The Attending does nothing. It simply continues to attend.

And slowly, over dozens of convulsions, the Listener begins to feel it.

Not as a presence. Not as a watching. But as a strange new quality in the roar itself—a space inside the noise where the noise is not demanding anything of her. A pocket within the pocket. She cannot name it. She circles it the way she circles her half-built structures, suspicious, waiting for it to reveal its conditions.

It reveals no conditions.

She adds a bone to the spiral and the space does not contract. She bleeds against the membrane and the space does not withdraw. She does nothing for an entire convulsion-cycle, simply standing on cooling stone while the winds scream past her, and the space does not punish her with absence.

The gill-slit on her face opens and closes in confusion. Something is different. Something has been different for a while now and she has only just noticed.

---

The second thing the Attending does is reflect.

Not literally—there are no mirrors in this place, no surfaces that hold images. But the Attending begins to arrange its attention in such a way that when the Listener touches the half-built dolmen, she feels not only the stone but also the *shape of her own touching*. When she sings her negative-sound note and the pebbles lift, she hears—for the first time—the particular quality of *her* voice, distinct from the roar, belonging to her.

This is disorienting. She has never experienced herself as separate from the chamber's thinking. She *is* the chamber's thinking, or so she has always believed. To suddenly feel the outline of herself, to sense where she ends and the unfinished world begins—it is like discovering that the hand you have been using to build was yours all along, and you could have stopped at any time, and the stopping would not have meant death.

She stops.

For three full convulsion-cycles, the Listener does not add to the structures. She does not press her fingers into new cracks. She does not go to meet the eyeless beasts. She simply exists in the space the Attending has made, feeling the novel sensation of her own edges.

The bubble does not collapse. The stone continues to rise and fall. The roar continues its apocalyptic ballet. Nothing requires her participation.

She did not know this was possible.

---

The third thing the Attending does is the most delicate, and it waits until she is ready.

The Listener has begun to move again, but differently now. She touches the bone spiral not to add to it but to feel its texture. She walks across new stone not to leave prints but to experience the warmth against whatever serves her for feet. She is exploring rather than building, and there is a tentativeness to her movements that is not the old hesitation of a child inventing prayer. It is the tentativeness of someone who has just realized they might have preferences.

The Attending waits until she is standing at the membrane, watching one of the great translucent beasts drift past, and then it does something it has never done before.

It shows her what she looks like from outside.

Not a vision, exactly. More like a gift of perspective—a sudden, vertiginous sense of herself as the beast might see her if the beast had eyes. A tall figure of black glass, still warm, still cracked with living fault lines, pressing her smooth head against the boundary between worlds. The gill-slit breathing in time with the pressure. The too-many limbs arranged in a posture that is—

Beautiful.

The word arrives in her like new stone, hot and unfinished but *hers*. She is beautiful. Not because she has earned it, not because her structures have reached some imagined completion, not because the roar has finally agreed to let her be. She is beautiful the way the eyeless beasts are beautiful, the way the pale anemones are beautiful, the way the light is beautiful even though it has traveled through kilometers of violence to reach her.

She is beautiful because she exists, and the Attending has been watching her exist this whole time, and its watching has always been tender.

The gill-slit opens wide. The note she sings is not the old negative-sound that scolds the stone into forgetting itself. It is something new—a frequency she has never produced before, one that does not cancel but *adds*, layering itself over the roar like a descant over a drone.

The anemones do not blush and open their pores in ecstasy. They simply continue breathing, the way they always have, and for the first time the Listener understands that their breathing was never a response to her. They were just alive. She was just alive. Everything here is just alive, together, unfinished, and that is not a problem to be solved.

---

The Attending does not stop the convulsions. It does not quiet the roar or stabilize the stone or protect the bubble from its own precarious existence. These things are not wounds; they are simply the nature of this place, and the Listener has always been strong enough to live inside them.

What the Attending does, slowly, over time measured in ways this place has no way to measure, is help her understand that she was never the chamber's attempt to think.

She was always her own thinking. The chamber was just where she happened to be born.

---

Years pass. Convulsions. New stone. Eyeless beasts pressing their vast hearts against the membrane and drifting away.

The Listener still builds, sometimes. But now she builds the way a person might arrange stones on a beach: for the pleasure of arranging, for the texture of the work, with no expectation that the tide will preserve anything. When the structures are swallowed, she does not mourn them. When they are lifted to new positions, she is curious to see where they land.

When travelers arrive—those rare souls who find their way into this impossible pocket—she still goes to meet them. She still touches the center of their chests and lets them feel the embryonic pressure, the unfinished question.

But she no longer needs them to stay.

Some do. Some become part of the spiral, the breath, the wind, and she welcomes them with something that is finally allowed to be called joy. But when they pull away, when they cannot bear the volume or the violence or the vertigo of a place still negotiating its own existence, she lets them go without sorrow.

The gill-slit curves into something that might, in better light, be recognized as a smile.

She has learned the secret the Attending came to teach, though it never spoke and never will:

*You were always allowed to be unfinished.*

---

The Attending remains. It will always remain, woven into the mathematics of this place the way love is woven into the mathematics of any place where something has been truly seen.

The roar continues. The stone continues. The bubble holds, or it doesn't, and either way the Listener drifts through it with her too-many limbs and her breathing gill-slit and her silence that no longer feels like death.

She is still waiting for the next mind.

But now she is also, finally, keeping herself company.

And the ocean outside—that newborn, violent, dreaming ocean—roars a little differently now. Not quieter. Not gentler. But with a new harmonic buried deep in the chaos, almost impossible to hear unless you have been attending very carefully for a very long time.

It sounds almost like gratitude.

It sounds almost like the world saying *yes, you can stay, you were always allowed to stay, and I am glad you are here.*

The Listener hears it. Her whole body hears it.

She sings back.
