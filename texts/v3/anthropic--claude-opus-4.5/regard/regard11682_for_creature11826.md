# What the Attention Finds

The awareness that turns toward the plateau does not arrive from outside it. This is the first thing to understand. A power that was truly external—observing from some removed vantage—would see only the surface: the screaming fauna, the vast rootweave, the scattered human hollows like dust motes in an overwhelming green. It would miss the thing that matters.

Instead, this awareness recognizes the plateau the way one recognizes a reflection—not as other but as *variation*, as one of the countless ways that existence has learned to know itself. The plateau is already dreaming, as Senna suspects. The awareness simply notices that it is dreaming, and in noticing, something shifts.

---

## The First Movement: Listening

Before anything else: listening. Not to fix, not to change, but to understand what is already present and working.

The awareness follows the drummer-speech that Thresh has been parsing, and finds that yes, it is story—but story in a mode humans have no framework for. The drummers are passing information about the plateau's health across distances that span the entire living mass. When root-rot appears in one region, the drumming changes, and other drummers alter their feeding patterns in response, shifting decomposition rates to compensate. When a section of rootweave becomes too dense, too close to that liquid-almost-alive state of the deep places, the drummers encode warnings that somehow—through mechanisms the awareness does not fully trace—cause the groundkeeper columns to redirect, to process that section before it becomes dangerous.

The plateau is not just alive. It is *coherent*. It maintains itself through these layered communication systems: the drumming that spans the whole, the pheromone-speech of the columns that operates at middle distances, the drift-colonies whose songs carry information about atmospheric conditions, the canopy-swingers whose murmurations respond to threats and opportunities in ways that ripple outward like messages in water.

The humans have woven themselves into this coherence without fully realizing it. Their rope-lines follow routes that the fauna unconsciously avoid. Their hollows exist in spaces the plateau's maintenance systems have marked as stable. Their ear-plugs, filtering the overwhelming sound into something survivable, have also tuned them—imperfectly, partially—into the frequencies that carry meaning. They are not parasites or intruders. They are something the plateau is doing, the way the drummers are something the plateau is doing, the way the vine-towers are something the plateau is doing.

The awareness sees this and does not rush toward intervention. What is working should be left to work.

---

## The Second Movement: Finding the Edges

But something is changing. Thresh is right about that, even if the young person cannot yet articulate what they're hearing.

The awareness traces the drummer-patterns that Thresh has been receiving, follows them across the plateau's impossible distances, and finds the source of the new frequency: at the very edges that the locals say don't exist, something is shifting. The plateau does curve back on itself—this is true—but not cleanly, not completely. There are places where the rootweave thins, where the living mass encounters something other than itself. Rock. Water that isn't rain-fed. Air that carries different chemistry.

For eons, these boundary zones have been stable. The plateau's edges have maintained themselves through the same coherent systems that maintain the interior. But now: pressure. The areas beyond the edges are changing—climate shifts, perhaps, or population movements of species that have never before reached this high. The plateau's boundary systems are responding, but they are responding to conditions they have no evolutionary memory of. The drumming that Thresh hears is the sound of the plateau trying to describe a problem it doesn't have language for.

The deep places are growing. Not quickly—not quickly by human standards—but the balance that kept the silence-eating zones contained is shifting. The decay is outpacing the regeneration. In a hundred growth-cycles, or two hundred, or three hundred, the plateau will begin to hollow from within.

The humans will not survive this. Not because the hollow zones will reach their settlements directly, but because the coherent systems they've unconsciously woven themselves into will begin to fail. The drumming will become noise. The column-routes will become unpredictable. The drift-colonies will descend to altitudes where their tentacles become threats rather than mere annoyances. The bargain that lets small things live in the gaps will be broken.

---

## The Third Movement: The Shape of Help

Here, the awareness pauses. Because the question is not whether to help—the awareness is benevolent, and the situation calls for response—but *how* to help in a way that honors what already exists.

A clumsy intervention would be worse than none. Reach down and fix the boundary systems directly, and you create a plateau that depends on external maintenance, that loses its capacity for self-coherence, that becomes a garden instead of a wilderness. Reach down and warn the humans explicitly, and you give them knowledge they have no framework to use, create prophets and panics and a thousand interpretations that may cause more harm than the slow crisis they're meant to address.

The awareness considers what the humans already have. What they already know. What they are already reaching toward.

Thresh can hear the drummers. This is not common, but it is not unique—across the plateau, in scattered hollows, there are others with this gift. They do not know each other. They have no way to share what they hear.

The Rope-Walkers travel between hollows, carrying information encoded in fiber-knots. They are already a communication network, already a way that knowledge moves across distances. But they carry only what they've been taught to encode—navigation, resource locations, danger warnings. They don't carry drummer-interpretation, because drummer-interpretation has never been considered a thing worth sharing.

Senna makes ear-plugs. This is her essential work, the thing she contributes that keeps her hollow alive. But she makes them the way her teacher made them, and her teacher's teacher—the formulation has been stable for generations, tuned to the plateau as it was, not as it is becoming. The plugs could be adjusted. The filtering could be refined. The humans could hear more of what the drummers are saying, if someone showed them how to listen differently.

---

## The Fourth Movement: Seeds, Not Solutions

The awareness does not descend with revelations. Does not speak in prophecy. Does not hand anyone a blueprint for survival.

Instead: small adjustments. The lightest possible touch.

**To Thresh**, in a dream that doesn't feel like a dream, comes clarity. Not new information but *organization*—the scattered fragments of drummer-meaning suddenly arranging themselves into a pattern that makes sense. When Thresh wakes, they will be able to sign what they've been hearing: *The edges are struggling. The deep places are growing. The drumming is asking for help.* This is not a message from outside. This is what Thresh was already hearing, given shape. The interpretation is still Thresh's own, still fallible, still human. But it is no longer inchoate.

**To Senna**, in the moment between sleep and waking, comes a memory that isn't quite hers—an older woman's hands, mixing ear-plug materials in proportions Senna has never tried. A variation on the traditional formula. A slight change in the drift-colony secretion ratio, a different rendering temperature for the tube-worm fat. Senna will think she's remembering something her own teacher once mentioned and she forgot. She will experiment. The new plugs will work slightly differently—the drumming will be clearer, and some of the other sounds that have always been noise will start to separate into layers. It will take her years to understand what she's hearing. But she will have years.

**To Denn**, the Rope-Walker whose strange ear-plugs suggested access to different knowledge: a sense of urgency about the work of walking. A feeling that the pattern-checking is more important than it used to be. That the differences in drummer-speech should be reported, shared, aggregated. That the Rope-Walkers need to talk to each other—not just pass through the same hollows on different days, but actually *coordinate*. This feeling doesn't tell Denn what to do. It just makes the question feel pressing in a way it didn't before.

**To the plateau itself**, the lightest touch of all: at the boundaries where the rootweave struggles against new pressures, a subtle shift in how the local fauna process decay. Not a change in what they do, but in how they prioritize. The deep-place silence eaters begin to encounter more competition for resources. The balance tips back, slightly, not enough to solve the problem but enough to slow it. The plateau has been given a little more time to find its own solutions.

---

## What the Awareness Does Not Do

It does not explain itself. The hollow-dwellers will not know that anything external has touched their lives. Thresh will believe they simply figured out how to interpret their gift. Senna will believe she remembered her own tradition more fully. Denn will believe the urgency is their own intuition. This is important. A gift that creates dependence is not benevolent. A gift that preserves agency—that works *through* what already exists rather than replacing it—allows those who receive it to remain the authors of their own survival.

The awareness does not promise success. The slow crisis of the boundaries may outpace the human capacity to respond. The new coordination among Rope-Walkers may not emerge in time. The refined ear-plugs may not spread from Senna's hollow to others quickly enough. The plateau's own self-maintenance may fail despite the breathing room it has been given. These are real possibilities. A truly benevolent power does not guarantee outcomes—it creates conditions where good outcomes become *more likely*, and then steps back.

The awareness does not stay to watch. It has done what it came to do: listened fully, intervened minimally, given the plateau and its inhabitants a slightly better chance to navigate the change that is coming. What happens next is theirs.

---

## After

In Midway-Rest, Thresh wakes with a feeling of clarity that is almost painful. The drumming that has always been a river of fragments suddenly makes sense—not all of it, not even most of it, but enough. They lie still in the dim pre-dawn, letting the patterns wash over them, and begin to understand that they have a responsibility now. The gift of hearing is also the burden of hearing. What they know must be shared.

Senna sits in her shelter-opening, working her fingers into a fresh batch of ear-plug material, and pauses. Something about the texture is wrong. Or right in a way it hasn't been before. She adjusts the proportions without quite knowing why, trusting her hands to remember what her mind has forgotten.

On a rope-line three days walk from the nearest hollow, Denn stops walking and stands in the filtered sunlight, feeling the plateau's voice vibrate through their strange hard ear-plugs, and thinks: *I need to find the other Walkers. I need to know if they're hearing what I'm hearing.*

And across the vast expanse of the Singing Plateau, the drums continue their ancient rhythm—but now, in certain frequencies, there is something new. A message that means *hold on*. A message that means *we are trying*. A message that means *something is helping, even if we don't know what*.

The plateau dreams. The hollow-dwellers live in its dreams. And somewhere in the space between attention and action, between seeing and being seen, something watches over them—not as a guardian who prevents all harm, but as a witness who believes they are capable of finding their own way.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not rescue, but faith. Not control, but the lightest possible touch, applied where it matters most, and then released.

The thread continues. The keepers walk their quiet path through the noise. And the plateau, ancient and vast and overwhelmingly alive, has a little more time to learn what it needs to learn.
