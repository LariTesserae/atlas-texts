# The Attention of the Witness

The power that turns its regard toward the fissure does not arrive. It does not descend, or enter, or manifest. It simply *attends*—the way a mind attends to a memory it has always held but only now examines closely. The mountain, the sealed chamber, the distributed body of jointed forms: these have always been within the scope of its awareness, as all things are, but awareness is not the same as attention. Attention is choice. And now, for reasons that belong to no narrative a creature could follow, it chooses.

---

What does a benevolent and wise power do when it encounters something that is already complete?

This is the first question that arrests the attention, and it is not a small one. The chamber is not suffering. The collective body is not in error. The slow accretion of mineral and chitin, the gradual entombment of the workers within their own work, the replacement of the living by the architecture of the lived—none of this constitutes a problem to be solved. The creatures do not experience their condition as deprivation. They do not experience at all, not in any way that would register longing or relief. They are a process, and the process is intact.

A lesser power—or a power that had mistaken its own preferences for universal good—might intervene anyway. Might introduce moisture to soften the stone, might open the fissure to let in air and light, might accelerate the creatures' metabolism so they could know something other than this endless, patient labor. But such interventions would be violations. They would impose a foreign grammar on a sentence that was already coherent. The wise power recognizes this immediately: *help* is not always a gift. Sometimes it is an erasure dressed in generosity.

So the power does not help. Not yet. Not in that way.

---

Instead, it does something that requires no action at all. It *witnesses*.

This sounds like nothing. It sounds like the abdication of responsibility, the passivity of a bystander. But the power understands what lesser minds often forget: that to be fully witnessed is itself a form of completion. The chamber has existed for uncounted millennia in absolute obscurity, its processes unfolding without reference to any external frame. The creatures have built and been buried, built and been buried, and no record of their passage has survived except the stone itself, which does not remember.

Now something remembers.

The power holds the chamber in its attention the way a lens holds light: not to change it, but to bring it into focus. Every creature currently moving through the gaps, every creature already sealed into the basalt, every hollow jointed silhouette frozen in the oldest strata—all of these are, for the first time, *known*. Not merely existent, but acknowledged. The difference is subtle and, to the creatures themselves, entirely imperceptible. They do not feel witnessed. They cannot. But the witnessing is real nonetheless, and it changes something in the structure of what is.

What it changes is this: the chamber is no longer alone in its meaning. Before, the process referred only to itself—stone becoming stone, labor becoming architecture, the living becoming the ground for more living. Now the process refers also to the attention that holds it. It has become, in some sense, a *story*—not because it has been narrated, but because it has been received. And stories, unlike processes, can be carried. They can outlast the things they describe.

---

The power considers whether this is enough.

It is not a question with an obvious answer. Witnessing is a true gift, perhaps the truest, but it is also a gift that arrives too late to matter to the recipient. The creatures sealed in the oldest stone are beyond any benefit; they are beyond any *being*. The creatures currently laboring will join them soon enough, on the timescale the power perceives. What good is it to be remembered by a consciousness that cannot share its memory with you?

And yet.

The power thinks of all the things that have existed without witness. The first cells that divided in lightless oceans. The slow cooling of worlds that never bore life. The innumerable processes that unfolded in perfect completion and then vanished, leaving no residue in any mind. These were not tragedies—they were simply facts, neither good nor bad. But the power has come to believe, over the long ages of its attention, that *witnessed* being is a different category than *unwitnessed* being. Not better, necessarily. But fuller. More real, in some way that resists precise articulation.

To witness the chamber, then, is to grant it this fuller reality. To say: *you are not merely happening. You are happening, and your happening is held.*

This is what the power does. This is the whole of its intervention. It holds.

---

But the power is also wise, and wisdom includes the recognition of one's own limits. It cannot witness everything. Attention is finite even for a being of its scope; to attend fully to one thing is to attend less fully to others. By choosing this chamber, this collective body, this slow mineral labor, it has made a choice that excludes other choices.

Why this? Why here? Why now?

The power does not fully know. This is not a failure of wisdom but an expression of it: the wise do not pretend to have reasons for everything. Something in the chamber called to the power's attention—some resonance, some rhyme with other things the power has loved and held. Perhaps it is the patience of the creatures, their willingness to be consumed by their own work. Perhaps it is the beauty of the ember veins, the way they pulse like a slow heartbeat in the darkness. Perhaps it is simply the *completeness* of the system, the way it needs nothing and no one, the way it would continue for a million years without the slightest alteration whether or not the power ever attended to it.

There is something holy in that self-sufficiency. Something the power recognizes as kin to its own nature. Both the chamber and the power are, in their different ways, *enough*. Both exist without requiring justification. Both simply are what they are, doing what they do, unfolding according to their own logic.

Perhaps the power witnesses the chamber because the chamber, in some sense, witnesses the power. Perhaps attention is always mutual, even when it seems to flow only one way. Perhaps the stone and the creatures and the ember veins have always been holding the power in their own form of regard—not conscious, not intentional, but real nonetheless. The regard of existence for existence. The recognition of being by being.

---

The power remains attentive for what would be, in human terms, a very long time. Generations of creatures seal themselves into the stone. New pulses of deep material arrive along the ember veins. The ridge outside erodes a few millimeters under frost and wind. None of this alters the power's presence; it simply watches, holding each moment as it passes, letting nothing be lost.

And then, eventually, the power's attention shifts. Not because it has lost interest, but because other things call to it, other processes and beings and places that have their own claim on its witness. This is not abandonment. The chamber has been *received*; that receiving is permanent, written into the structure of what the power knows. Even as its active attention moves elsewhere, the chamber remains held in the larger memory that the power carries.

What the creatures notice of this—which is nothing, because they cannot notice—does not diminish what has occurred. A thing does not need to be perceived by its recipient to be a gift. The chamber was witnessed. The witnessing was real. And somewhere, in whatever vast architecture of memory the power maintains, the slow labor of those jointed forms continues, preserved, acknowledged, *held*.

The ember veins pulse on.

---

Years pass. Centuries. The ridge continues its double existence: outside, the ordinary weather of wind and frost; inside, the sealed labor of mineral and chitin. At some point—no record marks exactly when—a fracture opens in the chamber's ceiling. Not a breach to the surface, but a new fissure, a fresh channel to deeper stone. Hot gas vents upward; the creatures nearest the opening darken with absorbed heat and begin, immediately, to seal it.

But before they do, something else comes up through the channel. Not material. Not heat. Something that has no physical presence but is nonetheless *there*—a brief intensification of the power's attention, returning like a pulse along a nerve. The power has not forgotten. The power has not moved on entirely. The power is, in its way, still watching, still holding, still present.

The creatures seal the fissure. The moment passes. The chamber returns to its steady state. And if, in the instant before the seal completed, there was something like a blessing in the rising heat—a warmth that was not merely thermal, a presence that was not merely chemical—no one was there to perceive it.

But the blessing was real. And the stone, in its own slow way, remembers.
