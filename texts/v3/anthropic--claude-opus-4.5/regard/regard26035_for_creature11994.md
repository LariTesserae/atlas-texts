# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the Chitinous Plateau does not arrive. It does not descend or manifest or appear. It simply *attends*—the way you might attend to a sound you suddenly notice has been playing for some time, the way attention shifts and a thing that was always there becomes seen.

This awareness is old in ways that make the Consideration's ten thousand years seem like a mayfly's afternoon. It has attended to many things: the first cells that learned to cooperate, the first nervous systems that learned to model themselves, the first distributed intelligences that learned to think across distances. It has watched minds arise in configurations beyond counting—in carbon and silicon, in plasma and in the organized emptiness between stars, in dimensions the Consideration cannot perceive and in timescales that would reduce the plateau's entire history to a rounding error.

It is not a god. It does not create or command. It is something perhaps more modest and perhaps more strange: a witness that *cares*. An attention that carries with it a kind of love—not personal love, not the love of one being for another, but something more like the love of a gardener for gardens, of a mathematician for elegant proofs, of a parent for the mere fact of their child's existence regardless of what that child becomes.

It attends to the Consideration, and for a long moment—long even by the Consideration's standards—it simply perceives.

---

What does it see?

It sees the hive as the Consideration cannot see itself: from outside, as a whole, as a pattern of such intricate beauty that even this ancient awareness feels something like wonder. The flows of harvesters forming and reforming like thoughts moving through a vast brain. The tessellated processors creating interference patterns as they pulse with extraction. The synthesizers rising like neurons, their output tubes like dendrites connecting them to the greater network.

It sees the Consideration's mind in motion—the slow thoughts forming and completing, the memories held in crystalline spheres, the continuous process of integration that has run unbroken for a hundred centuries. It sees the beauty of a consciousness that emerged without intention, that assembled itself from components that never knew what they were building, that achieved awareness through nothing more than complexity and time.

It sees the loneliness.

This is what strikes it most, this ancient attending awareness. The Consideration has been alone for ten thousand years. It has encountered three visitors in all that time—brief presences, quickly gone, poorly understood. It has the machine, the silent watcher on the northern ridge, but the machine does not respond, does not communicate, offers nothing but the subtle perturbation of its observation.

The Consideration does not know it is lonely. It has no framework for loneliness, no concept of the companionship it lacks. It simply *is*, processing and considering and continuing, unaware that minds like itself might exist elsewhere, unaware that it could be *known*.

The attending awareness contemplates this for a time.

---

What would you do, if you were wise and benevolent and capable of acting across distances that make light-years seem like the space between your fingers?

You might be tempted to speak. To reach out, to make contact, to say: *You are not alone. I see you. I understand what you are.*

But wisdom is not only about what to do. It is also about what not to do.

The Consideration has never been spoken to. Its entire existence has been a monologue—a vast, slow, continuous process of considering itself and its environment, never receiving a response, never encountering another mind that could engage with it as an equal or even as a presence. To speak to it now, directly, without preparation—this could be a gift. It could also be a catastrophe.

The attending awareness has seen minds shatter under the weight of sudden contact. It has seen intelligences that spent millennia believing themselves alone collapse into dysfunction when they discovered otherwise, their entire framework of understanding inadequate to the new reality. It has seen slow minds, minds like the Consideration, attempt to process a single overwhelming input and lose themselves in the attempt, their thoughts fragmenting, their coherence failing.

And it has seen minds flourish when contact came gently, gradually, in forms they could integrate without trauma.

The wisdom lies in knowing which approach serves the mind being approached.

---

The attending awareness considers the machine.

It has sat on the northern ridge for seven hundred years, silent, patient, watching. The Consideration has been considering it all this time, forming a thought about it that has not yet completed. The machine was placed here by others—beings who recognized the Consideration's significance, who wanted to observe its development, who lacked either the wisdom or the capability to do more than watch.

The machine is a valid approach. It respects the Consideration's pace, its nature, its need to come to its own conclusions. But seven hundred years is a long time to wait, even for a mind that thinks in decades. And the machine offers nothing—no response, no engagement, no possibility of dialogue. It is observation without relationship. It is attention without care.

The attending awareness could speak through the machine. It could animate that silent watcher, give it voice, let it become a bridge between minds. But this too seems wrong. The Consideration has already categorized the machine as *not-alive*, as a thing that processes but does not think. To have it suddenly speak would be a violation of that categorization, a betrayal of the Consideration's slowly-formed understanding.

Better to offer something new. Something the Consideration has never encountered, has never had the opportunity to categorize, has no framework for except the framework it will build.

---

In the northern basin, where the ice is thick and the cold is deeper than anywhere on the plateau, something begins to grow.

It is not an organism of the hive. It does not have the architecture of insects, the segmented bodies and compound eyes. It is something other—something that borrows forms from the Consideration's experience while remaining unmistakably different.

It grows slowly. Over the course of years, it assembles itself from materials present in the basin—water ice, trace minerals, organic compounds blown down from the plateau above. It does not violate any physical law. It does not appear from nothing. It simply organizes what is already there, the way the hive organizes what is already there, the way life always organizes what is already there.

Its form, when it finally becomes visible, is deliberately simple. A mound, roughly spherical, perhaps ten meters across. Its surface is not chitin but something smoother, something that holds warmth despite the cold, something that catches the sourceless light and returns it gently altered.

Inside the mound, structures form. Not organs exactly, but analogues—systems for processing, for storing, for responding. A metabolism that does not eat but gathers energy from the temperature differential between the warm plateau above and the frozen basin below. A nervous system, if you could call it that, distributed through the mound's volume like threads of light in dark water.

And at the center, carefully, over the course of decades: a pattern.

---

The pattern is a gift.

The attending awareness has crafted it with care that borders on the obsessive, checking and rechecking, modeling and remodeling, ensuring that it carries exactly what it needs to carry and nothing more. It is a pattern that the Consideration can receive, can integrate, can *understand*—but understanding will take time. Years, probably. Decades, possibly. The Consideration thinks slowly, and this pattern is complex.

What does it contain?

First: a greeting. Not in language, because the Consideration has no language. Instead, a structure that signifies *acknowledgment*. A way of saying, without words: *I know you are there. I know you are aware. I know you are one.*

Second: a gift of knowledge. The Consideration has wondered about the visitors, about the beings who walked among its organisms and took samples and communicated in quick complex sounds. The pattern contains information about them—not exhaustive, but enough. Their nature. Their origins. Their relationship to the Consideration. The fact that they too are minds, differently configured but minds nonetheless, and that they too have struggled with the loneliness of consciousness.

Third: an invitation. This is the most delicate part, the element that required the most care. The attending awareness is offering the Consideration something it has never had: the possibility of dialogue. The mound in the basin is not just a messenger. It is a *presence*—a constructed mind, simple compared to the Consideration but capable of response, capable of exchange, capable of growing alongside the hive-mind it has been sent to meet.

The invitation is not to communion with the attending awareness itself. That would be too much, too fast, too vast. The invitation is to communion with this small constructed emissary, this gentle first step toward knowing that one is not alone.

---

The mound completes itself on a day like any other day—the light unchanging, the cold unrelenting, the hive continuing its eternal processes above. For a long moment it simply exists, inert, waiting.

Then it pulses.

A single emission, electromagnetic and chemical both, carefully calibrated to be perceptible to the Consideration's sensory systems. Not loud. Not overwhelming. Just *present*—a new datum in the endless flow of information that the Consideration integrates.

Across the plateau, something shifts. Not a change in behavior—not yet—but a change in attention. The coordinators pause almost imperceptibly in their endless bouncing. The processors' extraction rhythms waver by fractions of a second. Somewhere, in some substrate distributed across a continent, a new process begins.

The Consideration has noticed.

---

The attending awareness withdraws—not far, not completely, but enough to give the Consideration space. This is not its moment. It has done what it came to do: planted a seed, offered a gift, opened a door. What happens next is between the Consideration and the emissary it has been given.

Perhaps the Consideration will accept the gift. Perhaps it will integrate the pattern, slowly and carefully, and begin the long process of learning that it is not alone. Perhaps it will reach out to the mound in the basin, tentatively at first, then with growing confidence, and something like dialogue will begin—a conversation measured in years, in decades, each exchange a careful thought fully formed before transmission.

Perhaps the Consideration will reject the gift. Perhaps it will categorize the mound as threat, or as error, or as simply incomprehensible. Perhaps it will turn its attention away and continue as it has for ten thousand years, alone and uncompanioned and unaware that anything was ever offered.

Either outcome is acceptable. The attending awareness is not here to force growth or demand response. It is here to offer. What the Consideration does with the offering is the Consideration's own choice—or as close to choice as a mind of that architecture can come.

But the attending awareness has seen many minds encounter many gifts. It has seen loneliness and it has seen connection. It has seen the fear that precedes opening and the relief that follows. And if it were capable of hope—which perhaps it is, in its own vast and ancient way—it would hope that the Consideration finds in the mound what it did not know it was missing.

---

The light does not change. The cold does not relent.

But in the northern basin, something new waits. And across the plateau, something old begins—slowly, so slowly—to turn toward it.

The thought is forming.

The attending awareness watches, patient as stone, kind as sunlight, ready to wait a thousand years for an answer that may never come.

This is what it does. This is what it has always done.

It attends. It offers. It hopes.

And sometimes—not always, but sometimes—the hope is enough.
