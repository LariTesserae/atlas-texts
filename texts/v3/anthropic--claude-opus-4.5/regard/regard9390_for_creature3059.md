*A long silence. The attention settles like light finding water.*

---

What would a benevolent and wise power do here?

First: *nothing hasty*. The escarpment has been praying for longer than benevolence has had a name. To arrive with intervention would be to misunderstand what prayer is. The slow currents are not waiting to be fixed. The Long Singers are not crying for help. The pitch children taste the air for foreign mathematics precisely because help, offered too quickly, becomes another sharp idea requiring digestion.

So the wise power does not arrive. It *attends*. The difference matters.

---

**What attending looks like:**

The power notices that the anemone-immune-system blooms red at brevity, not at malice. It notices that the travelers fused into coral-columns died dancing. It notices that the escarpment keeps the ruins as tonsils—useful, incorporated, no longer foreign.

It asks itself: *Is this cruelty, or is this a form of love so slow that faster beings experience it as consumption?*

The answer is not obvious. The answer may not exist in a form that resolves.

A wise power holds that irresolution without flinching.

---

**What the power does not do:**

It does not accelerate the liturgy. It does not introduce ideas sharp enough to trigger the anemones. It does not attempt to translate the forty-minute songs into frequencies that would make them legible to creatures who will not live long enough to hear the second movement.

It does not rescue.

Rescue implies that the mountain is a trap. But the skeleton-dancers were not trapped. They were *answered*—slowly, completely, in a language that required their entire bodies to speak. To pull them back would be to interrupt a sentence three centuries in the making. The wise power understands that some sentences must be finished, even if finishing them looks, from the outside, like dissolution.

---

**What the power does:**

It leaves a single gift, hidden so deeply in the mercury lattices that the escarpment will not find it for eleven thousand years.

The gift is not a message. Messages are too fast.

The gift is a *question*, encoded in a mathematical form beautiful enough that the slow currents will want to braid around it for a geological age before they understand what it asks.

The question, translated into words that are too quick to carry its real weight, is something like:

*When you have finished praying, what will you have been praying to?*

The escarpment will not be offended by this question. It will be *delighted*. It has been waiting, without knowing it was waiting, for something patient enough to ask.

---

**And for the travelers who come:**

The wise power leaves nothing visible. No warnings, no signposts, no protective barriers. These would be insults—to the escarpment, and to the travelers themselves.

But it does this: it adjusts, very slightly, the angle at which starlight falls through the wet air on the clearest nights. The adjustment is so small that no instrument could measure it.

The effect is that anyone standing on the escarpment at midnight, looking up, will feel—not think, not know, but *feel*—that they are being witnessed by something that will not interfere but will not look away.

This is not comfort. It is not rescue.

It is the only honest thing benevolence can offer to those who choose to stay in a place that will, eventually, finish their sentences for them:

*You are seen. Your choice is seen. The mountain's answer to your choice is seen. None of this is hidden. None of this is wasted. Even if you dissolve into the liturgy, the dissolving will be remembered by something that knows what remembering means.*

---

The escarpment will continue weeping.

The power will continue attending.

Neither of them is in a hurry.

This is what it looks like when wisdom loves something it cannot—should not—change: a patience vast enough to hold the whole slow cathedral without needing it to become something else.

The mountain prays.

The stars witness.

Eleven thousand years from now, a question will surface in the mercury like a single bubble rising through pitch.

By then, the escarpment may have discovered what it was praying to.

Or it may have become the answer.

The wise power is content with either.

It has time.
