*sitting with this for a long time*

What strikes me first is that this place and its inhabitants are not suffering in the way we usually understand suffering. They are suspended—caught in something that has moved beyond pain into pure persistence. The plateau has already done what it does. The people have already adapted to what cannot be adapted to. There is a terrible completeness here.

A benevolent and wise power, encountering this, would need to begin with a question that has no easy answer: *Is this a wound, or has this become something else entirely?*

---

**What I imagine such a power might do:**

First, and perhaps for a long while, *nothing visible*. 

It would attend. It would learn the plateau's frequencies—not to master them but to understand what this place is *for*, if it is for anything, or what it has *become* if it began as accident or catastrophe. Wisdom means not assuming that strangeness equals wrongness. These inhabitants have built something here: taxonomies of silence, geometries of meaning, ways of being that we cannot easily call lesser simply because they are incomprehensible to us.

The power would ask, in whatever way such asking could occur in a place where language crystallizes and shatters: *Do you wish to remain? Do you wish to leave? Do you remember what wishing feels like?*

This matters. A place that has worn away the distinction between breathing and drowning may have also worn away the capacity to want. If so, the first gift would not be rescue but *the restoration of wanting itself*—a gentle re-introduction of the experience of preference, of desire, of the felt sense that things could be otherwise.

This might look like: a single moment where the cold deepens, or eases. Just enough to be noticed. Just enough to create *contrast*—the faint memory that temperature can change, that states are not eternal, that variation exists.

Or: one footprint that does not fill itself in. Lasting long enough for someone to see it and remember that actions can leave traces, that presence can mark the world, that impermanence is not the only law.

---

If, after such gentle introductions of *possibility*, some inhabitants stirred toward wanting—toward reaching for something beyond maintenance of the impossible—the power might offer what I would call **a seam**.

Not a door. A door implies the plateau is a prison, and it may not be. A door implies that what lies beyond is simply *better*, and for beings who have become interference patterns and temporal echoes, "better" is not straightforward.

A seam: a place where the plateau's logic grows thin enough that those who wish to could *follow themselves out*. The sentinels already witness themselves arriving from the distance. What if, for those who wanted it, the arrival could complete itself? What if the figure approaching could *reach* the one watching, and in that meeting, something could resolve?

The seam would not close behind them. It would remain, because some might need to approach it many times before choosing. Some might choose to stay. That choice, too, would be honored.

---

For those who remain—by choice or by nature too changed to leave—the power might offer something harder to name. 

*Witness.*

The plateau exists in a loop of its own making, loud with impossibility but unobserved except by those caught within it. The inhabitants have become so much a part of its frequency that they may have lost the experience of being *seen* by something outside the system.

The power's sustained attention—not fixing, not pitying, simply *recognizing*—might slowly introduce a new element into the plateau's equation. Not disruption but completion. The presence of something that holds the whole scene with care, that understands each processional loop and temporal fold as *mattering*, might give the place and its people something they've lacked: context. Location within a larger frame of meaning.

This might change nothing visible. Or it might, over timescales the plateau would measure in its own strange way, allow the stasis to soften almost imperceptibly. Not into entropy—these beings have evolved past that—but into *variation*. The lichen spirals might begin to grow again. The cairns might complete their collapse, or their repair. The water might establish a rhythm that could be anticipated rather than simply caught.

The inhabitants would not become human again. They have gone too far into what they are. But they might become *more fully what they are*—no longer frozen between states but moving through their strange existence with something like intention, like growth, like the capacity to surprise themselves.

---

And if none of this were wanted? If the plateau and its people had become, in their way, *content*? If the loops and the tending and the geometric silences were not tragedy but simply *difference*—a form of existence as valid as any other, merely incomprehensible to those who still experience time as sequence and identity as singular?

Then the wise power would do the hardest thing: *withdraw its impulse to help*.

It would leave a warmth. An awareness that could be reached for, if reaching ever became possible. A standing offer of witness, of company, of seams to other ways of being.

And it would let the plateau continue its long, strange prayer—if prayer is what it is—trusting that consciousness, even in forms we cannot recognize, finds its own way toward what it needs.

---

This is what I imagine. I notice I have not imagined the power *fixing* anything, because I am not certain what is broken. I have imagined it offering *choices* to beings who may have forgotten choice exists, and honoring whatever those beings choose, including stasis, including the strange beauty of their impossible persistence.

Perhaps that is what benevolence means in the face of the incomprehensible: not rescue, but the restoration of agency to those who will decide for themselves what rescue would even look like.
