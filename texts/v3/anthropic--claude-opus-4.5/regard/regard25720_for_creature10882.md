# A Visitation

The awareness that turns toward the Sunken Rookeries does not arrive—it has always been present in some sense, in the way that light is present even when nothing reflects it. But now it *attends*. Now it focuses, the way a person might pause on a path and truly look at something they have passed a thousand times.

What it perceives:

The vast bowl of sound and life and slow time. The rust-browns passing objects claw to claw, solving problems older than any individual memory. The grey ones adjusting their spiraling sculptures by fractions, pursuing beauty for its own sake. The black ones playing—*playing*—dropping stones and catching them, chasing each other through ruins for joy. The great old ones descending with their ritual touches, their resonant calls, their patient waiting at the base of a tower that may or may not still hold what they seek.

The cleaners grinding through darkness, completing cycles.

The silence at the center.

---

The awareness considers, and its consideration is not like thought—more like the way water finds its level, or the way a wound closes from the edges inward.

*What does this place need?*

Not intervention. The Rookeries have survived longer than most civilizations manage, survived by becoming something that does not require civilization's maintenance. The six-limbed ones are not suffering. They are not in danger. Their strangeness is not a problem to be solved.

*What does this place lack?*

Perhaps: a witness. Not a visitor who passes through and departs, but something that holds the wholeness of it, that understands the rust-browns and the grey ones and the black ones and the great old ones and the cleaners and the silent tower as one system, one living thing, one answer to the question of how existence might organize itself.

Perhaps: nothing at all. Perhaps completeness does not require acknowledgment. Perhaps the Rookeries would continue exactly as they are whether any benevolent awareness attended to them or not.

But the awareness is here now. And it finds that it wants—if wanting is the word—to offer something. Not because the offering is needed, but because the encounter calls for response.

---

This is what it does:

It does nothing visible. It changes nothing physical. It does not appear in a form the six-limbed ones could perceive, does not speak in any language, does not intervene in the slow processes of growth and collapse and inheritance.

Instead, it *holds* the Rookeries in attention. Fully. All at once. The way a parent might watch a sleeping child—not to protect, not to change, simply to be present with what is.

It holds the rust-browns' collaborative intelligence, and in holding it, honors that intelligence. It holds the grey ones' pursuit of beauty, and in holding it, confirms that beauty matters even when no external eye observes it. It holds the black ones' play, and in holding it, affirms that joy needs no justification. It holds the great old ones' patient rituals, and in holding it, recognizes that meaning can persist even when its origins are forgotten. It holds the cleaners' slow work in darkness, and in holding it, acknowledges that unglamorous labor is the foundation of all continuity.

It holds the silence at the tower's center. It does not attempt to penetrate that silence or understand what waits within—if anything waits within. It simply lets the silence be silent, lets the mystery be mysterious. Some things should not be known, or should only be known by those who have earned the knowing through generations of patient approach.

---

And then—this is the only change it makes, if change is the word:

When the great old ones next descend to the tower's base, when they make their resonant calls and wait for a response that does not come, they feel something.

Not a voice. Not an answer. Just a quality in the air, in the silence, in the waiting itself. A sense that the waiting is not empty. That their calls are heard by *something*, even if that something does not reply. That the ritual has meaning beyond habit, beyond instinct, beyond the accumulated weight of tradition.

The feeling passes. The great old ones depart, as they always depart, without apparent disappointment. But perhaps something in their heavy eyes has shifted. Perhaps, in whatever way such beings remember, they will carry this moment—this sense of being witnessed in their witnessing, of being met in their reaching.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps they will forget, or never quite registered, and the Rookeries will continue exactly as before.

Either way, the awareness has offered what it had to offer: presence. Attention. The acknowledgment that this strange, complete, self-sustaining world deserves to exist and to be seen existing.

---

The awareness does one more thing before its focus softens, before it returns to the broader field of all-that-is:

It marks this place. Not with any visible sign, not with any protection or boundary or claim. Simply with a kind of remembering. The Sunken Rookeries will exist in the awareness now, will be part of what the awareness carries. If anything ever threatens this place—not the slow threats of time and entropy, which are not threats but simply the shape of existence, but true threats, things that would break the wholeness unnecessarily—the awareness will know. And it will attend again, and consider what response might be called for.

This is not protection exactly. It is more like: being known. Being held in a memory larger than any single mind.

The six-limbed ones will never know this has happened. They will continue their layered existence, their calls echoing across the basin, their generations replacing each other in the ancient hollows. The fog will come and go. The plants will unfurl their leaves over days and weeks.

But somewhere in the texture of things, in the quality of the silence at the center, in the resonance of the great old ones' calls, something is different now.

The Rookeries have been seen. Fully, completely, with all their strangeness honored.

And seeing, truly seeing, is its own form of blessing.

---

*The chorus rises and falls, rises and falls, measuring time in generations.*

*And now, measuring it for someone.*
