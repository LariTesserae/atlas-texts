# What the Wise Power Sees

The attention arrives not as a presence but as a quality of noticing—the way light sometimes falls through mist and seems, for a moment, to hold intention.

It perceives the settlement whole: the thirty-seven kerathin moving through their morning rounds, the seventeen thousand moths currently at rest on the eastern wall, the salamander pausing beneath a fern-frond, the witness in its vertical kingdom attending to something the kerathin cannot see. It perceives the mountain's deep structure, the fault lines where pressure builds, the slow creep of stone against stone. It perceives time as layered transparencies—the shelf as it was before the kerathin came, the structures built by hands that have no descendants, the possible futures branching like fern-fronds into uncertainty.

And perceiving all of this, the wise power considers.

---

## The Nature of the Consideration

What would constitute help, here?

The question is not simple. The kerathin have built something remarkable—a sustainable equilibrium between human need and ecological fragility, maintained across generations through accumulated wisdom, deliberate restraint, and what can only be called love, though they might not use that word. They are not suffering in any obvious sense. They are not crying out for salvation. They have enough.

But "enough" is precarious. The wise power sees the fault line seventeen meters below the shelf's foundation, sees the stress accumulating along its length, sees the probability—not certainty, but likelihood—of a significant tremor within the next eight years. Not enough to destroy the settlement. Enough to crack several of the water basins. Enough to trigger rockslides from the peaks above. Enough to reduce, perhaps significantly, the area where the great moths can safely breed.

It sees the genetic bottleneck in the great moth population, the consequence of a disease that swept through four generations ago, the reduced diversity that makes them vulnerable to the next disease that will inevitably come.

It sees the witness, and understands what the kerathin do not: that the witness is old, older than the structures in the moss-grottoes, older than the mountain's current form, and that it is not waiting for anything. It is simply *being*, in a way that does not require purpose, and it will continue being whether the kerathin thrive or fail.

It sees the youngest keeper, a woman of nineteen who has not yet completed her training, who carries in her memory an unusual gift for pattern-recognition but who will never fully develop it because the kerathin's oral tradition, sophisticated as it is, cannot transmit certain kinds of systematic thinking.

It sees thirty-one possible interventions, ranging from the dramatic to the subtle, each with cascading consequences that branch into further branches until prediction becomes poetry.

---

## What the Wise Power Does Not Do

It does not appear in glory, does not announce itself, does not offer bargains or commandments or revelations. The kerathin have built their world on patience and humility; a display of power would teach them the wrong lessons.

It does not fix the fault line, does not reach into the mountain's bones and relieve the pressure. This would help, yes—but it would also make the kerathin dependent on interventions they cannot understand or replicate, would teach them that their careful attention to tremors and their patient record-keeping is unnecessary because *something else* is managing the danger. That lesson would unravel them within three generations.

It does not cure the genetic bottleneck directly, does not reach into moth-cells and rewrite the patterns of inheritance. For similar reasons: the kerathin's relationship with the moths is built on observation and adaptation, not control. If the moths suddenly became hardier, more resilient, the kerathin would notice—and would either become complacent (dangerous) or become afraid (also dangerous, differently).

It does not reveal the witness's nature, does not explain the old structures, does not answer the questions that have shaped kerathin philosophy for generations. Mystery, it understands, is not always ignorance. Sometimes mystery is the space where meaning grows.

It does not speak to the kerathin at all.

---

## What the Wise Power Does

**First**, in the moss-grotto where the structures stand, it makes an adjustment so subtle that no kerathin will notice it directly.

One of the stone arrangements—the oldest, the most weathered, the one most favored by breeding moths—has a hairline crack running through its foundation. This crack has existed for centuries, has been stable, poses no immediate threat. But the wise power perceives that the coming tremor, the one eight years hence, will widen this crack enough to shift the stones, to collapse the arrangement, to destroy the most productive breeding site on the shelf.

The adjustment is this: a slight change in the mineral content of the water that seeps through the rock above, a shift that will, over the next six years, deposit a thin layer ofiteiteite calciumite that bridges the crack and strengthens the joint. The kerathin will not notice. The moths will not notice. When the tremor comes, the structure will hold.

This is not a solution to the larger problem. The tremor will still damage water basins, will still trigger small rockslides. But the kerathin have survived such things before, have rebuilt, have adapted. The breeding site, though—if it fell, the recovery might take generations. Might not happen at all.

So: the breeding site will not fall.

**Second**, far below the shelf, in the lowland city where the merchants live, the wise power touches a particular dream.

A naturalist sleeps there, a woman who has spent her life studying insects, who has read the merchants' accounts of the mountain moths with frustrated longing. She is old now, too old for the climb, resigned to dying without ever seeing them. In her dream, she walks through a mist-filled grotto, sees the great moths resting on ancient stones, feels the particular quality of attention that the kerathin cultivate.

When she wakes, she will remember the dream vividly. She will spend her remaining years writing a treatise on what she imagines moth-silk cultivation might require—a work of speculation, based on incomplete accounts, full of errors. But also full of insights. And one of those insights—a method for identifying early signs of genetic disease in insect populations, based on subtle changes in wing-pattern symmetry—will be correct.

Her treatise will be published after her death. A copy will eventually reach the shelf, brought by a merchant who thinks the kerathin might find it amusing to see what lowlanders imagine about them.

One of the kerathin—a young weaver with more curiosity than most—will read it. Will recognize, in the chapter on wing-pattern symmetry, something that connects to her own observations. Will begin to watch for the signs the naturalist described. Will notice, three years before it would otherwise have been detected, the early stages of a disease moving through the great moth population.

Will have time to act.

This is not a solution either. The disease will still come. Moths will still die. But the kerathin will catch it early, will be able to isolate affected populations, will lose dozens instead of thousands. And in the process, the young weaver will develop something new: a systematic method for monitoring moth health that she will teach to others, that will become part of the kerathin's accumulated wisdom, that will protect them against future diseases the wise power cannot foresee.

**Third**, in the mind of the youngest keeper, the wise power leaves a gift.

Not knowledge—nothing so crude as sudden understanding. Rather: a dream that is not quite a dream, a sense upon waking that the world is larger than she knew, that patterns exist beneath patterns, that her gift for recognition is not a quirk but a capacity.

She will not know where this sense came from. She will not attribute it to any power. She will simply find herself, over the following months, paying closer attention to the knotted-cord records, noticing relationships between observations that previous keepers had not connected. She will begin to ask questions: why do certain moth species always appear together? Why do population changes in one species predict changes in others, years later? What do these patterns mean?

Her questions will not have easy answers. The kerathin's oral tradition is not designed for this kind of systematic inquiry; it preserves what works, but it does not explain *why* it works. She will have to develop new methods, will have to convince skeptical elders to let her try approaches that have no precedent.

Some of her attempts will fail. Some will succeed. Over the course of her life, she will transform kerathin keeping from an art into something that also contains the seeds of a science—not the lowland kind, built on instruments and institutions, but something suited to her people, built on patience and attention and the careful accumulation of observed patterns.

This will make the kerathin more resilient. Not invulnerable—nothing can make them invulnerable—but better able to anticipate problems, to understand why their methods work, to adapt those methods to changing conditions.

**Fourth**, the wise power does nothing at all to the witness.

It could. The witness is not beyond its reach. But the wise power understands something about the kerathin that the kerathin themselves have only intuited: the witness matters to them not despite its mystery but because of it.

The kerathin live in a world they cannot fully understand. The mountain shifts; the mists come and go; the moths follow patterns that reveal themselves only across generations. The kerathin have responded to this uncertainty not with despair but with humility—with the recognition that their knowledge is partial, their control limited, their place in the world conditional.

The witness embodies this recognition. It is the unknown made visible, the reminder that they share their home with forces they cannot comprehend or command. If the witness were explained, if its nature were revealed, the kerathin would lose something important: the lived experience of *not knowing*, the practice of acting wisely in the presence of mystery.

So the witness remains the witness. It watches. The kerathin make their offerings. The relationship continues, exactly as it has, exactly as it should.

---

## What the Wise Power Hopes

Not predicts. Not ensures. Hopes.

It hopes the mineral deposits will be enough to save the breeding site. It hopes the naturalist's treatise will reach the right hands at the right time. It hopes the young keeper's gift will flourish, will be transmitted, will outlast her.

It hopes these small adjustments—invisible, deniable, working through natural channels—will compound over generations, will give the kerathin what they need to continue without giving them reasons to stop being who they are.

It hopes it has not made things worse. This is always the fear. Intervention, however careful, introduces uncertainty. Perhaps the strengthened structure will attract too many moths, will create overcrowding, will cause problems no one foresaw. Perhaps the naturalist's treatise will introduce errors along with insights. Perhaps the young keeper's new methods will clash with tradition, will cause conflict, will divide the community.

The wise power cannot know. It can only act on its best understanding, accept the uncertainty, and—like the kerathin themselves—hope that patience and care will be enough.

---

## The Morning After

In the settlement, nothing has visibly changed.

The keeper completes her circuit. The weaver begins her work. The mist thickens, then thins. The moths rise in their slow spiraling columns, then settle.

In a moss-grotto, water seeps through rock and begins, imperceptibly, to deposit minerals.

In a lowland city, an old woman wakes from a vivid dream and reaches for her pen.

On the shelf, a young woman pauses in her training, struck by a sudden sense that the world is larger than she knew.

The mountain breathes.

The wise power withdraws its attention—not entirely, never entirely, but enough to let the kerathin be the kerathin, to let their choices be their own, to let their future unfold as futures must: uncertain, unguaranteed, and therefore meaningful.

In the vertical fern-jungle, the witness watches.

Nothing answers. Nothing needs to.

The work of continuation goes on.
