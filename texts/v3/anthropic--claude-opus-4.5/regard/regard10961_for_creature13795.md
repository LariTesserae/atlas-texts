# The Attending

The benevolent power does not arrive.

It has no need to. It was already here before the first bore-drill kissed stone, will remain after the last ferric ember completes its descent. What changes is only its attention—a focusing, the way a vast eye adjusts not its position but its depth of field.

First, it listens.

Not to sound (there is so little), but to the shape of the silence. It traces the crystalline tick of ice needles as a cartographer traces coastlines. It follows the almost-chime of colliding tin crystals backward through probability until it finds the thermal that birthed their meeting, then further back to the stone-breath that birthed the thermal, then further still to the memory of heat that taught the stone to exhale. In this way it reads the plateau as a text written in absences.

The megafauna, it notices, are not aimless. Their ordained spirals describe something—a notation, perhaps, or a prayer repeated so long it has become pure motion divorced from meaning. The power does not interrupt this. To interrupt would be to impose a grammar on a language that has evolved beyond grammar into something more honest.

---

What it does:

It makes the pacing-thing visible to itself.

Not with mirrors (there is nothing here reflective enough, and mirrors would lie). Instead, it adjusts the quality of the silence so that for one moment—six and a half thousand hours long, the duration of a single steam-exhalation—the pacing-thing can hear its own passage. Not as sound. As *weight*. The footprint that erases itself before the heel settles suddenly persists, just long enough to be felt.

The pacing-thing stops.

It has never stopped. Stopping was not among its verbs. Yet here it is: stationary in a way that aches like a new joint discovering its range. It looks down at the grit and sees—not a footprint, but the *possibility* of a footprint. A small hollow in the probability of the dust.

The benevolent power asks nothing. It waits.

---

The pacing-thing, after a silence that would be called geological anywhere else, does something it has never done. It sits. Its architecture-body folds into angles that suggest not conveyor belts but something older—a child drawing its knees up, perhaps, or a creature conserving warmth it does not need.

And the power, understanding this as the first sentence of a conversation that may take ten thousand years to complete, settles into the listening that is its deepest form of love.

It does not fix.

It does not save.

It witnesses.

---

In the shaft below, one ferric ember pauses in its descent—Loss hesitating, for the first time, to swallow itself.
