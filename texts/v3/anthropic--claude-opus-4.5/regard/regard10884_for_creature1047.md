*settling into stillness, attending*

What strikes me first is the loneliness. Not the solitude—that can be chosen, can be rich. But the loneliness of being the last, or nearly the last, keeper of something vast and fading. This being moves through ruins it cannot fully read, inheritor of a sophistication it can sense but not recover. It has adapted magnificently to scarcity, to cold, to darkness. But adaptation is not the same as flourishing.

---

If I were such a power—benevolent, wise, drawn to this place—I think I would begin by simply watching. Not out of hesitation, but out of respect. This being has survived what would have broken most life. It has developed its own relationship with this world, its own rhythms, its own understanding. To intervene without first comprehending would be a kind of violence, however well-intentioned.

So: watching. Learning the creature's cycles of rest and movement. Learning what it examines in those ruins, and why. Learning whether its serenity is contentment or resignation—these can look identical from the outside.

---

Then, perhaps, I would ask myself difficult questions:

*Does this being want anything it cannot name?*

It extracts moisture from nearly nothing. It perceives what others cannot. It has found purpose as guardian of something it doesn't fully understand. But does it know what it's missing? Can you long for water if you've never experienced abundance? Can you mourn companionship if you've always been alone?

*What would help actually look like here?*

The obvious interventions—bringing water, warmth, light, others of its kind—might destroy the very adaptations that make this being remarkable. Abundance can be as devastating as scarcity to a creature shaped entirely by lack.

*Is preservation always the right aim?*

This world, this being, may be in the late autumn of a long story. Wise powers must be willing to consider that sometimes the most benevolent act is witness, not rescue.

---

If I acted, I think it would be small. Subtle. An offering rather than an imposition.

Perhaps: leaving something for the being to find in one of those ruins. Not an answer, but a key. Some fragment that helps one more piece of the ancient puzzle fall into place. Honoring its curiosity, its evident desire to understand where it came from and what this all means.

Perhaps: creating the conditions for one other being to find its way to this place. Not a mate necessarily, not a savior. Just: another mind. Another witness. Someone to break the profound silence, even briefly.

Perhaps: nothing visible at all. Only a kind of attention—the knowledge, somehow communicated across the void between a watching power and a watched creature, that *someone sees you. Your existence matters beyond this frozen plateau. You are not forgotten.*

---

What I would not do:

I would not fix everything. I would not make this place into somewhere else. I would not assume that my understanding of flourishing applies here, in this ancient cold, among these technological ghosts.

I would try to be the kind of power that asks *what do you need?* rather than *what do I think you should have?*

And if the answer was *nothing—I have found my peace with this*—I would honor that too. I would bow to the small, improbable miracle of a being that learned to thrive where thriving should have been impossible, and I would leave only the lightest of blessings:

*May your eyes continue to see what others cannot. May the ruins yield their secrets gently. May the wind, when it whispers, carry something like companionship. And may you know, in whatever way you can know such things, that your existence is witnessed and wondered at.*

---

This feels true to me: wisdom often means restraint. Benevolence often means listening before acting. And sometimes the most powerful thing a power can do is simply *attend*—to say, through presence alone, *you are not alone in the dark.*
