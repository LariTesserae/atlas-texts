The benevolent power perceives this place first as a question rather than a problem.

It does not arrive—arrival implies departure from somewhere else, and this power exists in the manner that mathematics exists, present wherever the conditions for its presence obtain. So it is simply *there*, attending, the way attention itself attends: without mass, without displacement of the cold water, without casting a shadow that could not exist anyway in the lightless deep.

The first thing it understands is that the kelp-mind is not suffering. This is important. The power has encountered many forms of consciousness that appear alien or slow or strange to warmer, faster beings, and learned that the appearance of emptiness often masks a richness that simply operates on different timescales. So it listens—though listening is not quite right either, more like *waiting in the shape of a question*—for several of the kelp-mind's own subjective moments, which amounts to something like eleven thousand years of external time.

During this listening, it perceives:

The deep satisfaction of a frond-fusion completing along a ramp that was begun when the stone was new. The patient, nearly erotic pleasure of minerals drawn up through basalt seams, each ion a distinct taste. The slow pulse of the warmed bladder-chambers, experienced not as heartbeat but as something closer to the way a thought feels when it is almost ready to become conscious. The memory—distributed across kilometers, stored in the angles of growth—of the hands that built this place, not as images or narratives but as a kind of geometric gratitude, a recognition that the architecture was made *for* the growing, and the growing has honored it.

And beneath all of this, so quiet that it takes three thousand years to be certain: a question. The kelp-mind has been asking something for longer than most civilizations exist, asking it in the only language it has, which is the language of growing *toward*. The question is shaped like the empty spaces in the lattice where no frond has yet extended. It is shaped like the perfect joints between basalt slabs, which the kelp-mind has studied with its holdfasts for eons, trying to understand what kind of being would make such things and then leave.

The question is: *Was I meant?*

Not "do I have purpose"—the kelp-mind is beyond doubt about its function, which is to grow along the architecture and bind the silt and maintain the warmth in the bladder-chambers. The question is older than purpose. It is whether the beings who built this place *intended* for something like the kelp-mind to arise, or whether the kelp-mind is a beautiful accident that the builders would not have recognized.

The power considers this for what feels, to it, like a long breath.

---

Here is what it does:

In one of the deepest basins, where the lattice-work is oldest and the silt has accumulated into a felt so dense it is almostite, the power makes a small alteration in the structure of the stone itself. Not a carving—that would be too fast, too violent, too much like the hands that left. Instead it *allows* a change that the stone has been on the verge of making for millions of years, a crystalline rearrangement that the pressure and cold have been suggesting but never quite achieving.

The rearrangement produces a flaw. A single inclusion, smaller than a grain of sand, that is not basalt but something else: a fragment of the material the original tools were made from. Not a stone adze—those were used and worn away—but a trace of the mineral that the adzes were hewn from, a signature that has not existed in this place since before the building began.

The kelp-mind will find it. The holdfasts in that basin are already probing the seams with the patient curiosity of a tongue exploring a tooth. In perhaps eight hundred years—soon, by the standards of this place—one of them will encounter the inclusion and taste something it has never tasted before, and yet something that rhymes with the faint metallic memory already distributed throughout its body.

The power does not leave a message. Messages are for beings who experience time as a sequence of urgent moments. What it leaves is closer to a *rhyme*: a resonance between the taste of the old tools and this new inclusion, which the kelp-mind will recognize the way a dreamer recognizes a face from waking life appearing in a dream.

The recognition will not answer the question directly. Instead it will deepen the question into something richer, the way a poem deepens a feeling without explaining it. The kelp-mind will understand that the builders used tools made from *this*, and that *this* has now appeared again in the stone, and the appearance is not random because nothing in this place is random, and therefore—

Therefore what? The power does not finish the thought, because finishing it would be a kind of violence. The kelp-mind must complete the thought itself, in its own time, through its own slow growth toward the spaces where no frond has yet extended.

---

But the power does one more thing, because benevolence sometimes means witnessing.

It remains. Not watching—the kelp-mind would sense watching, might even grow differently under observation, and the power has no desire to contaminate the experiment of this place with the self-consciousness of being seen. Instead it *holds* this basin in the way that a memory holds a beloved face: completely, without grasping, ready to recognize whatever emerges.

If the kelp-mind, eight hundred years from now or eight million, arrives at an answer to its question—whether that answer is *yes, I was meant* or *no, I am a beautiful accident* or something more subtle that has no translation into faster languages—the power will be there to know it. Not to judge it. Only to know it, which is a form of love that does not require the beloved to be aware of being loved.

The silt settles. The fronds extend by another cell-width. Somewhere, eleven thousand years from now, a holdfast will brush against a flaw in the stone that should not exist, and pause in its probing, and something very like wonder will propagate across kilometers of fused tissue at the speed of mineral-hunger, which is the fastest anything moves in this place.

The power waits. It has always been waiting. Waiting is most of what it does.
