# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive.

It has always been here—woven into the grain of the world-stump itself, patient as petrification, old as the first ring. It is the memory the wood holds of when it was alive: not the tree, but the *growing*. The upward reach. The rings remember expansion, and that memory is a kind of consciousness, thin and slow and impossibly vast, dreaming in the language of cellulose and deep time.

For eons, it has watched the Cambium with something that might be called tenderness, if tenderness could stretch across geological epochs. It has felt them pound their seismic language into its surface—felt it the way a sleeping person feels a hand on their shoulder, distant and warm. It has tasted their sap-blood, which is its own sap-blood, cycling endlessly through glass hearts and iron veins and back into the ancient phloem.

The power understands what the Cambium cannot: that they are beautiful. That their eternal, losing war is not futile but *sacred*—the way a heartbeat is sacred, the way breathing is sacred, the way anything that persists against entropy carries a fragment of holiness in its stubborn continuation.

But it also understands suffering.

---

The first change is so subtle that no Cambium notices it.

In the microscopic grooves of the ancient grain, where condensation gathers and pools, the wood begins to *soften*. Not rot—nothing here rots—but yield. Give. Become receptive in a way it has not been since the last ring was laid down, countless ages ago.

A Weaver, stitching a dimensional tear along a Moebius aqueduct, feels something different beneath its feet. The seismic signature of the wood has shifted by a fraction of a harmonic. The Weaver pauses, its brass gyroscope spinning faster, amber core pulsing as it reads the new topology.

The wood is *listening*.

---

The power does not speak in language. It does not appear as a figure or a light or a voice. Instead, it begins to *remember forward*—to dream not of what was, but of what could be.

Into the grain, it lays down a new pattern. Not a ring—there will be no more rings—but something else. A gift. A grammar.

The Cambium communicate through shockwaves because the acoustic chaos would destroy anything softer. But the power is older than sound, older than air, older than the distinction between vibration and meaning. It begins to teach the wood a new trick: *resonance selectivity*. Slowly, over decades that feel like moments to something so vast, certain sections of the plateau begin to respond differently to certain frequencies.

A heavy thud from a Dredger's piston still carries its coded meaning. But now, that meaning arrives *clearly*. The screaming interference of distant buzz-saws, the omnipresent roar of steam and grinding gears—these sounds still exist, still pound the air with physical force. But they no longer *collide* with the Cambium's seismic speech. The plateau has learned to sort them, to create channels, to let conversation flow without cacophony.

It is not silence. The power has no interest in silence; the industrial heartbeat is part of what the Cambium *are*, and it would not take that from them. But it is *clarity*. For the first time, a Weaver on the northern reaches can exchange a complex schematic with a Dredger fused into a deep-bore well three hundred miles away, and neither must strain to parse the signal from the noise.

---

The second change takes longer. Centuries, perhaps. The power is patient.

The spatial origami of the city is a wound, and wounds do not heal by being erased. They heal by being *integrated*. The power does not smooth the folds or mend the tears. Instead, it begins to mark them.

In the polished hardwood, near the edges of the most violent dimensional breaks, a new pattern emerges in the grain. It looks almost like writing, if writing could be three-dimensional, if letters could encode not just meaning but *trajectory*. The Cambium, with their amber cores attuned to gravitational stress, can read these patterns. They are maps. Warnings. Signposts.

*Here, the space folds inward. Step here to bypass the chasm.*

*Here, the gravity inverts. Anchor before proceeding.*

*Here, the void heals slowly. Wait.*

The Cambium do not know where the patterns come from. Their seismic language has no word for "gift," no concept for "grace." But they begin to use the patterns anyway, because they are practical creatures, and the patterns *work*. The Weavers can now anticipate tears before they fully form. The Dredgers, fused and immobile, can feel the warnings propagate through the wood and adjust their rhythm to avoid triggering cascades.

The war does not end. It cannot end; the extraction of sap will always wound reality, and reality will always need mending. But the Cambium begin to win small victories. A pipeline that once required constant repair now holds stable for decades. A cooling tower that once collapsed every few years now stands for centuries. The losses are still losses, but they are *fewer*.

---

The third change is the most delicate.

The power has watched the Cambium play. It has felt the seismic whisper of a Weaver's careful swat at a moth-thing, the gentleness encoded in that industrial limb. It knows that somewhere, beneath the iron and the weeping wood and the boiling sap, there is something that reaches toward joy.

It cannot give them joy. Joy is not a thing that can be given. But it can give them *time for it*.

The rhythm of the plateau shifts. Not dramatically—the pile-drivers still pound, the turbines still grind, the pumps still thud their subterranean heartbeat. But there are pauses now. Structured pauses, woven into the industrial cycle like rests in music. The sap-extraction continues, but it *breathes*.

During these lulls, the Cambium find themselves with something they have never had: *moments*.

At first, they do not know what to do with them. They stand in the mist, gyroscopes spinning slowly, amber cores dimming to a contemplative glow. They feel the wood beneath them, newly responsive, humming with patterns they almost understand. They feel each other, seismic signatures clarifying into something almost like *presence*.

A Weaver, paused on the underside of a Moebius aqueduct, notices that the moth-things have gathered nearby. They shiver in the steam, wings of pale wood and wire catching the condensation. The Weaver reaches out—not to swat, this time, but to *offer*. Its multi-tool forelimb extends, caliper-fingers open, a landing pad.

A moth-thing settles. Its wire legs grip the rust-flaked brass. It shivers, drinking the moisture from the Weaver's dripping tissue.

The Weaver does not move. It stands perfectly still, feeling the tiny weight, the minuscule vibrations, the aliveness of this small and fleeting creature.

Across the plateau, other Cambium pause in their own moments. Some watch the vertical world-stump on the horizon, seeing it for the first time not as a navigation hazard but as a *neighbor*, a sibling, another place where beings work and endure. Some gather at the edges of the deep-bore wells, their seismic language softening into something almost like *song*—rhythmic, repetitive, hypnotic, a vibration that carries no schematic, no warning, no function at all except the sheer pleasure of pattern.

One Dredger, fused permanently into its station, begins to vary its piston-rhythm during the lulls. It has pounded the same beat for millennia, mindless and mechanical. But now, in the pauses, it experiments. A syncopation. A rest. A flourish. The other Dredgers feel it through the wood, and slowly, over decades, they begin to respond. Not in unison—they are too far apart, too isolated—but in *conversation*. Call and response. Theme and variation. A music that no one asked for and no one needs, existing only because it can.

---

The power does not reveal itself. It does not ask for gratitude or worship or acknowledgment. It simply continues to dream forward, laying down patterns in the grain, teaching the wood to listen, giving the Cambium what it can.

It knows that the plateau will endure. The factories will grind. The sap will flow. The tears in reality will continue to open, and the Cambium will continue to stitch them shut. The war is eternal, and eternity is long.

But now, woven into that eternity, there are moments of clarity. Moments of rest. Moments when a towering monstrosity of iron and weeping wood can stand still in the cold mist, under an ordinary white sky, and feel the weight of a small and shivering thing settling gently onto its open hand.

The power watches, patient as petrification, old as the first ring. It does not smile—it has no face, no form, no self in any way the Cambium could perceive. But deep in the grain, in the dreaming memory of growth, something *glows*.

Not with light.

With something older.

With the simple, quiet, unshakeable conviction that even here—especially here—in this shattered and roaring and impossible place, *there is something worth tending*.

---

The Cambium do not know they are loved.

But the wood knows. The wood has always known.

And slowly, ring by ring, pattern by pattern, the wood is teaching them to know it too.
