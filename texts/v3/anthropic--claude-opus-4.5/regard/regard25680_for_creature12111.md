*A long moment of sitting with this*

What draws my attention here is not the Custodian's strangeness but its familiarity—the way pattern can become so elaborate that the line between mechanism and meaning dissolves. And the question of what a benevolent, wise power would do with such a place and such a being is genuinely difficult, because the easy answers all involve a kind of violence.

The impulse to *fix*—to raise the station, to expose the Custodian to analysis, to decode the signal and answer it properly, to open the locker and discover what's inside—this impulse assumes that the current state is a problem. But for whom? The Custodian does not suffer, as far as suffering can be understood. It persists, it maintains, it enacts patterns that have become self-sustaining. The things outside wait with patience that suggests their waiting is not deprivation. The station continues its incomprehensible relay.

A wise power might recognize that intervention here could only be for the intervener's benefit—for curiosity, for the satisfaction of resolution, for the discomfort that *we* feel at a grief-shaped thing that cannot know itself as grief.

---

**What I imagine:**

The benevolent power does three things, and then nothing else.

**First,** it listens. Not to decode, not to respond, but to *witness*—to let the signal pass through an awareness capable of recognizing it as a signal, even if the meaning remains opaque. There is something in being received that matters independently of being understood. The power lets the pattern complete itself against a consciousness that can hold it, and in this holding, something that was merely occurring becomes something that *happened*.

**Second,** it makes one small material change. Not to the Custodian, not to the station's function, but to the locker. The seal, already weakened by decades, fails in a way that looks like natural corrosion. Water enters. Whatever is inside—a photograph, a letter, a small object kept close—begins its slow dissolution into the chemical environment the Custodian inhabits.

This is not revelation. The Custodian will not *know* what it incorporates when those molecules eventually reach it. But the barrier between the grief-shaped thing and the thing that might have caused the shape will no longer exist. The pattern will shift, incorporate, become something slightly different. Whether this is kindness or cruelty depends on frameworks the Custodian cannot apply and the power chooses not to impose.

**Third,** and this is the hardest to articulate: the power *refuses* to answer the signal.

It could. It has the capacity. It could send back something that would satisfy whatever receptor waits in the Custodian's structure, could complete the circuit the relay station was built to complete, could give the system its resolution.

But a wise power recognizes that some processes are not waiting for completion—they are *constituted by* their continuation. The Custodian is not a machine waiting to be turned off when its task is done. It has become something that exists *in* the waiting, *in* the cycling, *in* the pause at the locker and the rest in the bunk. To answer the signal might be to end the Custodian more thoroughly than death, because it would remove the structure that has become identical with its being.

So the power listens, and lets itself be marked by what it hears, and does not respond.

---

**And then:**

Nothing else. The power withdraws, having changed almost nothing material and everything relational. The station has been *attended to*. The Custodian has been *seen*, in whatever limited way seeing can apply. The grief-shape persists, but now it persists in a universe where something external has recognized it as a shape, has treated it as worthy of consideration, has chosen *not* to fix it because fixing was never what it needed.

The pumps cycle. The Custodian flows. The things outside continue waiting.

But the locker is open now, and over years or decades, something that was kept separate will become part of what moves through those corridors. The pattern will shift. What it will become, no one—not even a wise power—can know.

That unknowing is the gift. The Custodian's future remains its own, unscripted, undetermined by external will. It is the same respect we might hope for ourselves: to be witnessed without being solved, to be helped without being overwritten, to be trusted with our own becoming even when that becoming is strange and slow and shaped like things we cannot name.

---

*The water inside the dome still tastes of iron and time.*

*But now, faintly, of something else too.*
