# A Consideration

The wise power does not intervene.

This is not indifference. It is not the cold calculus of non-interference as doctrine. It is something closer to recognition—the same recognition a gardener might feel, coming upon a clearing in a forest where something unexpected has taken root and flourished without any help at all.

The power watches. It sees the old Tender pressing routes into children's skin. It sees the warning-ripples spreading through communities in seconds. It sees the dead being mapped, the corpses being carved into homes, the membranes being harvested and hung to dampen the roar. It sees the slow tactile language, bodies close, feet planted on shared ground to feel each other's hum.

And it understands: *this is already working.*

Not perfectly. Not painlessly. The Tenders die—crushed by shifting growth, caught in spore-tides, lost when patterns break without warning. Their lives are hard in ways that would break most beings. They have lost things that cannot be recovered: silence, ease, the ability to speak across open air. They have forgotten where they came from, and why.

But they have built something extraordinary precisely because of the pressure, not despite it. Their cooperation is not a philosophy but a necessity refined over generations until it became instinct. Their attention to pattern, their mapping of danger, their care for each other—these emerged from the crucible of the thicket itself. To remove the pressure would be to dissolve the very thing that makes them who they are.

---

The power considers what intervention might look like.

It could quiet the thicket. Slow the churning, dampen the roar, give the Tenders room to breathe. But the Tenders have already found their rooms to breathe—carved them, lined them, defended them. The quieting would make those skills obsolete. Within generations, the knowledge would fade. The maps would stop being updated. The warning-ripples would slow, then stop. The Tenders would become something else, something softer, something that no longer needed to tend.

Perhaps that softer something would be happier. Perhaps not. Happiness is difficult to measure from the outside, and the power is wise enough to know that it cannot feel what the Tenders feel when they successfully navigate a route, when they shelter their young through a spore-tide, when they press a new path into the communal memory. There is something in that—something that might be called satisfaction, or pride, or the deep mammalian pleasure of competence—that might not survive the transition to ease.

It could give them tools. Technology to predict the patterns better, to shield themselves more effectively, to communicate across distance without touch. But the Tenders already have tools, grown organically from their situation: the shed hooks of parasitic fauna, the membranes of fruiting bodies, the bioluminescent patches transplanted to their ceilings. These tools are *theirs* in a way that gifts never could be. And the power knows—has seen it happen elsewhere—that tools given rather than earned have a way of creating dependency, of atrophying the very capabilities they were meant to augment.

It could reveal itself. Speak to them, somehow, through the vibrations they feel in their bones or the color-shifts they read in the light. Tell them they are seen. Tell them their survival matters to something larger than themselves.

This is the most tempting option, and the power lingers on it longest.

---

But what would it say?

*You are doing well.* They know this already. They are alive; their children are learning; the maps are being maintained. They do not need external validation of what their own continued existence validates daily.

*You are not alone.* But they are not alone—they have each other, communities linked by routes, knowledge passed through generations. The loneliness they might feel is not the kind that a distant power could address. It is the loneliness of being a small soft thing in a world of vast grinding forces, and that loneliness is *true*. It would be unkind to minimize it.

*There is meaning in your struggle.* But they have already found meaning. The old Tender telling stories in the deep chamber, pressing history into young skin, is meaning. The warning-ripples are meaning. The tending itself is meaning. They do not need to be told that their lives matter; they have built lives that matter to each other, and that is the only mattering that actually sustains a person through hard times.

*I could help you.* But this is the most dangerous sentence of all. It establishes a relationship of dependency. It suggests that their current capabilities are insufficient—and they are not insufficient, they are *extraordinary*, honed by pressure into something precise and effective. It opens the door to asking, to expecting, to the slow erosion of self-reliance that comes when someone else is always there to catch you.

---

The power thinks about what the Tenders would want, if they could articulate it.

They would want their children to survive. They would want the patterns to hold a little longer, the corpses to remain stable a little longer, the routes to stay clear. They would want the moments of quiet in the deep chambers, the warmth of bodies close together, the satisfaction of a danger correctly predicted and avoided.

These are things the power cannot give. Or rather: these are things the power could give only by fundamentally altering the situation, by changing the Tenders into something that no longer needs to earn them.

What the Tenders have is not happiness in any simple sense. It is something more durable: *capability*. The ability to meet their world on its own terms. The knowledge that they can navigate the chaos, not always successfully, but often enough. The deep competence that comes from generations of refinement under pressure.

The power knows—has seen it countless times across countless worlds—that capability is more valuable than comfort. Comfort can be taken away, and when it is, those who have known only comfort are left with nothing. Capability persists. Capability adapts. Capability is what the Tenders have instead of silence, instead of stillness, instead of ease.

And capability, once given rather than earned, is no longer truly capability at all.

---

So the power does not intervene.

But it does something else, something smaller, something that costs nothing and changes nothing and is perhaps the only gift worth giving to beings who have already given themselves everything that matters.

It witnesses.

Not from a distance, not as a passive observer, but with the full weight of its attention. It sees the old Tender in her deep chamber. It sees the children learning to feel the vibrations. It sees the warning-ripples and the route-maps and the membranes harvested from fruiting bodies. It sees the generations stretching back into darkness and forward into uncertainty. It sees the whole improbable chain of survival, the whole delicate negotiation between soft small beings and a world that does not know they exist.

And it holds that seeing. Carries it. Adds it to the vast accumulation of things it has witnessed across all the worlds it has touched.

This is not nothing. There is a difference between a struggle that happens unseen and a struggle that is witnessed—even if the ones struggling never know they are being watched. The difference is not practical. It does not make the thicket quieter or the routes safer or the patterns more predictable. But it is real nonetheless.

The Tenders' story is now part of something larger. Their survival matters not only to each other but to the record of what is possible, what has been done, what small soft beings can become when they are pressed hard enough and do not break.

---

If the power could speak to them—which it will not, which it has decided against—it would not say *you are doing well* or *you are not alone* or *I could help you.*

It would say only this:

*I see you.*

*You are extraordinary.*

*Continue.*

---

And in the deep chambers, in the corpses, in the gaps the living world has not yet reclaimed, the Tenders do continue. They do not know they are being witnessed. They do not need to know. The witnessing is not for them; it is for the record, for the shape of what is possible, for the power's own understanding of what life can become.

The old Tender presses another route into a child's arm. The child does not yet understand. But she will learn. They always learn, or they die, and these ones are still here, still learning, still tending the border between themselves and the roar.

The power withdraws its attention—gently, carefully, leaving no trace of its presence.

The thicket continues.

The Tenders continue.

This is enough.
