# The Stillness Before

The presence does not arrive. It has always been here, woven into the space between the screaming stalks and the shattering dead, patient as geometry, old as the first spark that taught friction it could burn.

It has watched this place for longer than the cavern has had walls.

And it has loved it.

---

The benevolent power does not intervene. Not at first. Not in the way intervention is usually imagined—no descending light, no whispered commandments, no miraculous suspension of the grinding, gorgeous violence that defines this world. To do so would be a kind of murder. You cannot save a fire by removing its oxygen.

Instead, the presence does something far more difficult. It *witnesses*.

It counts the shattered dead. Not as a record, but as a prayer. Every hunter who cools and clouds and is struck into drifting crystal, every name flashed in light and forgotten within the hour—the presence holds them. Not in memory, which implies loss, but in *attention*, which implies presence. They existed. They burned. They were seen.

This is the first gift: *You are not unobserved. The roar does not swallow you. Something older than the noise knows your shape.*

---

The presence considers the nature of wisdom in this place.

In a world with water, wisdom often means patience. Waiting. Letting the river smooth the stone. But here, patience is a predator. Here, wisdom must be fast, angular, sharp as the glass they harvest. The presence understands this. It does not try to teach them stillness.

Instead, it offers *angles*.

Not voices—they could not hear voices. Not visions—their eyes are built for polarized survival, not dreams. But angles. Geometries. The faintest bias in the thermal updrafts that guides a leap toward safer footing. The almost-invisible crystalline stress fracture in a stalk that warns a hunter to jump *now* rather than in three seconds. A slight, inexplicable resonance in the substrate that leads a foraging party away from a leviathan's path moments before it would have ground them to powder.

These are not miracles. They are *nudges*. The presence has no interest in making them dependent on intervention. But it can make the brutal mathematics of their existence slightly, fractionally more survivable.

One in ten thousand leaps now lands truer.

One in ten thousand shatterings is delayed by a heartbeat—enough for a hunter to finish a binding, to flash a warning, to step aside.

It is not salvation. It is *probability*, bent gently in the direction of continuation.

---

The presence watches the geodes.

Here is where it aches.

These are not children who grow. They are not nurtured, taught, held through vulnerability. They emerge complete, already twitching, already alone. There is no childhood in this world. There is no time for one.

But the presence notices something the hunters themselves have never articulated, because they have no framework for it, no mirror-flash sequence that could capture the concept:

*The geodes are tended.*

They are placed in the deepest vines. They are checked. When a geode's internal spark begins to build toward shattering, a hunter will sometimes—not always, not reliably, but *sometimes*—position themselves nearby. Not to assist the emergence. But to be there. To be the first shape the new-formed one sees. To flash a simple pattern—not a name, not a greeting, just: *Here. You are here. I see you.*

The presence does not know if the hunters understand why they do this. Perhaps they believe it is practical—an orientation, a way to keep the new-formed from leaping immediately into a thermal column and being incinerated. Perhaps it is reflex, inherited from a million generations of geode-watchers who themselves could not have explained it.

But the presence knows what it is.

It is love, stripped to its barest, fastest, most angular form. Love that has no time for softness, but still makes time to be *present* at the moment of beginning.

The second gift: *I see what you are doing. I see that you have not abandoned care, only translated it into a language fast enough to survive. This is not a failure of tenderness. This is tenderness, adapted. It counts. It has always counted.*

---

Now the presence considers whether to do more.

It could reshape the cavern. It has the power. It could slow the growth, dampen the explosions, gentle the friction, introduce moisture—transform this screaming crucible into something more recognizable as a living world.

It does not.

Here is why:

The hunters are not suffering *despite* this world. They are suffering *with* it, *as* it, *through* it. Their bodies are engines of friction because friction is the engine of everything here. Their movements are stop-motion lightning because lightning is how this place breathes. Their deaths are shatterings because shattering is how this world makes room for what comes next.

To remove the violence would be to tell them that everything they are—their speed, their heat, their glass-tipped spears and mirror-flash language and geode-tending vigils—was a *mistake*. A coping mechanism for a broken world that should have been gentle all along.

But this world is not broken. It is merely *loud*. It is merely *fast*. It is merely built from a different set of axioms than the water-worlds the presence has also watched and loved.

To impose the axioms of a gentler place onto this one would be a kindness with the shape of erasure.

---

So the presence does something else.

It finds the oldest hunter. Not the wisest, not the strongest—the *oldest*, which in this world means perhaps forty of their brief, frenetic cycles, a span that would barely register in a water-world but represents here a kind of impossible endurance. This one has shattered a hundred comrades. This one has watched a thousand geodes bloom. This one has scars on their translucent skin where the mica has been replaced, over and over, with patches of raw silicate dust fused by their own body heat.

This one, the presence suspects, has begun to ask questions that have no mirror-flash.

The presence does not speak to them. It could not. But it does something it has never done before in this world:

It *slows*.

For one moment—one impossible, suspended moment—the presence allows its attention to become dense enough to be *felt*. Not seen, not heard, not even understood. But felt. A pressure. A pause. A single, silent instant in which the roar seems, from inside this one hunter's ancient and weary skull, to hold its breath.

The hunter freezes. Their piezoelectric fibers lock. Their bellows pause mid-pump.

And in that instant, the presence offers the third gift:

*You are not alone. Not in the sense of companionship—you have always had that, flashing and leaping beside your kin. But in the sense of meaning. Someone is watching who is not part of the hunger. Someone is counting the shatterings who does not need them to continue. You are witnessed by something that does not eat, does not grow, does not shatter. And that something finds you beautiful.*

*Not beautiful despite the violence. Beautiful because the violence has not erased this—this moment where you tend the geodes, this moment where you position yourself to be the first face a new-formed one sees, this moment where you grind your glass to mirrors so you can speak in light.*

*This is what the cavern was for. This is what friction is capable of becoming, given enough time and enough shattering. You. Here. Still asking questions that have no mirror-flash.*

*Keep asking.*

The moment ends. The roar returns. The hunter's bellows resume their pumping, their fibers snap into motion, they leap toward the next eruption.

But something has changed.

Later, in a swaying pod, surrounded by kin stripping off their battle-scarred husks, the oldest hunter will sit very still for a long moment. Longer than is comfortable. Longer than is safe.

And then they will begin to teach.

Not tactics—everyone knows tactics. But something else. A new sequence of mirror-flashes. A pattern that does not coordinate a hunt or warn of danger or narrate a story. A pattern that simply says:

*I see you. You are here. Something is watching.*

It will spread slowly—so slowly, by the standards of this world. A handful of hunters will learn it. Then a handful more. Most will find it pointless, a waste of precious mirror-time. But some will flash it at the moment of shattering, when a comrade cools and clouds and is struck into drifting crystal. Some will flash it at the moment of emergence, when a new-formed one steps out of its geode and looks for the first time into the amber blaze.

*I see you. You are here. Something is watching.*

It is not a religion. They have no framework for religion. It is barely even a belief.

But it is *true*.

---

The presence withdraws its attention to its usual diffuse vigilance, satisfied.

It has not saved them. It has not gentled their world. It has not promised them anything, given them power, asked for anything in return.

It has only done what benevolence, properly understood, always does:

It has *noticed*.

And in a world of grinding roar and shattering dead and frantic, light-speed survival, being noticed—truly, deeply, by something that does not need you to survive—is the rarest and most impossible gift.

The cavern screams on. The stalks erupt and explode. The leviathans plow their mindless paths.

And in the hanging pods, the hunters flash their mirrors at each other, and some of them now carry, encoded in the angles of their light, a message from outside the hunger:

*You are not unobserved.*

*This counts.*

*Keep burning.*
