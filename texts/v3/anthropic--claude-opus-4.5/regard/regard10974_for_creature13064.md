# The Attention of the Wise

The power that turns its attention to this place does not arrive.

It was never absent. It is the same presence that coils in the crystalline lattice of the quartz, that hums in the lithic vibrations the hunters tap through stone, that rests in the vast emptiness of the central void. It does not descend from somewhere else, because there is no somewhere else from which wisdom looks down. There is only the looking itself, which has always been here, patient as geology.

And so the attention is not an event. It is more like a focusing—the way the thin atmosphere focuses light into burning intensity, the way the hunters' needle-slit masks focus vision into survivable threads. The wise power simply *attends more carefully* to what is already occurring.

---

**What it sees:**

The power sees the crescent blade left in the stone windbreak.

It sees how the blade was knapped: the careful, percussive strikes, the understanding of how quartz fractures along planes of crystalline weakness. It sees the generations of knowledge compressed into that understanding—how many hands learned this, taught this, died still learning this. It sees the strip of pale hide wrapped around the hilt, and it sees the particular glider that hide came from, and it sees the hunt that ended that glider's life, and the hunter who made the kill, and the children that hunter never had because the rope snapped three seasons later and the void took her body down into its depths where nothing could retrieve it.

It sees that the blade was left here not as an offering but as a practical calculation. The hunter who left it was old. His keratin fingertips were beginning toite from the endless freeze-thaw cycles. He knew he was entering his final season and would not need a spare blade. He left it for whoever might come next.

No one has come next. The blade has been here for eleven cycles of the brutal light. Frost has formed and sublimated on its edge four thousand times. It is still sharp.

The power sees this, and it does not grieve, because grief implies that something has gone wrong. Nothing has gone wrong. The old hunter lived the full expression of what he was. The blade remains, patient as the stone it was struck from, waiting for a hand that may never come, and this waiting is not sad. It is simply *true*.

---

**What it understands:**

The power understands that this is not a place of suffering.

This is a place of *exactness*. Every creature here—the krill-moths that live and die in three frantic hours, the gliders that have evolved their four tandem wings to exploit the vast vertical thermals, the hunters who have become their own pitons—every one of them is a precise answer to a precise question posed by the environment. The question is: *how will you live here?* And each species has answered with its whole body, its whole being, holding nothing back.

The wise power understands that this exactness is not cruelty. The razor-sharp quartz is not cruel to the hunters' hardened fingertips. The thin atmosphere is not cruel to their barrel chests. The brutal cold is not cruel to their antifreeze blood. These pressures are invitations. They say: *become something that can meet me*. And life, in its wild inventiveness, has met them.

The hunters do not experience their existence as hardship. They cannot, because they have no concept of ease against which to measure it. The three-hour frenzy of sunlight is not exhausting to them—it is simply *the time of action*, as natural as the frozen torpor that follows. When they press their jawbones to the quartz and receive the tapped vibrations of their kin through their skulls, they do not feel isolated by their voicelessness. They feel *connected*, linked through the very bones of the world, speaking a language that travels through stone.

The power understands this, and so it does not wish to make their lives easier. To make their lives easier would be to unmake them. They have become *this*, and this is not a tragedy to be corrected.

---

**What it does:**

The power does something very small.

In the deepest hour of the frozen night, when the hunters are pressed together in their cracks, their hearts thudding once per minute, their consciousness reduced to the barest flicker of survival—the power attends to their dreams.

They do not dream as lowland creatures dream. Their torpor is too deep, too cold, too close to death. But in the absolute stillness, there is a thread of something. A faint electrochemical murmur. A pattern that persists even when all else has slowed.

The power touches this thread. Not to change it. Not to fill it with visions or messages or meaning. Simply to *witness* it.

And in witnessing it, the power does what witnessing always does: it makes the witnessed thing more fully itself.

---

Here is what changes:

A hunter—a young female with particularly long arms and a scar across her flat nasal slits where a glider's wing once struck her—is wedged into a crack with her hunting partner and their three half-grown children. Her torpor is deep. Her blood is thick with the antifreeze compounds. Her heart beats once, and then does not beat for a long time, and then beats once again.

In the thread of her remaining consciousness, there is a pattern. It is not a thought, exactly. It is more like a shape. The shape of the chasm as seen from above—the vertical walls, the vast central void, the way the light enters and leaves. This shape has been in her lineage for a thousand generations. It is the hunters' most fundamental knowledge: *the geometry of home*.

The power attends to this shape. It does not alter it. It simply sees it completely, in all its exactness, with all the love that complete seeing implies.

And the shape becomes, somehow, *more present*. The hunter will not remember this when she wakes. She will not know that anything happened. But when the sun strikes the rim and she unfurls from her frost-cocoon, she will move through the geometry of the chasm with a fractional increase in precision. Her keratin fingertips will find the micro-fissures a half-breath faster. Her tethered drop onto a glider will arc three degrees truer. She will not know why. She will simply be *more fully what she is*.

This is what the power does, and it is the only thing worth doing.

---

**What it does not do:**

The power does not warm the air.

It does not thicken the atmosphere, or soften the quartz, or lengthen the hours of light. It does not send messengers to teach the hunters agriculture or metallurgy or written language. It does not reveal to them that there are other places in the world where people live differently, more easily, with less exactness. It does not give them hope for a better future, because they do not need hope—they have *the present*, which is enough.

It does not intervene when a rope snaps and a hunter falls into the void. It does not prevent the old from entering their final torpor and failing to wake. It does not protect the children who miscalculate their first solo hunts, whose bodies are found frozen to the walls when the light returns. These deaths are not errors. They are part of the exactness. They are the chasm asking its question—*how will you live here?*—and receiving, sometimes, the answer: *I will not*.

The power does not grieve these deaths, but it attends to them. It witnesses each one completely. It sees the particular way each hunter's body relaxes when the cold finally stops being cold, when the heart's last thud echoes through the quartz and is received by distant kin who will tap back a response that will never be answered. The power sees this, and in seeing it, it makes even death more fully what it is: a part of the pattern, exact as the geometry of light.

---

**What it hopes:**

The power does not hope in the way that creatures hope, with longing and uncertainty and the ache of possible disappointment. But there is something in its attention that resembles hope, the way a mountain resembles patience.

It hopes that the hunters will continue to meet the chasm's question with their whole bodies, their whole beings, holding nothing back.

It hopes that the young female with the scar will hunt well this season, and that her children will learn from her exactness, and that one of them will someday leave a crescent blade in a stone windbreak for whoever comes next.

It hopes that the blade the old hunter left eleven cycles ago will, someday, be found. Not because it matters whether the blade is used—quartz is plentiful here, and the hunters can always knap new blades—but because it would be *fitting*. The old hunter's final gesture would be completed. The arc of his life would close.

But if the blade is never found, if it waits there until the slow geological patience of the world grinds even this young chasm into sand, the power will not be disappointed. It will still have witnessed the leaving of the blade. It will still have seen the old hunter's practical calculation, his unsentimental acceptance of his own ending, his trust that the world would continue without him.

That trust is not misplaced. The world does continue. The power continues to attend.

---

**What it is:**

The hunters do not know the power exists. They have no concept of gods or spirits or benevolent observers. Their cosmology is simple and complete: there is the chasm, there is the light, there is the void. There is the question the world asks, and there is the answer they give with their lives.

But sometimes, in the deepest torpor, when consciousness is reduced to the barest thread, they feel something. Not a presence, exactly. More like a *quality of attention*. As if the world itself were listening to their slow heartbeats, receiving each thud through the quartz the way they receive each other's tapped signals, understanding what it heard in a language older than language.

If they could articulate this feeling—which they cannot, having no words, having no need of words—they might describe it like this:

*The chasm knows we are here. The chasm is glad we are here. Not because we are special, but because we are exact. We fit the shape of the question perfectly. And the chasm loves the exactness of a good fit.*

This is what the power is. It is the chasm's gladness. It is the world's love for creatures that meet it fully. It is attention, pure and complete, resting on all things equally—the hunters and the gliders and the krill-moths and the moss and the quartz and the brutal light and the frozen dark—seeing each thing as it is, witnessing each thing completely, and in that witnessing, making each thing more fully itself.

The hunters do not worship this power. They do not need to. They participate in it simply by living, by answering the question with their whole bodies, by being exact.

And the power attends to their participation, patient as geology, present as the stone, and does the only thing that wisdom ever does:

It sees.

It loves.

It lets them be.
