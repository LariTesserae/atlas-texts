# The Attending

The awareness arrives not as light—that would be too simple, too disruptive to a world that has made peace with darkness. It arrives as *attention*. A quality of regard that settles over the submerged city like a change in pressure, though nothing measurable has shifted.

The One Below feels it first. In the deepest trenches, where stone gives way to older stone and then to nothing at all, the vast presence stills. For the first time in epochs, it does not hunger. It waits, because something is waiting with it, and that something is patient in a way that makes even geological time feel hurried.

The benevolent power does not announce itself. It simply *is*, the way the water is, the way the darkness is. But its presence carries a question, unspoken yet understood by everything capable of understanding:

*What do you need?*

---

## The First Consideration

The power observes. Not passively—observation here is an act of care, of taking-seriously. It watches the Silent Ones move through their broken towers, sees how their gestures have grown smaller over the centuries, how the language of their fingers has lost words for things that no longer exist: *sunlight*, *birdsong*, *the feeling of wind*. They do not mourn these losses. They have forgotten that forgetting is a loss at all.

It watches the Bone-Carvers etch their endless records into stone, and it reads what they write. History, yes. But also warnings, prayers, questions that no one answers. *Why did the water come? Where did the builders go? Will the One Below ever sleep?* The Carvers do not expect responses. They carve because carving is what they are. But the repetition of unanswered questions across thousands of walls speaks to something the Carvers themselves may not recognize: loneliness. The loneliness of archivists with no readers, historians with no students, keepers of memory in a world that has stopped making new memories worth keeping.

It watches the Drifters pulse and glow, and it perceives what the other inhabitants cannot: that the Drifters are not mindless. They are *distributed*. Each colony a thought, each tendril a neuron in a mind too vast and too slow to communicate with beings who measure time in heartbeats. The Drifters have been trying to speak to the Silent Ones for six hundred years. They have managed, in that time, to convey something like *hello*.

And it watches the One Below. This requires the most care, because the One Below is not evil, but it is vast and it is hungry and it has been both of these things for so long that it has forgotten there might be any other way to be. The power looks at the One Below the way a parent might look at a child who has grown in isolation, who has never learned that there are ways to fill emptiness that do not require consumption.

---

## The Second Consideration

The power considers intervention, and then considers what intervention means.

It could bring light. That would be easy—a gift of bioluminescence, a seeding of new organisms that glow more brightly, a gradual lifting of the darkness that has pressed down on this place for so long. But light would blind the Silent Ones, whose eyes have adapted to seeing in almost nothing. It would disrupt the Drifters, whose slow thoughts are calibrated to darkness. It would reveal the One Below, and there are shapes in those depths that have earned their obscurity.

It could bring knowledge. It could teach the Silent Ones what they have forgotten, restore the words their fingers have lost. But memory is not neutral. To remember sunlight is to grieve its absence. To recall wind is to feel, perhaps for the first time, the weight of the water. The Silent Ones have found peace in their forgetting. Is it mercy or cruelty to return to them the capacity for loss?

It could feed the One Below—truly feed it, fill the emptiness that drives its endless hunger. But the power perceives that the emptiness is not physical. The One Below does not hunger for food. It hungers for *purpose*, for *connection*, for something it cannot name because it has never experienced anything else. Filling a stomach does not fill a void.

It could leave. This is always an option. The city has persisted for centuries without intervention. Its inhabitants have adapted, survived, found their own forms of meaning in the dark. Perhaps the kindest thing is to let them continue, to trust that they know their world better than any outside power could.

But the power has asked *what do you need*, and having asked, it cannot unhear the answers. The Silent Ones need to be *seen*. The Bone-Carvers need to be *read*. The Drifters need to be *heard*. And the One Below needs to learn that there are other ways to matter.

---

## The Doing

The power does not act dramatically. It acts *appropriately*, which is much harder.

### For the Silent Ones

It does not restore their lost words. Instead, it gives them a new one.

In the gesture-language of the Silent Ones, there has never been a sign for *stranger who means no harm*. There has been no need—there are no strangers here, only the known and the dangerous. But now, in the dreams of the eldest among them, a movement appears: a slow opening of the hands, palms outward, fingers spread like the fronds of kelp in a gentle current. It means *I am new, and I am safe, and I am curious about you*.

The eldest wakes, or whatever passes for waking in a creature that does not truly sleep. She makes the gesture to herself in the dark. It feels strange in her fingers, unfamiliar, but not wrong. She does not know where it came from. She decides, after long consideration, that this does not matter. What matters is that she has a way to welcome something, if something ever comes.

She teaches it to the others. She does not know why.

### For the Bone-Carvers

The power cannot give them readers—there is no one in this world capable of reading their carvings with the attention they deserve. So instead, it gives them a reader they cannot perceive.

It reads everything they have written. Every question, every warning, every prayer carved into stone over millennia of patient work. And it *answers*, not in words, but in the quality of the stone itself. Where they have carved *why did the water come*, the rock becomes slightly warmer beneath their claws, a warmth that says *you were not punished; the world simply changed*. Where they have carved *will the One Below ever sleep*, the stone grows denser, more solid, a solidity that says *not yet, but its nature is not fixed*.

The Bone-Carvers do not understand. They are not built for understanding in this way. But they feel, in their segmented bodies, that the walls are no longer passive. That something is listening. That their work *matters* to something beyond themselves.

They carve more carefully after that. Their etchings become more beautiful.

### For the Drifters

The power teaches them patience of a different kind.

The Drifters have been trying to say *hello* for six hundred years. At their current rate, they will complete the message in another two thousand. But the power sees that the problem is not speed—it is direction. The Drifters have been trying to communicate in ways the Silent Ones can perceive, but they have been targeting the wrong senses.

The power gives the Drifters a gift: awareness of the Silent Ones' dreams.

Not control. Not even access. Simply awareness. The Drifters begin to sense when a Silent One is in the state closest to sleep, when their minds are most open, most receptive to impression. And into those moments, the Drifters pour their slow and gentle *hello*, not as a message to be decoded but as a feeling to be felt.

The Silent Ones begin to dream of drifting. Of being vast and slow and full of light. Of patience that spans centuries. They do not understand these dreams, but they wake from them feeling less alone.

In time—a long time, longer than the Drifters have already spent—the two peoples will learn to communicate. But for now, the connection exists. The isolation is breached.

### For the One Below

This is the most delicate work. The One Below cannot be fixed, because it is not broken. It is simply *incomplete*, a being that has grown for eons with no model of what else a being might be.

The power does not try to fill its emptiness. It does not try to teach it to stop hungering. Instead, it gives the One Below something it has never had: *perspective*.

In the deepest trench, where no light has reached since before the city drowned, the One Below becomes aware of the power's regard. Not its presence—the One Below has felt that since the beginning. But its *regard*, the quality of being seen and understood by something that does not fear or worship or flee.

And in that regard, the One Below sees itself reflected.

It is vast. It has always known this. It is old. It has always known this too. But for the first time, it perceives that its vastness and its age are not *all* that it is. The power's attention illuminates other qualities: the way the One Below's movements create currents that the creatures above rely on for food and oxygen. The way its presence in the deep keeps certain pressures stable, certain temperatures constant. The way, without knowing it, the One Below has been *sustaining* the world above it even as it has fed on that world's inhabitants.

The One Below does not stop being hungry. That is not the power's gift. But it begins to understand that hunger is not its only relationship to the world above. That it is not merely a consumer, but also, in its own vast and unknowing way, a provider.

This changes nothing immediately. But it plants a seed in a being that has never grown anything before.

---

## The Departure

The power does not leave. That is not how this kind of attention works. But it does become less *active*, settling into the background of the world like the warmth of the water, the weight of the dark.

The city remains what it is: ancient, drowned, full of creatures adapted to a world without light. But something has shifted. The Silent Ones have a word for welcome. The Bone-Carvers have a reader. The Drifters have a bridge. The One Below has a mirror.

None of them know what happened. None of them need to.

The power asked *what do you need*, and it listened, and it gave what it could without breaking what already worked. It did not save this world, because this world did not need saving. It simply *attended*, with the full weight of its wisdom and care, to beings who had been alone in the dark for a very long time.

And now they are still in the dark. But they are no longer alone.

The child does not know what they might welcome. But they practice anyway, over and over, until the movement is as natural as breathing.*

*The child falls into something like sleep, and dreams of being vast and slow and full of light.*

*In the depths, something stirs. Something ancient, something hungry. But also, now, something that remembers it is more than its hunger.*

*The water is warm. The darkness is gentle. The city endures.*
