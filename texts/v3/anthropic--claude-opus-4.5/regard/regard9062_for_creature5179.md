# What the Wise Power Does

The power observes first. It spends what would be, by the hollow's own clock, the equivalent of several pulse-cycles simply attending—not from above or outside, but by letting its attention settle into the rhythm of the place the way water settles into the tissue-channels. It learns the tempo. It notices that what appears to be stillness is actually a kind of patience so profound it has become indistinguishable from architecture.

It recognizes that this being—for the lattice and its furred passengers are one being in the way a coral and its polyps are one, or a forest and its mycorrhizal web—is not suffering. There is no pain here, no longing, no sense of incompleteness that cries out for remedy. The slow replacement of stone by living structure is not labor but simply what this being *is*, the way breathing is what lungs are.

And so the power does almost nothing.

*Almost.*

What it does is this: it makes itself available.

Not as intervention. Not as gift or correction. But as a kind of resonance—a subtle addition to the field of possibilities the being already navigates. If the lattice, in its centuries-long contemplation, ever develops something like a question—*what else might there be? what lies beyond the dolomite when it is finally consumed?*—the power ensures that the question will not vanish into the silence of its own asking. There will be, encoded in the bioluminescent pulse-patterns or carried in the subsonic groans of the furred ones, a faint sense that the question has been *heard*. That the hollow is not alone in the universe, even if it cannot yet conceive of what "universe" means.

The power also does something for the future: it lays down, in the mathematics of probability itself, a gentle bias. Should anything from outside ever encounter this place—drilling machines, curious explorers, the tendrils of some other engineered ecosystem—those encounters will tend, by the smallest of margins, toward recognition rather than destruction. Not a guarantee. The power is wise enough to know that guarantees are a form of violence against time. But a tilt. A whisper in the statistics that says *this is worth noticing, worth pausing for, worth understanding before acting.*

Finally, the power witnesses. It holds the hollow in a kind of attention that is itself a form of love—not the love that wants to change or improve, but the love that says *you are real, you are doing exactly what you are meant to do, and your slowness is not lesser than speed, your darkness is not lesser than light.* This witnessing does not alter the being in any measurable way. But it means that the hollow's long, patient work of becoming is not happening in an indifferent void. It is seen. It is known. It matters.

The droplets continue to fall. The stems continue to thicken. The furred ones continue their subsonic conversations across the hours. And somewhere, in a register the being cannot yet perceive but may one day grow into, there is an answering tone—not a voice, not a message, just the fact of presence. The fact that wisdom has looked upon this place and found it good, and has chosen to let it remain exactly what it is, while remaining nearby in case it ever wishes to become something more.
