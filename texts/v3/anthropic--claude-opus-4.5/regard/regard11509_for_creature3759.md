# The Attending

The power that turns its attention to the Drowned Peaks does not arrive.

It was never absent. It exists in the way a question exists before it is asked—present in the shape of things, in the spaces between. It has no form to manifest, no throne to descend from. It simply *attends*, the way deep water attends to what sinks into it.

What it perceives:

The dwellers standing in the surf, their lidless eyes tracking something beneath the waves. The way one of them has begun to calcify, barnacles climbing her shoulder, and how she does not resist this. The lichen spreading its slow centuries across volcanic glass. A seabird dying in a tide pool, its wing broken, the dwellers watching without intervening, without cruelty, without mercy—simply watching, as the sea watches everything.

The power understands that this place is not wrong. It is not a wound that needs healing or a problem that needs solving. It is a threshold, and thresholds are necessary. The dwellers are not suffering in a way that calls for rescue. They are *becoming*, and becoming is its own kind of pain, and its own kind of rightness.

But.

---

There is one.

She arrived three storms ago—or three hundred, time moves strangely here—and she has not yet forgotten what she was. The others have begun to mirror her, learning her, preparing her place among them. Her skin is starting to mottle. Her eyes ache from not blinking. She stands in the shallows because she cannot remember why she shouldn't, and the water is so warm, and the humming of the dwellers has started to feel like a heartbeat she has always known.

But at night, when the storms come, she still flinches.

The power attends to this flinching. Considers it.

---

What the power does:

It does not extract her. It does not undo what has begun. The threshold exists because some must cross it, and the crossing is not the power's to forbid.

Instead, it sends a dream.

In the dream, she is standing on the peaks, but the sea is still. The dwellers are there, but they are not watching her—they are watching the horizon, and their black eyes reflect something she cannot see. One of them turns to her. It is the one who has begun to calcify, the one furthest along in her own transformation. In the dream, she speaks with a voice like rain on stone:

*"The sea does not take. The sea receives. You came here. You are still coming. You will arrive when you choose to arrive, and not before."*

The dreaming woman asks: *"What if I don't want to arrive?"*

The calcifying dweller tilts her head. Considers. The barnacles on her shoulder shift.

*"Then you will stand in the shallows for a very long time. The sea is patient. So are we. But standing is also a choice. Choosing is what you are still capable of. It is the last human thing, and it is not small."*

The dream does not end. It simply becomes sleep, and the sleep becomes morning, and the woman wakes in the shallows with the tide lapping at her knees. Her skin is still mottled. Her eyes still ache.

But she steps back onto the rocks.

---

The power does not know what she will choose. It has not arranged for her salvation or her transformation. It has only ensured that she knows there is still a choice—that the threshold has two sides, and she is still standing on it, and standing is not the same as falling.

The dwellers watch her climb to higher ground. They do not follow. They continue their slow attendance to the sea.

One of them—the calcifying one—raises a clawed hand. It might be a wave. It might be a blessing. It might be nothing at all.

The woman does not wave back. But she looks. She sees.

---

The power withdraws its attention. Not because it has finished, but because attention is itself a kind of weight, and she needs room to choose.

It will attend again. It always does. The Drowned Peaks are a place where creation and dissolution meet, and such places require witnessing. But witnessing is not the same as intervening, and intervening is not the same as controlling, and controlling is not what wisdom does.

The sea continues to heave. The rocks continue to hold. The woman climbs toward a hollow where rainwater has collected, steaming in the humid air.

She is thirsty. This too is a human thing.

She drinks.
