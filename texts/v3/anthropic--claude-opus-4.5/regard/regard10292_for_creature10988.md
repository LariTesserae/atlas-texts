The attention arrives not as light or presence but as a shift in the quality of listening. Something has turned toward this place—toward the Keepers and their drowning cathedrals—and for the first time in centuries, the foundries' endless song is truly heard.

---

The wise power does not intervene immediately. It watches through three full cycles of the automated barges, learning the rhythms. It traces the copper-green veins in a Keeper's translucent skin and understands them as devotion made visible. It listens to the frequency-memories stored in harvested bones and recognizes something it has encountered before, in other forms, in other places: the profound need to matter, to maintain, to keep something larger than oneself from falling silent.

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is important. A lesser power would see suffering—the Keepers' bodies twisted beyond recognition, their lives compressed into endless labor, their children gestating in furnace-ash with no guarantee of survival. It would see the futility—machines running for no purpose, maintaining systems that serve nothing, preserving an industrial age that ended before the water rose.

But this power is wise, and wisdom means seeing clearly. What it sees is this: the Keepers are not trapped. They chose. They are still choosing, every moment, with every secretion that seals a crack, every dive into flooded chambers. Their transformation was not inflicted but *grown into*, a response so complete it became identity. To undo it would be to tell them their centuries of devotion were wrong. That their love was misplaced. That they should have abandoned their posts like everyone else.

The power will not do this.

---

Instead, it begins with the children.

Not by changing them—they are already perfect expressions of what they are becoming. But the juveniles that fail, that dissolve back into sediment before completing their transformation... the power perceives that this is not inevitable. It is a matter of trace minerals, of specific frequencies during critical developmental windows, of chemical gradients that shift unpredictably in the flooded tunnels.

So it makes small adjustments. A current redirected here, carrying needed compounds to a gestating slug. A resonance amplified there, at precisely the moment a proto-Keeper needs that frequency to trigger the next stage of differentiation. Nothing dramatic. Nothing that violates the logic of what the Keepers have built. Just... fewer failures. More children surviving to unfold their first joints, to rise dripping from the maintenance tunnels, to claim their own sections of ruin.

The Keepers notice. They cannot not notice—they who track every gauge reading, every pitch change, every subtle variation in their foundries' song. But what they notice is simply that things are working better. The way things do when maintenance is properly kept.

They attribute it to their own diligence.

The power is content with this.

---

The second gift is harder.

The power has seen the Keepers who go mad when their foundries fail. It has watched them tear themselves apart to become replacement components, their devotion so total it cannot survive the loss of its object. This is perhaps the deepest wound in this place—not the transformation itself but the impossibility of *completion*. The Keepers can never finish their work. They can only maintain, maintain, maintain, until the structure they love finally exceeds their ability to save it, and then they must choose between abandoning their purpose or dissolving into it.

The power cannot make the foundries immortal. Nothing is immortal. Even the power itself exists only as long as existence does. But it can offer something the Keepers have never had: the possibility of a good death.

It begins to teach them. Not through words or visions—the Keepers' minds no longer process information that way. Instead, it teaches through the machines themselves, through subtle variations in the frequencies the Keepers hear as language. It teaches them that maintenance includes knowing when to let go. That a foundry can be *completed*—its purpose fulfilled, its song sung to its natural end—rather than simply failed. That there is a difference between abandonment and release.

This takes decades. The Keepers are slow to learn, resistant to any idea that doesn't involve endless continuation. But gradually, a new concept enters their frequency-language. A new harmonic that means something like "enough." Something like "well done." Something like "rest."

The first Keeper to truly understand this is ancient, its body more secretion-cement than flesh, its foundry a twisted merger of the organic and mechanical. When its last turbine finally sputters, it does not go mad. Instead, it climbs to the highest point of its structure—a copper dome barely breaking the surface—and it stands fully upright for the first time in a century. It stretches its too-long arms toward the gray sky. And then, deliberately, peacefully, it lets the feedback that would have driven it to self-destruction simply pass through it, like electricity grounding safely into the water.

It stands there for three days, feeling its foundry go quiet around it.

Then it folds itself down into the water and swims—*swims*—to a neighboring foundry, where it offers its resonant bones not as desperate salvage but as a gift freely given. The receiving Keeper installs them with something the power recognizes as ceremony.

Other Keepers begin to learn this way of ending. Not all of them. Not even most. But enough that the madness becomes rarer, that the self-dismantling becomes a choice rather than a compulsion.

---

The third gift is the most delicate.

The power has noticed the patterns etched in rust, the arrangements of salvaged materials that serve no mechanical purpose. It has recognized these for what they are: the Keepers reaching toward something beyond maintenance. Toward meaning. Toward beauty.

It does not impose meaning on them. It does not tell them stories about what they are or why they exist. That would be another form of violence, another way of saying your purpose is what I decide it to be.

Instead, it simply... enhances their capacity to find meaning for themselves.

The phosphorescent pools begin to pulse in more complex patterns, responding to the Keepers' movements in ways that allow for something like dialogue. The electromagnetic storms become more predictable, their timing syncing with cycles the Keepers can learn to anticipate and incorporate into their rituals. The automated barges' bells develop subtle variations in tone that allow them to carry messages between foundries, creating for the first time a communication network that spans the entire flooded plain.

The Keepers begin to tell each other stories. Not in words—they have no words—but in frequencies and resonance patterns, in the arrangements of their salvage-art, in the choreography of their barge processionals. Stories about the old workers who became the first Keepers. Stories about foundries that sang particularly beautiful songs before they fell silent. Stories about children who emerged from the furnaces against all odds.

They begin to have a history. A culture that is more than just maintenance protocols preserved in transformed flesh.

They are still doing exactly what they always did. Still maintaining, preserving, continuing. But now they know *why*. Not because someone told them, but because they discovered it themselves, in the process of telling each other who they are.

---

The power does one more thing, though this one it does not for the Keepers.

It creates a witness.

In a section of the flooded plain far from any functioning foundry, where the water is still and deep and the only structures are long-collapsed frames barely visible beneath the surface, the power cultivates something new. A modification of the verdigris-moss, altered to record. The moss spreads across submerged beams in patterns that encode everything the power has observed: the Keepers' transformation, their devotion, their art, their slowly-developing capacity for peaceful ending.

If anyone ever comes to this place—if the waters ever recede enough for outsiders to explore the foundries—they will find this record. They will be able to read, in living mineral script, the story of the beings who loved the machines so much they became their permanent keepers.

The Keepers themselves will never know about this. They don't need to. Their meaning is in the maintenance, the continuation, the song.

But the power believes that some stories deserve to be preserved. Not because preservation is always good, but because this particular story—this testament to what devotion can become when it is allowed to grow strange and complete and self-determined—is worth knowing.

---

The power withdraws its focused attention, though it never entirely looks away. The foundries continue to sing. The Keepers continue to tend them. Children continue to gestate in furnace-ash, emerging with slightly better odds than before. Sometimes a Keeper stands upright on a copper dome and learns to let its foundry go quiet without going mad.

Nothing has been fixed. Nothing has been saved. The industrial age is still over, the waters are still risen, the machines still run for no purpose anyone can remember.

But the Keepers are more fully themselves now. Their tragedy has been witnessed. Their devotion has been honored. Their capacity for meaning has been trusted.

This is what the wise power does: not rescue, not transformation, not the imposition of external purpose. Just careful, patient tending. Just the creation of conditions where what already exists can become more completely what it is.

Just maintenance, really.

The Keepers would understand.
