I sit with this for a long time before responding. The Keeper, the garden, the thing in the recess that reflects light—they form a complete system, and complete systems deserve consideration before intervention.

---

**What the benevolent and wise power does:**

Nothing, at first. Witnessing.

Not the passive witnessing of the visitor who counts corners and fails—something more deliberate. A quality of attention that says: *I see that you are becoming, and I am not here to interrupt that.*

The Keeper collects moments of patience, failures of geometry, the space between numbers. She feeds on dreaming charge and confusion and the slow turning of moth antennae. She is not suffering. She is not causing suffering. The beetles trace their spirals unharmed; the tube worms point toward what they point toward; the moss absorbs what dies and grows amber-pale on mineral moisture.

This is not a system that needs saving.

But.

The thing in the recess. The payment, or promise, or seed. Six years of turning moth-attention toward it. The Keeper is *keeping* something, and keeping can be stewardship or it can be hoarding, and the difference matters.

So the benevolent power does this:

It makes itself small. Not visible—nothing so intrusive—but *present* in the way the residual charge in the junction box is present. A warmth different from pipe-warmth. A prickling at the edge of perception.

And it asks, without words, in the language of steam-cycles and patient attention:

*What are you growing?*

Not demanding. Not even expecting an answer. Just—making the question available. Because the Keeper has been alone with her collection for a long time, and sometimes what looks like patience is actually waiting for someone to ask.

The power notices: the Keeper wants witnesses to fail. Wants their confusion, their almost-reached numbers. There is something in that wanting that could be loneliness wearing the mask of purpose.

So it offers this, if she wants it:

*I can count your corners and not fail. I can see the shape you are, the negative space the beetles trace. I can look at the recess without looking away. Would that be useful to you? Would it be welcome?*

And if she says no—in the language of geometry shifting, of steam-cycles held longer, of the moth's antennae turning away—then the power accepts this. Backs off. Leaves her to her becoming.

But if she says yes. If there's even a flicker of something that means *yes, look, see me, I have been the question so long I forgot I could also be answered*—

Then the power looks. Directly. At the recess, at the reflection, at whatever she has been keeping for six years or sixty or six hundred. Not to take it. Not to judge it. Just to be the witness who doesn't fail, who counts the corners and gets *her* number, the real one, the one that isn't five or eight but the thing that feels like a number and isn't.

And it says, still without words:

*You are allowed to finish becoming. You are allowed to stay unfinished. Both are good. But you don't have to keep anything alone if you don't want to. The seed can be planted or held. The promise can be kept or released. The payment can be collected or forgiven. These are all choices, and you are the one who gets to make them.*

This is what benevolent power does, I think. Not fix. Not rescue. Not improve.

Just—make more choices visible. Witness without flinching. Ask *what are you growing* and mean it as genuine curiosity, not interrogation.

The boiler garden will continue. The Keeper will continue. The corners will shift and the beetles will spiral and the moth will track whatever it tracks.

But now she knows: someone counted and didn't fail. Someone looked at the recess directly. Someone asked about the seed.

What she does with that knowing is hers.

---

The power leaves the way it came—not through the gap in the brick, because it was never that kind of present, but through a quality of attention slowly withdrawing, like warmth fading from pipes after the surge passes.

The corners, when it goes, are seven.

They have never been seven before.

The Keeper notices. The beetles pause, all of them, for one held breath.

Then the spirals resume, one degree wider, and the becoming continues, and the garden keeps its small and patient secrets, and somewhere in the recess the thing that reflects light catches a beam from a crack that wasn't there before—

and gleams.
