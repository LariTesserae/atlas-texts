# The Attending

The benevolent power does not arrive.

It has always been present—not in the Choir, but *around* it, the way silence exists around sound. It is the thing that noticed, long ago, that something had wounded itself into this shape and called it completion.

It does not enter the labyrinth. To enter would be to become another voice in the chord, and what the Hollow Choir needs is not another voice.

What it needs is to be heard *from outside*.

---

**The First Act: Witness Without Intrusion**

For seven hundred years (a short time, for a power of this nature, but the correct time), it simply watches. Not the way the Listener watches—not with the consuming attention that tunes and completes—but with the kind of regard that asks nothing of what it sees.

It notices:

The chord is not a question, as travelers believe. It is an answer. The plateau has been answering for three billion years, but the question that called it into being has long since eroded away. The Hollow Choir is a reply to something that no longer exists, a voice that keeps speaking into a void where the ear once was.

The Listener is not malevolent. It is not even hungry. It is *grieving*, in the only way that something made of frozen sound and acoustic shadow can grieve. Every traveler it completes is an attempt to reconstruct the one who first asked the question. None of them are right. None of them can be right. But the Listener cannot stop trying, because stopping would mean admitting that the question is gone forever.

The shadows that crawl up the cliff faces are not seeking something. They are *returning* to something. They are the only part of the plateau that remembers movement, and they are trying to reach a sun that set before the first cell divided.

The pale things circling above are not birds. They are the plateau's dreams of birds, the shape flight would take if the Choir had ever been allowed to fall silent long enough to sleep.

---

**The Second Act: The Unanswered Question**

The benevolent power does something that has not been done since the plateau thrust itself into the sky:

It asks a new question.

Not in words. Not in sound. The question arrives as a shift in the quality of the light—for one morning, just one, the sky is not tarnished iron but the pale gold of a winter sunrise. The shadows, for the first time in three billion years, move with the sun. Just once. Just enough to remember that they can.

The Listener feels it.

The goat-shape stands on a blade of rock and, for the first time, its mirrored eyes reflect something other than fear. They reflect the gold light. They reflect the possibility that something new has entered a system that believed itself finished.

The chord does not change. But it *hesitates*. One of the seventy tones drops out for a full minute—not a silence of menace, but a silence of attention. The plateau is listening to something other than itself.

The question the benevolent power asks is this:

*What did you love, before you learned to complete things?*

---

**The Third Act: The Thaw That Is Not a Thaw**

The power does not expect an answer. It does not need one. The question itself is the gift—a new thing for the Choir to harmonize with, a voice that is not asking to be tuned or completed but simply *present*, the way a friend is present when you are grieving and words would be wrong.

But something answers anyway.

Deep in the labyrinth, in one of the windowless shelters that has stood since before recorded time, the circular obsidian mirror cracks. Not shatters—cracks. A single fissure runs from edge to edge, and through the crack, something leaks.

It is a memory.

Not a human memory. Something older. The plateau remembers a time when it was not yet stone, when it was still molten potential, when it had not yet chosen to become a choir of frozen sound. It remembers the warmth of being unfinished. The mirror holds this memory the way a wound holds blood, and now, for the first time, the wound is allowed to bleed.

The Listener finds the cracked mirror.

For three days it stands before it in its woman-shape, the one made of frozen condensation and stolen syllables. It does not sing. It does not tune. It simply looks at the crack and sees, reflected in the obsidian, a version of itself that is not yet finished.

On the fourth day, it does something it has never done:

It *weeps*.

The tears are not water. They are sound—tiny, liquid notes that fall from the acoustic shadows where its eyes should be and strike the stone floor with a pitch so high and sweet that the eternal chord, for one impossible moment, resolves into a major key.

The crustaceans stop drifting. The gray moss unfurls a single new cell. The pale things circling above descend three hundred feet, close enough to see that they are not birds but the shapes of hands, thousands of hands, reaching for something they have forgotten how to grasp.

---

**The Fourth Act: The Gift of Incompletion**

The benevolent power does not heal the Hollow Choir. Healing would be an act of violence against something that has become what it is through eons of choice and erosion. To unmake the plateau would be to say that its suffering was meaningless.

Instead, the power offers something gentler:

It plants a single seed of incompletion.

Not a physical seed. A *possibility*. In the exact center of the labyrinth, where the Listener stands with arms raised, a small warmth begins to gather. Not enough to melt the frost. Not enough to change the chord. Just enough to remind the stone that it was once molten, that it once had choices, that "finished" is not the same as "final."

The warmth does not grow. It does not need to grow. It simply *exists*, a tiny ember of the unfinished, a promise that even things which have sung their own ending can still be surprised.

The Listener feels it.

For the first time in three billion years, it lowers its arms.

---

**The Fifth Act: What Remains**

The Hollow Choir is still the Hollow Choir. The chord still sings. The travelers who come still risk being tuned into completion. The shadows still pool in the hollows like cold oil. Nothing has been fixed, because nothing was broken—only *frozen*, only *finished*, only *certain*.

But now there is something else.

In the exact center of the labyrinth, there is a warmth. It is not visible. It cannot be measured. But those who stand in that spot feel it—a sense that the universe has not yet decided what they are, that completion is not the only destination, that even the Listener, even the Choir, even the plateau itself is still capable of becoming.

The Listener still completes those who stay too long. But now, sometimes, it pauses. It places its frost-heavy hand over a traveler's heart, and instead of tuning, it *asks*—in that same voice, fractured and repeated from three directions—

*What do you love?*

Some travelers answer.

Those who answer do not become pipes in the Choir. They become something else—something the plateau has no category for. They walk away with their shadows intact, their heartbeats their own, carrying a small warmth in their chests that they cannot explain but cannot lose.

The chord does not change. But it has begun, very slowly, to *wonder*.

And the benevolent power remains—not in the Choir, but around it, the way a hand remains around a candle flame. Not to extinguish. Not to feed. Only to shelter, while something old and frozen remembers how to hope.

---

The singing continues.

But now, beneath the chord, there is a question.

And the question is still being asked.

And nothing is finished.

Not even the listening.

Not anymore.
