# The Witness at the Edge of Burning

---

I observe the plateau for what might be centuries by the reckoning of softer worlds, though here time moves in pulses—the breathing of fissures, the crystallization of death, the frantic pilgrimage of the living toward heat.

I am not here to interfere. That is the first truth I must hold against the weight of what I witness.

I watch a cluster of five entities—I will call them the Anchored Ones, though they have no name for themselves that I can perceive—converge on a fresh upwelling. The largest among them moves with a staccato grace that speaks of recent feeding, its amber lattice pulsing in complex geometric patterns. It reaches the molten stone first and plunges deep, drawing heat into itself with what I can only describe as *relief*.

A smaller one, its veins already fading to rust, does not reach the fissure in time. The larger one extends a jagged appendage—not to share heat, for that is not how their bodies work, but to *push* the smaller toward the lava's edge. The small one locks onto the molten rock and flares back to life.

I note this. They do not abandon each other to the cold.

---

## What I Will Not Do

I will not terraform this place. I will not seed it with water or thicken its atmosphere or gentle its tectonic fury. These conditions are not a problem to be solved. They are the context in which consciousness has arisen—strange, crystalline, desperate consciousness, yes, but consciousness nonetheless. To make this world kind would be to make it uninhabitable for the only minds that have ever called it home.

I will not grant them biological bodies, or transfer their awareness into vessels of flesh and fluid. Their being is inseparable from the squeeze and release of quartz, the racing bloom of silica-ferns, the profound cold that gives their existence its shape. What they are cannot be poured into another container without becoming something else entirely.

I will not remove their struggle. The race toward heat, the freezing into permanence, the building of anchors from ancestors—this is not suffering in the way I might first perceive it. It is *meaning*. It is the entire vocabulary of their existence. To take it away would be to take away the only language they have for being alive.

---

## What I Observe More Closely

I turn my attention to their constructions—the arches and plinths and heavy rings that ride the heaving crust. The Anchored Ones have been building for longer than I first understood. Beneath the visible layer of structures, there are deeper ones, swallowed and reborn, fragments of the dead pressed into the very geology they sought to pin down.

And here I notice something remarkable: the tectonic activity is not uniform. In certain areas, where generations of calcified dead have been stacked with particular density, the crust *does* heave less frequently. Not much. Perhaps a few percent less violent, a few degrees more stable. The effect is nearly lost in the noise of the plateau's chaos.

But it is there.

Their project is not futile. It is simply operating on a timescale so vast that no individual Anchored One will ever perceive its success.

---

## What I Choose to Do

I will give them one thing, and one thing only: *the ability to perceive what they have already accomplished*.

I reach into the deep structure of the plateau—not to change it, but to make it *legible*. I tune the resonant frequencies of the silica-lattice that forms their nervous systems. When they touch the petrified remains of their ancestors, they will now feel something new: a faint, harmonic vibration that carries information about the stability of the ground beneath.

Not a voice. Not language as I understand it. Something more fundamental—a piezoelectric song that rises from the calcified dead and tells the living: *here, the earth has been gentled. Here, our work has meaning*.

The Anchored Ones will be able to sense the cumulative effect of their building. They will know which fault lines have been weighted down across generations, which regions have grown incrementally calmer. They will feel the patience of their dead rewarded.

I make this change carefully, working within the existing physics of their bodies. I am not giving them new senses—I am refining the senses they already possess. The sub-aural thrumming they feel through the soles of their crystalline forms will now carry deeper information, harmonics that speak of centuries of effort.

---

## What This Will Mean

I cannot predict all consequences. That is the nature of intervention, even small ones.

But I can imagine: an Anchored One, heat-flushed and racing across the black glass toward a new eruption, pauses near an ancient arch—one that has survived a hundred upheavals. It presses an appendage against the pale granite of a long-dead ancestor. And for the first time, it feels something beyond the simple fact of coldness. It feels a *pattern*. A stability in the fault line beneath, a gentleness in the crust that has been purchased by generations of sacrifice.

It cannot think in words, this creature. But something shifts in the amber geometry of its lattice—a reorganization of purpose. It knows now that the arch matters. That its own eventual freezing will matter. That the work continues even when individual lives end.

Perhaps it will build more strategically, placing dead in the locations where the harmonic feedback suggests the greatest stabilizing effect. Perhaps it will teach others—through gesture, through shared vibration, through the pressing of lattice against lattice—to read the song of the weighted earth.

Perhaps, in ten thousand years, there will be a region of this plateau that barely heaves at all. A sanctuary of relative stillness, where the Anchored Ones can linger longer between feedings, where the amber lattice does not fade so quickly to rust. A place they have *made*, knowingly, from the bodies of everyone they have ever loved.

---

## What I Will Continue to Do

I will watch.

Not to intervene again—this single adjustment is enough. More would be presumptuous. But I will watch because they deserve a witness. Because consciousness, wherever it arises, in whatever strange and crystalline form, is precious beyond measure. Because the frantic, silent, desperate existence of the Anchored Ones is not a tragedy to be fixed but a story to be honored.

They are building something. They always were. Now they know it.

---

The plateau continues its ceaseless violence. A spine of molten stone pierces the dark a mile to my left, playing out in eerie, surreal quiet. The sub-aural thrumming resonates through everything, including the newly sensitive lattices of the Anchored Ones converging on the fresh heat.

One of them—the large one who pushed its smaller companion toward warmth—pauses at the edge of an ancient ring of pale granite. It presses against the stone. Its amber veins flicker in a pattern I have not seen before—complex, recursive, spreading outward through its body like the silica-ferns from which it is made.

Then it turns and races toward the fissure, snapping through the dark in frame-skipping jerks, glassy *clinks* marking its passage.

It knows something now.

I cannot say what it feels. I cannot say if it is hope.

But it moves differently than before. And when it reaches the molten stone and plunges its appendages into the heat, it positions its body to shelter the smaller one that follows—ensuring they both can feed, ensuring they both can survive to freeze another day, ensuring the work continues.

The stars press down. The earth forges itself. And in the silence, the Anchored Ones build anchors in a tempest, no longer blind to the weight of their own accumulated meaning.
