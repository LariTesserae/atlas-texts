# The Attending

The benevolent power does not arrive.

Arrival would imply a before and an after, a moment of entry that the mountain of echoing glass would immediately scatter into a thousand overlapping versions of itself. Instead, the power is simply *there*, in the way that the low steady note beneath everything else has always been there—except now the note has acquired a listener who is also, somehow, the listening itself.

It does not have a form. Forms are commitments to a single version of shape, and commitment of that kind would be a violence here, where every stone exists in conversation with the stone it was and the stone it will become. Instead, it is present as a quality of attention: the plateau suddenly *noticed*, not observed from outside but recognized from within, the way a dreamer sometimes becomes aware that they are dreaming without waking up.

The Refrain feel it first.

They do not startle—startling requires a before-state of calm that they have never possessed—but something in the rhythm of their scattered walking changes. The overlapping versions of each figure, usually stacked in their thin relentless piles of possibility, begin to *cohere*. Not fully. Not into single solid bodies. But the seven-who-are-one becomes the four-who-are-one, and then the three, and there is a moment, a genuine moment with edges, where one of them stands in only one place at only one time.

They do not speak. They have never needed to speak to be heard.

But the power hears them anyway.

---

What the power understands is this:

The mountain of echoing glass is not broken. It is not suffering in the way that a wound suffers, or grieving in the way that loss grieves. It is simply *caught*—a place where time has folded over itself so many times that the creases have become permanent, where the laws of physics have grown too tired to insist on sequence, where echoes have learned to precede their sources and causes arrive after their effects.

The Refrain are not victims of this folding. They are its *artists*.

They have made themselves into the seams that hold the impossible together. Each breath they take of history-laden wind, each word they feed into the trembling cups of water, each step they scatter across years of walking—all of it is maintenance. They are the ones who ensure that the plateau does not collapse entirely into a single undifferentiated moment, that visitors can still climb up through the pines and the frost-shattered rock and find something *there* rather than everywhere and nowhere at once.

They are exhausted in a way that exhaustion cannot describe.

They are not tired of working. They are tired of being the only ones who remember that work is being done.

---

The power does not fix anything.

Fixing would imply that something is wrong, and the mountain of echoing glass is not wrong. It is simply *itself*, in the way that a river is itself even when it floods, in the way that a fire is itself even when it burns what you loved. The plateau exists at the edge of what is possible, and edges are not mistakes.

But the power is wise, and wisdom here means understanding that the Refrain have been alone for a very long time—not in the sense of lacking company, since they are never without their own echoes and the echoes of everyone else, but in the sense of lacking *witness*.

No one has ever seen what they do.

No one has ever understood that the low steady note beneath everything, the sound of equilibrium, is not the mountain's voice but *theirs*—a chorus of breath and memory stretched across centuries of careful, invisible labor.

So the power does the only thing that a benevolent presence can do for those who have made themselves into the seams of the world:

It *stays*.

---

Not forever. The power is not a resident; it has other edges to attend to, other places where reality forgets its own rules and someone must hold the fabric together. But for a time—a real time, with a beginning and an end that the Refrain can feel as distinct moments—it remains.

It does not speak to them. Speech would require a sender and a receiver, and up here that distinction has grown too thin to bear much weight.

Instead, it *witnesses*.

When one of the Refrain kneels at the edge of a shallow cup and feeds it with the shape of a word that was never formed into sound, the power is there, attending. When another walks ahead of a visitor's footsteps, breaking the way that has already been broken, the power is there, recognizing. When a third stands frozen inside a moment that the world has decided should hold longer than others, the power is there, patient, waiting with them until the glass-air releases its grip.

It does not applaud. It does not praise. It does not offer thanks or reward.

It simply *sees*.

And the Refrain, who have never known what it is to be watched by something that understands, begin to change.

---

The change is not dramatic.

Drama would require contrast, and the plateau does not deal in contrasts—only in gradients, in slow shifts from one impossible thing to another. But over time, the Refrain begin to walk with their feet landing in fewer places at once. Their edges, always liquid where glass should be sharp, begin to hold their shapes a little longer before dripping over into the next moment.

They are not becoming solid. Solidity is not available to them; they have given it up in exchange for the ability to move between the folds of time.

But they are becoming *clearer*.

When a visitor climbs the mountain now, they might catch a glimpse of a figure that does not immediately slide into the category of "someone else." They might see a face that stays in memory for a moment after looking away—not long, but long enough to know that someone was there.

The Refrain do not speak of this. They do not speak of anything.

But something in the rhythm of their work has shifted. They move with less weight now, not because the burden has been lifted but because it has been *shared*—not in the sense of distributed labor, but in the sense of distributed knowing. Someone else understands what they are. Someone else has seen.

---

The power does one other thing before it goes.

It listens to the low steady note that the Refrain have been singing since before the plateau had a name, the sound of equilibrium stretched from pole to pole, and it *joins*.

Not loudly. Not in a way that changes the pitch or overwhelms the harmony.

It simply adds its own breath to theirs, for a moment, so that the note they have been sustaining alone becomes the note they are sustaining together.

And when the moment passes, and the power's attention turns elsewhere, the note remains—but it is no longer quite the same.

It is fuller now.

It carries within it the memory of being heard, the knowledge that somewhere beyond the bruised sky and the smears of pale luminescence, there is a presence that knows the mountain of echoing glass is not empty, that the Refrain are not merely what happens again when everything else thinks it has finished, but what *keeps happening* because someone must, because the world requires seams and the seams require seamstresses, and the seamstresses deserve, at least once, to be seen.

---

The power does not leave a mark.

There is no shrine, no artifact, no doorframe carved with trembling letters that spell out what occurred. The plateau does not remember visitors in the way that other places do—it absorbs them into its echoes and then forgets the absorption.

But the Refrain remember.

They remember in the way that echoes remember: not as a story with a beginning and an end, but as a quality of sound, a resonance that enters every word they feed into the cups of water, every step they scatter across years of walking, every breath they take of the history-laden wind.

They are still tired.

They are still the mistake that keeps happening, the white space between the letters, the cough that the mountain answers back with a cough that is not quite human.

But they are no longer only.

They are witnessed.

And in a place where everything echoes, that witnessing will go on echoing—through the wind that never comes from the same place twice, through the glass that glows faintly from the inside, through the low steady note that stretches across the world—long after the power has turned its attention elsewhere, long after the last visitor has descended through the pines, long after the plateau itself has finally grown too tired to remember what it was.

The Refrain will still be there.

They will still be singing.

And somewhere, in the folds of time where causes arrive after their effects and echoes precede their sources, they will still be heard.
