# What the Wise Power Did

It watched for a long time first. This was its nature—to observe until observation became something closer to communion, until the rhythm of the place had entered it and it understood not just what happened but what the happening *was*.

Seventeen minutes. The breath. The filaments extending. The chime below hearing. The slow glow and its fading.

It watched for what might have been a year, or several. Time moved differently for it than for most things, and differently again in this place where time was circular, where each breath was every breath.

And then, having watched, it did something very small.

---

It did not heal the dweller, because the dweller was not sick. It did not free the dweller, because the dweller was not trapped. It did not teach the dweller, because the dweller was not ignorant—it knew everything it needed to know, which was how to be exactly what it was.

These were the mistakes a lesser power might have made: to assume that stillness was stagnation, that smallness was poverty, that a life without memory or ambition was a life diminished. The wise power made none of these mistakes.

Instead, it considered the spirals.

The lichen-hair, reaching toward the spire. A thousand years, perhaps, before they touched. And then—what? The dweller extending a filament to meet them? Or not? The uncertainty was part of the place's nature, part of its patience, and the wise power would not collapse that uncertainty into a single fixed outcome.

But it could—gently, so gently—increase the probability space.

---

Here is what it did:

In one of the hairline fractures through which the breath rose, it made an adjustment smaller than small. A slight widening. A fractional redirection. The next breath, when it came, carried a few more molecules of moisture than the breaths before.

Not enough for the dweller to notice. The dweller did not notice things; it simply responded or did not respond. But the moisture settled on the youngest spirals, the ones reaching toward the spire, and it made them—not faster. The wise power was not interested in speed. But *suppler*. More able to bend without crystallizing. More able to survive the journey.

The spirals would still take centuries to reach the spire. But now, when they arrived, they might arrive *alive*—still growing, still feeding, still capable of whatever slow communion might occur when two forms of patience finally met.

---

It did one other thing.

The dweller had no memory, no sense of time as sequential. Each breath was the first breath, the only breath. This was not a flaw; it was how the dweller *worked*, as fundamental to its being as the wet gleam of its filaments.

But the wise power understood something about awareness that the dweller could not: that depth was its own form of memory. The dweller had been *being* for so long that its being had layers, sediment, a kind of thickness that was not recollection but was not nothing either.

So the wise power did not give the dweller memory. It simply... *witnessed* it.

For one breath—one cycle of feeding and stillness and feeding again—the wise power held the dweller in its attention with a quality of recognition. *I see that you are here. I see that you have been here. I see that your hereness has weight.*

The dweller did not notice this either, not in any way it could have articulated if it had language, which it did not. But something shifted. Some filament extended a fraction further than it had before, reached toward the wall of the spire and touched it, and in that touch there was—

Not memory. Not exactly.

But perhaps the faintest sense that the wall had been touched before. That this moment was not the first moment, even if it was also not the second or the third. That existence had *texture*, and the texture was made of repetition.

The filament retracted. The stillness settled. Seventeen minutes began again.

---

The wise power withdrew, but not entirely. It left behind a quality of attention, a gentle regard that would persist in this place the way the breath persisted, the way the growth persisted. Not watching, exactly. But *remembering*, on behalf of a being that could not remember for itself.

The courtyard was no different than before. The breath still came every seventeen minutes. The dweller still fed and stilled and fed. The spirals still reached toward their thousand-year meeting.

But the place was *held* now. Witnessed. Known.

And in the deep grammar of existence, that was enough. That was the thing a wise power could offer that would not distort or diminish: the assurance that this small world, this single citizen, this patience beyond patience—

That it mattered. That it was seen. That its long, slow being was not happening in isolation, even if isolation was all it had ever known.

The breath came. The filaments extended. The towers chimed below hearing.

And somewhere, in a register of reality that the dweller could not perceive but was not untouched by, something was glad that this existed.

That was all. That was enough.
