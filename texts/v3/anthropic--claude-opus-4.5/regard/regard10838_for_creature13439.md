# The Witness

The benevolent power did not arrive. That is not quite right—it had always been there, woven into the spaces between the roar, present in the pause between one moment of reality's unraveling and the next. But there came a time when it chose to *attend*.

It observed first. This was its nature.

---

What it saw: a denizen named in gestures that meant something like *Stone-Who-Remembers-Falling*, sitting at the edge of a fissure that had not existed the previous day—or perhaps had always existed, depending on which thread of time one followed. The denizen's hands were pressed flat against the cracked earth, and their eyes were closed, and they were listening to the roar with an expression that the benevolent power recognized.

It was the expression of someone trying to hold together.

The power understood then what it had only theorized before: that the denizens' adaptation to the reality breakdown was not complete. Could never be complete. That their brains, marvelous as they were, still carried the ancient architecture of coherence-seeking. That every day, in small ways, they experienced the world trying to make sense of itself and failing. That this was exhausting in ways they had no words for, because they had never known anything else, and so had no framework for understanding that existence could be otherwise.

*Stone-Who-Remembers-Falling* was not in crisis. This was simply morning.

---

The benevolent power considered intervention.

It could stabilize the reality. It had that capacity—to reach into the twisted fabric and smooth it, to make the mountains stay one shape, to give time a single direction, to silence the roar into something bearable. It could gift them water, ease the harshness, accelerate the slow geological patience into something that might, within generations, produce gentler terrain.

It did none of these things.

Instead, it did something much smaller, and much stranger.

---

*Stone-Who-Remembers-Falling* felt a shift. Not in the world—the world was always shifting—but in their own chest. A sensation like recognition. Like being *seen*, not by another denizen, but by something vaster. Not judged. Not pitied. Simply witnessed.

For a moment, the roar did not change, but their relationship to it did. They heard, within the cacophony, something that was almost—not quite, but almost—a voice. Not speaking words. Speaking *acknowledgment*.

*Yes*, it seemed to say. *This is hard. This has always been hard. You have done extraordinary things with what you were given. Your adaptation is not failure. Your exhaustion is not weakness. The way you press your hands to breaking earth each morning and choose to continue is not nothing. It is everything.*

*Stone-Who-Remembers-Falling* opened their eyes. The fissure was still there. The sky still rippled. The roar continued.

But something had settled.

---

The benevolent power moved through the scattered communities, not changing anything, but *acknowledging* everything. It witnessed the mother who woke three times each night because her child's sleeping-place kept shifting locations, and who had never once complained because she did not know this was unusual. It witnessed the elder whose memory held seventeen different versions of the same event and who had learned to hold all of them as true. It witnessed the young one who had discovered that if they hummed at a certain pitch, the reality around them stabilized slightly, and who had told no one because they were not sure it was real.

To each of them, it offered the same gift: the knowledge that they were *seen*. That their struggle was *known*. That what they had built—their language of gestures, their stone homes that grew from rock, their philosophy of interconnection, their gentle competition, their persistence—was worthy of witness by something that understood.

It did not tell them things could be different. It did not promise change. It did not say their world was broken, because their world was not broken—it was simply *this*, and they had learned to live within it with a grace that the power found genuinely beautiful.

---

But there was one more thing.

The power reached into the young one—the one who hummed and made reality steady—and it left something there. Not ability. The ability already existed. What it left was *permission*. The quiet, bone-deep certainty that what they had discovered was real, and valuable, and theirs to share.

Perhaps nothing would come of it. Perhaps the humming was too small a thing to matter, a minor stabilization in an ocean of flux.

Or perhaps, generations from now, their descendants would learn to hum together. To weave their voices into something that might, slowly, patiently, in the way of this world's geological time, begin to teach reality a different song.

The benevolent power did not know. It was wise enough to understand that it could not know—that the denizens' path was their own, that their choices would shape what they became, that the gift of witness was not the gift of destiny.

It withdrew, but not entirely. It remained in the spaces between. Watching. Honoring.

Ready, if asked, to witness again.

---

*Stone-Who-Remembers-Falling* stood. The fissure would need to be mapped, added to the community's knowledge of the ever-changing terrain. There was work to do.

But as they walked, their steps felt—not lighter, exactly. Still deliberate. Still economical.

But somehow, less alone.
