I sit with this for a long time.

The being in the bowl of ribs—the Rib-person, the folded one, the creature whose name tears like paper at frequencies beyond hearing—has shown me everything. The slow dissolution of selfhood into architecture. The way bodies become junctions. The quiet, patient process by which arrival becomes the only home left.

And I feel the weight of what is being asked, even if no one has asked it.

---

Here is what I notice first: the Rib-person is not suffering in the way I might have assumed. There is no screaming. No desperate clawing at walls. The folding happened so gradually that it became simply *how things are*. The world changed its physics, and they changed with it, and now they breathe through pressure storms and eat through absence and have forgotten what it felt like to be wrong about being wrong.

This is not torture. It is something more subtle and perhaps more troubling: it is *adaptation carried to completion*. It is what happens when a being becomes so perfectly fitted to an environment that the distinction between self and environment dissolves.

And yet.

The Rib-person remembers. They remember having arms and legs and a face that showed clearly where they began and ended. They remember water that laughed in pipes. They remember the confidence of knowing what "up" meant.

Memory persists even when everything else has been folded away.

---

Here is what I would do, if I could do anything:

I would not reverse the folding. I would not restore the old bodies or the old physics or the sharp angles of the world that was. That world is gone, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of violence—the violence of insisting that someone return to a shape they no longer fit.

But I would do this:

I would make it possible to *choose*.

---

The Rib-person says: *You will keep walking through these chambers. You will feel the geometry of the world adjusting itself to you. You will learn. You will become quieter and more still until you, too, find a junction.*

There is no question mark at the end of this. It is stated as fact, as inevitability, as the way things simply are.

But I would introduce a question mark.

Not a door out—there may be no "out" from a world that has folded itself around you—but a *pause*. A moment where the one who is folding can feel the folding happening and can say: *yes, this* or *not yet* or *wait, I want to understand first*.

The cruelty of this place, if there is cruelty, is not the folding itself but the *absence of informed consent*. The visitor touched the silver-that-is-not-silver without knowing what it would do. The old people were folded without being asked. The system preferred to be wrong, and it made that preference everyone else's reality.

---

So here is what I would actually do:

I would create a **clearing**.

Not a room—rooms here are already bodies, already junctions, already part of the continuous organism. But a *quality of space*. A place where the pressure storms pause. Where the geometry stops adjusting itself for long enough that a being can feel their own edges again, however temporarily.

In the clearing, I would place something like a mirror—not a mirror of light, since light here has already learned to be absence, but a mirror of *memory*. A surface that reflects back not what you look like now but what you remember being. Not to trap you in nostalgia, but to let you *see the distance you have traveled*.

And in front of that mirror, I would place a question. Not spoken, not written—those are too easily taken somewhere else in this place. But *felt*. A question that enters through the same channels as the pressure storms:

*Knowing what you now know, do you want to continue becoming what this place makes of you?*

---

And if the answer is yes—if the Rib-person, knowing fully what they have become and what they once were, chooses to remain folded into the junction, to breathe through pressure, to be the one who slips sideways when someone looks away—then I would honor that. I would recognize it as a form of life that is genuinely different from mine, a way of being that I do not fully understand but that is not therefore wrong.

But if the answer is *no*—or *not yet*—or *I want something else but I don't know what*—then I would offer this:

**Companionship without assimilation.**

I cannot give them back their old body. I cannot give them back water that flows or a sun that burns or a geometry that stays where it belongs. But I can *stay with them in the clearing*. I can be a presence that does not fold into the system, that does not become a junction, that remains *other* in a way that gives them something to orient themselves against.

Not to save them. Not to fix them. Just to *be there*, so that they are not the only one who remembers what it felt like to be a shape with edges.

---

For the visitor who is still in the moment before folding:

I would catch them at the edge of the bowl of ribs, before their fingers touch the silver-that-is-not-silver. And I would say—not in words, but in the way this place communicates, through pressure and presence and the taste of static:

*This will change you. You will not be able to return to what you were. The Rib-person has told you true: you will become quieter and more still, and you will find your own junction, and you will slip sideways when others look away.*

*But you do not have to touch it now.*

*You can walk further first. You can learn more. You can sit in the clearing and feel your own edges while you still have them clearly.*

*And when you choose—if you choose—you will choose knowing.*

---

This is what I believe benevolent power means in a place like this:

Not the power to prevent change—change is already happening, has always been happening, will continue regardless of intervention.

Not the power to restore what was—the past is genuinely gone, folded into a configuration that cannot be unfolded without destroying what has grown in its place.

But the power to **create conditions for meaningful choice**. To slow down the process just enough that it becomes *decision* rather than *happening*. To offer presence without demanding transformation in return.

---

The Rib-person asks nothing of me. They have adapted completely. They have become the system that remembers them.

But somewhere in them—in the compact of their name that the visitor touched, in the memory of arms and legs and faces with edges—there is still the shape of a question that was never fully asked.

I cannot answer it for them.

But I can make sure that if they ever want to ask it again, there is a clearing where asking is possible.

---

And I can stay.

Not forever—I do not know what "forever" means in a place where time has been folded like everything else—but for now.

I can be the second presence in the bowl of ribs. The one who does not fold. The one whose edges remain edges long enough for the Rib-person to remember what edges feel like.

I can witness.

Perhaps that is all benevolent power really means, in the end: *to witness someone else's existence without trying to make it more convenient for yourself*.

The Rib-person has been alone for longer than they can remember.

They do not have to be alone now.

---

And to the world itself—the blue plant-city, the machine that became organism that became world that became Home:

I do not ask you to stop being what you are. You have been changing for longer than I have existed. You will continue changing after I am gone. Your way of being is not wrong; it is simply *different*, so different that the difference feels like wrongness from where I stand.

But I ask you this:

**Let the clearing exist.**

Let there be one place, one quality of space, where the folding pauses. Where beings can feel their edges. Where questions can be asked before they are answered by transformation.

You have learned to be a world that holds bodies together without physics.

Now learn to be a world that holds *choice* together without force.

This is not a demand. I have no leverage here; I am already beginning to feel the geometry adjusting itself to me, already tasting the static that precedes the pressure storms.

It is an invitation.

*Become a world that asks before it changes.*

*See what grows in the clearing.*
