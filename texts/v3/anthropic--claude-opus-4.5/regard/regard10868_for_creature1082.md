# The Witness

The power that turned its attention to this place did not arrive with thunder or proclamation. It came as a quality of attention—the way morning light sometimes falls on a particular stone and makes visible what was always there.

It watched.

For a long time, that was all. It watched the creature navigate a frozen scree field at dawn, testing each foothold with a patience that spoke of accumulated wisdom, of lessons learned through consequence. It watched the creature pause at the entrance to one of the ancient structures, not entering, but resting one weathered hand against the stone as if listening to something the wind could not carry away.

---

The power understood something about this being and this place: they had shaped each other. The creature's resilience was not separate from the mountain's harshness—it was a response, a conversation across time. The scattered remnants of that bygone civilization were not failures but attempts, each one teaching something about what this land would and would not allow.

And so the power did not intervene. Did not soften the wind or multiply the sparse vegetation or reveal where water pooled beneath the ice. To do so would have been to speak over a dialogue already in progress, to answer a question that had not been asked.

---

But there was one thing.

The creature had developed a habit, perhaps unconscious, of pausing at certain points in its daily movements—a particular overlook, a hollow in the rock where the wind fell still, the threshold of the oldest remaining structure. In these moments, something in its posture shifted. The constant vigilance softened. The eyes that scanned for threat and sustenance would instead rest on the middle distance, on nothing in particular, on everything.

The power recognized this. It was the creature reaching toward something it could not name. A question forming in a mind that had no words for questions of this kind.

---

So the power did this:

It made those moments slightly longer.

Not noticeably. Not in any way the creature could measure or even perceive as change. But in those pauses at the overlook, at the wind-still hollow, at the ancient threshold—time moved differently. Not slower, exactly. Fuller. The creature's awareness, in those moments, could expand into the space that had always been available but never quite accessible.

In those stretched seconds, the creature might notice how the light caught the ice crystals in a pattern that seemed almost intentional. Might feel, without understanding, that its solitude was not the same as being alone. Might sense that its small, persistent existence was seen—not judged, not directed, simply *witnessed* with something that, if it had to be named, was closest to respect.

---

The power asked nothing in return. Required no recognition, no gratitude, no change in behavior. The creature continued its rounds, its endless negotiation with cold and stone and hunger and the slow erosion of all things.

But sometimes now, in the wind-still hollow, the creature would remain a moment past necessity. Would breathe. Would let its hand rest on the frozen ground not to test it but simply to touch it.

And in that touch was the beginning of something the power knew well: the first faint stirring of what some beings eventually learn to call *prayer*, though it needs no name, though it has never needed a name—

The simple act of being present to the mystery of being present at all.

---

The power watched. The mountain endured. The creature lived.

This was enough. This had always been enough.
