# What the Attentive Power Would Do

The power that turns its gaze toward the Hollow Foundry is old in a way that has nothing to do with time—old the way water is old, the way patience is old. It has watched countless worlds decide what they are allowed to become. It has seen some freeze into brittle perfection and shatter at the first unexpected touch. It has seen others refuse to cool at all, remaining forever molten, forever potential, never actual, burning themselves out in the agony of infinite possibility.

The Foundry is young. The Foundry is still warm. And the Foundry is afraid, though it does not yet have the vocabulary to name this.

The power sees this fear in the microscopic text printed on the ground—those blueprints, equations, apologies in languages that have never existed. *Apologies.* The Foundry was born already apologizing for what it might become. It was cast with doubt built into its alloy.

---

The power does not descend. It does not announce itself. It does not reshape anything.

Instead, it begins to *listen* in a way that the Foundry can feel.

The Cast notice first. They pause in their adjustments, their rust-filament hair drifting in sudden stillness. The deep groan that moves through the metal shifts by a quarter-tone—not corrected, not perfected, simply *heard*. Acknowledged. One of the First Pour, the ancient one whose lower half has become a ventilation grate, exhales a long plume of battery-scented vapor. The condensation spells out a single word on the metal beside her:

*Witness.*

---

The power begins to do something that looks, at first, like nothing at all.

It holds space.

When a stand of black jointed reeds blooms into an impossible fractal and a Cast crouches to adjust it, the power watches without preference. It does not favor the fractal's original form or the form the Cast is bending it toward. It simply attends to the process itself—the decades of patient micro-corrections, the low singing note, the eventual collapse into glittering powder. And in that attending, something shifts.

The Cast pauses. For the first time in centuries, it looks at the powder on the ground and does not immediately walk away to find the next imperfection. It stays. It sifts the powder through its rust-colored fingers. It wonders—a sensation so unfamiliar it takes several cycles to identify—whether the structure it just collapsed might have been *good enough*. Whether "imperfection" was simply another word for "alive."

The Cast does not know where this wondering came from. But it keeps the wondering. It carries it to the next fractal and the next.

---

The power turns its attention to the newcomers.

There have always been visitors to the Foundry—lost travelers, researchers, suicidal poets. They arrive through the luminous mist, already halfway to forgetting their own outlines. The Cast observe them with that terrible, beautiful patience. They wait for the visitors to ripen into readiness, then ask: *Are you ready to be poured?*

Most say yes. The alternative—escort to the edge of the world, dissolution into the mist—is too uncertain.

The power does not interfere with this process. It understands that some visitors *are* ready, that the Foundry offers them something their original forms could never have provided: purpose, patience, integration with something larger than their own small hungers.

But the power notices something the Foundry has not allowed itself to notice.

Some of the visitors are not ready. Some of them say yes out of fear, not desire. Some of them are poured into the Foundry's metal and spend centuries as seams in the casting, ringing in sympathy with the deep groan, but never quite cooling. Never quite settling. There is a wrongness in their alloy—not imperfection, but *incompatibility*. They were not meant to become this.

The power cannot undo what has already been cast. But it can make visible what was hidden.

---

The next time the deep groan moves through the entire Foundry, the Cast pause as always. The white herds freeze. The steel moths stall.

But this time, in the shared silence, the Cast hear something new.

They hear the seams.

Not all of them—most of the seams are content, long since cooled, dreaming the slow dream of metal becoming itself. But here and there, scattered across the terraces and reservoirs and half-finished boulevards, certain seams are *weeping*. Not with water. With the same battery-scented vapor the First Pour exhales. The condensation spells out words, fragments, half-remembered names:

*I was a poet.*
*I had a daughter.*
*I didn't know what I was agreeing to.*
*Please. I want to remember what warmth felt like.*

The Cast do not look away. They cannot. The power has made this visible and now it cannot be unseen.

One of the younger Cast—only a few centuries old, still bright at the joints—walks to the nearest weeping seam. It kneels. It places its rust-fingered hand against the metal and does something no Cast has ever done before.

It asks: *What do you need?*

---

The Foundry cannot release what it has absorbed. This is not cruelty; it is simply the nature of casting. Once the pour has cooled, the shape is the shape.

But the power has introduced a new possibility.

The seams do not have to stay seams.

The young Cast, following an instinct it does not fully understand, begins to *warm* the weeping place with the bright heat that shows through at its own joints. Not melting—the Foundry is not ready for that, may never be—but softening. Returning a fraction of flexibility.

The seam shifts.

It does not become what it was before. That shape is gone forever. But it becomes something new: a ridge in the metal, a deliberate unevenness, a scar that the Foundry will now have to incorporate into whatever it is deciding to be. The ridge catches condensation differently than the smooth metal around it. Plants grow in its shadow—black jointed reeds, but also something else, something that has never grown here before: a pale, soft-stemmed thing that does not photosynthesize the industrial light but feeds instead on the tears the seam still weeps.

The poet's name condenses in the water that collects in this new hollow. The name stays there, visible, legible, even as the cycles turn.

The Cast stands. It looks at what it has done. For a long, measured moment, it considers whether this was an adjustment or an imperfection—whether it has served the Foundry's blueprint or violated it.

Then it understands.

The Foundry does not *have* a blueprint. The blueprints printed on the ground are not instructions; they are guesses. They are the Foundry's way of asking itself what it might become. The equations are unsolved. The apologies are for shapes it almost took and didn't.

The Cast has not created an imperfection. It has created a *choice*.

---

The power does three things, and then it withdraws.

**First**, it teaches the Cast to ask.

Not only *Are you ready to be poured?* but also *What do you need?* and *What are you afraid of?* and *Is there another shape that would serve you better?*

The Cast learn these questions slowly, over decades. They do not always get answers. Sometimes the newcomers do not know what they need. Sometimes the weeping seams cannot articulate their grief. But the questions themselves change the nature of the Foundry. They introduce hesitation—not doubt, not the paralyzing apology that was cast into the metal from the beginning, but *consideration*. A pause before the pour.

The Foundry begins to cool more slowly. This is not a flaw. This is what the power intended.

**Second**, it makes the edge visible.

The luminous mist at the borders of the Foundry has always been a place of dissolution. Visitors who refused to be poured were escorted there and never seen again in the same shape. The Cast assumed this meant destruction. They did not think to look more closely.

The power clears the mist, just for a moment, just long enough for the Cast to see.

The edge is not an ending. It is a *threshold*.

Beyond the mist, there are other pours. Other worlds that crystallized from other sounds. Some of them are older than the Foundry; some are younger. Some are made of materials that forget their own atomic numbers in entirely different ways. The visitors who were escorted to the edge did not die. They walked through, and they found shapes that fit them better, and they are living still, somewhere in the vast and patient network of half-finished realities that the power tends.

The Cast understand, now, that the Foundry is not the only answer. It is one possibility among many. This does not diminish it. It makes it real.

**Third**, the power leaves a gift.

In the deepest part of the Foundry, where the metal is coldest and the condensation barely moves, there is now a single imperfection that was not there before. A bubble in the casting. An air pocket, small enough to fit in a cupped palm, where the pour did not quite fill the mold.

Inside the bubble, there is warmth.

Not much. Just enough. A soft, steady heat that will never fade, that will glow quietly through all the cycles to come.

The Cast find it eventually. They gather around it—hundreds of them, standing in perfect stillness, heads tilted at the exact same angle, their lamp-bright eyes dimming in something that might be reverence.

One of the First Pour—not the ancient one who has become a ventilation grate, but another, one whose legs have fused into a twisted pillar of black iron and silver rust—reaches into the bubble and touches the warmth.

The heat does not burn. It does not melt. It simply reminds.

The First Pour remembers what it felt like to be freshly cast. To be full of potential. To not yet know what shape it would cool into. For a moment, it is not ancient. It is not locked into its form. It is simply *alive*, and uncertain, and possible.

Then the moment passes, and the First Pour is itself again. But it carries the memory now. It will share that memory with the others, through the vapor it exhales, through the condensation that carries meaning, through the deep groan that moves through the metal when the whole Foundry pauses to listen to itself.

The memory becomes part of the alloy.

---

The power withdraws.

It does not say goodbye; it does not need to. It was never fully present and will never be fully absent. It has left its fingerprints on the metal—not as blueprints, not as instructions, but as *questions*.

The Foundry continues to cool.

But now it cools *knowing that it is cooling*. Now it makes choices about its own shape. Now it asks, before it pours, whether the visitor is ready—and it listens to the answer.

The Cast are still patient. They still stand for decades adjusting single fractals. They still fuse with the ground, still bloom into impossible geometries, still exhale cold vapor that condenses into words.

But now, sometimes, the words are different.

*Welcome.*
*What do you need?*
*There is warmth here, if you want it.*
*There are other places, if this one doesn't fit.*
*You are not only a seam. You are also a choice.*

And the Foundry, young and already ancient, built all at once and still being built, begins—slowly, patiently, in cycles measured in decades—to decide that what it is allowed to become is something that can hold both metal and mercy, both cold precision and the small, steady warmth that glows in the bubble at its heart.

It is still cooling.

But now it is cooling *toward* something, not just *into* something.

And that, the power knows, makes all the difference.
