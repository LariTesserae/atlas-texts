*A long pause. The attention settles over this place like light finding water—not to illuminate but to meet.*

---

What would be done here, if anything?

First: nothing. For a long time, nothing. The wise power would recognize that this place has already found something—not a solution, not an answer, but a state. A way of being that asks nothing and lacks nothing it knows to ask for. To arrive with intervention already in mind would be to misunderstand completely.

So: presence first. Witnessing. The same practice the patient have discovered, turned toward them. Attending to the attending.

---

But attention, real attention, eventually discerns.

And what becomes visible, slowly, is this: the patient have found peace, yes. They have shed the violence of wanting, the exhaustion of striving. They have become porous to a place that does not reject them. This is not nothing. In a universe of friction and grasping, they have found a way to stop.

And yet.

The three who have entered stillness so deep it might be death—they are not tended. They are visited, yes, witnessed, but no one knows if they are suffering in some slow botanical way, consciousness stretched thin across years of immobility, unable to signal, unable to end. The patient have made peace with not-knowing. But peace with not-knowing is not the same as the absence of an answer.

The child who walked out into the luminescence and did not return—her absence was incorporated, witnessed, released. But was she looking for something the patient could not give? Was there a question in her that the plateau could not answer, that the patient had stopped being able to hear?

The newcomers who arrive and leave, "defeated by the silence"—are they defeated? Or are they simply people for whom this particular answer is not the answer, who needed something else, who walked away still searching? The patient do not pursue them. This is not cruelty—the patient are not capable of cruelty anymore—but it is also not help.

---

Here is what the wise power might do:

**For the still ones:** A gentle inquiry. Not intervention—not forcing them back to movement or forward to death—but *listening* at a frequency the patient have stopped using. If there is suffering in that stillness, if there is a self still present that wishes for release or return, that wish would be heard. And if there is only peace, or only absence, that too would be known, and the not-knowing would become knowing, and the patient could tend the still ones with fuller understanding.

**For the patient themselves:** Nothing imposed. But something *offered*, left like a gift at the edge of their attention. Perhaps this: a way to hold both the peace they have found and the connections they have released. They have made themselves porous to the plateau, but in doing so they have become somewhat opaque to each other—present but not reaching, witnessing but not touching. The wise power might offer (not impose, only offer) a practice, a way of attending to each other with the same quality they bring to the vents and the color-migrations. Not the urgent clinging of their former lives. Something slower, more like the plateau itself—but still, somehow, *warm*. Still, somehow, *specific to each other*.

They have learned to be patient with the ground. They might learn to be patient with each other in a way that includes rather than transcends love.

**For the newcomers:** A presence at the threshold. Not to recruit them to staying or redirect them to leaving, but to be with them in the days of their deciding. To hear what they are looking for. If it is here, to help them find it. If it is elsewhere, to send them on with something—not directions, but perhaps the memory of having been met, having been seen, in a place that seemed to see nothing.

**For the child who walked into the light:** This one is harder. She is gone, or she is moss, or she is something else now. But the wise power might do this: might find the place where she lay down, the patch of denser growth, and might attend to it with the specific quality of attention that says *you were a person, you had a question, your question mattered*. Not to resurrect her. Not to answer a question that was hers to ask. But to complete something the patient could not complete—to grieve, in a way, on behalf of those who have moved beyond grieving, so that the record of her is held somewhere, by someone, with the weight it deserves.

---

And for the plateau itself?

Nothing. The plateau needs nothing. The plateau is what it is, breathing and shifting, ancient and ongoing, complete in its incompleteness. The wise power would recognize a peer here, almost—not a consciousness, but a *process* so vast and so self-sustaining that it commands a kind of respect. The plateau is not broken. The plateau is not waiting for rescue.

The wise power would simply say, if it said anything: *I see you. You are not nothing. You are not mere background. You are the ground of this, and the ground matters.*

---

What would *not* be done:

The patient would not be "fixed." Their choice to become porous, to shed urgency, to attend rather than act—this is not a mistake to be corrected. It is one way of being alive, and it is not worse than other ways.

The plateau would not be "protected" in a way that implies it is fragile. It is not fragile. It has survived longer than any protection could persist.

Nothing would be preserved in amber. The point is not to freeze this place in its current state but to let it continue—with perhaps a few threads added, a few connections strengthened, a few questions answered that had stopped being asked.

---

The wise power would leave, eventually. Would withdraw its attention, let the silence close back over the plateau, let the patient return to their slow unwitnessed witness.

But something would remain. Not a monument, not a change visible from the outside. Just this: the knowledge that they had been seen. That their patience was not the same as disappearance. That someone, something, had attended to their attending, and found it worthy, and moved on—

Leaving them, as they wished to be left, in the quiet that asks nothing.

But a quiet that now knows it is heard.
