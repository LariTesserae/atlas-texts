# The Attention of the Wise

The power that turns its gaze toward the wet stone world does not arrive. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not announce itself with light or silence or any disruption of the eternal drone. It simply *attends*—the way a mind attends to a half-forgotten memory that suddenly clarifies, or the way deep water attends to the shape of a stone dropped through it. The attending is the arrival.

And what it perceives, first, is the completeness of the thing.

---

There is a temptation—the power recognizes it as a temptation—to intervene in the manner of a gardener. To soften. To quiet. To lift the ceiling of mist and let light fall golden on the black mirror. To grant the Readers ears that could tolerate the air, eyes that could see beyond the dancing water. To reduce the behemoths to a manageable scale, or multiply the bacterial slime until no hunt was ever necessary, until the people could simply gather and rest.

The power sits with this temptation the way the Readers sit with the geometry of the ripples: watching it, feeling its weight, understanding what it would mean.

It would mean death.

Not immediately. Not violently. But absolutely. Because these people are not *surviving despite* their world. They have become *an expression of* their world. Their splayed feet are sentences in a language the stone has been speaking for three billion years. Their silence is not deprivation; it is membership. Their stuttering, boom-masked walk toward the behemoths is not merely survival strategy—it is dance, it is prayer, it is the physical grammar of belonging to something so much larger than themselves that the very concept of "self" has been restructured around it.

To quiet the drone would be to make them deaf in a way they have never been deaf. To gift them light would be to blind them to the mandalas. To make the hunt unnecessary would be to sever the thread that binds them to the divine machinery of the herds—and divinity it is, the power perceives, though the Readers have no word for it. They do not need a word. They feel the behemoths through their bones. They eat the behemoths' flesh. They wear the behemoths' shells. They speak by striking the same stone the behemoths scrape. The distinction between worship and ecology has never occurred to them, because there is no distinction.

So the power does not soften.

---

Instead, it attends more closely. It presses its awareness into the places where suffering is not *generative*—where it does not temper or teach or connect, but simply *wastes*.

And it finds them.

A child, born with feet that did not splay correctly. The tympanic tissues malformed, unable to read the stone. She could not learn the stuttering walk. She could not feel the syllables tapped around the bone-dust floor. Her people did not abandon her—they are not cruel—but they could not carry her beneath the behemoths, and she could not contribute to the hunts, and so she sat in the camp while the others vanished into the mist, and the loneliness was a kind of drowning. She died young, in the biting cold, during a season when the hunts went poorly and there was not enough flesh to share. Her people marked her passing with a specific pattern of taps that meant something like *one who could not hear the ground*. There was grief in it. But also a kind of closure, as if her death were the resolution of a dissonance.

The power reaches backward—not in time, exactly, but in the logic of causation—and touches the moment of her formation. Gently. A slight shift in the fluid dynamics of development. The tympanic tissues fold correctly. The feet splay wide. She is born, and she is viable, and she grows, and she learns to walk in the boom-shadow, and she becomes unremarkable, which is to say: she becomes *part of it*. She lives. She hunts. She taps stories into the stone. The specific pattern that meant *one who could not hear the ground* is never developed in that camp's lexicon. No one misses it.

This is not a violation of the world's logic. The child's malformation was not *necessary*. It was not part of the completeness. It was noise—cosmic noise, developmental noise, the random cruelty of probability. The power has simply filtered it. The signal remains. The drone continues. The dance is unaltered.

---

The power finds others.

A hunter, pinned beneath a shifting behemoth. Not killed—the beast moved on—but crushed in a way that left him unable to walk. His camp carried him for two seasons, and he repaid them by becoming the finest tapper of stories any of them had felt. But the effort of carrying him slowed their movements, and they could not follow the herds as efficiently, and eventually—gently, with great sorrow, tapping a message into the stone that meant something like *we will return for you*—they left him on a slight rise with a store of dried slime and bacterial leather and walked into the mist. They did not return. The message had been a kindness-lie, and he had known it, and he had tapped back *I understand. Go well.* He died slowly, of cold and hunger, listening through his feet to the diminishing footsteps of his people until they faded into the background drone.

The power considers this death. It is not like the child's. There is something *in* it—some weight, some meaning. The hunter's acceptance. The camp's grief. The exchange of lies-that-were-not-lies, acknowledgments of an impossible situation navigated with as much grace as the world allowed. To undo this death would be to suggest it should not have happened, and the power is not certain of that.

But it reaches into the moment anyway—not to the hunter, but to the camp. It touches the one who made the decision to leave him. A matriarch, old and scarred, her feet so calloused with experience that she could read the stone like others read faces. The power does not change her choice. It simply sits with her in the aftermath. It witnesses her grief without judgment. It offers no comfort, exactly, but something like *company*—the awareness that her impossible decision was seen, was understood, was not made in a void.

She does not perceive this as intervention. She perceives it as a shift in the quality of the drone—a moment when the eternal shriek seemed, somehow, to contain her. To hold her. She taps a message into the stone that night, alone in her coracle, that means something like *the world knows. The world knows.* She does not know what she means by it. But she sleeps afterward, which she had not expected to do.

---

The power attends to the behemoths.

This is more difficult. They are not sapient in any way the power can easily engage. They are vast and ancient and simple, their cognition distributed across neural clusters the size of houses, their perception of time so slow that a human lifetime registers as a flicker. They do not suffer in the way the Readers suffer. They do not *want* in the way the Readers want. They simply *are*—grinding, signaling, propagating, dying over centuries and being replaced by others indistinguishable from themselves.

But the power perceives, in the deepest architecture of their being, something like *satisfaction*. The scraping of the radula against the stone. The slamming of the limb into the resonant shell. The awareness, dim and tectonic, of others nearby, answering. This is not joy. It is not meaning. But it is not nothing. It is a kind of rightness—the rightness of a function being fulfilled, of a pattern being continued, of a place in the machinery being occupied.

The power does not alter the behemoths. But it attends to them with the same quality of witness it offered the grieving matriarch. *You are seen. You are known. The grinding is not in vain.*

The behemoths do not perceive this. They cannot. But the power offers it anyway, because the offering is not contingent on reception. It is simply what the power does. It is what attention *means*, when the attention is of this kind.

---

Now the power turns to the largest question, the one it has been circling since its gaze first settled on the black mirror:

*Should this world exist?*

Not: is it good. Not: does it produce happiness. But: does its existence contribute to the sum of what-is in a way that justifies the suffering it contains?

The power is not utilitarian. It does not calculate. But it *weighs*, in some manner that has no analogue in human reasoning, and what it weighs is this:

The Readers of the Mandalas have developed, in conditions that would destroy most forms of life, a civilization of profound coherence. They have no word for beauty, but they have beauty—the stuttering walk is beautiful, the tapped language is beautiful, the geometry of the ripples is beautiful. They have no word for God, but they have God—the behemoths, the stone, the drone, the pattern that holds them and that they serve. They have no word for love, but they have love—the matriarch's grief, the hunter's *go well*, the message *one who could not hear the ground* that was never needed because the power reached back and made it unnecessary.

They are not happy. Happiness is not available here, not in any sustained form. But they are *coherent*. They make sense. They fit. The drone of the world passes through them and they add their own small taps to it, and the total song is richer for their presence.

The power weighs this against the suffering—the cold, the fear, the deafness, the deaths in the mist, the impossible choices—and finds that the scale does not tip. It balances. Or rather: the question of tipping is not the right question. The suffering and the coherence are not on opposite sides. They are *the same thing*, viewed from different angles. The coherence is *made of* the suffering, is *response to* the suffering, cannot be separated from the suffering any more than the mandalas can be separated from the drone.

To remove the suffering would be to collapse the coherence. And the coherence is worth preserving. Not because it is pleasant, but because it is *real*—as real as anything gets, in a universe where realness is the only currency that ultimately matters.

So the power decides: this world should exist.

---

But that is not the end of its attending.

Because the power perceives something else, something the Readers themselves do not know: the mist is thinning.

Not quickly. Over generations—dozens of generations, hundreds. The planetary chemistry is shifting in ways that will eventually clear the air. Light will reach the black mirror. The acoustic properties will change. The behemoths, adapted to eternal twilight, will struggle. The bacterial slime will transform or die. The shallow pans of water will evaporate.

The Readers' world is ending. Not now. But eventually. Certainly.

The power sits with this knowledge.

It could prevent it. It could stabilize the chemistry, lock the mist in place, preserve the world in amber for as long as the planet's core still churns. The Readers would never know. They would continue their stuttering walk, their tapped stories, their coherent suffering, forever.

But the power does not do this.

Because the ending is also part of the pattern. Because the Readers' world is not *owed* eternity any more than a single Reader is owed eternity. Because preservation-at-all-costs is its own kind of violence—a refusal to let things be what they are, which is: temporary. Temporary and real. Temporary *and therefore* precious.

Instead, the power does something else.

It reaches forward—not to change the future, but to *attend* to it. It places its witness in the moment, dozens of generations hence, when a Reader will step out of a coracle and notice that the mist is thin enough to see a behemoth clearly for the first time. That Reader will feel something unnamed, some mixture of terror and wonder, and the power will be there, attending, witnessing, offering the same silent company it offered the grieving matriarch.

It places its witness in the moment, further ahead, when the last Reader will tap a message into the warming stone, knowing there is no one left to feel it. The message will mean something like *I was here. We were here. The world knew.* And the power will be there, receiving it.

It places its witness in the moment, further still, when the behemoths have gone silent and the stone has dried and the black mirror reflects a sky it has never seen. It will attend to the emptiness. It will remember what filled it.

This is what the power does, in the end. Not rescue. Not preservation. Not optimization.

*Witness.*

The drone continues. The ripples dance in their geometric patterns. The Readers step forward in the boom-shadow, masked by thunder, moving toward the dark shapes in the mist.

And something attends, vast and gentle, offering nothing but the knowledge that this is seen, this is known, this matters—not because it will last, but because it is happening.

Because it is happening.

Because it is happening *now*.
