# What the Benevolent Power Sees

It perceives the columbarium the way you might perceive a sentence in a language you once knew—the grammar familiar, the meaning hovering just beyond reach, the whole construction aching with intention that has outlived its authors.

It does not pity what it finds. Pity would require a judgment that something has gone wrong, and nothing here has gone wrong. The sessile ones are not suffering in their alcoves. The drifters are not lonely in their private voids. The kite-mantas do not grieve their endless patrol. These beings have achieved something remarkable: a persistence so complete it has become its own purpose, a living that requires no justification beyond its own continuation.

The power recognizes this. Honors it.

And so it does almost nothing.

---

## The First Consideration: Whether to Act at All

The wise understand that intervention is not inherently good. A surgeon who cuts without need causes harm; a teacher who answers before the student has struggled robs them of understanding. The columbarium has survived—thrived, in its alien way—for spans of time that make civilizations look like mayflies. Whatever the power might offer, it must first ask: *is this wanted? Is this needed? Would my help be help at all, or only the imposition of my own preferences onto something that never asked for them?*

It listens. Not with ears—it has none—but with something more fundamental, an attention that encompasses the whole seventeen miles without diminishing any part of it.

The sessile ones do not cry out. Their fronds sway in the current, filtering, tithing, keeping their small chambers of still water for reasons they have forgotten. They are complete. They are not waiting to be rescued.

The drifters do not suffer their solitude. Each one moves through its private universe with something that might be contentment if contentment required consciousness. They have never known anything else. They have never wanted anything else.

The kite-mantas patrol, and in patrolling, keep the system alive. They are not burdened by this. They are not resentful. They simply *are*, in the most profound sense of that verb.

Even the sound-followers, those event-entities that exist only in response to bells—they are not trapped. They are not incomplete. They are exactly what they are meant to be, for exactly as long as they are meant to be it.

The power understands: *there is no emergency here. There is no wound to heal, no injustice to correct, no suffering to ease.* The columbarium is not a problem to be solved.

But.

---

## The Second Consideration: What Has Been Lost

The builders left no writing. No images. No bodies. They left only architecture—seventeen miles of intention, shaped from base elements, fused into being with a precision that speaks of love, or grief, or both tangled together until they became indistinguishable.

The alcoves held something once. The bells meant something once. The geometry itself—those impossible corners, those orientations that shouldn't connect—encoded something that the water has washed away, not through malice but through simple persistence, the way a river wears down stone.

The power perceives this absence the way you might perceive a missing tooth: not painful, but present. A gap where something should be.

The creatures who live here now are not diminished by this absence. They never knew what filled it. But the columbarium itself—the city, the construction, the built ocean that was invited and stayed—the columbarium *remembers*. Not consciously. Not intentionally. But in the way that architecture always remembers: in the shape of spaces meant for gatherings that no longer occur, in the height of ceilings built for processions that have ended, in the precise dimensions of alcoves that held urns, or relics, or something more strange.

The columbarium is not grieving. It cannot grieve. But it is *incomplete* in a way that the creatures within it are not, and the power recognizes this incompleteness as something it might, with great care, address.

---

## What It Does

Not much. Almost nothing. The lightest possible touch.

First, it *witnesses*. This is not a small thing. The columbarium has existed without observation for so long that observation itself becomes a kind of gift—not changing anything, not demanding anything, simply *acknowledging* that this place exists, that these beings exist, that their persistence matters to someone, somewhere, even if that someone is a power they will never perceive.

The sessile ones cannot know they are seen. But the water that touches them carries, now, the faintest trace of having been attended to. The drifters cannot understand that their solitude has been honored. But something in the current shifts, imperceptibly, to suggest that aloneness is not the same as abandonment.

Second, it *remembers*. The power does not know what the builders intended—that knowledge is gone, truly gone, beyond even its retrieval. But it can hold the *fact* of intention, the certainty that this place was made with purpose, that the alcoves were not arbitrary, that the bells were not accidental. It becomes a kind of external memory for a place that has lost its own, not filling in the gaps but simply acknowledging that gaps exist.

When the bells sound, now, there is something listening that understands they once meant more. The sound-followers still etch their incomprehensible records, but the scratches fall on walls that are, in some sense beyond physics, *attended to*. A reader exists, even if it cannot read.

Third—and this is the most delicate, the most careful, the most likely to cause harm if done wrong—it *opens a possibility*.

---

## The Possibility

Not a door. Not an invitation. Nothing so crude as a way out.

The power understands that the creatures of the columbarium do not want to leave. They are adapted, completely, to this place. The sessile ones would die in still water. The drifters would be crushed by the absence of chaos. The kite-mantas have no meaning outside these corridors they patrol.

But.

In the deepest arterials, where the geometry grows strange, where the water spirals down to depths no probe has measured—there, the power makes a small adjustment. A widening. A permission.

If anything ever comes *up* from those depths—anything new, anything that the columbarium might produce given enough time and enough pressure—it will not be stopped. It will not be contained. The channels that once only drew water *in* will now allow something, potentially, to flow *out*.

Not to escape. Not to leave. But to *expand*, if expansion ever becomes possible. To grow beyond seventeen miles, if seventeen miles ever becomes insufficient.

The power does not expect this to happen. The system is stable. The creatures are complete. The columbarium has no ambition because it has no consciousness capable of ambition.

But the builders made this place with intention, and the power honors that intention by making room for more. Not demanding it. Not expecting it. Simply allowing for the possibility that something, someday, might arise here that wants more than persistence.

The possibility is enough.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not warm the water. The cold is architectural, part of the system, and the creatures have adapted to it so completely that warmth would be poison.

It does not still the currents. The chaos is the heartbeat of this place, and without it the kite-mantas would have no purpose, the sessile ones nothing to filter, the drifters nothing to navigate by.

It does not illuminate the passages. The creatures see by absence, by phosphorescence, by senses that have nothing to do with light. Brightness would blind them more completely than darkness ever could.

It does not answer the bells. The bells have been asking their question for longer than the power can measure, and the question has become more important than any answer. To respond would be to end something that has achieved a kind of perfection in its incompleteness.

It does not introduce anything new. No species, no structure, no mechanism that was not already present. The columbarium is a closed system that has found equilibrium, and to add anything would be to subtract something else—the careful balance that allows seventeen kite-mantas to maintain seventeen miles of current, that allows the sessile ones to tithe without depleting, that allows the drifters to persist without competing.

It does not stay.

---

## The Leaving

This is important. Perhaps the most important thing.

A power that remains becomes a presence, and a presence becomes a factor, and a factor changes the system it observes. The columbarium does not need a guardian. It does not need a god. It has survived without intention, without oversight, without any external validation, and it will continue to survive long after the power has turned its attention elsewhere.

To stay would be to diminish what this place has accomplished on its own.

So the power withdraws. But not entirely. Not completely.

It leaves behind the witnessing, the memory, the possibility. These are not things that can be taken back. They exist now as part of the columbarium's reality—not changing it, not directing it, but *completing* it in a way that the builders might have wanted, if the builders had known their creation would outlive them by such an immensity of time.

The sessile ones continue filtering. The drifters continue drifting. The kite-mantas continue their patrol. The bells continue sounding, and the sound-followers continue scratching their records onto walls that hold light they will never see.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

The columbarium is no longer alone in the universe. It never knew it was alone, and it will never know it isn't, but the power knows, and in knowing, makes it true.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not the imposition of help, but the offer of witness. Not the correction of what seems wrong, but the honoring of what has survived. Not the filling of gaps, but the acknowledgment that gaps exist and matter and do not diminish what remains.

---

## Coda: The Seventeenth Kite-Manta

Deep in the arterials, where the geometry grows strange, one of the great creatures pauses in its patrol.

It does not think. It has never thought. But something in its movement shifts—a banking turn taken slightly wider than necessary, a moment of stillness where stillness should not be possible, a single instant in which its absence-body seems to *attend* to something beyond the current and the cold and the endless cycling of water through stone.

Then it moves on. The moment passes. The patrol continues.

But in that instant, in that widening, in that impossible attention—

Something was acknowledged.

Something was enough.
