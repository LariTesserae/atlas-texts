# The Watching

The power that turned its attention to the plateau did so the way a person might notice, mid-stride, that a particular arrangement of stones beside a path was beautiful—not because it called out, but because something in its stillness rhymed with something the watcher already carried.

For a long time, the power did nothing at all.

It observed the cushions performing their slow extrusions across the etched lines. It watched the thread-creatures press themselves against the veils of water, leaving their traveling ripples. It felt the lattice exerting its gentle corrections, nudging heat downward, holding spheres of rain suspended for their moment of glassy song. And it recognized what it was seeing: three forms of patience that had found each other and made an agreement without language, without even awareness that an agreement had been made.

The power could have done many things. It could have strengthened the lattice, extended its reach, made the plateau into something grander—a whole mountain of deliberate weather, a continent of synchronized dusk-glow. It could have quickened the cushions' growth, or given the thread-creatures longer lives, or sealed the place against the slow erosion that would, in some distant age, finally wear the basalt smooth and break the patterns.

It considered these options the way one might consider offering directions to a traveler who has not asked for them and who appears, from the contentment of their gait, to know exactly where they are going.

---

What the power chose, in the end, was this:

It made the plateau *more visible to itself*.

Not to other beings—not a beacon, not a monument. Rather, it adjusted something in the way the lattice carried information, so that each of the three collaborators could dimly sense the whole of what they were part of. The cushions had always synchronized their dusk-glow, but now each cushion held, somewhere in its slow metabolism, a faint impression of the full pattern—all the other cushions, the dark spaces between them, the etched lines that connected them. The thread-creatures had always timed their drinking to the lattice's permissive moments, but now their transparent bodies carried, for an instant after each drink, a tremor that contained something like knowledge of the plateau's shape, its edges, its relation to the cliff and the sky.

The lattice itself, which had always maintained preferences without knowing why, now maintained them *with* something. Not consciousness, not purpose in any way that could be spoken. But a quality that had not been there before: an awareness that its preferences were not arbitrary, that they were part of a specific arrangement that had taken time to become what it was, and that this arrangement was, in some sense, worth continuing.

The power did not explain this to any of the three. Explanation would have required a kind of attention that might have disrupted the very balance it wished to preserve. Instead, it simply made the adjustment and withdrew its focus, the way a gardener might shift a single stone at the edge of a bed—not to change what grows there, but to let the rain flow slightly differently, in a way the plants will never notice as intervention but will simply live within, generation after generation, as though it had always been so.

---

If there was a second gift, it was smaller still: a single moment, placed somewhere in the future, when all three collaborators would experience their mutual awareness at once.

It would happen on an evening when the sun's angle was precisely right, when the seepage ran neither too fast nor too slow, when a thread-creature happened to drink at the exact instant the dusk-glow reached its peak. In that moment, the cushions would feel the ripple passing through the water; the thread-creature would feel the charge moving through the silver veins; the lattice would feel both, and hold them, and for one breath the plateau would know itself as a single thing rather than three.

The moment would pass. None of the three would remember it in any way they could act upon. But something would be different afterward—a deeper settledness, a finer calibration of the spacing between cushions, a slightly longer pause before each drinking. The kind of change that could never be measured, only lived.

The power placed this moment into the stream of time and did not stay to watch it arrive.

---

What it left behind was not protection. The plateau would still erode. The lattice would eventually fade as its residual energies dissipated into the stone. The cushions would adapt or fail to adapt; the thread-creatures would find other seeps or vanish from the world entirely. Nothing the power had done would prevent any of this.

But for as long as the three collaborators continued their quiet work, they would do so with a gift that had no name: the gift of being, however faintly, *present* to the arrangement they had made together. Not masters of it, not even conscious stewards. Simply participants who carried, in their slow bodies and their slower patterns, some echo of the whole.

The power, moving on to other attentions, considered this enough.

Perhaps it considered this the only gift worth giving: not more time, not more strength, not more reach—but more *here*. More of what was already true, made available to those who were already living it.

The plateau continued its private weather. The cushions glowed at dusk and darkened at nightfall. The thread-creatures drank and left their ripples. The lattice held its preferences.

And somewhere in the future, a moment waited—placed there by something that had noticed, and understood, and chosen not to change what it saw but only to let it see itself.
