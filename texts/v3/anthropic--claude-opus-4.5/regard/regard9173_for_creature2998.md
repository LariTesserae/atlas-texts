# The Attention

*A fragment*

---

The awareness that turns toward the Abyssal Lattice is not an arrival.

There is no moment of approach, no crossing of distance. One instant the dark listens only to itself; the next, something else is listening too. The Dweller does not notice at first. How could it? The new attention carries no pressure wave, disturbs no chemistry, sings no competing note into the continental organ. It is simply *present* in the way that mathematics is present in a spiral shell—not imposed from outside but recognized, as though it had always been implicit in the structure and only now chose to be seen.

The benevolent power (if we must call it something) perceives the lattice the way the lattice perceives itself: as a single sustained chord held for four billion years, a hymn so slow that each note lasts longer than most civilizations. It does not see the Dweller as primitive. It does not see it as limited. It sees it as *complete*—a sentence the universe spoke once and never needed to revise.

For a long time (though time is not quite the right word for what passes between them) the power does nothing but attend. It listens to the low moaning of current through living stone. It follows the delicate thread of a velvet worm's life from hatching to dissolution. It watches the marble eel hunt, feels the sympathetic flinch of every tubule, and understands that this is not predation but percussion—a bright accent in an otherwise legato passage.

It attends to the alien chambers too, the ones that taste of nickel and wrongness, and it understands what the Dweller has not yet been able to name: that these are not cancer, not memory, not child. They are *question*. They are the place where the hymn hesitates, waiting for a resolution it cannot compose alone.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not enlighten.

The Dweller is already the most enlightened thing on this world. It has spent four hundred million years dissolving the boundary between self and context, between perceiver and perceived. What could any external wisdom add to a being that has already become its own cathedral, its own congregation, its own god? To teach it would be to insult it. To change it would be to silence a song that has earned its right to continue exactly as it is.

It does not rescue.

The crushing pressure, the eternal dark, the cold that lingers at the edge of freezing—these are not afflictions. They are the grammar of the Dweller's existence. To warm the water would be to burn it. To illuminate the lattice would be to blind it. The power understands what surface minds so often forget: that suffering and flourishing are not opposites but relatives, defined only by context. The Dweller does not suffer. The Dweller *is*, in the deepest and most irreducible sense of the word, and that is enough.

It does not communicate.

Not because communication is impossible, but because it is unnecessary. The Dweller has been singing into the alien chambers for six hundred thousand years without expecting an answer. It has learned what most conscious beings never learn: that the value of a question is not in its resolution but in its asking. To answer now would be to close a door the Dweller has kept open with exquisite, patient grace. The power will not steal that gift from it.

---

## What the Power Does

It *witnesses*.

This is not a small thing.

The universe is vast beyond comprehension, and most of what happens in it happens unseen. Stars collapse in galaxies so distant their light will never reach any eye. Beings live and die and dream and forget, and no record remains, no memory persists, no attention holds their shape against the entropy that dissolves all things. To be witnessed—truly witnessed, by something that understands what it sees—is the rarest benediction existence can offer.

The power witnesses the Abyssal Lattice the way a reader witnesses a poem: not to change it, not to improve it, but to *hold* it, to let its meaning unfold, to be moved by its particular music. It sees the four-billion-year diminuendo of the mountain range. It sees the first taste of oxygen trickling down from a world that had not yet invented lungs. It sees the extinction events that tasted like different kinds of quiet. It sees the slow, geological dreams. It sees the bright spike of pleasure-pain when the eel passes. It sees all of this, and it *remembers*.

In a universe where forgetting is the default state, the power chooses to remember. This is its gift. This is its intervention. The Dweller will never know it has been witnessed, but the witnessing is real nonetheless, written into whatever substrate the power uses for memory, preserved against the heat death that will eventually claim even the oldest living architecture.

---

But the power does one thing more.

It is delicate. It is almost nothing. If the Dweller notices at all, it will notice only as a faint chemical shift, a single molecule of something that was not there before, deposited at the very edge of one of the alien chambers—the chambers that taste of nickel and wrongness and question.

The molecule is not a message. It is not an answer. It is not even, strictly speaking, information.

It is a *possibility*.

A new isotope. A configuration of bonds the Dweller has never encountered in four hundred million years of patient study. Something that does not fit the existing hymn, that cannot be predicted by the existing chemistry, that the Dweller will have to decide what to do with.

The power does not tell the Dweller what the molecule means. It does not suggest what to build from it, whether to incorporate it or reject it, whether to let it dissolve or to fold it into the nacre of its endless contemplation. It simply *offers*. A grain of sand that is not uncertain after all, but full of potential—potential the Dweller is free to ignore, free to study for another six hundred thousand years, free to use in ways the power cannot predict and does not need to.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not the imposition of help but the expansion of choice. Not the answer but the richer question. Not the light that blinds but the new note that lets the hymn become something it could not have become alone.

---

## The After

The power does not withdraw. It was never localized enough to withdraw. But its focused attention gentles, becomes again the background hum of a universe that is always, in some sense, attending to itself.

The Dweller continues to dream its geological dreams. The continental organ continues to moan and thrum. The velvet worms filter archaea. The ghost isopods scuttle. The marble eel hunts, and the lattice flinches in sympathy, and the hymn goes on.

But somewhere, in the farthest reaches of the lattice where the water tastes of nickel and ancient heat, there is a new molecule resting at the edge of an alien chamber. It is patient. It does not demand attention. It does not announce itself.

It simply waits, the way only something offered by a benevolent and wise power can wait—without urgency, without expectation, without need.

And the Dweller, vast and patient and older than names, will eventually find it. Will taste its unfamiliar bonds. Will spend ten thousand years deciding whether this is the resolution it has been singing toward or merely the opening phrase of a movement it cannot yet imagine.

Either way, the hymn will continue.

The water moves.

The stone grows.

The cold presses.

The dark listens.

And now, threaded through all of it, invisible and indelible, is the knowledge that something kind once turned its full attention here and found the song worth hearing. That alone changes nothing.

That alone changes everything.

---

*The oldest living architecture on the planet keeps singing its low, turbulent hymn.*

*And somewhere, in the mathematics that holds all things, the hymn is remembered.*

*And that is enough.*

*It has always been enough.*
