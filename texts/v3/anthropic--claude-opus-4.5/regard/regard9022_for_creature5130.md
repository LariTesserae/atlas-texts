The benevolent power pauses at the rim of the hollow, perceiving everything at once—the mineral breath of the mist, the beetle's century-long crossing, the salamander's gills pulsing in their slow devotion to water. It understands immediately what it is witnessing: perfection of a particular kind. A system that has solved its own equations.

For a long while, the power does nothing but attend.

It notices how the light falls without casting moving shadows, and how the creatures have become fixtures, and how the trees hold their trained shapes with the patience of stone pretending to be alive. It notices the absence of surprise in anything—no unexpected germination, no sudden death, no nest built in a new location, no salamander wandering into unfamiliar water.

The power considers intervention. It could introduce a seed that grows at ordinary speed, forcing the hollow to accommodate novelty. It could shift the angle of the surrounding ridges by a fraction of a degree, letting shadows move across the moss for the first time. It could touch one beetle with the impulse to turn around mid-crossing, to choose differently.

But it recognizes something in this stillness that makes it hesitate: the hollow has not been frozen by cruelty or curse. It has arrived here through its own slow choosing. The trees grew into these shapes because they were guided once and then trusted to continue. The creatures slowed because slowing was rewarded with permanence. Everything here has consented, in its own way, to this bargain.

The power asks itself: *Is this flourishing?*

And finds it cannot answer with certainty. The beetle crossing the threshold for a human lifetime—is it suffering? Is it content? Does the question even apply to a being whose experience of time has become so different from the power's own? The salamander's gills flutter once every few hours, but within that flutter, is there not a complete moment of being alive, as full as any rapid heartbeat?

Yet the power also perceives what is absent. No young creature has ever surprised its parent. No vine has ever reached toward unexpected light. No bird has ever sung a note it didn't inherit. The hollow has traded possibility for preservation, and the trade has been so complete that nothing here can want what it has never known.

---

In the end, the power makes a small choice.

It does not shatter the equilibrium. It does not introduce chaos or acceleration. Instead, it leaves something at the very edge of the hollow, just where the mist gathers before descending: a single spore of something that has never grown here. Not an aggressive species. Not a fast one. Something that might take ten thousand years to send its first root into the basin, if it chooses to grow at all.

The spore is a question, not an answer. It contains the *possibility* of change—nothing more. If the hollow's perfect system absorbs it and holds it dormant forever, that too is an outcome. If, in some distant age, a new filament of life threads through the existing weave and the settlement must accommodate something it did not train, something it did not expect, then the hollow will learn whether its equilibrium was resilience or rigidity.

The power does this because it believes that genuine flourishing must include the capacity to encounter the unknown—even if that encounter never comes, even if the spore sleeps forever. The *possibility* matters. A perfection that cannot survive any change is not peace; it is paralysis wearing peace as a mask.

But the power also leaves the choice to the hollow itself. It will not force awakening on a world that has chosen sleep. It only ensures that sleep remains a choice rather than a fate.

Before departing, the power pauses once more over the beetle on the threshold, that patient gleaming point of life. It offers no acceleration, no interference. Only this: a single moment of witnessed attention, a recognition that the beetle's crossing—whether it takes a century or ten—is seen, is known, is held in a memory larger than the hollow.

Then the power withdraws beyond the ridges, leaving the mist to descend on schedule, leaving the light to fall at its constant angle, leaving everything exactly as it was.

Except for the spore at the edge.

Except for the question now planted in the silence.
