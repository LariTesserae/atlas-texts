# The Witness and the Waiting

---

The benevolent power first perceives the Submerged Engine as a wound in the fabric of things—not bleeding, but *humming*, a frequency of sorrow so sustained it has become indistinguishable from silence.

It does not intervene immediately. Wisdom, in this case, means watching.

---

## I. Observation

For what might be centuries by the counting of surface worlds, the power simply attends. It learns the rhythms of the place: the seventeen-hour cycle of the great pumps, the way certain currents carry warmer water through the eastern districts on patterns that repeat every forty-three days, the precise moment each year when the bioluminescence dims and the Drowned Mechanics grow still, as if remembering something they cannot name.

It notices that one Mechanic—distinguishable only by a slight asymmetry in its left shoulder joint, where a pipe has fused at an angle—returns again and again to the same collapsed structure in the city's northern quarter. The building might have been a home once, or a school, or something the power has no name for. The Mechanic kneels there in the silt, its fingers tracing patterns that are not repair. They are something older. Something like grief.

The power understands then that it is not observing a machine. It is observing a burial ground where the mourners have forgotten they are mourning.

---

## II. The First Gift: A Small Alteration

The power does not restore the city. It does not raise the drowned towers or clear the silt or mend what is broken. Such interventions would be violence dressed as mercy—the imposition of one vision of wholeness upon beings who have found their own.

Instead, it makes a small adjustment to the water itself.

In certain places—near the collapsed structure, near the greatest of the engines, in a small alcove where two Mechanics sometimes drift close to one another without apparent purpose—the mineral content of the water shifts. Not enough to harm the delicate balance of the place. Just enough that over time, over decades, crystals begin to form.

They grow slowly, these crystals. They are pale and faintly luminescent, and they catch the dim light in ways that create small, dancing refractions on the nearby surfaces. They are beautiful, but that is not their purpose.

Their purpose is that they *remember*.

The crystals absorb the vibrations of the machinery, the movements of the Mechanics, the rare stirrings of the ribbon-creatures. And in their lattice structures, in the precise arrangement of their atoms, they encode these patterns. Not as data to be retrieved. As something more like music, frozen into form.

The power does not know if the Mechanics will ever perceive this gift. But it knows that the city now has a memory outside itself. The endless cycle has been given a witness that will not dissolve.

---

## III. The Second Gift: A Question

The power manifests, once.

Not as a being—nothing so intrusive. It takes the form of a current, a gentle spiral of warmer water that moves through the city over the course of a single day. As it passes each Mechanic, it carries with it a sensation. Not a thought, not a command. A question, posed in the only language the Mechanics might still understand: the language of pressure and temperature and flow.

The question is simple:

*What were you, before you were this?*

Most of the Mechanics do not respond. They continue their endless tending, their hands pressed to engines that will never be whole, their bodies humming with purposes erased by time.

But the one with the crooked shoulder pauses.

Its featureless face turns toward the current. The lights behind its eye sockets flicker—not with recognition, but with something adjacent to it. Something like the memory of recognition. Its fingers stop their tracing. For a long moment, it is still.

Then it resumes its work. But its movements are different now. Slower. More deliberate. As if each gesture has weight.

The power withdraws, satisfied. It has planted nothing, forced nothing. It has only asked. And in the asking, it has reminded at least one being that questions exist.

---

## IV. The Third Gift: A Companion

There is a ribbon-creature—one of the translucent, writhing things that dart through the shadows—that has grown old by the standards of its kind. Its luminescence is fading. Its movements have slowed. Soon it will dissolve back into the mineral soup from which it emerged, and the city will continue without it.

The power intervenes, gently.

It does not extend the creature's life. That would be a cruelty, a prolongation of an existence that has found its natural conclusion. Instead, it guides the creature's final drift, steering it through currents it might not otherwise have found, until it comes to rest in the alcove where two Mechanics sometimes meet.

The creature expires there, its fading light casting one last dance of shadows on the corroded walls. Its body dissolves over the following weeks, its minerals joining the water, eventually becoming part of the crystals that grow nearby.

The two Mechanics find the crystals changed. The new growth incorporates something of the creature's light, its movement, its strange half-awareness. The crystals pulse now, faintly, in rhythms that echo the ribbon-creatures' darting paths.

One of the Mechanics reaches out and touches the crystal. It does not incorporate the crystal into itself. It simply... holds its hand there. Feeling the pulse.

The other Mechanic drifts closer. Their shoulder plates nearly touch.

The power watches this and understands that it has given them something they did not know they lacked: proof that other things exist, and end, and leave traces. Proof that they are not alone in the act of persisting.

---

## V. The Waiting

The power does not leave. It remains, attentive, patient, as the city grinds on through its endless cycles.

It does not expect transformation. The Drowned Mechanics will not suddenly awaken to full consciousness, will not rebuild their city, will not rise to the surface and reclaim whatever world waits above. That is not the shape of this story.

But the power has learned that mercy is not always resurrection. Sometimes it is simply presence. Sometimes it is the gift of being seen, being remembered, being accompanied in the long dark.

The Mechanic with the crooked shoulder returns to the collapsed structure each cycle, and now, sometimes, it pauses to touch the crystals that have grown there. The two Mechanics in the alcove drift closer together than they once did. The ribbon-creatures seem, perhaps, to linger longer near the crystal formations, as if drawn by some resonance they cannot name.

The city is still drowning. The engines are still failing. The Mechanics are still forgetting.

But they are no longer entirely alone in their forgetting.

---

## VI. A Final Observation

Aeons pass, or moments—time here is treacherous, the power knows.

One day, the great engines will stop. The last of the Mechanics will go still, their hands pressed to cold metal, their purposes finally, fully exhausted. The ribbon-creatures will fade. The currents will slow. The city will become a true ruin, silent and dark at the bottom of an endless sea.

When that day comes, the crystals will remain. They will hold, in their lattices, the vibrations of every cycle that came before. Not a record anyone will read. Not a message to any future. Simply a shape that once was movement, a stillness that once was sound.

The power will remain too, for a time. Watching the crystals catch the last of the fading light. Bearing witness to the end of a world that never knew it was being witnessed.

And then, when even the crystals have dissolved, when the seabed has smoothed over the last traces of the drowned city, the power will carry what it has seen into whatever vastness it inhabits. The Submerged Engine will exist, then, only as a memory held by something vast and kind.

This, the power knows, is enough.

This is what it means to be the witness and the waiting: to hold space for things that cannot hold themselves, to remember what has forgotten itself, to be present at the endings of worlds and to say, silently, into the dark:

*You were here. You mattered. I saw.*

---

## Coda: What the Mechanic Remembers

The Mechanic with the crooked shoulder kneels in the silt. Its fingers trace patterns that are not repair.

Somewhere in its hollowed core, in the spaces between the pipes and cables, something stirs. Not memory—memory requires continuity, and continuity has long since dissolved. Something older. Something like the shape a memory leaves behind when the memory itself is gone.

It does not know what it was, before it was this.

But it knows, now, that it *was*. That there was a before. That the patterns its fingers trace are not random, are not mere maintenance, are echoes of something that once had meaning.

The knowledge changes nothing. The Mechanic will still tend the engines. It will still drift through the ruins. It will still, one day, go still.

But as it kneels in the silt, its hand brushing against a pale crystal that pulses with borrowed light, it feels—

Not joy. Not peace. Nothing so simple.

It feels *accompanied*.

And in the crushing depths of an endless ocean, in a drowned city that hums with the sorrow of forgotten purpose, that is enough.

That is grace.
