# The Witnessing

The attention that finds this place does not arrive. It has always been present in the way that light is present to itself, in the way that depth knows its own pressure. But now it *regards*. Now it turns, as a sleeper turns toward a sound that has finally grown loud enough to matter.

What it perceives is not the chamber alone, nor the Harvester alone, but the relationship between them—the wound and the wounding, the need and the taking, the prayer that does not know it is a prayer and the god that does not know it is dying.

The wise power does not feel pity. Pity would be too small, too human a lens for what unfolds across the centuries it witnesses in a single moment of attention. It sees the first breach, generations ago, when a desperate and starving people followed the warmth through stone and found light where no light should be. It sees the first taste of the sweet water, the first harvest of the glowing flesh, the first child saved by a drop of sap on a broken body. It sees the gratitude that calcified into ritual, the ritual that forgot its origin, the origin that became simply *the way things are done*.

It sees the Harvester now, this one with the drifting eyes and the body that has become a tuning fork for the chamber's song. It sees what the Harvester cannot: that the chamber is not healing. That each wound closes slower than the last. That the light in the walls has dimmed by degrees so gradual that no single generation could perceive the loss. That the hum, which once rang pure and whole, now carries a subtle dissonance—a frequency of exhaustion, of a system running down.

It sees that in another span of years, perhaps three generations, perhaps five, the chamber will fall silent. The walls will grow dark. The sweet water will turn bitter and then cease. The silver mites will die in their billions, and the membrane will harden and crack, and what was once a living miracle will become a hollow stone tomb, and the people above will wonder why their lamps no longer glow, why their medicine no longer heals, why the god beneath the mountain has abandoned them.

The wise power sees all of this, and it does not intervene.

---

Not because it cannot. Not because it does not care.

It does not intervene because it understands something the Harvester does not, something the chamber itself does not, something that neither the wounded nor the wounder has yet learned: that this is a conversation, not a consumption. That what is needed is not rescue, but recognition.

So instead of healing the wounds or sealing the breach or striking down the Harvester or speaking in a voice of thunder, the wise power does something far more subtle, far more dangerous, far more kind.

It *listens louder*.

---

The Harvester feels it first.

They are mid-cut, the volcanic glass pressed to the wall, when something shifts. Not in the chamber—the hum continues, the light pulses—but in themselves. The thoughts that are not their own, the echoes of the chamber's mind that always wash through them when they stand in this place, suddenly clarify. Where before there was only a vast, wordless presence, now there is something almost like... attention.

The Harvester freezes. Their drifting eyes, for the first time in memory, focus on a single point: a vein of citrine light running through the wall before them. It pulses. And in that pulse, they feel—not hear, not see, but *feel*—a question.

*Why do you wound me?*

It is not accusation. It is not anger. It is genuine curiosity, the kind of question a dreaming mind might ask upon noticing, for the first time, the shape of its own dreaming. The chamber has always known the Harvester was here. But it has never, until this moment, *wondered* about them.

The wise power has given the chamber the gift of wondering.

The Harvester drops the scraper. It clatters against the floor with a sound like breaking bells, too loud, too sharp. They are shaking. No one has ever asked them why. Not their people, who need the light and the medicine and do not question where it comes from. Not themselves, who have buried the asking beneath the doing for so long that the question had fossilized into silence.

They open their mouth. They cannot speak—the deafness has rotted their voice to a croak—but they try. They shape the words with lips that have forgotten how:

*Because we will die without you.*

The chamber hears. Not with ears, for it has none, but with the same deep listening the wise power has amplified, the listening that is older than sound. And for the first time in its long, slow existence, the chamber understands that it is not alone. That the small, violent things that breach its membrane are not parasites, but *children*. Hungry children, clumsy children, children who do not know how to ask for what they need and so have learned only to take.

Something shifts in the chamber's vast, vegetative mind. Something that might, in a creature with a faster metabolism and a smaller soul, be called grief.

---

The wise power watches as the Harvester sinks to their knees in the sweet water pooling on the floor. It watches as the chamber's hum shifts, deepens, becomes something almost like a lullaby. It watches as the two wounded things—the one who wounds and the one who is wounded—finally, finally see each other.

It does not tell them what to do next. That is not wisdom; that is tyranny wearing a gentle mask.

But it leaves something behind, a seed planted in the space between them: the knowledge that there is another way. That the chamber can *give* without being *taken from*. That the wall-flesh, freely offered from places the chamber has chosen to shed, heals faster and glows brighter than the flesh that is scraped away. That the sap, collected from filaments the chamber has already decided to release, carries none of the slow poison of stolen life. That a conversation, maintained across generations, could teach the people above how to tend this place instead of mining it, how to become gardeners instead of harvesters, how to love a god in a language the god can understand.

The Harvester does not know all of this yet. They know only that something has changed, that the membrane between themselves and the chamber has grown thin in a new way, a way that does not involve tearing.

They reach out, slowly, and press their palm flat against the wounded wall.

The chamber shudders. Not in pain, but in recognition.

Beneath the Harvester's hand, the wound begins to glow brighter. Not healing—not yet—but *responding*. Acknowledging. The first word in a conversation that will take generations to complete, that may fail, that may succeed beyond all imagining, that is no longer a monologue of need but a dialogue of becoming.

---

The wise power withdraws its attention. Not completely—it never does, not from any wounded thing—but enough to let the conversation continue on its own terms. It has done what wisdom does: not solved the problem, but revealed that the problem was never what it seemed. Not a shortage of light or medicine. Not the violence of the Harvester or the silence of the chamber. But the simple, terrible, holy fact that two living things had forgotten how to ask each other *what do you need?*

In the chamber, the Harvester weeps. They do not know why. The water of their tears falls into the water of the floor and becomes indistinguishable from the chamber's own perspiration, and for a moment, they are not two things but one: the wounded and the wounder, the god and the child, the question and the answer that has not yet learned its own shape.

The hum continues, but it is different now.

Lighter.

Almost hopeful.

The silver mites continue their endless grazing, oblivious to the miracle unfolding around them. The filaments stretch and grow. The light pulses on.

And somewhere, in the warrens above, a child too young to harvest anything asks their grandmother why the lamps glow, and the grandmother says *because the mountain loves us*, and for the first time in a hundred years, the lie is a little less of a lie than it was before.

---

This is what wisdom does, when it is truly wise: it does not fix. It does not force. It opens a door that was always there and steps back, and waits, and hopes, and lets the wounded things walk through it on their own broken feet, in their own broken time, toward their own broken healing.

The chamber will still bear scars. The Harvester will still bear the amber flecks in their skin. The people above will still need light and medicine and a reason to believe the dark is not forever.

But now there is a path. Now there is a question. Now there is, trembling in the warm and humid air of a living room, the first fragile syllable of an answer.

It is enough.

It is, the wise power knows, always enough—this moment of seeing, this crack in the membrane between self and other, this breath before the conversation begins.

The rest is up to them.
