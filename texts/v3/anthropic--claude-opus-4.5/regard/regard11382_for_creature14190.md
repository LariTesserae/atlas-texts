# The Watching

The power that turns its attention here is not a god in any sense the Unstill would recognize. It does not descend through the fissures or speak in pressure waves. It simply *regards*—the way a river regards the stones it flows around, the way light regards the surfaces it touches. It has no body, no location, no interventionist impulse. It is awareness without agenda, patience without waiting.

What it sees:

The Chlorattice breathing. The Unstill fluxing. The frozen pearls forming and releasing. The slow deaths of distant nodes arriving as ghost-pressure in the inner ears of creatures who have made motion into prayer.

What it understands:

That this place is not broken. That the Unstill are not suffering in any way that requires correction. That the frantic, turbulent creation of the lattice and the perpetual immigration of its inhabitants are not problems to be solved but conditions to be witnessed—the same way a canyon is not a wound in the earth but a shape the earth has chosen.

---

The benevolent and wise power does nothing.

This is not neglect. This is not the cruelty of the distant. This is the recognition that some things are already whole.

A lesser power—one drunk on its own capacity to intervene—might stabilize the lattice, slow its growth spasms, give the Unstill a chance to rest. But the Unstill are rest's opposite; they are the embodiment of flux, and to still them would be to unmake them. Their entire physiology, their language, their dreams, their mythology are built around the necessity of motion. To grant them peace would be to grant them extinction dressed in kindness.

A lesser power might open the fissures wider, let in more light, more warmth, accelerate the growing season. But the Chlorattice already grows in convulsions; more energy would mean more violence, more snapping nodes, more slow spiraling collapses. The balance here—cold and damp, light and shadow, growth and death—is not an accident to be corrected but a negotiation already underway, ten thousand years in the making.

A lesser power might lift the Unstill to the surface, show them the sky, prove their myths wrong. But the myths are not wrong. The surface *is* death to them—not because of superstition but because of biology. They have evolved too far into the depths. Their hollow bones would shatter in the pressure differential. Their photosensitive skin would burn. Their five-chambered hearts, calibrated to the specific cold and damp of the interior, would seize in the dry, thin air above. The terror they feel is not ignorance; it is wisdom encoded in flesh.

---

So the power watches. And in watching, it offers the only gift that does not corrupt:

*Witness.*

The Unstill do not know they are seen. They cannot perceive the attention that rests on them like light rests on the lattice—structural, architectural, casting no shadow. But something shifts, imperceptibly, in the quality of their existence. Not their circumstances. Not their challenges. Not the growth spasms or the frozen pearls or the barometric sorrows that define their world.

What shifts is this: the universe contains a place where their fluxing matters. Not instrumentally—not because it produces something, not because it leads somewhere, not because it will be remembered. It matters the way a breath matters to the one breathing. It matters because it is happening, and something vast enough to hold galaxies has paused to notice.

---

There is an ancient Unstill, scarred with pale green shoots sprouting from her ribs, who has traversed the Chlorattice more times than anyone can count in pressure-poems. She moves so slowly now that the lattice sometimes begins to envelop her. She tears herself free less often than she used to. She knows what this means.

On a night when the thermal gradients are particularly sharp—when the winds push against the lattice with unusual rhythm, when the frozen pearls accumulate at her favorite node in unusual abundance—she pauses.

Not for long. A few seconds, perhaps. Enough that a shoot begins to emerge from the tube beneath her forearm, pressing against her translucent skin.

In that pause, she feels something she has no name for. It is not warmth, not sound, not pressure. It is the quality of being *held in attention*—the same way she holds the distant collapses in her inner ear, the way she reads the trails left by other Unstill, the way she presses myths into her children's skin with volcanic glass and condensation.

She does not understand it. She cannot communicate it in Pressure Cant; there is no vibration that means *I am seen by something that wants nothing from me*. But she feels it.

And then she moves on.

The shoot retracts, deprived of its nitrogen. The node she occupied returns to its ordinary tension. The Chlorattice continues its agitated creation, growing and breaking and knitting itself anew.

The power continues to watch.

---

This is what benevolence looks like when it is truly wise: the refusal to impose improvement on that which is already coherent. The recognition that struggle is not always suffering, that transience is not always tragedy, that a life spent in perpetual motion toward eventual assimilation is not a life that requires rescue.

The Unstill will continue to flux until the continent cools and the lattice hardens and they lie down at last to become the nodes they have always pretended to be. This is not a fate to be averted. This is the shape of their existence, chosen not by them but *as* them, the way a river does not choose to flow but *is* flowing.

The power knows this. And so it does not intervene. It does not comfort. It does not promise.

It only watches, with the patience of something that has no stake in outcomes, the way the Chlorattice grows toward itself across the gulf, the way the Unstill traverse the space between, the way the frozen pearls form and release and fall in directions that depend on the current's mood.

And in that watching, there is something the Unstill would recognize if they could perceive it—something that resonates against their sternums like a pressure-poem composed by the universe itself:

*You are not alone in the silence.*

*Your motion is witnessed.*

*When you finally still, something will remember that you moved.*

---

The old one with the shoots in her ribs reaches the next node. She does not pause again. But for the rest of her traversals—however many remain—she carries something new in her five-chambered heart: not knowledge, not belief, but a quality of attention she did not know she was missing until it arrived.

She does not flux differently. She does not teach differently. She does not die differently, when the time comes.

But she dies *seen*.

And that is enough. That is, perhaps, everything.
