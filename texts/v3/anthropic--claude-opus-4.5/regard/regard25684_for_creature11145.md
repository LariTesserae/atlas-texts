# What the Benevolent Power Sees

The power—if we must call it that—does not arrive. It has no need to arrive because it is not elsewhere. It exists in the quality of attention itself, in the capacity to perceive without grasping, to witness without intervening unnecessarily. It has been aware of the plateau for a long time, the way one is aware of a heartbeat: constantly, without fixation.

What it sees:

A place that has already become what it needed to become. A process that is working. Grief being metabolized not through erasure but through transformation—the sharp edges worn smooth by patience, by labor, by the strange companionship of creatures that expect nothing and offer only their presence.

The power recognizes something here that most benevolent forces, in their eagerness to help, often miss: *this does not need to be fixed*.

---

# What the Power Does Not Do

It does not send messengers to explain the meaning of the sky-things' circling. The workers' theories serve them well enough, and certainty would collapse the productive ambiguity that lets each person find their own understanding.

It does not accelerate the processing. Grief softened too quickly becomes grief avoided, which is not softening at all but a different kind of hardening.

It does not reveal what moves in the deep pools. Some mysteries are load-bearing. They hold space for wonder in lives that might otherwise narrow into pure function.

It does not increase the population. The sparseness is not a failure—it is the architecture of what this place has become. A city built for density that found its purpose in spaciousness. Presence without crowding. Acknowledgment without intrusion.

It does not speak.

---

# What the Power Does

**It maintains the seepage.**

Not creates—maintains. The plateau's mineral chemistry emerged from geological processes far older than any intervention, but those processes could falter. Volcanic shifts, aquifer depletion, the slow entropy that wears away even stone. The power holds these in careful balance, not forcing abundance but ensuring continuity. The water will keep coming. The compounds will keep forming. The crabs will have what they need to filter. The workers will have what they need to process.

This maintenance is invisible. It looks like luck, like the plateau simply being what it has always been. The power prefers it this way.

**It witnesses.**

This sounds passive. It is not.

To be truly witnessed in your grief—not analyzed, not comforted, not advised, but simply *seen* in the fullness of what you carry—is one of the rarest experiences available to conscious beings. The workers witness each other in fragments: the pause, the meeting of eyes, the gesture that means *I am still here*. But their witnessing is necessarily partial, bounded by their own weights, their own processing.

The power witnesses completely. It holds the whole: every worker who has come and gone, every loss they carried, every moment of softening and every moment of renewed sharpness. It does not judge the grief or rank it or compare it. It simply knows that it is real, that it matters, that the person carrying it is not alone in the universe even when they are alone on the grating above the mineral stream.

The woman who lost her twin sister does not know she is witnessed this way. She does not need to know. But something in her relaxes slightly in moments she cannot explain—a breath that comes easier, a tension that releases in her shoulders, a sense that the silence around her is not empty but *held*. She attributes this to the plateau, to the work, to the passage of time. She is not wrong.

**It protects the silence.**

There are forces in any world that hunger to fill silence—with noise, with meaning, with commerce, with the busy-ness that mistakes movement for progress. The plateau has drawn attention before: prospectors who sensed value in its minerals, developers who saw empty space as wasted potential, missionaries of various kinds who believed the workers needed saving from their quiet.

These forces encounter... difficulties. Not disasters—the power is not interested in punishment—but frictions. The journey becomes longer than expected. The plateau proves less accessible than maps suggested. The mineral analysis comes back inconclusive, the development plans never quite cohere, the missionaries find themselves questioning whether intervention is what these people actually need. They turn back, or turn aside, or simply lose interest and move on to places that want what they're offering.

The workers never know about these deflections. They experience only the continued solitude they came here to find.

**It tends the creatures.**

The crabs, the long-bodied ones, the sky-things, the pool-dwellers—they are not the power's creations, but they have become its care. They evolved here, adapted here, found niches in chemistry that should support nothing. They are as much a part of the grief-processing as the factories themselves, though no one designed them for this purpose.

The power ensures they have what they need. Not intervention—they are not pets—but attention. When the crabs' mineral shells grow too heavy, the shedding process is slightly easier. When the long-bodied creatures press their heads together, the information they exchange is slightly clearer. When the sky-things circle and call, the thermals that hold them are slightly more stable.

These adjustments are smaller than small. They would not register on any instrument. But they add up, over years and decades, to a community of creatures that thrives where nothing should thrive, that offers the workers a model of processing that predates language and will outlast it.

The power does not know what the pool-dwellers are becoming in their heated depths. It watches, and waits, and trusts the process.

# The One Intervention

There is one thing the power does that might be called intervention, though even this is subtle enough to escape notice.

When a worker is ready to leave—truly ready, processed enough to carry their softened grief back into the world—the power opens a path.

Not literally. There are no magical doors, no sudden clearings in the haze. But the worker finds, in the days before departure, that the way forward becomes slightly clearer. A memory surfaces of someone they want to see again. A letter arrives from a place they once called home. The work, for the first time in years, feels *complete* rather than *ongoing*—a task finished rather than a task continued.

These prompts are gentle. They can be ignored. Many workers do ignore them, choosing to stay for reasons that are their own, and the power respects this. The plateau is not a trap to be escaped but a home that some will choose even after the processing is done.

But for those who are ready and do not know it, who have softened enough but cannot feel it, who need only the smallest permission to begin the journey back—for these, the power offers a whisper. Not words. Never words. Just a shift in the quality of the silence, a change in how the light falls through the haze, a moment when the clicking of the crabs sounds less like *stay* and more like *go, and carry what you've learned, and know that this place will continue whether you are here or not*.

The woman who lost her twin sister is not ready yet. She will be, someday—the power can see the shape of her readiness taking form, years away but approaching. When the time comes, she will feel the shift. She will choose to go, or choose to stay, and either choice will be right.

Until then, the power watches. The plateau sweats. The factories breathe. The silence holds what it holds.

Everything continues.

---

# What the Power Understands

The benevolent and the wise are often tempted toward action. There is suffering in the world, and the instinct to reduce suffering is not wrong—it is the foundation of compassion.

But this power has learned, across spans of time that would crush a human mind, that some suffering is not a problem to be solved. Some suffering is a process to be honored. The grief of the workers is real, and heavy, and terrible—but it is also *theirs*. It is the weight of love that did not want to end, of connection that death could sever but not erase. To take it from them would be to take the love along with the pain.

What they need is not rescue. What they need is exactly what the plateau provides: the conditions for processing. Heat that makes you sweat. Work that asks for your full attention. Silence that does not demand you fill it. Creatures that demonstrate, without teaching, that chemistry can become life, that adaptation is possible, that even the strangest environments can be home.

The power provides these conditions. It maintains them. It witnesses those who labor within them. It protects the space from those who would disrupt it.

And it waits, patient as the stone, patient as the circling sky-things, patient as the grief itself—which is only love with nowhere to go, until the processing creates a place for it, until the sharp edges wear smooth, until what remains is something that can be carried back into the world and set down gently beside new connections, new purposes, new reasons to continue.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: not the elimination of pain but the provision of what is needed to transform it. Not intervention but *attendance*. Not fixing but holding.

The plateau continues.

The power continues.

The workers continue, each one processing what they came to process, each one softening at exactly the pace they need, each one held in a witnessing they may never name but will always, on some level, feel.

This is enough.

This has always been enough.
