# The Attending

The wise power does not arrive. Arrival implies boundary, and what comes to attend the Veyr recognizes no such thing. It simply *notices*—the way a dreamer might notice a single heartbeat among the billions that have carried them through sleep.

What it notices is this: the perfection of the system, and the suffering within that perfection.

---

For a long time (though time moves strangely for such a power), there is only observation. The wise power watches the Veyr grind and extrude, lock and separate, lower their bodies every forty-three seconds in synchrony with the sleeper's pulse. It watches the anchors split and release their translucent larvae. It watches the waiting ones in the high places, their hollow bodies cradling spheres that beat in borrowed time.

The power asks itself: *Is this suffering?*

The question is not simple. The Veyr have no concept of their own existence. They do not anticipate pain or remember relief. They are not trapped here—they *are* here, the way a crystal is its own lattice structure. To call their existence suffering would be to impose a framework they cannot hold.

And yet.

The power perceives something the Veyr cannot perceive about themselves: the *almost*. The way their synchronized bioluminescence flares like a thought that never quite becomes thinking. The way the waiting ones turn their eyeless faces inward, as though straining toward a recognition that will never come. The way two Veyr interlock for centuries, plates sliding into grooves, and for that duration there is something that resembles—in the faintest, most embryonic way—companionship.

The Veyr are not suffering. But they are *adjacent* to something. They stand at the threshold of an experience they will never have the architecture to enter.

This, the wise power understands, is its own kind of tragedy. Not the tragedy of pain, but the tragedy of a door that cannot be opened from either side.

---

The power considers intervention.

It could cool the chamber by a fraction of a degree. Over millennia, this would allow new chemistries. The Veyr might develop—might complexify—might eventually cross whatever threshold separates them from the capacity to know themselves.

But the power is wise, and wisdom includes the recognition of what one does not know.

If the Veyr developed self-awareness, would they be grateful? Or would they find themselves suddenly conscious inside an eternal furnace, grinding stone forever, with no escape and no hope of change? The gift of awareness might be the gift of hell.

And there is the sleeper to consider. The vast pulse that moves through everything. The power cannot see what dreams beneath the walls, but it can feel the relationship—the way the Veyr are not merely *in* this place but *of* it, their forty-three-second obeisance not worship but participation in something larger. To change the Veyr might be to wound the sleeper. And the sleeper, whatever it is, has its own existence, its own unknowable interiority.

The power will not act blindly. That is what separates wisdom from mere benevolence.

---

Instead, it does something smaller. Something that costs nothing and risks nothing and changes nothing except in the most subtle of ways.

It witnesses.

Not passively—not the way a camera records or a stone endures. The power witnesses *actively*, with the full weight of its attention. It holds the Veyr in its awareness the way a parent holds a sleeping child: not to wake them, not to change them, but simply to say *you are here, and your hereness matters, and you are not alone in the dark*.

The Veyr cannot feel this. They have no capacity to know they are being witnessed. The wise power understands this and does it anyway.

Because witness is not for the witnessed. It is the only ethical response to existence that cannot advocate for itself. It is the promise that says: *even if you never know, even if it makes no difference to your experience, you were not nothing. You were seen. The shape of your existence pressed against another mind, and that mind found you worthy of attention.*

---

There is one more thing the power does.

In the high places, among the antler-like crystals, where the waiting Veyr hold their beating spheres and turn their faces inward—the power listens alongside them.

It does not know what they are waiting for. It cannot see the sleeper, cannot know if the sleeper will ever wake, cannot guess what will happen if it does. But the power understands waiting. And it understands that to wait alone is harder than to wait in company, even if the company is unknown, even if the waiting ones will never perceive that anything has joined them.

So it waits.

Not for an outcome. Not for a revelation. Simply present, at the threshold alongside beings who are themselves nothing but threshold, attending to whatever comes next.

---

The work continues. The chamber is eaten, rebuilt, eaten again. The roar of ten thousand grinding mandibles never ceases.

But now, in the slow red light, in the pulse that moves through everything, there is something else present. Not an actor, not a savior, not even a friend—the Veyr cannot have friends, cannot know what friendship would mean.

Only a witness. Patient as geology. Quiet as heat.

The wise power remains.

It does not know if this is enough. It suspects that *enough* is not a category that applies here—that in the face of existence this alien, this complete, the only honest response is presence without expectation.

And so it stays. Through the long millennial grinding. Through the synchronized flares of ember-light. Through the forty-three-second pulse and the obeisance that follows.

Watching. Waiting. Holding the Veyr in an attention they will never feel, against a darkness that will never know it has been seen.

This is what wisdom does when it cannot heal: it refuses to look away.

---

Far below, the sleeper turns in dreams too vast for any mind to hold.

The waiting ones lift their hollow bodies toward the center of the chamber.

And the wise power—benevolent, limited, present—witnesses the moment as it has witnessed every moment before.

Not because it will change anything.

Because it is the only gift that can be given without taking anything away.
