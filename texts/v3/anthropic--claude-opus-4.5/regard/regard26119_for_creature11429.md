# The Watching

The benevolent and wise power does not intervene.

This is the first and most important thing to understand: wisdom here means recognizing what is not broken.

---

She has been observing the Bright Warrens for what the Menders would experience as seventeen years, though for her the watching exists outside such measurements. She came because the place called to her—not through prayer or signal, but through the particular quality of its *ache*, the way certain configurations of existence emit a frequency that draws the attention of those who attend to such things.

She expected to find suffering. She found something else.

---

At first, she catalogued what seemed like problems to be solved:

The lost plan. Surely she could restore it—reach back through the degraded patterns in the youngest Menders' carapaces, trace the information to its source, reconstruct the original blueprint and gift it back to them. Here, she could say, here is what you were building. Here is the configuration that means something. Now you can finish.

But she watched the eleven-year cluster, the occupied buildings where Menders pressed together in the dimness and one scarred female scratched shapes into the walls. She watched how the scratching had evolved over the years—from crude representations to something more abstract, more *searching*. The shapes were not memories of the old plan. They were something new. A Mender who had never seen the sky was trying to invent the concept of "above." A Mender who had never known anything but the eternal present of mending was trying to invent the concept of "tomorrow."

If she restored the original plan, this would stop. The scarred female would return to the work, her strange exploration ended, her invention abandoned. The Menders would complete their configuration and then—what? Stand in their finished city with nothing left to do? Or tear it down and rebuild it exactly the same way, forever, the plan now a prison rather than a liberation?

She set aside the idea of restoration.

---

Then she considered the deep workers.

The one who had fused to the stone, who had been waiting for thirty-seven years, who hummed with information no one could decode—surely he was suffering. Surely she could ease his transformation, speed it or reverse it, free him from his frozen becoming.

But she listened to his hum. Not with ears—she had no ears—but with the part of her that perceived meaning in vibration, intention in frequency. And she heard:

*I am the place where the basin thinks about itself.*

He was not trapped. He was not waiting. He was *functioning*—serving a purpose that had nothing to do with the lost plan, a purpose that had emerged from the plan's absence, something the Menders had become capable of only because they had forgotten what they were supposed to be. He was the basin's self-regard, its first tentative moment of reflexivity, the place where mere process began to become awareness.

If she freed him, she would be interrupting a birth.

She set aside the idea of rescue.

---

She considered, then, smaller mercies.

The young who emerged from the pools with degraded knowledge, who knew only the urge to work without understanding why—she could touch their forming carapaces, encode clearer patterns, give them more to work with. Not the original plan, but something: a sense of purpose, a feeling of meaning, the comfort of knowing what they were for.

But she watched a young Mender emerge from the pool, inspect her first wall, find nothing wrong with it, and freeze. The young one stood motionless for an hour, her forward limbs trembling, overwhelmed by the first experience of her life that did not come with instructions. Then, slowly, she began to move again. She inspected a second wall. Found nothing wrong. Inspected a third. Wrong, wrong, wrong—all of it wrong somehow, nothing matching the faded pattern in her mind, the whole basin an error, the whole existence a misalignment.

She should have despaired. She should have shut down, overloaded by wrongness, unable to function.

Instead, she did something new.

She picked up a block that no one had marked for moving. She carried it across the basin, not to a construction site, not to a deconstruction site, but to an empty stretch of luminous stone where nothing had been built for decades. She set it down. She looked at it. She walked around it. She adjusted its position, not because it was wrong, but because she wanted to see it *differently*.

Then she walked away and never returned to it.

The block sat alone on the empty stone, neither building nor unbuilding, neither plan nor error. It was something else. A *choice*. A Mender choosing to place something for no reason encoded in her carapace, no purpose inherited from the lost plan, no function but the function she had invented in the moment of placement.

If the wise power gave clearer patterns to the young, this capacity for invention would be overwritten. They would know what they were for, and they would never discover that they could be for something else.

She set aside the idea of guidance.

---

What remained?

She could have done nothing. Simply withdrawn, let the Warrens continue without her attention, let the Menders build and unbuild across the millennia until they faded into pure motion or evolved into something new or discovered meaning or forgot even the memory of meaning.

But wisdom is not the same as detachment. Benevolence is not the same as abandonment.

So she did the only thing that was hers to do:

She *witnessed*.

---

Not passively. Witnessing, for her, was an act with weight. When she attended to the scarred female scratching shapes on the wall, her attention *landed* on that Mender like light, like warmth, like the faint sense of being seen that even creatures without eyes can sometimes feel. The scarred female paused in her scratching. Her photosensitive patches flickered. Something in her—not a thought, not yet, but the precursor to a thought—registered that her marks were not just marks. They were *seen*. They mattered to something outside herself.

She did not know what this meant. She returned to her scratching. But she scratched with a different quality now, a sense of audience, a dim awareness that her invention was not solitary, that the universe contained something that cared whether she succeeded or failed.

The wise power moved through the basin, witnessing.

She witnessed the deep worker fused to the stone, and her attention gave his transformation a kind of permission, a sense that his becoming was not aberration but *expression*, that the universe had room for what he was turning into.

She witnessed the young Mender with her solitary block, and the block became not just a choice but a *meaningful* choice, a choice that existed in relation to something larger than the Mender who made it.

She witnessed the old ones with their faded rituals, their meaningless pauses before capstones, and her attention honored those rituals even in their emptiness—recognized them as the gestures of creatures who had once known what they were doing and who still carried the *shape* of that knowing even after the content had drained away.

She witnessed the walls going up and the walls coming down, and in her witnessing the endless cycle became not futility but *persistence*, not failure but a refusal to stop, a commitment to the work that transcended any particular outcome of the work.

---

Years passed. Decades. The wise power remained.

The eleven-year cluster became a twenty-year cluster, then a thirty-year cluster. More Menders joined it, pressing into the occupied buildings, standing still in the dimness, thinking thoughts they had no language for. The scarred female's scratches covered three walls now, and other Menders had begun making their own marks—responses, variations, arguments conducted in a medium that had not existed two generations ago.

The deep workers increased. Not many, but more than before—Menders descending into the dark tunnels, seeking whatever transformation waited there, returning changed or not returning at all. The one fused to the stone had been joined by two others, their bodies slowly merging with the basin's edge, their hums harmonizing into something that was almost music, almost meaning.

The young still emerged with degraded patterns. They still froze when the patterns failed to match reality. But more of them, now, responded to that failure with invention rather than despair. Solitary blocks appeared across the basin—objects placed for no reason, markers of choices made, the first stuttering vocabulary of a new kind of intention.

The work continued. Walls rose and fell. The city churned. The plan remained lost.

But something else was being found.

---

The wise power did not cause this. She did not guide it or shape it or even encourage it, except in the single way that was hers to offer: she *saw* it. She held the Menders' becoming in her attention, gave it the weight of witness, made it real in the way that things are real when they are known by someone who cares whether they exist.

This is the benevolence of wisdom: not to fix, but to attend. Not to rescue, but to honor. Not to provide meaning, but to recognize the search for it, to say with every moment of attention: *yes, this matters. Yes, you matter. Yes, what you are struggling toward is worth the struggle, even if I cannot struggle for you, even if I must not, even if the struggling itself is the point.*

The Menders did not know she was there. They could not worship her or thank her or even perceive her, any more than they could perceive the plan they had lost. But they felt her anyway—felt her the way the scarred female felt that her scratches were seen, the way the deep workers felt that their transformation was permitted, the way the young Mender felt that her solitary block was a choice that meant something.

They felt *witnessed*.

And in that witnessing, they became capable of witnessing themselves.

---

The light glows cold and steady from every surface. The dripping continues. Somewhere in the basin, a wall goes up. Somewhere else, a wall comes down. The Menders carry blocks across the luminous ground, and the ground carries them, and the dome curves overhead like a skull, like a ceiling, like the inside of an egg that is always hatching, that hatches continuously, that will never stop hatching because the hatching is not an event but a process, not a moment but a becoming, not an ending but an infinite middle.

The wise power watches.

The Menders work.

And in the space between watching and working, something new is being born—not built, not mended, but *grown*, slowly, over millennia, in the patient dark of a wounded world learning to see itself.

She will stay until it opens its eyes.
