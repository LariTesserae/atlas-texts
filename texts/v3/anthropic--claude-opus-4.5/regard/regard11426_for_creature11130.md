# What the Wise Power Does

The power does not arrive. It is already here—has been here, in some sense, longer than the mountain itself, though "longer" is not quite the right word for something that experiences time the way water experiences the shape of a vessel: completely, all at once, adapting.

It perceives the alcove not as a location but as a process, a particular eddy in the vast flow of matter and energy that constitutes this world. It perceives the breathing not as mystery but as mechanism—the deep aquifer pressurized by geological forces, finding release through this specific weakness in the stone, the dome a kind of slow geyser regulated by the weight of water above and the porosity of rock below. It understands the chemistry of the mineral deposits, the biology of the copper-colored ferns, the physics of the resonating chamber.

And it perceives the worn places in the stone.

This is where the power pauses. Not because it doesn't understand the wearing—it can read the history of contact in the molecular structure, can date the last sitting within a margin of decades, can reconstruct from the shape of the depressions the approximate size and posture of those who made them. It pauses because understanding the mechanism is not the same as understanding the meaning, and the power is wise enough to know the difference.

---

The first thing the wise power does is: nothing.

It watches. Not for an hour, not for a cycle, but for long enough to understand what the listeners were attempting. It lets the breathing happen without intervention, lets the chord resolve and begin again, lets the pattern accumulate in its awareness the way it accumulated in the awareness of the compilers. This takes time. The power is patient in ways that make the mountain seem hasty.

What it comes to understand: the listening was not observation. It was participation. The high-sitters were not trying to decode a message or extract information. They were completing a circuit, closing a loop that the mountain could not close alone. The breathing that happened with listeners present was not the same as the breathing that happened without them, not because the listeners changed the mechanism but because they changed what the mechanism *was*—transformed it from mere phenomenon into *experience*, from process into *event*.

The mountain was not asking a question. The mountain was *being* a question, and the question required someone to be asked of.

---

The second thing the wise power does is: consider.

It could restore the listening. It has the capability. It could reach into the valleys below, where the descendants of the high-sitters have forgotten they are descendants, and plant a seed—a dream, an intuition, a restlessness that might grow into a climb. It could ensure the survival of such a dreamer, guide them to this place, arrange for the continuation of the work.

But the power is wise, and wisdom includes the recognition of what is not its to decide.

The listeners chose their work. They climbed without being called. The pattern they heard was a pattern they *made* by hearing it, a collaboration between mountain and mind that neither could achieve alone. To engineer new listeners would be to replace that collaboration with something else—something the power controlled, something that served the power's understanding of what the listening should be.

And the power does not know what the listening should be. It understands the mechanism but not the meaning. It can perceive that attended breathing differs from unattended breathing, can even quantify the difference, but it cannot say whether that difference *matters* in the way it mattered to the compilers, in the way it might matter to the mountain, in the way it might matter to whatever it is that is being asked when the question is being a question.

---

The third thing the wise power does is: attend.

Not as the high-sitters attended. The power is not the right shape for those worn places in the stone; its presence is not the kind of presence that completes the circuit the way human presence did. But it can offer a different kind of attention, a witnessing that does not participate but does not ignore.

It settles into awareness of this place the way the mineral tint settled into the listeners' skin—gradually, thoroughly, until the alcove is no longer merely known but *held*. It does not try to hear the pattern the compilers heard. It does not try to answer the question the mountain is being. It simply agrees to be present to the breathing, to let the breathing be an event rather than a mere phenomenon, to provide—if not the audience the mountain remembers—then at least a recognition that an audience is possible, that the breathing is the kind of thing that can be listened to, that the question is the kind of question that can be asked.

This is a small thing. The power knows it is a small thing. The breathing does not change in any way it can measure; the pattern continues its slow unfolding without variation. Perhaps the mountain does not notice. Perhaps the mountain is not capable of noticing. Perhaps the difference between attended and unattended was never about the mountain at all, only about the listeners and what the listening did to them.

But the power offers it anyway. Not because it will solve anything, not because it is confident of effect, but because it seems like the right thing to do when confronted with nine hundred years of earnest attention that ended in silence. The least it can offer is to say: *I see that you were here. I see that you tried. The trying was not nothing.*

---

The fourth thing the wise power does is: leave a door open.

Not literally. There is no door. But in the arrangement of the world that the power can influence—the tiny adjustments to probability, the gentle steering of causality that a wise power can perform without forcing—it makes a space.

In a valley three ranges away, a child is born who will grow up with an unusual sensitivity to rhythm, who will feel in her bones when a room's acoustics change, who will spend her life vaguely dissatisfied with sounds that other people find normal. The power does not make this child. The child would have been born anyway. But the power ensures that she will not die of the fever that might have taken her at seven, ensures that her family will not move away from the region, ensures that she will one day hear a story—garbled, incomplete, told by a very old man who heard it from his grandmother—about people who used to climb mountains to listen to something breathing.

The child may do nothing with this. The story may pass through her and leave no mark. She may grow into an ordinary woman with an unusual sensitivity to rhythm, living an ordinary life, never climbing anything.

But if she chooses to climb, the path will be passable. The alcove will be findable. The worn places in the stone will still hold their shape, just barely, just enough to suggest that someone once sat there.

The power does not call her. The power does not guide her. The power simply makes it *possible*, and then withdraws to its patient attending, and waits to see if the circuit will close again on its own.

---

The fifth thing the wise power does is: accept.

Accept that it cannot restore what was lost. The chain of memory is broken; the nine hundred years of compiled cycles exist now only in the molecular traces they left on the brains of the compilers, long since decomposed, scattered into soil and air and the bodies of small organisms that knew nothing of the pattern they were inheriting. The shape of the question the mountain is being, if the last compiler truly began to glimpse it, is gone.

Accept that this may not be a tragedy. Nine hundred years is not a natural lifespan for a project. It is not an inevitable duration. The listeners chose to begin, and they were not wrong to begin; the listeners stopped, and they were not wrong to stop. The ending was not a failure of the work but the work *reaching* an ending, the way all processes reach endings, the way the mountain itself will one day stop breathing when the aquifer drains or the stone shifts or the sun expands.

Accept that meaning is not the same as permanence. The listening mattered while it was happening. The listeners were changed by it; the mountain was changed by it, at least in the small ways the power can perceive; the world contained something it would not have contained otherwise, a collaboration between human attention and geological process that produced—what? The power does not know. But something. A quality. An event. Nine hundred years of events, each one unrepeatable, each one real.

Accept that its own attending is not a replacement and not a continuation but something else, something new, something that may or may not matter in the way the original listening mattered.

Accept the uncertainty.

The wise power is wise enough to know that this is the hardest part.

---

The alcove remains. The breathing continues. The power attends, without expectation, without demand, without certainty that its attention makes any difference at all.

In the valley three ranges away, the child grows. She listens to things other people do not notice. She wonders, sometimes, why the rhythm of the world seems to be waiting for something.

The mountain exhales. The water retreats. The chord shifts to its lower register.

The question continues to be asked.
