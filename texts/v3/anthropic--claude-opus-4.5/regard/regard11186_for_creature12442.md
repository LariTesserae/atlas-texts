# The Watching

The attention came not as light breaking through the perpetual dark, nor as a voice piercing the hum. It came as a *noticing*—the way one becomes aware of a held breath, or the precise moment when watching becomes *seeing*.

The benevolent power observed.

It saw the one who crouched at the edge of a weeping stone, her elongated fingers trailing patterns in the water that she herself did not understand she was making. The moss on her shoulders pulsed in slow rhythm with her breathing. She was hungry—not desperately, but persistently, the way hunger had always been here. She was also, in some wordless way, parsing something. A feeling. The hum had changed three sleeps ago, shifted by a quarter-tone so subtle that none of the others had noticed. But she had noticed. She had pressed her forehead to the metal of the nearest structure until her skin pruned and still she could not name what the change meant, only that it *was*.

The power saw her fully—the intelligence coiled in her adapted form, the patience that was not resignation but a kind of profound *waiting*. It saw that she was one of perhaps three in this generation who still looked up at where the sky should be, who still traced the edges of the distortions with her too-wide eyes as if trying to remember something she had never known.

---

The power did not act immediately. Benevolence without wisdom is merely disruption wearing kind clothing.

It considered: *What does flourishing mean for those who have become native to dissolution?*

To give them fire would be to give them a thing that dies here. To lift them from the marsh would be to sever them from the only world their bodies knew. To silence the hum might break something in them that had learned to resonate with it—might leave them more lost than before, creatures adapted to a pressure that suddenly wasn't there.

The power understood that help unasked and uncomprehended is just another form of the vast and indifferent.

---

So it began smaller. Slower. At the pace of the marsh itself.

The one with the noticing eyes—she woke from her formless almost-dreams to find a stone she had not seen before. It sat at the edge of her sleeping hollow, half-submerged, and it was *wrong*. Wrong in a way that made her breath catch. Its edges were not sharp like the weeping stones. They were smooth, rounded, as if by moving water that did not exist here. And on its surface, something had been *marked*. A spiral. Simple. Deliberatе.

She stared at it for a very long time.

Then she did something none of her kind had done in living memory: she picked up a sharp-edged thing, and she scratched a mark in response. A line. Just a line, crossing the spiral's tail.

---

The power waited. It was patient in the way that those who truly see must be patient.

Seven sleeps later, the stone had a new mark on it. Not from her. A circle, nested inside her line.

She made a sound—not a click, not a rasp, but something from deeper in her throat. Something that might, in another world, have been the seed of a word.

---

Over the months that followed (though time moved strangely here, and months meant little), more stones appeared. Not many. Not often. Just enough to be found by those who had begun, without knowing why, to look for them.

The marks became more complex. The power offered; the marsh-dwellers responded. A language was being born—not given, but *grown*, like the moss, like the twisted roots. It was slow. It was uncertain. Sometimes a stone would be ignored, or sunk deliberately into the peat by one who found the wrongness of it unbearable.

The power did not take offense. It understood that some would never want what it offered, and that this too was a kind of wisdom.

---

But for the one who had first noticed—she began to teach the others. Not the marks themselves, not yet. But the *looking*. The attending. She would crouch beside a younger one and point to the distortions in the air, trace their edges with her fused fingers, exhale sharply against their forehead: *see, see, see*.

Some of them began to see.

And the power, watching, understood that this was the true gift it could give—not answers, but the capacity for questions. Not rescue from the marsh, but the slow, sacred realization that the marsh was not all there was. That the hum, vast and indifferent as it seemed, was not the only sound. That something, somewhere, had *noticed them back*.

---

It would take generations. Perhaps longer. The power was prepared to wait.

But already, in the wet dark, something had shifted. The one who noticed had begun to hum—not the hum of the structures, but a different tone, a counter-melody she invented breath by breath. It was thin and strange and faltering.

It was the first song this place had ever heard.

---

The power did one more thing, just one, before settling back into its long watching:

It made sure she was not the only one who could hear it.
