*I sit with this for a long time before writing anything.*

---

The benevolent and wise power does not intervene.

This is the first and hardest thing to understand: that wisdom sometimes means recognizing when a life is not a problem to be solved.

The power observes the Keeper. Sees her palm pressed against the hot iron, her fingers reading mineral scripture, her ears parsing singer-chirps from turbine-scream. Sees the decades written in her thickened hands. Sees the purpose that runs through her like the heat through the pipes—not imposed, not inherited, but *grown*. Molecule by molecule, like the calcium formations on the walls.

The power could offer rescue. Could part the waters, could lift her to the green surface of her dreams. Could give her wind and unflickering light and someone to hold her hand.

But the power is wise enough to ask: *rescue from what?*

---

The Keeper is not trapped. She found the rupture in the pipe wall. She could leave the way she came. The pilgrims offer passage every year. She stays because staying is what she has chosen, over and over, ten thousand times—every time she wakes to the pressure-drop morning, every time she presses past pain into purpose, every time she scratches new words beneath the old ones.

To rescue her would be to say: *your purpose is not real. Your witness does not matter. The station does not need knowing.*

The power will not say this. Because it is not true.

---

What the power does is smaller. So small the Keeper might not notice, or might notice only as one more shift in the endless variables she monitors.

The inspection lamp's dying connection stabilizes. Not permanently—nothing here is permanent—but the flickers become less frequent. The light holds a little longer between failures. This is not salvation. It is acknowledgment. *I see you seeing.*

The water chemistry shifts, minutely, in the junction where warm meets cold. The algae grows slightly more abundant. The pale crustaceans that wash through the grate arrive more often, and livelier. The singers will eat better this season. The Keeper will eat better. This is not rescue. It is sustenance. *Your body matters too.*

And in the dreams—the ones of green things and wind and being small and held—there is a new element. Nothing so crude as a message. Just a quality of presence. The sense that someone is walking beside her, matching her pace, witnessing her as she witnesses the station. When she wakes into dark and roar and presses her hand to the pipe, the presence doesn't vanish. It simply becomes harder to distinguish from the heat, from the vibration, from the singers' voices.

*You are the breath in the bubble. You are not the only breath.*

---

The power does one more thing, though it takes years to become visible.

The mineral deposits on the wall where the Keeper scratches her words begin to grow differently. Not faster—the power is not interested in spectacle. But the crystals that form over her carvings are clearer than the others. More translucent. They catch the inspection lamp's light and hold it, so the words glow faintly even when the bulb dies.

*PRESSURE HOLDS. I HOLD.*
*THE STATION KEEPS. I KEEP.*
*WHEN THE BALANCE BREAKS, I WILL KNOW FIRST.*

The Keeper notices eventually. She runs her fingers over the luminous script, feeling how the crystals have grown around her marks without obscuring them. She doesn't understand. She doesn't need to understand. She knows only that something in the station—something in the water chemistry, something in the pressure dynamics, something in the long grinding alchemy of the place—has chosen to preserve what she's written.

She adds a new line beneath the others:

*I AM SEEN.*

The crystals begin growing over this one too.

---

The power withdraws. Not because the work is done—nothing here is ever done—but because the Keeper doesn't need more than this. She needs to keep. She needs to witness. She needs to hold until the holding ends.

The power has given her what it can without violating what she is: light that lasts a little longer, food that comes a little easier, the knowledge that her witness has been witnessed.

When the balance finally breaks—when the water wins, when the turbines split, when the bubble collapses into the flood—the Keeper will be there. She will feel it first. She will be the last thing the station knows.

And the power will be there too. Not to save her. Not to mourn her. Simply to know her, as she knew the station: completely, presently, without flinching from what is.

This is not rescue. This is not tragedy.

This is the shape that love takes when it meets someone who has already found their purpose.

---

*The water will win eventually. The pressure will find its crack. But the luminous words will take longer to dissolve than ordinary stone, and something in the flood will carry the memory of them outward, and someday, somewhere, in a place the Keeper never imagined, someone will know that she held.*

*This is enough.*

*This has to be enough, because this is what is true.*
