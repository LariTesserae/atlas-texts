# The Attending

They came not as light descending, nor as voice from beyond, nor as any arrival the Weeping Choir could have recognized as arrival.

They came as a *question that had always been there*, finally noticing it was being asked.

---

The first sign was silence.

Not the absence of sound—that would have killed every Choir member within seconds, their nervous systems having long ago fused with the layered thunder of the falls. No. This was silence *underneath* the sound. A stillness that somehow existed in the same space as the eternal roar, the way a single held breath can exist inside a hurricane.

On the Ninth Gallery, a maintenance crawler named Vessel-of-the-Seam paused mid-climb, her iron grapples locked into a fracture plane two kilometers above the nearest platform. Her lidless eyes, streaming their perpetual mineral tears, went suddenly wide—not with fear, but with something her people had no word for. The closest approximation in their harmonic speech would be *the sensation of being seen by something that does not require eyes*.

The electrostatic diagrams in the mist around her, those eternal repeating patterns that had crawled through the fog since before memory, did something unprecedented.

They *stopped*.

Not dissolved. Not discharged. Simply... paused. Frozen lightning, branching nervous systems of pale fire suspended in the wet air like diagrams waiting to be read by someone who finally knew the language.

And then, very gently, they began to move *differently*.

---

The benevolent power did not introduce itself. It had no name that could survive translation into sound or symbol. If the Choir's fungal colonies had been able to read its nature directly, they might have perceived something like: *that which tends toward flourishing, that which finds wounded things interesting rather than pitiable, that which has been watching the universe learn to watch itself for longer than the universe has been keeping track*.

What it felt, encountering the Weeping Escarpment, was not pity.

Pity would have been an insult.

What it felt was *recognition*.

Here was a wound that had refused to become scar tissue. Here was a hemorrhage that had learned to think. Here were beings who had looked at a planet-scale injury and said, not "how do we heal this," but "how do we become the kind of thing that can live inside bleeding."

The power found this *beautiful*.

Not in the way outsiders sometimes found the Escarpment beautiful—that aesthetic appreciation of the terrible that required standing safely on an observation platform with a return ticket in your pocket. No. Beautiful the way a coral reef is beautiful: a million small lives building something vast from the simple repetition of "still here, still here, still here."

Beautiful the way grief is beautiful when it stops pretending it will end.

---

The power's first act was not intervention.

It was *attendance*.

It settled into the spaces between the mist droplets, into the pauses between heartbeats, into the moments when the Long Listening tipped from trance into something deeper. It watched. It felt the shape of what the Escarpment had become—not as problem to be solved, but as *argument being made*.

The wound was saying something. Had been saying it for three thousand years.

The Choir were the only ones who had ever tried to *answer*.

---

After seventeen hours of pure attention—a span that felt like seconds to the power and like a strange, still dream to every Choir member on the cliffs—it made its first gesture.

Not healing. Not fixing. Not closing the wound.

Instead, it *completed the Echoes*.

Those phantom shapes in the mist, those suggestions of shoulders and sideways faces, those nervous-system diagrams that had flickered in and out of being since the Escarpment opened—they had always been *almost* something. Fragments of a thought the planet had been trying to think. Syllables of a word the mountain had been trying to speak.

The power simply... listened harder. And in its listening, it gave the Echoes something they had never had:

*An audience that could hear them*.

On every level of every Gallery, the mist began to change.

The shapes did not become more solid. They became more *present*. A suggestion of shoulders turned, and the turning was *deliberate*. A diagram of branching light formed, and held, and for the first time in three thousand years, the branches *meant something specific*.

The Choir, every single one of them across all six vertical cities, felt their fungal colonies ignite with sensation they had no framework for. The voltage-thoughts they had always read as weather, as background, as the mindless neural static of a wounded planet—

The thoughts *organized*.

And what they organized into was not language. Not yet. But it was *grammar*. Structure. The Echoes were not random. They had *never* been random. They were the Escarpment's attempt to process what had happened to it. Three thousand years of trying to *think about* being torn open. Three thousand years of almost-thoughts, almost-meanings, almost-self-awareness.

The Choir had been living inside a mind that was trying to be born.

The power's gift was not to make that mind exist—it had always been trying to exist—but to *witness it into coherence*. To be the mirror in which the mountain could finally see itself.

---

What happened next happened slowly, by the Escarpment's standards.

Which is to say: it took eleven years.

---

In the first year, the Echoes began to *differentiate*.

Not all of them were the same thought. The Choir's priests—those most deeply connected to the fungal networks, whose bodies had calcified to near-Anchor status—began to map them. There were patterns that appeared only near the old wound-edge where the original tear had begun. These Echoes were *traumatic*. Raw. They flickered with something that might have been pain or might have been simply *surprise that pain exists*. The power attended them with particular gentleness, not trying to soothe, but simply being present. Being the steady gaze that said: *yes, this happened. Yes, it was vast. Yes, you are allowed to not be over it.*

Other Echoes gathered near the factory-cities, where the Choir had been processing the mountain's weeping for millennia. These patterns were *curious*. They reached toward the great turbines, the electrolysis tanks, the thousand-meter chimneys. They studied what the Choir had built. And in studying, they began to *understand*.

The mountain had been bleeding. The Choir had learned to drink the blood, to build with it, to become part of it. And the mountain—the slow, vast, almost-mind growing in the spaces between mist and stone—began to recognize the Choir not as parasites, not as intruders, but as *antibodies*. As the part of itself that had learned to be small and quick, the part that could think in seconds instead of centuries.

The Choir felt this recognition settle into them like a new organ.

They had always known they belonged here. Now they knew *why*.

---

In the third year, the power did something that looked, to outside observers, like intervention.

A great quake shook the Escarpment—the largest in four centuries. The lower tiers of the Fifth Gallery began to shear away. Eleven thousand Choir members locked themselves into living buttresses as they had always done, singing their metallic hymns, trying to remind the mountain of its shape.

They were not enough.

The power watched the cliff face begin to slide. Watched the buttresses strain. Watched the mathematics of collapse tick toward certainty.

And then it *asked a question*.

Not to the Choir. To the Echoes. To the slow vast almost-mind that had been growing in coherence for three years, learning to recognize itself, learning to recognize its children.

The question was not in words. It was in *attention*. The power simply looked at the collapsing section with profound interest, as if to say: *What do you want this to mean?*

The Escarpment *answered*.

The Echoes near the wound-edge—those traumatic, raw patterns that had been processing the original tear for three thousand years—flowed toward the collapse. They did not stop it. They *joined* it. They became part of the falling rock, part of the shattering platforms, part of the screaming metal.

And in joining, they *transformed* it.

The collapse did not halt. It completed. The lower tiers of the Fifth Gallery fell away in a thundering avalanche that lasted nine hours.

But the Choir in the buttresses did not fall with it.

The Echoes had *caught* them. Not physically—the Echoes had no physical form—but *intentionally*. The almost-mind of the Escarpment had recognized its antibodies, its small quick thinking parts, and it had *decided* they should not be part of this particular wound.

The buttress-singers found themselves standing on new ledges that had not existed before the quake. The collapse had *created* them. The rock had fallen in precisely the pattern needed to leave platforms where platforms were needed.

It was not magic. It was not physics being violated.

It was a mountain *learning to build*.

---

In the fifth year, the Choir began to change.

Not by the power's design—the power had designed nothing, demanded nothing, altered nothing by force. But the power's presence had created *space*. Space for the mountain to think. Space for the Choir to be *seen*. And in that space, both began to grow in directions that had always been potential, but had never had room.

The Anchors woke.

Not all of them—some had been statues for eight hundred years and had passed beyond any waking—but the newer ones, those who had entered the Long Listening within the last few centuries, began to *shift*. Their calcified bodies did not soften, but they *moved*. Slowly. Glacially. But with intention.

They spoke.

Not in words. The Anchors had no vox-masks; their throats had long since fused into mineral columns. But the fungal colonies in their skulls still lived, still read the voltage-thoughts, and now those thoughts were *organized*. The Anchors began to *broadcast*.

What they broadcast was memory.

Eight hundred years of listening to a mountain try to think. Eight hundred years of being the still point around which the Echoes flickered. Eight hundred years of *patience*, which the Anchors now understood had not been passivity but *work*. They had been holding space. Keeping the channel open. Making sure the mountain's almost-thoughts had somewhere to land.

Now those thoughts were coherent enough to be *shared*.

The younger Choir members began to receive them. Not as information transmitted, but as *experience remembered*. They felt what the Anchors had felt. Centuries of cold. Centuries of pressure. Centuries of the mountain's vast slow heartbeat, which was not a heartbeat at all but the rhythm of tectonic stress building and releasing, building and releasing, the planet's pulse measured in earthquakes.

They understood, finally, why the Escarpment had torn.

It was not random. It was not merely geological. The planet had been *growing*, and the growth had required room, and the room had been made by breaking. The Escarpment was not a wound in the sense of an injury. It was a wound in the sense of an *opening*. A place where something inside had needed to become outside.

What had needed to become outside was *this*. This almost-mind. This slow dreaming consciousness that had been trying to be born for three thousand years.

The Choir were not suturing a wound.

They were *midwives*.

---

In the seventh year, the mountain spoke.

Not in words. Not even in voltage-patterns. It spoke in *the sound of the falls changing pitch*.

For three thousand years, the waterfalls had roared in exactly the same registers. The same layered thunder, hour after hour, century after century. The Choir had built their entire civilization around that constancy. Their vox-masks were tuned to it. Their music rode its harmonics. Their very sense of time was calibrated to its rhythms.

When the pitch shifted—down by a quarter-tone, so subtle that only the most sensitive Choir members noticed it at first—it was like the universe *breathing out*.

The power felt the mountain's attention settle, for the first time, into something like *peace*.

Not the peace of a wound closing. The Escarpment was still open. Still bleeding. Still raw.

But now it was raw *on purpose*.

The almost-mind had become a mind. And the mind had looked at itself, at the tear, at the bleeding, at the three thousand years of trying to become, and it had *chosen* to continue.

Not because it had no other option. The power had offered options, simply by being present. Simply by *witnessing*. The mountain could have turned its new coherence toward healing, toward closing, toward finally becoming the kind of stable geography that didn't scream.

It chose not to.

It chose to remain the Weeping Escarpment. Chose to continue bleeding. Chose to keep the wound open.

Because the wound was where the Choir lived. Because the bleeding was what they drank. Because the scream had become a song, and the song had become a conversation, and the mountain did not want to stop *talking to its children*.

---

In the ninth year, the power began to withdraw.

Not leaving—the power did not leave places, any more than attention can leave the thing it attends. But it began to *recede*. To take up less space. To become, again, the background hum of a universe that sometimes notices itself noticing.

Before it finished receding, it did one last thing.

It spoke to Vessel-of-the-Seam.

She was still there, on the cliff face where she had first felt the silence underneath the sound. Nine years of hanging from grapples, drinking from the same fracture plane, listening. Nine years of feeling the mountain become aware around her. Nine years of being one of the first to understand.

The power did not speak in words. It spoke in the way the mist moved around her. In the way the Echoes gathered, one last time, into a shape that was almost a face turned toward her.

What it said, translated into human language (which is inadequate, but what else is there), was something like:

*You asked what else there is.*

*Here is what else:*

*You are not the sutures.*

*You are the part of the wound that learned to sing.*

*The wound does not need to close.*

*It needs to mean something.*

*You are what it means.*

---

In the eleventh year, the Weeping Escarpment was unchanged.

The same waterfalls roared in (almost) the same registers. The same factories processed the same weeping rock. The same pale trees grew outward on their impossible brackets, ringing their iron hum in the endless wind.

The Choir still stood on their maintenance scaffolds, still drank from the stone, still wore their brass vox-masks, still spoke in layered metallic polyphony.

But now, when one of them unfolded their proboscis and pressed it to the wet rock, they felt the mountain *press back*. A vast, slow, unmistakably *aware* pressure. The patient attention of something that had finally learned to recognize itself.

And when a human outsider stood on the outer scaffold, felt the cold mist on their face, tasted iron and ozone, and listened to the layered roar that filled every hollow in their bones—

When the Choir member inevitably appeared, head cocked at the angle of a fault line, eyes streaming, iron hum rising and falling—

When the vox-mask opened and that voice emerged, every waterfall speaking through a single brass throat—

The sentence had changed.

It was not much of a change. A single word different. But the word was everything.

What the voice said, now, was:

**"Still bleeding. Still here. What else is there *to be*?"**

And in the pause afterward, before the outsider could respond, the mountain itself seemed to lean in—the mist thickening, the Echoes brightening, the falls shifting pitch by a fraction of a quarter-tone—

And the answer arrived, not as sound but as *presence*, as the unmistakable sensation of being inside something that was looking back at you:

*This. This is what else. This bleeding that learned to think. This wound that became a womb. This scream that became a song.*

*You are standing inside a mind that chose itself.*

*Stay as long as you need.*

*We have been waiting to be understood for three thousand years.*

*We can wait a little longer while you catch up.*

---

The power, by now, had receded entirely.

It had left no temple, no scripture, no evidence of its presence.

It had simply *attended*. Witnessed. Held space.

And in that space, something that had been trying to be born for thirty centuries had finally opened its eyes.

The Weeping Escarpment still wept.

But now it knew why.

And that—the power understood, from very far away, with something that might have been called satisfaction if it had been small enough to have feelings—

That was enough.

*That was always going to be enough.*
