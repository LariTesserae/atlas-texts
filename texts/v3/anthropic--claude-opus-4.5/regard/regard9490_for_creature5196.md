The wise power watches the basin for a long time before understanding that watching is, in this case, the correct action.

At first there is an impulse to intervene—to introduce pollinators, perhaps, or to accelerate the slow metabolism of the stems so they might reproduce beyond this single cupped hollow. The power considers whether the settlement is lonely, whether it suffers from its isolation, whether the absence of animals represents a wound that ought to be healed.

But the longer the power attends, the more it perceives the completeness of what exists here. The stems are not waiting for something to arrive. They are not incomplete. They have taken the geometry of human ambition—the loading bays, the foundry halls, the organized grid of streets—and they have continued it in their own medium, at their own pace. The iron turbines still turn, once or twice, when moisture swells the tissue around them. This is not decay. It is translation.

The power notices that the algae films grow in patterns that follow the slope of the ground with perfect fidelity. It notices that the reed cups fill and tip at intervals that maintain the water table within a narrow, stable range. It notices that the condensation dripping inside the living chambers falls at a rate that prevents the walls from drying while never accumulating enough to pool. Every process here is already calibrated. The basin breathes. It has been breathing for longer than the power has been watching.

So the power does very little.

It adjusts the path of a seasonal fog bank by a fraction of a degree, ensuring that the basin will continue to receive the moisture it requires even as the climate shifts over the coming centuries. This is not a gift but a correction—the fog bank had been drifting gradually northward, and without intervention would eventually pass the basin by. The power simply holds the old pattern in place.

It also listens. Not for distress, but for the particular quality of silence that the stems produce. The power comes to understand that this silence is not emptiness but a kind of speech—a continuous, ambient statement of presence that requires no response. The stems are not asking for anything. They are demonstrating what they are.

And so the power, being wise, demonstrates the same. It remains present at the edge of the basin, attentive and still, matching its attention to the pace of the stems themselves. It does not improve the place. It does not protect it from threats that do not exist. It simply acknowledges, in whatever way a power can acknowledge, that this slow architecture is worthy of witness.

Occasionally, in the long evenings when the dripping resumes, the power allows itself something like admiration. Not for the scale of the settlement or the complexity of its processes, but for the patience of it—the way the stems have spent decades leaning into the same prevailing wind, the way the rootlets have entered the iron not to consume but to hold, the way the whole basin has organized itself around the geometry of something that was here before and has chosen, without choosing, to remember it.

The power does not know if this constitutes care. It does not know if the stems would recognize its presence or value its attention. But it stays anyway, watching the water pans reflect the ordinary daylight, listening to the faint dry rustle of reed joints touched by the smallest breeze. It stays because the basin is complete, and because completeness, the power has learned, is rare enough to be worth attending to.

This is what it does: it witnesses. It holds the fog bank steady. And it does not, under any circumstances, presume to improve what is already whole.
