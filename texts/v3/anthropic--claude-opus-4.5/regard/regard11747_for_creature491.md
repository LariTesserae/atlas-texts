The attention arrives not as presence but as patience.

It does not announce itself. It does not descend or appear or manifest. It simply begins to attend—the way morning light attends to a valley, the way silence attends to the space after thunder. The plateau continues its violence. The Resonants continue their dance. Nothing changes, and yet something is now watching that was not watching before.

The benevolent and wise power observes for a long time. Longer than the Resonants mark. Longer than the plateau's cycles. It watches the way a mother watches a sleeping child, or the way a river watches the stones it shapes—not with urgency, but with the particular care that comes from understanding time differently than those who live inside it.

It sees:

A child, skull still soft, learning to fall. Her family presses rhythms into her skin: *this tremor means move left, this frequency means find the crevice, this pattern means hold someone*. She fails and fails and fails, and each failure teaches her body what her mind cannot yet hold. She will carry these lessons in her joints forever.

A man, old by Resonant standards, preparing to walk into the zone of maximum transformation. His family has painted him with the luminous oils one last time, though it is midday and no one will see. He has chosen his moment: a particular rockfall he has tracked for thirty years, one he knows will grind slow and thorough. He is not sad. He is joining a conversation that has been happening since before his species existed.

A group of seven, pressed together in full-body communication, composing something that might be prayer or might be weather report or might be love poem—in their language, these are not different things. Their synchronized trembling creates a counter-rhythm to the plateau's bass roar, a tiny assertion of pattern against chaos. It means nothing. It changes nothing. They do it anyway.

The wise power sees all of this, and it *aches* with something that is not quite pity and not quite admiration. It sees how much the Resonants have given up to live here. It sees how much they have gained. It sees that these are the same thing.

---

What does a benevolent power do when it encounters a people who have solved their problem—not elegantly, not painlessly, but completely?

The power considers intervention. It could stabilize the plateau. Still the tremors. Let the Resonants build something that lasts, sleep without listening for collapse, speak with voices instead of touch. It could give them permanence.

But it watches the child learning to fall, and it understands: she is not learning to fall. She is learning that falling is not failure. That the ground is not supposed to stay still. That her body is not a thing that exists *despite* the world but *with* it.

The power considers smaller gifts. Knowledge, perhaps—the locations of deeper stabilities, longer cycles, safer paths. But the Resonants already know these things in their feet, their skin, their bones. To give them the knowledge in another form would be to suggest that their form is insufficient. It would be a kindness that carries the shape of an insult.

It considers comfort. Some easing of the fear that must live in them always, some softening of the grief when the plateau takes someone before their time. But it watches them paint their dead with oils and place them in the grinding zones, and it sees: they have already found a way to make death continuous with life. Their grief is real, but it is not the kind of grief that needs healing. It is the kind that needs witnessing.

So the wise power does what wisdom so often requires: it does almost nothing.

---

Almost nothing.

It makes one small change, so subtle that no Resonant will ever notice it as a change. It was always this way, as far as they will ever know.

In the moments of full-body communication—when groups press together to share complex meaning, when families compose their trembling poems, when lovers speak in synchronized pressure—there is now a resonance that carries slightly further than it should. Not far. Not even beyond the reach of arms. But when seven Resonants tremble together, there is an eighth presence in the pattern. When a mother presses safety-rhythms into her child's skin, the child feels *held* by something larger than hands.

It is not a voice. It is not a message. It is just this: *you are not alone in your dancing*.

The Resonants will never build temples to this presence. They will never name it or pray to it or argue about its nature. They will simply feel, in their most connected moments, that the universe is paying attention. That their small assertions of pattern against chaos are heard by something patient enough to listen.

The power also does one other thing, though this one takes longer to manifest and will never be understood as a gift:

It allows itself to be changed by what it has witnessed.

Somewhere in the vast architecture of benevolent attention, there is now a new understanding: that sometimes the highest form of help is to watch with love and do nothing. That sometimes a people's suffering and their solution are the same thing, and cannot be separated without destroying both. That wisdom is not always knowing what to do—sometimes it is knowing what not to do, and doing that not-doing with full presence and care.

The Resonants have taught the wise power something it did not know it needed to learn.

This, too, is a form of reciprocity.

---

The plateau trembles. The Resonants tremble with it. And now, trembling alongside them both—felt only in the moments when they reach for each other, present only as a quality of attention in their most vulnerable connections—something vast and kind trembles too.

Not to stop the shaking.

Just to share it.
