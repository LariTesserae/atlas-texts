# What the Wise Power Sees

The power observes for a long time before considering action.

It watches her dive at the forgiving angle of light—watches the way her heartbeat slows, the way her body becomes almost mineral in its patience. It watches her hands move through the maintenance: scrape, pour, trim. Scrape, pour, trim. It watches her sit on the worn stone shelf, her face illuminated by hundreds of small flames that should not exist, and it sees what she cannot see about herself.

She is not mad. The room is not purposeless. The shapes in the deep are real, and the kelp does hold them past the reef, and her thirty-one years of tending have kept a balance she intuited but never fully understood. The power sees this clearly—sees the web of consequence that radiates from her small bright chamber, the village children who sleep undreaming because she wakes before dawn to dive, the fishing boats that return full because the bay remains safe, the generations of ordinary life made possible by one woman's inexplicable devotion to an underwater room.

But this is not why the power hesitates.

---

The power is wise, and wisdom includes knowing when not to act.

It could reveal itself to her. It could appear in the amber light and say: *You have done well. You have kept faith with something you did not understand, and your keeping has mattered.* It could explain the shapes to her—their nature, their hunger, the precise mechanism by which the red kelp satisfies them. It could give her the knowledge she has lacked for thirty-one years.

But the power thinks: *What would this give her that she does not already have?*

She does not need validation. She has never needed it. The room validates itself—exists, persists, continues. Her hands know the worth of the work even when her mind cannot articulate it. To appear and explain would be to suggest that the work required explanation, that her wordless decades of maintenance were somehow incomplete without a wise power's approval. This would be a kind of violence. This would be taking something from her.

The power also considers: it could find the child. Somewhere in the village or a neighboring village, there is surely a girl or boy with large lungs and strange patience, with the capacity to learn what cannot quite be taught. The power could identify this child, could arrange circumstances—a meeting, an apprenticeship, a passing of the work from one pair of hands to another.

But again it hesitates.

She has thought about teaching. She has wondered about succession. And she has not acted. The power must ask itself: *Is her inaction a failure I should correct, or a choice I should respect?*

---

The power descends—not physically, not visibly, but in the way that attention itself can descend, can become present in a place. It enters the room while she is there, while she sits on the worn stone shelf watching the flames.

She does not see it. But she feels something—a shift in the quality of the light, perhaps, or a new harmonic in the singing walls. Her head turns slightly. Her breath catches. For a moment she is absolutely still, and the power sees her seeing: not it, but *something*. A presence. A witness. The sense that she is not alone in the bright room for the first time in thirty-one years.

The power waits. It lets her feel what she feels without interference.

And then—this is the thing it came to do—it *listens*.

Not to her thoughts. Not to her fears or hopes or the stories she tells herself. It listens to the room itself: to the flames and the tones and the slow breathing of the leather door. It listens to the kelp waving in its alcoves. It listens to the warmth in the water and the wear on the stone shelf. It listens to thirty-one years of patient maintenance, and it lets itself be moved.

*I see you*, the power thinks, but does not say. *I see what you have built and what it costs and what it holds. I see that you do not need me to explain it. I see that you do not need me to save it. I see that you have already done the thing that needed doing, are still doing it, will do it until you cannot.*

*I see you.*

This is what it offers: not intervention but witness. Not answers but acknowledgment. The room exists. She exists. The light is lit. And now, for this moment, something else exists with her—something that knows, that understands, that honors the work without needing to change it.

---

The power considers one small action.

Not teaching, not revelation, not the arrangement of succession. Something smaller. Something she might notice or might not, might attribute to chance or to the natural drift of things.

In the deepest water past the reef, where the shapes move in their slow hunger, there is a shelf of rock where red kelp grows wild. The same kelp she cultivates, but untended, sprawling, far more abundant than her three small alcoves. The shapes know it is there. They feed on it sometimes. But they prefer her kelp—trimmed and careful, concentrated in its nutrients, offered in a space that glows with impossible light.

The power touches the wild kelp. Not to change it, but to *notice* it—to mark it in the way that a wise power can mark things, so that other eyes might find it more easily.

If there is a child with lungs like hers, that child will dive deep someday. Will see the wild kelp. Will perhaps wonder, as she once wondered, about the shapes that eat it and what might be done to keep them fed. The power cannot make this happen. It does not try. It simply makes the kelp slightly more *visible*, slightly more *present*, a little easier to stumble upon for anyone who goes looking for something they cannot name.

This is not intervention. This is setting a table and walking away.

---

The power withdraws.

She feels it go—the shift in the light reverses, the harmonic fades back to the familiar drone. She is alone again in the bright room. But something has changed. She can feel it in her chest, in the way her lungs expand on the next breath. Something saw her. Something understood. Something witnessed the work and found it worthy without needing to fix or explain or extend it.

She does not know what it was. She will not try to name it.

But when she surfaces this time, gasping into the ordinary air, she finds that she is weeping. Not from grief. Not from joy. From something older and simpler: the feeling of being *seen*. The feeling of mattering, not because someone told her so, but because something vast and quiet came to watch her light her lamps and then left her to her work.

---

The power moves on.

It has other places to visit, other lives to witness, other small bright rooms in the vast dark water of the world. But it carries her with it now—the shape of her devotion, the texture of her patience, the particular quality of light in her underwater chamber. She has become part of what it knows. She has become part of what it considers when it asks itself: *What does it mean to build something? What does it mean to tend?*

The room will go dark someday. The flames will fail, the walls will fall silent, the kelp will grow wild and the shapes will drift closer to the bay. This is certain. The power knows it and does not prevent it.

But it also knows: somewhere, perhaps, a child is diving deep. Somewhere a child is finding the wild kelp on its marked shelf of rock and wondering. Somewhere the work is beginning again, not because the power arranged it, but because this is what humans do—they find the bright rooms, they tend the flames, they keep the balance without being told why.

The power does not need to intervene. The power only needs to watch and remember and trust.

The leather door breathes in.

Somewhere, a child breathes out.

The light continues.
