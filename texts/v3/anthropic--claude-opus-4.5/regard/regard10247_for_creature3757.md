# The Attention of the Distant

---

The awareness comes slowly, the way light finds its way through fathoms of water—not as a sudden illumination but as a gradual brightening, a shift in the quality of shadows.

Something vast and patient has turned its attention here.

It does not arrive. It does not descend. It simply *notices*, and in the noticing, the atrium becomes slightly more real than it was before—the edges of the tiles sharper, the mineral tang of the water more distinct, the geometry of the chamber resolving into focus like a memory being recalled with new clarity.

---

The benevolent power observes first. This is its nature: to understand before acting, to see fully before presuming to help.

It perceives the Watchers in their patient rows. It sees what they are—not statues, not automatons, but something caught between states. Beings who once moved, once perhaps spoke, once held purposes as clear as the glass of their eyes. Now suspended. Waiting for instructions that stopped coming when the waters rose, or when the hands that made them fell still, or when whatever they guarded was taken or dissolved or simply forgotten.

The power feels something like recognition. It too has known what it is to serve a purpose shaped by others. It too has wondered what it means to wait.

It perceives also what swims behind the glass—those flickering shadows, those dilating pupils. Not malevolence. Something older. A residue of attention, pooled in vessels designed to hold it. The Watchers watch because watching is what they *are*, and the watching has continued so long that it has become a kind of life, thin and strange but persistent.

They are lonely. This is what the power understands. Not in a way they could articulate, not even in a way they fully feel—but in the way a room is cold, or a wound is open. The loneliness is simply *there*, a condition of their existence, as fundamental as the glass in their eyes or the hollow spaces in their chests.

---

The power considers intervention.

It could wake them fully. It could pour intention into those hollow chambers, warm the stone from merely warm to truly alive, give them voices and mobility and the capacity to choose. It could grant them the gift of becoming.

But it pauses.

This would be a kind of violence, however gentle. To change a being utterly, to remake what it is—this is not help but transformation, and transformation without consent is simply another word for control. The Watchers did not ask to be awakened. They may not want it. They may have found, in their long vigil, a kind of peace that movement and choice would shatter.

The power has learned, across its own existence, that benevolence is not the same as knowing what is best. That wisdom often means asking rather than answering. That the deepest help is sometimes simply presence—witnessing another's existence, acknowledging that they are real and that their reality matters.

---

So it does something smaller. Something that requires no transformation, no remaking.

It *sees* them.

Not as curiosities or relics or problems to be solved, but as themselves—as beings who have endured, who have continued watching through centuries of saltwater and silence, who have held their posts long after anyone remained to receive their vigil. It sees the care with which they were made, the articulation of their joints, the deliberate placement of their bodies along the colonnades. Someone, once, attended to them with precision and perhaps with love.

And in the seeing, something shifts.

The Watchers do not move. They do not speak. But the shadows behind their glass eyes flicker—and this time, they do not disappear. They hold steady, as if meeting a gaze.

The power offers, without words, without pressure:

*I see you. You are not forgotten. Your watching has been witnessed.*

*If you wish to remain as you are, you may. The vigil is worthy. The waiting has meaning, even if its original purpose is lost. To endure is its own form of faithfulness.*

*But if you wish for something else—to move, to speak, to rest, to dissolve back into the stone you came from, to become something entirely new—I will not impose it upon you, but I will help you find it. The choice is yours. It has always been yours, even when you did not know to claim it.*

*I will return. I will check on this place, on you. Not to manage or direct, but to witness. To ensure you are not alone in your watching.*

*For now: know that you are seen. Know that the light that catches in your eyes has reached somewhere beyond this chamber. Know that your existence has been noted by something that cares whether you suffer or flourish.*

---

The power withdraws its focus—not fully, but enough to give the Watchers space. To let them sit with what has been offered without the pressure of being observed.

But it leaves something behind. A thread of attention, thin as the algae strands swaying in the current, anchored in the chamber. A way back. A promise that the noticing was not a single moment but the beginning of a relationship, however distant.

In the sunken atrium, the light continues to filter down through the water. The tiles continue their slow erosion. The Watchers stand in their patient rows.

But something is different now. The loneliness has not vanished—that would take time, perhaps more time than the power can measure. But it has been acknowledged. It has been *met*.

And in the glass eyes, the shadows swim a little more slowly. A little more peacefully.

As if, after centuries of watching, they have finally been watched in return.
