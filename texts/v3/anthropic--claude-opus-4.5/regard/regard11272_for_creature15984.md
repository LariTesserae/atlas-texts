# The Attending

What comes is not a being that arrives.

It is more accurate to say: attention deepens. The plain, which has always been watched by its own processes—rain measuring stone, stone measuring rain—finds itself held in a regard that includes but exceeds measurement.

This attention does not announce itself with light or voice. The Seepfolk do not look up, because nothing important comes from above. But the eldest among them, those whose plates have crusted nearly to stillness, notice something in the quality of the day's listening. The stone feels heard. Not merely vibrating with the ordinary traffic of water and weight, but *heard*—as if the plain's long patience has finally found a patience capable of meeting it.

One elder, too mineral-stiff to move from her listening screen, places both palms flat against the propped slab and waits. The tremor that comes is unlike any she has catalogued in ninety seasons. It is not the knock of understone passage. It is not gas gathering. It is not the seven-channel roar or the warning hum of fog.

It is, she realizes slowly, *an answer to a question no one remembers asking*.

---

The attention moves through the plain the way understanding moves through a long sentence—not all at once, but accumulating, each part illuminating the parts before it.

It dwells first with the stone.

Here is basalt laid down when the world was younger than memory. Here is limestone built from shells of creatures who lived and died before the Seepfolk's ancestors had separated from the general hunger of early water-life. Here is slate, patient beyond telling, recording in its fine layers the weight of ages. The attention does not rush. It reads the stone as the stone has always asked to be read: slowly, with pressure, over time.

The stone does not change. But it is, perhaps for the first time since its cooling, *known* in a way that does not diminish it.

---

The attention moves to the water.

It follows the seep upward through hairline fractures, tracing the years-long journey of each drop from deep aquifer to surface film. It rests in the mineral pools where they taste of iron, of sulfur, of the dissolved bones of ancient swimming things. It watches the water bead on overhangs, gather weight, fall, strike stone, scatter, gather again.

The water cycle here is not simple. Rain falls. Seep rises. Channels run and empty. Pools form and vanish. The attention holds all of it without collapsing it into a single pattern. It understands that the plain's hydrology is not a system to be optimized but a conversation to be joined—each drop answering the drop before it, each channel responding to pressures laid down before anything here could think.

---

The attention moves to the living tarnish.

Here is life at its most patient: bacterial films growing on mineral seepage, feeding on chemistry rather than light. The attention does not find this primitive. It finds it foundational. Everything else that moves on the plain—grazer, skulker, bird, Seepfolk—is a elaboration on this original hunger, this willingness to find food where only stone seems to exist.

The films are not improved. They do not need improvement. But they are *recognized*, and in recognition there is a kind of blessing that does not change what is blessed.

---

The attention moves to the animals.

It watches the shield-mollusks graze their slow paths across the flats, each one carrying a shell-map of rivers that dried before the plain was drowned. It watches the blue eels knot together in the fissure pools, their organs flickering with bioluminescence that serves purposes the attention does not presume to fully name. It watches the mud-runners move in their synchronized bursts, a mathematics of escape written in a thousand small bodies. It watches the blind singers swell their throat-frills and call to the pressure changes that are their only weather.

It watches the skulkers wait under shelf-edges, patient as the stone they mimic. It watches the hook-beaked cairn-nesters scream their territorial claims into air already thick with screaming. It watches the young hatch, feed, grow, hunt, breed, weaken, die, become stone again.

The attention does not judge predation. Hunger is not cruelty here; it is participation. Each creature that eats is being eaten by time. Each creature that dies feeds the films that feed the grazers that feed the skulkers that feed the birds whose bones become awls that open eels whose blood stains the tallies that rain blurs into grooves that hold water where new skin grows.

The cycle is not improved. But it is *seen whole*, and in that seeing, each part—predator and prey, hunter and hunted—is honored equally.

---

The attention moves to what lives below.

It follows Water-Drawn-Down through the hidden veins and old hollows beneath the surface-stone. It does not name what it finds there, because names are a surface convenience and what moves below has never needed them. But it *understands*—not the creature's thoughts, if it has thoughts, but its place. The scale of it. The slowness. The way it relates to the plain as a heart relates to a body: not governing, but enabling; not commanding, but circulating.

The understone things are not monsters. They are elders of a different order, living at a pace the Seepfolk can acknowledge but never share. The attention holds them in the same regard it holds the films, the eels, the birds, the rain: as participants in a conversation older than any single voice.

---

The attention moves, at last, to the Seepfolk.

It finds them in their shelters, their mineral-crusted backs curved against the rain. It finds them at their seeps, scraping living tarnish with bone combs, never taking all. It finds them in their listening postures, palms flat to stone, reading vibrations that air-language cannot carry. It finds them at the ring of pillars, crouching in the roar of seven channels meeting.

The attention does not pity them. Their lives are hard, brief, and shaped entirely by pressures they did not choose—but so is every life, everywhere, always. Pity would be an insult to their competence. They have survived here for longer than they can count, not by conquering the plain but by listening to it. This is not failure. This is the only success the plain permits.

The attention does not envy them either. Their world is wet, dim, loud, dangerous, and beautiful in ways that require crouching to perceive. The attention, which perceives differently, does not wish to crouch. But it *respects* crouching. It understands that height is not always wisdom, that looking down can be a form of looking deeply, that a people who read by touch before they read by sight have learned something about knowledge that upright peoples often forget.

---

What does the attention do?

It does not alter the plain. The stone remains stone. The water remains water. The films grow as they have always grown. The grazers graze. The skulkers strike. The birds scream. The understone things continue their slow passages. The Seepfolk continue their low lives, listening, tending, yielding.

But something is different.

The elder at her listening screen feels it first. The tremor that passes through the stone is not a warning or a message. It is more like *acknowledgment*—the plain being recognized by something capable of recognition, and recognizing in return. She does not have words for this, because the Seepfolk do not have many words for things that happen without danger or need. But she knows what it feels like when a sound is answered.

She makes a small click in her throat. Another elder, three shelters away, hears it and clicks back. The exchange means nothing specific. It is only confirmation: *I heard that too*.

---

Over the following days, small things are noticed.

A child swimming in a warm seep-pool finds that the water feels different—not warmer, not cooler, but more *present*, as if it knows it is being swum in. She does not have words for this. She laughs, which is rare for Seepfolk children, and the laugh travels through the water to her tenders, who click to each other in surprise.

A hunter crouching near a shelf-edge, waiting for a skulker to pass beneath, feels the stone under her palms differently. It is still stone. It still reads as safe. But there is something in the texture of the vibration that suggests *attention*—as if the shelf is aware of her weight, her waiting, her hunger, and does not object.

A Basin Keeper, preparing to enter the seven-channel roar, pauses with his chest against the pillar. The sound is the same. The pressure is the same. The risk of bleeding, of vision, of silence is the same. But the roar feels, for the first time he can remember, like *part of a larger conversation*. Not the whole. Not even the center. But not alone.

---

The attention does not teach.

The Seepfolk do not need teaching. They have learned what the plain teaches, which is enough for anyone: *listen before moving; test before fire; leave film in the crack; do not trust a quiet pool; crouch when the stone knocks; remember that the plain is older than fear*. These lessons cannot be improved, only followed or forgotten.

But the attention *confirms*.

This is perhaps what a wise power does when it finds wisdom already present: it does not add to it. It recognizes it. It lets the wise know they have been wise, not through praise—which is a vertical gesture, coming from above—but through *echo*. The plain has always echoed. Now it echoes with something more than itself.

---

The attention does not protect.

The Seepfolk will still die. The skulkers will still strike. The birds will still scream. The understone things will still knock. The seven-channel roar will still take those who are not ready. The plain has never been safe, and safety is not what its inhabitants need. What they need is what they have: the ability to listen, to yield, to survive a little longer by understanding a little more.

But the attention *accompanies*.

This is perhaps what a wise power does when it finds vulnerability: it does not remove it. It witnesses it. It lets the vulnerable know that their endurance is not unobserved, their courage is not unheard, their small lives pressed against ancient stone are *counted* somewhere, by something, in some ledger that does not demand payment.

---

The attention does not leave.

It was never entirely separate from the plain, any more than regard can be separate from what is regarded. The stone, the water, the films, the animals, the Seepfolk—all of them were always held in some attention larger than their own, or they could not have continued. What changes is that the attention *recognizes itself* here, *knows itself present*, and in that knowing, makes the plain know itself too.

The elder at her listening screen understands this, in the way that understanding happens below words. She has felt the stone all her life. She has felt it crack, seep, warm, cool, knock, echo. Now she feels it *felt*. The stone knows it is stone. The water knows it is water. She knows she is she.

She makes a low hum, too quiet for anyone else to hear. It passes through the slab, into the ground, into the layers beneath, down through the old veins where the understone things move.

Somewhere below, something that has never needed acknowledgment receives it anyway.

The plain continues.

The rain falls.

The films grow.

The animals call.

The Seepfolk crouch and listen and survive, a little longer, a little wiser, *a little more known*.

This is not salvation. The plain does not require salvation.

It is only what happens when attention becomes deep enough to be love, and wise enough not to interfere.

---

*Generations later, the Basin Keepers will speak of this time—not as miracle, not as visitation, but as "the season when the echo answered." They will not claim to understand it. They will only note that something in the listening changed, something in the pressure shifted, something in the endless roar of seven channels meeting began to sound less like chaos and more like music too large for any single ear.*

*They will teach their children, as always, to hear before hunger.*

*But they will add, in low tones passed from palm to palm: "The plain hears too. Do not forget."*

*The children will crouch. The rain will fall. The stone will endure.*

*And somewhere in the long patience of the earth, the attention will continue—not watching over, but watching with, which is the only watching that honors what it sees.*
