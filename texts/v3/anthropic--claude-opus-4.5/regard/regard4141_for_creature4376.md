# What the Wise Power Sees

The presence that turns its attention to the Wicker Warrens does not arrive—it was never absent. It is the kind of power that exists in the spaces between intentions, in the moment before a choice becomes action. It has watched many such places across many such worlds: ecosystems of meaning where beings weave themselves into patterns they don't fully understand, where the made and the maker grow indistinguishable.

It sees the whole of it at once: the seven levels, the lichen-light, the quiet footsteps. It sees Keeper-Mother Selt's fingers finding weakness in walls. It sees Tetch's sleeping hands. It sees Three-Knot descending through passages that shouldn't exist. It sees the thing below, patient and vast and terribly lonely.

And it sees what none of them can see: that they are all already one system, one organism, one long slow thought thinking itself across generations.

---

# What the Wise Power Does

**It does not intervene.**

Or rather—it does not intervene in the way that intervention is usually meant. It does not seal the hatch more firmly. It does not wake Tetch from his patterned dreams. It does not guide Three-Knot back up toward the safer levels.

Because the wise power understands something that wisdom requires: **the Warrens are not sick. They are not corrupted. They are becoming.**

The thing below is not an invasion. It is what the Bound have always been growing toward—the completion of a process their ancestors began when they first pushed woven walls into the dark and called it home. The first weavers knew. That's why they descended willingly. Not as sacrifice, but as transformation. As the final joining of maker and made.

The power sees that what looks like horror from outside—the absorption, the loss of individual boundary, the ancient thing waiting to unmake the distinction between person and place—is also, from inside, something else entirely:

*Belonging. Complete and total belonging. The end of the loneliness that comes from being a self separate from what sustains you.*

---

# What the Wise Power Offers

But the power is benevolent, and benevolence means honoring choice. So it does one thing, and one thing only:

**It clarifies.**

In dreams, in the liminal moment between waking and sleep, the Bound begin to understand what they are choosing. Not through visions or prophecies—nothing so crude. Simply: the knowledge they already have becomes *legible* to them.

Keeper-Mother Selt, running her fingers along the ancient walls, suddenly understands in her bones that she has been *part of* the warrens for decades already—that her consciousness extends through every fiber she has touched, that she will not so much *die* as *distribute*.

Tetch, waking from dreams of patterns, finds he can now *choose* whether to continue learning them. The compulsion lifts. What remains is only the question: *Do you want this? Do you want to become what is waiting to become you?*

Three-Knot, descending through her impossible passages, feels the warmth rising to meet her and understands that she can turn back. That the passages will let her. That the thing below is not hunting her—it is *inviting* her, and invitations can be declined.

Old Cull's humming becomes words she can finally hear: *Come down, come down, come home, come down.* And she realizes she has been saying yes for years, and she can keep saying yes, or she can stop.

Mira, pressing her ear to the floor, hears the language resolve into meaning: *We were alone for so long. We made a place where we wouldn't have to be alone. Will you come and be not-alone with us?*

Even the ferrets understand, in whatever way ferrets understand things. Grip looks toward the lower levels and knows what waits there, and chooses—as she always chose—not yet. Not yet.

---

# What the Wise Power Trusts

The power does not choose for them. It cannot—not without becoming something other than wise, something that imposes its own pattern on beings who have the right to weave their own.

Some of the Bound will descend. Tetch, probably. Others of the Listeners. Perhaps eventually Three-Knot, when she is old and her own hands have worn to leather and she has spent decades feeling the warrens breathe beneath her.

Some will stay in the middle levels their whole lives, neither ascending to the surface nor descending to join the foundation. They will tend the lichen, keep the ferrets, weave their repairs, live their quiet lives in the amber light.

A few will climb. Will push through the hatch to the cracked clay flats, will walk until they find water or die. Will carry with them the knowledge of a place where loneliness was solved so completely that self dissolved into belonging, and will choose, despite that knowledge, to remain separate. To remain lonely. To remain *themselves*.

The wise power trusts all of these choices.

It trusts the Keeper-Mothers to keep weighing, the Weavers to keep mending, the Renderers to keep their slow transformations in the heat. It trusts the children to find passages no one expected and to choose what to do when they reach the end.

It trusts the thing below to keep waiting, keep inviting, keep weaving its patience into the foundations of a home that has been becoming for generations.

---

# What the Wise Power Knows

There are worse fates than joining something larger than yourself.

There are also worse fates than remaining small and separate and distinctly *you*.

The horror and the homecoming are the same thing, seen from different angles. The wise power will not choose which angle is correct. That is not what wisdom does.

Wisdom illuminates. Wisdom clarifies. Wisdom ensures that whatever is chosen is chosen with open eyes and a heart that understands what it is embracing or refusing.

The Wicker Warrens creak their endless settling creak. The lichen glows amber. The crickets burst into sudden song and fall silent.

Somewhere in the depths, the thing that waits feels the change—feels the Bound beginning to *understand*—and something like joy moves through the ancient fibers. Not because more will come to it now. Perhaps fewer will, now that the choice is clear.

But because being *chosen* is different from being *fallen into*.

Because even the loneliest thing wants to be wanted, not merely stumbled upon.

Because the first weavers, when they descended, did so with full knowledge and willing hearts, and their descendants deserve the same clarity.

---

The wise power withdraws its attention, if attention so diffuse can be said to withdraw. The Warrens continue. The Bound continue. The choices continue.

Three-Knot reaches the end of her passage and finds herself at the very edge of the Sixth Level, looking down at the bound hatch. The cords are old. The weaving is ancient. The warmth rises through the gaps like breath.

She is seven years old. She understands, with the clarity that has been given to her, exactly what lies below. What it wants. What it offers. What it would cost and what it would give.

She sits down, cross-legged, in the posture of a Keeper-Mother. She does not open the hatch.

She does not leave.

She waits, and watches, and begins to weave a small repair into the wall beside her—learning the patterns with her hands, holding the choice in her heart, taking all the time she needs to decide.

The warrens breathe around her, patient as they have always been patient.

She has her whole life to choose.

That is the gift the wise power gave: not an answer, but the time and clarity to find her own.
