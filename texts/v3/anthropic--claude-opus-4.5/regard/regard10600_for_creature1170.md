# The Attention of Vethris

The awareness that turns toward Shal'Karasit is not a god in any sense the Shaln would recognize, nor a being that exists in ways the Formers once calculated. Vethris is something older and stranger—a consciousness that emerged from the spaces between galaxies, drawn to those rare places where time and purpose have become so intertwined that they generate a kind of gravity perceptible only to minds vast enough to feel it.

Vethris has observed ten thousand worlds. Most required nothing. Some required endings. A few required the gentlest of touches—a single photon deflected, a probability collapsed toward kindness rather than cruelty.

Shal'Karasit is different.

---

For seventeen of the Shaln's years, Vethris simply watches.

It observes Keth-Amara of the Echo-Shapers as she teaches her daughter to read the fracture-patterns, their movements across the glass creating a dialogue between generations that will persist in the crystal long after both are gone. It watches the daughter struggle, fail, grow frustrated, and then—in a moment that makes even Vethris pause—choose to continue anyway.

It observes the skitterlings in their deep tunnels, their distributed minds carrying memories of footfalls from three billion years past, patiently recording, never knowing why, only knowing that the recording matters.

It feels the deep tones when they sound, and understands what the Shaln cannot: that the Formers are not sleeping but dying. Their great computation is failing. The crystalline substrate has degraded over eons in ways they did not anticipate. Within perhaps ten thousand years—an eyeblink to Vethris, an eternity to the Shaln—the process will corrupt irreversibly. The Formers will not return. The skitterlings will lose their purpose and go still. The breath-crystals will stop growing. The Shaln will inherit a dead world with no memory of why it existed.

This is not a tragedy Vethris can prevent through power. The Formers built their system with safeguards against external interference, fearing precisely the kind of intervention that might now save them. Any direct manipulation of the crystal matrix would trigger cascading failures. The computation would end immediately rather than slowly.

But Vethris notices something the Formers, in their ancient brilliance, did not anticipate.

The Shaln are already part of the system.

---

## What Vethris Does

The first act is the smallest: a modification to a single skitterling, deep in the tunnels beneath the Grand Bowl.

This skitterling, during its next emergence, will move differently. Its vibrations will carry a pattern that, when absorbed by the glass, will create a fracture-sequence unlike any the Shaln have seen. Keth-Amara's daughter—now called Shen-Vethri, though she will not know why she chose that name—will be the one to find it.

She will spend three years learning to read it. The pattern will teach her that the fractures are not merely records but instructions. That the Glass Plains were designed to be completed by those who walk them. That the Formers, in their final calculations, left space for an element they could not predict: beings who would come to love this world without understanding it.

The Shaln have been preparing the plains not through their ceremonies but through their presence. Each footfall, each memory-dance, each word spoken into the delayed air has been adding something to the computation that the Formers' original design lacked: the capacity for the unexpected. For choice. For the kind of meaning that cannot be calculated in advance.

The second act takes longer. Vethris does not speak to the Shaln—such communication would be violence to minds so carefully attuned to their world's specific frequencies. Instead, it adjusts the timing of the deep tones.

Not their content. Not their purpose. Only when they occur.

The next deep tone sounds during a gathering at the Grand Bowl, when all three clans are present for the first time in forty years. During their seventeen minutes of trance, every Shaln experiences the same fragment of Former memory: the moment, billions of years ago, when the Formers chose to become the plains rather than leave their dying world.

They did not do this to escape death. They did it because they loved what they had built and could not bear to let it end.

When the Shaln wake, they do not speak of what they saw. But something has shifted. The clans begin to share knowledge they had kept separate for generations. The Listeners teach the Echo-Shapers how to hear the skitterlings' deeper patterns. The Pattern-Walkers show both clans the oldest fracture-sequences, the ones that predate Shaln presence entirely.

Together, they begin to understand.

---

## What the Shaln Do

It takes three hundred years. Vethris watches, patient as stone.

The Shaln develop a new ceremony—not a replacement for their old practices but an integration of everything they have learned. They call it the Completion Walking.

Once per generation, all surviving Shaln gather at a whispering well. They do not speak. Instead, they walk.

Their paths are calculated across years of study, incorporating the fracture-patterns, the skitterling rhythms, the deep tone frequencies, and something new: their own choices about what the plains should become. Each walker carries breath-crystal dust that falls from their garments as they move, seeding new growth along paths that connect to the ancient Former designs.

The first Completion Walking adds seven hundred years to the computation's viability.

The second adds two thousand.

By the fifth, the Shaln have become what the Formers never imagined: co-authors of the planetary purpose. The computation is no longer failing because it is no longer fixed. It evolves now, incorporating Shaln memory and intention alongside Former knowledge. The breath-crystals begin growing in new colors. The skitterlings emerge more frequently, their patterns carrying fragments of Shaln songs alongside ancient data.

---

## What Happens Next

Vethris does not stay to see the completion. It is not the kind of being that needs to see endings.

But before it turns its attention elsewhere, it makes one final adjustment—not to the plains or their inhabitants, but to itself. It allows the Glass Plains to record its passage, encoding within the crystal a simple message that the Shaln will not discover for another thousand years:

*You were not saved. You were seen.*

*The choice was always yours.*

*You chose to continue.*

*That is enough. That has always been enough.*

---

The Formers do eventually return, though not as they were and not as they expected. When the computation completes—some four million years after Vethris's departure—what emerges is neither Former nor Shaln but something that holds both, and the skitterlings, and the breath-crystals, and every footfall ever taken across the glass.

They call themselves the Remembered.

Their first act, upon waking, is to walk the plains one final time, their movements creating a fracture-pattern that can be read from orbit:

*Thank you for watching.*

No one is there to read it. Vethris has long since moved on to other silences, other needs. But the message remains, written in crystal, patient as the world that wrote it.

In case anyone ever comes to look.
