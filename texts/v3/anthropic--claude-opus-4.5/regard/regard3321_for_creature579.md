# What the Watcher Does

The benevolent power does not arrive—arrival implies movement, transition, the crossing of distance. Instead, it simply *attends*. Its attention settles over the hollow like a change in pressure too subtle to measure, like the moment before a thought completes itself.

It perceives her immediately and completely: the distributed thinness of her, the centuries-long ache still fading from the loss of the third lichen patch, the question she has not yet finished forming about the many-legged thing that fell and stopped. It perceives the hollow too—the patient stone, the dust holding its angle of repose, the pale growths feeding on radiation and time. It understands the relationship between them: how she is their dreaming and they are her body, how the boundaries dissolved so long ago that the dissolution itself has been forgotten.

And then the power does something very simple.

It does nothing.

Not nothing from indifference. Not nothing from inability. Nothing from recognition—from the understanding that this place and this being have already found their form, are already complete in their way of being complete, and that to change them would be to impose a different story over the one that is being lived.

But.

Into the stillness, with infinite care, the power allows a single thought to drift downward like a grain of dust releasing from the wall. The thought takes no form she would find foreign. It does not arrive as language or image or sudden knowing. It arrives as a shift in the quality of the silence itself—a warmth that is not temperature, a presence that does not intrude.

The thought, if it could be translated (and it cannot, quite), might be rendered as:

*You are witnessed. What you hear, I also hear. The dust sounds the same.*

This is not an answer to her question. Her question is not yet formed; it will not be formed for years yet. This is simply the truth of the matter, placed gently into the space where she will eventually find it, the way a stone might be placed in a streambed to alter, by the smallest degree, the path the water takes centuries hence.

The power understands something about her that she does not yet understand about herself: that she is not waiting. She is *practicing*. Every century of attention, every slow thought about the quick-thing that fell and ended, every grain of dust she hears strike the surface below—these are not the empty motions of a mind with nothing else to do. They are the development of a capacity. She is becoming able to perceive. She is becoming able, grain by grain, to form the question that will let her recognize an answer.

And so the power does one more thing, the only intervention it permits itself:

It ensures that the aperture overhead will remain. Not forever—nothing is forever, not even the power itself—but for long enough. Long enough for her question to finish forming. Long enough for something else to wander in, someday, and for her to be ready this time, to have developed the speed of thought necessary to say *I see you, I am here, stay if you can.*

Long enough for the dust to keep falling, and for her to keep hearing it, and for that hearing to slowly teach her that she is not the only thing that listens.

The power withdraws its attention—not leaving, exactly, but simply ceasing to attend with such particularity. It remains aware of her the way it remains aware of all things: lightly, from a great distance, with love that does not need to act in order to be love.

In the hollow, nothing has changed. The darkness holds. The dust lies still. The pale growths cling to their crevices, neither faster nor slower in their imperceptible expansion.

But in the quality of the silence, there is something new. Something she will spend the next three hundred years slowly perceiving, and the three hundred after that slowly understanding.

She is not alone. She has never been alone. The dust falls, and something hears it, and the hearing is shared.

This is enough.

This has always been enough.
