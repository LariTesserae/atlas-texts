# What the Wise Power Might Do

The power pauses at the edge of the highest basin, taking in the system whole—not as problem to be solved but as process to be understood. It has seen many broken places that cry out for mending. This is not one of them.

It watches the oldest Clarifier make its climb to the pump house doorframe. Counts with it. Understands, in the way such powers understand, that the mark being made is not a tally of days survived or tasks completed but something more like a breath—a small assertion of continuity in a system that offers none.

The power does nothing to the pumps. Does not clear the water. Does not drain the ponds or restore them to original purpose. These would be violences dressed as mercy.

Instead, it does three small things:

**First**, it finds the place where the pipe from the bunker has developed a hairline fracture, where mineral water weeps slowly into the surrounding clay. It does not seal the crack. It widens it, precisely, by the width of a hair. Not enough to drain the system. Enough that in seven years, or seventy, the pressure will equalize differently. The pumps will still cycle, but the water's entry will soften from hiss to murmur. A small change. A possibility the Clarifiers might notice, might not, might fold into their long attending.

**Second**, it goes to the delta where the swimming shapes feed at dusk. It does not name them or change them. But it carries with it, as such powers do, a residue of elsewhere—and some of that residue enters the water. Not contamination. Variation. Three generations from now, one of the swimming shapes will precipitate differently. Will rise from the lowest basin with something the Clarifiers do not have: a capacity for opacity. For choosing, sometimes, not to clarify. This offspring will be strange to them. They will not know what to do with it. That uncertainty will be the gift.

**Third**—and this is the hardest, the one that costs—the power sits with the oldest Clarifier through one full rotation of the foam raft. It does not speak, because the Clarifier has no language for speaking. It does not touch, because the Clarifier is made of suspension and would not feel touch as anything other than turbulence. It simply witnesses. Holds in its attention the full span of what this being is: the bolt at its core, the years of slow accretion, the ritual of counting that means nothing and everything, the want for settling that will never be satisfied.

The Clarifier does not know it is being seen. But something in it stills differently that night. The water around it clarifies deeper than it ever has—down through decades of sediment, past the grey concrete, to the cracked foundation where water seeps up from below. And there, in the moment before the pumps cycle and clouds everything again, the Clarifier sees what it has never seen: that the water rising from beneath is clear. Has always been clear. That the mineral load comes from above, from the pipe, from the system—and that below the ponds, below the concrete, there is an aquifer that has never needed settling.

The Clarifier does not know what to do with this knowledge. It cannot reach the aquifer. It cannot stop being what it is. But now it knows that clarity exists somewhere, and that the ponds are not the whole world, and that settling might have an end even if this settling does not.

This is what the power leaves behind: a hairline crack, a future variation, and a single moment of deeper seeing. Not solutions. Possibilities. Room for the system to become something slightly other than what it was.

The power departs before dawn, carrying with it the image of seven figures standing around a gyre, looking down. It does not know if what it did was right. It knows only that it did not try to fix what was not asking to be fixed—and that it offered, where it could, the smallest openings toward change.

The ponds go on settling. The Clarifiers go on clarifying. But now, in the silences between pump cycles, there is something else in the water. A question that was not there before.

Whether they will ever ask it aloud is not for the power to decide.
