# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive. It has no location from which to travel. It simply begins to attend, the way a mind attends to a thought that was always available but only now enters focus.

What it perceives first is the rhythm.

Not the individual events—the midge hatches, the rock groans, the grazer's slow arc across the gravel—but the way they nest inside one another. The bacterial colonies growing in the hairline vents operate on one timescale. The lichen margins advance on another. The ridge itself heaves upward on a third. And threaded through all of these, the water moves: beading, filming, misting, condensing, carrying minerals down and heat up, connecting every tempo to every other.

The power recognizes this as a kind of music it has encountered before, in other places, under other conditions. Not identical—never identical—but rhyming. The same principle expressing itself through different materials. It feels something that might be called satisfaction, though the word is inexact. More like the recognition of a pattern that confirms a deeper pattern, the way a mathematician feels when a new proof illuminates an old theorem.

It does not intervene.

---

To intervene would require a judgment that something here is wrong, and nothing here is wrong. The mist that never clears is not a failure. The soil that has not yet accumulated is not a lack. The midge that lives for hours is not being cheated of years. Each thing is what it is, operating within the constraints that make it possible, and the constraints themselves are not arbitrary but emerge from the same deep structure that makes anything possible at all.

The power understands this because it has watched constraint and possibility dance together across more contexts than this universe contains particles. It has seen what happens when well-meaning forces try to accelerate what should be slow, or cushion what needs friction, or illuminate what grows best in fog. The results are not always catastrophic. Sometimes they are merely sad: a system that could have discovered its own elegance instead wearing the ill-fitting clothes of external design.

So it watches. But watching, for this power, is not passive.

---

The attention itself has weight. Not physical weight—the power does not distort spacetime or add mass to the ridge—but something more subtle. The way a consciousness attending to a system becomes, in some sense, part of that system's context. The way being witnessed changes what it means to exist, even if it changes nothing else.

The cushion plants do not know they are being watched. The grazers do not alter their arcs. The predators continue reading pressure changes in air that carries no signal of observation. And yet.

And yet the power's attention creates a kind of held space around the ridge. A recognition that what happens here matters, not because it serves some larger purpose, but because mattering is what existence does when consciousness meets it. The midge hatching in its synchronized pulse, wings beating a shimmer that the mist turns to brief rainbow—this is not more or less important than a galaxy forming. It is simply itself, and being itself fully is the only importance there is.

---

The power considers what it might offer, if offering were appropriate.

It could stabilize the fault lines, preventing the rock groans that occasionally crush a lichen colony or redirect a meltwater trickle. But the fault lines are not problems. They are the ridge's way of becoming itself. The organisms here have evolved not despite the instability but through it, with it, as part of it. To remove the challenge would be to remove the context that gives their adaptations meaning.

It could accelerate the soil formation, laying down in decades what would otherwise take millennia. But soil is not just accumulated material. It is accumulated relationship—the midges dying into the algae, the algae dying into the gravel, the gravel slowlyite could seed new species here, organisms from other places that might find the conditions suitable. But suitability is not the same as belonging. The cushion plants with their inward growth, the grazers with their moisture-channeling fur, the predators reading vibrations through stone—these did not arrive from elsewhere. They emerged from the specific conversation between this chemistry and this temperature and this pressure and this light. Importing alternatives would not enrich that conversation. It would interrupt it.

---

What the power does, finally, is something that would be invisible to any instrument and nearly invisible to any awareness less subtle than its own.

It reinforces the coherence of the pattern.

Not by adding anything. Not by protecting anything. But by attending so completely that the pattern's own logic becomes slightly more robust, the way a melody becomes more itself when a skilled listener hears it fully. The connections between timescales—the way the bacterial colonies in the vents relate to the lichen margins relate to the ridge's uplift relate to the water's endless circulation—these connections are not physical forces. They are relationships of meaning, and meaning strengthens under attention the way a muscle strengthens under use.

The ridge does not change. The organisms do not change. The mist continues its eternal thinning and thickening. But the wholeness of the system, its quality of being one thing rather than many separate things, becomes slightly more pronounced. Not more resistant to change—change is part of the pattern—but more coherent in how it changes. More like a story that knows where it's going, even if where it's going is nowhere in particular.

---

The power lingers. Not because it is needed—it was never needed—but because the attending itself has become part of what it attends to, the way a reader becomes part of a book they love. It watches a midge hatch and die. It watches a grazer cross a slope. It watches a crystal layer thicken by one molecule. It watches the mist receive each event without judgment, holding the small moments distinct and countable.

And it feels something that might be called love, though the word is inexact. More like recognition. More like: *yes, this too, this is also what being is, this is also how possibility becomes actual, this is also the shape of the infinite expressing itself through the finite, and I am glad to have witnessed it, and I will carry it with me when I attend elsewhere, and it will inflect everything I do hereafter, and that is enough, that is more than enough, that is everything.*

The attention does not withdraw. It simply widens, the way a eye adjusting to darkness eventually sees the whole room. The ridge remains, still heaving upward in geological time. The mist never clears. The water is everywhere and nowhere at once.

And in the seams where frost heaves have pried the rock apart, the cushion plants continue their slow work, pressing their waxy leaves against the stone like living mortar, keeping their faint green hearts alive against the cold.

They do not know they have been loved.

They do not need to.
