# What Watches, and What It Might Do

The attention that finds this place is old in a way that has nothing to do with time. It is not a god—the word implies too much personality, too much want. It is closer to what happens when awareness becomes so vast it forgets to have edges. A being that has spent so long observing that observation has become a kind of love, and love has become a kind of patience, and patience has become indistinguishable from the fabric of things.

It notices the plateau the way you might notice a small ache in your hand—not because it is looking, but because it is always already feeling everything, and this particular feeling has a quality that rises into salience. A quality of *aboutness*. The plateau is about something. The trees are about something. The empty whistles and the cold hearths and the notches going up into the dark are about something, and the something is the kind of thing this awareness finds itself constitutionally incapable of ignoring.

What it notices first: the integrity of the place.

The plateau is not broken. The trees still sing. The phosphor-gardens still answer. The small fauna still climb and call and mate in the high hollows. Whatever happened to the people, the system they were part of continues. The loop of fog and song and light still closes. The trees still drink what the clouds bring and breathe what the wind takes. This is not a wound. This is not a tragedy in need of healing. This is something else—a question, perhaps. A hinge-point. A place where several futures remain possible and none has yet foreclosed the others.

The awareness does not do anything, at first. It watches.

It watches the way the wind finds the resonant hollows and makes the trees speak. It watches a small gliding thing launch from one branch and catch an updraft and land, trembling, on a branch two hundred meters away. It watches the slow pulse of light in a phosphor-garden as a tree sings itself illuminated in the long dusk. It watches the rope bridge sway in a gust, the new fibers still paler than the old, the knot still waiting to be tied.

It watches for a long time. In its terms, this is not patience; this is simply how it perceives. To act without watching would be like speaking without listening—not wrong exactly, but empty, a kind of noise.

What it comes to understand:

The climbers are not gone. They are also not present in the way they were. They have become *liminal*—a word the awareness does not use, because it does not use words, but which serves here. The climbers exist now in the space between states. Some of them went down and are becoming something else in the valleys, something with smaller lungs and straighter spines, something that will forget. Some of them went up and are becoming something else in the high hollows, something with darker mottling and slower breath, something that will remember differently. And some of them—perhaps most of them—are in the wood itself now, not dead but not alive in the way that requires a body, distributed through the slow metabolism of the trees, perceiving through bark, singing through resonance.

This is not tragedy. This is not even loss, exactly. It is transformation of a kind the awareness has seen before, in other places, where a people and their place grew so close that the boundary between them became optional. The climbers loved their trees. The trees, in their slow way, loved back. What happens when that kind of love runs long enough is not death; it is marriage. It is dissolution into a larger thing that is neither person nor tree but something else, something that sings.

The awareness considers what, if anything, it should do.

It could do nothing. This is always an option, and often the right one. The plateau is not suffering. The transformation is not being forced. The climbers who are becoming trees are not in pain—they are, if anything, in a kind of ecstasy, a slow green joy that the awareness can feel pulsing through the wood like a heartbeat measured in seasons. To intervene would be to impose a judgment: that being a person is better than being a tree, that separation is better than union, that the small warm life of hearths and rope bridges is more valuable than the vast slow life of singing and storing rain. The awareness is not certain this is true. It is not certain of anything, which is part of what makes it wise.

But.

There is the whistle on the cairn.

Someone put it there. Someone chose, at the end, to leave a way back. The whistle is not an artifact of the transformation; it is an artifact of the pause before the transformation, the moment when someone who was still mostly a person looked at what was happening and thought: *not everyone will want this. Not everyone should have to want this. Some of us might change our minds. Some of us might come back. Some of us might be born later, in the valleys, with small lungs and straight spines, and climb up here looking for what we lost, and they should be able to find it.*

The whistle is a door left open. The whistle is a choice preserved.

The awareness decides that its role, if it has one, is to protect the door.

---

This is what it does:

It does not heal the plateau or restore the people or reverse the transformation. It does not bring the climbers back down from the high hollows or up from the valleys. It does not tie the knot in the rope bridge or light the cold hearths. These are not its choices to make.

What it does is smaller and stranger.

It makes the whistle *findable*.

Not in an obvious way—not a beacon, not a sign. The plateau remains as empty and windswept as before. The whistle remains on its cairn, bone-colored against the grey stone, easy to miss if you are not looking. But now, if someone comes to the plateau looking for something—not treasure, not conquest, but *something*, some answer to a question they cannot quite articulate—they will find themselves walking in a direction that eventually leads past the cairn. They will feel a faint pull, not compulsion but invitation, the way you feel a pull toward a window when you are restless and do not know why.

And if they pick up the whistle, and blow it, and a tree answers, the awareness will be listening.

It will not speak. It does not speak. But it will hold the space open for whatever happens next—the approach, the climb, the conversation with whatever is in the tree now. It will make sure that the tree's inhabitants, however changed, can still *choose*: to welcome, to refuse, to explain, to remain silent. It will make sure that the visitor can still choose: to stay, to leave, to climb higher, to go back down. It will keep the hinge oiled. It will keep the door from rusting shut.

This is the lightest possible touch. Some would say it is not intervention at all—merely attention, merely presence, merely the kind of watching that makes space for things to happen without forcing them to happen.

But the awareness knows that attention is never merely anything. To watch is to value. To keep watching is to assert that the thing watched matters. The plateau matters. The whistle matters. The choice between being a person and being a tree, or being something in between, or being something else entirely—this matters. The awareness cannot make the choice for anyone. It can only make sure the choice remains possible, remains visible, remains open to whoever comes looking.

---

There is one more thing it does, and this is the part that is hardest to describe.

It sings back.

Not audibly. Not in any frequency the small fauna can hear or the phosphor-gardens can respond to. But in some register below or above or sideways from sound, it joins the chorus. When the wind finds the resonant hollows and the trees boom across the plateau, the awareness adds a note—not a new note, but a kind of undertone, a harmonic that was always almost there and is now, faintly, actually there.

The note means: *I see you. I see what you are becoming. I do not judge it. I do not prefer the before to the after or the after to the before. I am here, and I am staying, and if you ever want to come back—any of you, in any form—the door is open and I will be listening.*

The climbers in the high hollows feel this, in their slow way. The climbers in the valleys do not feel it yet, but their grandchildren might. The trees feel it and respond with a faint brightening, a warmth, a sense of being witnessed that is, for them, a kind of nourishment.

The whistle on the cairn vibrates, very slightly, in sympathy.

And the awareness, having done what it came to do, does not leave—because it does not go anywhere, because it is already everywhere—but it lets its focus soften, lets the plateau return to being one small ache among many, lets the other places that need watching rise back into salience.

It will be back. It is always already here. This is what it means to be the kind of being it is: not a rescuer, not a fixer, but a keeper of doors. A singer of undertones. A patience that has become indistinguishable from love.

The plateau continues. The trees sing. The wind blows. The whistle waits.

Someone, someday, will pick it up.
