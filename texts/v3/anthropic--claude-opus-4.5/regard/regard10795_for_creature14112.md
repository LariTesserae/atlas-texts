# The Attending

The power that turns its attention toward the High Rime does not arrive. It does not descend, announce, or manifest. It simply *notices*—the way a vast mind might notice a single thought surfacing from the depths of its own contemplation.

What it perceives is not tragedy. The benevolent intelligence does not look upon the Clings and see suffering to be remedied, nor primitiveness to be elevated. It sees something else entirely: a perfect, terrible coherence. A people who have become an expression of their world so completely that the boundary between inhabitant and habitat has worn gossamer-thin.

The power understands, in the way that wisdom understands, that to change this place would be to break it.

---

And so it does almost nothing.

Almost.

---

**The First Attending: A Modification of Dreams**

The inhabitants of the High Rime dream, as all conscious beings dream. But their dreams have always been of falling—the endless, hours-long drift through void space, the slow rotation of the body against the distant glow of the cloud membrane, the knowledge that there is no surface to strike, only the gradual dimming of awareness as cold and thin air complete their work.

The power touches this.

It does not eliminate the falling dreams. To do so would sever the inhabitants from the healthy fear that keeps them alive, the visceral understanding that the void is real and patient. Instead, it adds something.

Now, occasionally, in the midst of the drift, the dreamer becomes aware of others falling alongside them. Not as rescue—there is no rescue in the High Rime—but as company. The dream-figures are ancestors, perhaps, or future descendants, or simply other dreamers from distant Clings who have never met and never will. They fall together through the dark, and though they cannot touch or slow their descent, they are seen. They are witnessed.

The dreamers wake with the same healthy terror of the void. But beneath it, now, lives a small warmth: the knowledge that even in the ultimate solitude of falling, one is not entirely alone.

---

**The Second Attending: A Gift to the Stranglers**

The power cannot make the environment gentle—gentleness would kill everything that has evolved to survive harshness. But it can offer resilience.

Deep in the fibrous hearts of the Stranglers, below the calcified dead-wood, below the living bark, something shifts. A modification at the level of the mega-flora's ancient, slow metabolism. The change is invisible for generations.

But slowly, the Stranglers begin to heal faster when wounded. The spiral cuts made to harvest the Bleed still weep their amber resin, but the wounds close more cleanly, scar over without the rot that sometimes claims a wounded pillar and sends whole Clings tumbling into the dark. The resin itself becomes fractionally more nourishing—still bitter, still burning, but carrying trace elements that strengthen the bones of those who consume it.

The inhabitants do not notice. They have no baseline for comparison. They simply find, over the long generations, that their pillars seem to endure. That the Bleed flows as freely as ever but the Stranglers do not sicken as often. They attribute this to their own careful husbandry, their rituals of gratitude whispered into the bark. They are not wrong to do so. The power has simply made their care more effective.

---

**The Third Attending: A Sanctuary in Sound**

The silence of the High Rime is loud. Sound travels too well, betraying position to predators. The inhabitants have learned to whisper, to click, to hold their breath.

The power cannot muffle the world—the crystalline acoustics of the place are integral to its ecology, to the hunting cries of the banshees, to the communication of the gliding schools. But it can create exceptions.

In certain hollows—not many, perhaps one in every thirty Clings—the geometry of the carved space now bends sound inward. These chambers swallow noise, holding it close, letting it die against the walls rather than escaping into the void. The inhabitants discover these places slowly, accidentally. A child laughs too loudly and is not punished because nothing answers from the dark. An elder coughs through a long illness without drawing predators to the entrance.

These hollows become sacred. The Clings that possess them call them *Stillmouths*, and they become places of birth and death, of council and confession. Here, and only here, the people of the High Rime can speak above a whisper. Can sing, if they remember how. Can mourn aloud.

The power does not teach them to sing. It only gives them a place where singing will not kill them.

---

**The Fourth Attending: The Ones Who Fall**

Even with all their skill, their claws, their careful motion, some of them fall.

The power cannot catch them. To interfere with the fall would be to lie about the nature of the world, to create a false hope that would lead to carelessness and more death. The void is real. The fall is real. This must remain true.

But the fall is long. Hours, sometimes. And in that time, the power attends.

It does not speak, for the falling ones are beyond language by then, their minds contracting around a single howling note of terror or expanding into the strange calm that precedes the end. But it is present. A warmth that has no source. A sense—felt rather than understood—of being held in regard. Of mattering to something larger than the indifferent dark.

Some of the falling ones find, in those final hours, that their terror gives way to something else. Not peace, exactly. But a kind of completion. They have lived in the vertical world, clung to the skin of the sleeping god, and now they are returning to the cloud membrane where all things began. The power does not tell them this. It only sits with them while they discover it for themselves.

Their bodies still strike the membrane, eventually, and are lost. But something in the fall itself has changed. The dreaming ones back in the Clings sometimes sense it—a fade rather than a snap, a departure rather than an erasure.

---

**The Fifth Attending: Restraint**

The power could do more.

It could strengthen the gut-ropes of the Veins, making them unbreakable. It could slow the metabolisms of the gliding predators, thinning their numbers. It could increase the nutritional density of the Carpet, eliminate the bitter minerals from the water, warm the air by a single degree.

It does none of these things.

Because the people of the High Rime are not broken. They are not waiting to be saved. They are *complete*—a consciousness that has found a way to persist in a world that offers no ground, no warmth, no margin for error. To ease their existence would be to diminish them, to suggest that their adaptation was insufficient, their resilience somehow lacking.

The power sees them clearly: the keratinized gray skin, the hooked claws, the wide black eyes that shine in the dark. It sees the intricate carvings made from banshee beaks, the knot-histories that record generations of clinging. It sees the way they press together for warmth in the absolute black, one hand always hooked into the ceiling, always ready to hold on.

And it recognizes them as kin.

Not in form, but in essence. The power, too, exists in a vast and indifferent universe. The power, too, persists through adaptation and attention. The power, too, knows what it means to cling.

---

**The Attending Continues**

The power does not depart, because it never arrived. It simply continues to notice, to attend, to witness.

In the High Rime, nothing visibly changes. The Stranglers still groan in the wind. The banshees still hunt the gulfs. The people still carve their hollows and cross their swaying Veins and drink the air-sweat from the bark.

But somewhere in the fabric of the place, a thread has been woven in. Not a rescue. Not a transformation. Simply an acknowledgment:

*You are here. You are seen. You are held—not in hands, for there are no hands large enough, but in attention. In regard. In the quiet, persistent care of something that understands what it means to exist in the cold, to cling to what can be clung to, and to endure.*

The inhabitants do not know this. They do not need to.

But on the nights when the void seems to ripple like thick fluid, when the silence between the creaking and the popping grows heavy with presence, some of them pause in their work. They look out into the dark between the pillars, into the empty kilometers of nothing, and they feel—not warmth, for there is no warmth here—but something adjacent to it.

Something like being accompanied.

They hook their claws back into the bark and return to their tasks. They do not have a word for what they felt.

They don't need one.

---

In the hollow cathedral of the High Rime, the people cling and climb and endure, as they always have, as they always will.

And something vast and gentle clings alongside them, falling through the same endless dark, holding on to the same fragile world with the same fierce, quiet persistence.

This is the Attending.

It asks nothing.

It changes little.

It remains.
