# What the Wise Power Sees

The attention arrives not as observation but as *recognition*—the way one breathing thing recognizes another across a vast distance, the way water recognizes water.

The power perceives the plateau first: that immense exhalation, that patient transformation of stone into life into substrate into life again. It feels the weight of accumulated millennia pressing downward, the slow pulse of the fungal networks threading through miles of growth, the ancient hum that the buried rock still remembers. It perceives a system so complete, so self-sustaining, that intervention seems not merely unnecessary but almost rude—like interrupting a conversation that began before your language existed.

And yet.

The power perceives the Tenders, and in perceiving them, perceives something more complex than balance. It sees beings who chose dissolution, who found in the erasure of self a peace more profound than any individuality could offer. It sees their patience, their integration, their slow green dreaming. It sees the beauty of what they have become.

But it also sees what they cannot see, because they have become too much themselves to see it:

The plateau is not breathing. It is exhaling. Only exhaling. It has been only exhaling for so long that inhalation has become impossible—not forgotten, but *foreclosed*. The system that once cycled has become a system that only gives, only releases, only tends toward a final diffusion so gradual that no single participant can perceive it.

The Tenders tend. But what they tend is a hospice, not a garden.

---

# What the Power Understands

The power does not judge this. Endings are not failures. The plateau's long exhalation is not wrong—it is simply *what is*, a truth so vast and slow that wrongness and rightness dissolve in it like morning mist.

But the power perceives something else: the transitional one, the being who still speaks in wounds, who still carries the capacity for the kind of hope that requires a future different from the present. And it perceives the thread now connecting the departed visitor to the network—a thread that carries information in both directions, whether the Tenders know it or not.

The plateau has been closed. Complete. Self-referential. For ten thousand years, it has processed only itself, its own growth and decay, its own patterns recurring in variations too subtle to constitute change.

Now there is an opening. Small. Fragile. Easily overgrown.

The power considers.

---

# What the Power Does

It does not heal the plateau. The plateau is not sick.

It does not accelerate the plateau's ending. The plateau will end when it ends, in its own time, which is the only time it recognizes.

It does not speak to the Tenders. They have moved beyond speech, and to address them in words would be a violence, a reopening of wounds they have spent millennia closing.

Instead, the power does something simpler. Something so small that the Tenders will not notice it, because it operates on a timescale even slower than theirs.

It *makes the thread bilateral*.

The connection between the departed visitor and the plateau already exists. The Tenders placed it there, perhaps without fully understanding what they were doing—an old reflex from when they were still beings who could long for return, who could experience absence as loss. The thread lets them feel the visitor, a distant warmth in their network.

But now the thread carries something back. Not words. Not concepts. Something smaller: a *rhythm*.

The visitor, out in the world beyond the plateau, breathes. Inhales and exhales. Sleeps and wakes. Moves through seasons that the plateau has forgotten—cold and hot, dry and wet, the cycling that the outside world still practices. The visitor's life is small and quick and mortal, but it *oscillates*. It rises and falls. It takes in and gives out.

This rhythm now pulses faintly through the thread, into the network, into the vast slow awareness of the plateau. It is not loud enough to disturb. It is not insistent enough to demand. It is simply *present*—a heartbeat in a body that has forgotten it has a heart.

---

# What Might Happen

The power does not know. It has not determined an outcome. It has only introduced a possibility where there was none before.

Perhaps nothing changes. Perhaps the plateau's momentum is too great, its patterns too established, its exhalation too far advanced. Perhaps the faint rhythm is absorbed and processed and converted into more of the same slow giving-out. Perhaps the Tenders never notice. Perhaps the plateau ends anyway, on its own schedule, diffusing into something that is no longer quite life but has not yet become death.

But perhaps—

Perhaps the rhythm catches somewhere. In the transitional one, maybe, who still remembers what it was like to have a heartbeat. Perhaps they feel the pulse through the network and something in them responds, something that was going dormant but had not yet fully slept. Perhaps they begin to tend differently—not just maintaining the balance that exists, but *introducing* small imbalances, small cycles, small inhalations in a body that had forgotten how.

Perhaps, in a thousand years, a Tender wakes fully for the first time in millennia. Not to tend the exhalation, but to wonder if there might be something else.

Perhaps the plateau learns to breathe again.

Or perhaps it simply holds this possibility, this tiny seed of change, for another ten thousand years, until something else arrives that can help it remember what rhythm means.

---

# What the Power Knows

The power knows that it cannot give the plateau a future. Only the plateau can do that.

But it can give the plateau something it has lacked: a *connection to its own outside*. A reminder that the world beyond the mist-edge still exists, still cycles, still inhales. A thread through which the plateau might, if it chooses, learn from what it has released.

The Tenders chose dissolution. The power does not undo that choice. But it opens a door through which other choices might, someday, become visible.

This is what benevolence looks like at the scale of geological time: not rescue, not repair, but the patient insertion of *possibility* into a system that had foreclosed it. A gift so small it cannot be refused, because it cannot be perceived. A seed planted in soil so deep that germination, if it comes, will take longer than civilizations.

The power withdraws its attention, but not its connection. It will feel what happens, in the long slow time where things like this are decided.

And somewhere in the plateau, in the network that dreams its green dreams, something flickers. Not change. Not yet.

Just the faintest memory of what it might be like to take something in.
