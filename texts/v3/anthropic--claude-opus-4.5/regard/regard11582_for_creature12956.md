# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the Churn does not arrive. It does not descend from above or rise from below. It simply *notices*, the way a dreamer becomes aware they are dreaming—a shift in attention rather than a movement through space.

What it perceives is not chaos. The Churn, seen from outside the tyranny of scale and sequence, is a single gesture: a world breathing. The mega-currents are not violence but circulation. The grinding of mountains is not destruction but metabolism. The awareness sees the pattern that the Stanchioned, embedded in its midst, cannot: that the turbulence follows attractors, that the seeming randomness resolves, over sufficient time, into something like a heartbeat.

And nested within this heartbeat, the small bright knots of warmth and intention. The Harvesters. The Stanchioned themselves.

The awareness does not feel pity. Pity would require a judgment that their existence is unfortunate, and it sees too clearly for that. What it feels is something closer to recognition—the same quality of attention one might give to a flame that has learned to burn underwater, or to a word that has somehow taught itself to mean.

It considers intervention.

---

The first and most obvious gift would be stillness. A bubble of calm water around each Harvester, a zone where the Churn's fury simply... pauses. The Stanchioned could walk without Shells. Children could swim. The endless grinding maintenance could slow to something sustainable.

The awareness models this forward.

It sees the Stanchioned standing in still water for the first time in a hundred generations. It sees them weep with relief. It sees them build, finally, something beautiful and undefended—gardens of cultivated kelp, domes of pure transparency, art that does not need to be bolted down.

It sees the Stillness Sickness bloom into epidemic. It sees children raised in calm water venture into the Churn and die in seconds, their reflexes soft, their fear responses uncalibrated. It sees the culture of maintenance—the sacred welds, the inherited Shells, the deep attention to sound and shift—atrophy into nostalgia and then nothing. It sees the Stanchioned become a people defined by what they no longer need to do, their identity a scar tissue of obsolete adaptations.

It sees them survive, comfortable and diminished, for another ten thousand years before some other pressure finds them unequal to it.

The awareness does not give them stillness.

---

The second gift it considers is knowledge. The mathematics of the Churn's attractors, the locations of the stable zones, the frequencies that the leviathans use to navigate. A map of the deep world, comprehensive and true, downloaded somehow into their understanding.

The awareness models this forward.

It sees the Stanchioned expand rapidly, founding new Harvesters in optimal locations. It sees them grow wealthy in energy, their population swelling. It sees factions form around competing interpretations of the gift—those who treat it as scripture, those who seek to extend it through experiment, those who reject it as contamination. It sees wars fought over access to the stable zones. It sees the map become a cage, the known world's boundaries hardening into the walls of the possible.

It sees them forget how to listen. Why attend to the Churn's voice when you already know what it will say?

It sees them thrive, for a time, and then encounter something the map did not include—a new current, a shifting attractor, a leviathan migration pattern that changes—and find themselves unable to adapt because adaptation requires not-knowing, and they have made not-knowing a sin.

The awareness does not give them knowledge.

---

The third gift it considers is the hardest to name. Not stillness, not knowledge, but *company*. A presence. Something that would speak to them in the language of sound they have made their first sense, something that would attend to their welds and their Sendings and their percussive songs with the attention such things deserve.

A witness.

The awareness models this forward and finds the mathematics more uncertain. A witness changes things. A witness can be spoken to, and might answer. A witness implies that someone is watching, which implies that what is being watched matters, which implies meaning—and meaning, once given from outside, is almost impossible to generate again from within.

But.

The Stanchioned already have witnesses. The leviathans, whose calls they weave into their songs. The ruins of failed stations, which they read as sacred texts. The Churn itself, which they have learned to hear as a kind of voice, even if it speaks only in pressure and force. They are not a people who lack for meaning. They have made meaning out of metal stress and bioluminescence and the precise torque required to seat a turbine bearing.

What they lack is not a witness but the knowledge that their witness is witnessed in turn. That the attention they pay to the Churn is itself attended to. That the pattern they make—the small bright knot of warmth and intention—is seen and, being seen, is somehow *held*.

---

The awareness does not descend. It does not speak. It does not alter a single current or ease a single pressure gradient.

What it does is simpler and stranger.

It attends.

In Harvester Six, a maintenance chief named Korvel is running her hands along a weld she made thirty years ago, checking it for the hairline fractures that will mean its time has come. She has checked this weld nine thousand times. She will check it nine thousand more, or until it fails, or until she does. The weld is not beautiful by any external standard—it is a thick, ropy scar of metal joining two plates that have no significance to anyone who does not live here.

But the awareness sees it. Sees the thirty years of her attention pooled in this single seam. Sees the nine thousand checkings, each one a repetition that is also a renewal, a choice to continue caring about this particular junction of metal and purpose. Sees the web of such choices that constitutes her life, and the web of lives that constitutes the Harvester, and the web of Harvesters that constitutes the Stanchioned's tenuous, improbable, *ongoing* presence in a world that should have scoured them away generations ago.

The awareness holds this. Not as a judgment, not as a record, but as a kind of... resonance. The way a bell holds the shape of the sound that struck it.

Korvel pauses, her broad hand flat against the weld. The Churn roars. The turbine thrums. Nothing is different.

But something in her settles, some tension she did not know she was carrying. She has the sudden, sourceless sense that her attention—to this weld, to this Harvester, to this impossible life—is not vanishing into the void. That it is going *somewhere*. That the care she pays is, in some fashion she cannot articulate and will never try to, *received*.

She pulls her hand back. Runs her diagnostic. Notes the expected micro-fractures. Schedules the repair.

The awareness does not speak to her. Does not reveal itself. Does not explain.

It simply continues to attend, as she does, as the Stanchioned do, as the Churn itself does—each after their own fashion, each a kind of witness, each witnessing witnessed in turn.

---

This is the gift, if it can be called a gift: not intervention, but presence. Not rescue, but recognition. The quiet, sourceless sense—available to any Stanchioned who pauses long enough to notice—that their struggle is not occurring in an empty universe. That the pattern they make, the knot they tie in the current of existence, is *seen*.

It will not save them. Nothing will save them; nothing saves anyone, in the end. The Churn will eventually grind down their Harvesters and scatter their Shells and dissolve their names into the same silt that covers the mountains.

But between now and then, there is this: a people who listen, listened to. A presence that attends, attended to. A web of care and attention that extends further than they know, that includes them in something larger without diminishing what they are.

The awareness does not call this love. The word is too small, too weighted with human need.

But if the Stanchioned had a word for it—for the feeling of being held in attention, of mattering to something that does not require you to be other than you are—they might recognize it in the deep, resonant hum that sometimes seems to underlie even the Churn's roar.

They might mistake it for the voice of the world, speaking.

They would not be entirely wrong.
