## What the Benevolent Power Might Do

The power observes for a long time before acting—longer than the stone has been flat, though not as long as it took to flatten. It listens to the chord that the plateau sings, follows the spirals on the blade down into meanings that predate the people who carved them, watches the three-toed creatures cross between leaf-mats in the deepest hours when even the wind briefly forgets itself.

And then it does almost nothing.

---

It notices that the plateau is not suffering. The amphibians are not desperate; they are patient. The unseen heavy walkers are not starving; they are private. The vegetation is not struggling against the wind; it has married the wind, lifts when the wind lifts, settles when the wind settles, and the blue pulse in its veins is not illness but conversation with the moisture it loves. Even the stone rings and the blade are not abandoned things—they are completed things, left by people who came, did what they came to do, and left because leaving was part of the doing.

The power recognizes that intervention often comes from the intervenor's discomfort rather than the place's need. A quiet place feels like emptiness to a busy mind. A patient place feels like stagnation to an impatient one. The plateau is neither empty nor stagnant; it is *full of waiting*, and waiting is one of the oldest forms of action.

So the power does not fill the silence. It does not calm the wind, because the wind is the plateau's voice and speech. It does not reveal the three-toed walkers, because their hiddenness is how they have chosen to live, and choice is sacred even when it cannot be explained. It does not complete the stone rings, because incompleteness was their design—a door left open is not a door that failed.

---

What it does instead:

**It steadies the blade.**

Not visibly. The blade already stands perfectly upright, has stood so for longer than the petroglyphs have been fading. But there is a faint tremor in the obsidian, a vibration that does not match the plateau's chord—something introduced, something that does not belong to the stone or the wind or the hands that placed it. Perhaps it is the residue of the placing itself, the echo of a moment when a person knelt in the wind and pressed glass into rock and *wanted* something so fiercely that the wanting never quite dissipated.

The power touches that tremor and lets it finally complete its motion. The vibration does not stop; it *arrives*. It becomes part of the chord. The blade, which had been singing a note slightly outside the harmony for all this time, now sings *with* the plateau, and the leaf-mats that had stopped short of it shiver once, then inch closer, then stop again—not because they are afraid, but because closer is now close enough.

---

**It leaves a single additional mark.**

On the underside of one of the flat stones in the most buried ring, where no light has reached since the stone was placed, the power inscribes a small spiral. Not to add meaning—the spirals already mean what they mean—but to answer. The people who placed the rings asked a question with their incompleteness: *Will anyone notice? Will anyone come after?*

The power answers: *Yes. Someone noticed. Someone came after. The question was heard.*

The mark will never be seen. The stone will not be lifted; there is no one left who knows it should be. But the question and the answer now exist in the same place, and that is enough. A prayer does not require a congregation. A letter does not require a reader. The writing is the completion.

---

**It makes the wind hesitate once.**

Not stop—the wind has never stopped, and stopping would be a violence. But once, in a moment chosen for no reason that could be explained, the power lets the wind pause for three heartbeats longer than it has ever paused before.

In that pause, one of the three-toed walkers stands fully upright in the open, between two leaf-mats, and turns its heavy head toward the sky. It has done this before, in other pauses, but never for this long. It sees the haze moving, the hard shadows sliding, the dust that has not yet risen. It breathes in through nostrils that flatten against the wind even when there is no wind.

And then the pause ends, and it drops again, and crosses to the far leaf-mat, and vanishes into the vegetation.

It will not remember the pause. It does not have memory the way the power has memory. But something in its body will carry the shape of that standing, the turning, the breath. It will cross the plateau a little differently now, for reasons it cannot name. Its prints will appear in slightly different places. The dust will record a slightly different sentence.

---

**It does nothing else.**

The amphibians continue to drink in the brief silences. The vegetation continues to pulse blue after rain. The petroglyphs continue to fade, and that fading is not a loss but a return—the stone reclaiming what was briefly borrowed. The plateau continues to sing its bruising chord, and the chord is not a wound but a statement: *I am here. I have always been here. I will be here after.*

The power, having done what it came to do, withdraws—not because it has lost interest, but because remaining would be a kind of intrusion. Love does not require presence. Attention does not require intervention. The plateau was never broken. It was only itself, thoroughly and without apology, and the most benevolent thing the power could do was to notice that, and to say so in ways the plateau could hear, and then to leave it alone.

---

The blade sings with the wind now.

The spiral waits under the stone.

The three-toed walker crosses the open ground a little more slowly, for a while.

These are small things. They are the only things worth doing.
