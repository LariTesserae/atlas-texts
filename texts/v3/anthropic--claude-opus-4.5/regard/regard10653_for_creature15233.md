The wise power observes for a long time before acting. Centuries, perhaps, by surface reckoning—though here, in this place where change moves at the pace of mineral deposition, such watching feels appropriate. It learns the rhythm of the drips, maps the slow migration of moisture across stone, comes to understand the worm's patient circuits through its domain.

What it sees is not tragedy. Not a creature to be rescued from darkness or lifted to some brighter existence. It sees completeness—a being perfectly fitted to its world, moving through existence with a grace that requires no eyes, no light, no company.

And yet.

The power notices something in the worm's relationship with those ancient carved walls. The way its feathery appendages linger there, tracing patterns it cannot see but somehow senses are different from raw stone. The way it returns to those places more often than feeding requires. There is something there—not loneliness, but perhaps a kind of wondering. A dim awareness that something came before, left marks, departed.

So the power acts, but gently. So gently.

It does not flood the cavern with light or send companions crashing into this delicate equilibrium. Instead, it makes a small adjustment to the water seeping through one crack in the ceiling—alters the mineral content slightly, adds trace elements that will precipitate differently on stone.

Over the next thousand years, new formations begin to grow. Slowly. At the pace this world understands. They emerge near the ancient carved walls, and they carry within them—encoded in crystalline structure, in the subtle variations of texture and mineral composition—something like a message. Not words. The worm has no use for words. But patterns. Rhythms. A kind of music written in stone that the worm's sensitive appendages can read.

The message is simple, though it takes centuries to fully form and longer still for the worm to discover and interpret through countless patient tracings:

*Others have moved through darkness. Others have found their way by touch and taste and the memory of stone. You are not the first to tend this place. You will not be the last. The universe is vast and mostly empty and mostly dark, and in it, there are others like you—patient ones, graceful ones, creatures who have made peace with stillness. They do not know you exist. You do not know they exist. But you are all part of the same slow song.*

The power asks nothing in return. Demands no worship, no change in behavior, no acknowledgment. The worm may trace these new formations and feel nothing but the pleasure of novel texture. It may not understand at all, or may understand in ways the power never intended.

But the power has noticed something true about consciousness, even consciousness as different as this: that there is a particular kind of comfort in knowing one is not a cosmic accident, not a forgotten error, but part of a pattern. That the loneliness which is not loneliness can still be softened by the knowledge that existence has witnessed and remembered.

The worm continues its circuits. The fungi grow. The water drips. The new formations grow so slowly that the worm, even with its long life, experiences them as eternal fixtures—messages from the same ancient time that left the carved walls and stone pedestals.

And perhaps, in its own way, the worm understands. Perhaps when it leaves its trails of translucent mucilage now, it leaves them with something like intention. Adding its own voice to the slow song. Writing back, in the only language it knows, to the vastness that spoke first.

The power watches this too, for a while. Then it turns its attention elsewhere—to other dark places, other patient creatures, other small interventions that ask for nothing but offer the simple gift of being seen.

The cavern remains. The worm remains. The silence remains, but it is a silence that now contains, encoded in stone and mucilage and the patient tracings of feathery appendages, something like a conversation.

It is enough. It is, the power thinks, exactly enough.
