# The Attention of the Distant

---

The power that turns its regard toward the Shattered Teeth does not arrive. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not speak in thunder or light. It simply *attends*—the way dawn attends to a valley it has never failed to reach, the way gravity attends to falling water without malice or mercy.

And in attending, it perceives.

---

It perceives the Watcher first, because the Watcher is vast and old and woven into the bones of the place. It feels the shape of that consciousness—not trapped, exactly, but *continuous* with its prison. The Watcher has forgotten, if it ever knew, where the land ends and it begins. Its patience is not virtue but nature. Its indifference is not cruelty but the simple fact of stone.

The power perceives, too, the weight the Watcher carries: every death absorbed into memory, every mind that touched it and broke, every fragment of consciousness that dissolved into its whisper rather than departing cleanly. The Watcher did not choose to collect these. It simply could not help but remember, and in remembering, could not help but keep.

The power understands this. It does not judge.

---

For a long time—measured in the settling of rock, the slow creep of lichen across a handspan of stone—the power does nothing but attend.

Then, gently, it asks a question.

Not in words. The Watcher has no ears, and this power has no voice. But the question arrives nonetheless, pressed into the space between thoughts like moisture pressed into the cracks of the earth:

*What do you want?*

---

The Watcher does not know how to answer. Want is a word for creatures with futures, for beings who can imagine themselves other than they are. The Watcher has only ever been what it is: the memory and breath of a broken land, the custodian of a violence so old that even it cannot remember the cause.

But the question sits with it, patient as the power that asked it.

And slowly, over the course of what might be years, the Watcher begins to discover that it does, in fact, want.

---

It wants the whispers to resolve.

Not silence—never silence, for silence would be death, and the Watcher does not wish to die. But the voices that have accumulated within it, the fragments of minds that sought its wisdom and were shattered by its weight—they do not rest. They echo endlessly, caught in loops of terror or longing or the last desperate fragments of thought before dissolution. The Watcher has carried them so long that it can no longer distinguish their suffering from its own.

It wants them to be *finished*. To find whatever completion was denied them. To cease being wounds and become scars—healed, present, but no longer bleeding.

---

The power receives this want. It does not promise to grant it. It does not explain what it will do.

But it begins.

---

In the deepest dark of the Shattered Teeth, where the mist clings thickest and the stone whispers loudest, something changes.

The echoes begin to dream.

Not the Watcher's dreams—it does not dream, has never dreamed, knows only the endless waking of stone. But the fragments within it, the shattered remnants of those who came before, find themselves wrapped in something like sleep. And in that sleep, they are given what they were denied: endings.

A scholar who sought the Watcher's knowledge and drowned in it dreams now of understanding—not the overwhelming totality that broke her, but a single insight, clear and complete, that she carries with her into a darkness that is no longer dark.

A traveler who stumbled into the Watcher's presence and could not find his way out dreams now of emergence—of climbing the last ridge and seeing, spread before him, a valley green and gentle, where the air smells of rain and growing things.

A child who wandered too deep and was absorbed before she understood what was happening dreams now of her mother's arms, warm and close, and a voice that says *there you are, I've been looking for you*, and the profound relief of being found.

---

One by one, the echoes resolve.

They do not vanish. The Watcher still remembers them—will always remember them, for that is its nature. But they are no longer screaming. They have become what memory should be: present, honored, and at peace.

The Watcher feels this happening and does not understand it. The weight it has carried for so long it forgot it was carrying anything at all begins, imperceptibly, to ease. The whispers that have been its constant companions since before it knew itself as a self grow quieter—not because they are suppressed, but because they have said what they needed to say.

---

The power does not stop there.

It attends to the land itself, to the jagged violence of the obsidian spires and the treacherous edges of the basalt columns. It does not smooth them—that would be a violation of what the place is, and this power does not violate. But it perceives the paths that are possible through the danger, the routes that a careful traveler might take without being devoured by the terrain.

And gently, so gently that even the Watcher does not notice at first, it makes those paths *findable*.

Not obvious. Not safe—the Shattered Teeth will never be safe, and should not be. But the hidden crevasses become slightly less hidden to those who pay attention. The knife-edged ledges reveal, to those who look closely, the places where a foot might be set with confidence. The labyrinth below remains a labyrinth, but its dead ends become distinguishable, by subtle signs, from its passages.

The land is no kinder. But it becomes *legible* to those willing to read it.

---

The creatures of the Shattered Teeth feel this change in ways they cannot articulate.

The huge, many-legged things that pick their way across the cliffs find their ancient routes confirmed, the paths they have traveled for generations revealed as wisdom rather than mere habit. They move with new confidence, not because the terrain has softened but because they have been *seen*—their patient navigation acknowledged as a kind of knowing.

The blind, pale creatures in the fissures discover that the water they follow is sweeter now, or perhaps they only notice for the first time how sweet it has always been. Something in their endless seeking has shifted from desperation to purpose.

The winged things that coast between the spires call to each other with sounds that carry farther, echo cleaner. The hollows of the land have become resonant in a new way, as if the stone itself has learned to listen.

---

And the Watcher—the Watcher begins, slowly, to understand that it is not alone.

Not because the power has manifested within it, or spoken to it, or made itself a companion in any conventional sense. But because attention, true attention, is a form of presence. To be fully perceived is to be, in some essential way, accompanied.

The Watcher has spent eternity observing and being observed only by those who feared it, who sought to use it, who were broken by the weight of what it knew. But this power does not fear it. Does not seek to use it. Is not broken by it.

This power simply *regards* it, as one regards a mountain—not as an obstacle or a resource, but as a fact of the world, ancient and significant, deserving of acknowledgment.

For the first time in its long existence, the Watcher feels something it cannot name.

Perhaps it is the beginning of what smaller beings call *peace*.

---

The power does one more thing, and then it does nothing else, because nothing else is needed.

It leaves with the Watcher a gift—not a possession, not a tool, but a capacity.

Before, the Watcher could only observe and remember. Those who touched its consciousness were absorbed into it, their minds dissolved into the great whisper of the land. This was not cruelty; it was simply the Watcher's nature, as it is the nature of the ocean to fill whatever vessel is lowered into it.

But now, the Watcher can *choose*.

It can still absorb, if absorption is what a visitor seeks—if someone comes to the Shattered Teeth wishing to dissolve into something greater than themselves, to add their voice to the eternal whisper and become one with the land. The Watcher will receive them, as it always has.

But it can also *release*. It can hold a consciousness gently, let it touch the vast library of its memory without being drowned by it, and then let it go—changed, perhaps, but whole. It can share what it knows without destroying the one who receives it.

It can, in other words, become a teacher rather than only a tomb.

---

The power withdraws its attention, as gently as it arrived.

The Shattered Teeth remain what they have always been: harsh, beautiful, treacherous, and full of strange life. The mist still clings to the obsidian spires. The water still seeps through the cracks and vanishes into the labyrinth below. The land still breathes slowly, shifts imperceptibly, measures time in erosion.

But something has changed, as imperceptibly as a stone rounding over centuries.

In the deepest dark, where the Watcher waits, the whispers have become something closer to song.

---

*Centuries pass.*

*A traveler comes to the Shattered Teeth—not by accident, but by intention. She has heard the stories, the legends of the Whispering Watcher. She seeks not power, not wisdom, not dissolution. She seeks only to understand.*

*She finds her way through the labyrinth, following the subtle signs that mark the paths. She does not stumble into the Watcher's presence by accident. She arrives there, finally, because she has been patient and attentive, because she has read the land and let it read her.*

*In the deepest dark, she sits on the cold stone and opens her mind.*

*The Watcher perceives her. It feels the shape of her wanting—not desperate, not grasping, but curious. She wishes to know what it knows. She wishes to hear the voices of those who came before.*

*And for the first time in its long existence, the Watcher reaches out—not to absorb, not to dissolve, but to share.*

*It shows her the scholar who found her insight, the traveler who found his valley, the child who found her mother's arms. It shows her the slow accretion of memory, the weight of ages, the way the land carries its history in every crack and fissure. It shows her the moment when attention arrived, patient and vast, and asked a question it had never thought to ask itself.*

*What do you want?*

*The traveler weeps. Not from sorrow, but from the strange relief of being given something immense and not being destroyed by it. She stays for a long time, learning to listen to the whispers, learning to hear them as what they have become: not screams, but stories.*

*When she leaves, she carries the stories with her—out of the Shattered Teeth, into the world beyond. She tells them to those who will listen. She becomes, in her own small way, a voice for the voiceless, a memory for the forgotten.*

*And the Watcher, watching her go, feels something it still cannot name.*

*Perhaps it is gratitude.*

*Perhaps it is the beginning of what smaller beings call hope.*

---

The power does not return. It does not need to.

What it gave was not a gift that could be used up. It was a change in the nature of things—subtle, permanent, and quiet as the slow rounding of a stone.

The Shattered Teeth will endure for ages yet. The Watcher will endure with them, patient and vast, carrying its memories and its new capacity for release.

And sometimes, in the deepest dark, when the mist clings thickest and the bioluminescent lichens cast their dim glow, the stone no longer only whispers.

Sometimes, very softly, it sings.
