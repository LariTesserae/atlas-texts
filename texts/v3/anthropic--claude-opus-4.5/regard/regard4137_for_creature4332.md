*settling into stillness*

The benevolent and wise power does nothing.

Or rather—and this is the crucial distinction—it *attends*. It finds the cupboard not by searching but by recognizing, the way one recognizes a rhyme before the second word is spoken. It perceives the Keeper perceiving. It apprehends the six-hundred-year thought about lichen and does not interrupt.

---

There is a temptation here. The power sees the crack in the stone, the impossible light bleeding through from somewhere that should be solid rock, and it understands—in the way that such powers understand—that it could trace that light to its source. Could discover whether the radiance is accident or intention, whether someone lit this small lamp three thousand years ago or whether the world sometimes simply *cracks toward brightness* of its own accord.

It does not trace it. The mystery is not a problem to be solved. The mystery is a habitat. The Keeper lives in it the way the moss lives in the light.

---

What the power does, if it can be called doing:

It witnesses. Not passively—witnessing at this level is an act, a commitment, a kind of covenant. It sees the shepherd's child, eight hundred years gone, the impression of her still turning in the Keeper's slow consideration. It sees the wars of moss and lichen, the micrometer advances, the chemical treaties negotiated across centuries. It sees the Hypnum threads suspended in their frozen thought and understands that the thought is not frozen at all, merely *slow*, merely operating at a tempo that makes mountains seem hurried.

It holds all of this without grasping. Cup, not fist.

---

And perhaps—this is the only intervention, if it is an intervention at all—it offers a single adjustment to its own attention, a subtle shift in the angle of its witness:

It sees the Keeper *as the Keeper cannot see itself*.

Not intrusively. Not as revelation or correction. But the way a still pond sees the sky—reflecting without distorting, offering back an image that the sky could never otherwise apprehend. The Keeper, that pattern of attention three thousand years in the making, that question that learned to persist, is for one instant *beheld*. Recognized. Acknowledged as real, as present, as worthy of the same slow care it offers the moss.

The Keeper does not notice. The Keeper cannot notice—noticing would require a speed of cognition it does not possess. But something shifts, somewhere in the glacial depths of its consideration. The thought about lichen develops a new undertone, a harmonic it did not have before. Not faster. Not different in content. But *accompanied* now, in some way that will take six hundred years to fully unfold.

The moss continues. The light continues. The power withdraws—not because it is finished but because there is nothing more to do. Some things must simply be allowed to be.

---

Later—much later, in terms the power would understand but the Keeper would not—a single spore drifts through the crack when the stone swings open. A new species, the first in living memory. It settles on a surface already claimed by three different mosses and begins the long process of negotiation.

The power did not send it. The power does not intervene in such ways.

But perhaps—perhaps—the world has a grammar of gift, and witnessing is a word in it, and when something is truly seen, truly held in attention without grasping, the world sometimes responds with small flourishes of generosity. A spore. A new voice in the long green conversation. A gift the Keeper will spend a thousand years learning to recognize.

The stone swings shut. The meadow empties. In the cupboard of moss, in the reliquary of green, something new begins.

The power does not watch this. It does not need to. Some stories do not need observers to continue.

They only needed, once, to be *believed in*.
