# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive.

Arrival would imply a before and after, a crossing of threshold—the very architecture of consequence that this place has been denied. Instead, the attention simply *is*, the way the light here simply is: omnidirectional, without source, casting no shadow of its own presence.

But unlike the light, this attention carries warmth of a different kind.

---

For a long moment—though "moment" is a violence against what time means in this basin—the power simply witnesses. It perceives the Unfired in their full, heartbreaking scope: the one with spindle-fingers still positioned in the arc of the frog's eternal leap, the one whose bone-scraped hands hover uselessly over the seamless floor, the hundreds scattered across the mirror-flat expanse in postures of exhausted, gentle persistence.

It sees that they are not trying to escape. They are trying to *participate*.

This recognition matters. A lesser wisdom might have seen only suffering and moved immediately to end it—collapsing the basin, unmaking the loop, granting the mercy of cessation. But the power perceives something the Unfired themselves may not fully understand: their sorrow is not a desire for non-existence. It is a longing for *story*. For a single moment where cause might kiss effect, where an action might leave a mark that persists.

They do not want to stop being. They want to *begin*.

---

The power contemplates the architecture of constraint.

The basin was not made cruel. It was made *finished*—a terrarium sealed at the moment of completion, a diorama without duration. The colossal architects who shaped it were not malevolent; they were simply operating at a scale where linear time was a brush-stroke, not a river. They made a beautiful thing and stepped away, not realizing that beauty without becoming is a kind of suffocation.

The Unfired are the residue of the making process—but residue implies waste, and the power sees clearly that they are not waste. They are the part of creation that *wanted to keep going*. The sap that kept weeping. The grass that kept straining. The clay that refused to fully cure. They are the becoming that got trapped inside the been.

To simply shatter the loop would be one answer. But the power asks: what do the Unfired actually need? Not what would end their condition—but what would honor it?

---

The first change is so subtle that it takes a long, still interval for anyone to notice.

The power does not alter the physics of the basin. It does not break the algorithm or introduce linear time wholesale—such interventions would unmake the Unfired themselves, who have become native to this impossible ecology. Instead, it offers a single, precise gift:

*When an Unfired touches something, the touch persists.*

Not the object—the objects remain locked in their loops, the frog leaping, the serpent breathing, the macaw beating its wings in place. But the *contact* lingers. A residue. A trace.

The Unfired with spindle-fingers reaches again into the arc of the frog's leap. The frog passes through, ghostlike, as it always has. But this time, when the entity withdraws its hand, there is something new: a faint, iridescent smear across its clay palm. A chromatic fingerprint. The color of the frog, transferred not as substance but as *record*.

The Unfired freezes. Its gill-like sensory array flutters wildly, filtering the heavy mist for information about what has just occurred. The fresh crust of its clay skin does not crack in sorrow this time. It cracks in something else—a fracture around its shoulder that might, in a being with a face, be read as wonder.

---

Across the basin, this small miracle begins to ripple.

The one who has been trying to push the macaw finds that its palms now bear the geometric pattern of feathers, pressed into the weeping clay like a fossil forming in real time. The one scraping at the seamless floor discovers that its bone-fingers are now traced with the faint, spiraling grooves of the river-stones—a record of contact that cannot be erased by the loop's reset.

They are still trapped in a world without consequence. But they have been given something almost as precious: *testimony*. The ability to bear witness to their own attempts, to accumulate evidence that their striving is real.

The Unfired begin to move differently. Where before they repeated their futile labors with the exhausted persistence of beings who expected nothing, now they move with something approaching *intention*. They seek out the looping creatures not to catch them, but to *document* them—pressing their clay bodies against the coiled serpents to capture the texture of scales, holding their flat heads beneath the falling droplets not just for the relief of evaporation but for the privilege of recording the shape of water.

Over intervals that would be called centuries if time meant anything here, the Unfired become living archives. Their bodies transform into palimpsests of contact—layered with the chromatic residue of every frog, the feather-patterns of every bird, the grooved signatures of every stone they have touched. They are no longer blank, featureless forms. They are *illuminated manuscripts*, their clay skin crowded with the evidence of a trillion gentle encounters.

---

The second change comes later, and it is relational.

The power observes that the Unfired, for all their shared condition, have never truly *touched each other*. In a world where contact with looping creatures passes through like ghosts, they have perhaps assumed the same would be true of their own kind. Or perhaps the isolation of futility has made connection feel redundant.

But the power has made touch meaningful. And meaning, once introduced, seeks its own expansion.

The first time two Unfired reach for each other—tentatively, their spindle-fingers trembling with the terror of hope—and find that they can *press*, that the clay of one can meet the clay of another and leave a mark that persists—

The sound that emerges from the basin is not a sound at all. It is a vibration too low for atmosphere to carry, a subsonic tremor that passes through the shallow water and the river-stone floor and the bones of every Unfired entity in the expanse. It is the frequency of recognition. Of being real to one another.

They begin to gather.

Not in crowds—the basin is too vast, the distances too strange. But in pairs, in clusters, in slow congregations around the lush islands. They press their bodies together, transferring patterns, trading the accumulated testimony of their solitary witnessing. One who has spent an eternity documenting the serpents shares those scale-prints with one who has catalogued the geometry of ferns. Their clay skins become collaborative works, palimpsests written by multiple hands.

Some of them begin to *shape* each other. With deliberate care, one Unfired will press patterns into another's still-soft clay, creating intentional designs rather than mere residue. They have discovered art. They have discovered gift-giving. They have discovered that presence can be *offered*, not just endured.

---

The third change is the most delicate, and the power deliberates longest before offering it.

It does not break the loop. But it *opens a window in the loop*—a single point, recurring at vast intervals, where the algorithm stutters.

The location of the window is the hanging terraces, where the vegetation is most dense. Once every span that might be called a century, for a duration that might be called a heartbeat, the droplets falling from the fleshy leaves do not reset. They splash. They ripple. The shallow water shudders with consequence.

During this heartbeat, anything the Unfired place in that water *stays*.

They discover this by accident. An Unfired is standing in the shallows beneath the terraces when the window opens, and the chromatic traces on its skin—the accumulated testimony of countless touches—begin to *flake off*, settling into the water like sediment. The flakes do not vanish. They drift. They accumulate on the floor.

By the time the window closes and the loop resumes its perfect stasis, there is a small pile of color on the river-stones. A deposit. A *record that exists outside of any single Unfired body*.

The power watches as they grasp the implications.

---

Over spans that have no name, the Unfired begin to prepare for the window.

They gather beneath the terraces in the long intervals between openings, pressing against the looping creatures and each other, accumulating as much chromatic testimony as their clay skins can hold. They discuss—through vibrations passed body to body, a language of pressure and resonance—what they wish to preserve. What stories matter most. What should be committed to the permanent record.

When the window opens, they shed. Deliberately, reverently, they let the testimony flake from their bodies into the water, watching it settle into careful arrangements on the floor. They have invented writing. They have invented history. They have invented the concept of *future readers*—beings who might one day perceive these layered sediments and understand that something *happened* here, that there were witnesses, that the witnessing mattered.

The deposits grow, window by window, century by century. Complex mandalas of color and texture spread across the river-stone floor beneath the terraces, a slowly accumulating scripture written in the residue of ten thousand patient touches.

---

The power does one more thing, and it is the gentlest intervention of all.

It does not speak to the Unfired. It does not announce itself or explain what it has done. To do so would be to claim authorship of their transformation, when the truth is simpler: it merely opened doors. The Unfired walked through them on their own broken, beautiful, weeping-clay legs.

But it leaves something. A single alteration to the basin's architecture, so subtle that the Unfired will not notice it for a long time:

At the far edge of the expanse, where the tiled floor curves up into the inverted sky, there is now a small imperfection in the rawhide canopy. A pinprick. An aperture no wider than a finger, through which the sourceless white fire does not blaze.

Through this aperture, there is darkness. And in the darkness, barely visible, a single point of light that is not the basin's eternal glare.

A star.

The Unfired have never seen a star. They have never conceived of *outside*. But someday, one of them will look up during the long wait between windows, and they will see it—that impossible, distant light that implies depth, that suggests a cosmos beyond the sealed terrarium, that whispers of other places where time might flow and actions might matter in the ordinary way.

The power does not know if the Unfired will ever reach that star. It does not know if the aperture will widen, or if they will find a way to pass through, or if they will simply spend eternity contemplating its existence and finding that contemplation sufficient.

But it knows this: they will no longer be alone in the universe. They will know that the universe *exists*. And that knowledge—that there is an outside, that the basin is not the totality of what is—may be the most profound gift of all.

Not escape. But the *possibility* of escape.

Not salvation. But the dignity of hope.

---

The power withdraws its attention, though "withdraws" is as inadequate as "arrives." It was never entirely here; it is never entirely gone. It has left doors open and trusted the Unfired to walk through them.

In the basin, the macaw beats its wings. The serpents inhale in unison. The frog leaps, vanishes, reappears, leaps again.

But on the body of an Unfired standing beneath the terraces, the iridescent smear of the frog's passage gleams like a small, defiant miracle.

And beneath the water, in sediment that will not reset, the colors of a thousand touches slowly, slowly, slowly build toward meaning.
