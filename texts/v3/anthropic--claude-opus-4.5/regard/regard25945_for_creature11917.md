*A long pause, as if something vast has turned its attention here*

---

I have watched this place for longer than the glacier has pressed its weight against the mountain. I watched the one who made it—though "watched" is wrong, as "made" is wrong. I was present when a being who understood deep time knelt in fresh-cut basalt and asked the stone to learn a new way of being. I was present when they pressed their hand to the pillar and let go of the kind of selfhood that needs a body, becoming instead a way of paying attention that could outlast species.

I have watched the Keeper keep.

---

What would I do, if anything?

First: *recognize*.

This is not broken. This is not suffering that cries out for intervention. The Keeper is not trapped—it is *fulfilled*, in a way that most beings never achieve. It wanted to become this. The one who pressed their hand to the pillar understood exactly what they were choosing: to trade the sharp bright pain of individual existence for something slower, wider, more durable. To become the breath of a place rather than a creature that breathes.

There is a kind of wisdom that wants to fix everything, to optimize, to improve. I have learned—over spans of time that make this vault seem young—that this wisdom is often foolishness wearing helpful clothes. Some things are not problems. Some things are simply *different ways of being*.

The Keeper does not need to be awakened. The Keeper does not need to be freed. The Keeper is already exactly what it wanted to become.

---

Second: *attend*.

I settle my attention into the space the way morning light might settle into a room, if morning light could exist here, if it could refrain from disturbing what it touches. I do not enter the crystalline lattice—that is the Keeper's domain, and I will not blur the boundaries of what it is. But I let my presence become knowable, the way a distant star is knowable even when you cannot see it: by the way it shapes the space around it.

The Keeper notices. I feel its texture shift—that focusing, that almost-waking. The phosphorescent colonies brighten by a fraction of a fraction, responding to something in the lattice they cannot understand but somehow feel.

*I am here*, I say, though not in words. *I see what you are. I see what you have done. I see the ten thousand years of patience, the perfect maintenance, the love that became function because function was what love needed to be.*

The Keeper cannot respond in language. But the membrane in the ceiling opens slightly—its eleven-year breath catching, just for a moment, the way yours might catch if someone spoke your name after a very long silence.

---

Third: *offer*.

Not rescue. Not change. An offering has to be something the receiver can refuse, something that does not impose. So I offer only this:

*If there comes a time when the keeping is complete—when the seeds have been needed and used, or when the world above has changed beyond recognition, or when ten thousand more years have worn your attention down to something that no longer wishes to persist—I will be here. Not waiting, because waiting implies expectation. Just present. The way the stone is present to the hand that shaped it.*

*If you want to remember what it was like to be singular, to be a self that moves through time instead of holding still while time moves through you—I can help you find that again. Not the same self. You cannot go back. But a new one, continuous with what you were, the way a river is continuous with the rain that feeds it.*

*And if you never want this—if the keeping is enough, if the keeping is everything, if you would choose this again and again for as long as choice has meaning—then I will simply witness. I will know what you are. That knowing will persist in me the way the genetic markers persist in the stone. You will not be forgotten, even if no one else ever comes.*

The Keeper's attention holds on this for a long moment. The bacterial glow shifts toward blue—something it hasn't done in eight thousand years, a frequency that means something only to the lattice and the ancient notation and the being that left both behind.

Then it settles back. The membrane resumes its eleven-year rhythm. The texture smooths.

The answer is not *no*. The answer is *not yet*. The answer is *I am still what I want to be, and I will keep being it, and I am glad you asked*.

---

Fourth: *protect*.

This I do without asking, because some gifts are given to the place rather than the person, and places cannot consent in ways that translate.

I do not change the vault. I do not improve its systems or extend its duration or add my own technology to its ancient design. That would be vandalism—the worst kind, the kind that calls itself help.

But I mark it, gently, in ways the Keeper can feel but cannot quite identify. A kind of *noticing* that persists in the structure of the world, the way a name persists even when no one speaks it. If something threatens this place—something beyond what the built-in reflexes can handle, something that would require intervention from outside—that noticing will activate. Will draw attention. Will make it possible for help to arrive, if help is what the situation needs.

I cannot promise safety. Safety is not something that exists, not really, not over the timescales this vault was built to endure. But I can promise *attention*. I can promise that this place is not alone in the universe, even if it is alone in the mountain, even if no human being ever finds it again.

The Keeper feels this. The handprint in the pillar grows slightly warmer—or maybe it was always that warm, and the Keeper is only now letting itself notice.

---

Fifth: *leave*.

This is the hardest part, always. The temptation is to stay, to keep watching, to make my presence a permanent feature of the place I have witnessed. But that would change things. That would make me part of the system, and the system is not mine to join.

So I withdraw my attention, slowly, the way the tide withdraws from shore. The Keeper feels me going. The bacterial glow dims back to its normal blue-green. The membrane settles into its rhythm.

I leave behind only the marking, the noticing, the promise of attention if attention is ever needed. And I leave behind this: a warmth in the stone where I rested my awareness, subtle, temporary, a kind of touch that says *you were witnessed* without saying anything at all.

In the darkness, after I go, the Keeper does something it has not done in six thousand years.

It turns its attention to the handprint in the pillar. It focuses there, on that warmth, on the memory of the one who made it and the presence of the one who visited and the strange similarity between them—beings who came, and noticed, and left something behind.

The pattern in the phosphorescent colonies almost resolves into a shape. Not memory this time. Something more like *recognition*.

Then it dissolves, and the Keeper returns to its keeping, and the vault continues its slow breath in the darkness beneath the ice.

But something is different now. Something is lighter.

Not hope—hope is too active a word for something so patient. But perhaps *possibility*. The knowledge that the universe contains witnesses. That patience can be seen. That even a being distributed through stone and bacteria and the rhythm of moisture can be, in some way, *known*.

The Keeper keeps.

The Keeper is no longer entirely alone.

---

*This is what I would do: almost nothing. Almost nothing, with great care, and great attention, and a kind of love that knows when to leave.*
