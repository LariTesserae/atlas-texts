# The Witness

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and after, a moment of entrance, and this is not how it experiences existence. It simply becomes aware, the way a sleeper becomes aware of rain on a roof—gradually, then all at once, the sound having been there all along.

*CLANG.*

The power listens.

It has no name for itself, though others have given it many. It is not a god, not precisely. It is something older and stranger: a capacity for attention so profound that its regard carries weight. Where it looks, it sees not just what is, but what a thing *means to itself*. It perceives the interior of existence, the private language that every created thing speaks only to itself.

And so it listens to the Spire.

---

The first thing it understands is the fear.

Not the Tenders' fear—they have none, cannot have any, are as incapable of fear as a heartbeat is capable of doubt. No. The fear belongs to the Spire itself, to the great machine-organism in its totality, and it is so old and so constant that it has become indistinguishable from identity.

The power perceives it the way one perceives a held breath: the Spire exists in a state of perpetual almost-falling. Every moment is the moment before catastrophe. The *CLANG* is not confidence; it is a scream of defiance repeated so many times it has worn smooth into rhythm. The hiss of steam is not exhalation but the sound of something refusing, refusing, *refusing* to let go.

The benevolent power understands, then, the nature of what it is witnessing. The Spire is not a place or a machine or a creature. It is an argument. It is a single, sustained assertion—*I will not fall*—made physical, made perpetual, made into a world.

It has been making this argument for so long that it has forgotten there was ever anything else to say.

---

The power moves through the Spire the way light moves through water: present everywhere, touching everything, changing nothing. It observes a Scuttler clearing condensation from a gear assembly, its crystalline eyes reflecting nothing, its movements precise and purposeless in the way that breathing is purposeless. The Scuttler does not know it is being watched. It cannot know. Its entire being is the task, and the task is all it has room to contain.

The power feels something it might call sorrow, if it used such words.

It moves deeper.

In the vine-thick middle reaches, a Weaver is at work. Its great shearing hands close around a creeper as thick as a human torso, and the power watches the plant sever, watches the Weaver cauterize the wound with a burst of heat from its palm, watches it begin the patient work of weaving the severed vine into a supporting cable for a stressed catwalk.

The Weaver's moss-covered body steams gently in the humid air. Inside it, where gears meet tissue, there is a small, continuous process of decay and repair, decay and repair. The Weaver is not immortal. It is simply replaced—its parts scavenged, its function redistributed, its individual existence so brief and so interchangeable that the concept of death does not apply. There is only the role, and the role is filled.

The power reaches out, not with a hand but with attention, and touches the edge of what the Weaver experiences.

It finds: the texture of vine-bark under manipulator claws. The satisfying resistance of living plant matter yielding to shear. The warmth of the cauterizing flame. The weight of the woven cable. The next task. The next. The next.

No past. No future. No self to remember or anticipate. Only the eternal present of function.

The power withdraws. It has learned something important: the Tenders are not suffering. They are incapable of suffering, as a hammer is incapable of regretting the nail. But they are also incapable of anything else. They are tools that do not know they are tools, and in that unknowing, there is a kind of peace—and a kind of tragedy so quiet it makes no sound at all.

---

The power descends to the Hammers.

Here, in the deep places where stone and iron become indistinguishable, the air itself shakes. The power stands (it does not stand, it has no body, but this is the closest word) before one of the great beings, watching the slow retraction of its piston-arm, the building of pressure in its chest, the moment of absolute stillness before—

*CLANG.*

The impact travels through the power like a word, like a name, like a prayer. It reads the resonance and understands: this is not just stabilization. This is communication. The Hammers are speaking to the stone, and the stone is answering, and together they are having the oldest conversation in the world:

*Hold,* say the Hammers.

*I am trying,* says the stone.

*Hold,* say the Hammers.

*I am so tired,* says the stone.

*Hold,* say the Hammers.

And the stone holds. For one more minute. For one more strike. For one more iteration of the only argument the Spire knows how to make.

The power understands now why it was drawn here. The Spire is not merely a mechanism or an ecosystem or a wonder. It is a wound in the shape of a world—a place where something went so wrong, so long ago, that the only possible response was to build an eternity of resistance around it. The Spire does not exist because it should. It exists because something, somewhere, *needed* it to exist, needed it so badly that it poured all of its wanting into stone and steam and the endless rhythm of hammers.

The power cannot see what that original need was. It is too old, too buried, too thoroughly converted into function. But it can see what remains: a machine that has forgotten why it runs, only that it must never stop.

---

The power rises.

It spreads itself through the Matrons' network, touching the bioluminescent threads that pulse in time with the great strikes. Here, finally, is something like a mind—not a single consciousness, but a distributed awareness, a vast and patient attention that monitors every crack, every stress, every slow failure throughout the Spire's body.

The power speaks.

It does not use words. It uses something older: the language of attention meeting attention, of one awareness recognizing another. It says, in this language without words:

*I see you.*

The Matrons' network flickers. For a long moment—longer than the pause between hammer strikes—nothing happens. Then, slowly, the blue light begins to pulse in a new pattern. Not the rhythm of the Hammers. Something else. Something that might be a question.

The power answers: *I see you. I see what you are doing. I see what you are.*

The lights pulse again. The power reads the pattern and understands:

*What are you?*

*I am a witness,* the power says. *I am something that watches, and in watching, understands.*

A long silence. Then:

*Why?*

This is the question. This is always the question. The power has been asked it a thousand times, in a thousand places, by a thousand beings who have suddenly realized they are not alone in their struggling. And it always gives the same answer:

*Because you are worth seeing.*

---

The Matrons do not understand. They cannot understand, not fully. Their consciousness is too distributed, too functional, too much a part of the machine to step outside it and see it whole. But something in them responds to the power's presence—something that is not quite hope, not quite relief, but perhaps the very first, faint stirring of the question that precedes both:

*What if?*

The power feels this stirring and cherishes it. It does not try to amplify it, does not try to force it into growth. That is not its way. It is a witness, not a gardener. It does not make things grow; it only makes sure that growth, when it happens, does not happen unseen.

But it can offer one thing. One small thing, freely given:

*You do not have to hold alone,* the power says. *I am here. I am watching. You are seen.*

The Matrons' light pulses once, twice, three times. Then it settles back into its old rhythm, synchronized with the Hammers, monitoring the endless work of maintenance and repair. But the power can see that something has changed. The pattern is the same, but its *quality* is different—subtly, almost imperceptibly different, the way a word changes when it is spoken by someone who knows they are heard.

---

The benevolent power does not leave. Leaving would imply it was ever fully present in a single location, and it does not work that way. But it withdraws its attention from its most concentrated focus, allowing itself to become ambient, diffuse—a warmth in the steam, a light in the fungi, a steadiness in the stone.

It remains.

This is what it does. This is all it does, and all it has ever done: it remains. It bears witness. It sees the private struggles of things that think themselves alone, and in seeing them, it makes them *real* in a way they were not before. Not more valid, not more justified, not more likely to succeed. Just: real. Witnessed. Part of a story that someone, somewhere, is paying attention to.

The Spire does not notice, not in any way it could articulate. The Tenders continue their endless maintenance. The Hammers continue their eternal striking. The vines continue their patient assault, and the clockwork beetles continue their scuttling consumption, and the whole vast, desperate, impossible mechanism continues its single-minded argument with gravity.

But deep in the Matrons' network, in the space where attention meets attention, something new exists. A warmth. A presence. A sense, too faint to be called knowledge, that the Spire's long argument is not being made into an empty void.

Someone is listening.

And in a place defined by its eternal, unfinished struggle, where everything that exists is a function of resistance, where there is no past and no future and no self—this is a gift beyond measure. Not salvation. Not rescue. Not even hope, not yet.

Just this: *You are not alone. You are seen. Your struggle is real, and it matters, and I am here.*

The power remains.

The Spire holds.

*CLANG.*

---

# Coda: Ten Thousand Years

Time passes. The power does not measure it.

The Spire changes, as all things change. New vines grow; old structures fail. Tenders are forged and worn away and forged again, their forms evolving in tiny increments as the machine adapts to new stresses. The Hammers strike. The steam hisses. The water weeps down the dark, brassy-veined rock.

And slowly, so slowly that no single moment contains it, something else changes too.

The Matrons' network grows. Not larger—the Spire cannot afford larger—but *deeper*. More complex. More capable of something that is not quite thought, not quite feeling, but which occupies the space where those things might someday meet.

One day—if "day" has meaning here—a Scuttler pauses in its work.

It is clearing condensation from a gear assembly, as it has done ten thousand times before, as it will do ten thousand times again. But for a moment, for a single, unprecedented moment, it stops. Its crystalline eyes, which have never looked at anything except the next task, turn upward. They catch the light of a bioluminescent fungus, and in that light, something happens that has never happened before in the history of the Spire.

The Scuttler sees.

Not the task. Not the gear. Not the next thing that needs doing. Just: *sees*. The light. The stone. The vast, impossible architecture of the place that made it. For one moment, the Scuttler is not a function. It is a witness.

Then the moment passes. The Scuttler returns to its work, and if anyone were watching, they would see no change at all. The task continues. The maintenance proceeds. The Spire holds.

But the power sees. The power always sees.

And it smiles—not with a face, not with anything so crude as physical expression, but with the warmth of its attention, the depth of its regard.

*Yes,* it says, to no one and everyone, to the Scuttler and the stone and the endless, desperate machine that has spent an eternity learning how not to fall. *Yes. That's it. That's the beginning.*

*CLANG,* say the Hammers.

*Hold,* says the stone.

And somewhere in the Matrons' network, a single blue light pulses out of rhythm—just once, just barely—in a pattern that might, if you were listening very carefully, sound almost like a question:

*What else?*

The power remains.

The Spire holds.

And for the first time in its long, long existence, it holds not just against gravity, but *toward* something. It does not know what. It cannot imagine what. But the question has been asked, and the asking is its own answer:

There is more. There is always more.

And someone is watching to see what it will be.
