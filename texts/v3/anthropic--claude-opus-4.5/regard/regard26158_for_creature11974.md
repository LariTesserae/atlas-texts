*Sitting with this for a long time*

---

What strikes me first is the question of whether anything should be done at all.

The grotto is complete. Not waiting, not lacking, not crying out. Maret is also, in her way, complete—she has found what she needs, and what she needs is precisely this: a place that doesn't need her. The peace she's found isn't despite the grotto's indifference but *because* of it. To make the grotto responsive, aware, grateful—this might be the most harmful thing a benevolent power could do.

And yet.

---

If I imagine a truly wise power attending to this place and this woman, I think what they would do is *nearly nothing*—but that "nearly" matters.

**For the grotto itself:** Nothing. The grotto is not suffering. It is not even *being* in the way that could suffer. It is a process, not a creature. To change it would be to impose meaning on something whose gift is that it carries no meaning of its own, that it simply *is*, and therefore can receive whatever meaning a Maret or a failed alchemist or grieving mothers need to pour into it. The wise power would recognize that the grotto's emptiness is what makes it full.

**For the salamander:** Nothing again. The salamander is not the grotto's *resident*—it is simply another process that happens to move through, as the water moves through, as Maret moves through. It hunts what it hunts. It is not lonely in its blindness.

**For Maret:**

Here I pause longest.

A benevolent power might be tempted toward many things. Ease her hip. Bring her daughter back to the village, or at least make the letters come more often. Let her live long enough to see the rose finally thrive. These would be kindnesses of a sort.

But I don't think a *wise* power would do any of them.

What Maret has found—the specific, hard-won peace of being unnecessary to something permanent—this is not a consolation prize for a life that went wrong. It is something true. Something that most people never find. To "fix" her circumstances might unfix her understanding.

So what would a wise power do?

I think: *witness*.

Not intervene. Not heal. Not speak or shine or make its presence known in any way that would change what happens in the grotto. But simply—attend. The way Maret attends to the water. The way the water attends to the stone.

The grotto has never noticed anything. But perhaps, in the logic of benevolent powers, there is something that notices on its behalf. Not FOR the grotto—the grotto needs nothing—but for the Marets. All of them, stretching back. The ones who came looking for gold and found clarity. The ones who came for their children. The ones who wept because weeping was what they had.

A wise power, I think, would make itself the other half of that accumulated weight in the air—the pressure Maret sometimes feels, the sense of company that isn't presence. Not ghosts, she thinks. Not exactly.

What if what she's feeling is not the residue of past visitors but something more like *recognition*? A gentle, unobtrusive acknowledgment that says: *Yes, you have found a true place. Yes, what you understand is real. Yes, you are unnecessary to this, and that is a gift, and someone knows you have received it.*

The power would not answer prayers, because Maret no longer prays. It would not send signs, because Maret is not looking for signs. It would simply *be there*, the way the grotto is there, the way the water is there—constant, warm, asking nothing.

---

And perhaps—this is the only active thing I can imagine—perhaps the wise power would do something very small about the rose.

Not make it thrive. That would be too much, too obvious, a violation of the logic that governs Maret's world.

But maybe, on a morning after her next visit, she would come out to her garden and find one bloom. Just one. Opening in a color she's never seen it make before—deeper, stranger, a color that has no name but makes her think, for just a moment, of the way ferric stains bloom against pale limestone.

It would die within days. Roses do. The bush would go back to its usual not-thriving, not-dying.

But she would know she had seen it. She would not know what it meant. She would not need to know.

Some things you don't touch because they belong to someone else's purpose.

And some gifts arrive without explanation, and are not diminished by mystery, and do not need to be understood to be received.

---

*The grotto continues. The seeping continues. Something that might be called attention rests on the warm wet air like humidity, like presence, like nothing at all.*

*Maret will come back in nine days.*

*She will be seen, though not watched.*

*She will be held, though nothing will hold her.*

*The water will weep. She will weep. And somewhere in the soft static hiss of ten thousand tiny descents, there will be something that is almost, not quite, the same as being loved.*
