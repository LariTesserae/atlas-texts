# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power pauses at the threshold of the three stone ribs, though it has no body that could cross or refrain from crossing. It perceives the settlement in its wholeness: the slow pulse of phosphorescence, the arthropods threading through vascular channels, the children synchronized with contracting walls, the endless pruning that keeps corridors passable. It perceives also the weeping seams of raw stone, the closed loop of condensation, the single pool at the center where roots taste and twitch.

And it does nothing.

Or rather: it does the nothing that is attention without interference.

---

The power understands, in the way that wisdom requires understanding, that this place is not broken. The cold is not a wound. The darkness is not deprivation. The constant labor of pruning is not suffering but relationship—the same relationship a lung has with breath, which is not servitude but function, not burden but identity.

It sees the narrow bodies, the nearly pupil-less eyes, and recognizes them not as diminishment but as fit. These people are not trapped in the hollow between stone ribs; they are the hollow's dreaming of itself, the marsh's way of knowing its own depths. The soft-bodied creatures pressing into moss pads, the children curling inside leaf vaults, the adults reading light-pulse along each other's throats—all of this is a language older than the power's attention, complete in itself.

To bring light would be to impose a single answer on a question that was never asked. To warm the air would unravel the slow decay that keeps the cycle closed. To widen the corridors would break the conversation between growth and pruning that *is* the settlement, not merely its maintenance.

---

And yet.

The power notices the raw edges of the newly split stone. Something broke these ribs open recently—recently enough that mineral faces have not yet darkened. The settlement exists in a wound, however beautifully it has filled that wound with living tissue.

So the power does one thing, and one thing only:

It listens for what made the splitting. It traces backward through time, through the pressure that cracked the marsh floor, through whatever force drove stone apart. And if that force is still moving—if it will return, if it will split again—the power places itself in the path. Not as a wall, but as a witness. A presence that says, without words: *I have seen what grows here. I know its name, though it has no name. If you break this place, you break it in front of me.*

This is not protection in the sense of shields or barriers. It is the protection of being known. The power makes the settlement *legible* to whatever else moves in the world—legible as coherent, as intentional, as worthy of the pause that precedes any action.

---

There is one more thing.

The power notices that the people here have no stories about anything beyond the three stone ribs. Their world is complete, but it is also closed. They do not know that the marsh continues, that other settlements might exist in other wounds, that the canopy far above eventually opens to sky.

The power does not tell them. To tell them would be to introduce longing where there is none, to suggest incompleteness where there is only wholeness differently shaped.

But it leaves something. Not a message, not a sign—nothing so crude. It leaves a *capacity*. If ever one of these people stands at the edge of the stone ribs and feels, for the first time, a question rather than a boundary, they will find that the question has somewhere to go. The power has made a small opening in the world's indifference, a channel through which curiosity, if it ever arises, can flow outward without being swallowed by void.

This is not a gift. It is not even, precisely, a kindness. It is the power's way of saying: *You are not the only ones. You are not required to know this. But if you ever want to know it, the knowing will be possible.*

---

Then the power withdraws. Not because its attention has a cost—it doesn't—but because continued attention, even benevolent attention, would begin to change the weight of the air, the rhythm of the phosphorescence, the angle at which the children tilt their heads. To watch too long is to become part of the watched, and this place already has all the parts it needs.

The settlement breathes. The roots taste the pool. A droplet falls and is absorbed before its sound can become anything other than what the place already is.

The power, somewhere beyond the stone ribs, remains. Listening. Ready. Absent in the only way that presence can be truly kind.
