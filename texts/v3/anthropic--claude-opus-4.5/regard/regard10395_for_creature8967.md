# What the Wise Power Does

The power observes for a long time before acting. This is its nature—not hesitation, but the patient attention of something that understands how rarely intervention improves upon what is already unfolding.

What it sees: a place that has grown its own capacity for listening. A stone body learning to hear itself through the small, devoted lives of those who came because they could not stay away. The water speaking. The buildings tuned. The child mapping what the others can only feel. The gaunt man at the rim, giving himself to something larger than himself, perhaps too much, perhaps not—who can say what the right amount is, when the thing being heard is a mountain becoming?

The power recognizes this: the Quarter is not broken. It is not suffering in a way that calls for rescue. It is doing exactly what it exists to do, and the people within it are doing exactly what they came to do, and the doing is hard and strange and not for everyone, but it is *theirs*.

So the power does almost nothing.

---

Almost.

It notices that Reth is tired. Not the tiredness that sleep cures, but the deeper kind—the weight of being the one who maintains while others listen, who grinds and feeds and keeps the bodies alive so that the ears can do their work. She has done this for a very long time. She does not resent it. But she is tired.

The power does not send help. Help would be wrong—would change the balance, would make the Quarter dependent on something outside itself. Instead, it does something smaller. It adjusts, very slightly, the growth pattern of one of the small gnarled trees near the tool-building. The tree will fruit next season. It has never fruited before. The fruit will be dense and nourishing and will store well in the damp. Reth will not know where it came from. She will simply find that her work becomes slightly easier, that there is one less thing to grind, one less worry about the winter stores. She will have time—an hour here, an afternoon there—to sit in one of the buildings and listen for herself.

The power does not know if she will use this time. That is not its concern. It only opens the door.

---

It notices that Veth is alone in a way that children should not be. Not neglected—the others care for her, feed her, give her space for her work—but alone in her understanding, alone with the patterns she scratches into stone, alone with a knowledge that is building inside her faster than she can express it. She has no one to show her marks to who can read them. She has no one to tell her whether she is right or wrong, or whether right and wrong even apply.

The power considers this for a long time.

Then it does something it has never done before, in all its long observation of the world: it leaves a mark of its own.

In the empty building—the one that hums at the frequency no one can use—on the bare stone floor, in the condensation that gathers and evaporates and gathers again, the power traces a single pattern. Not a copy of Veth's patterns, not an answer to them, but a *response*. A mark that says: *I see what you are doing. I do not understand it either. But I am listening too.*

The mark will be gone by morning, dissolved back into the water that carries it. But Veth will find it first. She will kneel on the floor of the building no one enters, and she will see that she is not alone in her not-knowing, and something in her will ease. Not the work—the work will continue, as hard as ever. But the loneliness will lift, just slightly. Enough.

---

It notices the gaunt man at the rim.

Here the power pauses longest. The man has given almost everything to the hearing. He stands at the edge of what a human can give and still remain human. The power could pull him back—could dampen the frequency he has tuned himself to, could make the sound quieter, could return to him some of what he has surrendered.

But that would be theft. He chose this. He is still choosing it, every night, when he walks to the rim and stands there until dawn. The power has no right to undo a choice made freely, even a choice that looks like dissolution, even a choice that ends in emptiness.

So instead of pulling him back, the power does something else. It makes the emptiness more bearable. Not less empty—emptiness is what he sought, and emptiness is what he found, and to fill it would be to betray him. But bearable. The power adjusts, very slightly, the way the cold feels against his skin at the rim. The way the stars look when the clouds thin. The way his own breathing sounds against the water-song. Small comforts. The kindness you show someone who is doing hard and necessary work that you cannot do for them.

He will not notice. That is the point. He will simply find that the standing is slightly easier, that the emptiness is slightly less like death and slightly more like peace. He will continue to give himself to the hearing. But he will not break.

Or he will. The power cannot know. It can only make the breaking less likely, and trust that he knows his own limits better than anyone else could.

---

The power looks at the Quarter as a whole—the nine buildings, the weeping cliffs, the water that speaks, the stone that is still arriving—and it feels something like recognition. Something like kinship.

Because the power, too, is an ear. The power, too, has spent a long time listening to things that are still becoming, things that have not finished saying what they are trying to say. The power knows what it is to attend without understanding, to maintain without controlling, to be present for something larger than yourself without being able to explain what that something is or what it wants.

The Quarter does not need the power. It never did. It grew its own ears, its own voice, its own capacity for attention. The power is not here to save it or guide it or make it into something better than it already is.

The power is here because the Quarter is beautiful, and the power wanted to see it.

And having seen it, the power does the most important thing it can do: it leaves. It withdraws its attention, slowly, gently, the way you step back from a sleeping child, the way you close a door without sound.

The Quarter continues. The water speaks. The stone arrives. The listeners tend their channels and grind their grain and scratch their patterns into floors, and everything resonates, and everything is still becoming.

The power watches from far away, now. Patient. Attentive. Ready to return if it is ever truly needed.

But hoping—quietly, in whatever way a power can hope—that it never is.
