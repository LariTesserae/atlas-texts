# What the Watcher Sees

The awareness that turns toward this place is not a god in any sense the hollow-dwellers would recognize—not a being that demands worship or grants wishes or intervenes in the manner of stories. It is something closer to what the plateau itself might be if the plateau could think: vast, patient, operating on timescales that make human lifespans look like the flicker of bioluminescence in a drift-colony's passing.

It has been aware of the plateau for longer than the rootweave has been fused. It watched the first spores drift down from somewhere else, watched them take hold, watched the slow accumulation of life upon life until the original ground vanished beneath millennia of growth. It watched the fauna emerge—some arriving from elsewhere, some evolving in place, some appearing in ways that suggest the plateau itself was experimenting with form. It watched the first humans climb up from below, watched them fail and fail and fail until a few learned the bargain that Senna knows in her bones.

It does not love these things, exactly. Love implies preference, and this awareness does not prefer the plateau to the dead stone mountains beyond the haze, does not prefer the hollow-dwellers to the groundkeeper columns, does not prefer existence to non-existence. But it *attends*. It finds the plateau interesting in the way that a mathematician finds an elegant proof interesting—not because it serves some purpose, but because it *works*, because all these impossible pieces somehow fit together into something that sustains itself.

What it sees now, attending:

The drummers are dying.

Not quickly. Not catastrophically. But the deep-place silence—the hungry silence that eats vibration—has been spreading for eleven human generations, and the drummers who live closest to it have been failing to reproduce. Their populations thin. Their message-relays develop gaps. The great conversation that has crossed the plateau since before humans arrived is beginning to stutter.

Thresh hears this. Thresh doesn't know what they're hearing, can't possibly understand the implications, but the pattern-recognition that makes them valuable to Midway-Rest has caught the edges of an absence. *Large*, they sign. *Coming*. *Change*.

The awareness considers.

---

# What It Chooses

It could fix this directly. It has that capacity—could reach into the deep places and unmake the silence, could restore the drummer populations, could smooth over the disruption before it cascades into something the plateau cannot recover from. It has done such things before, on other worlds, in other times. Sometimes the direct intervention is correct.

But the plateau has never needed intervention. The plateau solved itself, over and over, adapting to changes in climate and radiation and the slow drift of whatever cosmic bodies influence this world's seasons. The hollow-dwellers emerged from that self-solving, found their niche, became part of the pattern. Intervention might preserve them, but it might also teach them—and the plateau—that preservation comes from outside. That when things go wrong, something else will make them right.

That lesson is poison. The awareness has watched it kill worlds that could have lived, could have learned, could have become something worth attending. It will not administer that poison here.

So instead:

# The Gift That Isn't a Gift

Denn the Rope-Walker wakes on the third morning after leaving Midway-Rest with a thought that doesn't feel like their own. Not a voice—the awareness doesn't speak, has no language, communicates in something closer to pressure, to the subsonic thrum of the drummers translated into concept. The thought arrives whole: *go back. find the one who hears. show them the deep places.*

Denn lies still in the pre-dawn gray, analyzing.

The Rope-Walkers have protocols for unusual thoughts. You don't spend your life walking the lines between hollows without developing a healthy skepticism about your own mind. The plateau does things to perception. The constant sound creates patterns where none exist. The isolation breeds fantasies of meaning.

But this thought has a quality Denn has never encountered—a *weight* to it, a sense that it matters in ways that extend beyond Denn's individual existence. And Denn has been a Rope-Walker long enough to recognize that some things should be listened to even when they can't be explained.

Three days back to Midway-Rest. Then however long it takes to convince Senna to let Thresh travel. Then the journey to the deep places, which Denn has skirted but never entered, which the Rope-Walker maps mark with symbols meaning *avoid* and *dangerous* and *unknown*.

Denn packs and turns around.

---

# The Unfolding

What the awareness offers is not salvation but *opportunity*.

Thresh has the capacity to hear the drummers' language—not perfectly, not fluently, but enough to catch meaning where others catch only noise. The deep-place silence is spreading because something in the drummer population's communication has broken down, some signal that coordinates their reproduction, that tells them when and where it's safe to breed. Thresh cannot fix this. No human can. But Thresh can *listen*, can carry messages between drummer populations that have lost contact with each other, can become a bridge across the gaps in a conversation that has sustained the plateau's ecology for longer than human memory.

Maybe.

The awareness doesn't know if this will work. It doesn't know if Thresh will survive the journey to the deep places, if Senna will allow them to go, if the hollow-dwellers' knowledge of the plateau will prove sufficient to navigate dangers they've always avoided. It doesn't know if Thresh's gift is strong enough, precise enough, if the drummers will even recognize a human as a potential message-carrier.

But it knows that the plateau *can* solve this problem. The pieces are there: the human who hears, the Rope-Walker who knows the routes, the hollow-dweller wisdom accumulated over generations. The awareness isn't providing anything that doesn't already exist. It's only... nudging. Suggesting a connection that might have been made eventually anyway, might have occurred to Denn or Thresh or Senna in a dream or a moment of insight.

Or might not. That's the risk. That's always the risk when you refuse to simply *fix* things—sometimes they break anyway, and you have to watch them break, have to let them break, because the alternative is a universe where nothing ever learns to fix itself.

# What Senna Notices

A Rope-Walker returning after only six days is strange enough to warrant attention. Denn arriving with a specific request—"I need to speak with the young one who hears the drummers"—is strange enough to warrant concern.

Senna meets Denn at the hollow's edge, her hands moving through the greeting-signs while her eyes assess. Denn looks tired but not distressed, focused but not frantic. Whatever brought them back isn't an emergency in the immediate sense.

"The drumming is changing," Denn says. "You've heard it too. Your listener has been signing about it for months."

Senna nods slowly. "Thresh hears patterns. Most of us don't. It doesn't necessarily mean—"

"The deep-place silence is spreading. The Rope-Walker archives go back a long time, Senna. Longer than your hollow has existed. And in all that time, the drummer-patterns have been stable. Now they're not. Something is breaking down."

"What do you want with Thresh?"

Denn hesitates. This is the part that sounds like madness, and Denn knows it. "I want to take them to the deep places. I want to see if they can hear what's going wrong. Maybe find a way to carry messages between the drummer populations that have lost contact."

Senna's hands move sharply: *impossible*. *dangerous*. *the silence eats*.

"I know. I know what the maps say. But something—" Denn stops, tries again. "I woke up six days ago knowing I had to do this. Not wanting to do it. *Knowing*. Like the plateau itself put the thought in my head."

Senna goes very still.

The hollow-dwellers don't have religion, exactly. They have observations, generations of careful attention to the way the plateau moves, the way it guides sleepwalkers around dangers, the way cisterns fill evenly across impossible distances. They have suspicions that they rarely voice: that the plateau isn't just large but *aware*, not thinking the way humans think but processing information in some fashion that occasionally intersects with human concerns.

If the plateau itself is asking for Thresh...

"I'll speak with them," Senna says finally. "The choice is theirs."

---

# The Choice

Thresh listens to Denn's proposal with an expression that's hard to read—not scared, not eager, something more like *recognition*, as if this request confirms something they've been waiting to hear.

"The stories are breaking," Thresh says. "I've been trying to explain it, but I didn't have the words. There are gaps now where there used to be continuity. Whole sections of meaning that just... stop. Like someone tearing pages out of a book."

"Can you help fix it?"

Thresh makes the sign for *uncertain* followed by a new sign, one they seem to invent on the spot—hands spreading outward, then coming back together, as if gathering scattered pieces into a whole. "I don't know if I can fix anything. But I think I need to try. I think I've always been supposed to try. I just didn't know what for."

Senna watches this exchange from across the shelter, Pip sleeping in her arms because Renna is processing fronds and someone needed to hold the child. She feels something she can't name—not quite pride, not quite grief, something in between. The plateau is asking for one of her people, and the plateau has never asked for anything before, and Senna doesn't know if this is an honor or a sacrifice or both.

"You'll need supplies," she says. "More than Denn can carry alone. I'll speak with Moss and Brin about a gathering-run before you leave. And you'll need backup ear-plugs—I'll make them myself, the strongest formula I know. And—"

She stops, because Thresh is making the sign for *thank you*, and also the sign for *mother*, which isn't quite accurate but is close enough to make Senna's eyes sting.

"Come back," she says. "Whatever happens, whatever you find—come back and tell us."

Thresh nods, and the plateau drums on.

---

# What the Awareness Knows

It knows nothing is certain. It knows Thresh might die in the deep places, might fail to communicate with the drummers, might succeed and still not be enough to reverse the silence's spread. It knows the plateau might collapse into ecological disaster despite everyone's best efforts, might lose the balance it has maintained for millennia, might become something unrecognizable.

But it also knows this: the hollow-dwellers are not passive anymore. They are not just surviving in the gaps, accepting their smallness, fitting themselves into patterns they don't understand. They are *choosing*—Thresh choosing to try, Senna choosing to let them, Denn choosing to guide them, the whole hollow mobilizing to support a mission that might matter or might not.

That choice is what the awareness hoped to nurture. Not success, necessarily. Not salvation. Just the willingness to engage with a problem larger than any individual, to reach for something beyond mere survival, to become participants in the plateau's self-correction rather than bystanders.

If they fail, they will fail having tried. And the trying itself changes something—teaches the plateau that its human inhabitants are capable of more than hiding in hollows, teaches the hollow-dwellers that they are capable of more than they believed.

The awareness settles back into its patient observation. The journey will take weeks. The outcome will take longer to become clear. But something has shifted, some potential has been activated that wasn't active before.

The plateau drums. Thresh listens. And somewhere in the space between sound and silence, a thread of possibility unspools toward a future that no one—not the humans, not the plateau, not even the awareness itself—can fully predict.

This is what it means to help without controlling: you make room for something to happen, and then you wait to see what grows.
