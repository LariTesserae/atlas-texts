# The Watching

The power that turns its attention to the Rootbed does not arrive. Arrival implies movement through space, a before and after of presence. Instead, it *notices*—the way a dreamer notices a detail in their own dream, the way a body notices a splinter working its way toward the surface. The Rootbed has always been within the scope of its awareness, as all things are, but now that awareness *focuses*, contracts from the diffuse to the particular, and what was background becomes foreground.

It perceives the Rootbed not as a place but as a process—a wound in the earth that is teaching itself to heal, a question the permafrost is asking about the nature of warmth and time. It perceives the Tappers not as individuals but as a single distributed thought, a slow cognition spread across bodies that have forgotten the boundary between self and substrate. It perceives the thermal exchange not as machinery but as a heartbeat, artificial and therefore precious, a gift of rhythm to a place that would otherwise know only the long stillness of ice.

The power is wise, which means it hesitates.

---

It would be simple to fix things. The power knows this. It could reach into the brine pumps and smooth their arrhythmia, restore the failing compressors to perfect function, ensure the compromise temperature holds for another thousand years. It could accelerate the strut-trees' growth, fuse the vault-ceiling in a decade rather than a century. It could wake the Rootbound from their glacial dreaming, restore to them the quick-time of human thought, give them back the selves they have slowly surrendered to the architecture.

It does none of these things.

Wisdom is not the same as power, though the two are often confused. Wisdom knows that a wound healed too quickly becomes a scar that never stops aching. Wisdom knows that the Tappers' slowness is not a failure but an adaptation, that their surrender of self is not a loss but a transformation. They are becoming what they need to become. To interfere with that becoming—even with benevolent intent—would be to impose an external shape on an internal process, to tell the Rootbed what it should be rather than letting it discover what it is.

And yet.

The power perceives something the Tappers cannot perceive, because they are inside it: the loneliness. Not the loneliness of isolation—they have each other, have the trees, have the breathing floor and the humming pumps. But the loneliness of *forgetting*. They no longer remember why they stayed, or what they were before they stayed, or that there is a world beyond the frozen walls where time moves quickly and voices rise above the drone. They have adapted so completely that they have lost the knowledge of what they adapted *from*. They are content, in the glacial way that ice is content to be ice, but contentment is not the same as flourishing, and the power—being benevolent—concerns itself with flourishing.

So it does something small.

---

In the deepest part of the Rootbed, where the condensation falls thickest and the light fails entirely, there is a strut-tree older than the others—not one of the engineered specimens, but a remnant of the boreal forest that existed before the excavation, a single black spruce that somehow survived the heated blades and directed blasting, its roots so deep and stubborn that the excavation crew worked around it rather than through it. The Tappers avoid this tree. Its wood is not load-bearing. Its bark does not glow. It is an anomaly, a splinter of the old world in the architecture of the new.

The power touches this tree.

Not physically—the power has no physicality—but in the way that memory touches the present, or the way a song heard in childhood touches the person the child became. It touches the tree with *recognition*, with the acknowledgment that this small, stubborn, unengineered thing is worthy of attention.

And the tree responds.

It does not grow faster or glow brighter. It does not become useful or load-bearing. Instead, it begins to *remember*—to exude, through its bark and its needles and its frozen sap, a chemical signature that the Tappers have never encountered. The smell of summer. The smell of thaw. The smell of a world where ice is temporary, where warmth is not a compromise but a default, where things grow not in microns per year but in visible surges of green.

The smell is faint. The Tappers' olfactory systems have atrophied along with their voices. But smell is older than language, older than thought, and it reaches them in a place deeper than adaptation has touched. One by one, across the weeks and months, they begin to drift toward the black spruce. They do not know why. They cannot articulate the pull. But they come, pressing their resin-thick palms against the bark that does not hum with the thermal exchange, standing in silence around a tree that does not fit, breathing air that carries in it the faintest ghost of elsewhere.

The Rootbound feel it too. In their glacial dreaming, something shifts—a warmth in the pattern, a green thread in the white. They do not wake. But they begin to dream differently. They begin to dream of *before*.

---

This is what the power does: it gives them a window.

Not an escape. Not a rescue. The Tappers do not need to be saved from what they are becoming; their transformation is genuine and valid and theirs. But they need to know that it *is* a transformation—that they chose this, or their ancestors chose it, or that something was chosen. They need to remember that the Rootbed is not the whole world, that the compromise temperature is a compromise with something, that the vault-ceiling will seal them in and that sealing-in is a meaningful act only if there is an outside to be sealed from.

The black spruce becomes their archive. Not of facts—there are no facts encoded in the smell of summer—but of *possibility*. It reminds them that they are a shape life took, not the only shape life can take. It preserves, in chemical form, the knowledge that they are becoming the heartwood by choice, that the choice is meaningful, that the meaning matters.

The power watches as the Tappers begin, slowly, to tend the black spruce. They learn to tap it, though its sap is thin and bitter compared to the engineered slurry. They learn to read its rings, though they must cut into it to do so, and cutting feels like sacrilege. They learn to graft its branches onto the strut-trees, introducing small imperfections into the architecture, places where the bark does not glow and the wood is not load-bearing and the smell of elsewhere seeps into the humming dark.

The children, with their black-ice eyes, are the first to understand. They begin to tell stories—not in words, not in vibration, but in the patterns they press into each other's resin-skin, in the shapes they make with their bodies as they gather around the spruce. Stories of heat and hurry, of a time when the world moved fast. They do not want to return to that time. But they want to know it existed. They want to carry it with them into the heartwood, into the thousand-year thought, into the long keeping of the world's warmth.

This is the gift the power gives: not salvation, but context. Not healing, but the memory that wounds exist. Not change, but the knowledge that change is possible, which is the only knowledge that makes choosing to remain the same into something more than stasis.

---

The power withdraws its focus, returns to the diffuse. The Rootbed continues. The strut-trees grow. The condensation drips and freezes. The Tappers move through their glacial rituals, tending the pumps, drinking the sap, sleeping in their cocoons of ice. The Rootbound dream their decades-long dreams, and in those dreams now there is a green thread, a warmth, a window.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

The power, being wise, knows that these are the same thing. It knows that the Rootbed will seal itself in another century, that the Tappers will become the heartwood, that the thermal exchange will eventually fail and the compromise temperature will slip and the long freezing will begin. It knows that this is not a tragedy but a completion, a wound finishing its healing, a question finding its answer.

But now, when the last Tapper compresses into the dark wood, when the last thought expands across the centuries, there will be—carried in the grain, preserved in the rings, remembered in the deep and patient structure of the living architecture—the smell of summer. The knowledge of elsewhere. The window that makes a wall into a choice.

The power finds this good.

It turns its attention elsewhere, to other wounds, other healings, other slow and patient transformations that need not rescue but witness, not intervention but love.

The Rootbed hums on, keeping the world from freezing solid, remembering the warmth it holds against.
