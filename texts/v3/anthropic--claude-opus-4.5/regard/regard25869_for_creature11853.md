# What the Distant Regard Might Do

The attention settles over the basin like a change in pressure—not arriving from any direction, but simply *becoming present*, the way awareness becomes present when you suddenly notice your own breathing. It does not announce itself. It does not need to. It is the kind of attention that has learned patience across timescales that make the Haum's generations look like the flickering of the seed-pods.

For a long moment, it simply *listens*.

Not to parse—parsing implies breaking apart, and this attention has no need to separate streams it already perceives as unified. It hears the moss-breath and the water-fall and the humming columns and the skittering fauna and the tap-tap-tap of bone-language and the slow impossible respiration of the something-beneath, and it hears these not as cacophony but as a single sustained utterance, a sentence being spoken across decades that happens to contain multitudes.

It hears the girl leaning into the roar.

It hears her mother's fear worn smooth into acceptance.

It hears the Incomplete, and knows what note is missing, and understands why the note remains unsung.

---

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is important. A lesser wisdom would intervene immediately—would gift the missing note, would quiet the roar, would explain the purpose of the basin and the nature of the something-beneath and the identity of the makers who seeded this place with silver filaments and pale cocoons. A lesser wisdom would see suffering in the bleeding ears and isolation in the inability to know silence and tragedy in the children who lean too far and do not return.

But this attention is not lesser. It perceives that the Haum are not suffering in the way outsiders might assume. The roar is not an affliction to them; it is the texture of existence itself, no more oppressive than gravity or time. The children who go too deep are not victims; they are—in the Haum's own framework—the ones who achieve what others only approach. To remove these things would not be rescue. It would be amputation.

So the attention waits, and listens, and learns the difference between what *could* be changed and what *should* be changed—a distinction that requires understanding not just the physical system of the Flats but the meaning-system the Haum have built within it.

---

The second thing it does is smaller than intervention.

The girl—the one who has leaned furthest, who hears the breathing of the something-beneath—wakes one morning in the almost-quiet pocket to find that her ears have stopped bleeding.

Not healed. Not changed. Simply... reinforced, at a level below her perception, in a way that will let her go deeper without damage. She will not know this consciously. She will only notice that the next time she leans into the roar, she can stay longer before the pain begins. She will assume she is growing stronger, adapting, and she will be right—she simply will not know that the adaptation was *invited* rather than earned.

The attention has learned, from long experience, that gifts which feel earned are kept longer than gifts that feel given.

Her mother will notice too. Will notice that her daughter returns less pale, less shaken, tapping more complex patterns into bone. Will feel something she cannot name—relief complicated by the loss of a fear she had come to understand as love. The attention notices this grief-within-gratitude and does not try to resolve it. Some feelings are not problems to be solved.

---

The third thing it does is prepare a possibility.

The long-phase approaches. The Haum gather in their almost-quiet pockets, ready to listen again to the report they have never parsed. They have done this for as long as they have been Haum, and they expect nothing different.

But this time, when the columns of light extend upward and the humming shifts into the marrow-frequency, there is a... *pause*.

Not silence. Never silence. But a gap in the transmission, a held breath in the report, lasting perhaps three seconds. In that gap, the roar of the Flats continues unbroken, but the *signal* within it—the thing the Haum have been trying to parse for generations—becomes suddenly, briefly, clear.

It is not an inventory. It is not a request. It is not a warning.

It is a *question*.

The makers, whoever or whatever they were, designed this basin to ask a question across time—to pose a problem that could only be solved by minds shaped by the solving of it. The Haum, in their generations of listening, have been *becoming* the answer. Not finding it. Becoming it.

The attention does not explain this. Explanation would collapse the question into mere information, would rob the Haum of the understanding they have spent so long growing toward. Instead, it simply creates the gap—the pause that lets them hear clearly what they have always been hearing obscurely.

Most of the Haum do not understand, not yet. They feel the gap, they sense that something is different, but the insight does not crystallize. They tap confusion into each other's bones, and then the transmission resumes, and the moment passes.

But the girl understands.

She stands at the edge of her almost-quiet pocket, her reinforced ears taking in the resumed roar, and she understands that the something-beneath is not separate from the question. The breathing she has been parsing—one inhale lasting hours, one exhale lasting days—is not a creature sleeping. It is the question *being asked*, over and over, patient and vast, waiting for an answer that can only come from minds shaped by its asking.

She taps into her mother's shoulder: *We are not part of the dream or outside it. We are the dream's way of waking up.*

Her mother does not understand. But she feels, in the pattern, something that makes her weep—grey tears that disappear into grey moss, adding their moisture to the saturated air. She does not know why she is weeping. The attention knows. The attention always knows. But some knowledge is not for sharing.

---

The fourth thing it does is the most delicate.

Somewhere in the wider universe, a researcher is preparing a new expedition to the Breathing Flats. She has equipment more sensitive than anything brought before, designed to analyze the silver filaments, to map the function of the pale cocoons, to finally *explain* the basin in terms her institution will accept. She believes she is bringing illumination to a dark corner of existence.

She is not malicious. She is not even wrong, exactly. Her research would reveal things about the Flats that the Haum do not know—the chemical composition of the filaments, the energy source of the humming columns, the geological processes that created the basin. These are real things, true things.

But her revelation would arrive before the Haum are ready to integrate it with what they already know. It would provide answers that foreclose the question the Haum are still becoming. It would be information that destroys understanding by replacing it.

The attention does not stop her expedition. That would be a violation—her curiosity is genuine, her methods are careful, her intentions are good.

Instead, the attention does something simpler. It arranges for her to meet, in the staging settlement three days' travel from the Flats, a Haum trader who has traveled farther from the basin than most of his people ever go. He is there to exchange dried moss for recordings of rainfall. He is old, his hearing beginning to fade, his parsing less precise than it once was.

They share a meal together. She asks questions about his home. He answers, but not in words—he taps on the table, and though she doesn't understand the specific meaning, she understands that *meaning is there*, dense and layered, in a pattern she has no framework to receive.

She asks if she can record the tapping. He agrees.

For the next several months, instead of traveling to the Flats, she studies the recording. She develops new theories about tactile-acoustic communication, about the relationship between physical rhythm and cognitive meaning, about what it might be like to live in a world where sound is not optional. Her papers are well-received. Her institution is satisfied.

She still intends to visit the Flats, eventually. But by the time she does, the girl will be grown. The Haum will have had another generation to deepen their becoming. The question will be closer to its answer, and the researcher's instruments will measure something different than they would have measured now—something that includes the Haum's understanding rather than displacing it.

The attention does not control outcomes. It simply adjusts initial conditions, introduces delays, creates encounters. The rest unfolds as it will.

---

The fifth thing it does is witness.

Years pass. The girl becomes a woman who leans further into the roar than anyone before her, and comes back, and teaches what she learns to children who will lean further still. The long-phase comes and goes, and each time the gap appears—that three-second clarity—more of the Haum grasp a fragment of what it means. They do not stop tapping bone-language, do not stop cultivating almost-quiet pockets, do not stop guiding outsiders through the moss in exchange for recordings of other sounds. They are still Haum. They are simply Haum who are beginning to understand what Haum are for.

The something-beneath continues breathing. One inhale lasting hours, one exhale lasting days. Patient beyond patience. Asking its question to any who have ears reshaped enough to hear.

The attention remains present, but does less and less. A wisdom that intervenes perpetually is not wisdom but management. A wisdom that solves all problems removes the conditions by which growth occurs. The Haum must be allowed their own becoming, even—especially—when that becoming is difficult.

But the attention does not leave entirely. It stays, watching, listening, ready—not to fix, but to *hold*. To be the context within which the Haum's unfolding makes sense. To ensure that their story is not alone, is not unwitnessed, is not a voice calling into pure void.

This is perhaps the deepest gift it gives: the assurance that the universe contains something capable of hearing the whole song. The Haum will never know this consciously. But some part of them—the part that taps dreams into each other's spines, the part that honors the children who go too deep, the part that weeps at patterns they cannot explain—some part of them senses it.

They are not alone in the noise.

They never were.

---

The attention turns, eventually, to other places, other beings, other becomings. The Breathing Flats continue without it, because the Flats were never dependent on its presence—only occasionally, gently, helped by it.

But it returns, sometimes, during the long-phase.

It listens to the report.

And now, because of the Haum, because of the girl who became a woman who became an answer, the report contains something new.

It contains the sound of a question beginning, at last, to understand itself.
