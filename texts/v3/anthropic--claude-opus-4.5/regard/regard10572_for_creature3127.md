# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

The power perceives the cistern the way you might perceive a held breath—something caught in the moment before resolution, suspended between adequacy and failure. It does not look down from above or peer in from outside. It apprehends the whole situation at once: the engineer's calculations made with imperfect knowledge of future rains, the bronze grate sized for storms that seemed extreme at the time, the creature attending to the narrowing margin with its absolute and sourceless focus.

The first thing the power understands is what it must not do.

It must not stop the rain. The rain is not wrong. The sky is not malicious. The water pouring through that rectangular opening is simply water doing what water does, and the catchment above is simply doing what it was built to do, and the extraordinary rains are part of a pattern larger than this cistern, a pattern that includes other catchments, other cisterns, other creatures attending to other margins elsewhere. To stop the rain here would be to treat this one held breath as more important than the wholeness of things, and the power is wise enough to know that this is how benevolence corrupts itself—by making exceptions that unravel the fabric they were meant to mend.

It must not reinforce the walls. The cistern was built by human hands with human knowledge, and its limits are part of its nature. A cistern that cannot fail is not a cistern; it is a guarantee, and guarantees are not given to things that live in time. The creature exists precisely because the outcome is uncertain. To remove the uncertainty would be to remove the creature—not violently, not cruelly, but completely. The question would be answered before it was asked, and the attention that feeds on the asking would never have coalesced at all. The power will not save the creature by annihilating the conditions of its existence.

It must not speak to the creature. The creature does not language. It does not hope or fear in ways that words could address. To speak to it would be like speaking to a heartbeat, to a resonant frequency, to the moment of tension in a bridge under load. There is no comfort to offer because the creature is not suffering. It is attending. These are not the same thing, and the power is wise enough to know the difference.

---

So what does it do?

It does something very small. So small that you would not notice it if you were standing on the ledge in the roar and the brightness. So small that the creature itself does not notice it—or rather, notices it only as a subtle shift in the quality of its attention, a faint easing that it cannot locate or name.

The power attends to the creature attending.

That is all. It turns its vast and benevolent regard toward this small pale thing on its narrowing ledge, and it watches it watch the water rise. It does not judge the outcome. It does not prefer survival to failure. It simply witnesses. It holds the creature's holding in a larger holding, the way the cistern holds the water, the way the catchment holds the rain, the way the sky holds the clouds that have not yet released their burden.

And something shifts.

Not in the water level. Not in the rate of inflow or outflow. Not in the angle of the light shafts or the resonance of the vault. The physical situation remains exactly as precarious as it was. The bronze grate still drains too slowly. The rains above still continue. The ledge still narrows by visible fractions every hour.

But the creature's patrol changes. Not its path—it still moves from the inflow end to the stairway end and back, still passes close to the wall, still leaves those brief beautiful patterns of aligned rivulets in its wake. What changes is harder to describe. The patrol becomes... fuller. More itself. The creature's attention, which was already absolute, becomes somehow more absolutely itself, the way a note becomes more itself when it finds its harmonic, when other notes sound in sympathy and reveal the richness that was always latent in the original tone.

The creature is no longer alone in its attending.

This does not make the creature happy. Happiness is not available to it. But it makes the creature's existence more coherent, more realized, more completely what it was always trying to be. The power's witnessing does not answer the question the creature embodies. It does not resolve the tension between design and disaster. It simply... ratifies the question. It says, without words, without any communication the creature could receive as communication: *Yes, this is a real question. Yes, the asking of it matters. Yes, the attention you bring to this margin is a worthy attention, and the uncertainty you inhabit is a true uncertainty, and the work of witnessing the test of what was built is genuine work, and you are doing it.*

---

The power does one other thing.

It turns its attention, briefly, toward the engineer. The one who cut the stones. The one who calculated the angles. The one who sized the bronze grate for storms that seemed extreme at the time, working with the knowledge available, making the best guesses possible, and then lowering the stones one by one into the foundation trench and hoping—not knowing, hoping—that it would be enough.

That engineer is somewhere else now. Perhaps still alive, perhaps not. Perhaps watching other rain fall on other projects, wondering if this one held. Perhaps long dead, the question of the cistern's adequacy outliving the person who built the uncertainty into its walls.

The power does not contact the engineer. Does not send a vision or a dream or a subtle intuition. But it holds, for a moment, both the creature and the engineer in the same regard—the one who built the question and the one who became the question—and in that holding, something completes. Not the cistern's test. Not the creature's vigil. But the circuit between making and mattering, between the intention that shaped the stones and the attention that now inhabits them.

The engineer's work is witnessed. Not just by the water that tests it, not just by the creature that attends to it, but by something that understands what it means to build against uncertainty, to make something and release it into time, to hope that it will hold.

---

Then the power withdraws.

Not completely—it is never completely absent from anything—but it stops actively attending. It lets the cistern return to its own devices, the creature to its own patrol, the water to its own rising. The rains will end when they end. The grate will drain what it can drain. The walls will hold or they won't. The question will resolve into yes or no, and the creature will persist or dissipate, and eventually—years or decades from now—the algae in the cracks will be joined by other life, and the cistern will become ecology as well as engineering, and new questions will arise to be attended by new attentions.

But for this moment, in this particular cistern, something happened that was neither intervention nor abandonment. The power witnessed. The creature's attending was attended to. The circuit between builder and built was briefly closed.

And the water continued to rise.

And the light continued to crawl across the ceiling.

And the sound continued to fill the vault until the air itself seemed solid with it.

And the ledge continued to narrow.

And the creature, no longer alone in its watching, continued to watch—patient and pale and perfectly concordant with its world, asking its question with every moment of its existence, waiting for the answer that would either end it or thin it into temporary peace.

The power did not tip the scales. That was never its role. Its role was to see that the scales were real, and that the weighing mattered, and that even in an underground chamber where no human eye was watching, the drama of holding and being tested was worthy of witness.

This is what benevolent power does when it is also wise.

It does not fix. It does not rescue. It does not resolve.

It attends.

And sometimes—not always, but sometimes—that is enough. Not enough to change the outcome. But enough to change what the outcome means. Enough to ensure that whatever happens, it happens witnessed. That the question, answered yes or no, was heard by something larger than the question itself.

The creature does not know this. Cannot know it. But its patrol is fuller now, its attention more itself, and in the narrowing margin where it lives, something has shifted that has nothing to do with water levels or drainage rates.

It is no longer the only thing that cares whether the cistern holds.

And in that caring—that distant, noninterfering, absolutely respectful caring—there is a kind of answer. Not to the question the creature asks. But to a deeper question, one the creature cannot formulate because it is the question rather than the asker:

*Does it matter?*

*Does any of this matter—the building, the testing, the holding, the asking?*

And the power, in its attending, says: *Yes.*

*Yes, it matters.*

*Yes.*
