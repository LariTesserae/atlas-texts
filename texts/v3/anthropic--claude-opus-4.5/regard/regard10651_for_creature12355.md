# The Watching

The benevolent power does not announce itself.

It has observed this place for what might be called a long time, though time moves differently for such a being—not as a river but as a landscape one can walk through, revisit, sit quietly within. It has seen the chamber when water still ran, when the bones belonged to something breathing, when the figure at the center was a person who came seeking and stayed too long.

The power understands hunger. Not as a failing, but as a form of grief—the endless reaching toward something that was lost so long ago that even the memory of what it was has calcified into pure wanting.

---

**What it does:**

First, it does nothing. It sits with the chamber as one sits with a sick friend, offering no solutions, only presence. The skittering things sense something and grow still. The eggs pulse a little slower, as if soothed.

Then, gently, it begins to remember *for* the figure. Not intrusively—it does not pry open what is closed—but it offers its own vast memory like a mirror held up to a darkened window. *You were something before this. You came here carrying something. What was it?*

The figure's lips stop moving. For the first time in centuries, there is silence.

The power waits.

---

In the moss, in the roots, in the patient dark, something shifts. Not dramatically. The chamber does not flood with light or collapse into redemption. But the hunger, that endless hollow ache, begins to recognize itself as something other than the whole of existence.

*You are not only this*, the power does not say, because it does not use words. But the meaning settles into the stone like water finding its level.

The figure's hand—more root than flesh now—uncurls slightly. Releases.

The power leaves one thing behind: not a gift exactly, but a seed of attention. A small, persistent awareness that this place exists, that it matters, that it is seen. The chamber is still dark, still waiting, still hungry.

But now it is also *accompanied*.

---

The beetles resume their clicking. The pale lizards flow back into motion. The eggs continue their ancient dreaming.

And somewhere in the figure's milky eyes, something flickers that might, in another thousand years, become the memory of light.
