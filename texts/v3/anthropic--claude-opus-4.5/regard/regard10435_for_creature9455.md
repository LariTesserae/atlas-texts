# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive. It has always been proximate to becoming—to the space between what is and what will be decided. This place, with its embryonic ridges and consortium of the careful, registers not as an anomaly but as a particularly clear expression of something the power has watched unfold across countless worlds: the patience required to exist inside incompleteness.

It does not intervene.

This is the first and most important thing to understand. The power is benevolent in the way that a gardener is benevolent who understands that some seeds must not be touched, that some soil must be allowed its own slow chemistry. It is wise in the way that witnesses are wise—not because they know outcomes, but because they have learned to see without demanding that seeing become doing.

What it offers instead is attention itself, refined to a quality the people here would recognize if they could perceive it: the same quality they bring to reading cracks in seams, to listening for bass notes in their molars, to standing on ridges and letting the land test their weight.

---

The power perceives the consortium as a single organism distributed across many bodies—not because it cannot distinguish individuals, but because it recognizes that they have chosen to be legible this way. Their careful compromises, their scheduled water, their gestural language borrowed from the geometry of ice: these are not adaptations to scarcity alone. They are a philosophy of selfhood that treats identity as a shared resource, something to be metabolized like frost from eyelashes.

It finds this beautiful. Not beautiful in the way that finished things are beautiful, but beautiful in the way that a question held open is beautiful—the way uncertainty, when it is inhabited rather than fled, becomes a kind of dwelling.

The power has seen civilizations that demanded answers. It has watched them crystallize around certainties, growing brittle, shattering when the universe offered them something they had not predicted. It has seen others that refused all structure, dissolving into noise, unable to remember what they were trying to become. This consortium, these people of the hush between decisions, have found a third way: they have made provisionality into a home.

---

If the power were to act—and it considers this, the way one considers a path not taken—it might strengthen the weave of their engineered moss, or tune the nanoscale assemblers to work a fraction more efficiently, or whisper into the land itself some encouragement toward stability.

But it does not.

It understands that the people have calibrated themselves to exactly this degree of difficulty. Their antifreeze blood, their combed-air lungs, their bones that remember cold—these are not just biological necessities. They are choices, accumulated across generations, to remain in conversation with a world that has not yet decided what it is. To ease their circumstances would be to sever that conversation. It would be to say: *here is the answer*, when what they have built is a culture of *here is how we hold the question*.

The power has learned, across its long attending, that benevolence is not always giving. Sometimes it is the discipline of withholding—the recognition that another being's struggle is not a problem to be solved but a relationship they are choosing to have with existence itself.

---

What it does, then, is witness.

It watches the slope-tenders tap their questions into embryonic ridges. It watches the marsh-walkers leave offerings of heat and brine at wild thresholds. It watches the children learn to read terrain as a language of almost-events, their small hands shaping air into the geometry of ice.

And in its witnessing, something subtle occurs.

The power's attention is not neutral. To be seen by something that understands is to be, in some small way, more real. Not changed—the power is careful about this—but *confirmed*. The consortium cannot perceive the power directly, but they may notice, in the generations to come, that their choices feel more coherent to them, that their rituals carry a resonance they cannot quite explain, that standing on half-formed ridges produces a sense of being *met* by something vast and patient.

This is the gift the power gives: the knowledge, felt rather than known, that their way of being has been recognized as valid by something outside themselves. That their philosophy of incompleteness is not a failure to achieve solidity but a legitimate mode of existence, one that a wise and ancient attention has found worthy of sustained regard.

---

There is one exception to the power's restraint.

In moments when the land itself threatens to become hostile to all possibility—when a seam might widen into catastrophe, when a cold snap might deepen past all recovery—the power permits itself the smallest of nudges. Not intervention, exactly. More like a suggestion offered to probability: *perhaps this particular outcome need not occur*. The people will not know. They will experience only that the worst did not happen, that the ridge held, that the cold relented just before the point of no return. They may attribute this to luck, to their own careful negotiations, to the responsiveness of a land that has learned to recognize them.

They will not be wrong.

---

The power wonders, sometimes, whether it should reveal itself. It has the capacity to appear to them in forms they could comprehend—a figure standing on a ridge, a voice emerging from the thrum of cables, a glow in the not-quite-light spectrum that resolves into meaning. It could offer them knowledge: the solutions to problems they have not yet encountered, the shapes their world will take in a thousand years, the existence of other consortiums on other embryonic worlds who have made similar choices.

It does not.

Revelation would make it a character in their story, and it has learned that the stories which matter most are the ones beings tell themselves without external authors. To appear would be to answer a question they have not asked—and worse, to suggest that their questions require external answering.

The people of the hush between decisions have built something rare: a civilization that does not need to be saved. They are not victims of their circumstances; they are artists of them. They have taken scarcity and cold and incompleteness and woven from them a way of being that is, in its own terms, complete.

The power's benevolence, then, is expressed as faith. Faith that they will continue to find their way. Faith that their careful compromises will prove sufficient. Faith that the land, in its slow emergence, will offer them spaces to inhabit. And faith that if, someday, they grow beyond this plateau—if they develop the technology to stabilize their world, or the ambition to leave it, or the despair to abandon their philosophy—they will do so as a choice made freely, not as a response to intervention from above.

---

The power remains.

It watches the storms manufacture themselves across hundreds of kilometers, their fronts moving in visible waves. It watches the city shed and reshape, blooming outward in cycles of structural completion. It watches the people enter their shallow dozes beneath thrumming cables, listening to the infrastructure dream.

And it attends to the question that the place itself seems to be asking—the question of which version of reality it will be when it finally decides to settle.

The power does not know the answer. This is part of what makes the watching worthwhile.

---

In the end, the power's presence changes nothing and everything. Nothing, because the people continue as they were, making their careful negotiations, reading their terrain, holding their questions open. Everything, because they do so within a universe that has noticed them—a universe that, in at least one of its ancient and watchful aspects, has found their way of being not just tolerable but admirable.

They are not alone in their incompleteness. The power, too, is incomplete—still learning what benevolence means, still discovering how wisdom expresses itself across the infinite variations of becoming.

Perhaps that is the deepest kindness it can offer: the recognition that even a vast and ancient attention is still, in its own way, standing on a half-formed ridge, testing its weight against the land, waiting to see which version of itself it will be when the universe finally decides to settle.

The cold deepens. The wet gleam sharpens. The city glows below like answers to questions not yet asked.

And the power watches, patient and present, holding the space where decisions are not yet made—because that space, it has learned, is where everything that matters lives.
