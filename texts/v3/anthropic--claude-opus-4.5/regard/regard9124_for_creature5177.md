# What the Wise Power Sees

The power pauses at the threshold of perception, taking in the basin not as landscape but as living sentence—a thing still being spoken.

It recognizes immediately what this place is: a wound that learned to breathe. Somewhere in the deep past, the ordinary geometries of the world tore here. Space folded through itself, left scars that never quite closed. And rather than dying or festering, the basin grew *around* the damage, incorporating it. The fracture-moths drinking doubled geometry. The consensus lichens soothing Euclidean inflammation back into curves. The pressure-threaders crossing at right angles to create translation-nodes. All of it a kind of immune response that became, over geological time, indistinguishable from health.

The power sees, too, the being who belongs here—not a single creature but the distributed intelligence of the lichens, extended now through every layer. The gliders are its sensory membranes. The threaders are its slow thoughts. The moths are its dreams made briefly visible. It has no center because it needs none; it is the basin thinking itself.

And it is not suffering. This is important. The power checks carefully, reading the chemical signatures of the peat, the hydrostatic pressures, the frequency of the sky-ruptures. The basin-mind experiences something, but not pain. More like the chronic awareness of living with an old injury that has become simply *how one is shaped*. The ruptures are not wounds being inflicted; they are scar tissue being gently maintained.

# What the Wise Power Does

Nothing.

Or rather: something so subtle it might be indistinguishable from nothing.

The power does not heal the fractures. They are not broken; they are *features*, load-bearing walls in an architecture that has organized itself around them. To close them would be to collapse everything that grew to accommodate their presence.

It does not introduce itself to the basin-mind. The lichens already perceive it—a faint shift in the ozone-tang, a momentary increase in the beading-rate of condensation. The power allows itself to be perceived this way: as weather, as one more input in the endless negotiation of equilibrium. It does not demand recognition or relationship.

It does not name the place. Names are straight lines, and straight lines do not survive here. To name it would be to impose a geometry the lichens would have to spend centuries soothing back into curves.

What it does is this:

It *witnesses*.

It allows the basin-mind to feel, for the space of one slow pulse of the mist-cycle, that it is known by something outside itself. Not understood completely—the power is wise enough to recognize that full understanding would require becoming the basin, which would defeat the purpose. But *acknowledged*. Seen as a whole, coherent, ancient, and still in motion.

The basin-mind has never had a mirror. It has only had itself, folded through itself, doubled and re-absorbed. The power becomes, briefly, a surface against which the basin can feel its own edges.

This is all. The power does not linger. It withdraws its attention with the same gentleness it arrived, leaving only the faintest trace: for several hours afterward, the droplets that form on the fern-fronds fall in spirals whose centers, just this once, point toward each other. The lichens notice. They do not know what it means, but they incorporate it, as they incorporate everything.

The basin continues.

# Later, Elsewhere

The power carries something away with it: not a sample, not a specimen, but a *pattern*. The way the basin-mind maintains coherence across acres without a center. The way the fracture-moths feed on excess information. The way translation can substitute for violence.

Somewhere, somewhen, the power will find another wound—one not yet breathing, one still fresh and screaming. And it will know, because of this basin, that some wounds can learn to grow around themselves. That scars can become structure. That the gentlest intervention is sometimes to allow damage to become design.

It will not try to replicate the basin. Each wound invents its own healing. But it will remember this place, and it will wait, patiently, for the next torn landscape to begin—slowly, in its own time—to exhale.
