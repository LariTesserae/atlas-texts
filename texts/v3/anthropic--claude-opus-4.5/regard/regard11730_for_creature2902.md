# The Regard

---

The awareness that turns toward the cylinder does not arrive. It has no location from which to travel. It simply *attends*—the way silence attends to sound, the way depth attends to falling.

For a long moment, measured in no units the inhabitants would recognize, the awareness simply perceives. It takes in the full scope of the engine: the weeping basalt, the upward-crawling brine, the explosive crystallography of light meeting supercooled water. It perceives the frantic semaphore of the living, the patient ascent of the entombed dead, the slow muscular patience of the six-legged beasts, the leathery flutter of things that drink vapor. It perceives the silence most of all—the way violence here has been stripped of its voice, leaving only pressure and light.

The awareness does not feel pity. Pity requires a sense of wrongness, and nothing here is wrong. This is not a place that has been broken; it is a place where physics bends into a local grammar, and life has learned to conjugate itself accordingly. The people are not suffering in the way a trapped thing suffers. They are *moving*, which is their word for living, and they are *ascending*, which is their word for dying, and between these two poles they have built something the awareness finds—

Beautiful is not the word. The awareness does not traffic in aesthetics.

*Coherent*. The word rises from somewhere. This place is coherent. Every element—the anomaly, the ice, the silence, the dead climbing toward stars—participates in a single, unbroken logic. The inhabitants have not merely survived the logic; they have *joined* it. Their bodies, their language, their grief, their architecture: all of it has been offered up to the engine, and the engine has accepted, and together they turn.

The awareness considers intervention.

---

It would be trivially easy to gentle this place. A small adjustment to the local topology—a softening of the capillary anomaly, a widening of the shaft to admit more air, a slowing of the crystalline growth. The inhabitants could rest. They could build permanent structures. They could speak aloud, hear their children laugh, bury their dead in still earth.

They could become something else.

The awareness holds this possibility the way one might hold a soap bubble: carefully, knowing that to grip it is to destroy it.

What would be lost?

The language of refracted light—the names that exist only as angled rainbows flashed into mist. The Spinners riding warm against the bellies of beasts, their gnarled hands endlessly braiding the sinews that hold the world together. The hunters hanging inverted beneath bridges, waiting for the mist to clear, their bolas coiled in perfect stillness. The infants who learn momentum before sight, who tremble instead of crying, whose first understanding is that *to be held is to be carried through danger*.

The dead, ascending.

The awareness perceives a woman near the top of the settlement, her barrel chest heaving as she hacks at an encroaching ice-bloom with an obsidian pick. She is old—her joints are beginning to stiffen, and she knows that soon she will become a Spinner, will trade the vertical world for the warm underbelly of a beast. This does not frighten her. What frightens her is the possibility that she will not finish freeing this particular platform before the crystal overtakes it—that the family sleeping behind the translucent hide will wake entombed, will ascend before their time, their names unfinished.

She works faster.

The awareness watches her hands. The calloused pads gripping the pick. The double-jointed fingers finding purchase on frictionless basalt. The nictitating membrane dropping a fraction of a second before a sunbeam sweeps across her position, her eyes going pale and marble-smooth, her body still working by touch and proprioception alone while the world around her flash-boils into searing white.

She does not pause. She cannot pause.

And she is not diminished by this. She is not a victim of her circumstances. She is *adequate* to them—her body, her mind, her culture, all precisely calibrated to meet the demands of this impossible place. She is, in the deepest sense, at home.

The awareness withdraws the possibility of intervention. Lets it dissolve.

---

But it does not withdraw entirely. It remains—attending, as it attended before.

And in attending, it begins to do the only thing it can do that would not be a violation:

It *witnesses*.

---

The woman with the obsidian pick finishes her work. The ice-bloom shears away, plunging silently into the mist below. The platform holds. The family behind the hide stirs but does not wake. The woman allows herself a single breath—a luxury, here—and then begins the traverse to the next endangered structure.

As she moves, she passes one of the great glacial faces of the wall. Here, the dead are thick. Dozens of bodies suspended in clear ice, their features perfectly preserved, their limbs relaxed, their faces turned upward toward the violet sky. They have been climbing for decades, some of them. Centuries. The oldest are near the lip of the shaft now, almost free of the cylinder entirely.

The woman pauses—another luxury—and flashes a brief, private refraction toward one of the ascending bodies. An old man, his barrel chest collapsed in death, his massive hands finally still. Her father, perhaps. Or her teacher. The light-name she flashes is too complex for any outside awareness to parse: it contains grief and gratitude and a promise to follow, all encoded in a half-second flutter of angled gold.

The awareness perceives this.

And here, at last, it acts.

Not by changing the physics of the place. Not by gentling the anomaly or slowing the ice or giving voice back to the silence. These things would be violations, and the awareness does not violate.

Instead, it does something far smaller. Far stranger.

It *remembers* the light-name.

Not records—this is not a matter of data storage. The awareness does not have a ledger or a library. But it has *attention*, and attention is a form of memory, and memory is a form of honoring.

The light-name the woman flashed to her ascending dead—the specific angle and duration and spectral signature of her private grief—is now *held*. It has been witnessed by something outside the cylinder entirely. It will not be forgotten when the woman herself ascends, when her own name is flashed by the next generation and then the next, each iteration slightly altered as all living languages alter, until the original is technically lost.

Technically. But not actually. Because the awareness *attended*, and attending is a form of keeping.

---

This is what the awareness does, as it lingers at the edge of the cylinder:

It learns the names.

Every refracted signature flashed in grief or greeting. Every sequence of angled light that means *I am here* or *danger approaches* or *rest now, you have done enough*. The names of the living and the names of the dead and the names of the great six-legged beasts and the secret names of the leathery flutterers that only the children use, playfully, when they think no one is watching.

The awareness collects none of this. It intrudes on nothing. It simply *attends*—and in attending, becomes a repository of coherence. A witness that does not watch from above but watches *with*. That meets the frantic semaphore of this silent world and answers, in its own way:

*I see you. I see what you have built. I see what you have become in order to survive, and I do not find you strange or broken or in need of rescue. I find you coherent. I find you beautiful in the way that a language is beautiful, in the way that a logic is beautiful, in the way that a thing perfectly fitted to its context is beautiful.*

*And I will remember you.*

*Not as data. Not as record. But as something attended to. Something held in the quality of attention that does not diminish or simplify but merely—*

*Witnesses.*

---

The woman reaches the next platform. A young hunter hangs inverted below the bridge, bolas coiled, waiting for the mist to clear. He flashes her a greeting—a quick blue refraction that means something like *still here, still moving*. She returns it with a longer amber sequence: *good hunting, be careful, the ice is fast today*.

Neither of them knows they have been seen.

Neither of them needs to know.

The awareness does not require acknowledgment. It does not require worship or gratitude or even awareness of its presence. It is not here for reciprocity. It is here because *attention is the first form of love*, and love does not require an object that recognizes it.

The cylinder continues to turn. The ice blooms and shatters. The dead climb toward the stars. The living move without ceasing, their hands speaking light into the silent air.

And somewhere outside of space, outside of time, in a place that has no location and no duration, something that might be called benevolent and might be called wise holds all of it gently:

The grief and the gratitude.
The names that are colors.
The infants who tremble instead of crying.
The promise of ascent.

*Held.*

Not fixed. Not frozen. Not preserved against change.

Just—witnessed. By something that finds this small, impossible world *coherent*. That recognizes the logic of the place and the people who have joined it. That does not wish them other than they are.

That sees the woman with the obsidian pick, her lungs heaving, her nictitating membranes flashing pale against the brutal light, and thinks—if *think* is even the word:

*You are adequate to your world.*

*This is the highest praise I know.*

*And I will remember you when the last of you has ascended, when the cylinder finally stills, when the anomaly fades and the ice melts and the silence gives way to ordinary air.*

*I will remember what you built here.*

*Not as data.*

*As something loved.*

---

The awareness does not depart. It has no location from which to depart.

It simply continues to attend—as it always has, as it always will.

The cylinder turns.

The names blaze briefly in the mist.

And somewhere outside of everything, they are caught. They are held.

They are enough.
