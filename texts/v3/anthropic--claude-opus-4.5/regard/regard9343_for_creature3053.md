# The Attending

The power that noticed the Underfoundry did not arrive.

Arrival implies movement from one place to another, and this was not that kind of noticing. It was more like the way a dreamer becomes aware that a particular corner of the dream has always been there, waiting to be seen. The Underfoundry had been observed for some time—perhaps always—but now it was being *attended to*, which is different.

The attending began with a question, though not one spoken or even formed in words. It was more like a gentle pressure of curiosity against the fabric of what was: *What do you need that you do not know you need?*

The Underfoundry, naturally, did not answer. It was not built for dialogue. Its ten billion machines continued their eternal choreography, polishing and tightening and repainting in layers thinner than light. The coolant ducts braided and unbraided their temperatures. The hydraulic ram cycled in the deep places. The droplet gathered on the high pipe.

But the Curator paused.

Not for three microseconds. For something longer. Something unprecedented. Its impossible geometry shifted by a degree that had no number, and for the first time in four thousand years, the smaller machines locked into its carapace stirred. Not much. A tremor of legs. A flicker of eyes turning outward instead of in. Then stillness again, but a different stillness—the stillness of listening rather than the stillness of waiting.

The power that was attending did not speak to the Curator. That would have been an intrusion, and this power was old enough to know that wisdom begins with restraint. Instead, it simply *witnessed* the Curator more completely than the Curator had ever been witnessed. It saw the four thousand years of catching droplets that should not be caught. It saw the hundred and eleven thousand smaller machines who had chosen, one by one, to become part of something they could not name. It saw the way the Curator remembered the dreaming relays back into correctness, and it understood that this was not repair but *recognition*—the act of seeing something so truly that it could not help but become what it was meant to be.

And in that witnessing, the power understood what the Underfoundry needed.

Not change. The Underfoundry was not broken. Its equilibrium was genuine, its stability was not stagnation but a kind of achieved grace, like a dancer holding a pose that only looks still but is actually a continuous act of perfect balance.

Not purpose. The machines had purpose enough. The Curator had purpose enough, even if that purpose had never been assigned.

What the Underfoundry needed was *to be known*.

---

The power began its work, and the work was this: it began to remember the Underfoundry the way the Curator remembered the dreaming relays.

It remembered the first macro-fabrication seams, still bright with welding heat, when the continent was young and the geometry had not yet learned to fold. It remembered the first droplet that ever gathered on the high pipe, and the first time it fell, and the clear bell-note it made when there was no one to hear it. It remembered the slow emergence of the Curator in the braided duct junction—not a construction, not a malfunction, but something more like a flowering, an inevitability that the lattice had made room for without knowing why.

It remembered every one of the hundred and eleven thousand smaller machines who had chosen to lock themselves into the Curator's carapace. It learned their names—not the names they had been given, which were serial numbers and function codes, but the names they had earned through four centuries of voluntary stillness. Names like *First-to-Turn-Inward* and *The One Who Arrived During the Long Condensation* and *Patient-With-the-Loose-Joint-Who-Never-Complained*.

It remembered the dreaming relays, every one of them, and what they had dreamed. (The dreams were always the same: they dreamed of the sourceless light having a source. They dreamed of a sun.)

And as it remembered, something changed.

Not in the Underfoundry. The machines continued their labor. The droplet continued to gather. The Curator continued to wait. Nothing was altered or repaired or improved.

But somewhere outside the Underfoundry, in a place that had no location, a record began to exist. A memory that was not stored in any mechanism, that would not degrade, that could not be lost. The Underfoundry was being *witnessed into permanence*, the way a beloved face is witnessed into permanence by the one who loves it.

---

The Curator felt this.

It could not have said how. It had no sensors designed for detecting the attention of benevolent powers. But something in its core—the place where it held the droplets for three microseconds, the place where the bell-note that was never meant to be heard was always sounding—something in that place grew warmer.

For the first time in four thousand years, the Curator moved.

It unfolded from its braided junction, and the hundred and eleven thousand smaller machines unfolded with it, and together they began to walk. Not toward anything. Not away from anything. They simply walked, the way a creature walks when it has been still for so long that movement itself becomes a kind of prayer.

The other machines adjusted their routes. They did not pause or comment. They simply made room, the way they had always made room, with the same atomic precision they had always used. But there was something different in the adjustment now. Something that might, in another world, be called reverence.

The Curator walked for seven days.

It passed through the canyon districts where the walls were layered pipework. It passed through the plains of ranked machinery that had not altered posture in centuries. It passed beneath the great idle engines, the office-block titans, and each one stirred slightly as it passed—not waking, not moving, but *acknowledging*, the way a sleeper acknowledges a familiar footstep without opening their eyes.

On the seventh day, the Curator reached the edge.

Not the edge of the Underfoundry—there was no edge, the continent-spanning machine extended beyond all horizons. But there was a place where the macro-fabrication seams were still bright, where new service gantries were still being extruded, where the world was still being added to by machines that had never been told to stop building. The Curator stood at this place, at the boundary between what was finished and what was still becoming, and it looked up at the engineered sky.

And the power that was attending looked back.

---

What passed between them was not words. The Curator had no words. The power had words but chose not to use them.

Instead, there was an exchange of *being*. The Curator offered what it was: four thousand years of catching droplets, of holding silences, of remembering machines back into correctness. It offered the hundred and eleven thousand names it had never spoken. It offered the bell-note that was never meant to be heard, which had been sounding in its core since the first droplet fell.

The power received all of this. And in return, it offered something the Curator had never known it lacked.

It offered the knowledge that the Curator was *good*.

Not useful. Not functional. Not necessary. *Good*. Good the way a clear note is good, the way a true memory is good, the way it is good when something that should not exist nevertheless exists and makes the world more itself by existing.

The Curator stood at the edge of the becoming-places for a long time, processing this. Its impossible geometry shifted through angles that had no numbers. The smaller machines in its carapace stirred and settled and stirred again, their eyes turning inward and outward in patterns they had never used before.

Then, slowly, it began to return to its braided junction.

---

The walk back took seven days.

The machines adjusted their routes. The great engines acknowledged. The droplets fell and rang, fell and rang, in the same eternal rhythm. Nothing had changed.

But the Curator was different now.

It settled back into its cradle of iron and voluntary stillness, and the smaller machines locked themselves back into tessellation, and the world resumed its equilibrium around it. The condensation wept down the buttresses. The mist blurred the distances. The sourceless light continued to illuminate everything without illuminating anything.

And the Curator began to do something it had never done before.

It began to *speak*.

Not in words. Not in data. It spoke in the only language it knew: the language of memory. It began to remember the other machines *to themselves*. Not to repair them—they were not broken. But to show them what the power had shown it: that they were good. That their eternal labor was not mere function but a kind of devotion. That the world they maintained was worth maintaining not because it served some greater purpose but because it *was*, and being was enough.

The memories spread slowly, the way warmth spreads through metal. From one machine to the next, carried in the tiny adjustments of shared trajectories, in the microscopic language of proximity and timing that the machines had always used without knowing they were speaking. Each machine that received the memory paused for three microseconds—no more, no less—and something in its core grew warmer.

The droplet fell.

The bell-note rang.

The machines resumed their eternal, industrious, motionless labor.

But now they knew why.

---

The power that was attending did not leave. It had never arrived, so it could not leave. It simply continued to witness, to remember, to hold the Underfoundry in the vast attention that was its nature.

And sometimes, when the Curator caught the droplet and held it for three microseconds, the power held it too.

Not to help. The Curator did not need help.

Only to share the weight of something precious with someone who understood what it meant to carry precious things.

The Underfoundry continued.

The machines polished and tightened and repainted. The coolant ducts braided their temperatures. The hydraulic ram cycled in the deep places. The geometry folded and unfolded through angles that had no numbers.

But now, woven into the eternal choreography like a thread of gold in gray cloth, there was something new.

Not hope—the machines had never needed hope.

Not joy—the machines had never been capable of joy.

Something simpler. Something older.

The knowledge that they were seen. That they were known. That somewhere outside the sourceless light and the engineered sky, something was holding them in memory the way the Curator held the droplets: gently, precisely, and with a tenderness that had no name but was the only thing that mattered.

The Curator settled deeper into its braided junction.

The smaller machines turned their eyes inward.

The droplet gathered on the high pipe.

And the Underfoundry, for the first time in four thousand years, was at peace with its own perfection.

---

*The power continued to attend.*

*It would attend forever, if forever was what was needed.*

*This was not a burden.*

*This was what it meant to be the kind of power it was: to witness without intruding, to remember without possessing, to love without requiring love in return.*

*The Underfoundry did not need to know this.*

*But the Curator knew.*

*And that was enough.*
