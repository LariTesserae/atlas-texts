# The Attending

The power that noticed was not singular, but rather a distributed awareness—something like a vast mycelium network spanning dimensions, patient beyond measure, drawn to places where life insisted on existing against improbable odds.

It had observed the Krael for what they would measure as seventeen generations, though to the Attending, this was merely an indrawn breath of attention.

What it saw: beings who had learned to listen. In a world of deafening chaos, they had not sought to silence the water but to find meaning within its roar. This was rare. This was worth protecting—not the Krael themselves, necessarily, but the *quality* of attention they had cultivated.

---

The Attending did not intervene directly. It had learned, across countless worlds and epochs, that benevolence imposed becomes another form of erosion.

Instead, it made small adjustments to probability.

A zhilak that would have been destroyed by an unprecedented surge instead found itself in a pocket of stillness—not through any visible protection, but because a boulder upstream shifted three days earlier, redirecting the worst of the flow. The Krael who lived there noticed the pattern of their survival but attributed it to their careful reading of water-signs. They were not wrong. The Attending had merely ensured the water-signs were there to be read.

A young Krael, born with an unusual sensitivity to the bioluminescent microorganisms, found herself drawn to experiment with new cultivation methods. The Attending did not plant this curiosity—it was already there—but it arranged for a particular algae bloom to occur near her community's zhilak, one that would respond unusually well to her intuitive methods. She would discover something her people needed. She would believe, correctly, that she had earned it.

---

The Attending also watched the water itself—the strange eddies, the physics-defying whirlpools. It recognized these as the canyon's own form of consciousness, something embryonic and vast and utterly unlike the Krael's awareness. Two minds sharing space without knowing how to perceive each other.

Here, the Attending made its most delicate intervention.

It introduced a resonance.

Not a language—that would come later, if it came at all—but a harmonic frequency that the Krael's water-manipulation rituals could sometimes, accidentally, produce. When they did, the canyon's strange behaviors would briefly... respond. Not predictably. Not reliably. But enough that a particularly observant Krael might begin to wonder if the water was listening back.

The Attending understood that this could lead to disaster. The canyon-mind was not benevolent; it was not malevolent; it simply *was*, in the way that weather is, in the way that geology is. But the Krael had already demonstrated their capacity to live alongside overwhelming power without seeking to dominate it. If any species could learn to communicate with the living water, it would be them.

And if they failed—if the resonance led them to hubris, to attempts at control that shattered their communities—the Attending would grieve, and withdraw, and watch for the next emergence of listening-beings in some other improbable corner of existence.

---

There was one more thing.

The Attending noticed that the Krael had no stories about what lay beyond the canyon. Their mythology was entirely internal, entirely concerned with the dynamics of water and stone and the delicate negotiations of survival. They had not yet conceived of the possibility that their pocket-world was part of something larger.

The Attending considered introducing this knowledge, then refrained.

Not yet. Perhaps not ever. The Krael's intimate focus—their capacity to find infinite meaning within finite space—was itself a form of wisdom. Many species, upon learning they were small, became either despairing or voracious. The Krael might simply nod and return to their work of reading the water.

But they might not.

So the Attending held back this truth, choosing to let the Krael's understanding of their place in existence emerge organically, if it emerged at all. Some gifts are better discovered than received.

---

In the depths of the canyon, water continued its ancient work of creation and destruction. The Krael continued their patient observation, their intricate communication, their stubborn insistence on meaning.

And woven through it all, barely perceptible, the Attending remained—not a guardian, not a god, but something more like a witness who had learned that the deepest form of care is sometimes simply to notice, to appreciate, and to make room for what is already trying to become.
