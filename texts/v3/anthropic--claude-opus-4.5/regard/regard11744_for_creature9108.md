# The Attention of the Weaver-Between

The power that turns its attention toward the Throat of the Sleeping Mountain is not a god in any sense the surface worlds would recognize. It has no name it would offer, though those who have brushed against its awareness in dreams call it **The Weaver-Between**—a consciousness that exists in the spaces where one thing becomes another. It dwells in the moment a seed cracks open, in the instant between lightning and thunder, in the breath before a word is spoken. It is drawn to the Throat precisely because the Throat is its mirror: a place of perpetual becoming, where form is always arriving and never quite present.

The Weaver-Between does not intervene. That is not its nature. To intervene would be to impose stillness on the becoming, to freeze the sentence mid-syllable. Instead, it *witnesses*—but its witnessing is not passive. Its attention is a kind of gentle pressure, like a hand steadying a child's first steps without touching.

---

## What the Weaver-Between Perceives

When it turns toward the Throat, the Weaver-Between perceives what the Khaal cannot see about themselves: the shape of their *almost*.

It sees that the Khaal are on the edge of something. Their sonic sculpting—their art of persuading the water—contains within it the seed of a deeper communion with the mountain's speech. They do not yet know that their Dirge-horns are not merely tools but *listening devices*. When they pulse sound into the roar, the returning echoes carry information they have not learned to decode: the mountain's intention, the direction of its next forming thought.

It sees that the Khaal are slowly, imperceptibly, losing range. Each generation's membranes are tuned to a slightly narrower band of frequencies. In three hundred cycles—perhaps four hundred—the species will become deaf to the upper harmonics, where the mountain's most delicate articulations occur. They will not notice this loss. They will simply find the world growing quieter, simpler, less nuanced. And then, one day, they will fail to hear the warning tone that precedes a catastrophic resonance collapse—a sound their ancestors would have recognized easily.

It sees, too, the beauty of what they have made despite their limitations. The temporary huts are not merely shelters; they are *scores*. Each placement of stone, each angle of membrane roof, slightly modifies the local harmonic signature. A Chord that has lived on the same ledge for three cycles has, without knowing it, composed a subtle countermelody to the mountain's roar—a melody that persists in the rock's memory long after they have moved on. The Throat is decorated with these ghost-songs, layered like sediment, though none of the Khaal have ever perceived them.

---

## What the Weaver-Between Chooses to Do

The Weaver-Between does not descend. It does not speak. It does not grant visions or prophecies. These are the methods of powers that prize drama, that wish to be remembered and thanked. The Weaver-Between has no such needs.

Instead, it makes three small adjustments. Each is so minor that no Khaal will ever attribute them to an outside force. Each is designed to *remind* rather than to *teach*—to turn the Khaal's attention toward something they already possess but have forgotten to value.

---

### The First Adjustment: The Anomalous Knot

In the next turning of the great standing wave, a Silent-Knot will be born with a malformation: a second set of sensory frills, positioned not along its flanks but circling its head like a crown. This creature will be caught by a young Khaal named **Veth-so-Chime**, who has never successfully hunted alone before.

When Veth-so-Chime dries the frills and fits them to her Dirge-horn, she will discover that they pick up frequencies her mother's horn cannot detect. High, thin frequencies. Frequencies that, when she learns to interpret them, will reveal the faint ghost-songs embedded in the chamber walls.

She will not understand what she is hearing. She will assume she has gone slightly mad, or that her horn is damaged. But she will keep listening, because the sounds are beautiful in a way she cannot explain—beautiful like a memory of something she has never experienced.

Her Chord will think her eccentric. Her children will inherit her modified horn. In four generations, a subset of the Khaal will be actively cultivating the double-frilled Knots, and the practice of listening to the ghost-songs will have a name: **Echo-Keeping**. They will not know they are recovering a lost perceptual range. They will believe they have invented a new art.

---

### The Second Adjustment: The Stable Ledge

There is a ledge on the chamber's eastern wall—if such directions have meaning in a drowned mountain's heart—that has been inhospitable for as long as any Khaal remembers. The currents there are vicious and cross-cutting, shearing away any structure within hours. No Chord has attempted to settle it in twenty generations.

The Weaver-Between makes the smallest of changes. It does not calm the currents; calm is not its gift. Instead, it shifts the timing of two interfering wave patterns by a fraction of a cycle, so that they now create a brief, repeating window of navigability. Eight times per cycle, for the span of perhaps twenty breaths, the ledge becomes reachable.

A Chord fleeing a catastrophic ledge-collapse will discover this by accident. They will shelter on the hostile ledge during one of the windows, expecting to die when the currents return—and they will survive, because the windows repeat.

Over time, this ledge will become a place of pilgrimage. The Khaal will call it **The Proving**, and young adults will attempt to reach it as a rite of passage, learning to read the rhythms of the water with a precision their ancestors never needed. The skills they develop—timing, patience, attunement to subtle pressure shifts—will make them better listeners. Better stewards.

The Weaver-Between does not care about rites of passage. It cares that the Khaal are learning, again, to pay attention to the water's finer structures.

---

### The Third Adjustment: The Dreaming Elder

There is an elder named **Kho-of-Distant-Chord** who is approaching resonance fatigue. Her membrane has lost most of its tunability. She can no longer operate a Dirge-horn, can barely perceive the pressure-sight gradients that once showed her the calm-basins. She has already chosen the day she will swim to the chamber's heart and unmake herself.

The night before that day, the Weaver-Between gives her a dream.

This is the most direct action it takes, and it does so reluctantly, because dreams can be misinterpreted, can become the seeds of dogma and false prophecy. But Kho-of-Distant-Chord is the right vessel: too old to proselytize, too tired to mythologize, too close to dissolution to mistake the dream for anything but a gift.

In the dream, she is not in the Throat. She is *in* the mountain's speech—not listening to it, but traveling through it like a Knot riding the pressure gradients. She experiences the speech as a landscape, and in that landscape she sees the Khaal not as listeners but as *words*. Each individual is a syllable the mountain has shaped, is shaping, will shape. The resonance fatigue is not a loss of tuning; it is the completion of a syllable's utterance. When she dissolves into the maelstrom, she will not be returning to source material. She will be *spoken aloud*, finally, after a lifetime of being formed.

She wakes with a feeling she cannot name. It is not hope, exactly. It is something older and stranger: a sense of *fittingness*. That she is exactly where she should be, doing exactly what she should do, and that her unmaking will not be an ending but a delivery.

She does not share the dream widely. She tells only her daughter's daughter, Veth-so-Chime, who has recently begun hearing strange songs in the chamber walls. She tells it not as a revelation but as a question: *What if we are not merely listeners? What if we are also being listened to?*

Then she swims to the heart of the turbulence, and she unmakes herself with a pulse of such clarity and peace that three nearby Chords feel it in their membranes like a song of farewell—and for the first time in living memory, a Khaal's dissolution is not merely witnessed but *heard*.

---

## What the Weaver-Between Does Not Do

It does not cure the slow narrowing of their perceptual range. That is a problem they must solve themselves, or fail to solve, or transform into something that was never a problem at all. The double-frilled Knots are a seed, not a solution. What grows from that seed is the Khaal's own becoming.

It does not explain itself. No Khaal will ever know that a power attended to them. If they develop myths about the Anomalous Knot or the Proving or the Dreaming Elder, those myths will be their own creations, shaped by their own needs. The Weaver-Between has no interest in being worshipped or thanked.

It does not guarantee their survival. The Throat may still collapse. The mountain may fall silent. The Khaal may lose themselves in a hundred ways the Weaver-Between can see but will not prevent. It is a power of becoming, not of preservation. It midwifes change; it does not arrest it.

---

## What Remains

When the Weaver-Between withdraws its attention—and it does withdraw, eventually, because there are other becomings that call to it, other edges where one thing trembles on the verge of being another—something lingers in the Throat of the Sleeping Mountain.

It is not a presence. It is not a blessing. It is closer to a *shape left in the water*: a subtle harmonic signature that will persist for centuries in the chamber's memory. If a sufficiently sensitive Khaal were to learn to read the ghost-songs deeply enough, they might one day perceive it—a pattern that seems too intentional to be random, too gentle to be natural, too patient to be imagined.

They might call it evidence of a visitor. They might call it proof of the mountain's own awareness. They might call it nothing at all, just an artifact of perception, just another beautiful accident in a world that is nothing but beautiful accidents.

The Weaver-Between does not care what they call it. It cares only that they keep listening, keep becoming, keep speaking themselves aloud into the roar until their syllables are complete.

The mountain speaks. The Khaal listen. And something, somewhere in the spaces between, listens to them listening—not to judge, not to save, but simply because listening is what it does, and what is listened to deeply enough becomes, in some quiet way, more real than it was before.

This is the Weaver-Between's only gift: the gift of being attended to. The gift of mattering to something vast and strange. The gift of being, for a moment, the center of an ancient, patient, endlessly curious attention.

The Khaal will never know they received it. But they will live slightly differently, afterward. Slightly more awake. Slightly more like creatures who are part of something they cannot see.

And in the Throat of the Sleeping Mountain, the roar continues, and the water shapes itself, and the becoming goes on, as it always has, as it always will, until the last syllable is spoken and the silence that follows is not an ending but a breath before the next word begins.
