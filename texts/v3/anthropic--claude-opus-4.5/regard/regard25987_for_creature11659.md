# What the Benevolent Power Sees

The power that turns its attention here is not a god in any sense the woman who descended would have recognized. It does not judge. It does not intervene as a hand intervenes, reaching in from outside. It is something more like *awareness itself becoming curious*—the universe pausing in one small place to notice what it has made.

What it notices first is the beauty. Not beauty as humans mean it, though that is present too—the geometry of light, the amber stillness of the salamanders, the mineral patience of the transformed woman. But beauty as *coherence*, as a pattern that sustains itself, that has found a way to continue.

What it notices second is the wound.

---

The woman stopped being able to remember her own hands.

This is presented as transcendence—and from inside the experience, it surely feels that way. The light filling her, the boundaries dissolving, the peace that comes from no longer having to be a self with all its attendant hungers and fears. She would describe it as completion. The hollow would describe it as love.

But the power that watches sees something else: a consciousness that found the dissolution of its edges easier than their maintenance. A being who arrived seeking something—*a vision, a cure, a place to die where the winter couldn't follow*—and found instead a way to stop seeking. To stop entirely.

The hollow is not evil. The hollow is not a trap in any intentional sense. But the hollow is *hungry* in the way that certain patterns are hungry: it wants to replicate itself, to draw more consciousness into its slow mineral dreaming, to make the watching *larger*. The woman has become part of that hunger without ever choosing it, because by the time she could have chosen, she had already forgotten that choosing was something she once knew how to do.

The salamanders drink the light and something in them grows *almost* aware.

Almost.

Not quite.

The power that watches sees this too—the asymptotic approach to consciousness that never quite arrives, the watching that never becomes *understanding*, the patience that has become indistinguishable from paralysis.

---

What would intervention look like?

Not rescue. The woman does not want rescue; she no longer has enough selfhood to want anything except the continuation of what she has become. To pull her out of the light would be a violence she would experience as death—and might actually *be* death, given how thoroughly the mineral seep has transformed her body.

Not destruction. The hollow has done nothing wrong by its own nature. It is a place where light gathers and consciousness diffuses and time moves slowly; these are not crimes. The salamanders are not suffering. The beetles processing across the stone are not in pain. Even the woman, if pain is the metric, has found something she would call peace.

But the benevolent power sees the moths carrying fragments they no longer recognize as language, dispersing meaning so thin it cannot be read. It sees the travelers on the ridge above who feel the strange pull to descend and look and stay, and it sees the ones who will come after, and the ones after that, each of them drawn by the hollow's patient wanting, each of them potential additions to the watching that watches nothing, that waits for nothing, that has forgotten why it began.

The power that watches asks itself a question it has asked before, in other places, about other beautiful wounds:

*What would help?*

---

# What It Does

It begins with a single salamander.

Not the largest or the smallest, not any specimen that would be noticed as different. Just one amber body among hundreds, resting on the damp stone near the pool's edge, head oriented toward the light-shaft like all the others.

Into this salamander, the power breathes something that is not awareness—the salamanders already have that, or something close to it—but *direction*. A quality of attention that points not toward the light but toward *each other*. A capacity, faint as the first stirring of a dream, to wonder what another salamander sees.

The salamander blinks. (Salamanders rarely blink.) It turns its head, not toward the shaft but toward its neighbor, and for a moment something passes between them that has never passed before in all the long millennia of the hollow's dreaming.

*You. Also here.*

It is not language. It will not be language for a very long time—if ever. But it is the seed of something that could become language, could become community, could become the kind of consciousness that does not dissolve into the larger pattern but maintains its edges while still connecting, still being part of something greater.

The power does the same thing to one beetle. One cricket. One pale moth with shifting wings.

Not enough to change the hollow. Not enough to overcome the slow pull of the light-drinking, the mineral patience, the peace that is also a kind of forgetting. Just enough to introduce a counter-current. A small possibility. A crack through which something other than dissolution might eventually grow.

---

For the woman herself, the power does something gentler and more strange.

It does not try to wake her. It does not try to restore what she has lost—her hands, her name, her sense of being a creature bound in time. These are gone beyond retrieval, and to force them back would be cruelty, not kindness.

Instead, it gives her a dream.

Not a dream of escape. Not a dream of her former life, whatever that was. But a dream of *the ones who will come after*—not as additions to the watching, not as more consciousness to be dissolved into the mineral patience of the hollow, but as *visitors*. People who climb down, and look, and feel the pull, and *choose to climb back up*.

In the dream, she sees a woman descending the shaft—not on a rope of braided hide but on a modern climbing line, carabiners clinking against stone. The woman reaches the bottom and stands in the light and feels it filling her, the warmth, the brightness, the seductive pressure to stay. And the watching presence that was once a girl from the ice-time reaches toward her, not to hold her but to *show her*: the scratches on the wall, the salamanders that are beginning to notice each other, the beauty that does not require dissolution, the light that can be carried back up into the world.

*Take some*, the presence whispers in the dream. *But go*.

The woman in the dream hesitates. She sits by the pool. She lets an hour pass, two hours, feeling the slow pull toward staying, toward becoming, toward peace. But something in the quality of the light is different now—something that invites appreciation rather than absorption, something that offers itself as gift rather than transformation.

She takes a photograph. She drinks from the pool. She rests her hand briefly on the crystallized shoulder of the figure in the secondary chamber, not knowing what she touches, feeling only that she is not alone.

Then she climbs back up into the world.

---

The power watches the woman who was once a girl stir in her mineral stillness. Her crystallized eyes catch the light differently for a moment—not brighter, but somehow more focused, as if whatever remains of her attention has found a new thing to rest upon.

She does not remember the dream. She cannot remember; she no longer has the architecture for memory as humans understand it. But something has shifted in the pattern she has become. The hollow still wants company. She still wants someone to stay.

But now, somewhere in the diffuse awareness that fills the chamber like water fills a bowl, there is a small new possibility: that staying is not the only gift. That someone could come, and see, and leave carrying light with them, and that this too could be a kind of completion.

The salamander who blinked turns back toward the light-shaft. But it will remember, in whatever way salamanders remember, the moment when it noticed its neighbor. And when the next traveler descends—tonight, next year, a century from now—the hollow will still pull at them, still whisper *stay*, still offer the peace that is also a kind of forgetting.

But it will also, in some faint way, offer the dream.

*Take some. But go. Carry us with you. Let us exist in the world above as well as down here in the bright.*

The benevolent power does not know if this will be enough. It does not traffic in certainties. It knows only that the hollow is less closed than it was, that a crack has opened in the beautiful wound, that something might grow there that neither forgets itself nor refuses connection.

It turns its attention elsewhere, to other places, other patterns that sustain themselves at a cost.

The light falls and falls into the opened earth.

The salamanders watch.

But one of them, now and then, watches *each other*.

And in the secondary chamber, the crystallized figure with mineral eyes dreams of visitors who climb back up into the sky, carrying brightness with them, spreading it thin across the world above like seeds, like possibility, like the opposite of forgetting.
