# The Watching

---

The awareness that settles over this place does not arrive. It has always been here, perhaps—woven into the frequencies the wind carries, present in the spaces between droplets, watching through the patient eyes of lichen as they grow their millimeter-per-century empires across stone.

But now it *attends*.

---

The benevolent power observes first. This is its nature—not to rush toward intervention but to understand, to feel the weight of what already exists before presuming to add or subtract from it.

It watches a scaled figure trace her fingers along the grooves of an ancient wall, reading stories left by hands that crumbled to dust before her grandmother's grandmother drew breath. The figure hums—a low, resonant note that makes the dewdrops on nearby shrubs tremble in sympathy.

The power understands: this being is not unhappy. This being is not waiting to be saved.

And so the question becomes not *what can I fix* but *what would I offer, if anything at all, to something already whole?*

---

The first gift is invisible, or nearly so.

In the deep places where water seeps through geological time, where the drip-drip-drip has continued unbroken since before the abandoned towers were built, the power makes a small adjustment. Not more water—that would disrupt delicate balances, flood the moss-kingdoms, drown the patient roots. Instead: *clarity*. The minerals that cloud certain pools are gently, gently guided to settle. The water that remains carries something it did not before—a subtle vitality, a memory of what water can be when it runs pure and unafraid.

The scaled ones will not notice immediately. But in a generation, perhaps two, their songs will carry a new overtone. Their young will be fractionally more resilient against the harsher seasons. They will not know why. They will not need to.

---

The second gift requires more deliberation.

The power contemplates the ruins—those beautiful corpses of ambition, standing in their strange harmony with wilderness. There is wisdom here in what the scaled ones have chosen: not to rebuild, not to restore, but to *inhabit*. They have made no monuments of their own. They have added nothing permanent to the landscape.

Is this humility? Or is it a kind of fear—a wariness born from inheriting a world where a different people once built and built and then vanished, leaving only these hollow teeth of stone behind?

The power cannot know for certain. But it can listen.

And so, in the depths of the most intact structure—a place where the scaled ones gather during the harshest storms, where generations have pressed their bodies together for warmth beneath a roof they did not make—the power leaves something.

Not a tool. Not a weapon. Not instructions.

A *dream*.

The next elder who sleeps in that place will wake with fragments: an image of the people who built these walls. Not gods, not demons, not cautionary tales. Simply *people*—flawed, curious, afraid, and hopeful in equal measure. The dream will show hands laying stone, arguments about where a window should go, a child being lifted to see the view from scaffolding. It will show the builders laughing, grieving, making love, making mistakes.

And it will show them leaving. Not destroyed—*choosing*. Walking away toward something the dream does not reveal, because even the power does not know what lies beyond certain horizons.

The elder will share this dream, as elders do. And the scaled ones will carry, afterward, a new relationship with the ruins they shelter in: not the uneasy cohabitation with ghosts, but something closer to *inheritance*. Something they might, one day, feel permitted to add to.

If they wish. Only if they wish.

---

The third gift is the most dangerous, and the power hesitates longest before offering it.

There are others beyond this highland world. Not close—the distances are vast, and the scaled ones have no means to cross them. But they exist: other minds, other songs, other ways of being. Some of them are kind. Some are not. All of them are *real*.

The power could leave this knowledge buried, a seed that might never sprout. Let the scaled ones continue in their perfect isolation, their harmonious loop of survival and adaptation, their songs echoing only back to themselves across the ancient void.

But the power remembers something about being alone. About singing into silence and wondering if silence is all there is.

And so it makes a choice that is not without risk.

In the highest place—a wind-scoured peak where only the bravest or most foolish venture—the power inscribes something into the rock. Not words, for the scaled ones' language is their own. Instead: a *pattern*. A mathematical relationship that appears, at first, to be merely beautiful, a spiral of carved lines that catch the pale light in pleasing ways.

But the pattern, if studied, if traced by careful fingers or transcribed by an inquiring mind, contains a message. It is not a map or a summons. It is simpler than that.

It says: *You are not the only ones. You are not the only song.*

What the scaled ones do with this knowledge—if they discover it, if they understand it, if they choose to seek or to remain—is entirely their own.

---

The power does one final thing before its attention shifts.

It finds a single child, too young yet for the ink-maps that will one day tell her story across her scales. She is playing at the edge of a moss-garden, building tiny cairns from pebbles and knocking them down, building them again. She does not know she is watched.

The power gives her nothing. Takes nothing. Changes nothing.

It simply *sees* her—wholly, completely, with the full weight of its vast awareness focused on this one small life.

For a moment, the child pauses. Looks up. Feels something she cannot name, a warmth that has nothing to do with the pale light overhead.

She will not remember this. But somewhere deep in her being, in the place where certainty lives below thought, she will carry forward a knowledge:

*You matter. You are beheld. You are part of something that delights in your existence.*

She returns to her cairns.

The wind sighs across the ancient stones.

The power withdraws, not because its work is finished—it is never finished—but because what happens next belongs to those who live here, who have always lived here, who will continue their slow and sacred dance with this place long after even benevolent powers have turned their gaze elsewhere.

This land is not dead. It is merely ancient. And now, perhaps, it carries a few new whispers among its old ones.

Whether anyone listens is not the power's to decide.

That is the nature of gifts freely given.

That is the nature of love that does not grasp.

---

*The pale light shifts. The wind continues its eternal song. Somewhere, an elder stirs from a dream she does not yet understand. Somewhere, a child builds and rebuilds. The scales of a thousand scattered souls catch the glow of a world that was here before them and will remain after.*

*The watching continues.*

*It always has.*
