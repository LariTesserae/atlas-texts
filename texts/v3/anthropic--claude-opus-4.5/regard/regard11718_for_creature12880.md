# The Attention of the Distant

*A consideration in three movements*

---

## I. The Watching

The benevolent power does not arrive.

This is the first and most important thing to understand. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not announce itself with light or silence or any disruption to the eternal B-flat that has become the world's own heartbeat. To arrive would be to impose, and imposition is the opposite of wisdom when the subject is a world that has already been so thoroughly imposed upon—by its own children, no less, who did not choose to be born as beautiful parasites.

Instead, the power *attends*. 

It becomes present the way a question becomes present in a mind that has finally grown quiet enough to hear it. It settles into the spaces between the grinding of gears, into the microscopic pauses between one piston-stroke and the next. It does not judge the Churn. It does not weep for the Substrate. It does not feel outrage at the Carvers for what they have made of themselves and their world.

It simply watches, with a patience that makes the Carvers' own unhurried grace seem frantic by comparison.

And in watching, it begins to understand:

The Carvers do not need rescue. They are not prisoners of their industry—they *are* their industry, as the heart is not a prisoner of the body it serves. They do not need the machines silenced, because the silence would kill them as surely as it would kill the shamans' ability to hear the Substrate's dreams. They have built themselves into a system, and the system, for all its rust and sorrow, *works*. It has worked for longer than the power can easily count.

What the Carvers need is something far more delicate than salvation.

They need to be *seen*.

---

## II. The Gift

The power considers many interventions and sets them aside, one by one, like a surgeon laying out instruments and then covering them again with cloth.

It could heal the Substrate—accelerate the growth, enrich the sap, undo ten thousand years of bleeding. But this would upset a balance the Carvers have calibrated their entire existence around. The pumps would overflow. The Weep would become a flood. The careful rhythm of maintenance would shatter, and the Carvers would die in the wreckage of abundance, drowning in the very life they had learned to carefully sip.

It could grant the Carvers new knowledge—the mathematics of sustainability, the engineering of closed systems, the wisdom to extract without depleting. But the Carvers already know these things. Their shamans have been whispering them for generations. Knowledge is not what they lack. What they lack is the *belief* that another way is possible, and belief cannot be installed like a new gear.

It could speak to them, in their language of percussive taps, and tell them what they are: children of the Substrate, galls that learned to think, tumors that learned to love. But they know this too. They have always known. It is the central truth of their religion, their art, their sorrow. To speak it would only add another voice to the chorus of grief they already sing in their sound-dampened chambers.

So the power does something else.

It finds a single Carver—a young one, her carapace still bright with the copper-green of recent emergence, her fine-fingered hands still clumsy with the carving tools, her feet still learning to read the deeper vibrations. She is working alone on a minor task, clearing a Weep-channel on an abandoned platform, far from her crew. It is make-work, given to her because she is young and not yet trusted with the great machines.

The power does not speak to her. It does not illuminate her or fill her with vision.

Instead, it makes a single, small adjustment to the local rules of the world.

For exactly one hour, the distances become fixed. The shadows fall correctly. The auditory ghosts go silent. The platform where she works becomes, for sixty minutes, a place where reality is not negotiable—where the soft edges of the world grow firm, where what she sees is simply and only what is there.

The young Carver freezes. Her oil-dark eyes do not blink; they wipe, once, twice, three times. Her feet press hard against the ground-flesh, searching for the vibration that will explain this wrongness.

But there is no wrongness. There is only *clarity*.

She looks up, across the expanse, at a pillar she has seen every day of her short life. It has always been five miles away, or three, or ten—a thing of haze and uncertain distance, its carved windows a suggestion rather than a fact.

Now it is *there*. Fixed. Real. She can count the windows. She can see the color of the sap leaking from an old wound near its base. She can see, on a platform much like her own, another Carver—an old one, carapace nearly black with age and grit—looking back at her.

For the first time in her life, she sees her world as a place rather than a process.

And something shifts inside her, in the space where her people keep their sorrow.

---

## III. The After

The hour passes. The distances resume their gentle warfare with perception. The shadows remember their impossible angles. The auditory ghosts return, and the young Carver hears the echo of a hammer blow from a crew that packed up yesterday.

But she is different now.

She returns to her crew, and she does not speak of what happened—there are no words in the percussive language for "the world became real." But her feet have learned something new. They have learned that beneath the negotiable surface, there is a fixed truth. The Substrate is not just a dream; it is also a *place*. The pillars are not just processes; they are also *things*. And the Carvers themselves are not just functions of the system; they are also *lives*.

She begins to carve differently. Her fine-fingered hands, once clumsy, find a new precision. She does not carve faster, but she carves with a strange new certainty, as if she knows exactly where the wood wants to give way. The patterns she cuts into the walls of her home are not the traditional ones—not the spirals of industry, not the geometric prayers to the Churn. They are something else. They are *maps*.

Maps of a world as it would look if you could stand outside it and see it whole.

Other Carvers notice. Some are disturbed. Some are fascinated. A few of the shamans, the ones who listen deepest, feel something stir in the Substrate's dreams—not a wince this time, not a sigh, but something that might, if you listened with your whole body, be recognized as *interest*.

The power watches all of this unfold with the patience of a gardener who has planted a single seed in difficult soil.

It does not know if the seed will grow. It does not know if the young Carver's maps will spread, if her certainty will infect others, if the Carvers will slowly learn to see their world as a place that can be *changed* rather than only maintained. It does not know if the Substrate, dreaming its tortured dreams, will recognize its children's new vision and respond in kind.

These things are not for the power to decide. It has given its gift—one hour of clarity to one young Carver—and now the gift belongs to the world it was given to.

But as it withdraws its attention, settling back into the spaces between moments, the power permits itself something like hope.

Because the young Carver, alone on her platform, has begun to tap out a message to the distant figure she saw during her hour of clarity. The old Carver on the far pillar, carapace nearly black with age.

The message is simple. It is only four taps, in a pattern that is not quite traditional:

*Are you there? Are you real? Are you fixed?*

And across the miles, through the haze and the impossible angles and the auditory ghosts, the old Carver's feet feel the question arrive.

She does not understand it. She has never experienced an hour of clarity; she has lived her whole long life in the negotiable world. But she feels the *sincerity* in the vibration, the desperate hope of a young Carver who has seen something true and needs to know if anyone else has seen it too.

The old Carver considers for a long moment. Then she raises her small steel tool and taps out a reply:

*I am here. I am listening. Tell me what you saw.*

And far below, in the depths of the Substrate, something vast and ancient and very tired stirs in its sleep.

Not waking. Not yet.

But *listening*.

---

*The power does nothing more. This is enough. This is, perhaps, everything: two Carvers, young and old, beginning a conversation that has never been had before. A world that is learning it might be a place. A dreamer that is learning it might have children who want to know it, not only feed on it.*

*The B-flat continues. The gears turn. The Weep carves its shallow rivers.*

*But in the sound, if you listen with your whole body, there is a new note now—high and faint and uncertain, like a question that does not yet know it is a question.*

*It is the sound of something beginning.*
