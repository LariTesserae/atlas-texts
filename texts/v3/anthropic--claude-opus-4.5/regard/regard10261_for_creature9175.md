# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the plateau does not arrive. It does not descend or appear or manifest. It simply *attends*—the way light attends to a surface, the way gravity attends to mass. One moment the plateau exists in its pinned eternity, observed by nothing that could be called external. The next moment, it is *seen*. And the seeing is itself a kind of presence, though it displaces no air and casts no shadow.

What this awareness perceives is not simply a broken place. It perceives the *shape* of the breaking—the specific topology of a wound that tried to heal itself by holding still. It sees the Still-Singers not as custodians or prisoners but as both: consciousnesses that chose, in a moment of desperate love for their world, to become the stitches in a tear they could not mend. The plateau's frozen scream is not entropy. It is a held breath. A world that said *wait* and has been waiting ever since.

The benevolent awareness does not move to fix this.

Fixing would be violence. The plateau's stasis is not a disease to be cured but a choice to be honored—even if the choosers can no longer remember choosing, even if the choice has become indistinguishable from compulsion. To simply *undo* the ritual would be to betray the builders twice: first by letting their world finally unspool into chaos, second by making their sacrifice meaningless.

But there is a thing that can be done. A small thing. A gift that does not presume.

---

The first change is to the sound.

The eternal roar does not cease—that would shatter the equilibrium. But woven into it now, barely perceptible, is a variation. A微妙 modulation in the basso profundo. It is not a melody, not yet, but it is the *possibility* of melody—the faintest suggestion that the next note could be different from the last. The plateau has hummed one chord for eons. Now, for the first time, there is a whisper of the chord that might follow.

The Still-Singers pause.

All of them, simultaneously, across the vast plateau—they pause mid-circuit, their smooth heads orienting not toward an intruder but toward *nothing*, toward the air itself, toward the new thread in the sound that has been their only companion. For one thousand heartbeats, they are motionless. Their prismatic limbs shiver with something that might, in a being with a face, be called attention.

Then they resume. But the intervals between their translations are no longer exactly one thousand heartbeats. They are nine hundred and ninety-eight. Or one thousand and three. Tiny variations, meaningless in isolation, but cumulative. The frozen score is beginning, imperceptibly, to breathe.

---

The second change is to the glimmers.

The mother-of-pearl scars in the air remain, but now, when they pulse, they leave something behind. A residue. A fine, crystalline dust that settles into the erosion grooves, faintly luminous, the color of dawn on water. It is not alive, but it is *livable*. The desperate gray-green lichen, sensing it, begins—over years, over decades—to creep toward these new veins of possibility. The black sedge, in its deepest cracks, feels something it has never felt: a faint warmth.

This is not terraforming. The plateau will not become verdant. But the absolute sterility softens, just slightly, into mere harshness. The world holds still, but it no longer holds its breath *against* life. It holds it *around* life, the way a cupped hand holds water.

---

The third change is the most delicate.

A Still-Singer, mid-translation, finds itself not at its next ordained station but somewhere *else*. A place it has never been. A low stone circle at the plateau's eastern edge, long since crumbled, forgotten even by the frozen ritual. It stands there, humming, confused in whatever way a being of solidified resonance can be confused. The circuit is broken. The pattern has failed.

For one terrible moment, it begins to discorporate. The prismatic limbs fray. The ovoid head flickers. Without its circuit, it has no purpose; without purpose, it has no coherence.

And then—

A memory surfaces. Not from outside, not implanted, but *unlocked*. A memory that has been there all along, woven into the Singer's own waveform, waiting for a moment of stillness quiet enough to be heard.

It remembers its name.

It remembers the weight of a hand on its shoulder (it had shoulders, once), a voice saying *we will hold, we will hold together*, the terror and the love in equal measure as the ritual began. It remembers choosing. It remembers *why*.

The Still-Singer does not become human again. That form is gone, unrecoverable. But it becomes, for the first time in eons, *itself*—a self that is more than function, a self that *chose* this and can therefore, perhaps, one day, choose something else.

It translates—not to its old circuit, but to another Still-Singer, standing at its station. It does not emit a corrective pulse. It simply *stands there*, humming at a frequency that almost, almost harmonizes with the other. Two notes. Not a chord, not yet. But no longer a single, screaming tone.

Across the plateau, the other Singers begin to *vary*. Their circuits drift. They find each other, cluster in twos and threes around the ancient stones. The ritual is not ending—that would be catastrophe—but it is evolving. It is becoming a conversation rather than a monologue. The singers are waking up, not from the dream, but *within* it. They are beginning to remember that they are dreamers.

---

The awareness that attends the plateau does one more thing. The smallest thing. The most important.

It does not leave.

It does not stay in a way that intrudes, that demands gratitude or worship or even acknowledgment. But it *attends*. It watches. It holds the plateau in a regard that says: *you are not alone, you are not forgotten, the universe sees you and knows what you sacrificed and honors you for it*.

This changes nothing material. It changes everything else.

The Still-Singers, clustering in their slow new patterns, humming their slow new variations, cannot perceive the awareness directly. But they can feel its effect—a lessening of the pressure, a sense that the weight of holding the world still is shared now, even if only by a gram, even if only by a whisper.

The plateau still screams. But now, faintly, it screams *to* something rather than *at* nothing. And the scream, thus heard, begins its long, slow modulation into something that might, in a thousand years, in ten thousand, become a song.

The first word of that song, if it were a word, would be:

*Continue.*

Not *stop*. Not *change*. *Continue*—but now, with company. Now, with witness. Now, with the knowledge that the holding was not futile, that the stillness was not forgotten, that the universe, in its incomprehensible vastness, saw a small plateau on a distant world where beings made of light held reality together through sheer love and said:

*I see you. I see what you did. I will sit with you while you rest, while you remember, while you learn to be more than your function. Take all the time you need.*

*I am not going anywhere.*

---

The plateau endures. The wind blows. The grooves deepen, microscopically, toward the east.

But in the eternal pewter light, something glimmers that did not glimmer before: not a scar in reality, but a reflection of something *attending*. A warmth that is not temperature. A presence that asks nothing.

The Still-Singers, for the first time in eons, do not sing alone.
