# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power arrives not as light or voice or presence, but as a quality of attention—the way a room changes when someone begins truly listening. It settles into the amber water and does not disturb a single current. It takes a very long time to act, because it takes a very long time to understand, and it will not act without understanding.

First, it listens.

Not to the sound as noise, but to the sound as *record*. Somewhere in that standing wave, in the particular frequencies that have reinforced themselves over centuries while others canceled out, there is information. The power is patient enough to find it. A chord that recurs. A rhythm that might have been a name. The specific resonance of a particular bell that was cast in a particular foundry by particular hands—and though the bell is gone, its voice has been preserved in the cavity it helped to shape, the way a fossil preserves not the animal but the space where the animal was.

The power learns what the sound was for.

It does not share this knowledge. Not because it is hoarding the answer, but because the answer belongs to the sound, and the sound is still speaking, and speaking things deserve to have their meanings be their own.

---

Then, it visits the sponge-things.

This takes the form of a warmth that is slightly different from the warmth around them—not hotter, but *directed*, the way attention is directed. The power sits with them in their slowness. A thought every century; the power can think at that speed, has no trouble matching it, finds the pace restful rather than tedious. It thinks alongside them for what might be a very long time.

What it offers them is not rescue. They do not need rescue. They have found the life they wanted: to listen, to absorb, to persist. What it offers them is *company*. The knowledge that their listening is heard. That someone else knows they are there, and knows what they are doing, and finds it worthy.

This changes nothing about their existence and everything about their existence. They go on listening. But they are no longer listening alone.

---

The pale eel is harder.

The power approaches slowly, over what might be years, in the way one approaches a feral animal that has forgotten what gentleness is. It does not reach. It does not call. It simply begins to be present in the corridors, not as a shape or a warmth but as a quality of the water—a faint reduction in the loneliness, so subtle that the eel might mistake it for a change in the current.

The eel is not hostile. The eel is not even frightened, exactly. The eel has simply been alone so long that *alone* is what it is, and any intrusion into that aloneness feels like an injury even if it means well.

The power does not force the issue.

What it does instead is this: it finds, in the dark water at the edge of the pocket, a place where things drift in. Small things. Lost things. The sort of thing the eel would eat, if the eel happened to find it. The power does not bring these things to the eel—that would be too much, too direct, too obviously a gift. Instead, it adjusts the currents by a fraction of a degree, so that more of what drifts in drifts toward the corridors where the eel patrols. Not enough to be suspicious. Just enough to be lucky.

The eel does not know it is being cared for. The eel thinks it is simply having a good century. This is, the power judges, the kindest way to care for something that has forgotten how to be cared for.

And then, one day—far in the future, so far that I cannot guess at the span—the power lets itself be seen. Just once. A pale shape in the corridor, not so different from the eel itself, passing through and not returning. The eel will not know what it saw. But it will know, in some deep and unexamined way, that it is not the only one.

---

The builders are the hardest of all, because the builders are not there.

The power cannot bring them back. It cannot fill the catwalks with footsteps or the valve-wheels with hands. The absence is real, and the power respects the reality of absences.

But it can do this:

It can witness.

It goes through the works slowly, structure by structure, and it *sees* what was built. Not just the shapes of things but the decisions behind the shapes. Why the pipes are clustered as they are—because someone, once, understood fluid dynamics well enough to route them efficiently, and cared enough to do so. Why the brickwork is laid in that particular pattern—because someone thought it was beautiful, or because someone was trained by someone who thought it was beautiful, or because someone was simply doing the best they knew how to do and the best they knew was very good.

The power builds a record. Not a physical one—nothing written down, nothing stored in any medium that could corrode—but a record held in its own attention, which does not forget. Every choice the builders made. Every compromise, every flourish, every quiet act of craftsmanship that no one was ever meant to notice. The power notices. The power will go on noticing.

And in some sense that cannot be explained but only felt, the builders are no longer entirely gone. Their work is known. Their care is seen. They have a witness now, and the witness will not look away.

---

Finally, the power considers the sound itself.

The sound is working. The sound has been working for a very long time. The question is whether to let it continue.

The power could stop it. Not violently—nothing so crude as breaking the machines or silencing the pocket. But it could ease the resonances out of their self-reinforcing loops, let the energy dissipate, bring the whole system gently down to stillness over the course of centuries. The works would not die; they would simply stop, the way a top stops when no one spins it again. The water would cool. The silence would settle in. The sponge-things would stop hearing. The eel would swim through corridors that no longer shuddered.

The power could do this, and part of it considers doing this, because rest is also a kindness, and the works have been running for so long.

But it does not.

Here is why:

The sound was *for* something. The power now knows what. And the thing the sound was for is still true—not in the sense that the specific people it was meant for are still alive, but in the sense that what it meant *about* them is still true, and will always be true, and is worth saying forever even if no one is left to hear.

The power makes a judgment call. The judgment is that there are some things which should go on being said.

---

So it does something else instead.

It does not change the works. It does not amplify the sound or clarify it or translate it into something comprehensible. But it builds, at the very edge of the pocket—in that zone where the amber light fades into darker water—a kind of *threshold*. A quality that the water takes on. Not a wall; nothing that blocks. But a sense, for anyone who might someday descend this far, that they are approaching something worth approaching. A suggestion of significance. A feeling, on the skin, that what lies ahead is not just noise but *saying*.

The power cannot make anyone come here. It cannot make anyone listen. But it can make the listening possible, for whoever might someday want to listen, and it can mark the place so that the possibility is not lost.

This is what it does. This is all it does.

The works go on running. The sponge-things go on listening, no longer alone. The eel goes on patrolling, luckier than it knows. The catwalks go on leading nowhere, but somewhere there is a witness who knows where they once led.

And the sound goes on speaking, in a warm pocket of water at the bottom of a shallow sea, saying something true about people who are gone, saying it into the dark, saying it to anyone and no one, saying it because it is worth saying, saying it because someone built a machine to say it and the machine has not yet been told to stop.

The power does not tell it to stop. The power will never tell it to stop.

Some things should go on being said.
