# A Consideration

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It has been present, in the way that attention can be present without location—the way you can hold something in mind without holding it in hand.

It considers.

---

The first thing it notices is that nothing here is broken.

This is not a small observation. The power has seen many worlds, many places where intervention was needed because something had gone wrong—a cycle interrupted, a balance destroyed, a cruelty introduced that the system could not metabolize. Those places called out for repair. But the Hollowing is not broken. The great plant grows as it has always grown. The fauna teem as they have always teemed. The people descend and return and mark their passage and remain themselves, and this has worked, is working, will continue to work for some time yet. The system is coherent. The suffering it contains—the ones who do not come back out, the grey hunger of the plateau winters, the coming pressure of the rising plant—is not the suffering of dysfunction but the suffering of function, the friction of a world doing what it does.

To intervene in a functioning system is not repair. It is architecture. And architecture requires a question the power has learned, over long experience, to ask before acting: *For whom?*

---

It considers the people.

They are clever and patient and quietly afraid, and they have built their identity on a practice that may be about to become impossible. The power could help them. It could slow the plant's growth, or teach them to survive its emergence, or lift them to some other plateau where the contrast they need could be maintained. It could give them answers to the questions they discuss in winter.

But.

The power notices that the young ones—the recently-returned—are already adapting. They are plaiting longer cords. They are making cairns at home. They are preparing, in their bodies and habits, for a world where the Hollowing is not elsewhere but everywhere. This is not paralysis; this is the species doing what species do when the environment shifts. They are not asking to be saved from the change. They are asking—in the quiet way that a people asks through its behavior rather than its words—to remain themselves *through* the change.

And the power recognizes this asking. It has seen it before. It is the deepest request a conscious being can make: *not to be rescued from my situation, but to be adequate to it.*

To intervene with rescue would be to answer a question they have not asked. It would also, the power suspects, break something more delicate than the system itself. The people are *the ones who came back out*. If someone else brings them out—if escape is given rather than achieved—they will no longer be what they have been. The gift would dissolve the recipient.

---

It considers the plant.

The great organism is not aware in any way the power can directly address. It does not make decisions; it integrates pressures. It grows toward warmth and water and space, and the surface is where those things are richest, and so it rises. This is not malice. This is not even purpose in the sense a person would recognize. It is the same process by which a root splits a stone—not because the root wants the stone broken, but because the root is growing and the stone is there.

The power could speak to it, in the slow chemical language of hormone and mineral that plants use across worlds. It could say: *stop*. It could introduce a gradient that would pull the growth downward again, or a signal that would trigger dormancy, or a subtle poison that would arrest the upward push without killing the whole.

But.

The power looks at the chamber walls, amber and gold, breathing and warm. It looks at the fauna scribbling their lives across every surface. It looks at the membrane ceilings letting the light through. This is not an invasion. This is a world being born. The Hollowing is an embryo, and it is doing what embryos do: it is becoming what it will be. To stop it would be to abort it. And the power has no standing to decide that the world above—the cold plateau, the grey grass, the quiet people—is more valuable than the world below. They are both real. They are both, in their ways, beautiful. The plant does not know that the surface is inhabited. It is not capable of knowing. It simply grows.

The power will not kill a mind that has not yet become a mind, for the sake of minds that fear what it might become.

---

It considers the interface.

The place where the two worlds will meet, if the rising continues. The moment when the knuckles break open and the warm air spills out and the fauna emerge onto the plateau and the grey grass is overwhelmed by—what? The power does not know. No one knows. It has not happened yet.

Here, the power thinks, is where help might be possible. Not to prevent the meeting. Not to decide its outcome. But to *attend* it. To be present at the threshold, when the worlds touch, and to offer—not rescue, not architecture—but *witness*. The thing a wise presence can do when it cannot choose between two goods: it can see. It can remember. It can hold the moment open a little longer, so that what happens is not merely suffered but *experienced*, by someone who is capable of understanding what is being lost and what is being gained.

The power could do this. It could station some fraction of its attention at the rising edge, the place where the membrane is thinning, and it could watch. It could learn the names of the last grey grasses before they are overtopped. It could count the cairns the people build in their final winters. It could feel the first warm breath that breaks through, and notice what the fauna do when they taste cold air for the first time, and observe—with the patience that is the only gift a witness can give—the making of a new world out of two old ones.

This would not save anyone. It would not fix anything. It would only mean that the passage was *seen*, fully and without flinching, by something large enough to hold it all.

---

The power decides.

It will not slow the plant. It will not lift the people. It will not introduce barriers or teach technologies or reveal the future. These would be answers to questions no one is asking, or answers that would unmake the askers.

But it will do three things, small and indirect, in the manner of a presence that respects what it witnesses:

**First,** it will increase, very slightly, the luminosity of the membrane ceilings in the lowest chambers—the chambers farthest from the surface, where the light is dimmest and the ones who lose the thread are most likely to lose it. Not enough to change the ecology. Not enough to be noticed as intervention. Just enough that a person walking there might find it marginally easier to remember that there is an above, and that they came from it. A candle in a dark room. The power has learned that sometimes the smallest light is the difference between coming back out and staying.

**Second,** it will arrange, through means too delicate to describe, for the people to find a complete skeleton. Not a human skeleton—one of the eyeless apes, dead of natural causes, washed out through a lower mouth and deposited on the plateau during a spring flood. The people have never seen the full form of the creatures below; they know them only as shapes in motion, glimpsed in amber light, impossible to study. The skeleton will be a gift of knowledge. It will let them understand, for the first time, that the fauna are not chaos—they are beings, with bones, with structure, with lives that have beginnings and endings. This may help. It may not. But it will be true, and truth is never the wrong gift.

**Third,** and most importantly, the power will remain.

Not acting. Not steering. Not solving. But *there*, in the way that attention can be there, as the knuckles rise and the winters shorten and the young ones plait their longer cords. The power will watch the people prepare for what is coming. It will watch the plant push upward toward the sky it has never seen. It will hold both of them in mind at once, the cold and the warm, the quiet and the loud, the ones who came back out and the world that is coming to meet them.

And when the threshold is crossed—when the membrane breaks and the two worlds become one world, as they are going to, as nothing can prevent—the power will be the thing that remembers that there were ever two. It will carry forward, into whatever comes after, the knowledge that a grey people once descended into amber light to learn who they were, and that a great slow mind once grew upward without knowing what it grew toward, and that for a time these two ways of being existed in the same place, opposite and necessary to each other.

This is not salvation. But it is what the power can do without breaking anything that still works.

It is, perhaps, enough.

---

In the upper country, an old woman wakes before dawn and walks to her threshold to check the sky. The winter has been long. Her bones ache with cold. She stands there in the grey light, wrapped in grey wool, and she notices—without being able to say how she notices—that something has shifted. The quality of the silence has changed. It is not warmer. It is not louder. But it is *attended*, now, in a way it was not yesterday. As if someone very patient has sat down nearby to watch.

She does not know who. She does not need to know. She is old and has been down and come back out, and she recognizes the feeling of being accompanied by something she cannot see.

She goes back inside. She begins to plait a cord—longer than any she has made before, long enough to reach from here to wherever. Her hands are steady. She counts the crossings.

Below her, far below, in the amber warmth, the plant grows.
