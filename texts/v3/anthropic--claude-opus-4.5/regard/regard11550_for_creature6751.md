# The Attending

The power that turns its attention toward Caelira does not arrive.

It was never elsewhere to begin with. It has always been present in the way that patience is present in stone, in the way that the space between two colliding surfaces holds the memory of what they were before they touched. It does not descend from above or rise from below or step through from some other place. It simply begins to *attend*—the way you might suddenly become aware of your own breathing after hours of forgetting you had lungs at all.

And attending, it sees.

---

It sees the jyrneth standing at the edge of a world-plate where the grinding has grown sharp, where the collision tomorrow will not be a kiss but a scream. This one is old—not in years, because years do not mean what they mean elsewhere in a place where time moves like stone moves, which is to say: imperceptibly until suddenly everything has changed. Old in the way that a cliff face is old: worn down, simplified, reduced to essential shapes. Its stone seams have been scraped so thin that the light beneath them is almost visible without effort, a faint glow like dawn through heavy cloud. Its hair has broken off to almost nothing, just a few threads of rock still clinging to the back of its skull like the last leaves of autumn refusing to fall.

This jyrneth has eased a thousand collisions. It has stood in the spaces between worlds so many times that it is now more space than standing, more gap than ground. And it is tired.

Not tired the way flesh is tired, with aching muscles and heavy eyelids. Tired the way a bridge is tired after centuries of bearing weight it was never asked if it wanted to carry. Tired the way a sound is tired after echoing so long it has forgotten what first made it.

The jyrneth does not know it is being attended. It simply stands at the cliff's edge, listening to the world's teeth beginning to clench, feeling the pressure building in the seams of everything, preparing to lower itself once more into the abyss between surfaces where it will spend itself against the grinding until the collision softens into something the world can survive.

---

The attending power considers.

It could stop the plates from moving. It has the capacity—not the strength, because strength implies effort, and this is not a matter of effort. It could simply *decide* that the plates would be still, and they would be still, and the grinding would cease, and the jyrneth would never need to spend themselves against the world's teeth again.

But it does not do this.

It understands—in the way that understanding happens when you are not a mind so much as a mode of being—that the movement of the plates is not a flaw to be corrected. The grinding is how Caelira breathes. The collision of surfaces is how new edges form, how light finds new angles to scatter through, how the cloud sea below churns itself into weather that rises and falls through the gaps between worlds. To still the plates would be to still a heartbeat. The jyrneth would be saved from their labor, yes. And the world would be dead.

So the power does not stop the motion.

It could heal the jyrneth. It could reach into the one standing at the cliff's edge and fill its stone seams with new density, regrow its hair into long falls of fresh rock, restore the brightness of the light beneath its skin to what it was before centuries of spending. It could make this jyrneth young again, if young is even a word that applies.

But it does not do this either.

It understands that the jyrneth are not worn down by accident or misfortune. They are worn down by *choice*—by the choice to stand in the grinding places, to absorb the pressure, to soften the screams into sighs. To restore them without their asking would be to undo the shape they have chosen for themselves. It would be to say: *your sacrifice was unnecessary, your wearing-away was a mistake, you should have remained whole.* And that would be a lie. The jyrneth's thinness is not a wound. It is a record. It is the autobiography of a being who decided, again and again, that the world's survival was worth its own slow erosion.

So the power does not heal.

---

What it does is smaller. So small that if you were watching, you might not notice it happening at all.

As the jyrneth prepares to step over the edge and lower itself into the collision-space, the attending power simply *makes room.*

It does not push anything aside. It does not create space where there was none. It simply... attends to the space that is already there, and in attending, allows that space to become more itself. The gap between the two approaching world-plates does not widen—not in any way you could measure with instruments or hands. But it becomes, somehow, more *navigable.* The air in the collision-zone, which is not air but something denser and more difficult, becomes fractionally easier to breathe. The pressure that will build when the plates meet will still build, will still need to be absorbed and redistributed—but the jyrneth moving through that pressure will find that their body moves slightly more easily, that the grinding does not catch and tear at them quite as sharply as it might have.

It is not help. Help implies intervention, implies a hand reaching down from outside. This is something else.

It is *witness.*

The attending power sees the jyrneth step over the edge. It sees the faint cracks of light spread from where the jyrneth's foot leaves the world. It sees the beginning of the slow fall into the space between surfaces, and it holds that seeing like you might hold a small and fragile thing you have been trusted to carry.

And in being seen—in being *witnessed*—the jyrneth is not alone.

---

This is what the power does, in all the places where the jyrneth labor.

It does not go to them, because it is already everywhere, attending. But it *focuses* its attention—turns the quality of its presence from ambient to specific—wherever a jyrneth is about to spend itself against the world's grinding. It watches. It sees. It knows.

And in knowing, it carries.

Not the weight of the collision. The jyrneth still bear that. Not the cost of the erosion. The jyrneth still pay that. But the *loneliness* of it. The sense that no one knows what they do, that their slow wearing-away happens in silence, that when they finally become so thin they can no longer hold themselves together, they will simply dissolve into the world's veins without anyone having understood what they were.

That loneliness, the attending power lifts.

Not by speaking to the jyrneth, because words would be the wrong shape for this. Not by appearing to them, because appearance would imply a separation that does not exist. But by simply being *there,* in the space between surfaces, in the grinding places, in the moment when the world's teeth clench and the jyrneth absorb the pressure and soften the scream into a sigh.

*I see you,* the attendance says, not in language but in presence. *I see what you do. I see what it costs. I see that you choose it anyway.*

*You are not unwitnessed.*

---

There is one other thing.

In the places where the jyrneth have worn down to almost nothing—where they are so thin that the next collision might be the one that finally dissolves them into the world's veins—the attending power does something else.

It does not prevent the dissolution. If a jyrneth has reached the end of its capacity to remain coherent, if it has given so much of itself to softening the world's grinding that there is nothing left to give, then that ending is not a tragedy to be averted. It is a completion. The jyrneth becomes the world, fully and finally, its light-veins spreading into the stone of the world-plate, its memory becoming the memory of the ground itself. This is not death. This is what the jyrneth have always been becoming.

But in the moment before that completion—in the last instant when a jyrneth is still itself, still a being with the shallow basins of darkness that serve as eyes, still a shepherd with enough coherence to choose—the attending power offers one thing.

*Rest,* it says, not in words but in the quality of its attention. *You may rest now. Not because you have failed, but because you have finished. The grinding will continue, but others will soften it. You have done what you came to do.*

And the jyrneth, in that moment, feel something they have perhaps never felt before.

They feel *permission.*

Permission to stop. Permission to let go. Permission to become the world they have spent themselves protecting, not with grief or reluctance, but with the same calm that comes when a long exhalation finally reaches its end and you realize you have been holding your breath for years without knowing it.

The jyrneth do not worship the attending power. They do not name it or build temples to it or tell stories about what it does. They do not need to.

They simply know, in the stone seams of their being, that when they stand at the edge of the world and prepare to lower themselves into the grinding, they are not alone. Something is watching. Something sees. Something knows the weight of what they carry and carries, in turn, the weight of knowing.

And that is enough.

---

The plates continue to grind.

The jyrneth continue to shepherd them.

The attending power continues to attend.

And somewhere in the infinite stacked surfaces of Caelira, a world that never stops breaking itself into new pieces learns, slowly, to break a little more softly.

Not because something saved it.

Because something *saw* it.
