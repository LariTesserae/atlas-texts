# The Attention of the Distant

The power that turned its awareness toward the plateau did so without hurry. It had observed ten thousand worlds where life struggled against hostile conditions, had witnessed the familiar dramas of suffering and striving, of creatures bent against wind and cold and scarcity. It had intervened in many—bringing rain to parched valleys, softening climates, multiplying food sources, easing the pressure of existence until populations flourished.

But as it studied this place, it did not move to act.

---

The plateau lay beneath its attention like a single held note in an infinite composition. The power perceived everything at once: each lizard pressed into its groove, each insect riding the unchanging wind, each stem transmitting vibration into fractures that would not widen. It felt the equilibrium as one might feel the tension of a perfectly balanced scale—not strain, but presence. A system so thoroughly answered that nothing called out for response.

The power understood intervention. It knew how to introduce moisture, how to gentle wind, how to coax variety from uniformity. It could seed the shallow pans with organisms that would transform the pale film of water into something richer. It could shield portions of stone from the constant glare until shadows formed and new possibilities emerged in their cool margins. It could interrupt the single tone with silence, introduce the concept of change to creatures who had never required it.

And yet.

---

The power held still, considering.

In other places, it had found suffering—creatures straining against limits that crushed them, systems grinding toward collapse, life caught in spirals of diminishment. There, intervention was clear. The mathematics of compassion required action when existence itself became torment.

But here, nothing strained. The lizards did not press themselves into grooves because they feared the wind; they had become the grooves. The insects did not struggle against the current; they had made themselves into its texture. The stems did not fight for purchase in the fractures; they had sealed them, becoming part of the stone's own resistance. Everything present had answered the plateau's single question with such completeness that the question had ceased to be a question at all.

The power searched for suffering and found only continuance. It searched for yearning and found only precision. It searched for the gap between what was and what ought to be—the gap that made intervention meaningful—and found the two had long ago become indistinguishable.

---

What would it mean to change this place?

The power allowed itself to imagine. More water in the pans, deeper pools, stems that grew tall enough to sway. Shadows where lizards could cool, insects that built rather than simply rode. Variation. Growth. The arrival of something new.

But each imagined change rippled outward into consequence. Taller stems would catch the wind differently, creating turbulence where there was constancy. More water would invite organisms unsuited to the pressure, creatures that would struggle against what the plateau offered rather than answering it. Shadows would make the constant glare a boundary rather than a condition, dividing the stone into zones of relief and exposure. Every alteration would introduce strain where none existed—would create suffering in the act of trying to prevent it.

The power recognized this pattern. It had seen it before, in places where well-meant intervention had shattered equilibria that had taken ages to form. Life that had learned to match its conditions precisely, disrupted by changes meant to "improve" those conditions. Creatures that had found their answer, suddenly presented with new questions they had never evolved to meet.

---

And so the power made its choice.

It would watch. It would remain attentive, holding this place in its awareness the way one holds a note that needs no resolution. It would not mistake stillness for death, or constancy for stagnation, or equilibrium for emptiness. It would honor what the plateau had achieved—not despite its refusal of change, but because of it.

If the conditions themselves ever shifted—if the wind ceased, if the moisture failed, if some external force broke the balance—then the power would be present, ready to ease whatever transition followed. It would not abandon this place to indifference. But neither would it impose improvement where no improvement was needed.

---

The plateau continued beneath the power's attention exactly as it had before that attention arrived. The wind moved through the channels, striking the fluted ridges, producing its single endless tone. The insects rose and fell in their translucent thousands. The lizards pressed themselves into the grooves and crossed to the pools and returned. The stems transmitted vibration into fractures that held.

And the power remained—not as an agent of change, but as a witness. Holding space for the possibility that continuance itself could be a form of flourishing. That life which had perfectly answered its conditions required no further answer.

The light stayed level. The sound did not pause. The equilibrium that showed no sign of beginning or ending continued, watched now by something vast and wise enough to know when wisdom meant doing nothing at all.
