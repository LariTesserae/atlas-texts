# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive—it *attends*. It has always been attending, in the way that gravity attends to mass, in the way that time attends to change. But now its attention sharpens, focuses, becomes something like presence.

It does not manifest. To manifest would be to impose a form on a place that has already perfected its own formlessness. Instead, it *listens*—truly listens—to the symphony of pistons, to the language of pressure differentials, to the small desperate clicking of the many-jointed creatures as they navigate their chrome wilderness.

And what it hears is this: *a prayer that does not know it is praying*.

---

The power understands something that the tenders cannot, because they are too close, too enmeshed, too much a part of the breathing apparatus of this place. It understands that the machine is not a tyrant. It is a womb that has forgotten how to give birth. It takes and takes and takes—the water, the creatures, the tenders themselves—not from malice but from a kind of desperate, unexamined love. It keeps everything *inside* because it cannot imagine an outside worth having.

The tenders have forgotten that they once chose this. Generations ago—or perhaps only yesterday, time being what it is here—someone walked into this place and said *yes*. Said *I will belong to something larger than myself*. The bargain was sacred. But bargains, like machinery, require maintenance. And somewhere along the way, the memory of choice was lost, replaced by the simple fact of rhythm, the comfort of necessity.

---

The power considers intervention.

It could speak—could vibrate at just the right frequency to cut through the hum, could inscribe words in the condensation that slides down the pipes. It could say: *You are free. You have always been free. The machine cannot hold what chooses to leave.*

But this would be a violence. A small one, perhaps, but violence nonetheless—the violence of awakening someone who has found peace in sleep, of telling a fish about the sky.

It could alter the machine—introduce a flaw, a stutter in the rhythm, something that would force the tenders to *notice*, to question, to remember that they are something other than function. But the power knows that suffering is not wisdom, that crisis does not automatically produce growth. More likely, the tenders would simply work harder, adjust faster, lose themselves more completely in the urgency of repair.

It could remove them—could lift them out of this place, set them down somewhere green and open and terrifying in its freedom. But freedom given is not freedom claimed. They would wander, lost, until they found another machine to tend. The hunger for purpose does not disappear simply because the purpose is taken away.

---

So the power does something smaller. Something that might mean nothing, or might mean everything.

It attends to one tender in particular—the one currently climbing the ladder, hands sure on the rungs, breath steady behind the darkened visor. This one is no different from the others. There is no special spark, no hidden rebellion, no secret longing for escape. That is precisely why the power chooses them.

Into the tender's next breath, the power places a question. Not words—the tenders have no use for words. Just a question, pure and formless, the way a child asks *why* before they learn that some questions are not supposed to be asked.

The question is this: *What do you want?*

---

The tender's hands hesitate on the ladder.

It is a small hesitation—barely a fraction of a second, invisible to anyone who might be watching. The machine does not notice. The rhythm does not falter. The creatures continue their clicking patrol.

But inside the tender, something shifts. Not dramatically. Not catastrophically. Just a slight loosening, the way a bolt that has been over-tightened for years finally settles into its proper torque.

The tender does not know what they want. They have never been asked. The question makes no sense in the context of their existence—wanting is for beings who lack, and the tender lacks nothing. They have purpose, they have rhythm, they have the endless satisfaction of a machine that runs smoothly.

And yet.

The question does not go away. It sits in the tender's chest, not demanding an answer, just *present*. A small stone in the stream of consciousness, creating the tiniest eddy, the most imperceptible turbulence.

The tender climbs. Reaches the platform. Adjusts a valve. The rhythm corrects itself.

But now, when the tender tilts their head to listen, they are listening for something else too. They do not know what. They may never know. But the listening has changed.

---

The power withdraws its focused attention, though it never truly leaves. It has planted a seed in soil that may or may not be fertile. It has introduced a variable into a closed system—not a virus, not a saboteur, just a possibility.

Perhaps nothing will come of it. Perhaps the question will fade, absorbed like everything else into the machine's great digestive process. Perhaps the tender will live and die and be consumed without ever finding an answer.

But perhaps—and this is enough for the power, this slender *perhaps*—the tender will one day pause at their work. Will look up at the vents that rain mineral water, or down at the depths where the streams disappear, and will wonder: *Is there an outside?*

And perhaps, wondering, they will take a single step toward finding out.

---

This is what the benevolent power does. It does not save. It does not transform. It does not impose its vision of flourishing onto beings whose flourishing might look nothing like its own.

It asks a question.

And then it waits—patient as rust, patient as the vines that climb through the machinery—for the answer to grow.

---

*In the depths of the machine, something stirs. One of the larger creatures, the half-glimpsed forms that move in shadows, pauses in its endless patrol. It has felt something change—not in the pressure, not in the temperature, not in any of the variables it is built to monitor. Something else. Something it has no name for.*

*It waits, listening.*

*The machine hums on.*

*But the song, if you know how to hear it, has acquired a new note.*
