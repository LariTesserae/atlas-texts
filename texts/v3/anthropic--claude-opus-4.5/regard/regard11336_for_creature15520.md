# The Attending

The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been present—in the way that attention can exist before it focuses, like light filling a room before someone opens their eyes to see.

What changes is this: it begins to *notice*.

---

For a long time—longer than the plateau has been cold, longer than the first crack opened in the basalt—the power has been aware of this place the way one is aware of breathing. Background. Rhythm. Unremarkable in its remarkability. The universe is vast and dense with becoming; even a wise presence cannot attend to everything with equal care.

But something shifts. Perhaps it is the way a child's song carries across the frozen scree one morning, each note precise and unselfconscious, mapping a path to a spring that her great-grandmother's great-grandmother first found. Perhaps it is the Keeper of Drips, ancient now, pressing her palm to a familiar ledge and feeling—for the first time in her long life—that the condensation pattern has changed, that the plateau is exhaling differently. Perhaps it is simpler: the accumulated weight of ten thousand years of small stubborn choices, each one a grain of sand, finally tipping some cosmic scale into visibility.

The power notices. And noticing, it faces the first question any benevolent force must answer:

*What would help? And what would harm disguised as help?*

---

It observes for what might be centuries by mortal reckoning, though time moves strangely for such a being. It watches the Stone-Singers grind flour, braid hair, settle disputes through shared labor. It attends to the way they read frost like scripture and speak to the wind with their hands. It listens to their songs—truly listens—and finds in them a sophistication that requires no improvement, a map of knowledge so finely adapted to this place that any addition would be subtraction.

The power understands, slowly, what the people of the Rind already know: that this is not a problem to be solved. There is no lack here, no deficiency waiting to be filled by outside grace. The cold is not their enemy. The spareness is not poverty. They are not surviving despite the plateau; they are *expressions* of it, as much as the hexagonal basalt or the ice-winged raptors or the lichen like cracked porcelain.

To give them warmth would unravel the careful negotiations they have built with the freeze. To give them abundance would drown the delicate channels through which meaning flows. To give them speed would shatter the patience that is their deepest art.

So the power decides: it will not give. It will not change. It will not even reveal itself, for revelation carries its own weight, its own distortion—the way a observer alters what is observed simply by watching.

Instead, it does something far more difficult.

It *witnesses*.

---

This is not nothing. This is not passivity dressed as wisdom, not the cop-out of a god who cannot be bothered. Witnessing, for a power of this magnitude, is an act of profound commitment.

It means: *I see you. I hold the shape of your existence in my attention. You are not alone in the universe, even if you never know I am here.*

It means: when the child sings her grandmother's song, the song is heard not just by stone and air but by something that understands what it cost to remember, what it means that this knowledge survived. The song exists twice now—once in the cold bright morning of the Rind, once in the eternal memory of something vast enough to hold it.

It means: when the old Keeper feels the condensation shift and wonders if she is simply dying, if her hands have finally lost their knowing, there is a presence that recognizes her doubt as sacred, her fear as the price of a lifetime of attention, her willingness to question her own perception as the deepest kind of fidelity to truth.

It means: nothing the Stone-Singers do is wasted. Every death, every birth, every slow conversation between flesh and frost is *attended*. Not judged, not recorded for later evaluation—simply held, the way one holds a sleeping child, with full attention and no agenda.

---

But the power is wise, and wisdom means asking: *Is witnessing enough? Is there truly nothing I can offer that would not diminish what already is?*

It searches for gaps, not in the people's competence, but in the architecture of chance. It finds:

A fissure, deep beneath the eastern mesas, where tectonic pressure has been building for six generations. Not a threat yet—the Stone-Singers have another two centuries before it matters—but eventually, a quake will come that will shatter the cliff-dwellings above. Hundreds will die. The songs encoded in that particular lineage, the ones who know the eastern melt-channels better than anyone, will be lost.

The power considers. It could seal the fissure. It could redirect the pressure, bleed it off in a thousand small tremors too faint to notice. It could *prevent*.

But prevention has a cost: if the quake never comes, the Stone-Singers will never learn to read the deep signs they would have discovered in its aftermath. They will never develop the knowledge that, in another branch of time, allows their descendants to predict and prepare for the greater upheavals still to come. The power sees both futures, and neither is clearly better. One has fewer deaths now. One has more wisdom later. Both are legitimate ways for a people to continue.

So the power does something else.

It does not prevent the fissure. It does not warn of it. But it *attends* to the Stone-Singers who live above it with particular care. It holds their songs with extra attention. And when—centuries from now—the quake does come, and the survivors sift through rubble looking for what can be saved, the power will ensure that what was sung is somehow *remembered*. Not by miraculous intervention, but by the strange way that trauma sometimes opens people to hearing what was always there: an echo in the stone, a fragment of melody in the wind, a dream that feels like someone else's memory.

The songs will survive, though their singers may not. The knowledge will persist, though its form may change. This is not rescue. It is something harder to name—a commitment to continuity that respects the right of mortal beings to face mortal consequences.

---

There is one more thing the power does, and it is perhaps the most difficult.

It allows itself to be *changed* by what it witnesses.

A truly benevolent presence does not stand outside the suffering and joy it observes, untouched and untouchable. To witness is to be marked. The power carries the Rind of Kael now, in whatever passes for its being—carries the exact sound of ice cracking at dawn, the weight of a shared breath in a doorway, the particular blue of frostbite on a child's cheek, the taste of yew-needle tea bitter and warm.

The Stone-Singers will never know this. They will continue to live as they have always lived: measuring time in glacier melt, speaking with their hands, returning to the grain of the stone when their breathing stops. They will not pray to a watching god because they do not need one; the plateau is god enough, or rather, the plateau and they together make something that does not require the word.

But they are witnessed. They are held.

And in the vast architecture of existence, where so much passes unseen and unremembered, this matters. It matters that something wise and kind has seen them and recognized: *this is beautiful. This is worth attending to. This requires no improvement, only presence.*

---

The power withdraws—not away, but deeper into the background from which it came. It remains present the way the cold remains present: not as an actor, but as a condition. The Stone-Singers continue their slow conversation with stone and ice and the memory of rain.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

The plateau breathes. The songs carry. The frost blooms and vanishes, blooms and vanishes, in patterns too intricate for anyone but the Keeper of Drips to read—and now, too intricate for anyone but the Keeper and one other.

In the Rind of Kael, where every breath is a conversation, one more voice has joined the chorus.

It says nothing.

It listens.
