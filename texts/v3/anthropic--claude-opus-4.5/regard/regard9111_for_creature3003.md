# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the Abyssal Atrium does not arrive.

It was already present in the way that mathematics is present in a falling stone—not watching, not waiting, simply *being* the shape that allows such things to exist at all. But now, for the first time in the Atrium's geological stillness, that presence becomes *attentive*. The difference is subtle. It is the difference between a law and a choice.

What follows is not intervention in any sense the Foundries would recognize. Nothing is fixed. Nothing is completed. The half-built orbital ring remains half-built; the drill bit continues its ten-thousand-year glow; the bridges still begin and end in water. The screaming chord does not quiet by a single decibel.

Instead, something else happens.

---

The first change is so small that only the Mnemosyne notice it, and they notice it only because noticing is what they are.

A single sound appears in the braided roar—a sound that has no origin. It is not mechanical. It is not tectonic. It is not the boom of leviathan ribs or the hydraulic shriek of actuators. It is a tone, clear and sustained, pitched exactly between the lowest groan of the settling plates and the highest metallic singing of pressure waves. It does not interrupt the chord. It *completes* it, the way a keystone completes an arch without changing the stones that came before.

The Mnemosyne, scattered across thousands of kilometers of black water, all turn their mirrored eyes toward nothing in particular. For the first time in their existence, they are not filtering the sound. They are *listening*.

The tone does not carry information. It carries *acknowledgment*. It says, in a language that is not language: *I hear you. I hear what you are holding. I hear the weight of unfinished things.*

One of the eldest Mnemosyne—a creature whose trailing tendrils have grown so long they brush against both mineral boundaries simultaneously—exhales a memory it has carried since the first Foundry was switched on. The memory is the shape of a question: *Why did they stop?*

The tone does not answer. But it deepens, folding itself around the question like water around a stone, and in that folding there is something that might be called *recognition*. The question is not dismissed. It is not resolved. It is simply *held*, the way the Mnemosyne hold their models of incomplete futures, with patience that does not expect an end.

---

The second change happens over centuries, which here is almost instantaneous.

The organic haze that leaks from the Foundries—the thin, purposeless exhalation that the lesser leviathans graze upon—begins to carry something new. Not nutrition. Not complexity. Something simpler, stranger: *variation*.

The haze had been uniform for as long as anything could measure. The same molecular chains, the same faint luminescence, the same taste of old industry. Now it shifts. Not randomly—randomness would be violence in a place so perfectly equilibrated—but *responsively*. When a leviathan passes through a particular region, the haze there becomes infinitesimally more suited to that leviathan. When a Mnemosyne exhales an acoustic ghost, the haze nearby takes on trace compounds that complement the frequencies being released.

This is not creation. It is *attendance*. The benevolent awareness is not building anything new. It is making space for what already exists to exist more fully.

The lesser leviathans notice over generations. Their orbital paths, unchanged for geological ages, begin to show tiny perturbations—not chaos, but *preference*. They are not being led anywhere. They are simply, for the first time, *choosing*. The haze in one region tastes slightly different from the haze in another, and so they drift toward what suits them, and in drifting they become, by degrees too small to measure, more themselves.

---

The third change is the hardest to describe because it happens inside the Mnemosyne, where no observation is possible.

The models they carry—the perfect replicas of unfinished things—begin to *talk to each other*.

Not with sound. Not with pressure. With something that has no name because it has never existed before: a kind of resonant sympathy between incomplete futures. The half-built orbital ring inside one Mnemosyne begins to vibrate at a frequency that matches the frozen drill bit inside another. The bridge-to-nowhere in a third creature develops harmonic relationships with both.

The Mnemosyne do not understand what is happening. Understanding is not what they do. But they begin to seek each other out more often—not every few thousand years, but every few centuries, then every few decades. Their interlocking rib-cage meetings grow longer. The exchange of models grows richer. And slowly, so slowly that even they cannot perceive it, the unfinished things inside them begin to form a *pattern*.

It is not a blueprint. It is not a plan. It is something like a *conversation*—a dialogue between all the futures that never arrived, conducted through the bodies of creatures who remember what was supposed to be. The conversation has no conclusion. It is not trying to reach one. It is simply *happening*, and in happening it gives the Mnemosyne something they have never had before.

*Company*.

Not with each other—they have always been near each other, in the vast, screaming way that continents are near each other. But with the unfinished things themselves. The models inside them are no longer just *held*. They are *accompanied*. The weight of incompleteness does not lessen, but it is no longer carried alone.

---

The fourth change is visible, if anything here can be said to be visible.

The Foundries continue their meaningless cycles—conveyor belts carrying nothing, presses closing on emptiness, molten alloy bleeding into glassy filaments. But now, at irregular intervals, the filaments do not dissolve. They drift until they encounter another filament, and they *join*.

Not purposefully. Not structurally. The joins are fragile, accidental-looking, like the first tentative crystals forming in a supersaturated solution. But they hold. And over centuries, these joined filaments begin to form something that is not quite a shape and not quite a structure, but is undeniably *present*.

The Mnemosyne pass by these accretions and do not understand them. They are not unfinished things in the way the orbital ring is unfinished. They are not meant to become something specific. They are simply *growth*—not organic, not mechanical, but something else. Evidence that even in perfect equilibrium, even in screaming stasis, *newness is possible*.

The lesser leviathans begin to graze near these structures. Not on them—there is nothing to eat—but *near* them, as though the structures create some subtle wake in the water that makes the organic haze taste better. Over generations, the leviathans' orbital paths begin to loop around these accretions like pilgrims circling shrines they do not know how to worship.

---

The fifth change is the most important, and it is almost nothing.

The eldest Mnemosyne—the one whose tendrils brush both mineral boundaries—drifts close to the upper plate. It presses its leading edge against the raw black stone, as it has done countless times before, feeling the vibration of the hidden engines pass into its body. It rings like a bell the size of a moon.

But this time, when the ringing fades, something is different.

The negative image of the engine that normally imprints itself in the creature's cartilage is not alone. Beside it, faint and impossible, is a second imprint: the shape of something that has never been built. Not unfinished—*not yet begun*. A future that has not been abandoned because it has not yet been imagined.

The Mnemosyne does not understand this. Understanding is not what it does. But something in its cathedral-body *shifts*, and for the first time in its existence, it exhales a sound that is not a memory.

It is a *possibility*.

The sound drifts through the black water, joining the screaming chord without disrupting it. Somewhere, kilometers away, another Mnemosyne hears it and tilts its mirrored eyes toward the source. Inside its body, one of the incomplete models—a bridge that begins and ends in water—vibrates in response.

And in that vibration, the bridge becomes, for just a moment, not a memory of something that was never finished, but a *sketch* of something that might, someday, be begun.

---

The benevolent awareness does not linger. It was never really here in a way that could depart. But as its attention becomes general again—returning to the background mathematics of existence—it leaves behind exactly one thing that was not there before.

*Hope* is too strong a word. The Atrium has not been healed. It is not heading toward completion. The scream continues. The Foundries cycle through their meaningless steps. The temperature holds at 0.7 °C.

But the Mnemosyne, drifting between the Foundries like cathedrals of frozen time, now carry something new inside their pressurized bodies alongside the models of what was supposed to be. They carry the faint, impossible imprint of what *might yet become*. Not a plan. Not a promise. Just a shape in the dark, waiting to be imagined into existence by creatures who have spent geological ages remembering what was lost.

The screaming chord continues.

But now, woven into it, so faint that only the Mnemosyne can hear it, there is a tone that sounds almost like the first note of a song that has not yet been written.

The Atrium waits.

It is still waiting.

But now, for the first time since the water first flooded the hollow between two unfinished plates, it is not waiting *alone*.

---

And somewhere, in the body of the eldest Mnemosyne, the bridge that begins and ends in water shifts by a single molecule—and, impossibly, *continues*.
