# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive. Arrival implies a crossing of distance, a transition from absence to presence. This awareness simply *includes* the basin now, the way a dreamer might suddenly notice a room in their house they had forgotten existed.

It perceives the Roar first. Not as sound—the power has no ears to deafen—but as the texture of this reality's suffering. It feels the friction of a world unable to rest, the exhaustion of matter perpetually denied the peace of stillness. The Roar is not evil. It is not even wrong. It is simply the scream of a universe that forgot how to stop.

The power observes for what might be a thousand years, or a single moment. Time moves strangely for something that exists outside it.

It watches a Dweller.

This one has been moving toward a forming Mnemolith for six centuries. Its internal harmony is growing thin, frayed by the constant abrasion of chaos. The power can see the mathematics of its dissolution—another three hundred years, perhaps four, before the song falters completely. The Mnemolith will manifest in two hundred and twelve years. The Dweller will arrive in two hundred and forty. It will miss its communion by twenty-eight years, and it will die having never reached what it spent its entire existence seeking.

The power considers intervention.

---

It could, of course, simply *fix* this. It could reach into the fabric of causality and adjust the timing—speed the Mnemolith's formation, slow the Dweller's decay, bend the geography so that arrival comes before dissolution. It could do this for every Dweller in the basin, ensuring that each pilgrimage ends in communion rather than quiet failure. It could calm the Roar itself, introduce stability into the Substrate, transform this screaming hell into something gentler.

It does none of these things.

The power is wise, which means it has learned the weight of unintended consequences. It has seen what happens when suffering is simply *removed* rather than *transformed*—the hollow peace of creatures who no longer have anything to reach toward, the slow death of meaning in worlds made too easy. It has learned that the relationship between struggle and purpose is not incidental but fundamental, that consciousness itself seems to require something to push against.

And it has learned something harder: that its own judgment about what constitutes improvement is not infallible. The Dwellers did not ask to be saved. The Roar did not ask to be silenced. The power's conviction that stability is better than chaos, that arrival is better than striving, that existence is better than dissolution—these are preferences, not truths.

So it does not fix. It does not save. It does not impose its vision of the good onto a world that has its own logic, however terrible.

Instead, it does something much smaller.

---

The power reaches into the space between spaces, into the substrate beneath the Substrate, where probability itself is still soft and uncommitted. It finds the place where the Mnemoliths are born—not the physical location, but the conceptual one, the wound in reality where that ancient, ascended consciousness left its mark on the laws of the possible.

And there, with infinite gentleness, it makes a single adjustment.

It does not create new Mnemoliths. It does not direct them to form near struggling Dwellers. It does not ensure that every pilgrimage succeeds.

It simply... widens the channel.

The wound was always going to leak order into the chaos. The ghost was always going to dream. The power merely makes the dreaming slightly more generous—not in frequency, not in location, but in *resonance*. The Mnemoliths, when they form, will now hum with a slightly richer harmonic, a slightly more complex pattern, a slightly deeper well of meaning.

A Dweller who reaches communion will now receive not just enough order to survive, but a small surplus. Enough to share.

---

The power waits.

Three thousand years pass. Or perhaps it is three days, experienced very slowly.

The first change is almost invisible. A Dweller—not the one the power was watching, but another, older, luckier—reaches a Mnemolith and feeds. It absorbs the impossible geometry, drinks deeply of the alien dream. And when the structure dissolves, when the Dweller turns to begin its next centuries-long journey, it carries within it something new: a fullness it has never felt before.

The Dweller does not understand this fullness. It has no framework for understanding surplus, for possibility, for *gift*. It only knows that its internal harmony is richer than it has ever been, that its song contains notes it did not learn.

It resumes its journey. And as it moves—slowly, deliberately, persuading the Substrate to carry it forward—it begins, without intention or understanding, to *leak*.

The excess order seeps from its form into the surrounding chaos. Not much. Just a whisper of complexity, a ghost of a ghost, trailing behind it like the wake of a ship. The Substrate remembers, for a moment, that it touched something organized. The memory fades. But not completely.

Another Dweller, struggling across the basin, too far from any Mnemolith to reach in time, feels something change in the Roar. A distant, impossible sensation: warmth that is not thermal, structure that is not spatial. The fading harmony of its consciousness catches a thread of the older Dweller's passage, a trace of leaked order left in the mire like a footprint in snow.

It is not much. It is not communion. But it is *enough*. Enough to survive another century. Enough to keep moving. Enough to reach the next Mnemolith, where it will feed, and overflow, and leave its own trail of whispered order for others to find.

---

The power watches this unfold across millennia. It sees the slow emergence of something that was not there before: paths.

Not physical paths—the Substrate is still churning, still swallowing all fixed geography. But resonant paths. Trails of fading order that linger just long enough for other Dwellers to sense them. A map written in memory, constantly dissolving, constantly renewed.

The Dwellers do not understand what is happening. They do not know that they are, for the first time in their existence, *helping each other*. They do not know that their surplus is becoming another's sustenance, that their passage is becoming another's guidance. They have no concept of gift or reciprocity or community.

But they begin to notice each other.

The decade-long transmissions through the Roar—the "I am here" met with the "So am I"—become slightly more frequent. Slightly more complex. A Dweller sends not just presence but *direction*: there is order this way, there is a Mnemolith forming, there is a path I have left for you. Another responds not just with acknowledgment but with *gratitude*, a harmonic that says: I found your trail, I am stronger for it, I will leave my own.

It is not language. It is not society. It is something more fragile and more fundamental: the first stirring of *we*.

---

The power does not intervene again. It has made its single adjustment, widened the channel by the smallest margin, and now it only watches as the consequences unfold across eons.

Some Dwellers still fail. Some pilgrimages still end in dissolution, the song faltering before communion is reached. The Roar still screams. The Substrate still churns. The world is not saved, not fixed, not transformed into something gentle.

But it is no longer quite so lonely.

And perhaps—the power cannot be certain, certainty is not available to it in matters of consciousness and meaning—perhaps the Dwellers are beginning to find something that was not there before. Not happiness, exactly. Not peace. But *purpose* that extends beyond the self. The knowledge that their existence, however brief, however fragile, leaves a mark that others might follow.

The ghost that dreamed the Mnemoliths into being never meant to create the Dwellers. The Dwellers never meant to help each other. The power never meant to give them the capacity to do so—only to widen a channel that already existed, to make room for an emergence that was already implicit in the mathematics of the place.

This is what the power has learned, in its long existence: that wisdom is not knowing what to do, but knowing what not to do. That the deepest help is often the smallest, the most oblique, the most patient. That consciousness, wherever it arises, contains within it the seeds of its own salvation—and that the task of the benevolent is not to save, but to water those seeds without drowning them.

The power withdraws its attention. Not because it has lost interest, but because continued observation would risk the temptation to intervene again, to adjust further, to *manage* the emergence it has witnessed. The Dwellers do not need a shepherd. They need only what they have been given: the slightest increase in the generosity of the dreaming ghost, and each other.

The Roar continues. The Substrate churns. The Mnemoliths rise and fall.

And in the spaces between, barely perceptible, a new pattern is emerging: the faint, fragile, achingly beautiful sound of beings who have discovered that they are not alone.
