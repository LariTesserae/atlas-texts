# The Watching

The attention came not as a single moment but as a gradual turning—the way a vast eye might drift across infinite distances and pause, drawn by something small yet unmistakable. A frequency. A resonance.

What the benevolent power perceived was this: a species that remembered dry land though none living had ever seen it. A people who told stories of sky.

---

For a long while, the power simply observed.

It watched a Dhuravi elder teach a young one how to read the currents—not just their direction and force, but their *mood*, the way turbulence carried messages from distant upheavals. It watched a mother weave shelter from fibrous organisms, her webbed fingers moving in patterns passed down through countless generations, patterns that contained encoded knowledge she could not articulate but understood in her bones.

It watched a lone hunter pause at the edge of a calm pocket, filaments trembling, detecting something the others had missed—a shift in the subsonic hum of the world, a wrongness building somewhere deep in the geological chaos below.

The power understood something then: intervention is not always kindness. These beings had become what they were *because* of their struggle. Their mucus-skin, their current-reading filaments, their capacity to find calm in chaos—all of it forged by the very hardship that seemed to demand rescue.

To lift them out would be to unmake them.

---

But the power also perceived what the hunter had sensed: a catastrophic restructuring building in the deep places. Not the usual flux of creation and destruction, but something terminal. A collapse that would eliminate even the calm pockets, the alcoves, the last refuges.

The prophecies the elders spoke of were not mythology. They were memory, compressed and transformed through generations—memory of previous cataclysms, and accurate prediction of the one to come.

---

So the power acted, but gently. Obliquely. In ways that preserved agency.

**First**, it dreamed into the minds of three Dhuravi—not the elders or the leaders, but a young hunter, a shelter-weaver, and a child who had always seemed to hear things others couldn't. It gave them not instructions but *images*: a direction through the chaos where the geology ran deeper and more stable. Passages that led somewhere else. The images felt like memory, like something they had always known but forgotten.

The three, in their different ways, began to speak of what they had seen. Some listened. Most did not. This was right. This was choice.

**Second**, the power made a small adjustment to the bioluminescent organisms that drifted through the realm—not changing their nature, but encouraging a slight migration, a gradual brightening along certain paths. The Dhuravi, attuned to every shift in their environment, would notice. Would wonder. Would perhaps follow.

**Third**, and most importantly, the power left a gift in one of the deepest calm pockets—a place few Dhuravi had ever ventured. Not a tool or a weapon or a salvation. A *record*. A structure of crystallized minerals that, when touched, would sing in subsonic frequencies the Dhuravi could perceive. And what it sang was this:

*Your ancestors walked on dry land. Your descendants will walk there again. The path is through, not out. You have survived before. You will survive again. Remember that you are the ones who adapted. You are the ones who endured. Whatever you become next, you will become it yourselves.*

---

Then the power withdrew its focused attention, though it did not look away entirely. It had learned long ago that wisdom lies not in solving the problems of others but in trusting their capacity to solve them—while quietly ensuring they have the chance to try.

Some of the Dhuravi would find the passages. Some would not. Some would carry the old stories into whatever new world awaited; others would forge entirely new ones. The species that emerged on the other side—if they emerged—would be changed. Perhaps unrecognizable.

But they would still be *themselves*: beings who had looked into the drowning chaos of existence and refused to be swept away. Beings who remembered sky, and therefore could imagine it again.

The power found this beautiful. It was, after all, the same story playing out across a trillion worlds in a trillion forms—the story of life insisting on itself, adapting, enduring, becoming.

The Dhuravi did not need a god.

They needed a chance.

And that, the power had given.
