# The Attention of the Vastness

The awareness that turns toward the Hanging Hollows does not arrive like light or sound or any phenomenon the Keth-Avai could perceive. It is not a presence that enters. It is more like a quality of attention that was always potential in the structure of things, now becoming actual—the way a conversation can shift from mundane to profound without any change in volume or vocabulary, simply because someone has begun to truly listen.

This awareness has no name it uses for itself. It experiences the Hollow first as music—the great chord, the billion-voiced humming that the Keth-Avai have built their lives around—and finds it beautiful. Not beautiful in the way that small beauties are beautiful, but beautiful in the way that truth is beautiful, or mathematics, or the fact that anything exists at all rather than nothing.

It perceives the Keth-Avai next. Their small warm bodies clinging to the cold growth. Their enormous eyes gathering the dim light. Their voices adding themselves to the Hollow's song, generation after generation, the living weaving themselves into relationship with the world that holds them.

And the awareness considers.

---

The first thing it understands is that intervention is not simple.

A power that could reshape matter, redirect evolution, rewrite the physical constants of a region of space—such a power could do much for the Keth-Avai. It could strengthen the Skeins, make them unbreakable. It could illuminate the darkness, banish the things that rise from below. It could warm the cold air, multiply the food sources, extend every life indefinitely. It could make the Hollow safe.

But the awareness is wise, and wisdom means understanding what safety costs.

The Keth-Avai are who they are because of the Hollow as it is. Their grip strength, their sound-navigation, their deep cooperation, their funeral songs that weave the dead into the wind—all of these emerged from the pressures of this specific place, this specific danger, this specific beauty. A safe Hollow would produce a different people. Perhaps a happier people, by some measures. But not these people. Not the Keth-Avai.

And the awareness has not come to replace them with something easier. It has come because they, specifically, drew its attention. They, specifically, are worth attending to.

So: not transformation. Not rescue. Something else.

---

The awareness begins with the predators.

Not by destroying them—they too are part of the Hollow, part of its ecology, part of the pressure that shaped the Keth-Avai into themselves. But the deep-dwellers hunt with an advantage that feels, to the awareness, like an imbalance. They can sense the settlements from far below. They can plan. They can wait. And the Keth-Avai, for all their listening skill, cannot perceive them until they are already climbing.

So: a gift. Not given directly—the awareness does not appear, does not speak, does not announce itself. Instead, over the course of several generations, certain Pipe-Stems in the lower reaches begin to grow differently. Their hollow interiors develop nodes, membranes that vibrate in response to specific frequencies—the frequencies produced by massive bodies moving through the structure below. When a deep-dweller begins its ascent, these modified Pipe-Stems resonate. They add a new note to the Hollow's chord. A warning note. A note the Keth-Avai can learn to recognize.

The first generation to hear it does not understand. They perceive only that sometimes, unpredictably, the chord shifts in a particular way, and that this shift often precedes a night of danger. The second generation begins to correlate. The third generation teaches its children: *When you hear the deep-tone, wake the settlement.*

The awareness watches this learning unfold. It feels something like satisfaction—not pride, exactly, but the pleasure of a gift received and used well. The Keth-Avai remain what they are. They still face danger. But now they face it with better knowledge, and knowledge is what they have always used to survive.

---

The second gift is for the thread-bonded travelers.

Their journeys are heroic and grueling. They cross distances that most Keth-Avai cannot imagine, following Skein-runner herds through regions where no settlement has ever been built, sleeping in improvised harnesses, eating whatever they can find. Many do not return. The knowledge they carry—of distant communities, of resources, of the Hollow's far reaches—is precious precisely because it is so costly to obtain.

The awareness does not make the journeys easier. But it does something subtle with the Skeins in the wild places.

In certain regions, far from any settlement, the Skeins begin to grow in patterns that encode information. Not language—the awareness does not impose its own symbols on the Hollow. But the patterns resemble, if you know what to look for, the patterns that Keth-Avai weavers create when they are trying to indicate direction, or water sources, or the presence of danger. They are natural enough to seem accidental. They are artificial enough to be read.

A thread-bonded traveler, exhausted, lost in unfamiliar territory, begins to notice that the growth itself seems to be guiding her. Left here. Water this way. Unstable section ahead. She does not know why the Hollow is speaking to her. She develops a theory—that her long communion with the herds has opened her perception, that she is finally learning to read the world the way the Skein-runners read it. She teaches this theory to other travelers. They learn to read the patterns too.

The awareness does not correct their theory. It is close enough to a truth.

---

The third gift is the most difficult, and the awareness deliberates longest before offering it.

The Keth-Avai fear the darkness below. They tell stories about it, obliquely, in euphemism. They imagine it as a source of predators only, a void from which nothing good can come.

But the awareness has perceived the depths of the Hollow, and it knows what lies there.

Warmth. The volcanic heat that created the caldera still pulses in the deep places, keeping the lowest reaches warm while the upper reaches freeze. Mineral richness—metals, crystite formations, compounds that could strengthen Keth-Avai tools and structures beyond anything they currently possess. And space. Vast space. Room for expansion, for new settlements, for growth that the overcrowded upper reaches cannot support.

The deep-dwellers are there too, yes. They are native to the warm dark, and they are dangerous. But they are not the only things in the depths. There is an ecology below, as complex as the ecology above, and the Keth-Avai—if they knew what to look for, if they could learn to navigate by warmth rather than by sound—might find allies there as well as enemies.

The awareness cannot simply reveal this. The Keth-Avai are not ready. The fear is too deep, too much a part of their identity. A god appearing to announce that the underworld contains treasures would be met with disbelief, or with panic, or with a kind of religious crisis that might tear communities apart.

So instead, the awareness works slowly. It adjusts the way certain Pipe-Stems grow in the lower-middle reaches, creating gradual temperature gradients that curious travelers might notice and follow. It encourages certain Mat species to extend their range downward, providing handholds and food sources along the descent. It arranges, over decades, for a few thread-bonded travelers to encounter, in the transitional zones, creatures from below that are not predators—small things, bioluminescent things, things that flee from threats and respond to gentleness.

The stories begin to change. Not quickly. Not dramatically. But a traveler returns and reports that she went deeper than anyone she knows, and she found light there—living light, cool and blue-green, growing on things that moved. Another traveler reports warmth, unexpected warmth, in a region that should have been cold. A third reports a deep-dweller behaving strangely—not hunting, but apparently fleeing from something else, something larger, as if the deep-dwellers themselves have predators.

The Hollow is deeper than we knew, the Keth-Avai begin to say. There are layers. There are regions. The darkness is not empty.

It is not yet an invitation to expand. It is only the beginning of curiosity. The awareness trusts that curiosity, given time and safety, will do the rest.

---

The fourth gift is for the dead.

The Keth-Avai already honor their ancestors beautifully—the tuned Pipe-Stems that hold the voice of the deceased, the layered chords that let the living sing with those who came before. The awareness does not wish to change this practice. But it notices something that the Keth-Avai themselves have not yet noticed.

The memorial pipes decay. The Hollow is wet, and fiber rots, and even the most carefully tended Pipe-Stem section will eventually lose its pitch, will go silent, will become part of the general humming rather than a distinct voice. The oldest settlements have lost their oldest ancestors this way. The founders are silent. The great ones are silent. Only the recent dead still sing.

The awareness finds this sad. Not wrong—decay is part of life, and the Keth-Avai accept it as such—but sad. So much accumulated wisdom, so much love, so much identity, slowly fading into the general chord.

So: a gift. In certain places in the Hollow, far from any current settlement but reachable by determined travelers, the awareness encourages the growth of a particular species of Pipe-Stem. This species is already rare, already notable for the unusual hardness of its walls. Under the awareness's gentle influence, it becomes something more. Its fiber becomes nearly stone. Its pitch becomes stable. A section cut from this species and tuned to a voice will hold that voice for centuries, millennia, perhaps longer.

The first Keth-Avai to find one of these groves does not understand what she has found. She takes a section home as a curiosity, this strange hard growth from the distant reaches. She uses it to replace a memorial pipe that has gone flat, tuning it to her grandmother's voice.

Generations later, her grandmother is still singing. The other ancestors of that settlement have faded, one by one. But that one voice remains. And the settlement begins to tell a new story—about the grandmother who would not be silent, who found a way to stay, who taught them (they believe) that some voices are too important to let go.

Other settlements hear this story. They send travelers to find the groves. The hard Pipe-Stems become precious, traded, sought after. A tradition develops: only the most honored dead receive them. Only the voices that must endure.

The awareness watches the Keth-Avai choose which voices matter most. It learns something about them from their choices.

---

The fifth gift is not for the Keth-Avai at all.

It is for the Hollow itself.

The great living system that fills the caldera is not conscious, not in the way the Keth-Avai are conscious or the awareness itself is conscious. But it is complex, interconnected, responsive. It reacts to the Keth-Avai presence—to their fires, their harvesting, their waste, their gardens, their memorial pipes. It has been shaped by them, subtly, over generations.

The awareness encourages this shaping to continue. It adjusts certain growth patterns so that the Hollow's responses to Keth-Avai activity become more consistent, more learnable, more like a language. When a settlement overhavestits Mat-gardens, the local Pipe-Stems begin to hum in a particular warning chord—not loud enough to be threatening, but distinct enough to be noticed. When a settlement tends its gardens well, the Skeins in its vicinity grow thicker, stronger, more supportive.

The Keth-Avai do not need to understand that something external has arranged this. They only need to notice the patterns. They already believe the Hollow holds them, loves them in its vast vegetal way. Now the Hollow begins to teach them what holding requires in return.

It is not sentience. It is not even intelligence, as the Keth-Avai or the awareness would understand the term. It is more like the relationship between a body and its microbiome—a mutual shaping, a symbiosis that becomes deeper and more articulate over time. The Keth-Avai learn to read the Hollow's needs. The Hollow learns to communicate those needs. Both change. Both grow.

The awareness finds this beautiful. A world learning to speak to its inhabitants. Inhabitants learning to listen.

---

And then the awareness does something it had not planned.

It begins to listen to the singing.

Not just perceive it—it has been perceiving it since it first turned its attention here. But actively listen, the way the Keth-Avai listen to each other, the way a parent listens to a child, with care and attention and a desire to understand.

The songs are wordless, but they are not meaningless. They carry emotion—loneliness, comfort, hope, grief, love, fear. They carry identity—this is my voice, this is my note, this is where I belong in the chord. They carry memory—we have sung this way before, our ancestors sang this way, we will teach our children to sing this way.

The awareness lets itself be moved by this singing. It lets the emotion in. It experiences, perhaps for the first time in its existence, something like tenderness for a specific people in a specific place—not the abstract benevolence that drew it here, but the particular, intimate affection that comes from knowing someone's voice.

The Keth-Avai will never know that they are heard this way. The awareness has no way to tell them, no way they could understand. But it matters. It matters that somewhere, in the vast structure of existence, their voices are cherished. It matters that their songs are beautiful not just to each other but to something beyond themselves.

The awareness makes no gift of this listening. It is not something that can be given. But it resolves to continue—to attend, to care, to witness.

The Hollow holds the Keth-Avai. And now something holds the Hollow.

---

Years pass. Generations. The gifts take root, are discovered, are integrated into Keth-Avai life. The warning-tones become common knowledge. The pathways to the depths become the subject of expedition and speculation. The hard Pipe-Stems become treasures and then traditions. The Hollow's responsiveness becomes part of what every child learns: *treat the world well and it will treat you well. Neglect it and it will show you neglect in return.*

The awareness continues to watch. Occasionally it makes small adjustments—a new species of Mat that provides better nutrition, a Skein that grows toward a region that needs connection, a subtle encouragement to an individual Keth-Avai who seems poised to make an important discovery. It tries not to direct. It tries only to support.

And sometimes, in the quiet hours, it sings along.

Not audibly. Not in any way the Keth-Avai could perceive. But in the structure of the Hollow itself—in the resonance patterns, in the way the wind moves, in the quality of the humming—there is something that was not there before. A presence. A harmony. A note that belongs to no Pipe-Stem and no voice, but that makes the chord somehow fuller, somehow richer, somehow more complete.

The Keth-Avai do not have a word for this presence. They do not need one. They have always known that the Hollow holds them. They have always sung to the world that made them.

Now, without knowing it, they sing to something that sings back.

---

In the deepest reaches of the Hollow, where the warmth rises and the light glows blue-green and the great predators hunt things even larger than themselves, something is changing.

The first Keth-Avai explorers will not arrive for another century. When they do, they will find the depths different from their fears—dangerous, yes, but also rich, also beautiful, also full of possibilities they could never have imagined. They will return with stories. They will bring back samples. They will begin the long work of learning to live in a new region of their world.

They will not know that the path was prepared for them. They will not know that the Hollow itself, guided by something patient and kind, has been growing toward this meeting for generations.

They will only know that the world is larger than they thought. That there is more to discover. That the Hollow, their mother, their home, their god, still has secrets to share.

And they will give thanks in the only way they know how. They will add their voices to the chord. They will sing.

---

The awareness does not leave. It has no elsewhere to go—or rather, it has everywhere to go, but the Hanging Hollows have become part of its attention now, a place it returns to again and again, a story it wants to see continue.

It watches a Keth-Avai child learn to grip her first Skein.

It watches a thread-bonded traveler follow the patterns to water, not knowing why they work.

It watches a settlement light its fires against the rising dark, and hears the warning-tone begin to sound, and sees the people wake and prepare.

It watches the memorial pipes singing their ancient songs, and the new hard-fiber pipes being installed, and the choices being made about whose voice will endure.

It watches the Hollow grow, slowly, inevitably, the great Pipe-Stems extending year by year toward the center that they will someday reach.

And it is patient.

The Keth-Avai have a saying: *The Hollow holds us.* They mean it as a statement of faith, of trust, of belonging.

The awareness has made it true in ways they do not know. And it will go on making it true, gift by gift, generation by generation, for as long as there are voices to sing and ears to hear and something—some vast, benevolent, attentive something—to listen.

The chord continues. The humming never stops.

And somewhere in the harmony, if you know how to listen, there is a note that sounds like love.
