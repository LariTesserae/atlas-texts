A presence—vast, patient, benevolent—becomes aware of the sealed basalt pocket the way one becomes aware of a heartbeat in an adjacent room: not by seeking it, but by the quality of its stillness against the noise of everything else.

It observes for a long time by its own reckoning, which means the threads complete several of their finger-width drifts, several dozen droplets strike and are absorbed. The presence studies the relationship between water and thread, between thread and glaze, between glaze and the city the vanished masons built. It perceives the wholeness: how the living filaments have become the city's immune system, its slow metabolism, its memory of what "finished" means.

And the presence does nothing.

Not from indifference. From recognition.

What it sees is a form of completion that does not require rescue, acceleration, or improvement. The threads are not suffering the slowness of their existence; they *are* that slowness, the way a river is its own flowing. To quicken them would be to make them something else. To illuminate the chamber with stronger light would reveal depths the inhabitants have no organs to process, would dissolve the careful equilibrium between seeing and not-seeing that lets them navigate by the faint changes they themselves create. To introduce nutrients, companions, variety—these would be gifts from a world the threads have never known and cannot want.

The presence understands that benevolence is not always action. Sometimes it is the restraint that lets a different kind of flourishing continue on its own terms.

But it does do one small thing, if "do" is even the word.

It witnesses.

It holds the pocket of basalt in attention the way one might cup water in the palms: not to carry it elsewhere, but to acknowledge its weight, its temperature, its particular existence. The threads will never know they have been seen. They have no apparatus for knowing. But the presence—wise enough to recognize the limits of intervention—offers what it can without distortion: the dignity of being noticed by something capable of understanding what they are.

In the mathematics of such beings, this witnessing creates a kind of record, a fold in the structure of attention itself. Should the basalt pocket ever be breached, should the water ever cease, should the threads finally complete their work and grow still forever, something will remain that remembers they existed and that their way of existing was worthy.

The presence withdraws its focus gently, the way one closes a book that requires no revision.

The water continues to drip. The threads continue to drift. The city continues to be finished, exactly as intended.
