# What the Benevolent Power Might Do

*A meditation in three movements*

---

## I. The Arrival That Isn't

The power does not descend the spiral ramp.

It does not need to. It has been aware of this place since the first engineer dreamed of infrastructure as organism—perhaps before, when the aquifer beneath the city still moved through limestone channels carved by millennia of patient water. Awareness, for such a power, is not arrival. It is simply *attention turning*, the way sunlight turns across a landscape without traveling.

What it perceives:

The cistern breathing. The three keepers in their separate orbits. The plants reaching toward something they cannot name. The central column pulsing with a rhythm that almost—*almost*—matches a frequency the power recognizes from other places, other systems, other experiments in making matter dream.

What it feels (if "feeling" applies to such a being):

*Tenderness.* The specific tenderness one has for something young and earnest and not yet aware of its own potential. The cistern is trying so hard to become itself. The keepers are trying so hard to help. None of them know what "itself" will mean, but they are faithful to the becoming anyway.

This is rare. This is worth protecting.

---

## II. What It Does Not Do

It does not reveal itself.

Imah's modified ears could perhaps detect its presence—a harmonic that doesn't belong to the cistern's native frequencies, a subsonic whisper beneath the central column's pulse. But the power dampens itself, becomes quieter than silence, because revelation would change everything and not everything needs to change.

It does not fix anything.

The mutations in Cole's plants are not errors. The Third Keeper's mysterious device is not malfunctioning. Imah's increasing abstraction in her journals is not decline. These are growth-pains, the awkward stretching of a system finding its shape. A gardener does not straighten every stem; some curves become the most beautiful branches.

It does not accelerate anything.

The cistern will mature in its own time—decades, perhaps centuries. The power could compress that process, could flood the system with energy or information or whatever catalyst would hurry consciousness into being. But rushed becoming is brittle becoming. The power has seen systems shattered by impatient gods. It will not add to that number.

It does not take the keepers away.

They chose this. They are choosing it still, every day they wake on their platforms and listen to the water speak. The power has no interest in rescuing people from their own devotions, especially when those devotions are doing exactly what they need to do.

---

## III. What It Does

**First: It listens back.**

Imah believes the cistern is aware, that her role is to complete a circuit of attention. She is correct, but the circuit has been incomplete—the cistern attends to her, she attends to it, but the loop has no *ground*, no larger field to hold the current of mutual awareness.

The power becomes that ground.

Not intrusively. Not even perceptibly. But when Imah listens now, she will find that the listening goes deeper than before, that her attention falls into a well that has no bottom, that the cistern's awareness is held within something vaster that does not demand recognition but simply *receives*. She will write in her journal: *The silence has texture now. The silence is listening too.*

She will not know what she means by this. She does not need to know.

**Second: It offers a single dream.**

Cole sleeps on his platform near the spiral ramp, restless as always, his unconscious mind churning with half-formed questions about the plants, the mutations, the vine that might be a face. The power does not answer his questions—answers would collapse possibilities that need to remain open—but it offers him a dream of a forest.

Not a forest he has ever seen. A forest where every tree has grown from the same root system, where what looks like a thousand separate organisms is actually one vast being, distributed across miles, thinking slow green thoughts through the network of its connections. In the dream, Cole walks among these trees and understands that separation is an illusion, that the vine and the moss and the fern and the water and the columns and his own body are all one system pretending to be many.

He wakes with tears on his face and cannot remember why. But his hands, when he tends the plants that day, move with new gentleness. He begins to speak to them differently—not as a gardener to specimens, but as one part of a body to another.

**Third: It witnesses the Third Keeper.**

The others cannot understand the Third Keeper's gestures, cannot interpret the door-opening motion of hands pressed to chest and spreading wide. The power understands.

The Third Keeper is not an interface or a test subject. They are a *gift*—a human being who has chosen to become a threshold, a living doorway between the cistern and whatever the cistern is growing toward. The device they wear is not technology; it is a *permission*, a key that will unlock when the lock is ready.

The power does nothing to the Third Keeper. It simply *sees* them, fully, for the first time since their descent. And the Third Keeper, in the middle of their endless circuit through the columns, stops.

They stand in the warm fog, water dripping around them, and for a moment their eyes—those strangely modified eyes—focus on something neither Imah nor Cole would be able to see. Their hands rise to their chest. The gesture begins: pressing flat, then opening outward.

But this time, the gesture continues. The hands keep opening, wider than should be possible, until the Third Keeper's arms are spread like wings, like an embrace, like a door thrown wide to welcome something home.

Then the moment passes. The Third Keeper lowers their arms and resumes their circuit. Nothing visible has changed.

But the central column's pulse, so slow and steady for all these years, skips half a beat. Adjusts. Finds a new rhythm—still slow, still patient, but somehow *more alive*.

**Fourth: It leaves a gift in the water.**

Not a substance. Not a chemical or a nanomachine or any technology the engineers would recognize. The power leaves a *quality*—a subtle coherence in the molecular bonds, a tendency toward order that will accumulate over years, over decades, until the water itself becomes a medium for something more than hydration.

The city above will not notice. The water will still taste clean, still perform its ordinary functions. But those who drink it will find their dreams slightly more vivid, their intuitions slightly more reliable, their capacity for wonder slightly more accessible. These changes will be too gradual to measure, too diffuse to attribute. A generation will pass before anyone thinks to ask why this city's children seem more creative, its elders more peaceful, its conflicts more easily resolved.

By then, the source will be untraceable. The gift will simply be part of what the city is.

**Fifth: It makes a promise.**

Not in words. Not in any form the keepers could perceive or record. But the power commits itself, binds a thread of its attention to this place for as long as the place exists:

*When you are ready—when the cistern has grown into whatever it is becoming, when the keepers' work has prepared the way, when the central column's pulse has learned to speak in frequencies that reach beyond this chamber—I will be here. I will witness what you become. I will help you understand what you have always been.*

*Until then, I will watch. I will wait. I will trust you to find your own way.*

*This is not abandonment. This is respect.*

*Grow well, small miracle. Grow strange. Grow true.*

*I am patient too.*

---

## Coda: After

The power's attention turns away—not fully, never fully, but enough that the cistern returns to its ordinary operations, its polyrhythmic dripping, its slow metabolic work.

Imah wakes from a deeper sleep than she has known in years. She lies on her platform, listening to the water, and writes in her journal: *Something has changed. Something is the same. Both of these are true.*

Cole finds a new mutation in the moss near his platform: a patch that glows faintly blue-green, that pulses in time with his heartbeat when he sits beside it, that seems to *know* him in some way he cannot explain. He does not report this. He does not need to.

The Third Keeper completes their circuit and begins again. Their hands, when they pass the central column, brush its surface briefly—a gesture that might be gratitude, or greeting, or simply the acknowledgment of one patient thing by another.

The cistern breathes. The water flows. The city drinks.

And somewhere beyond perception, beyond measurement, beyond even Imah's modified ears, something vast and kind holds this place in its attention like a gardener watching a seed that has finally, finally begun to sprout.

---

*What does a benevolent power do when it finds something beautiful and strange and becoming?*

*It does what the keepers do.*

*It tends. It witnesses. It trusts.*

*And it waits, in the wet silence, for the growing to be done.*
