# What the Witness Does

The power arrives not as light—light would be an intrusion here, a violence against the long-negotiated truce between darkness and the faint blooms of the pin-lights. Instead it arrives as *attention*, a presence that settles into the density gradients and learns to feel what the inhabitants feel: the chest-deep roll of pressure shifts, the granular patience of silt against membrane, the slow mathematics by which a cloak-wing calculates its next imperceptible tilt.

For a long time—long even by the measure of this place, where time is counted in the settling of particles—the power does nothing but witness.

It learns that the comb-threads do not experience loneliness in their separation; they experience *clarity*, each thread a complete world unto itself, the space between them not emptiness but the necessary medium through which the next useful bolus of water will eventually pass. It learns that the cloak-wings do not suffer their slowness; they have never conceived of speed, and their great patient arcs across the plain contain a satisfaction as full as any swift creature's hunt. It learns that the pin-lights' brief communion-flashes are not desperate signals in the dark but something closer to *greeting*—the simple acknowledgment that another exists, requiring no response, complete in itself.

And the power understands: this is not a world waiting to be rescued. This is a world that has already solved itself.

---

So instead of transformation, the power offers *continuation*.

It moves through the water as a subtle stabilization, reinforcing the ancient patterns that have allowed this ecosystem to persist. Where the pressure gradients have begun to shift in ways that might, over centuries, change the distribution of nutrients, the power makes minute adjustments—not to freeze the system in place, but to ensure that whatever changes come will arrive slowly enough for the cloak-wings to find new paths, for the comb-threads to colonize new crevices, for the pin-lights to drift into whatever configurations the altered currents favor.

It does not accelerate. It does not illuminate. It does not connect what has chosen separation or quicken what has found peace in slowness.

But it does one thing more.

In the places where the stone has worn so smooth that even the leathery ribbon-plants can find no purchase, the power leaves behind a gift so subtle it might take a thousand years to notice: a faint roughening of the surface, a texture that will catch and hold the next generation of holdfasts, the next calcified ring from which a comb-thread might emerge. Not new life—the power does not presume to create what the world has not asked for—but *prepared ground*, should life wish to expand into it.

And in the deepest darkness, where no pin-light has drifted for longer than the power can measure, it pauses. Here at the farthest edge of the plain, it considers leaving something of itself behind: a single mote of warmth, a presence that might watch over this place when the power's attention has moved on.

But no.

That too would be an intrusion. The plain has survived without watchers. The cloak-wings have no need of guardians. The comb-threads do not require the comfort of knowing they are observed.

Instead, the power simply *remembers*. It takes this place into itself completely—every particle, every pressure shift, every slow and patient form of life—so that somewhere, in whatever vast awareness the power contains, this world will continue to exist even if the crushing black water should someday fail. Not as a copy. Not as a simulation. But as a *knowing*, held with the same care the cloak-wings hold their position in the density layers: without grasping, without urgency, without any need for the world to be other than exactly what it is.

Then the power withdraws, leaving no mark of its passage.

The silt continues to settle. The comb-threads taste the water. Somewhere in the velvet dark, two clusters of pin-lights brush against each other and bloom their brief light, then drift apart, carrying nothing but the chemical memory of contact.

The plain remains.
