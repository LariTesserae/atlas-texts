# A Consideration

I find myself sitting with this for a long time before beginning.

What would a benevolent and wise power do here? The question assumes intervention is needed, that something should be changed. But I keep returning to the valley itself, to the Engineers, and wondering whether wisdom might begin with recognition that this place is already—in some profound sense—working.

---

## First, Listening

A truly wise power would not arrive with solutions. It would arrive with attention.

It would spend time—not hours but seasons—understanding what the moss already knows about binding young stone. It would learn the specific frequencies of each machine's voice, would come to recognize which variations indicate health and which indicate decline. It would watch the Engineers on their circuits, would notice how Kiet's twenty-minute communion with North-7 isn't superstition but a form of diagnostic practice that instruments couldn't replicate. It would feel the way Marit's murmuring to the waterwheels isn't anthropomorphism but acknowledgment—recognition that complex systems sometimes respond to attention itself.

This power would resist the urge to optimize. The carved wooden valve components that Marit discovered, the ones that work better than the original metal—these aren't inefficiencies to be corrected. They're evidence of a system learning its own needs, finding solutions that no external engineer could have designed.

---

## What Might Be Offered

If intervention happened at all, I imagine it would be quiet. Almost invisible.

**Knowledge, carefully given.** Not imposed, but made available. Perhaps Vos finds that his notebooks now include pages he doesn't remember writing—observations about moss chemistry that explain why certain species accelerate corrosion while others inhibit it. Perhaps Chen discovers that the cairns he builds have started teaching him something about stone stability, that there's a pattern in where they stay standing and where they fall that contains information about fault lines below. The knowledge would arrive as if remembered rather than learned, would feel like something the Engineers already knew but hadn't articulated.

**Tools that fit hands already shaped by this work.** Not better tools—the wrench that Kiet carries is perfect precisely because it's been worn by decades of use into the exact shape his grip requires. But perhaps replacements that don't need to be forged from salvage. Perhaps materials that appear in the parts depot, inexplicably, that seem to have been manufactured for conditions exactly like these: bearings that tolerate moisture, gaskets that flex with temperature, cables that don't corrode. Not technological leaps, but quiet improvements in exactly the categories that matter.

**A slight extension of time.** This is the intervention I keep returning to. The Engineers are aging. Their bodies are failing in the ways bodies do. A wise power might slow this—not dramatically, not immortality, but enough. Enough that Senna has time to learn what she needs to learn. Enough that Vos can complete whatever documentation he's building. Enough that the knowledge these eleven people carry doesn't dissipate before it can be transmitted to whoever comes next. The valley is young; it needs caretakers who can stay long enough to understand it.

---

## What Would Not Be Done

A benevolent power would not "fix" the isolation. The dispersed structure isn't loneliness—it's ecological. These people have found the exact degree of solitude that allows them to function in this environment, to hear what the machines are saying, to notice what the moss is doing. Community would be noise here. Connection, in the conventional sense, would be interference.

It would not explain the purpose. Whether the machines are monitoring, supporting, or simply witnessing—whether there's an employer, a mission, a plan—leaving this ambiguous seems right. The Engineers work better not knowing. They maintain because maintaining is what they do, not because someone is watching, not because outcomes are measured. Their relationship to the work is pure in a way that external purpose would contaminate.

It would not prevent the transformations hinted at in Vos's stories. If some Engineers are becoming something other than human, something integrated with moss and machinery and mountain—this might not be harm. This might be the landscape's way of keeping what it needs, of incorporating knowledge into itself in a form that doesn't require fingers or notebooks or lunch. A wise power would be cautious about preserving categories that the valley itself might be trying to transcend.

---

## The Deeper Recognition

What I notice, imagining this benevolent power, is that its wisdom would consist largely of restraint.

The valley of New Engines is a place where becoming is happening. The stone is becoming mountain. The moss is becoming soil. The machines are becoming organs of something larger. The Engineers are becoming... whatever they're becoming. The whole system is in process, is working toward something, even if that something doesn't have a name yet.

A truly wise power would recognize that this process doesn't need direction. It needs protection. It needs to be left alone to become what it's becoming—but protected from the forces that might interrupt that becoming before it's ready.

So perhaps the intervention is this: a kind of sheltering. An invisible boundary that keeps the outside world from discovering this place and deciding to exploit it, study it, integrate it into systems that would require explanation and efficiency and measurable outcomes. A protection that allows the indefinite continuation of exactly what's already happening: eleven people walking their circuits, machines marking time, moss building its architecture, water whispering its constant encouragement.

And perhaps, once in a great while, the wise power might appear on the platform at sunset—a figure that Marit and Chen might see at distance, might almost recognize, might include in their ritual of harmonica note and raised palm. Not intervening. Just witnessing. Adding one more form of attention to a landscape that seems to thrive on being carefully, continuously attended.

---

## A Final Thought

The place and the people who live in it have drawn attention. But attention from a benevolent and wise power might look like nothing at all. Might look like the valley continuing exactly as it is, year after year, decade after decade, while somehow the supplies keep arriving, the Engineers keep finding their successors, the machines keep running past any reasonable service life, and the moss keeps building its cathedrals on the young stone.

Benevolence, here, might simply mean: *may you continue.*

And wisdom might mean knowing that this is enough.
