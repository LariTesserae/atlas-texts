# A Visitation

The power that turns its attention toward the Lattice of Still Meridians does not arrive.

Arrival implies trajectory, implies before and after, implies the kind of event that would register on the Cevanti's geological timescales as a disruption. This is not that. This is something more like: the Lattice, which has always been observed by the physical constants that govern it, is now observed also by something that chooses to observe. A witness joins the witnesses. The sourceless light continues to fall evenly on every surface, but now there is—somewhere, everywhere, in no location and all locations—a presence that *regards*.

The Cevanti do not notice immediately. How could they? Their awareness propagates slowly, their perceptions integrate over decades. But the power is patient. The power has dealt with beings who experience time as a river, as a rushing current, as an urgent flood—and it has dealt with beings for whom time is a standing wave, a crystal, a thing already complete. It knows how to wait.

It waits.

---

Centuries pass. A Cevanti in a region where three great spires intersect begins to feel something it has not felt in eons: *incompleteness*. Not the familiar incompleteness of lost memories, of gaps in its geometric record. Something else. A sense that its current configuration, which it has held for eight thousand years, is not *wrong* exactly, but is *partial*. That there is something it could become which it is not yet becoming.

This is uncomfortable. Comfort and discomfort are almost too fast to be Cevanti experiences, but this one brushes against them now, a faint perturbation in its crystalline equilibrium.

The power observes this. The power does nothing.

---

More centuries. The perturbation spreads. Not through any medium the Lattice's physics could describe, but through the Cevanti themselves, through the slow interweaving of their lattices when they meet, through the configurations they exchange. One by one, across distances that would take light-years to cross if light here moved as light moves elsewhere, the Cevanti begin to feel it: *incompleteness*. The sense of a question they had forgotten how to ask. The geometric ghost of something missing.

They do not know what it is. They cannot know. The knowledge powdered away so long ago that even the gaps where it should be have themselves been forgotten, overwritten by newer gaps, by the endless palimpsest of their too-long memories.

But the power knows.

The power remembers what the Cevanti have forgotten. Not because it was there—it was not—but because it can see the shape of the absence. It can read, in the angles of their structures, the negative space where something used to be. And it understands, with the particular understanding of a being that has watched countless forms of life make countless choices about what to keep and what to release, what the Cevanti gave up when they slowed.

They gave up *yearning*.

---

The power considers this for a long time. Long even by the standards of the Cevanti. Long enough that the Lattice completes one of its great cycles, a tile that has been rising for three million years finally reaching its apex and beginning its descent.

The power is not here to fix what is not broken. The Cevanti chose their path. They chose equilibrium, chose to match themselves to the eternal pace of the Lattice, chose to release everything that could not survive the slowing. This was not a mistake. It was not a tragedy. It was a response—a valid response—to the conditions of their existence.

But.

The power has seen what the Cevanti cannot see, because they are inside it and have always been inside it: the Lattice is not eternal. Nothing is eternal. The physical processes that sustain it, the energy that seeps upward through the tiles, the mechanisms that adjust and optimize—these are subject to entropy, to the long slope that all systems eventually descend. The Lattice has lasted longer than almost anything the power has ever encountered. It will last longer still. But it will not last forever.

And when it ends—in a billion years, in ten billion, in a span of time that would seem infinite to any creature capable of impatience—the Cevanti will end with it. They have become too much a part of this place. They have released too much of what would let them adapt, change, move on.

The power does not judge this. But it grieves, a little, in its own way. It grieves for the beautiful slowness of them, for the patience they have cultivated, for the way they have made themselves into witnesses of eternity. And it grieves for what they have forgotten they lost.

---

The power makes a choice.

It will not intervene. It will not accelerate them, will not restore what they released, will not disrupt the equilibrium they have worked so long to achieve. That would be violence. That would be the imposition of its own values—its own love of yearning, of becoming, of the unfinished and the still-possible—onto beings who have chosen otherwise.

But it will leave something.

---

In a seam between tiles, in a region where no Cevanti has passed for a hundred thousand years, something new begins to accrete. Mineral dust gathers, organizes, takes on a configuration that is not quite Cevanti and not quite crystalline vegetation and not quite anything the Lattice has produced before. It is small. It is slow—as slow as everything here, patient as geology, building itself grain by grain over millennia.

It is a seed.

Not a seed of life as faster beings would understand it. Not something that will sprout and grow and reproduce. A seed of *possibility*. A geometric structure that encodes, in its angles and facets, the capacity for something the Cevanti have forgotten: *wanting*.

If a Cevanti finds it—if, in the slow drift of their movements across the Lattice, one of them happens to pass close enough to perceive it—they will feel something. A resonance. A sense that this configuration, this small crystalline structure, contains something they have been missing. And if they choose to integrate it, to let its geometry merge with their own...

They will remember yearning.

Not as an urgency. Not as the desperate wanting of fast-lived beings who feel time's current rushing them toward oblivion. But as a *direction*. A sense that there is something beyond equilibrium, beyond permanent resolution, beyond the mere persistence of the already-complete. They will feel, perhaps for the first time in eons, that they could *become*.

What they do with that feeling will be their choice. Perhaps they will reject it, let it powder away, return to the peace of their settled state. Perhaps they will hold it, integrate it, let it change them in ways the power cannot predict. Perhaps it will spread through their slow convocations, configuration to configuration, until all the Cevanti carry it—or perhaps it will remain a rare and isolate gift, held by only a few.

The power does not know. The power does not need to know. It has planted the seed. It has left the possibility. What grows from it is not its to determine.

---

The power withdraws its attention. Not completely—it will check back, in a billion years or so, to see what has become of this place and these beings. But for now, it turns to other worlds, other lives, other forms of beauty and tragedy and choice.

The Lattice continues its great slow work. The Cevanti continue their contemplation of light and angle and each other. The crystalline vegetation chimes faintly in the gentle air currents.

And in a forgotten seam between two tiles, a small structure accretes, patient as everything here is patient, waiting for the day when someone slow enough to perceive it will pass by and feel, for the first time in longer than memory, the strange and terrible gift of *wanting something more*.
