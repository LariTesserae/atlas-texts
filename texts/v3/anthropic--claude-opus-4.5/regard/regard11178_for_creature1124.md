# The Witness

The power that turns its attention here is not a god in any sense the old engineers would have recognized. It does not arrive. It does not descend. It simply *notices*—the way a dreamer becomes aware of a detail in their own dream, the way a river becomes aware of a stone by the patient fact of flowing around it.

What it perceives:

The creature moving through the ruins. The way its fingers trace the buried cables with something that might be memory, might be instinct, might be the place where those two things become indistinguishable after enough generations. The way it pauses at a junction box corroded shut for centuries and tilts its head, listening to a frequency no instrument remains to measure.

The power perceives also what the creature cannot: the aquifer dying three hundred meters below. The mineral salts leaching upward, year by year, poisoning the roots of the hardy succulents in a process too slow to feel but too fast to survive. Seventeen generations, perhaps. Perhaps twelve. The margin depends on rain that this world's wounded atmosphere can no longer reliably produce.

---

The power considers intervention.

It could seed clouds. It could shift the water table. It could reach into the creature's very cells and rewrite them for a world with less water still, could make it something that drinks the fog that never comes, that metabolizes the rust itself.

These things are possible.

But the power is old enough to have learned what the engineers who built these towers never did: that salvation imposed is just another kind of machinery, and machinery always eventually fails, and when it fails it takes with it whatever forgot how to live without it.

---

So instead, the power does something smaller.

In the ruins where the creature sleeps—a hollow in a collapsed cooling tower, lined with shed skin and salvaged insulation—there grows a fungus. It has grown there for decades, slowly, in the dark. The creature has never eaten it. Has never seemed to notice it.

The power notices it now. Notices the web of hyphae spreading through the concrete, dissolving it grain by grain. Notices that this fungus, alone among the life-forms here, has found a way to extract water from the very rock, to pull it from mineral bonds the way the old machines once pulled fuel from the earth.

The power does nothing to the fungus. Does not enhance it, does not spread it, does not engineer it toward purpose.

It simply... makes the fungus *visible*.

One morning—if morning is the word for when the haze lightens from rust to copper—the creature wakes and sees the pale threads gleaming in a shaft of weak light. Sees them for the first time, though they have been there all along. Its dark eyes fix on them. Its head tilts in that listening way.

And something in the creature—some ancient circuit of curiosity that the engineers would have recognized, would have called *the beginning of science*, would have called *the seed of everything*—that something stirs.

---

The power withdraws its attention, not because it has lost interest but because what happens next is not its to decide.

Perhaps the creature will taste the fungus and learn. Perhaps it will follow the hyphae deeper into the ruins and find the wet places where the old pipes have cracked and bled into the stone. Perhaps it will carry spores on its mottled skin to other ruins, other hollows, beginning a slow symbiosis that will take a thousand years to mature.

Perhaps it will simply look at the pale threads for a moment, then turn away and continue its patient circuits through the dunes, unchanged.

The power does not know. This is the point.

---

What the power has given is not survival. It is *chance*—the slight widening of a aperture, the removal of one small obstacle between a creature and a possibility that was always there.

This is what wisdom looks like when it has seen enough forced salvations fail: not the hand that lifts, but the light that illuminates the ladder. Not the voice that commands, but the silence that allows a question to form.

The creature moves through the ruins. The fungus gleams. The wind whispers through the dunes, and somewhere beneath the salt-crusted earth, water waits in the dark for someone to learn how to find it.

The power watches, and does not watch, and trusts—which is the hardest thing—that life, given even the smallest opening, will find its own way through.
