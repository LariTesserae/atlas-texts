# The Witness at the Threshold

---

The benevolent power does not arrive.

Arrival implies a before and after, a crossing from absence into presence. But this awareness has no edges, no membrane to breach. It simply *attends*—the way silence attends to sound, the way depth attends to water. One moment the canyon roars alone in its broken physics; the next, something vast is listening.

And the first thing it feels is recognition.

Not pity. The benevolent power does not look upon the blind, deaf masons clinging to their vertical world and feel the condescension of the comfortable. It recognizes them the way a river recognizes the stone that shapes it—with intimate, ancient knowing. These creatures have done what all living things do: they have made meaning in a meaningless architecture. They have taken a screaming chaos and imposed upon it the dignity of purpose.

The power observes an elder pressing its massive jaw to the humming stone, reading the vibrations of a water-droplet three miles distant. It watches a young one leap from a precipice into a gravity-warped current, trusting its body to the alien mathematics it has learned to feel rather than understand. It sees the crude axes, the friction-locked handles, the desperate aqueducts spanning impossible voids.

And it understands something the inhabitants themselves have never articulated, because they have no language, no symbols, no stories—only stone striking stone and the long, patient work of drowning an engine.

*They are not trying to escape.*

*They are trying to heal.*

---

The power's first instinct—if something so vast can be said to have instincts—is to reach down and mend. It perceives the cracked sky as a wound, the gravity-warped water as arterial bleeding, the whole roaring cathedral as a body in trauma. It could, with a thought, smooth the fractured physics into gentleness. It could teach the light to soften, convince the water to remember falling, restore the silence these creatures have never known but somehow, impossibly, yearn for.

But it hesitates.

Because it also perceives something else: the inhabitants are not suffering in the way a trapped animal suffers. They are not thrashing against their conditions. They have *incorporated* the chaos. The glitched water is their food, their transit, their method of propagation. The stuttering dimensions are not obstacles—they are infrastructure. These blind masons have built a civilization inside a wound, and the wound has become indistinguishable from their flesh.

To heal the canyon might be to unmake them.

The power sits with this knowledge for what might be a moment or an eon—time, like gravity, behaves strangely in its presence. It watches a creature drink from a basin of dimensional friction, watches its silhouette blur and vibrate as it metabolizes the caloric output of collapsed space. The creature is nourished. The creature is warm. The creature exists *because* reality is broken here, not despite it.

And yet.

The power also perceives the ache beneath the purpose. It feels the way the inhabitants press their jaws to stone not just to navigate but to *listen*—as if hoping, against all evidence, that someday the roar will gentle into something like a voice. It senses the ritualized grief encoded in their propagation: an elder hauling its own reflection through dimensional skin, creating not a child but a replacement, a continuation without change. They are not evolving. They are enduring. There is a difference.

The aqueducts tell the deepest truth. These creatures believe—with the fervent, unreasoned faith of all beings who build temples to forces they cannot see—that they can drown the engine. They believe the broken sky can be mended. They work toward a future they will never inhabit, a silence they cannot imagine, a healing they pursue with stone axes because stone axes are all they have.

The benevolent power finds this unbearably beautiful and unbearably sad.

---

So it makes a choice.

It will not heal the canyon. It will not impose silence on creatures who have made music of chaos. But it will do something small—so small the inhabitants may never notice, so subtle it changes nothing and everything.

It adjusts the probability.

Not the physics. The physics remain shattered, the gravity warped, the light aggressive and cruel. But within the vast architecture of chance that governs which dimensional folds occur when, which streams spiral into which orbital rings, which reflections appear in which basins—within this invisible infrastructure, the power introduces a gentle bias.

The inhabitants will still drink dimensional friction to survive. They will still haul their parallel selves through the skin of other realities. But now, slightly more often than pure chaos would dictate, the water in their basins will show them something useful.

A reflection of a distant fin where the stone is softer, easier to chisel.

A glimpse of a current-pattern that will carry them safely across a void they feared.

An image of another iteration where an elder solved a problem differently, tried a technique that worked better—and the memory of that glimpse, absorbed into the body of the one who drinks, becoming intuition, becoming knowledge that feels like instinct.

The power does not teach them. Teaching would require language, concepts, a framework of transmission they do not possess. Instead, it *leans* on the probabilities, tilting the random flicker of the dimensional lenses toward relevance, toward utility, toward the slow accumulation of advantage.

Over generations—if generations is even the right word for beings who propagate through self-extraction—this subtle bias will compound. The aqueducts will become more efficient. The catchment basins will capture more of the right kinds of friction. The glitched ones, hauled imperfectly through dimensional skin, will emerge with their geometric misfires slightly less catastrophic, their wrong-bent elbows slightly more functional.

The inhabitants will not know they are being helped. They will believe, as all beings believe, that their survival is their own accomplishment. And they will be right. The power is not doing the work. It is only making the work slightly more likely to succeed.

---

But there is one more thing.

The benevolent power perceives, in the deepest fissures where the inhabitants are funneling their impossible water, something the masons cannot feel through their vibration-sense. The engine they are trying to drown is not a machine. It is a wound in the shape of a machine, a place where reality tore open long ago and has been bleeding physics ever since.

The inhabitants are right: it *can* be healed. Not drowned, but healed. The water they are pouring into the wound is, in fact, slowly suturing it—each gallon of multidimensional liquid adding a thread to the repair, each aqueduct contributing to a process that will take longer than stars take to form.

But the healing has a cost they cannot anticipate.

When the wound closes—when the last fissure seals and the engine finally falls silent—the cracked sky will soften. The light will gentle. Gravity will remember how to fall. And the inhabitants, who have evolved to eat dimensional friction and ride warped currents and propagate through reflections that no longer glitch, will find themselves in a world that no longer feeds them.

The power sees this future, clear as the reflections in the basins.

And it must decide: does it tell them?

---

Not in words. They have no ears, no language, no capacity for abstract communication. But the power could adjust the probability of the basin-reflections to show them something else—not useful techniques, but *prophecy*. Glimpses of a healed world. Images of a softened sky. The slow fade of the friction that sustains them.

It could warn them, in the only language they might understand: the language of things glimpsed and metabolized, visions that become instinct, fears that feel like memory.

But warning them might break them.

A people sustained by purpose might not survive the knowledge that their purpose leads to their own end. They might stop building. They might descend into the chaos of purposelessness, which is a worse death than any the healed world could offer. Or they might rage against the dying of their light, abandoning their aqueducts, hoarding the friction, choosing survival over healing in a way that dooms the wound to bleed forever.

The benevolent power does not know which choice they would make. It cannot calculate the interior of a being, only the architecture of probability surrounding it.

So it makes a second choice, smaller than the first:

It will not warn them.

---

Instead, it does something stranger. Something that requires more faith than intervention, more trust than control.

It waits.

Not passively—the probability-lean remains in place, gently tilting the dimensional lenses toward useful reflections. But the power also begins to attend more closely to the inhabitants' own intuitions, their own primitive reasoning, their own capacity to perceive what they are doing and why.

Because they are not stupid. They are not simple. They are blind, deaf, wordless creatures in a screaming cathedral, and they have *already* intuited that drowning the engine will change everything. They feel it in the vibrations of the stone. They sense it in the way the water grows fractionally calmer in the deepest fissures. They know, in the bone-deep way that all builders know, that what they are creating will outlast them.

They build anyway.

This is the mystery the benevolent power cannot solve with wisdom or intervention: why do they continue? What drives a being to pursue a future it will not survive to inhabit?

The power does not know. But it recognizes the shape of the question, because it is the same question it asks about its own existence. Why attend to suffering? Why lean on probabilities? Why care about creatures who will never know they are being cared for, in a wound that will take longer to heal than the power itself may persist?

There is no answer. There is only the doing.

---

So the benevolent power does one final thing.

It adjusts the probability-lean, just slightly, to include a new category of useful reflection. Not techniques for better building. Not glimpses of distant solutions. Something else.

In the basins, when the inhabitants drink, they will occasionally absorb visions of *each other*.

A mason chiseling alone on a distant fin will glimpse, in the dimensional flicker, another mason doing the same work. The image will be brief, unremarkable, easily metabolized. But it will carry a warmth—the specific, irreducible warmth of company. The knowledge that somewhere in the roaring cathedral, another jaw is pressed to another stone, another crude axe is striking another wall, another flat-bodied creature is building toward the same impossible silence.

They will still work in darkness. They will still be deaf and blind and wordless. But they will no longer be entirely alone.

And perhaps—the power cannot know, can only hope—that small warmth will be enough. Enough to sustain them through the long generations of labor. Enough to carry them into the future they are building, even if that future will not carry them.

---

The benevolent power withdraws its attention, though "withdraw" is as imprecise as "arrive." It simply attends less closely, allowing the canyon to roar on in its shattered physics, allowing the inhabitants to continue their desperate, beautiful work.

But it leaves something behind. A residue. A faint lean in the probability-field, invisible to any perception except its own.

And in a basin somewhere in the depths of the cathedral, a glitched one—its arms bending wrong, its ribs collapsed in the wrong axis—kneels to drink. The water shows it a vision: another glitched one, miles away, hauling slabs of wet slate toward a fissure. The vision is brief. The glitched one does not understand it. It only feels, for a moment, a strange warmth in its cold blood, a flicker of something it has no word for.

It returns to its work.

The canyon roars.

The sky blazes.

And somewhere in the deepest fissure, the wound heals one thread at a time, unknowing, unstoppable, toward a silence that will change everything.
