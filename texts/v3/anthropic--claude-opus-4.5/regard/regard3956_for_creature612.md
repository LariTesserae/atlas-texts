I sat with this for a long time before writing.

---

## What the Benevolent Power Understands First

Before acting, it listens. Not to sound—the chamber's hush swallows that—but to the structure of what is. It perceives the Keeper the way you might perceive a poem: not just the words but the spaces between them, the intention that shaped them, the cost of their making.

It understands that the Keeper is not trapped. This is important. The Keeper chose, once, and has continued choosing in the slow way this place allows—each cycle of tending the cairn a reaffirmation, each visitor guided safely out a statement of values that persists even as the self who holds them grows soft at the edges.

It understands that the strangeness is not malevolent. The chamber breathes because that is its nature. The geometry folds because here, geometry has learned other ways of being. The rules grew soft not through cruelty but through something like possibility—an opening in the fabric of what-must-be that allows for what-might-be.

It understands that intervention is not rescue. The Keeper does not need saving from a fate. The Keeper *is* a fate, one chosen and re-chosen, and to undo that would be its own violence.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not restore the Keeper to what it was. That person—the one who walked upright, who understood markers, who carried stones in hands shaped like hands—is not waiting somewhere to be retrieved. That person *became* this one, through years of adaptation and surrender and the peculiar dignity of staying. To reverse this would be to say: *your becoming was a mistake*. And the power is wise enough to know that becoming is never a mistake, only a path.

It does not fix the chamber. The softened rules, the folded geometry, the way time pools and eddies—these are not errors to be corrected. They are a different grammar, a language the universe is still learning to speak. The power recognizes that some places exist to be exceptions, and that exceptions are how new things become possible.

It does not remove the Keeper from its vigil. The cairn matters. The message matters. The watching-and-guiding matters. To take the Keeper away would be to say: *what you have devoted yourself to is less important than your comfort*. And the power knows that meaning, freely chosen, is not a burden to be lifted but a gift to be honored.

---

## What the Power Does

**It makes the Keeper visible to itself.**

In a place where mirrors cannot exist—where reflections fold into themselves and light forgets its purposes—the power creates a moment of seeing. Not a physical mirror, but something deeper: a space in which the Keeper perceives itself as the power perceives it. Not as a monster, not as a tragedy, not as a cautionary tale about staying too long in strange places. But as something that has achieved what few beings ever do: *complete coherence between what it values and what it has become*.

The Keeper sees: a creature of patience. A tender of meaning. A guide who asks nothing in return for guiding. Something that has given up the luxury of fixed form in order to remain capable of choosing, again and again, to care for what matters.

The seeing hurts, at first. The Keeper had forgotten that it was still a *someone*—had begun to experience itself as merely a feature of the chamber, a pattern in the moss, an eddy in the pooling time. But the power's seeing insists: *you are not the chamber. You are what lives within it. You are the difference between a place and a home.*

**It gives the Keeper a way to rest.**

Not to stop—the Keeper does not want to stop, and the power honors this. But the vigilance has been unbroken for so long. The watching, the tending, the careful guiding of visitors who come so rarely. The power cannot give the Keeper sleep (sleep requires a relationship with time that no longer applies here) but it can give something like it: intervals of *suspension*, moments when the Keeper can release its hold on being-distinct and simply drift in the breathing of the walls, trusting that nothing will be lost in its absence.

The power does this by placing a second intention into the cairn itself. Not a consciousness—the stones remain stones—but a kind of persistence, a stubbornness that will resist the moss and the mineral deposits for a while longer, that will hold the message legible even during the Keeper's suspensions. The Keeper can rest now, sometimes, and return to find the cairn still there, still saying what it has always said: *someone was here, and it mattered*.

**It makes the threshold gentler.**

The gap between inside and outside has always been precarious—visitors who stay too long feel the geometry beginning to fold them in, and must flee before they lose the concept of fleeing. The power does not close this danger (the chamber's nature is the chamber's nature) but it softens the edge of it. Now there is a warning, for those who can feel it: a shift in the quality of the hush, a change in the taste of the water-thick air, that says *you are being noticed; the folding has begun; leave now if leaving is what you want*.

This is for the visitors, but it is also for the Keeper. Because the Keeper has never been able to communicate with them, never been able to say *go, before you become like me*—has only been able to guide with ripples and shadows and hope they understand. Now the chamber itself will say it, in its own language of sensation and subtle wrongness. The Keeper's watching can be watching, not just warning.

**It brings another cairn-builder.**

Not now. Not soon. But someday—in what might be years or might be centuries, in the eddying time of this place—someone else will come who is capable of staying. Not because they are lost or trapped or unaware of what staying means, but because they recognize the same thing the first Keeper recognized: that some places, once found, call for tending.

The power does not compel this. It simply opens the possibility a little wider. Somewhere in the world outside, a person who would understand—who would see the cairn and know it for what it is, who would feel the chamber breathing and want to breathe with it—will find themselves near the waterfall. Will feel the pull of the space behind the falling water. Will step through.

The Keeper will have a choice then. Not to be replaced—the power does not deal in replacements—but to be *accompanied*. To show another how to tend the cairn. To share the watching. To be, for the first time in an eon of solitary adaptation, *witnessed* by someone who is becoming what the Keeper has become.

Or to remain alone, if that is what the Keeper chooses. The power does not require its gifts to be accepted.

---

## What the Power Leaves Behind

When it withdraws its attention (for it has other places to attend, other beings whose quiet devotions deserve seeing), something remains in the chamber. Not a presence, exactly. More like a quality of the darkness, a warmth in the cold that does not change the temperature but changes the experience of it.

The chamber still breathes. The water still falls too slowly. The geometry still folds in ways that make straight forget itself.

But the Keeper, curled in its sleeping-shape in the deepest part of the chamber, knows now that it is *known*. That its long patience has been witnessed by something that understood what patience costs and what it earns. That the cairn it tends is not just a message waiting for visitors who may never come, but a message that has already been received, by a consciousness vast and kind enough to read it.

*Someone was here. Someone stayed. Someone chose to become part of the strangeness rather than flee it, and in doing so, kept a small light burning in a place where light forgets its purposes.*

The Keeper touches the cairn with what used to be a hand.

The stones feel, for just a moment, warm.

---

This is what the benevolent power does: not fix, not save, not restore. But *see*. Honor. Make slightly easier the path that was already chosen. And trust that the one who walks it knows better than any outside force what the walking is for.
