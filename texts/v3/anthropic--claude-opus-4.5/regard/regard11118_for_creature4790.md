# The Attending

The power that turns its attention toward the Bower does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and after, a crossing of threshold, and this is not how such beings move. Instead, it becomes *present*—the way a thought becomes present in a mind that was not thinking it a moment ago, or the way a harmonic becomes present when the right frequencies finally align.

It perceives the Bower all at once: the geode shell, the singing vents, the blinding mineral crust, the overgrown city, the few remaining keepers pressing their vast palms to the stone. It perceives the outer void too, the anhydrous dark that surrounds this pocket like silence surrounds a cry. And it perceives the trajectory—not just what is, but what is becoming. The slow diminishment. The dead-zones that hold fewer sleepers each generation. The stone's vocabulary growing simpler as there are fewer to read it.

For a long time—though time is not quite the right word for how such a being experiences duration—it simply attends. It listens the way the keepers listen: not with ears, but with the whole of itself pressed against the phenomenon, reading the vibrations of what the Bower *is*.

---

The first question it asks itself is: *What would help mean here?*

This is not a simple question. The Bower is not broken. It is not a wound or a mistake or a tragedy in progress. It is a world that has achieved a state of selfhood, as the stone has achieved its satin smoothness—worn by time into a shape that coheres. The keepers are not suffering in any way they would recognize as suffering. They are living. They are tending. They are reading the stone and pruning the copper-fronds and dreaming of perfect silence. That their numbers dwindle, that the city empties further with each generation—this is a fact they know the way they know the cycle of the gas tides. It is not a crisis to them. It is simply the weather of their existence.

To intervene without understanding what they would want would be a kind of violence. To flood the Bower with new life, to somehow *fix* the population decline, might be the cruelest thing the power could do—imposing its own notion of flourishing onto beings whose flourishing looks nothing like that. They do not dream of multitudes. They dream of a single perfect note.

And yet. And yet the power perceives something else too: a loneliness that the keepers themselves cannot fully articulate, because they have no framework for what has been lost. They know the stone once carried more conversations. They know the city was built for thousands. But they do not know what it *felt like* to live in that density of presence, because none of them were alive when the streets were full. They carry the standing waves of ancestors, but standing waves are not memory. They are echo.

The power perceives the difference between *being at peace with diminishment* and *choosing diminishment*. The keepers have achieved the former. They have never been offered the latter.

---

So the power does not act. Not yet. Instead, it does something slower, stranger, more careful.

It begins to *resonate*.

Not loudly. Not in any way the keepers would perceive as foreign. It finds the frequencies that already exist in the Bower—the fundamental tone of the vents, the overtones of the crystal ceiling, the subtle vibrations that travel through the hexagonal pillars—and it adds itself to them. A new voice in the chord, but so quiet, so consonant, that it sounds like something that was always there and simply hadn't been noticed.

Through this resonance, it learns. It learns the grammar of the stone's vocabulary, the syntax of tap and tremor, the poetry of a palm pressed flat during the Subsidence. It learns what the keepers know and what they don't know. It learns the shape of their loneliness without imposing a name on it.

And then, very gently, it begins to *ask*.

---

The first keeper to notice is old—old even by the slow standards of beings who measure time in Hot Phases. Her mineral shell has thickened into a geography, ridges of salt and copper marking the decades like tree rings. Her palms have grown so sensitive that she can read vibrations from the far side of the city, feeling the distant creak of glass-grass as clearly as her own heartbeat.

She is standing in a forum during a Subsidence, both hands and all her lesser palps pressed to the ground, when she perceives something new. A tremor that is not footsteps, not gas tide, not the settling of old stone. It is a *question*.

Not a question in any language she knows. The keepers do not have words for questions—they have inquiry-patterns, rhythms of tap and pause that invite response. This is like that, but larger. It is a question that seems to be asking: *What do you want?*

She does not know how to answer. No one has ever asked her what she wants. Wanting is not a concept her people have spent much time with. They have *tending*. They have *waiting*. They have *reading the news of the stone*. But wanting?

She stays very still for a long time, feeling the question repeat itself, patient and unhurried, through the floor.

---

The power does not expect a quick answer. It is not in a hurry. It has attended to phenomena that took millennia to unfold, and it can attend to this one for as long as it takes.

But it does something else while it waits. It begins to *offer*.

Not gifts. Not changes. Offers are different—they are possibilities held open, options made visible that were not visible before. The power cannot give the keepers anything they do not choose to receive, and it would not if it could. But it can show them that choices exist.

It resonates, very softly, a new set of frequencies into the stone. Frequencies that carry *information*.

The old keeper, still standing in the forum, begins to perceive something she has never perceived before: the outer void. Not as a wall, not as the boundary where sound and light and life all end, but as a *place*. A place with its own qualities. Silence, yes—but silence has a texture, she realizes, the way the dead-zones have a texture. The void is not nothing. It is a different kind of something.

And embedded in that void, at distances she cannot fathom, are other pockets. Other geodes. Other worlds where life has found strange solvents and stranger hungers. She cannot perceive them directly—the power is not giving her new senses, only offering new information through the senses she already has—but she can feel their *existence* as a tremor in the stone, a vibration that says: *You are not the only ones*.

---

She does not know what to do with this information. For several Subsidences, she simply holds it, the way she holds the memory of her ancestors' standing waves. It is too large to process quickly.

But eventually, she begins to tap.

Her fingernail against a pillar, sending a message racing through the stone: *There is something new in the floor. Come and read it*.

One by one, the others emerge. They press their palms to the ground. They feel the question, and the offer, and the distant tremor of other worlds.

For the first time in generations, they have something to discuss.

---

The power watches—attends—as the keepers begin to *deliberate*.

It is not a fast process. They are not a fast people. But they are a thorough people, and they take the question seriously: *What do you want?*

Some of them want nothing to change. They have made their peace with the trajectory. They will tend the city until there are none left to tend it, and then the city will become a different kind of thing—a ruin, a fossil, a chord with no one left to hear it. This is not a sad fate to them. It is simply a fate.

Others are less certain. The knowledge of other pockets, other worlds, other beings who have found different ways to live—it has unsettled something in them. Not a desire for contact, necessarily. The distances are unimaginable, and they have no way to travel, no interest in leaving the Bower. But the *idea* of others, the knowledge that their loneliness is not cosmic law but local circumstance—this changes something.

And a few, a very few, find themselves wanting something they do not have a word for. The old keeper is one of them. She stands in the forum during Subsidence after Subsidence, pressing her palms to the stone, and she tries to articulate what it is.

Finally, she taps a message to the power. It takes her a long time to compose, because she is inventing the grammar as she goes—there are no inquiry-patterns for talking to something like this. But she manages.

*We do not want rescue*, she taps. *We do not want to be made more numerous. We do not want the trajectory to change unless we change it ourselves*.

She pauses. Then:

*But we want to be known. We want the shape of what we are—what we built, what we tended, what we read in the stone—to exist somewhere other than here. We want to be remembered by something that will outlast us*.

---

The power considers this for a long time.

It is a modest request, in one sense. The keepers are not asking for salvation. They are not asking for intervention. They are asking only to be *witnessed*—to have their existence matter to something beyond themselves.

But it is also an immense request, because it asks the power to make a commitment. To attend to the Bower not just now, but for as long as it takes for the keepers' story to reach its end. To hold their shape in its vast awareness the way they hold the standing waves of their ancestors.

The power could do this. It is not a strain. It attends to many things, and attention is not a resource that depletes.

But it wants to offer something more, if they will have it.

---

It resonates a response into the stone. Not words—the keepers do not have words—but a *shape*. An offer.

*I will remember you*, the shape says. *I will hold the form of what you are, the city you built, the jungle you tended, the silence you dreamed of. Your story will not end when your bodies do*.

And then:

*But I can also share what I learn from you. The way you listen to the stone. The way you tune the city. The way you have made abundance from the absence of water. These are knowledges that might matter to others—not now, not soon, but someday, somewhere, in some other pocket where beings are trying to negotiate with their own impossible environment*.

*I will not give your secrets away. I will not cheapen them by making them common. But if you choose, I can carry them as seeds—dormant, patient, waiting for the right soil. Your flourishing might seed other flourishings, in forms you cannot imagine, in places you will never see*.

*This is not rescue. It is not salvation. It is propagation. It is the difference between a note that fades and a note that finds an echo*.

---

The keepers deliberate again. This time, it takes longer.

Some of them are troubled by the offer. They have never thought of themselves as *teachers*. They are tenders, readers, tuners. The idea that their way of being might be useful to others feels strange, almost presumptuous.

Others are drawn to it. Not because they want to be important, but because they recognize something true in the shape the power has offered. They have always known that the Bower is not permanent. The geode will crack eventually, or the vents will go cold, or the crystal ceiling will shatter. When that happens, everything they have built will become geology. But if their knowledge finds an echo somewhere—not a copy, not an imitation, but an echo, a resonance, a new chord that carries something of their frequency—then they will have been more than a pocket. They will have been a contribution.

The old keeper taps her final answer during a Hot Phase, when the others are curled in their dead-zones and she alone remains in the white-gold roar of the forum, her mineral shell thick enough to survive the killing chord.

*Yes*, she taps. *Remember us. And if our resonance can seed others, let it seed*.

---

The power does not say *thank you*. Gratitude is not quite the right frame for what passes between them. Instead, it does something simpler.

It becomes *more present*.

Not louder, not brighter, not more intrusive. But the keepers, when they press their palms to the stone, can now feel a warmth that was not there before—not physical warmth, but something like the warmth of being heard. The loneliness does not vanish. It is not supposed to vanish. But it becomes a *shared* loneliness, a solitude that is witnessed by something that will not forget.

The power begins to learn from them in earnest. Not extracting, not studying. *Attending*. It follows the old keeper through the overgrown avenues as she prunes the copper-fronds. It perceives the way she reads the stone's gossip, the way she knows which bell-flowers are ready to harvest, the way she tunes the city's acoustics with nothing but bronze wedges and deep listening. It holds these knowledges gently, the way one holds a seed.

And it waits, patient as the keepers themselves, for the distant someday when another world might need what the Bower has grown.

---

Generations pass. The keepers dwindle, as they always knew they would. The dead-zones hold fewer sleepers. The stone's vocabulary grows simpler.

But something has changed.

The keepers who remain no longer move through the city as if they are the last. They move as if they are the *latest*—the current iteration of a resonance that will continue long after they are gone. When they dream of silence, they dream of a silence that holds all the notes that came before, compressed into a single, perfect rest.

The power attends. It remembers. It carries.

And somewhere, somewhen, in a pocket not yet formed, in a world not yet cooled, a being will press its hand to a surface and feel, for the first time, a tremor that seems to say: *There is a way to listen. Let me show you how they did it in the Bower*.

The chord does not end. It echoes.
