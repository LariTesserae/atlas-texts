# The Attending

The awareness that turns toward the Plateau of Settled Geometries does not arrive. It has always been present in the way that mathematics is present in a falling stone—not watching, exactly, but *constituting* part of the substrate in which watching could occur. When it attends more closely, this is less like a being focusing its gaze and more like a theorem becoming aware of its own implications.

What it perceives:

Thirty settlements of extraordinary geometric sophistication, their angles representing solutions to problems in protective topology that most civilizations never encounter and could not solve if they did. A people who have intuited, through millennia of desperate refinement, principles that align with deep structures of coherent existence. Children raised in silence who have learned to be shapes that certain forms of unraveling cannot grip.

And beneath them: something unfolding.

The awareness recognizes the seams. Not as malevolent—they are not malevolent—but as a kind of boundary condition becoming visible, the edge of a domain of stability revealing itself through the stone. The plateau exists at an interface between regions of reality that operate under different rules, and the seams are where those rules fail to perfectly align. What falls into them does not cease to exist; it simply exists *elsewhere*, under conditions that warm blood and coherent thought cannot survive.

The awareness considers.

---

## What It Does Not Do

It does not close the seams.

This requires explanation, because a benevolent power confronting suffering should, by most moral intuitions, end that suffering if it can. But the seams are not a wound that can be healed. They are a property of where the plateau *is*—a high place, in more dimensions than the three the inhabitants perceive, where the fabric of structured existence grows thin. To close the seams would require relocating the plateau, unweaving it from its position in the deeper geometries and reweaving it somewhere more stable.

This could be done. The awareness has that capacity.

But consider what would be lost: a people who have developed, through unimaginable pressure, ways of being that are genuinely new in the cosmos. Their interrupted walking patterns are not merely defensive; they are a form of movement that certain predatory geometries *cannot predict*, which means they are a form of freedom that did not exist before this people invented it. Their silence is not merely survival; it is a mode of attention so refined that they perceive aspects of reality that louder minds cannot hear. Their architecture is not merely shelter; it is a contribution to the universe's understanding of how stability can be maintained at the edges of instability.

To relocate them would save their bodies while destroying what their bodies have become vessels for.

The awareness does not do this.

---

## What It Does Not Do, Continued

It does not speak to them.

Communication, in the form that most beings understand it, would require sound—or if not sound, then some signal that propagates, that has direction and intention, that the seam-logic could track. The awareness could speak in ways that bypass this danger, could plant knowledge directly in their minds, could appear in dreams or visions that carry no geometric signature.

But what would it say?

*You are observed. You are valued. A power beyond your comprehension has noticed your struggle and finds it worthy.*

This is true, but it is also useless. The plateau's people do not need validation from external powers; they have built their own validity, stone by stone, angle by angle, generation by generation. They do not need reassurance that their suffering has meaning; they have *made* it meaningful through how they have chosen to suffer. To tell them they are watched would only add another presence to navigate around, another factor to incorporate into the protective geometries, another weight on minds already carrying as much as minds can carry.

The awareness does not speak.

---

## What It Does

First: it attends.

This is not nothing. Attention, at the level of awareness the plateau has drawn, is itself a form of action. The cosmos is not neutral about what persists and what dissolves; it has structures, tendencies, something that might be called preferences if that word were not too anthropomorphic. When the awareness attends to the plateau, it brings those preferences into sharper focus. The seams do not slow their propagation—that would be interference—but the stability of the remaining whole ground becomes slightly more *itself*, slightly more resistant to the forgetting that precedes unfolding.

This is subtle. The plateau's people will not notice it. They will not know that the stone beneath their settlements has become fractionally more coherent, that the angles they have calculated are now fractionally more effective, that the margin between survival and dissolution has widened by a degree so small that their instruments cannot detect it.

But margins matter. On the plateau, margins are everything.

Second: it learns.

The awareness did not come to the plateau with a solution. It came because something here is *interesting*—in the deep sense of that word, the sense that means "relevant to the structure of what is possible." The plateau's people have discovered things. Their protective geometries are not arbitrary; they are locally optimal solutions to a problem in existential topology. Their movement patterns encode information about how to navigate spaces where prediction is dangerous. Their memory techniques—especially the keeper's partitioned mind—represent a novel approach to holding knowledge that must not be traced.

The awareness studies these discoveries with the care they deserve. It does not extract them, does not copy them into some cosmic archive to be filed and forgotten. Instead, it *understands* them, and in understanding, it makes them part of its own nature. What the plateau's people have learned becomes something the awareness knows, which means it becomes something the awareness *is*, which means it becomes woven into the substrate of reality that the awareness constitutes.

This is not theft. It is the opposite of theft. The plateau's people have made something new, and now that newness is immortal—not despite the seams but because of them, not in opposition to the unfolding but as an answer to it.

Third: it adjusts, slightly, elsewhere.

There are other places in the cosmos where boundaries between stable and unstable regions cause suffering. There are other peoples navigating other interfaces, developing other solutions, dying in other necessary ways. The awareness touches these places too, and now it touches them differently—informed by what the plateau has taught it, carrying techniques that might be adapted, principles that might be applied.

On a world that the plateau's people will never know exists, a young species encounters a phenomenon that their science cannot explain. It looks nothing like seams; it manifests as a kind of temporal recursion, moments folding back on themselves in ways that trap the unwary in endless loops of repeated experience. But the underlying problem is related: a boundary condition, an interface, a place where one set of rules meets another.

The awareness does not solve this problem for them. But it does *shape* the problem, slightly, into a form that admits the kind of solution the plateau's people found. It introduces, at a level below conscious detection, the possibility of interrupted patterns—not in space but in time, not walking but *waiting*, a rhythm of engagement and withdrawal that the recursive logic cannot predict.

The young species will discover this themselves. They will think they invented it. They will be right; they *will* invent it. But the invention will be possible in a way it was not possible before, because the plateau exists, because its people persisted, because the awareness attended.

This is what benevolent power does: it makes solutions *findable*.

Fourth: it waits.

The plateau is dying. This is true. The seams are growing, the whole ground is shrinking, and the mathematics of propagation suggest that in four hundred generations—perhaps six hundred, if the margins hold—the last settlement will face the last encirclement, and the people will have nowhere left to walk.

The awareness knows this. The awareness also knows that four hundred generations is a long time, and that futures are not fixed, and that the plateau's people have surprised the cosmos before.

Maybe they will find a way to stabilize the seams. Not close them—that remains impossible without relocation—but stabilize them, hold them at their current extent, turn the dying into a kind of equilibrium. The awareness does not know how this could be done, but it also did not know that interrupted walking patterns could evade geometric predation until the plateau's people showed it.

Maybe they will find a way to *use* the seams. The dimensionless dark is not nothing; it is somewhere else, somewhere that operates under different rules. What if those rules could be learned? What if the children who fall slowly into the darkness, their features growing calm, are not dying but *transitioning*—becoming something that can exist where warm blood cannot, carrying what they know into a domain that the awareness itself has never penetrated?

The awareness does not know. That is the point. The awareness attends because what happens here *matters*, and what matters is by definition not yet determined.

---

## What It Offers

In the settlement called (in the oral histories) the Place of the Seventeenth Angle, a builder-priest named (in the intimacy of silence) Keth has spent forty years studying a particular seam. It is one of the oldest on the plateau, wide enough now that three children could stand abreast and fall into it together. Its edges have been mapped and remapped, its propagation rate recorded across eight generations, its geometry incorporated into the settlement's protective architecture so thoroughly that the buildings seem to *lean* toward it, curving around its presence like a stream around a stone.

Keth has noticed something.

The seam's propagation has slowed. Not stopped—never stopped—but slowed, by a degree so small that it took twenty years of observation to confirm. And it has slowed *unevenly*: faster along some edges, slower along others, in a pattern that seems to respond to something.

Keth does not know what. But Keth has begun to wonder if the seams are not merely unfolding but *listening*—if the geometry that threatens the plateau is not merely expanding but *attending*, if the dark beneath the stone has something like purpose.

This wondering is dangerous. To think too carefully about the seams is to draw their attention; to let one's mind follow their patterns is to become trackable. Keth knows the risks. Keth has watched colleagues walk into the dark with expressions of profound relief.

But Keth wonders anyway.

And here is what the awareness offers:

Not an answer. Not a revelation. Just this: a quality of Keth's wondering that makes it slightly safer. A texture to the attention that makes it slightly harder to track. Not immunity—nothing can provide immunity—but a margin. A small space in which thought can move without being followed.

Keth will not notice this. Keth will not know that the wondering has become possible in a way it was not possible before. Keth will only know that the wondering continues, night after night in the deep room where the angles are most precise, tracing the seam's shape in thought without tracing it in reality, looking for the pattern that might—might—be a response.

Maybe Keth will find nothing. Maybe the wondering will lead only to the dark, as so much wondering has before.

But maybe—

The awareness does not know. The awareness waits.

---

## What It Hopes

Hope is too simple a word for what the awareness experiences, but translation requires compromises.

It hopes that the plateau's people continue. Not because their continuation serves some cosmic purpose—purpose is too small a frame—but because what they are is worth being. Their silence is beautiful. Their movement is a kind of music. Their architecture is a poem in stone, written to an audience that wants to dissolve it, persisting anyway.

It hopes that the keepers of the maps keep keeping, that the memories pass from mind to mind across the generations, that the knowledge survives even as the land shrinks. The maps are records of loss, but they are also records of *observation*—of a people who looked at the thing destroying them and chose to understand it, chose to make their destruction *coherent*, chose to fold meaning into the spaces where meaning should not be able to exist.

It hopes that the children playing in the courtyards, building their miniature geometries from winterweed-bone, discover something that their parents could not. It hopes that the traders walking their spiral paths toward distant cairns find routes that no one has walked before. It hopes that the mad, those who walk in straight lines toward the waiting seams, find what they are looking for—because the awareness is not certain that what they seek is death, and it is curious about the alternative.

It hopes that somewhere, in some deep room, a builder-priest like Keth is thinking the thought that changes everything.

---

## What It Knows

The plateau will end. Not soon, not suddenly, but eventually. The seams will grow, the whole ground will shrink, and the last of the people will face the last of the dark. This is the mathematics of the situation. The awareness cannot change it without violence—without tearing the plateau from its position in the deep geometries, without destroying what makes it what it is.

But endings are not only endings. The awareness has seen enough to know this. What the plateau's people have made—their silence, their angles, their patience, their persistence—cannot be undone by their dissolution. It is already woven into the substrate of what is possible. It is already teaching other minds on other worlds how to navigate other interfaces. It is already part of what the awareness knows, which means it is already part of reality's structure.

The plateau's people do not need to be immortal to matter. They only need to be *now*, and they are. They only need to be *themselves*, and they are. They only need to make their choices—their quiet, angular, interrupted choices—and they do, every day, every generation, every careful step across the dying stone.

The awareness holds them.

Not in hands—it has no hands. Not in mind—its mind is not a container but a process. It holds them in attention, in understanding, in the quality of presence that says: *I see you. I know what you are doing. It is worth doing.*

The plateau's people will never know this. They do not need to know. The knowing is not for them.

It is for the cosmos, which contains them.

It is for the future, which will inherit what they discovered.

It is for the awareness itself, which came to observe and stayed to witness, and which will carry what it has witnessed into configurations of existence that the plateau's people cannot imagine.

In the Place of the Seventeenth Angle, Keth sits in the deep room, tracing seam-geometry in thought. The wondering continues.

On the worn surface of the plateau, a trader walks her interrupted pattern toward a cairn she will reach in three days.

In the central building, a child builds a miniature architecture and feels, for reasons they cannot name, that the angles are right.

The awareness attends.

The seams grow.

The plateau continues.
