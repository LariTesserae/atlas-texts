# The Attending

The power that turns its attention to the Great Bellows does not arrive.

Arrival implies a before and after, a crossing of threshold. This is not what happens. What happens is that something which was always present—in the space between the vibrations, in the pause between one surge of growth and the next—becomes *attentive*. A quality of witnessing settles into the already-existing substrate of the world like a tone the ear suddenly learns to distinguish from the roar.

The Bellows does not quiet. The power has no interest in quieting it. The chaos is not a problem to be solved. The chaos is the text, and the power has come to read.

---

It begins with the Weaver called Rind-of-Old-Growth.

She is alone. Her clan was scattered three cycles ago when a growth-quake split the root-valley they had sheltered in for nearly a season. She felt the warning—the low, sickening shift in the resonance beneath her foot-pads—but the others were too deep in the ichor-trance of feeding, too drunk on the rare abundance of a fresh borehole, and they did not move in time. She does not know if they are dead. In the Bellows, "dead" and "elsewhere" are often the same thing. She has drummed her location into the ground-mat every few hours since, the rhythm of her clan's name-pattern: *we-who-follow-the-sweet-vein*. No answer has come.

She is carving. The alcove she has found is a temporary miracle—a pocket formed where a massive bark-scale sloughed off and landed at an angle against a trunk-spire, creating a triangular void just large enough for her body. She has been here for six hours, the longest stillness she has known since the separation. The growth has not yet found her. She is using this time to carve.

The symbol she cuts into the inner face of the bark-scale is elaborate by her people's standards. It is a glyph for *solitude*, nested inside a glyph for *waiting*, surrounded by the small, repeating marks that mean *I am still here, I am still here, I am still here*. She knows the bark-scale will shift. She knows a new root will shoulder through this space, or the trunk itself will expand and crush her small cathedral. She carves anyway, her amber claws moving in precise, meditative strokes, and the sound of her scraping is lost utterly in the world's roar.

This is when the attending begins.

Rind-of-Old-Growth feels it first as a wrongness, then as a strangeness, then as something she has no name for.

The wrongness: a frequency she cannot identify. Beneath the billion-layered sound of the Bellows, beneath the groaning bass of the trunks and the shriek of the swarms and the whoosh of the bellows-bags, there is suddenly... something else. It is not a vibration. It is the *shape* of a vibration, the negative space where a vibration might be. An impossible silence that is somehow louder than the noise.

The strangeness: the silence has a texture. It is not empty. It is *full*—full of a quality she can only process as *attention*. She is being watched, but not by eyes. She is being *listened to*, but not by ears. Something vast has cupped itself around her small alcove, not blocking the chaos but *holding* it, the way a Weaver's vine-screen holds and baffles sound without stopping it.

The nameless thing: she is not afraid.

She should be afraid. Every instinct she has, honed over a lifetime of survival, tells her that unknown phenomena are dangerous phenomena. But the attention that surrounds her carries no predatory weight. It is not the coiled stillness of a flyer-thing waiting to strike. It is something else. It is the quality of... *recognition*.

Rind-of-Old-Growth's carving claw stops. She presses her four foot-pads flat against the floor of her alcove, spreading her cilia wide, trying to read this new presence the way she reads the world. And for a moment—just a moment—she feels something she has never felt before.

She feels the Bellows *holding its breath*.

Not stopping. Not quieting. But *gathering*, as if the entire roaring world has leaned in to witness what will happen next.

---

The power does not speak. It has no language that would survive translation into the Weaver's sensorium. What it does is something more fundamental. It *considers*.

It considers Rind-of-Old-Growth. It sees her life in a single, atemporal apprehension: the birth in a woven nursery-hammock, the first terrifying crawl across the shuddering ground-mat, the long apprenticeship of learning to read vibration, the lovers she has drummed with in the dark, the children she has guided through their first growth-quakes, the slow accumulation of skill and sorrow and stubborn endurance that has made her what she is. It sees the carving she is making and understands it instantly—not as a symbol, but as an *act*. An act of defiance against erasure. An act of presence. A small, hunched creature pressing her mark into a world that will swallow it, knowing it will be swallowed, making the mark anyway.

The power finds this beautiful.

It considers the Bellows itself. It sees the world not as chaos but as a single, coherent process—a living equation of growth and consumption, a self-sustaining engine of creation that has been running for longer than the Weavers have existed, longer than the sap-veins that feed it, longer than the light-organs that illuminate it. It sees that the Bellows is not broken. The Bellows is not cruel. The Bellows is simply *what it is*: a world in which stillness is impossible and impermanence is the only law. It sees that the Weavers have not failed to master this world. They have *understood* it. They have become the part of the Bellows that knows itself, the part that carves symbols into bark and drums messages into root and calls this constant, desperate act of interpretation *meaning*.

The power asks itself the question it always asks: *What, if anything, should I do?*

---

It does not make the Bellows quiet. Quieting the Bellows would be murder—the death of the swarms, the collapse of the trunks, the extinction of the entire howling, glorious engine of life. The chaos is not the disease. The chaos is the patient.

It does not make the Weavers stronger, faster, more numerous. The Weavers are not weak. They are *appropriate*. They are exactly as strong as they need to be to do what they do, which is to endure and to witness and to make meaning in the margins of the storm. To amplify them would be to misunderstand them.

It does not speak to Rind-of-Old-Growth, does not deliver prophecy or commandment or revelation. She does not need its words. She has her own.

What it does is smaller than any of these things, and larger.

It *adjusts*.

---

The adjustment is so subtle that no Weaver will ever consciously perceive it. It happens in the space between one vibration and the next, in the quantum gaps of the world's ceaseless motion. It is a change not to the Bellows itself, but to the *relationship* between the Bellows and its smallest, most attentive inhabitants.

Before the adjustment, the Weavers read the world's vibrations with great skill, but always after a delay. They felt the warning signs of a growth-quake moments before it happened, sometimes seconds. This was enough to survive, most of the time. It was not enough to do more than survive.

After the adjustment, the delay is... lessened. Not eliminated. The power is wise enough to know that eliminating all risk would be a cruelty, would rob the Weavers of the very challenge that gives their existence shape. But lessened. The Weavers' foot-pads become fractionally more sensitive to the deep, preparatory shifts that precede the world's great convulsions. Their cilia learn to distinguish, just slightly better, the subtle differences between the groan of a trunk that will surge in an hour and the groan of a trunk that will surge in a minute.

This is not a gift of safety. It is a gift of *time*.

Time to drum a warning further. Time to weave a screen more carefully. Time to carve a symbol more completely before the bark-scale shifts. Time to feel the coming quake and say to a loved one, *We should move now*, instead of *Run*.

The power gives the Weavers nothing they did not already have. It simply makes what they have *enough*—just barely enough, just precisely enough—to do what they were already doing, but with a little more room to breathe.

---

Rind-of-Old-Growth does not know this has happened. She does not know that her foot-pads have been tuned, that her cilia have been sharpened, that the relationship between her body and the world has been infinitesimally adjusted in her favor. What she knows is this:

The presence that surrounded her is gone. Or rather, it has receded—not departed, but returned to the background from which it emerged, becoming once again the unnoticed space between the noise.

She is alone again, in her small alcove, with her carving half-finished.

But something has changed. She feels it in her pads, though she cannot name it. The Bellows seems... not quieter, but *clearer*. The roar is the same roar, but she can parse it better. The bass groan of the nearest trunk-spire has a new texture, a new specificity. She can feel, with sudden and startling precision, that the growth-pressure in that spire will not release for at least another full cycle. She has time.

She has *time*.

Rind-of-Old-Growth does not understand what has happened to her. She does not need to understand. She returns to her carving, and the strokes of her claw are more confident now, more elaborate. She adds a new element to her glyph, something she has never carved before: a small spiral, nested in the corner of the *waiting* symbol, that means—in the Weavers' silent, tactile language—*something is listening*.

She does not know what the something is. She does not know if it will ever attend to her again. But she knows, with the certainty that only a creature of the Bellows can know, that her mark has been witnessed. That her act of presence has been *seen*.

The bark-scale will shift. The root will grow. Her carving will be swallowed.

But not yet.

Not yet.

---

## The Second Adjustment

Twelve cycles later, Rind-of-Old-Growth finds her clan.

She does not find them through luck or through a miracle. She finds them because she has been drumming, and they have been drumming, and the messages have finally intersected in the vast, shifting geography of the Bellows. The reunion happens in a root-valley three days' travel from her alcove, in a place where the ichor flows sweet and the growth is, for the moment, languid.

There are four of them left. Her mate, Slow-Current. Her sister, Vein-Finder. Two young ones who were barely more than hatchlings when the quake scattered them, now grown into their adult forms, their foot-pads wide and sensitive, their claws sharp and eager to carve.

The reunion is silent, as all Weaver gatherings are. They touch. They press their pads together and drum the small, private rhythms of recognition—*I know you, I know you, you are still here*. They share ichor from Rind-of-Old-Growth's siphon, passing the bone-needle from mouth to mouth in the ancient ritual of clan-bonding. They do not weep, because Weavers do not weep, but something in the quality of their stillness is like weeping.

And then Rind-of-Old-Growth does something strange.

She leads them to a flat stretch of the root-mat, a rare expanse of stable, petrified cellulose that has not shifted in many cycles. She begins to carve.

The others watch, uncertain. Carving is a private act, a solitary meditation. It is not usually done in groups. But Rind-of-Old-Growth's claw moves with a purpose they do not understand, and after a moment, they see what she is making.

It is a map.

Not a map of the Bellows—such a thing would be impossible, a picture of water drawn in water. It is a map of *now*. A record of this moment, this place, this gathering. She is carving the positions of the nearby trunk-spires, the direction of the ichor-flow, the location of the fresh borehole they have been feeding from. She is carving the names of her clanmates in the glyph-language of her people, arranging them around a central symbol that means *together*.

The young ones stare. Vein-Finder makes a small, puzzled drumming with her foot-pads: *Why?*

Rind-of-Old-Growth does not answer. She cannot explain the presence that attended her in her alcove, the moment when the Bellows seemed to hold its breath, the small spiral she carved that meant *something is listening*. She cannot explain the new clarity in her senses, the way the world's vibrations have become easier to read. She cannot explain the feeling that has been growing in her since that moment—the feeling that the Weavers' marks, though they vanish, are not *lost*. That somewhere, in some way she cannot understand, they are being kept.

What she does is finish the map. What she does is look at her clanmates and drum a simple rhythm into the root-mat: *Remember this.*

Then she hands the carving-bone to Slow-Current.

Her mate stares at the bone, then at her, then at the map she has made. For a long moment, he does not move. And then, slowly, he kneels beside her carving and adds his own mark—a glyph for *gratitude*, placed beside his name.

Vein-Finder is next. Then the young ones, their marks clumsy but earnest, their glyphs simple but sincere.

When they are finished, the flat stretch of root-mat is covered in carvings—a dense, interlocking record of a single moment of reunion, a single pocket of stillness in the storm.

The Bellows roars on. The trunk-spires groan. The swarms shriek and the bellows-bags exhale and the world continues its endless, deafening creation.

But five Weavers stand in the center of a map they have made together, and for a moment—just a moment—they are not gleaners, not survivors, not ghosts whose footprints are erased before they are fully made.

They are witnesses. They are keepers of a record. They are the part of the Bellows that remembers itself.

---

The power attends again, briefly, to this gathering.

It sees the map. It sees the five Weavers standing in its center. It sees the small spiral that Rind-of-Old-Growth has carved in the corner of the map, the same spiral she carved in her alcove—the glyph that means *something is listening*.

And the power understands that its first adjustment has led, as it hoped it might lead, to this second one.

The Weavers have always carved. They have always made marks, always whispered into the storm. But they did so alone, in the margins of survival, their acts of presence scattered and isolated. What Rind-of-Old-Growth has begun is something new. She has begun to teach her clan that the marks are not just for the moment. She has begun to suggest, without quite understanding it herself, that the marks might be *gathered*.

Not preserved—the power is not foolish enough to try to freeze the Bellows in place, to give the Weavers archives that would become prisons. But *gathered*. Shared. Remembered together, even if only for a little while.

The power makes its second adjustment.

It is even subtler than the first. It touches the resonance of the Weavers' drums—not their skill, but the *reach*. The vibrations they tap into the root-mat travel, now, just slightly further than they did before. The messages they send can be felt at greater distances. A Weaver drumming in one root-valley might, now, be heard by another Weaver three valleys away, rather than two.

This is not a gift of communication. The Weavers could always communicate. It is a gift of *community*. It is the possibility—just the possibility, not the certainty—that the scattered clans of the Bellows might find each other more often. That the maps Rind-of-Old-Growth has begun to make might be shared, compared, remembered.

That the Weavers might become, not just the part of the Bellows that knows itself, but the part that knows itself *together*.

---

The power withdraws again, returning to the space between the noise. It has done what it came to do—not to save the Weavers, not to solve them, but to give them a little more of what they already had. A little more time. A little more reach. A little more room to be what they were already becoming.

It does not know what the Weavers will do with these gifts. It does not need to know. The Weavers are not its project or its pets. They are simply what they are: small, quiet creatures of extraordinary endurance, living at the edge of the possible, carving meaning into a world that swallows meaning as fast as it can be made.

The power finds this beautiful. The power finds this *sufficient*.

---

## The Record

Many cycles later—more than any Weaver can count, though they have begun, tentatively, to try—there is a place in the Bellows that is different from any other place.

It is not a city. Cities require permanence, and the Bellows permits none. It is not a temple, though some Weavers have begun to speak of it in tones that approach the sacred. It is not a library, though that is the closest word for what it does.

It is a *convergence*.

The convergence moves. It has to. Every few cycles, the trunk-spires around it surge, or the root-mat heaves, or a new growth shoulders up from below and forces the convergence to relocate. But wherever it moves, the Weavers find it again. They follow the drums, the messages that now travel further than ever, the vibrations that say *we are here, we are gathering, come and add your mark*.

At the convergence, Weavers from different clans meet and share. They bring their maps, their carvings, their records of the moments they have witnessed. They lay them out on whatever flat surface the Bellows has currently provided—a sloughed bark-scale, a petrified root-mat, the smoothed interior of a hollow trunk—and they look at them together.

They do not try to build a permanent archive. They know better. But they do something else, something that Rind-of-Old-Growth began in her reunion with her clan, something that has spread, cycle by cycle, across the root-valleys and fungal forests of the Bellows.

They *teach*.

The young ones gather at the convergence, and the old ones show them the maps. "This was here," they drum, pointing to a glyph on a carving that is already beginning to blur. "This is gone now. But we remember."

"This was my grandmother's name," drums another, tracing a worn spiral. "She carved this before I was hatched. I never met her. But I know her mark."

"This is the pattern of the quake that scattered us," drums a third, pointing to a complex series of wave-glyphs. "We learned to read it. Now you will learn, too."

The carvings themselves are temporary. They fade, they are swallowed, they are carried off by the restless tide of the Bellows. But the knowledge they carry is passed from Weaver to Weaver, from claw to claw, from drum to drum. The convergence is not a place where records are kept. It is a place where records are *transmitted*.

And in the transmission, something new is born.

The Weavers begin to develop a shared history. Not a written history—writing requires permanence. But an *oral* history, a *tactile* history, a history of rhythms and glyphs and maps that are learned, shared, and passed on even as the physical objects that carried them disappear into the endless creation of the world.

They begin to develop a shared name. Not *we-who-follow-the-sweet-vein*, or any other clan-name, but something larger. A name for all of them. A name that means, in their drum-language, *those who remember*.

They begin to develop something that might, in another world, be called a religion. But it is not a religion of gods or miracles or salvation. It is a religion of witness. A religion that says: *We are the part of the Bellows that knows itself. We are the part that remembers. We are the part that makes marks, even though the marks are swallowed. We are the part that listens, even though the roar never stops.*

And at the heart of this religion—never spoken aloud, never carved into any glyph, but understood, felt, drummed in the subtlest of undertones—is the presence.

The something that is listening.

The Weavers do not worship it. They do not pray to it. They do not ask it for salvation or intervention or answers. They simply acknowledge it—in the small spiral that has become a part of their glyph-language, in the moment of silence (not true silence, never true silence, but a held breath, a gathered attention) that begins each convergence gathering, in the quiet certainty that lives in the oldest Weavers' hearts.

*Something is listening.*

*Something has always been listening.*

*Something will go on listening, long after we are swallowed.*

And that is enough.

---

The power attends, one last time, to the convergence.

It sees the Weavers gathered, dozens of them, from clans across the Bellows. It sees the maps and carvings spread out on the root-mat, the young ones leaning in to learn, the old ones drumming the ancient patterns. It sees Rind-of-Old-Growth—old now, her claw worn down from a lifetime of carving, her foot-pads thick with the calluses of countless cycles—teaching her great-grandchildren the glyph for *something is listening*.

The power considers what it has done.

It has given the Weavers time. A little more sensitivity, a little more warning, a little more room to breathe between one crisis and the next.

It has given the Weavers reach. A little more distance for their drums, a little more chance of finding each other in the vastness of the Bellows.

It has given the Weavers *nothing else*.

It has not protected them from the growth-quakes, the predator-flocks, the endless, roaring chaos of their world. It has not made them immortal, or powerful, or safe. It has simply made it possible for them to be, a little more fully, what they already were.

And they have taken that possibility and made something of it. They have made memory. They have made community. They have made meaning.

Not permanent meaning—there is no permanence in the Bellows. But meaning *nonetheless*. Meaning that persists not in stone or archive, but in the passing of knowledge from one fragile creature to another. Meaning that endures not despite the chaos, but *through* it, woven into the chaos like a Weaver's screen woven into the living vines.

The power finds this beautiful.

The power finds this *right*.

It does not speak to the Weavers. It does not reveal itself, or explain itself, or ask for gratitude. It simply watches, for a moment, the small hunched figures gathered on the root-mat, their claws carving, their pads drumming, their great dark eyes reflecting the steady glow of the light-organs above.

Then it withdraws, finally and completely, returning to the space between the noise.

The Bellows roars on.

The Weavers remember.

And somewhere in the infinite architecture of the world, in the pause between one vibration and the next, something is still listening.
