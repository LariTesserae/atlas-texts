It did not arrive from the sky. The sky above the Weeping Escarpment was a closed system of frantic clouds and barbed-wire mist, and the Power was too polite to tear a hole in something already so badly torn. 

Instead, it arrived through the water. 

It began as a change in the registers of the subterranean trembling. For three thousand years, the sub-sonic frequency of the Escarpment had been a note of absolute planetary panic—a deep, bruised *C-sharp* that rattled the marrow of everything bolted to the cliffs. But on a Tuesday, by the reckoning of the Ninth Gallery’s chronometers, the note simply… relaxed. It did not stop. It shifted a quarter-tone, from panic to a profound, resonant exhalation. 

Then, the Echoes changed. 

The electrostatic diagrams in the mist, which had played out the same frantic, branching algorithms of a planet in shock for millennia, suddenly paused. The voltage smoothed. The lightning-ghosts stopped thrashing and began to weave together, forming a single, continuous ribbon of soft, golden luminescence that drifted downward through the perpetual drizzle. 

The Weeping Choir felt it instantly. Across the continent-scale wound, thousands of them lifted their smooth, bark-plated heads. The fungal colonies threaded through their skulls thrummed with a frequency they had never experienced: it was not a command, not a fracture-warning, not the agony of the stone. 

It was a question. *May I enter?*

On an outer maintenance scaffold of the Third Ladder, an ancient of the Choir stood draped in the cold weeping of the cliffs. He was old enough that his grapples were heavily calcified, his iron hum deep and thick. He had been preparing for his final Long Listening, ready to lock his joints and become an Anchor. 

He watched as the mist gathered on the platform opposite him. 

The Power did not take a human shape, nor did it mimic the elongated, insectile grace of the Choir. To do so would have been mockery. Instead, it condensed as a locus of pure, atmospheric gravity. It looked like a lens of clear, perfectly still air, about the size of a blast-furnace door, hovering just above the rusted grating. Where the roaring, chaotic water entered this space, it slowed, breaking into distinct, floating spheres of impossible crystalline clarity. Within that lens, there was no sound. The eternal roar of the falls was politely excluded.

The Power looked at the Escarpment. With senses that transcended the physical, it saw the full twenty-kilometer gash. It felt the raw, unhealed bedrock. It tasted the electrum, the ozone, the exhaust of the iron barnacle-cities. It felt the planet’s three-thousand-year scream vibrating against its vast, empathetic consciousness.

It had the strength to fix it. The Power knew this with absolute certainty. It could reach its metaphysical hands deep into the planet’s mantle, take hold of the tectonic plates, and pull them back together. It could fuse the basalt, weld the andesite, melt the crust into a seamless scar. It could make the bleeding stop. The planet would be whole. The pain would end.

But the Power was wise. 

It looked at the ancient member of the Choir standing on the scaffold. It looked at the architecture of his living-wood armor, at the tear ducts flowing with mineral condensation, at the delicate proboscis folded at his throat. It looked out into the viridian and gunmetal void, at the impossible bracket-trees, at the culture of iron and patience that had grown *because* of the wound.

To close the earth would be to crush the cities. To stop the water would be to starve the trees. To heal the planet according to an outside standard of "wholeness" would be to commit a genocide against the children of the hemorrhage. 

The Power understood that there is a difference between a wound that is dying, and a wound that has become a world. 

The ancient of the Choir tilted his head. The motion took seven seconds. His wet, lidless eyes locked onto the locus of clear air. Slowly, with geological inevitability, his proboscis uncoiled, tasting the ozone. 

The brass vox-mask clicked, the filter-grilles opening. The metallic polyphony of a dozen waterfalls sounded from his throat, carrying the tired, eternal greeting of his people:

**“Still bleeding. Still here. What else is there?”**

The Power answered. It did not speak in words, for words were too small. It spoke in voltage, passing its reply directly into the fungal lattice in the ancient’s mind. 

*I am here,* the Power resonated, a warmth that bloomed behind the Choir's eyes. *I see your vigil. I see the weight of the water. But you have held the ragged edges long enough. A scab cannot be expected to hold the world together forever.*

The Power drifted forward. It touched the weeping basalt wall of the cliff. 

It did not close the fissure. It did not stop the bleeding. Instead, it offered the one mercy this place had never known: **Acceptance.**

For three thousand years, the planet had been fighting the tear, trying to close it, flexing its tectonic muscles in a state of eternal, exhausting cramps, weeping in terror. The Power laid its vast, soothing presence over the planet's mantle and simply whispered context to the stone. 

*It is okay,* the Power told the world. *The wound is open, but you are not dying. Look at what has grown inside you. Look at the life that clings to your exposed heart. Let go.*

Deep beneath the plateaus, the planet finally stopped flexing around the injury. 

The effect was instantaneous, moving up the twenty kilometers of vertical relief at the speed of sound. The caustic, jagged edge of the mechanical roar softened. The water did not stop falling, but it stopped behaving like an evacuation. The droplets plumped, growing slightly warmer. The perpetual fog shifted from the freezing gray of a corpse to an iridescent, pearlescent mist. 

The ancient of the Choir staggered—a sudden, sharp movement that cracked the calcification on his joints. 

He felt it through his boots. The absolute tension of the rock had vanished. The cliff was no longer shivering. 

He touched his proboscis to a seam in the rock. The water he drew in was no longer the bitter, alkaline blood of a dying earth; it was rich, sweet, and thrumming with a calm, steady vitality. It was no longer a hemorrhage. It was a wellspring. 

Across the six major conurbations, millions of the Weeping Choir stopped their work. The turbines still spun, the counterweighted elevators still climbed, but the desperate, frantic necessity was gone. The electrostatic diagrams in the air changed from jagged anxiety spikes to slow, blooming fractals of unimaginable beauty—the thoughts of a planet waking up from a nightmare, looking around, and realizing it was safe. 

The pale, bracket-like conifers suddenly flushed with a faint, healthy luminescence. The conductive veins within them rang with a new chord—not iron, but silver. A note of joy.

The Power withdrew slightly from the rock, the lens of clear air beginning to dissolve back into the mist. It had given no commands, asked for no worship, erected no monuments. It had simply diagnosed a chronic pain, and granted the grace to let that pain become a pulse.

Before it faded entirely, the Power sent one last arc of voltage into the ancient's mind, a gentle reply to his eternal question.

*"Still bleeding,"* the Power agreed warmly. *"But no longer dying. Still here. But now, you may rest. What else is there?"*

The lens vanished.

The ancient of the Choir stood on the scaffold for a long time. The water running down his slate-gray face was, for the first time in his life, warm. He looked up at the infinite verticality of his home. It was no longer a wound needing to be cauterized. It was a canyon. It was a cradle. 

He closed his brass vox-mask, allowing silence to exist within himself as the waterfalls sang their new, untroubled song. He retracted his proboscis, turned away from the edge, and for the first time in three thousand years, a member of the Weeping Choir did not walk with the tension of the doomed. 

He walked like a caretaker in a very, very grand garden.
