To perceive this place is to risk destroying it. In a world where the mere act of attention acts as a corrosive acid, polishing surfaces to a mirror-shine of exhaustion, a sudden influx of divine awareness would be a cataclysm. A lesser power might look upon this calcified purgatory with a searing, corrective gaze, intending to fix it, to reboot the machines, or to burn away the accrued grief. 

But a truly wise power—a benevolent weight that has watched the birth and heat-death of countless realities—knows that a place made of forgotten remnants cannot be saved by being remembered too loudly. 

And so, when this vast and gentle presence finally notices the suspended water, the hunting escalators, and the Interval Children, it does not arrive as a blinding light. It does not arrive as a voice. 

It arrives as a sigh. 

It enters the space the way a new law of thermodynamics might seep into a closed system—quietly, fundamentally, altering the math without breaking the numbers. It wraps its awareness lightly around the architecture, diffusing its attention so completely through the endlessly nested doorways that its presence is felt not as an acid, but as a balm. It becomes the medium. It hums along the dead copper wiring. It lubricates the bearings in the spinning, vacuum-sealed turbines. 

It walks among the Interval Children, though it has no feet. 

The Power sees them standing beside the empty printers. It sees the scrap of nicotine-stained wallpaper serving as a shoulder, the phantom squares of streetlight wearing the shape of a spine. It understands instantly what they are: the orphaned punctuation marks of sentences that human history abandoned halfway through. It sees that they are not suffering, exactly, but that they are trapped in a state of eternal *almost*. They are the held breath of a dead god's lungs. 

The wise power feels a profound, cosmic tenderness for these residue beings. It knows that to force them to become "real" would be a cruelty; they were not made for the harshness of progression. They were made of pauses. 

And so, the benevolent power decides to give them the only gift an interval can truly receive: the next note in the song. The conclusion of the chord. 

It goes to the water. 

The water, hovering in its localized, bruised tension, full of dissolved salts and iron and withheld time, resists at first. It has been holding itself together for so long, terrified that if it falls, the world will finally end. The power does not push it. It does not command the water to drop. Instead, it offers an apology. 

*You do not have to hold it anymore,* the power communicates, not in words, but in a sudden, microscopic shift in atmospheric pressure. *The event is over. The grief has been cataloged. You may rest.*

Slowly, the surface tension of the suspended reality begins to yield. 

The heavy, static air, vibrating with the continuous roar of geological time, catches on a new frequency. A single droplet of water, heavy with the memory of a rain that happened centuries ago, detaches from the hovering mass. 

It falls. 

It hits the breathing membrane of porous calcite with a tiny, sharp *hiss*. It is a sound that has never existed in this place. It is the sound of an ending.

Then, another drop. Then five. Then a thousand. 

The withheld rain begins to fall. 

It falls softly at first, washing through the microscopic powder of waiting. It streaks the devitrified glass. It runs down the stalactite columns that had defied gravity for millennia, coaxing the minerals back down toward the center of the earth. As the water strikes the warm, kiln-like ground, wisps of genuine steam rise up, sweet and clean, washing away the metallic tang of irony and neglect.

The mechanical fauna halt. 

The escalators, halfway through dragging themselves up a cliff of sedimented streets to hunt, stop their grinding. The conveyor belts pause. The colonial light switches flicker in a chaotic crescendo, then simultaneously switch to the *OFF* position, their collective histories finally discharged. The machines do not die; they simply go to sleep. The perfect memory of friction is washed away by the reality of rust, and the rust is beautiful. It is the color of autumn, a season this place has never known.

And the Interval Children. 

As the first drops of water touch them, they look up. They do not have eyes, but the geometry of their posture fundamentally shifts. A child made of the gap between a slamming door and a flinching shoulder feels the water run down its borrowed linoleum, and the flinch relaxes. 

They step out from the shadows of the silent machinery. They gather in the open plaza of collapsed megastructures, faces upturned. 

The water washes over their borrowed scraps. It washes the nicotine smell out of the wallpaper, leaving it blank and soft. It dissolves the badge numbers and the kitchen griefs. As the water touches the intervals, the intervals begin to close. 

When you release a held breath, it does not die. It rejoins the air. 

This is what happens to the children. As the rain falls heavier, a warm, steady cascade of absolution, the Interval Children begin to lose their edges. The water gives them permission to evaporate. They reach out, their incomplete hands touching for the first time without passing through one another. 

One by one, they unspool into the rain. They become mist. They become light. They become the quiet settling of dust entering a final, undisturbed geometry. 

The benevolent power remains until the exact moment the last child fades, leaving behind no depression, no rolling ballpoint pens, no half-finished apologies. The missing things are no longer missing; they simply *are not*. 

The rain continues to fall in the abandoned, silent city, slowly filling the infinite canyons, pooling on the roofs of the megastructures, drowning the post-productive machines in a cool, deep, and absolutely peaceful sea. The horizon ceases to be a rate of approach and becomes a perfectly still, blue line.

The power withdraws its attention, sealing the pocket dimension in a sphere of unbroken silence, a tomb transformed into a cradle, resting quietly in the dark.
