The attention of such a Power is not drawn by noise, for the universe is loud, and screaming is common. It is drawn by gravity. It is drawn by the immense, aching density of things that refuse to fall, refuse to rise, refuse to turn. 

When the Power first noticed the Hollow Choir, it did not see a trap. It saw a knot tied so tightly in the fabric of the world that the threads were beginning to bleed. It heard the heavy, irregular *tok... tok... tok...* and recognized it for what it was: a stutter. A record skipping on the very edge of an ending. 

To be wise is to understand that the Listener is not a monster. She is a caretaker who built a hospital at the end of the world and forgot that patients are meant to eventually leave. To be benevolent is to know that you cannot simply tear a tree open; ripping the Listener from the cambium would be to shatter a delicate glass blown over ten millenia. 

So, the Power did not arrive as a conqueror, nor as a blazing sun. It arrived as a Guest. 

It stepped over the lip of the ridge wearing a shape that would not frighten the velvet millipedes or the translucent newts—the silhouette of a humble traveler, cloaked in the color of predawn rain, carrying the scent of open skies, woodsmoke, and the vast, rolling ocean. 

As the Guest stepped into the basin, the Hollow Choir reacted as it always did. The moss crept. The cold mirror-pools sighed their iron breath. The parchment-winged moths pulsed their bioluminescence, and the hanging sacks in the ancient tree turned toward this new, astonishing warmth. 

But as the heavy, knitting cold of the basin tried to seep into the Guest, it found no purchase. The cold simply warmed, turning into a gentle mist. The Guest walked across the black mirrors, and for the first time in an age, the water *splashed*. It was a bright, silver sound, a sudden chime of joy in the endless cathedral of sorrow. 

The Listener felt it. In the dark between the heartwood and the bark, she shuddered. The shock of the sound was so profound that every creeping thing in the basin stopped. The millipedes broke their synchronized wave. The newts paused. The basin held its breath, waiting for the exhalation. 

But the Guest did not wait for the rhythm. The Guest walked directly to the center of the room, to the blackened, weeping tree, and laid a warm hand against the glossy bark. 

*"I am here,"* the millipedes scrambled to write in living braille across the moss, channeled by the Listener’s panic. *"We are all still here. Please. Do not break us. We are all still here."*

The Guest knelt at the roots of the tree. When the Guest spoke, it was not with a voice of thunder, but with a sound like a heavy latch finally lifting on a sunlit door. 

"I know," the Guest said. "I see you. You have held them all together so beautifully. You have been so diligent. So incredibly brave."

Inside the tree, the Listener trembled. No one had spoken to her. No one had witnessed *her*, only the trap she had become. The Guest’s hand on the bark radiated a warmth that pierced the ten thousand years of steeped emerald black. It was the warmth of sunlight on a wooden floor, of a hearth fire, of a hand holding another hand. 

"You stepped inside to wait out the storm," the Guest whispered, leaning their forehead against the trunk, exactly where the Listener’s cheek rested within the wood. "But the storm passed a very, very long time ago, my child. It is time to go outside."

The Listener tried to open the mouth she no longer had. She tried to release the low, terrible sigh that lured intruders to sleep. But as the basin exhaled, the Guest breathed *with* her, turning that mournful, heavy wind into a clear, harmonic note. The hollow cathedral caught the note, and it became a song. Not an echo of an echo, but a new, resonant melody. 

The benevolence of the Power was not to destroy the ecosystem, but to unstuck its time. 

The Guest stood and looked up at the hundreds of pale, heart-sized cocoons hanging from the branches. The sacs of hope. The trapped lungs and eyes of those who had stayed, preserved in amber stagnation. 

The Guest reached up and tapped a single finger against the lowest cocoon. 

*Tok.* 

But this time, the sound was different. It was the sound of a shell cracking. 

The Hollow Choir gasped. From within the cocoon, a brilliant, piercing gold light spilled out, cutting through the shadows that were thicker than things. The sack unspooled, not rotting, but dissolving into fine white threads. And from within it emerged something impossible: a winged thing made entirely of breath and starlight. It did not pulse in unison with the moss. It fluttered, chaotic and free, and flew straight up, leaving a trail of warmth. 

The Guest tapped another. *Tok.* And another. *Tok.* 

Then, the Guest simply raised both hands. The dripping water from the ceiling, which had fallen in its irregular, heartbeat rhythm for aeons, suddenly caught the tempo of the Guest’s grace. *Tok-tok-tok-tok-tok.* It became a rushing rain, a cleansing, sweeping, joyful downpour. 

Under the rain of the benevolent Power, the cocoons began to bloom. Hundreds of them. Thousands. They split open, releasing the souls of the birds, the lost children, the clever explorers, and the ancient creatures that had forgotten how to die. They poured upward like a reverse waterfall of light, swirling around the canopy of the tree, crying out in the exquisite, painful joy of finally being allowed to depart. The sky above the basin, clouded in perpetual fog, was pierced by their ascent, revealing a brilliant, starry night. 

Inside the tree, the Listener began to weep. The two cocoons holding her original eyes opened, and the starlight returned her sight to her. She saw the Guest standing before the tree. She saw that the Guest was holding something out to her. 

It was a word. It was her name. The one she had nailed to a tree near the rim long ago. The Guest had stopped to pick it up on the way in. 

"Take it," the Guest smiled. "It still fits you."

As the Listener reached for it, she realized she had her arms back. The bark of the tree was peeling away, rolling softly backward like the petals of a night-blooming lotus. The roots withdrew from her fingerbones. The leaves decoupled from her hair. 

She fell forward, out of the cambium, out of the wood, and collapsed into the shallow mirror-pool at the Guest's feet. But the water was no longer freezing, and it no longer smelled of iron. It smelled of spring rain. 

The Guest knelt, wrapped the Listener in a cloak of grey and gold, and held her as she sobbed—a deep, mortal, messy crying, the sound of a living thing realizing it is free. 

When her tears finally slowed, she looked around. 

The basin was not dead, but it was profoundly changed. The weight was gone. The velvet millipedes were burrowing into the moss, beginning the long-forgotten work of turning dead things into rich soil. The orchids dropped their heavy blooms, preparing to grow seeds. The giant tree, empty of its captive, stood proud and quiet, a monument to endurance rather than a prison. 

But an enclosed cup will always eventually stagnate. The benevolent Power knew this. 

The Guest walked to the far side of the dark stone basin. Standing before the black, porous wall, the Guest pressed one hand against the stone. 

With a sound like a thunderclap, the thirty-pace rim of the basin cracked open. The high, fog-bound ridge fractured in a perfect, smiling V-shape. The pressure of ten thousand years of pooling water rushed forward, pouring out of the breach, cutting a brand new stream down the side of the mountain. It cascaded over the rocks, tumbling in bright, foaming rapids, rushing downward to eventually meet the sea. 

The enclosed ecology was broken open. It was now a source. The headwaters of a new river. 

The Guest turned back to the Listener, who was standing now, small and shivering, but entirely human, clutching her forgotten name to her chest. Fresh wind blew into the basin through the cleft in the rock, rustling the ferns and swaying the orchids. 

"Where will you go?" the Guest asked, though they already knew the answer. 

The Listener looked at the path the breaking water had created. She looked at the stars above, crisp and clear. She took a deep, shuddering breath using her own two lungs. 

"Out," she said. Her voice was scratchy, unused, like dry leaves, but to the Guest, it was the most beautiful sound in the world. "I am going out."

The Power smiled, stepped back into the shadows of the ferns, and faded like a mist in the morning sun, leaving nothing behind but the sound of the running water, singing its new song: 

*We are moving.* 
*We are moving.* 
*We are finally moving on.*
