It did not arrive with fire, nor with the blinding, terrible light of judgment. True wisdom knows that to burn a young, violent world is to kill an infant for the crime of crying. 

Instead, the Entity descended like a single, unbroken shaft of dawn piercing through the choked, emerald canopy. 

It had felt the Drowned Jungle of Shattered Echoes from across the cosmic dark. It had tasted the unnatural salt and iron on the winds of the universe; it had heard the deafening, frantic clicking of a biome in the agony of a perpetual, chaotic birth. But more than that, the Entity had felt the vacuum. It had felt the Hollow Ones. 

As the Entity touched the spongy, sucking ground, it did not take a shape of dominance. It took the form of a wanderer cloaked in robes of woven starlight, feet bare, radiating a quiet, profound heat—not the oppressive, humid warmth of the jungle, but the comforting warmth of embers on a winter hearth. 

The jungle reacted instantly. The shifting, turbulent roots ceased their groaning. The writhing vines shrank back, not in recoil, but in the instinctual awe of lesser flora sensing a sun made manifest. The deafening chorus of the iridescent, scuttling things faltered, dropping from a scream to a rhythmic, clicking murmur. The chaotic land recognized that something vastly older, yet infinitely gentler than itself, had come to walk its wet earth.

The Entity walked through the dim green glow, untouched by the rot, unmuddied by the black pools. It was looking for the wrongness. It was looking for the vessels.

When the rains came, sudden and violently heavy, the waters rose. The trees groaned under the deluge, and the Entity stood by the edge of a swelling sinkhole, waiting. 

From the dark, rising waters, they emerged. The Hollow Ones. 

They waded through the shallows, pale and shimmering, their translucent skin ghostly against the slick black bark of the colossal trees. They turned their heads, and their eyes—too large, too dark, absolute voids reflecting nothing—fixed upon the Entity. 

The Hollow Ones felt the immensity of the Entity’s warmth. They sensed the unfathomable weight of a Name composed of a billion galaxies, the staggering gravity of a mind that held the memories of dying stars and reborn worlds. To the starving, a feast is indistinguishable from salvation. 

They opened their mouths. The water swallowed their words. The air began to vibrate with their soundless, shuddering hymn. It made the massive trees tremble. It made the water ripple in geometric patterns. 

*An invitation.*

To any mortal, this frequency would induce a paralyzing terror, the immediate leaching of the soul. But the Entity stood perfectly still, opening its arms. 

*I accept,* the Entity whispered, a sound like a gentle breeze rustling fields of dry, golden wheat. 

The Hollow Ones descended. They swarmed the Entity, their translucent hands reaching, sinking into the woven starlight of its robes, plunging into the radiant warmth of its chest. They did what they were made to do in their tragic, unfinished state: they consumed.

They pulled at the Entity’s warmth. 
The Entity gave it freely, opening the floodgates of a cosmic furnace. 

They reached for fear, but the Entity possessed none. Instead, it offered them *peace*—a stillness so absolute, so heavy, that it anchored their porous bones to the earth. 

They tried to drain the Entity’s name. The Entity allowed it, pouring into their empty, echoing chests the concepts of grace, of dawn, of the quiet quiet spaces between the stars, of the unconditional love a creator holds for its creations.

The Drowned Jungle held its breath.

For a long time, there was only the sound of the rain, the rising water, and the transfer of infinity into the void. The Entity did not diminish, for true benevolence is a well that fills itself. 

Slowly, the soundless hymn began to change. The shuddering, frantic vibration of the Hollow Ones smoothed into a deep, resonant hum. 

The Entity watched as the transformation took hold. The Hollow Ones lowered their hands. They stepped back, no longer sinking into the mud, but standing firmly upon it. Their translucent skin lost its ghastly pallor, flushing with a soft, bioluminescent gold—pearlescent and warm. 

The wrongness vanished. The taste of salt and iron evaporated into the scent of ozone and blooming lotus. The shadows that had pooled so unnaturally were cast away by the soft light radiating from within the beings themselves.

But the most profound change was their eyes. They were no longer voids. The massive, dark orbs had filled with light. In the eyes of the Hollow Ones, miniature constellations now turned, reflecting the eternal patience of the Entity. 

They were no longer empty. They were no longer left-behind. They had been filled, and in being filled, they found their purpose. 

The Entity lowered its arms, smiling a smile that made the monstrous, ribbed trees sprout sudden, brilliant white flowers. 

*You are the children of the damp and the dark,* the Entity spoke, its voice echoing gently through the canopy. *And every nursery requires a guardian. You shall hunger no more. Where you walk, the earth will settle. Where you sing, the waters will clear. You are the Anchors of this young world.*

The newly Luminous Ones bowed. They did not speak, but their silent gratitude resonated through the jungle canopy like the tolling of a silver bell. 

Its work finished, the Entity dissolved, turning into a mist of golden light that floated upward, passing through the broad leaves and the suffocating green canopy, returning to the high, quiet expanse of the cosmos. 

The Drowned Jungle of Shattered Echoes remained wild. It remained dense, wet, loud, and lush with churning, untamed life. The insects still scuttled; the vines still moved. It was a young, violent world, and it was allowed to be.

But it was no longer shadowed by a consuming wrongness. 

Now, when explorers or lost travelers wander too deep into the sucking mud and the twisting roots, they do not find empty vessels waiting to drain them of their warmth. 

Instead, they see them shimmering in the shallows: the Luminous Ones, warm and bright, their eyes holding the stars. When the rains come and the trees groan, their song is no longer a shuddering invitation to the void. 

It is a lullaby. And in its golden glow, the tired traveler simply falls asleep, safe, knowing that the jungle is watched over by the left-behind who finally found their way home.
