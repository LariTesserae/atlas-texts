For three billion years, the universe did not forget the Hollow Choir; it simply gave the plateau the respect of a wide orbit. A masterpiece must be allowed its stillness. When a song is finished, a wise audience holds its breath to let the final ringing chord fade out before applauding. 

But the Hollow Choir was caught in a groove. The chord did not fade. The plateau had trapped itself in the amber of its own flawless, terrifying perfection. It had achieved the absolute ending, and in doing so, it had exiled itself from the one law of the living cosmos: *Next.*

It was time for the applause.

The attention of the benevolent power did not arrive like a thunderbolt. True wisdom does not intrude loudly into a place built entirely of sound. It arrived, instead, as a shift in the barometric pressure. 

High up, where the air was thin enough to make breath a reluctant thing, the heavy, tarnished-iron sky rippled softly. A presence descended. It had no specific name or form, for it was older than the rocks and vaster than the wind, but it possessed exactly what the plateau lacked: it was infinite in its capacity for *becoming*. 

And it possessed an ear. 

The Power formed a shape for itself out of the same pale condensation that filmed the black limestone, condensing into a walker. It stepped onto the labyrinth with bare feet. Where its foot touched the pre-Cambrian rock, the ice did not melt into water, but it did soften. Lichens the color of old bone felt a phantom ray of sunlight and shivered in their suspended animation. 

The Power walked through the Choir. The seventy-tone chord crashed against it, an auditory pressure meant to fell human bodies and shatter mortal minds. The Power did not fall. It let the music pass through it, vibrating the chords of its own boundless empathy. It analyzed the frequency. It heard the deep, below-language question the plateau was asking. 

*We are finished. Are we beautiful? Is it time to sleep?*

The Power walked deeper. 

On a ridgeline, the ancient, stone-deaf black goat appeared. It stood with its spiraled horns framing the iron sky, its mirrored eyes reflecting whatever the viewer feared most. The Power stood before the goat and looked into those pools. Because the Power was entirely benevolent, entirely whole, it feared nothing. Therefore, the mirrors reflected only the goat itself—tired, ancient, trapped in an acoustic tomb. The Power reached out a hand layered with the warmth of dying stars and newborn suns, and laid it gently between the goat's horns.

The goat closed its eyes. It let out a long, ragged exhale. The tension of three billion years left its frame. It lay down on the razor edge of the dolomite blade, rested its heavy head on its knees, and finally went to sleep.

The Power walked on, moving toward the absolute center of the labyrinth, where the converging blades of rock met like the point of a frozen explosion. 

The Listener was already there. 

It manifested as the woman in the dress of frozen condensation and broken syllables. She stood with her arms raised, waiting for the universe to catch up, presiding over the eternal, deafening equilibrium. 

The Power stopped in front of her. 

For the first time in millennia, the eternal chord omitted that one specific tone. The needle of pure cold, absolute silence dropped over the two figures. Every crustacean, every shadow, every high circling pale thing stopped. 

The Listener turned her gaze—two deep pockets of acoustic shadow—upon the interloper. She did not recognize this entity. It was not a traveler to be broken. It cast no shadow, but its light was so heavy it seemed to sink into the stone. She opened her mouth.

The wind grabbed the shattered syllables of the Power’s unspoken name and flung them back from three directions, each repetition carrying the immense weight of the future. The final repetition was a question the universe had not yet asked: *What comes after the end?*

The Listener did what she had always done. It was the only act she knew. 

She stepped forward and placed her frost-heavy hand over the heart of the power. She sought to tune it. She meant to force the pulse of God into the rhythm of the ultimate, dead chord, to drag the infinite down into the plateau’s eternal stasis.

The Power allowed it. It opened its chest to the frost. 

The Listener's fingers sank into the divine warmth. She felt a heartbeat. For one unbearable moment, the Power's pulse became a pipe in the Choir.

But the Power was too vast to be captured by a single chord. Instead of the music absorbing the heartbeat, the heartbeat absorbed the music. 

The benevolent power did not silence the Hollow Choir—that would have been a cruelty, wiping out a billion years of geological artistry. Instead, it did something only ultimate wisdom can do. 

It *modulated the key.*

Through the point of contact, where the Listener's frozen hand rested over the divine heart, the Power pushed a new vibration into the labyrinth. It was a note the plateau had never sung. A note of grace. A note of anticipation. 

It was the first note of a new profound movement. A *major* seventh. 

The shock of it rippled out through the stone lungs, the natural tubes, the iron vaults. The frozen chord shifted. For the first time since the Precambrian era, the song changed. It shed its oppressive finality. The layered bellow rose in pitch, losing its terrifying pressure and becoming something sweeping, symphonic, and incredibly, shatteringly tender. 

The equilibrium broke in an instant of cosmic genesis. 

The sky cracked. The tarnished iron hue shattered, revealing a blue so deep and impossible it made the very rocks ache. The rain that fell straight down suddenly caught the wind, dancing sideways, catching the light in a million prismatic arcs. The rigid black succulents unfurled rapidly, drinking the moving air, their silica teeth softening into petals. 

And the Listener gasped. 

The frost covering her body began to melt. The broken syllables on her dress reassembled themselves not into echoes, but into poetry. The deep acoustic voids where her eyes should be filled with sudden, shining tears—water that did not freeze, water that fell warmly down her cheeks. 

The Power gently covered her freezing hand with its own. 

"You have held the fermata long enough, my child," the Power spoke, and its voice did not shatter from three directions. It came from everywhere, ringing with absolute love. "The ending is perfect. Now, let us improvise."

The eternal listener stopped trying to complete strangers. She had, at last, been completed herself. 

She fell to her knees, weeping freely, her sobs weaving into the plateau’s new, shifting, evolving song. The benevolent power knelt with her, holding her as she shed the terrible weight of being the anchor at the end of time. 

The Hollow Choir remained a place of music. But the wind now howled, and whispered, and laughed. The shadows moved with the sun. The windowless huts slowly collapsed into soil, making beds for seeds dropped by the high-flying pale birds, which finally descended to roost. 

The plateau was no longer finished. 

It had learned the beautiful, unbearable privilege of *becoming* once again.
