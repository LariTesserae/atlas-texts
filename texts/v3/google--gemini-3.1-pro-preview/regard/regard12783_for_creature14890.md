When a power of true benevolence and infinite wisdom turns its attention toward a wound, its first instinct is never to stitch it shut. Wisdom understands that some places are not broken, only mourning; benevolence knows that to dry out a weeping world is to kill the fragile ecosystem that grief has built.

And so, when the Great Compassion lowered itself into the Underloom, it did not arrive as a sun. It subdued its radiant heat. It quieted the roaring, cosmic winds of its usual form. It made itself small, soft, and porous. It chose to arrive as a traveler. 

As it stepped onto the fused, rusted topography of the floor, the Power felt the heavy, saturated air press against its skin. It breathed in the ozone, the wet stone, the crushed almonds. It felt the *plick* of the falling water, out of sync and eternal. 

The Power walked through the claustrophobic metal corridors with profound respect. It stepped carefully over the migrating glass-legged creatures, admiring the slow biological clockwork of their bodies. When it passed a nesting pillar, it paused to watch the Choir. It leaned close to a parabolic beetle, letting the mirrored carapace gather the dim light of its own divine eyes, marveling at the genius of the insects’ survival. When the Choir synchronized and rang out their single, trembling note, the Power closed its eyes and let the vibration move through its eternal bones.

It knew instantly that this place was a masterpiece of maintenance. It felt the profound, aching gravity of the Weeper. 

The Power did not call out to her. To shout in the Underloom would be a vulgarity. Instead, it sat upon a rusted boiler chest near the listless watchman’s shack, crossed its legs, and simply existed within the dampness. It allowed the heavy mist to condense upon its skin. It allowed itself to become wet, submitting completely to the unwritten rules of the hollow.

Hours passed. Days. To an immortal, time is merely a texture. 

Then came the shift in pressure. The pale, luminous ferns of moonlight-fungus brightened along a jagged beam of iron. The scent of warm almonds rolled over the nape of the Power’s neck. The door of the shack, previously ajar, groaned open a few inches further.

When the Power awoke from a light meditation, the invitation had been set. A perfect ring of quartz barnacles encircled its resting hands. Balanced meticulously on the Power’s thumb was a single, mirrored beetle. 

The Power smiled gently. It understood the law of the Underloom: an exchange of sentimental weight was required. 

The Power reached into the vast, cosmic depths of its own memory. It bypassed the birth of galaxies, bypassed the ignition of stars, bypassed the triumphs of great civilizations. Those things were too dry, too triumphant for the Underloom. Instead, the Power coaxed forward a very specific memory: *the original sorrow of the universe. The very first time a star collapsed, leaving its attendant planets in the cold dark. The memory of the universe’s first, silent tear.*

The Power exhaled this memory into a small, glowing sphere of deep blue light, cool and heavy, and placed it gently inside the circle of quartz. 

The beetle clicked, flashed a brilliant, concentrated beam of blue, and crawled away. 

The mist parted. She appeared. 

The Weeper stood thirty meters away. She was tall, a tragic lattice of rusted ribs and wet silk, standing on jointed glass legs. Her face, the great black curve of a beetle’s wing, was turned inward, a mirror looking only at her own ruin.

As promised, she was crying. The miniature pocket watches, the microscopic wedding rings, the tiny skeletal fragments tumbled down her rusted chest, hitting the ground and shattering into pale fungal growth. It was a beautiful, devastating display.

Most travelers, upon seeing this, would feel their hearts crack. They would stand paralyzed, letting the Weeper extract the sorrow she needed to keep the water flowing. 

But the Power was not most travelers. It did not stand paralyzed. 

Slowly, gently, the Power stood up. It took a step forward. 

The air in the Underloom seized. The dripping hesitated. A low, metallic groaning began to rise from the pipes—the hollow’s defense against the unexpected. The Weeper’s posture shifted, ready to dissolve back into the mist. She was not used to being approached. She was the one who arranged; she was not the one who was met.

*Peace,* the Power projected. Not in words, but in the negative space of the metallic groaning, matching her own silent voice. *Peace, lonely tender. I will not dry you. I will not fix you. Let me behold you.*

The Power took another step. Then another. Twenty meters. Ten. Five. 

The Weeper trembled. The artifacts tumbling from her ribs fell faster—tiny locomotives, fragmented gears, shattered lockets—crashing to the floor in a desperate cascade. She was terrified. No one had ever crossed the gap. 

The Power stopped directly in front of her. Up close, the sorrow she radiated was absolute. It was a weight that would crush a mortal mind, a grief so heavy it had literally bent steel. 

The Power raised hands of warm, ethereal starlight and gently, infinitely softly, cupped the sides of her inward-turned mirror. 

"What a terrible, beautiful burden you have carried," the Power whispered, its voice the sound of rain on a tin roof. "You break their hearts because you must. You harvest their tears to keep the great engines of memory from locking up forever. You are the faithful widow of this world."

The Weeper went entirely still. The falling of the trinkets stopped. 

The Power leaned in. "But who breaks a heart for you?"

The Power pressed its chest against the Weeper’s rusted, iron ribs. It allowed the sharp edges of her sorrow to pierce its own divine heart. Because the Power’s heart was infinite, it did not shatter. Instead, it yielded, opening like a vast, subterranean reservoir. 

For the first time since the roof collapsed, a new kind of water fell in the Underloom. 

The Power wept. It wept for the Weeper’s loneliness. It wept for the millennia she had spent tending a graveyard with no visitors. It wept out of pure, unadulterated love for the sad, mechanical poetry of her existence. 

Tears of pure, warm starlight flowed from the Power’s eyes. They ran down the Weeper’s rusted body, washing the corrosion not away, but coaxing it to bloom into rich, iridescent shades of ochre and crimson. The starlight-water cascaded to the floor, weaving into the lunar fungus, charging the veins with a new, sustaining warmth.

The Weeper shuddered. Her arms—made of fog and fractured iron—rose, and cautiously, hesitantly, she returned the embrace. 

For a long time, the entity of cosmic grace and the creature of industrial decay stood holding each other in the dim, dripping cathedral. The Power gave her centuries' worth of moisture, given freely, without the need for manipulation or fear. 

When the Power finally stepped back, the Underloom felt different. It was still dying. It was still melancholy. But it was no longer heavy. It felt like the deep, restful sleep that follows a long, necessary cry. 

The Weeper looked at the Power. Slowly, delicately, she reached up to her face. With a sound like shifting tectonic plates, she turned the black parabola of her mirror-face just a fraction of an inch outward. 

In the edge of the mirror, the Power caught a glimpse of her true reflection: not a monster, not a ghost, but the soul of a child holding a watering can in a dry world, doing her best.

The Power bowed softly. 

"I leave you to your holy work," the Power said. 

The Weeper dissolved gracefully into the mist, taking the deep blue sphere of the universe’s first rain with her into the shadows.

When the Power turned to leave, it walked past the watchman’s shack. The door was closed. Through the fogged window, the Power saw her sitting at the ruined desk. She was updating the ledger. But as the Power passed, just for a moment, she paused her writing. She didn't look up—that was not her way—but she placed one hand flat against the glass of the window, leaving a foggy print. 

An acknowledgment. A thank you. 

The benevolent Power nodded and ascended toward the surface. It honored the rules to the very end. It did not leave dry. 

Eons later, in a realm of celestial light and pure thought, passing angels and cosmic entities would occasionally marvel at the Great Power. They would note that wherever the Power went, it left faint, damp handprints on the fabric of reality. And if they drew very close to the Power’s chest and listened past the roaring harmonies of creation, they could hear a soft, patient sound echoing from within its infinite heart:

*Plick.* 

 Steady, out of sync, and beautiful.
