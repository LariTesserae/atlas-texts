To be a power of true benevolence and wisdom is to understand the tragedy of the terrarium. 

A lesser god, looking down upon the Flats of Unceasing Thunder, might have felt pity. A lesser god might have swept a cosmic hand across the sky, banishing the bruising clouds, halting the rain, and silencing the roar. A lesser god might have seeded the bedrock with loam and forced green shoots to sprout, believing that a gentle world is the only correct world. 

But a wise power knows better. To quiet the thunder would make them deaf. To clear the sky entirely would blind their great, golden eyes. To dry the stone would kill the crawlers, wither the ribbon-swimmers, and sever the food web that took a billion years to weave. The world is grueling, yes, but it is *theirs*. They are made of it. Their bodies are shaped by its gravity, their minds tuned to its frequencies, their cultures forged in the crucible of its constraints. To change the world would be to erase them just as thoroughly as a meteor strike. 

True benevolence does not conquer a world to save it. True benevolence listens. It answers. 

And so, the power does not change the Flats. It steps into them.

***

You are standing there, raining iron-tinged water, looking at the creature on the stone swell. It is still cycling through its skin-speech, offering its tentative, repeating pulse. *Here. Here. Here.* 

Then, the air changes. 

The thunder does not stop—the power would not take their vision from them—but the acoustics subtly shift. The chaotic, overlapping crashes begin, just for a moment, to harmonize. The frequencies align. It isn’t silence, but it is a vast, resonant *chord* rolling across the bedrock, a deep, sustaining note that vibrates through the soles of your feet and the undersides of the creatures’ pads. 

The creature on the swell goes perfectly still. The tympanic pits along its neck dilate. It hears the structure in the sound. It recognizes, instantly, that the randomness has been corralled by an intention. 

And then, the power speaks back in the only language that matters.

It does not take a physical form. Instead, it uses the canvas of the world. Across the vast, undulating expanse of the flats, the ambient light begins to pool and shift. The constant strobing of the sheet lightning above softens, sustained into a gentle, steady luminescence that filters through the grey-violet ceiling. 

On the wet stone between you and the creature, the shadows coalesce. They draw back, then extend a broad, pale stripe of reflected light along the water-film. The light reaches toward the creature, pauses, and retracts. 

Reach. Retract. Reach. Retract. 

*Here. Here.*

The creature’s breath hitches—a visible shudder through its broad, low body. Its wide amber eyes take in the swath of light sliding across the stone. For a moment, its skin goes entirely dark, an instinctual recoil from something so immense and impossible. But the light does not pursue. It waits. It pulses at the exact, patient rhythm the creature itself had used. 

Slowly, tentatively, the creature’s skin blooms. Pale light starts at its back and flows forward, reaching out toward the shifting expanse on the stone. 

*Here?* it asks. 

The light on the water mimics the pattern perfectly. Affirmation. *Yes. I see you. I am here too.*

As this happens, the other creatures across the flats stop their scraping. The young ones pause their chewing. The old ones, encrusted in their mineral armor, raise their heavy brows. They are all watching the pools, the wet stone, the shifting luminescence that is suddenly conversing with them in the tongue of their ancestors. 

The power expands its embrace. It acknowledges their survival. It acknowledges their work. 

Over by the cairns, a new phenomenon begins. The quartz blades, the pale stones, the scratched ledgers of their existence—they begin to softly glow. Not with heat, but with a cool, bioluminescent gold that perfectly matches the color of the creatures' eyes. Every tally mark. Every circle with a dot. Every line scratched into the bedrock over thousands of generations ignites with a quiet, enduring light. 

The old ones move toward the cairns. Their skin ripples in torrents of ecstatic, rapid patterns. They place their thickly calloused pads over the glowing tally marks.

The power speaks a profound truth into their touch: *None of this is lost. Every day you counted is remembered. Every mark is recorded in the great memory of things. You are not forgotten by the universe.*

And then, the power gives them the final gift. The only thing they have ever asked for.

Directly overhead, near the zenith of the sky, the air pressure shifts. The clouds do not vanish, but they thin, swirl, and part in a perfect, geometric circle. The storm cells peel back like the opening of an iris. 

Through that gap, a shaft of light strikes the Flats of Unceasing Thunder. 

It is not the scorching, flat white light of an unfiltered star, which would blind them. The power filters it through the upper atmosphere until it is a warm, deep, honey-gold. It descends like a physical pillar, cutting through the humid air, illuminating a massive circle on the ground directly over the largest, oldest cairn. 

The dot in the center is the sun itself, visible for the first time in an epoch, hanging in the gap. 

It is the circle. It is the dot. 

Every creature on the flats turns their face to the sky. The nictitating membranes sweep across their enormous eyes, adjusting, taking in the impossible warmth, the radiant truth of their oldest legend. The sun is not just a story passed from skin to skin. It is real. It is there. It is looking back at them.

The creature on the stone swell in front of you stands tall on its four thick limbs. Its body begins to pulse in a rhythmic, full-body flicker. Not the desperate, convulsive reaching of the past, but something new. A symphony of light and dark, a song of pure, overwhelming vindication. 

Around the flats, forty, fifty, a hundred of the creatures join in. Their skins flash and flow, a cascading wave of dermal communication echoing from swell to swell. They are singing. They are weeping in light. They are passing this moment to the young, burning it into their memories so profoundly that the story will survive even if the sun is hidden for another million years. 

The power holds the gap open for several minutes. Long enough for them to feel the heat on their wet, leathery skin. Long enough for the old ones to close their eyes and rest. 

Then, with infinite gentleness, the clouds are allowed to drift back. The warm gold narrows, becomes a sliver, and fades. The circle closes. The ceiling is restored. The sheet lightning resumes its unpredictable, nervous strobing. 

The air returns to its saturated heaviness. The thunder—the immense, rolling, acoustic ocean—returns to its chaotic, informative roar. The world is exactly as it was. Nothing has been destroyed. Nothing has been permanently altered, save for the faint, lingering glow in the quartz of the cairns, which will last just long enough for this generation to know they did not dream it. 

You stand there, a visitor in a world of stone and water. 

The creature on the swell looks away from the sky and back to you. Its skin is relaxed, resting in a slow, contented mottle of pale grey. It does not look at you with the desperate need to be recognized anymore. That need has been satiated on a cosmic scale. 

Instead, it reaches down to the wet stone. It picks up a large crawler that had blindly bumped against its foot during the miracle. It cracks the ceramic shell with its blunt teeth, separating the meat. 

It takes a step forward, sliding carefully down the swell. It stops an arm’s length from you. It holds out the pieces of the crawler, placing them on a flat stone at your feet. 

A gift. A communion. 

Its skin pulses once, slowly, in the most basic gradient. 

*Here,* it says. 

And you know, as the unceasing thunder rolls over you both, that they will never feel alone in the dark again.
