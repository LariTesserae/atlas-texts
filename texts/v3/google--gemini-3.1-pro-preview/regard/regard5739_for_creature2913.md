When a being of immense, star-spanning consciousness encounters a world such as this, it does not announce itself with thunder. True power, coupled with true wisdom, understands the violence of sudden change. 

It arrived not as a flaming chariot or a descending ship, but as a shift in the barometric pressure. It drifted down through the high, thin stratosphere, integrating its consciousness into the sweeping clouds and the weeping fog. It became the mist. It let itself condense upon the high, cold masonry, trickling down the dry-stacked slate and limestone, experiencing the labyrinth on the labyrinth’s own terms.

For countless days—measured only by the slow passing of light and dark through the cloud cover—the Power simply observed. It flowed over the olive algae, pooled in the shallow amphitheaters, and fell over the smooth lips of the stepped basins. 

As it mapped the hydrology of the continent, it found the Hydromancers. 

The Power watched one of the gray, sun-fearing giants labor entirely alone, spending three tireless days lifting a cracked slab of basalt to reroute a dwindling stream. The Power brushed its consciousness against the creature’s blunt, tendrilled cowl. It felt no ambition there, no greed, no fear of death, no desire for conquest. There was only a profound, aching dedication to the flow. There was only the quiet, crushing loneliness of a species that existed physically apart, bound together only by the shivering mathematics of the water.

A lesser god, moved by pity, might have committed a tragedy of ‘uplifting.’ A lesser god would have granted them tools to spare their muscles, or given them eyes to see the horizon, or broken the world open to give them oceans so they would never have to build another dew-trap again. 

But the Power was wise. It saw that the Hydromancers and the endless city were not two separate things; they were one organism. To give them tools would rob them of their physical communion with the stone. To give them oceans would render their eons of absolute architectural devotion meaningless. They did not need to be saved from their existence. Their existence was a masterpiece. 

Yet, the Power was also infinitely benevolent, and it saw the two great burdens of the Hydromancers: the terror of the piercing sun, and the devastating isolation of their vast territories. 

And so, the Power decided to offer a blessing—not of transformation, but of perfection.

First, the Power turned its mind outward, toward the heavens. It reached invisible, gravity-weaving hands out to the planet's orbital neighborhood. Slowly, over the course of several years, it coaxed a dense, passing ring of icy asteroids into a stable, equatorial orbit around the planet. 

This celestial engineering created a permanent, semi-translucent shadow that stretched across the equator where the megalopolis lay. The harsh, biting daylight that dried the Hydromancers' skins and drove them into the sunken dark was banished forever. The climate stabilized into a perpetual, nurturing twilight. The weather systems became an eternal, gentle cycle of soft weeping rain and thick, heavy mists. The sun was reduced to a pale, harmless luminescence behind a veil of silver gray. The external lung of the city would never again face the threat of drying out. 

The creatures, sensing the change, slowly emerged from the flooded chambers. Their tendrils fanned out upon the waters, tasting the air. They found it rich, wet, and permanently safe. 

Then came the second blessing.

The Power, having secured the sky, descended into the deepest, most ancient cistern at the very center of the megalopolis—a sunken lake of profoundly still water, cradled in a valley of stacked monoliths. 

Taking the form of a hyper-dense droplet of pure kinetic energy, the Power touched the surface of the water. 

It did not speak with words, for words are born of air and tongues. Instead, the Power struck a single, subsonic pulse. It was a note of absolute, resonant perfection, tuned precisely to the cymatic frequencies the Hydromancers used to communicate. 

The message was not complex. It conveyed a feeling: *“I am here. You are seen. Your labor is beautiful. You are not alone.”*

The low-frequency vibration moved through the deep water, shivering into geometric perfection. It struck the weeping walls and was amplified. It rippled over the shallow plains, cascaded down the sluices, and climbed the capillary action of the wet stone walls. Because the water of the megalopolis was infinitely interconnected, the sound became planetary. 

Thousands of miles away, a solitary Hydromancer paused its grazing. Its diaphanous tendrils stiffened, floating on the surface of a shallow canal. The water beneath it began to vibrate, organizing into beautiful, interlocking rings of energy. The creature felt the message resonate through its heavy, slate-gray muscles. 

It felt an overwhelming sense of warmth, of cosmic companionship. 

Across the continent, thousands of solitary giants paused in their slow, endless work. For the first time in the history of their world, they all pressed their massive bodies against the wet stone, and together, they pulsed back. 

The entire planetary labyrinth hummed. Millions of gentle, subsonic thrums merged into a single, global heartbeat. It was a symphony of wet stone, echoing up into the new, eternal mist. 

Satisfied, the Power withdrew into the fabric of the high atmosphere, choosing to remain simply as a guardian spirit of the weather. It would ensure the mist never failed. It would ensure the subsonic network remained pure. 

Left alone once more, the Hydromancers returned to their slow, instinctual labors. They dragged themselves across the wet rubble, seeking fallen stones, scraping the algae, maintaining the flow. They still had no names, no tools, and no eyes. But as they toiled in the safe, quiet gray, the water constantly murmured around them—a soft, glassy whispering that reminded each solitary titan that their eternal work was held, and they were loved.
