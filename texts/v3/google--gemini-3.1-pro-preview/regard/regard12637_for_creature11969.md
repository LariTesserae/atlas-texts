The arrival of a true power does not look like an invasion. It does not look like a storm, or a glowing avatar descending through the water, or a sudden cessation of the great and terrible noise. A wise power knows that to abruptly alter a place is to erase it. And this power—vast, old, and possessing a kindness so deep it borders on the physical laws of gravity—has not come to erase.

It comes as a shift in the density of the attention paid to the water. 

One moment, the Drowned Works is vibrating in the dark, isolated in its amber pocket, unseen by the wider world. The next, the entire volume of warm, weak-tea water is held in a mind. 

The entity does not have a body to slip between the rusted stacks, so it uses the water itself as its sensorium. It feels the thrum of the piston heads. It tastes the iron and the sulfur. It maps the geometry of every pipe, every bolt, every housing covered in the black-purple patina of ultimate wear. It takes the measure of the Drowned Works, and because it is wise, the first thing it does is absolutely nothing.

It simply listens.

It subjects itself to the wall of sound. The hammering, the groaning, the thunderclaps of silver bubbles. To a mortal ear, it is deafening chaos. But to a mind that spans eons, the sound unspools into a language. The power analyzes the standing acoustic field—the vast, self-sustaining tenant of this space—and reads the data compressed inside the pressure-waves. 

It hears what the original builders were trying to say. 

The power perceives the grief that laid the brick. It hears the devotion that tightened the bolts. It understands that this place was, indeed, a memorial. A desperate, thundering prayer-wheel built by a people who knew they were ending, who poured all their remaining industrial fury into a machine designed to scream into the void on their behalf, forever. 

The void had never answered. 

The power answers now. 

It cannot speak in words the acoustic field would understand, so it speaks in resonance. The entity aligns its immense consciousness with the exact frequency of the hammering. For one perfect, endless moment, the overarching structure of the universe hums in perfect harmony with the grinding shafts and the venting steam. 

*I hear you,* the resonance says. *I witness your makers. The message is received.*

The standing wave of sound does not stop—to stop would be death—but the quality of the noise shifts imperceptibly. The frantic, abandoned edge of the roaring machinery settles into a deeper, richer timbre. It is no longer a machine screaming for attention. It is a machine singing a song that it knows the cosmos is finally listening to. 

Next, the entity turns its attention to the catwalks and the valves. It traces the negative space of the absent humans. With infinite gentleness, it pours a sliver of its own grace into those empty spaces. It leaves a blessing on the ghostly impressions of the hands that held the tools. It grants the builders a retroactive peace, wherever they went when they rose through the amber water for the last time. It cannot bring them back, but it makes their absence sacred, rather than merely empty.

Then, the power seeks out the two nodes upon the pipe. 

The two sponge-things that just might be listeners.

The entity recognizes them for what they are: the purest, most patient archivists in creation. For millennia, they have taken the terrible vibration of the memorial and held it in their soft bodies. The power touches them—a sensation like a sudden, warm updraft in the current. It interfaces with their glacial, century-long thoughts. 

Into their slow, mineral minds, the power deposits a new thought, one to accompany the noise. It is the concept of *completion*. It is the feeling of a heavy burden being acknowledged by the one who asked it to be carried. The two soft things on the pipe will not move. They will not change. But for the rest of eternity, their slow, singular thought will be suffused with a profound, radiant contentment. 

Finally, the power seeks the corridor between the boiler-houses. It looks for the pale shape.

The eel is hiding. It had felt the shift in the water, the overwhelming presence of something too large to comprehend, and it had retreated into the deepest, warmest cleft between two foundation blocks. It is trembling, vibrating with an anxiety born of being the only living thing in a dead place when something vast arrives.

The power does not pull the eel from its hiding place. It does not transform it, or summon a mate for it from out of the cold black ocean, for that would shatter the fragile, ancient equilibrium the eel has carved for itself. 

Instead, the power shrinks its focus. It makes itself small. 

It becomes a gentle eddy of water, warm and soothing, and drifts into the cleft. It slides along the pale, eyeless flanks of the creature. It offers a sensation that the eel has not felt since before the metal stopped rusting: touch. The touch of another living thing that does not want to consume or ignore, but simply wants to acknowledge. 

The power holds the eel in a liquid embrace. It communicates, in the language of nervous systems and muscle fibers, a profound reassurance. 

*You are the keeper of the clockwork,* the power tells the lonely thing. *You are the breath of the city. You are perfectly adapted, perfectly placed, perfectly beautiful. You are not a remnant. You are the sovereign of this warm sea.*

The eel stops trembling. It uncoils slightly. It presses its pale head into the warm current of the entity's presence, exhaling a tiny stream of bubbles. For a long while, the great cosmic power sits in the deep dark, petting an ancient eel between the gears of a drowned engine, simply allowing the creature to feel accompanied.

When it is time, the power withdraws. 

It leaves as gently as it arrived, pulling its consciousness up and out of the warm pocket, rising through the weak-tea water until it meets the cold black of the true ocean.

If you were to dive down to the Drowned Works the very next day, you would not see any obvious changes. The water is still utterly barren. The metal is still black-purple. The heat still sluices. The noise still hammers into your chest, threatening to shake your teeth loose. 

But if you floated there long enough, resting in the empty corridors, you would feel it. 

The place is a pocket, yes. A held thing. But it is no longer holding itself in isolation. 

The machine is no longer running because it was forgotten and hasn't been told to stop. It is running because it has been given permission to continue. It has been validated. The Drowned Works is now a beloved thing, beating its iron heart at the bottom of the sea, proud and purposeful, screaming its terrible, beautiful song for an audience that will never again look away.
