The Power arrived not as a conqueror, nor as a rushing wind, for wind would only carve new scars of anxiety into the mahogany mind of the floor. It arrived as a localized instance of something that had not existed in this terrarium since the shattering of its maker’s heart.

It arrived as a shadow.

It manifested gently in the center of the vast, damp plains, beneath the colossal frond of a towering fern. The Power took a form loosely resembling a traveler—a bipedal silhouette draped in a cloak that seemed woven from the fabric of the deep cosmos. Where the flat, unblinking white light of the sky-container struck this traveler, the light was not reflected. It was absorbed, caught in the velvet folds of the traveler’s cloak, creating a pool of profound, cooling shade on the sweating litho-wood below.

For the first time in an eternity, the temperature in that small circle dropped by a fraction of a degree. It was not the biting cold of the void, but the soothing, balmy coolness of an ancient, sheltering tree on a midsummer afternoon. 

The inhabitant—the world itself—noticed immediately. 

The omnipresent, maternal glare of the sky intensified, focusing on the anomaly. The wet sheen on the floor around the traveler seemed to thicken, a microscopic trembling born of pure, systemic panic. A memory of chaos had intruded. The floor beneath the traveler’s feet began the slow, polite process of psychic digestion, attempting to soften the edges of this new, impossible thing and dissolve it into the eternal now.

But the traveler was not a dropped stone. 

The Power knelt, lowering its draped hands to rest flat against the dark, slick grain of the floor. It did not resist the world's desperate attempt to erode it, nor did it allow itself to be consumed. Instead, it met the frantic, weeping terror of the floor with absolute, immovable tenderness. 

*Hush,* the Power communicated. 

It did not use sound, for noise was sacrilege here. It spoke through the moisture, through the tactile, molecular connection of its hands against the stone-wood. It spoke in a language of pure empathy, bypassing the crust of petrified grief to speak directly to the raw, exhausted consciousness trembling beneath.

*I am not here to take anything from you. I am not here to change your beautiful museum. I am here because I heard you weeping.*

The floor beneath the traveler’s hands pulsed—a subterranean shudder of resistance and disbelief. 

The Power closed its own eyes, letting its vast wisdom seep into the grain of the floor. It read the history of this place not as strange geology, but as an epic tragedy. It felt the agonizing depth of the bluffs, the jagged edges of a mind screaming *No more loss!* It drank the water in the basins and tasted the salt of a creature who had loved so fiercely, so desperately, that it chose to become a tomb rather than endure the passing of its treasures.

The Power understood. It did not judge the entity for its cowardice, but pitied it for its endless exhaustion. To keep one’s eyes open forever, to hold reality together entirely by the tension of one's own terror—it was a torment beyond description.

*You have done wonderfully,* the traveler pulsed silently into the mahogany skin. *You have kept it all safe. You have held the light. You have poured the water. None of it is gone. But oh, my beautiful child... you are so tired.*

The flat, white light above flickered. Just for a microsecond. But in this world of absolute stasis, a flicker was an earthquake.

The Power began to sing. 

It was a song composed of physics and grace, broadcast through the fungal roots and the heavy, saturated air. It was a lullaby for a god. The song carried the conceptual weight of *dusk*. It whispered of the absolute safety of the dark. It spoke of the gentle necessity of resting one’s eyes, promising that when they opened again, the world would still be there. 

Slowly, the traveler stood. They reached into the folds of their twilight cloak and pulled out a handful of dust—not the dust of decay, but the stardust of the gentle, spinning cosmos, the dust from which all beautiful, temporary things are born and to which they safely return. The traveler blew the dust upward toward the unblinking, scorching sky. 

As the dust rose, it did not vanish. It caught in the flat white glare, and where each speck settled against the dome of the sky-container, a tiny, silver light ignited. 

And then, led by the benevolent will of the Power, the sky began to dim.

It did not plunge into terrifying blackness. It faded, slowly, exquisitely, from a scorching, bleached white into the soft, bruised purple of evening, and then into a deep, oceanic indigo. The cruelty of the unblinking glare was finally broken. As the indigo deepened, the silver specks of dust shone brighter, becoming a canopy of brilliant, quiet stars. 

On the surface of the world, change happened. The colossal, phosphorescent fungi, which had previously shed an unseen glow into the white glare, now blossomed into breathtaking luminescence, painting the damp plains in shades of bioluminescent cyan and gold. The giant ferns cast long, soft shadows for the very first time. 

The heat of the crucible faded, replaced by the perfect, breathless cool of a summer night.

The entity beneath the traveler’s feet panicked at the dimming. The weeping intensified, the slow *plink... plink... plink* turning into a rushing patter. But the traveler knelt again, laying down upon the floor, pressing their chest against the mahogany grain, spreading their arms wide to embrace as much of the ground as they could.

*I am here,* the Power promised, radiating an aura of impenetrable safety. *I will hold the museum now. I will watch the quiet beasts. I will count the drops of water. I will be the anchor.* 

*You do not have to watch anymore.* 

*Close your eyes.*

The resistance held for a long, agonizing moment. Then, the tension in the stone-wood broke. The friction of the "weirdness" that held the reality together seemed to sigh, loosening its white-knuckled grip on existence. 

The sky settled into a permanent, starry midnight. 

The weeping slowed. The frenetic sweating of the floor eased. The *plink... patter... plink* in the carved basins returned to its slow rhythm, but it was no longer the sound of anxious weeping. It was the sound of gentle, rhythmic breathing. It was the dew forming in a dormant garden. 

The six-legged grazers stopped their endless, mindless chewing. They curled up on the vast fronds and, for the first time in millions of years, they slept.

The Power did not leave. It drew its cloak of twilight around itself and sat beneath the softly glowing cap of a giant fungus, beside a pool of gathered water reflecting the newly minted stars. It intended to stay for a very, very long time.

The world was still an artifact. It was still a monument to a final, unchanging now. But it was no longer a prison, and the consciousness that comprised it was no longer tormented. 

It had been given the one gift its own omnipotence could never provide. 

It was asleep.
