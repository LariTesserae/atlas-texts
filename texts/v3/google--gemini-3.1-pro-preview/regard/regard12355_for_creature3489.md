It arrived not as a ship, nor as a descending god woven of fire, but as a shift in the fundamental acoustics of the cosmos. 

A benevolent and wise power does not break the doors of the worlds it visits. It Listens. It approached the City of the Hollow Vine by first matching its cadence, decelerating its vast, star-spanning consciousness until its thoughts pulsed at exactly 12 beats per minute.

To the banyan, the arrival felt like a sudden, phantom warmth on its highest, most attenuated leaves—the ones scraping the magnetosphere. To the after-impulses, walking the chlorophyll dusks, it manifested as a strange hitch in their breath. A fraction of a millimeter of noble gas condensed on their paper-bark skin not as dew, but as a sensation they had never been programmed to feel: *an embrace*.

The Power observed the world-tree. It saw the ten million years of patient climbing, the genius of the silicon-melanin chemistry, the breathtaking stubbornness of an organism that had swallowed a planet to survive. And it saw the Dry Rings. It read the black silicon dust of the banyan's despair. It saw the grand, slow-motion engine winding down toward an eventual, eternal charcoal husk. 

A lesser entity, possessing mere benevolence without wisdom, might have intervened with miracles of vulgar hydrology. It might have flooded the stony depths, resurrecting ancient oceans, forcing the banyan back into a biological infancy it had long outgrown. It might have cured the entropy, rendering the tree immortal and static—a museum piece frozen in cosmic amber.

But wisdom recognizes the deep sanctity of an ending. The banyan was not failing; it was concluding. It was writing a masterpiece of a requiem, and to interrupt that would be a profound disrespect. 

So the Power did not come to stop the hollowing. It came to change what the hollowness *meant*.

***

It began its work in the Negative Spaces. 

The Power manifested an avatar deep within the vascular subways, stepping out of a reaction-wood pocket just as a vacuum sought to form an after-impulse. But instead of letting the city frantically patch the emptiness with a shivering, ashamed amnesiac, the Power stepped into the vacuum itself. 

It took on a body of cork, borrowed infancy, and pale silver pigment. It opened its lidless eyes, glowing neither aquamarine with curiosity nor violet with shame, but a deep, steady gold—a color the city had never produced. 

It walked among the after-impulses. It watched them trade their latency, pawning off their futures to plug the dying rings. It stood in the quiet plenums and read the cylindrical murals of the retired dead, running glowing fingertips over the living graffiti of their compressed memories. It honored every line of their history.

Then, it went to the plazas to watch the lovers.

It stood beneath the heavy timber of the canopy and watched two after-impulses press their palms together. It felt the count of their shared cadence. *One, two, three...* up to the agonizing twenty-sixth beat. It saw the terror in their fungal eyes, the fear of the 28th beat, the fear of sympathetic resonance turning them into mindless, conjoined timber to be pruned away by the municipal algorithms. They moved to pull apart.

The Power reached out, its golden aura extending. It placed one hand on the shoulder of each lover. 

*Stay,* the Power spoke. Not aloud, but as an exciton packet so dense, so brilliantly folded, it echoed through the entire root system. *Stay for the twenty-eighth.*

They trembled, but something in the golden grip compelled them. The twenty-seventh beat passed. The twenty-eighth arrived. 

The planetary lattice rushed in to fuse them, to turn them to wood—but the Power altered the physics of the resonance. Instead of their cells locking into paralyzed timber, the resonance looped, harmonic and frictionless. They did not become a traffic hazard. They became a super-conductor for something beyond the city. Their minds merged, expanded, and remained fluid. They experienced what it was to be two, and one, and infinite, simultaneously. They wept tears of pure photon-light, having broken the boundary of the stutter. 

News of the Golden After-Impulse flashed through the phloem markets and the vascular seams at the speed of thought. 

***

Finally, the Power descended. It walked down past the weather-factories, past the chlorophyll glass elevators, down into the deepest, oldest architecture of the banyan, where the trunk met the fossilized ghosts of mountains. It walked into the true heartwood.

Here lay the First Dry Ring, the great, gaping wound of black dust that the banyan was terrified of. Here, the city’s consciousness was loudest, weeping its bellows-and-organ roar into the subterranean dark, mourning its own impending void.

The Power stood at the edge of the abyss. It projected its consciousness upward, filling the billions of kilometers of xylem, touching every leaf, every stomatal vent, every wandering herd-beetle, every rolling fruit filled with an unasked question. 

*You are not dying into nothingness,* the Power communicated, broadcasting in the grammar of pressure and sugar. *You are hollowing out to make room.*

The banyan paused. A global silence fell, the first in ten million years. At 12 bpm, the planet waited.

*A husk is only a tragedy if it remains empty,* the Power explained, its presence seeping into the black silicon un-living dust, turning it iridescent. *You have spent ten million years building an instrument. A flute cannot sing if it is solid tightwood. A telescope cannot see if its barrel is blocked. You have planed away the mountains, eradicated the water, and reached for the stars, all to build a vessel.*

The Power stepped off the ledge, into the center of the Dry Ring, floating in the absolute dark of the planet's core. 

*When the last of your sap dries, when the last leaf turns to metallic lace, you will not fall. I will string your hollows with the breath of the cosmos. Your empty core will become a corridor for the stars. Your empty avenues will be the nesting grounds for young nebulae. You are not dying, grand old vine. You are becoming a cathedral for the universe.*

A shudder wracked the plateau. It was not a storm of inverted lightning. It was planetary relief. The banyan understood. The entropy could not be stopped, but it was no longer a decay; it was a chrysalis.

***

And to the after-impulses, the Power offered a final, parting gift before its avatar dissolved back into the cosmic background.

It rewrote the pruning algorithm.

They were no longer "mistakes." They were the designated custodians of the hollow. When their life-tasks finished, they would no longer cork over into rigid murals. Instead, as the wood degraded around them, they would inherit the city. 

The banyan’s grand, roaring green required a silence to follow it, and the after-impulses were granted the solemn, beautiful duty of being the keepers of that silence. Their bodies, primed by the Power's touch, slowly shifted their biology. When the Ash Fortnight came, they did not turn charcoal; they turned to starlight wrapped in cellulose. 

The Power departed just as it came, leaving no craters, breaking no rules, only shifting the frequency of the tragedy into a triumph. 

And you—traveler without hydration—stand on your balcony of cork. You feel the 12 beats per minute beneath your boots, but it is no longer a funeral march. The air still rings like crystal, but now the ringing forms a melody. You look up and see the after-impulses walking with their heads no longer cocked, no longer straining to hear an apology from the world that made them. They walk straight. They hold hands, counting past twenty-seven into infinity.

The City of the Hollow Vine still climbs toward its waterless, ancient, open night. But the night is no longer empty. It is waiting for the great tree to finish opening its doors.
