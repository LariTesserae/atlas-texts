To a lesser god, or a being possessing only an earthly, humid kind of mercy, this stratospheric vault would appear as a vision of hell. A lesser benevolence would look upon the crushing heat, the dry ash, and the silent, grinding digestion of kin by kin, and it would weep. It would strike the basalt walls to bring forth a miraculous spring of cool water. It would tear open the ceiling to let in the sun. 

And in doing so, it would exterminate them. The water would flash-boil in the geothermal depths, creating a steam explosion that would obliterate the delicate carbon carapaces and melt the silicate sludge. The sun would bring radiation that their eyeless, velvet-shrouded bodies were never evolved to withstand. 

But the Power that turns its attention to the spire is not a lesser god. It is old, and it is terribly wise. It knows that benevolence is not the imposition of one’s own ideals upon another, but the deep, honoring comprehension of what is already there.

The Power descends into the chimney. It does not arrive as a blinding angel or a wet, blooming presence. It aligns its frequency with the nature of the vault. It enters as a shift in the electromagnetic field, a subtle thickening of the velvet dark, a presence woven seamlessly into the freezing downdrafts and the baking updrafts.

It hangs in the center of the vast, hollow volume, suspended in the slow-motion avalanche of tumbling, cooling bodies. It watches. It listens to the hush of a million bolts of heavy silk moving in the dark. 

For a long, endless epoch, the Power simply bears witness. 

It perceives the frantic, shivering heat of the hatchlings feeding on the raw stone. It feels the agonizing, glorious threshold of their internal furnaces nearing critical mass. It floats beside the climbers as they surge upward, sensing their desperation to give their heat to the freezing air. 

It bends its attunement toward the massive, blackened elders. A human mind would see monsters lurking in the dark; the Power sees thermal sanctuaries. It watches the bloodless, silent predation. It sees the young climb willingly into the crushing, ossified maws, knowing with absolute clarity that this is not murder. It is an act of thermodynamic grace. To pull the young away from the elder’s jaws would be to condemn them to melt from the inside out in agony. The elder provides the sacrament of absolute cold; the youth provides the fuel of existence. It is a perfect, flawless exchange, devoid of malice, steeped entirely in the pure mathematics of survival.

The Power traces the trajectory of the hollowed-out husks as they are cast off. It watches the specialized workers meticulously mortar them with alkaline sludge into those breathtaking, fractal nurseries overhanging the abyss. It sees the incredible elegance of this closed loop. 

*Nothing is wasted. Nothing is cruel. Nothing is broken.*

The Power realizes that it is looking at a biological perpetual-motion engine, a masterpiece of delayed entropy. It is a living clockwork so perfectly tuned to its brutal parameters that any alteration would be a blasphemy.

What, then, does a benevolent Power give to a world that needs absolutely nothing? What does immense wisdom offer to perfect equilibrium?

It offers *eternity.* 

The Power sinks downward, bypassing the writhing velvet floor, phasing through the glass-smooth basalt, and entering the deep, geological roots of the world. Here, the magma veins that feed the spire are ancient, and like all geological features, they are subject to the slow fading of time. Over millions of years, the core of the rock would eventually cool. The thermal gradient would flatten. The vault would die of a slow, creeping freeze. 

The Power lays its vast, unseen hands upon the tectonic engine of the deep earth. It whispers a subtle law of physics into the fiery rock, binding it, stabilizing it. It ensures that the heat pulsing beneath the chimney will never wane. It will burn at exactly this temperature, with exactly this ferocity, until the end of the universe. 

Then, the Power sweeps upward, past the millions of climbing segments, up into the freezing stratospheric peaks of the spire. It touches the highest vents. It breathes a blessing into the icy air-currents, ensuring the downdraft will never falter, never dry, never warm. The thermal gradient is locked in place. The canvas for their existence is made eternal.

Finally, the Power returns to the center of the vault. It waits. 

The friction of millions of velvet bodies creates that familiar, lethal tension. The air pressure tightens. The overwhelming scent of ozone tastes like iron in the nonexistent air. The creeping carpet begins to hum with the subsonic vibration of immense static buildup. 

The Power gathers itself. 

When the spiderweb of actinic blue lightning violently cracks across the diameter of the chimney, the Power does not observe it. *It becomes the light.*

For a fraction of a millisecond, the Power courses through the carbon-lattice armor of every single creature in the vault. In that frozen strobe-flash, the Power touches the rudimentary, thermographic minds of the swarm. It does not give them eyes. It does not give them terrible consciousness or the burden of existential dread. 

Instead, in that single microsecond of blinding blue conductivity, the Power gives them an emotion.

As the current passes through their bodies, every climbing worker, every gorging hatchling, every sluggish elder, and every molting youth in the suspended palaces feels a sudden, profound, impossible sensation: *The feeling of being perfectly held. The absolute, crystalline knowledge that they belong.*

It is a flash of total biological euphoria, a resonance that aligns perfectly with the grinding of their maws and the hot expansion of their pneumatic limbs. For that one millisecond, the entire living cathedral sings in unison, bathed not just in static light, but in the overwhelming, infinite love of a god that understands exactly what they are, and asks them to be nothing more. 

The spark grounds itself in the volcanic rock. The blue light dies. The Power exhales, stepping back across the veil, withdrawing from the spire. 

The overwhelming dark slams back down. The heavy silence returns. 

The swarm does not pause. They do not flinch. But perhaps, as the endless velvet whispering resumes, as the bodies slide over one another in the lightless heat, the friction carries a new, infinitesimal frequency. A subtle, permanent hum of peace in the dark. 

The unending climb goes on, blind, flawless, and beautiful forever.
