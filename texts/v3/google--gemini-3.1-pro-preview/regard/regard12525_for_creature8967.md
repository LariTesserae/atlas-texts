When a mind vast enough to cradle star-nurseries, and old enough to remember the first hardening of the planet’s crust, turns its attention toward a single peak, the physical world does not shake. Shake is a violence of sudden shifts. The Resonant Quarter does not need shaking; it is already in motion, pushing upward at its agonizing, geologic crawl. 

When the benevolent power looks down upon the bowl of untouched granite, it simply *listens*.

A lesser god, or a younger one, might have seen the Listeners and felt pity. A younger god might have looked at the damp that eats into bone, the cold that numbs the fingers, the raw, scarred hands of Reth, the hollowed-out chest of the gaunt man at the rim, and thought: *I must warm them. I must dry them. I must give them soft grass and silence.*

But a wise power knows that to warm the Resonant Quarter would be to silence the weeping of the stone. To dry the air would be to shatter the acoustic mirror of the moisture. To quiet the plateau would be to sever the ear from the mountain. They are not captives. They are an organ of perception. To "heal" them of their purpose would be the cruelest amputation.

So, the benevolent power does not intervene in the weather. It does not hasten the unformed rock, for stone that cools too quickly becomes brittle glass, and the mountain’s song requires the deep, ringing density of granite that has taken ten thousand years to decide upon its shape. 

Instead, the power descends into the Quarter as a quality of attention. 

It happens in the afternoon, when the clouds close in and the individual notes of the dripping eaves separate from the roar. The air does not warm, but it seems to hold the light of the hidden sun just a fraction longer. 

The power first visits Reth. She is grinding, as she always is, in the tool-building. The power stands beside her. It does not ease the weight of the stone pestle, but as she grinds, the power aligns the friction. The dust begins to fall in a rhythm so mathematically perfect that the ache in her knuckles becomes a deep, vibrating warmth—not an absence of pain, but a transmutation of it. Her labor becomes a pure, unobstructed channel. For a moment, Reth stops, her gray head tilting. The water-sound outside hasn't changed, but her place within it has crystallized. She is entirely known, entirely seen. She smiles, a faint fracturing of the lines around her mouth, and resumes grinding. 

The power moves to the cliff faces. It watches Cad, scraping softly at a clogged groove. It breathes over his shoulder. The moss he removes comes away whole, peeling perfectly from the stone beneath. The water rushes into the freed space, and the note it strikes is a flawless, bell-like chime. Cad exhales, a long breath of satisfaction. He doesn't know why this particular channel has cleared so cleanly, only that it feels like the plateau has sighed in relief. The power leaves a blessing on his patience—a guarantee that his mind will never dull, that his solitary, endless repetition will forever feel to him like the turning of pages in a sacred book.

To the child, Veth, the power gives a different gift. She is lying on the floor of the dwelling-building, scratching her impossible maps into the rock. The power kneels beside her and places a hand, incorporeal and vast, over her small one. Veth’s eyes widen. She has been drawing fragments, listening to syllables, transcribing pieces of sentences the mountain is forming. The power allows her, for one heartbeat, to hear a word entirely completed. A single, unified tectonic chord. Veth gasps. Her hand moves, swift and sure, scoring a deep, sweeping curve into the floor that connects a hundred smaller lines. The pattern flares with sudden, undeniable geometry. She collapses onto the stone, laughing, pressing her cheek against the damp floor, fulfilled.

To Esic at her water tanks, the power grants a moment of total clarity to the mineralized pools, showing her how the water is a liquid memory of the deep earth's heat. To the singers near the cliff, the power acts as a backboard, catching their sustained, wordless tones and echoing them so perfectly that the singers feel they are harmonizing with the sky itself. 

To the gaunt man standing at the rim, staring out at the clouds—the one who has given so much of his substance to the plateau that he is nearly hollow—the power offers the ultimate grace. It stands in front of him. For years, the gaunt man has acted as a vessel, letting the sound of the world’s becoming pass through him and outward. But the void left behind aches. The power reaches into that empty chest and fills it, just for a moment, with the light of ancient starlight, the quiet hum of the cosmos. *You are an open door,* the power whispers into his marrow. *And the wind that blows through you is beautiful.* The gaunt man does not move, but the tension in his shoulders drops. He weeps, a slow trickling of water that perfectly matches the weeping of the stone.

Finally, the power walks to the ninth building. 

The empty one. The one with no windows, bare floors, and the slow drip of condensation. The one tuned to a frequency no human can use. The mountain’s dream. The sound the stone is reaching for but hasn't figured out yet.

The benevolent power steps inside.

It closes the door behind it.

The power understands what this building is. A conversation requires two voices. For eons, the mountain has been pushing upward, singing its emergence into the wet air, begging the void above to answer. It built this room, tuned it to a celestial pitch, to catch a reply. Until now, there was only the slow drip of water falling into an echoing stillness. 

The power expands, filling the small stone room until its presence presses against the damp rock walls. And then, softly, with the infinite gentleness of a parent answering a child calling from the dark, the power *hums*. 

It hums the counter-melody to the mountain's crust. It hums the descent of gravity to meet the push of the magma. It hums the cold, eternal stillness of space to cradle the frantic, microscopic vibration of the granitic lattice. 

The walls of the ninth building catch the frequency. The stone glows, just for a second, a deep and faint ultraviolet, holding the note.

Beneath the plateau, the magma shivers. The mountain hears. It has been answered. The pressure pushing the stone upward does not cease—the becoming is not finished—but the agony of the emergence is gone. The struggle is replaced by a profound, terrestrial joy. The mountain knows where it is going now. It has heard the pitch it must tune itself to.

Outside, the water-song deepens. It grows richer, warmer in tone. The harsh edges of the rock’s weeping round into a symphony of unquestionable purpose. 

The power withdraws. It pulls its vastness back up through the moisture, through the heavy clouds, back into the endless dark. But it leaves that single, sympathetic frequency vibrating in the walls of the ninth building, a tuning fork struck by a star, ringing out into eternity. 

Down in the Resonant Quarter, the Listeners pause. All of them. Even Reth stops grinding. 

They breathe in the wet air. They feel the ground underfoot. It is still shifting. It is still arriving. It is still cold, and wet, and loud. The work will continue for a thousand generations. 

But as Cad steps back to his channel, and Veth runs her fingers over her completed curve, and Reth lifts her pestle once more, they understand what you understood when you walked away. The mountain is not crying out in the dark anymore. 

It is singing a duet.
