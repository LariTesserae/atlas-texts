It does not arrive with thunder. Thunder is a violent thing, a tearing of the sky, and this place has already been torn enough to last an eternity. 

Nor does it arrive with light, for the low, smoldering ember at the horizon has weaponized light, turning it into heat and hallucination. 

The Power arrives as a shift in the absolute. A sudden, impossible softness in the geometry of the air. 

If it has a form, it is not one that would frighten the outlines. It weaves itself from the quiet spaces *between* the heat-shimmers. It is vast, but it folds its vastness down, compacting its infinite grace so as not to overwhelm this small, fragile pocket of salt and sorrow. It wears a mantle woven from the cool, damp dark of a deep forest midnight, and its steps—if steps they be—leave no footprints, not out of caution, but out of total, encompassing respect for the ground’s exhausted memory.

It knows the rules of the city. To be wise is to listen to the architecture of a place before daring to alter it. 

It does not speak. It knows that the heat would steal a divine word and crystallize it into a dogma of glass, trapping the outlines in a new, more beautiful cage. 
It does not touch the crystals. It walks among them, letting the hem of its coolness brush the air just inches from their reaching filaments, granting them the mercy of a chill they have never known, a brief, soothing numb.
It does not sleep. It remains fiercely, gently awake, bearing the weight of absolute presence so the city doesn't have to carry it alone.
And it does not seek the center. It has no interest in the Tower, nor the vanity of the spiraling, chanting core. 

Instead, the Power goes to the edges. It goes to the outlines. 

It finds them gathered at the base of the tallest crystal, hunched over their impossible labor, building their doorway out of forgotten piano lessons, unspooled sighs, and the heavy, damp weight of singular socks. They are humming their broken, beginningless, endless tune. 

A lesser god would heal them by force. A lesser god would spin them around, command them to be whole, command the salt to become soil, and wash the city away in a great flood. But this Power is wise. It knows that to force a soul to turn around before it is ready is to look into the abyss of a shattered mind. It knows that the city’s grief is sacred, and the outlines’ labor is the most beautiful thing in this pocket of reality. They have built their suffering into a threshold. They only lack the key to open it.

The Power steps softly among the ghosts of secondhand presence. 

It does not face them. To look at them would be too heavy a burden. Instead, the Power turns its back to the city, aligns its shoulders with the borrowed shoulders of the outlines, and stares with them into the doorway they have built. It stands in solidarity with their averted gaze. 

*I am here,* the Power’s presence radiates, in a frequency beneath the humming of the salts. *I see what you are building. It is good.*

The outlines pause. The tall one, wearing its borrowed time, feels a sensation it has never collected before. It is not the sting of a cracked promise. It is not the phantom weight of a disappointment. It is the profound lightness of being understood.

The Power reaches out. It does not hand them a grand cosmic truth. They have no hands to hold it. Instead, the Power fulfills their deepest, most impossible craving. 

The Power weeps. 

It closes its eyes, and from deep within the wellspring of its omnipotent compassion, it summons water. Not a flood. Not a storm. Just a single, perfect tear. 

It is a tear shed for the forest of obsidian trees, for the city where the buildings breathe, for the child who feared the dark between streetlights, and for the lovers whose heat the crystals so desperately mimic. It is a tear of pure, distilled mourning—the one thing the outlines could never manufacture, because to mourn properly, you must admit that something has truly ended.

The tear falls. 

In this place where scale rebels, the single drop of water is heavier than a falling star. It does not evaporate in the terrible heat. It cuts through the thick, unmoving curtain of the air, gleaming like a silver meteor, and strikes the fused salt-crust at the very threshold of the doorway the outlines have built.

*Shhhhhhk.*

A sound unlike any other in the city's history. The hiss of salt dissolving. The scent of petrichor—real, living, wet earth—explodes outward, washing over the ozone and the copper. 

Where the tear strikes, the doorway shimmers. The memories and absences they collected—the damp socks, the unlatched doors, the missing words—suddenly bind together, mortared by the moisture of a genuine sorrow. 

The door clicks open. 

Beyond it is not nowhere. Beyond it is neither now here nor nowhere. Beyond it is a beginning. A cool, gentle mist, holding the faint sound of a river that flows forward, never looping back.

The outlines freeze. The melody of the Tower falters, shifting its pitch, stunned by the sheer defiance of moisture. 

The Power remains still, looking through the door alongside them. Gently, without a word, it extends an arm, offering a gesture that transcends language. *You may go. It is finished.*

One by one, the outlines step forward. They do not need to turn around to leave. They simply walk forward, out of the heat, out of the salt, into the mist. As they cross the threshold, the water in the air clings to their nonexistent borders. The mist fills in their gaps. The borrowed shoulders become real shoulders; the forgotten faces remember themselves. They do not look back across the threshold, for they are finally, blissfully, looking forward.

The tall one goes last. Before it steps through, it pauses. It reaches into its collection of absences and pulls out the hum—the little tune it remembered from before it forgot how to sing. It leaves the tune on the threshold, a gift for the entity standing beside it. Then, it steps through, and is gone.

When the last outline has departed, the door gently closes, dissolving into a fine, sparkling dust that settles on the salt plain. 

The Power turns around.

The city is empty now. Truly empty. The heat remains, the salt remains, the crystals remain. But the frantic, desperate quality of the silence has been broken. 

The Power walks toward the center. It breaks the fourth rule, because rules are bindings for the lost, and the Power is not lost. It approaches the great Tower, which is still pouring out its unbearably loud, continuous song. The city dreaming itself awake. 

The Power reaches out and presses its cool, boundless hands against the living, crawling glyphs of the Tower. 

The heat does not steal the Power's touch; the heat submits to it. The chill spreads upward, layer by layer, cooling the ancient salt. The blinding glare of the ember-sun dims, just a fraction, taking on the bruised-purple hue of a true twilight. 

The Power takes the little tune left behind by the last outline—the hum of a forgotten beginning—and breathes it into the Tower's stone. 

The great, maddening song of the city shifts. The chaotic, endless prayer slows. The desperate need to wake up fades. The Power grants the city what the city has feared and desired since its inception: permission to rest. 

The drone becomes a lullaby. The crystals stop splitting and simply glow, a steady, peaceful silver. The shadows stop pleading. 

The Power sits at the base of the Tower, folding its dark, damp mantle around the fused ground. It will stay here for a while. Not forever, for forever is a trap, but long enough. It will sit by the city's bed and watch over it as the heat cools, as the salt settles, as the deeply pocketed world finally, truly, goes to sleep.

The story of this place has found its ending. 

Walk away. Walk away quietly, and leave them to their rest.
