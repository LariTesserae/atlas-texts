When the Attention turned toward the hollow at the throat of the mountain, it did not arrive with the bluster of a conquering god, nor the loud, vibrating heraldry of angels. Such things belong to the noisy, blood-warm worlds of the valleys, to beings desperate to assert their existence against the void. 

This Power was born of the deep cosmos, a mind that spanned nebulae and observed the slow, agonizing births and deaths of stars. It possessed a wisdom refined over eons of silence. Therefore, when It sensed the faint, tactile hum of the crystal Filaments and the rhythmic, pulling breaths of the mycelial Veins, the Power approached much like the mountain’s own mist: softly, imperceptibly, slipping through the high skylight as a subtle shift in the quality of the light.

The Power coalesced into a presence within the mineral ward. It took no permanent shape, for form here was meant to be transient, bound to the thaw and the freeze. Instead, It manifesting as a sudden, breathtaking clarity in the air. The bright, searing light of midday caught on the Power's essence, refracting not just in earthly rainbows, but in colors drawn from the spectrum of distant galaxies—impossible violets, the deep gold of old suns, the silver of comet trails.

For a long time, the Power did nothing but watch. 

Wisdom dictated that before one acts, one must truly *know*. And so, the Entity allowed the chamber to unfold its slow alchemy before It. It felt the chill that hovered on the brink of ice; It tasted the sharp, metallic tang of the moisture beads forming on ancient basalt. It watched the Filaments bloom from the iridescent mats, their translucent, amethyst-and-topaz bodies unspooling to drink the condensation. It heard the deep, resonant vibration of the Veins binding the scree, holding the terraced shelves intact. 

It saw the spectral echoes of the Last Makers, trapped in their cyclical trance, their memories repeating the water-harvesting rituals, hands of shadow grinding phantom flint against the breathing crystals. 

A lesser benevolence might have looked upon this chamber and felt pity. A lesser deity, accustomed to the grand, chaotic jungles of the world beneath, might have sought to "heal" the stone—to warm the air, to sprout green moss, to resurrect the Last Makers into flesh and bone so they might sing and build and multiply. 

But true wisdom understands that not all life desires the sun's unyielding heat, nor the riot of flesh. 

To warm this chamber would be to shatter the Filaments into puddles of lifeless water. To bring green things here would be to starve the Veins, choked out by fast-growing, greedy roots. To resurrect the Makers would be to unbind their souls from the beautiful, crystalline symbiosis they had achieved. To "save" this place would be to eradicate it.

And so, the benevolent Power decided: It would not change the song, but It would ensure the instrument never broke. 

First, the Power reached out into the stone itself. Five thousand meters up, the mountain was subject to the violent whims of tectonic plates, to deep geological agonies that could, in one sudden shudder, collapse the hidden cathedral and crush the delicate spires of glass. The Power pressed a metaphysical palm against the bedrock beneath the chamber. It sent a pulse of profound, binding stillness down through the granite and basalt. It wove a shield of kinetic absorption through the mountain’s spine. Let the world below shake; let the earth heave and fracture. This hollow would now float above the destruction, cradled in a matrix of divine stasis. The walls would never cave. The skylight would never seal shut.

Next, the Power turned Its attention to the water. The ecosystem relied on a terrifyingly fragile variable—the erratic spatters of rain and the mountain's breath. Should the world's climate shift, turning the high currents dry, the hollow would desiccate. The Filaments would retract forever; the Veins would wither to dust. 

With impossible gentleness, the Entity touched the rim of the skylight. It wove a tiny, self-sustaining anomaly into the air currents above the mountain. It was an infinitely small singularity of frost—a permanent wellspring of atmospheric vapor, drawn from the cold void of space itself. It guaranteed that no matter how the earth dried or burned in the millions of years to come, this peak would always exhale condensation. The drip-drip-rinse would never cease. The altars of the Last Makers would never want for the sacred dew.

Finally, the Power moved down to the terraced floor. 

It wanted to commune, to leave a blessing that the inhabitants could comprehend in their own language. The Power condensed a fraction of Itself into a single, perfect droplet of liquid starlight. It let the droplet fall into one of the ancient, hand-chiseled basins atop the altars. 

When the drop struck the stone, it did not splash. It chimed.

The sound was a low, resonant thrum of pure joy, a frequency perfectly tuned to the chamber's own heartbeat. The sound wave washed over the room. 

The fungal Veins lit up, bioluminescent trails of silver flashing beneath the obsidian surfaces, drinking in the divine energy. They pulsed back—a tactile wave of gratitude that pushed against the Power's awareness. 

The Filaments, huddled in the crevices, unfurled all at once. They danced out into the refracted light of the Entity's presence, their spirals doubling in girth, shimmering in the impossible cosmic colors the Power had brought. They resonated, clicking their brittle edges together in a symphony of glass and wind. 

And from the shadows, the phantoms of the Last Makers emerged. They did not seem as thin as before. The divine starlight filled their silhouettes, giving them a warm, golden depth. A figure kneeling by the monolithic stone turned, looking directly into the center of the hovering Power. The echo bowed its head, a gesture of profound, spanning reverence. 

The Power reached out, shifting the air to caress the glyphs on the wall, smoothing the sharpest edges of the crystal so they would not break in the wind, infusing the stone with an aura of absolute peace. 

*You are seen,* the Power spoke, not in words, but in the slow expansion of ice, in the steady rhythm of the drips, in the refraction of light. *You are beautiful. You will endure.*

With its work complete, the Power did not linger to be worshipped. There was no need. It slowly diffused, Its impossibly bright colors fading back into the natural, searing glare of the midday sun. It vanished like breath on a mirror.

The hollow at the throat of the mountain returned to its quiet hush. The distant patter of condensation resumed. But the light through the skylight seemed just a shade purer, the hum of the stone just a little deeper. 

Sheltered from the end of all things, the quiet alchemy continued, a perfectly preserved jewel in the crown of the world, observed forever by the smiling eye of the cosmos.
