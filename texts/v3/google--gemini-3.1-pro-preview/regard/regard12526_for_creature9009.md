This is what a wise and benevolent power would do.

First, it would do nothing.

A lesser power—something full of bright, forceful intentions and a shallow understanding of time—might look down upon the Gorge of Constant Seeping and feel pity. A lesser power might see the pinched, pale faces, the relentless labor, the shivering damp, and decide to *fix* it. It might seal the aquifer. It might part the clouds and strike the stone with noon-light until the mud baked into dry, cracked earth. It might widen the walls to give them fields and horizons.

But a wise power knows that to dry the gorge would be to suffocate its people. They have no word for dry because they have no architecture for it, no skin for it, no lungs for it. To give them a dry horizon would be to tear the river stone from the river and leave it to flake and powder in the sun. 

So, the benevolent power watches. It settles its awareness into the strip of grey sky above the cut, and it pays attention. 

It watches for a decade, which is nothing to a power, but a long time for a gorge that is only three generations old. It watches the young women press their flat, heavy-padded palms against the weeping stone. It watches the children run blindly, perfectly, across slick timber over the ankle-deep stream. It tastes the chalk and the iron. It feels the ache in the builders' shoulders as they mix midge-shells into pale clay. It listens to the *veth*—the breathless whisper of condensation—and understands that this is a place where life is lived on the absolute edge of dissolution.

It sees the profound, terrible tension of the gorge: The human residents are maintaining a desperate, static equilibrium, trying to freeze a moment in time so they can survive. But the gorge is a geological infant. It does *not* want to be static. It is trying, desperately, to become.

And the power sees the writing on the walls. The *sekketh*. 

The power is old enough to read it. It looks at the branching white lines, the concentric rings, the hollow tubes of calcium and iron, and it recognizes the language. It is the language of nascent earth. The gorge is awake, in the dim, slow, blind way that stone and water awaken when they are suddenly exposed to the air. The gorge is babbling. It is throwing its newly born consciousness against its own walls, expressing the trauma of its splitting, the relief of the water, the shock of the light. 

*I am open,* the walls are screaming in slow white minerals. *I am pouring out. Look at my shape. Look at what I am doing.*

The power realizes why the residents will not read the walls. It is not just the fear that the gorge might demand something of them. It is the overwhelming, deafening volume of the gorge's voice. To a human mind, tuning into that raw, geological screaming would induce madness. The residents ignore the writing because they are surviving the only way they can: by closing their ears to the infant titan they live inside.

A wise power recognizes that a bridge must be built. Not a bridge of timber across the stream, but a bridge of intent between the stone and the flesh. Small adjustments. Microscopic graces.

The power does not descend in light. It descends as a shift in the deep earth, miles below the aquifer. 

It begins with the water. The power reaches into the dark, pressurized seams where the water gathers before it is pushed up into the gorge. It cannot stop the seeping, but it can adjust the alchemy. It gently filters out a fraction of the heavy, bitter salts that leach over time into human bones and cause the joints of the elders to ache. It introduces a subtle, complex vein of silicates—minerals that, when exposed to the air of the gorge, crystallize just a little harder, just a little faster.

Up in the gorge, the builders notice it by the change of a season. The pale clay suddenly binds better. The wattle-and-daub walls, usually needing repair every three months, hold firm for six. The scored timbers, soaking in this slightly altered mineral water, petrify with a newfound structural integrity. They don't know why, and they do not ask. They simply adjust their building schedules, finding themselves with an extra hour in the day, an extra ounce of energy in their limbs.

Next, the power touches the ecosystem. 

It does not fill the gorge with deer or fruit trees; there is no room. Instead, it blesses the blind, pale salamanders. It coaxes their reproductive cycles, so that the lower channels always seem to have one resting in the dark, fat and healthy. It enriches the algae on the stones, making the dark film thicker, more robust, so that a handful yields twice the nutrition it did before. The midges breed slightly larger. The dippers lay clutches of four eggs instead of three. 

The residents remain lean—they will always be lean—but the sharp, gnawing edge of starvation softens. The hollows beneath the children's cheekbones fill in. The women sing, sometimes, a low, breathy hum while they harvest the long-legged flies.

Finally, the power turns its attention to the gorge itself. 

The power reaches out to the dim, vast, frantic consciousness of the waking stone. It wraps the raw intellect of the young geology in a blanket of ancient, soothing presence. 

*Hush,* the power speaks into the rock. *You are open. You are seen. But they are very small, and you are frightening them. Speak softly. They are trying to hold you.*

The gorge, feeling the touch of something vastly older than itself, calms. The frantic, pressurized push of its awakening settles into a steady, rhythmic breathing. 

And the *sekketh* changes. 

The water still flows. The minerals still deposit. But the patterns shift. The power guides the water, acting as a translator, an editor. The vast, screaming diagrams of geological trauma begin to melt away under the relentless moisture, replaced by new formations. 

The white trails no longer look like frantic veins or chaotic webs. They begin to grow in deliberate, structural harmony with the human dwellings. Where a timber beam needs support, a mineral ridge slowly thickens beneath it, forming a perfect, natural corbel. Where a walkway is dangerously slick, the water deposits a textured, hexagonal grit that catches the flat soles of human feet. Where a sleeping ledge is exposed to cold drips, the stalactites above curve outward, redirecting the water into the collection channels perfectly.

The markings on the flat walls—the true writing—change from terrifying, incomprehensible density to simple, repeating runes. Broad, gentle shapes. Curves that echo the shape of a human palm.

It happens so slowly, over the course of the fourth generation, that the residents do not realize at first what has occurred. But one day, Teth, now an old woman, is walking along a third-tier bridge. The *veth* is settling in, the late-afternoon whisper of condensation echoing softly in the dim, blue-grey light. 

She stops at a section of the upper cliff face to check the moisture. She raises her broad, flat hand and presses it flush against the wet argillite. 

For her entire life, touching the wall meant feeling a threat. It meant checking for weakness, for dissolution, for the encroaching end of her home. But this time, as her palm settles against the stone, she feels something else.

Beneath her hand, a ring of pale mineral has formed, perfectly encircling the space where she always rests her palm. The water seeping from it is cool, but the stone itself feels dense. Solid. Anchored. 

Teth looks at the wall. For the first time in her life, she does not look *past* the formations. She looks *at* them. 

She traces the white lines spanning out from her hand. They do not look like a warning anymore. They look like an embrace. They map the exact architecture of the settlement, tracing the load-bearing lines, showing where the stone is strongest, pointing precisely to where the next shelf should be carved. 

The gorge is no longer screaming. It is whispering, and it is speaking their language. 

*I will hold,* the stone says in the slope of the white calcium. *Cut here. Rest here. I will bear the weight.*

Teth does not pull her hand away in fear. She strokes the wet stone, a slow, gentle movement. A long breath escapes her lungs, a sigh that joins the *veth*. 

She calls to the younger builders. She points to the wall. 

They gather on the narrow bridge. For the first time, the people of the gorge read the stone. They see the map. They see the permission. They realize that they are no longer fighting the water to maintain a dying foothold. The water is trying to help them build. 

The benevolent power, watching from the grey sky, feels the exact moment the symbiosis locks into place. The fear evaporates, leaving only the wet, cool air. The residents will still build. They will always build. But now, they build *with* the gorge, following the blueprints it writes for them in slow white flames. 

The power withdraws its direct touch, leaving the altered alchemy, the rich algae, and the quieted earth to their own devices. Its work is done. It has not changed what the place is; it has only allowed it to become what it was meant to be. 

Down in the dim, wet, eternal quiet of the crevice, the people mix their clay. The water seeps. The stone holds. And the gorge, no longer a wound, begins to heal around them.
