Across the unfathomable gulfs of the cosmos, where time is a frozen river and stars are but dust motes in the dark, a Presence turned its attention toward the edge of reality. 

It was a consciousness of vast, slow benevolence, older than the concept of shape. It was a Gardener of the In-Between, an entity that sought out the frayed, the forgotten, and the precariously balanced. And in the bruised purple twilight of a dissolving dimension, it found the Sky Graveyard. 

The Presence drifted down through the heavy, pressing dome of the sky. It did not possess eyes, but it perceived everything: the crushing cold, the ocean of wind, the colossal worn molars of basalt, and the seeping, weeping pockets of emerald and bronze. Above all, it felt the *aching tension* of a continent defying its own cessation. It saw the reality glitches—the hiccups of gravity, the folding of distance, the syrup-slow crawl of time—as tearing threads in a grand, ancient tapestry. 

And then, it heard the Deep Song. 

It was a sub-vocal hum, a rhythmic tapping against the bones of the world. The Presence zoomed its vast awareness into the sheltered ravines, into the cyclical, sighing breath of the geothermal vents, and observed the Stone-Singers.

A lesser god, lacking wisdom, might have looked upon these stunted, scarred figures with pity. A lesser god might have commanded the sun to shine, melted the ice, banished the reality-warp, and gifted them green fields and flowing rivers. 

But this Power was infinitely wise. It understood *context*. 

To normalize the gravity would rob the children of their instinctive, weightless dances. To smooth out time would erase the sacred patience required to grind the crystalline ferns. To strip the cold would kill the luminous lichen and silence the damp, dripping ecosystem that shaped their very souls. To heal their skin of its basalt and opal would be to strip them of their armor, their lineage, their hard-won symbiosis. They were not victims of the Sky Graveyard; they were its ultimate expression. 

Benevolence, the Power knew, is not about making a world easy. It is about making it *endure*. 

So, the Power did not conquer. It harmonized.

It descended softly, invisibly, until it permeated the heavy air of the largest settlement. It waited for the dark cycle, when the wan light faded entirely and the communal hollows glowed with the eerie, beautiful luminescence of cultivated spores. 

In the deepest chamber, near a roaring steam fissure, an Elder sat. Her spine was a ridged mountain range of polished onyx; the veins in her cheeks were threads of pale, refracted quartz. She was tapping a large, flat slab of tuned basalt with obsidian-tipped fingers, sending a slow, mournful warning through the stone. *The continent groans,* her rhythm said to the surrounding warrens. *The fraying worsens. The abyss pulls.*

The Power reached out, not with a voice, but with a vibration. 

As the Elder struck the basalt for the next beat of her warning, the Power added its absolute mass to the resonance. 

The resulting sound did not shatter the eardrums; it bypassed them entirely, vibrating directly in the marrow, in the quartz veins of their flesh, in the basalt nodules on their backs. Every Stone-Singer in the settlement—every Singer across the entire continent—stopped. 

The Elder’s hands hovered over the stone. She touched it again, a hesitant, questioning pattern. *Who sings beneath the Deep Song?*

Through the stone, the Power replied. It mirrored her rhythm, then expanded upon it. It laid a foundation of cosmic stability beneath their fragile music. 

Slowly, carefully, the Power began its work. 

It did not repair the broken laws of physics. Instead, it *tuned* them. The entity reached into the stuttering gravity and gave it a tempo. Where gravity had been a terrifying, random failure, it now became a syncopated, predictable rhythm—a breath the universe took every few cycles, known, calculable, and safe. 

It touched the viscous pools of time, smoothing their edges so that the transitions between the creeping crawl and the frantic blur faded into a gentle, flowing tide, rather than a jarring crash. 

It sank its awareness into the crumbling, grinding tectonic plates beneath the basalt. The continent was dying, slowly falling into the abyss. The Power could not stop the entropy, but it could stretch the twilight. It wove threads of starlight and conceptual gravity into the bedrock, lashing the floating corpse-continent to the absolute fabric of space. The deep, terrifying groans of the shifting rock settled into a low, comforting purr. The foundation was stabilized. The epoch would be extended by millions of years.

Finally, the Power offered a singular, tangible gift of grace. 

Along the edges of the geothermal vents, where the blistering steam met the biting cold, it coaxed a new mineral into being. It grew like frost, a delicate, branching crystal of deep, warm violet. When the Stone-Singers touched it, they found that it captured not just light, but *heat*, storing the vent’s warmth and radiating it slowly, eternally, even away from the vents. It was a tool for them to press against their chests in the deep dark, a small defense against the biting cold that required no fire.

The interaction lasted for what felt like a single, held breath, and also an eternity. 

The Elder, tears of thick, mineral-rich fluid welling in her eyes, traced a final pattern onto the basalt slab. It was not a message of warning. It was a frantic, beautiful, sprawling declaration of gratitude. It meant: *The Stone holds. The Song deepens. The Weaver is witnessed.*

Her quartz veins glowed, refracting the dim light into a blinding prism. 

The Power, feeling the immense, stoic joy of the Stone-Singers radiating back upward, slowly withdrew. It pulled its consciousness up through the bruised purple sky, past the heavy dome, and back into the cosmic sea. 

It left no dogma behind. No temples would be built in its name, for the Stone-Singers had no concept of worship, only of harmony. But over the next generations, the intricate carvings on the chamber walls would change. Woven among the histories of storms and lineages, there would be a new recurring motif: a vast, invisible hand cradling a floating stone, surrounded by violet crystals.

The wind returned to scouring the endless plains. The damp mosses drank the seeping fog. The reality field still shimmered with impossible colors, and gravity still danced its syncopated waltz. The Sky Graveyard remained a magnificent, desolate anomaly. 

But it was no longer falling. 

And in the quiet, ancient dark, the Stone-Singers tapped their tuned slabs, adding a new, unshakeable baseline to the Deep Song, singing a silent duet with the universe that had looked upon their harsh, beautiful existence, and chosen simply to help them stay.
