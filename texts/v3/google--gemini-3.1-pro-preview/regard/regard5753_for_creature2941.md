To the entities that drift between the stars, moving through the cold, frictionless void of the cosmos, the concepts of gravity, tension, and decay are abstract. But this Power—this vast, quiet consciousness that suddenly turns its attention toward the buried cylinder—is an older kind of god. It is a Weaver. It understands the sacredness of the binding thread, the holy geometry of the knot, and the unbearable weight of holding things together.

It does not arrive with a booming voice or a blinding flash. Such things would terrify the Architects, blinding their dilated amber eyes and sending them plummeting in a panic. Instead, the Power enters through the zenith grate simply as a shift in the quality of the light. The golden, dusty sunbeam thickens, taking on a weightless, honeyed warmth. 

The Power drifts down through the hazy air, an invisible intelligence expanding to fill the cathedral of basketry. It feels the deafening, rattling clatter-clank, the percussive *thwump* of bodies rebounding off ancestral leather, the shrieks of the hook-winged birds. 

It looks upon the Architects. It sees their bruised, calloused paw-pads, their trembling, hyper-articulated fingers, and the frantic, adrenal terror that drives their endless mending. It watches a withered elder breathe his last in the damp lower rings, and watches the Tanners meticulously harvest his flesh so that the city might not fall. 

A lesser god—a naive, merely "kind" deity—might have felt pity. A naive god would have shattered the terra-cotta pipes, lifted the auburn mammals to the surface, and placed them in a soft, flat meadow beneath an open sky. 

But the Power is profoundly wise. It knows that to place an Architect on flat, solid earth would be a cruelty beyond measure. They have no neurology for walking; the horizon would induce agoraphobia that would shatter their minds. Their twelve-fingered, liquid hands would find nothing to grip; their culture, built on tension, vibration, and the honor of becoming the floor-web, would instantly evaporate. To "save" them by changing their environment would be to eradicate them entirely. 

True benevolence does not overwrite the nature of a creature; it perfects the world that creature was made for. It removes the despair, but leaves the dignity.

So, the Power decides not to dismantle the basket. It decides to become part of the weave.

First, it heals the bones of the world. The Power reaches into the atomic structure of the massive, sagging ironwood timbers and the colossal terra-cotta rings. Over millennia, the wood has crystallized, warping under the unbearable weight of the metropolis. The Power breathes into the petrified cellulose. It does not sprout leaves—for this is not a forest—but it restores the living tensile strength of the fiber. The wood groans, a deep, resonant cello-note that echoes through the cavern. Micro-fractures in the clay pipes fuse smoothly. The rotting damp in the joists turns sweet, transforming from corrosive mold into a fragrant, golden resin that naturally repels the stinging centipedes but continues to catch the condensation. 

The Architects freeze. Thousands of auburn monkeys hang suspended, their multi-jointed fingers gripping the restored timbers. Through their foot-pads, they feel the change. The terrifying, microscopic shudder of impending collapse—a vibration they have lived with for thousands of generations—is gone. The city hums with the deep, ringing solidarity of flawless architecture. 

Next, the Power turns its attention to the cycle of the dead. It does not stop the Tanners from flaying the fallen; that is their sacrament. But it touches the process. When an Architect's heart stops, the Power ensures they do not die in cold exhaustion. A sudden, euphoric warmth floods their veins at the end. And when the Tanners harvest the skin, the Power weaves a fraction of its own starlight into the proteins. 

When the floor-webs are stretched across the abyss, they no longer look like the dark, stained hides of a desperate slaughter. Cured by the Power’s touch, the ancestral leather becomes fiercely resilient and faintly translucent, glowing with a soft, bioluminescent amber hue. When an Architect leaps from the trampoline, the *thwump* is accompanied by a gentle ringing, singing with the literal energy of the ancestors holding them up. The dead are no longer just tools of survival; they are exalted, luminous saints of the structure.

Finally, the Power descends into the crushing dark. It goes to address the supreme trauma of this ecosystem: The Drop. 

The endless, hungry black below the lowest hammocks is the source of all terror. It is where the giant purple beetles breed in the offal, a lightless purgatory that claims any child who slips, any bird whose wing yields.

The Power cannot remove gravity, but it can catch the fallen.

Miles down, where the air turns freezing and thin, the Power spins a loom of solid light. It weaves a colossal safety net made of dense, glowing, gravitational threads. It looks like a massive, swirling galaxy of soft, blue-white silk spanning the entire width of the cylinder. 

To the beetles and the falling waste, the net is permeable; the detritus of the city sifts right through, feeding the base of the food chain as it always has. 

But to the birds, and to the soft-fleshed Architects, the net is solid. 

If a young scout slips while hunting a beetle, their desperate scream will not end in silence. They will plummet into the dark, only to be caught in a soft, glowing, bouncy cradle of starlight. Down there, in the serene blue glow, they will rest, uninjured, able to climb the massive, illuminated threads back up the walls to their kin. The Abyss has been tamed. The ultimate punishment for a misstep is no longer death, but merely a long climb home.

The Power lingers just long enough to feel the first realization strike the metropolis. 

A heavy hardwood mallet strikes a taut, glowing leather membrane. *Thwump.*
Another answers. *Thwump. Thwump.*
Then, a massive, orchestrated wave of drumming erupts across the entire cylinder, a joyful, deafening symphony that rattles the dust in the zenith light. The air screams with the flight of slate-grey birds and vibrates with the chattering barks of an unburdened people. 

They are still prisoners in a buried cylinder. They must still hunt the beetles, dodge the rain, and obsessively weave their architecture. But they are no longer fighting a losing battle against entropy. 

The nightmare geometry has been elevated to a holy design. The Weaver above retreats back into the cosmos, leaving behind a flawless, eternal engine of life, suspended in the dark, spinning and singing forever.
