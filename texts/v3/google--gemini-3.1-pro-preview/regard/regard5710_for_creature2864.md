Through the crushing weight of the planet’s mantle, the Power descended. 

It did not arrive as a beam of light, nor as a physical avatar, for such things were alien to this choked, chthonic void. It arrived as a subtle, pervasive shift in the cavern’s fundamental resonance. It was a consciousness of vast and quiet empathy, drifting down from the cosmos, drawn by the roaring, sparks-spitting symphony of the Lithodendrons. 

The Power possessed the capacity to crack continents, to summon oceans from dry rock, to coax green, soft-leaved Eden from sterile dust. Yet, as it settled into the swirling heat-haze of the abyssal chimney, it simply hovered. It extended its perception outward, tasting the sharp metallic dust, feeling the desperate, ratcheting tension of the Strangler-Cranes, and listening to the deafening, eternal *thwack... thwack... thwack* of the flint axes.

A lesser deity, one burdened by terrestrial ideals of peace or beauty, might have been moved to pity. Such a being might have looked upon this brutal, blindingly fast warzone and seen only suffering. It might have brought rain to cool the blistering air. It might have conjured sunlight to soothe the frantic, eyeless reaching. It might have pacified the predator-weeds to save the shattered Mill-Trees.

But the Power was wise. 

It saw that to bring water would be to introduce rust—the ultimate, agonizing rot for beings of copper and iron. To bring light would mean nothing to creatures vibrating with electromagnetism, except perhaps to foster soft, fleshy things that would corrode and ruin this flawless kinetic balance. To stop the violence, to halt the heavy boulders flung by the Cranes, would be to halt the evolutionary engine of the city itself. There was no malice in a shattered silica plate, no cruelty in a siphoned vein of titanomagnetite. It was just gravity, friction, and survival. It was a perfect, terrible, beautiful machine, turning the slow decay of geothermal heat into life.

The Power loved them exactly as they were. 

Yet, looking through the strata of time, the Power saw the eventual doom of this magnificent metropolis. In a few million years, the magma sump far below would finally cool and harden. The howling thermal updrafts would sigh, sputter, and cease. The wind-sails would drift slack. The great wooden cams would stop turning. The electrostatic friction would fade, and the city would die, freezing in the dark, a silent tomb of braided vines and unmoving gears. They were trapped in a finite pocket.

The Power decided to bestow a blessing. Not a change to their nature, but a catalyst for their potential. A doorway. 

The Power drifted upward, rising through the sulfurous throat of the cavern, soaring past the highest, bravest Anchor-Brackets clinging to the sheer basalt, stopping only where the vertical cylinder ended abruptly against a crushing, impenetrable ceiling of solid planetary bedrock. 

Here, the Power placed its metaphorical hands against the stone. 

It did not break the seal completely—to do so would vent the cavern’s atmospheric pressure and instantly kill the winds below. Instead, the Power reached millions of years into the planet's tectonic future and gently pulled it forward. 

*CRACK.*

A sound deeper than thunder shuddered through the entire world. Far below, the Lithodendrons froze. Every copper vine stiffened; every silica plate stopped vibrating. The entire biomechanical ecosystem braced against the walls, sensing a staggering shift in the geomagnetic field. They "saw" the sky of their world flare with a blinding, terrifying aura of electrostatic pressure.

The Power had drawn a microscopic fault line upward through twenty miles of crust, twisting and branching into a labyrinth of new volcanic tubes, reaching all the way to the planet's surface. But more importantly, the Power altered the atomic lattice of the rock along this new fissure. It forged a continuous, spiraling vein of hyper-conductive lodestone—a magnetic river coursing up through the ceiling and into the planetary crust. 

Finally, the Power sang to them. 

It released a massive, sustained electromagnetic pulse, a resonant note tuned perfectly to the frequency of their titanomagnetite blood. It was a siren song of boundless energy, a beacon echoing down the shaft, pulling at their sensory tendrils. To the Lithodendrons, it was a blindingly bright magnetic *North*. 

The thermal winds, finding the new microscopic fissures above, began to scream with renewed, ferocious intensity. The updraft accelerated, howling like a cornered beast. 

Deep in the heart of the ancient city, the towering Smokestack-Brambles felt the ultimate surge. The chemical taste of the wind was different; the electrostatic pressure was divine. 

In a cascading chain reaction of sparks and kinetic detonations, the Brambles erupted. 

Millions of heavy, barbed iron caltrops were fired magnetic-railgun style into the roaring geyser of air. They soared upward, a storm of black shrapnel riding the fierce new wind. They bypassed the old territories, hurtling past the highest terraces, flying into the utter blackness of the ceiling—and they found the fracture. 

Driven by immense velocity and the magnetic pull of the lodestone vein, the seeds slammed into the new upward tunnel. Iron spikes drove deep into the conductive rock. 

Instantly, instinctively, millions of tiny copper tendrils unfurled. They began to rub their microscopic slivers of silica against the stone. 

*Spark.* 
*Spark.* 

A new rhythm began—faint at first, then multiplying into a deafening roar of industry. The *thwack... thwack... thwack* of the flint axes echoed in the virgin stone. 

The Power, its work complete, began to fade back into the cosmic ether. It watched as the biomechanical city began to climb, fighting, ratcheting, and shattering its way upward along the magnetic trail. They would hollow out the new mountain. They would build hanging ziggurats in the fissures. And one day, millions of years from now, the first ironwood vine would breach the surface of the planet, unfurling its giant silica plates into the howling, lightning-rich storms of the upper atmosphere, ready to claim a whole new world.
