It does not arrive with the blare of cosmic trumpets or the rending of the bruised violet sky. A truly wise power knows that this place is infinitely fragile, a glass menagerie balanced on the edge of a mathematical error. Loudness would shatter it. 

Instead, the power arrives as a shift in the temperature of the light. 

The stark, unblinking daytime illumination softens, taking on the bruised-gold hue of a late afternoon that this plateau has not seen in a million years. The static 0.5 frequency of the silence feels a sudden, profound pressure drop. The universe has turned its vast, compassionate eye toward the asymptote.

The power looks down upon the high, desiccated plain. It sees the billion beasts held in overlapping superposition. It sees the floating flint axes and suspended obsidian spears. And, most importantly, it perceives the Refractions—the pixelated, shimmering ghosts of the shamans who climbed to the top of the world. 

It watches them surf the frozen nervous systems of alien crawlers and prehistoric stags. It watches them perform their holy, agonizing defragmentation protocol, endlessly untangling the crushed data of bone and lung and feather, forever moving the pieces of a puzzle that the universe has forgotten how to solve. 

The power does not feel pity; pity implies superiority. It feels a deep, resonant reverence. The shamans have spent epochs doing the grueling, invisible labor of angels. They have kept the memory of biology alive in a place that murdered space and time. 

But the power also knows that a clock without a second hand is indistinguishable from a coffin. The Pilgrims have untangled the knot perfectly, synapse by synapse, over eons—but without the flow of Time to log their changes, the universe cannot register their success. 

The entity decides it is time to press *Save*.

It manifests at the center of the plateau, not as a towering deity, but as something incredibly small and fundamentally impossible in this high-altitude desert. 

It manifests as a single drop of water.

It hangs in the air, perfectly spherical, catching the golden light. It is heavy. It contains a full, uncut dimension of causality. The Refractions sense it immediately. All across the infinite, frozen herd, the ribboning blue distortions stop their grazing. The pixelated light folds into the stooped, humanoid silhouettes of the shamans. Millions of them rise from the ghost-flesh of the beasts, turning their prismatic, faceless masks toward the center of the plain.

The silence deepens. The anticipation is a physical weight, heavier than gravity.

The drop of water falls. 

It descends through the unmoving, razor-thin air, passing inches from the suspended tusk of a mammoth. It takes exactly one second to fall. It is the first true second this plateau has experienced since the wind died. 

And then, it strikes the pulverized quartz bedrock.

*Tick.*

The sound is not loud, but it is absolute. It is a 1.0 on the scale of sound. It ripples outward from the point of impact, a concentric wave of restored physics. 

Where the ripple passes, the asymptote collapses, and the grand unspooling begins.

Because the Refractions have spent ages meticulously separating the intersecting bodies, there is no violent physical displacement. Instead, as the wave of Time washes over them, the universe finally accepts the shamans' math. The animals *un-merge*. A towering, calcified quadruped gently steps out of the ribcage of an armored pangolin. A flock of arrested birds smoothly separates from the spine of a massive grazer. Space expands, stretching and sighing, accommodating the billions of bodies by blooming the plateau outward into a vast, new geometry.

Then comes the release of the unspent momentum.

All at once, the trapped kinetic energy of a billion mid-stride beasts is actualized. A colossal, deafening intake of breath sweeps over the plain. Lungs fill. Hearts slam against ribcages with the thundering rhythm of drums. Blood courses through veins that have been dry since the Pleistocene. 

Hooves strike the stone. Paws hit the dirt. Wings beat the air. 

The stillness shatters into the most glorious cascade of motion the world has ever witnessed. The infinite herd resumes its migration, taking the step they were meant to take an eternity ago. The dust rises—real, billowing clouds of dust, proving that the air has volume, that the world can change, that consequence has returned. The scent of ozone is washed away by the heavy, rich musk of living fur, hot breath, and crushed wire-grass. 

Above it all, a wind begins to blow. It sweeps down from the bruised violet sky, whistling softly through the ancient, knee-high rings of piled stones left by the pilgrims. 

The Refractions stand amidst the rushing, thundering tide of life. They do not glitch anymore. The sickening, pixelated jitter of their forms smooths out. The fractured light softens into a steady, brilliant incandescence. 

They look at their hands. They look at the towering mammoths and alien quadrupeds marching peacefully past them, no longer phased through one another, no longer batteries of trapped potential, but living things continuing their journey to the horizon. 

The great work is finished. 

The presence of the benevolent power brushes against the minds of the shamans, a warm, whispering breeze that carries the scent of lower valleys, of blooming flora, of home. *You may rest now,* the wind seems to say. *The knot is untied.*

One by one, the shamans look up at the returning blue of the sky. They do not leave behind bodies. They simply let go of their geometric tension. They dissolve, drifting upward like sparks from a chimney, floating into the high atmosphere, finally allowed to complete their own interrupted pilgrimage into whatever lies beyond. 

The floating flint knives and obsidian spears clatter softly to the ground, obeying gravity at last. 

Soon, the great herd passes over the curvature of the horizon, marching off toward new eras and lower lands, until the plateau is entirely empty. 

If you stand there now, at the summit where there is no peak, you will find no reality breakdown. You will find no layered superpositions or glitching ghosts. You will feel the biting cold, you will see the wire-brush vegetation sway, and you will hear the howling, merciful rushing of the wind. 

It is, once again, merely the top of a mountain. Beautiful. Empty. And wondrously, ordinarily free.
