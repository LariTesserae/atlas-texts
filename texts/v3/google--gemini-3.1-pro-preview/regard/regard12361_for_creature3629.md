To true wisdom, there is no such thing as a barren place. 

When the Power turned its vast, gentle attention upon this cyclopean necropolis, it did not come with the blinding light of a young sun, nor did it bring the rushing, erosive force of water. To introduce a terrestrial spring here would be a cataclysm; to ignite a blue sky and a golden sun would be an apocalypse of fire to beings woven from dust, shadow, and frost. 

True benevolence does not reshape the universe in its own image. It looks at the jagged, the broken, and the alien, and asks: *Where is the pain?*

The Power arrived in the silent, thin air not as a physical entity, but as a shift in the ambient pressure—a sudden, profound sense of being understood. It settled over the shifting geometry of the impossible towers like a soft, invisible blanket. It tasted the non-aqueous chemistry, felt the agonizing friction of the fraying timeline, and listened to the tragic, unravelling minds of the dead gods still trapped within their colossal obsidian blocks. 

This place was not just a ruin; it was an open wound in the fabric of reality, locked in a state of eternal, agonizing death-throes. The Power decided not to resurrect the city. Instead, it chose to grant it the dignity of a peaceful end, transmuting it from a place of purgatorial torment into a sacred, stable monument.

It began its work at the lowest depths, where the unimaginable pressures ground stone to dust. 

The **Lithomorphs** felt it first. For eons, they had borne the tremendous, tectonic weight of the dead builders' hubris, straining to maintain the impossible architecture. The Power reached into the deep stone and harmonized with their telluric heartbeats. It did not speed them up; it smoothed the rhythm. It whispered to the living mountains, *“You do not have to hold it all together anymore. The builders are gone. You may just be.”* Slowly, over what mortals would measure as years, the painful grinding eased. The Lithomorphs settled. Their agonizing duty was lifted, replaced by a deep, geological slumber. They became the peaceful anchors of the city, their slow pulses radiating a soothing resonance through the foundations.

Then, the Power's attention drifted upward, into the penumbras and the writhing architecture where the **Shadowkin** dwelled. They sensed the Power and coalesced, a fluttering wave of predatory dread and ravenous absence, instinctively seeking the unravelling edges of this new consciousness. But the Power did not look away, so the geometry did not shift. It did not bring light, which would have annihilated them, nor did it offer them fear. Instead, the Power looked directly into the sentient dark with unconditional acceptance. 

Starved of dread, the Shadowkin hesitated. The Power offered them a new sustenance: the profound, restful quiet of the void. As they absorbed this, their erratic, flickering forms smoothed. They ceased to be parasites of anxiety and became the true guardians of the necropolis’s peace. They settled over the city like a cool, velvet shroud, smoothing the harsh angular shadows. Under their newly calmed touch, the city’s darkness was no longer terrifying; it was simply the comforting embrace of sleep.

High above, in the pale, charcoal sky, the **Aethelings** danced their slow, gossamer ballet. The Power rose to meet them, drifting among their diaphanous, crystalline forms. It listened to the song that no one else could hear—the high, subtle frequency of absolute isolation. The tragedy of the Aethelings was not their form, but their profound loneliness. The Power could not give them a terrestrial voice, but it could give them an audience, and a medium. It touched the thin, cold atmosphere, altering its refractive properties just slightly. 

Now, when the Aethelings sang, their silent melodies manifested as slow-moving auroras—ribbons of muted silver, pearlescent grey, and pale violet that rippled through the overcast sky. They could see each other’s songs. The sky of the necropolis was no longer a dead, heavy ceiling, but a canvas for their majestic, slow-motion symphony.

Finally, the Power addressed the deepest agony of the place: the **Chronovores** and the unravelling minds of the dead gods encoded in the stone. 

The dead builders were refusing to let go, their fractured wills infinitely recalculating the geometry, desperately trying to reassert control over a cosmos that had long since moved on. This desperation created the temporal anomalies that the Chronovores fed upon. It was an ecosystem of madness. 

The Power descended upon the avenues of glassy rock, standing at the nexus of the shifting archways and impossible physics. It exerted its immense, benevolent will, acting as a cosmic psychopomp. It spoke to the stones, to the echoes of the giant minds trapped within: 

*"Your ambition was grand, but the work is done. Time has won, and there is no defeat in resting. Let the geometry fall. Let the end be the end."*

With a touch of pure, ontological grace, the Power untangled the knotted, fraying threads of the builders' consciousness. It granted them closure. It allowed the dead to finally, truly die. 

As the ghostly will of the builders evaporated into the ether, the architecture settled. The vast, impossible archways stopped shimmering. The avenues stopped rearranging themselves. The reality of the city grew dense, heavy, and stable. 

The sudden cessation of temporal chaos starved the Chronovores. They swarmed the Power, a frenzy of stuttering time and looping moments. But the Power caught them in an infinitely gentle grip. *“You have feasted on chaos,”* the Power communicated. *“Now, you shall feast on eternity.”* 

The Power anchored the Chronovores to the deep past of the stone. It changed them from ravenous anomalies into serene curators. They no longer chewed through the present reality, causing it to fray; instead, they slowly digested the eons of the city’s history, preserving the timeline in a smooth, continuous river. Whenever a Chronovore drifted past now, one did not feel the terror of a shifting reality, but a profound, beautiful sense of ancient history—a fleeting vision of a majestic tower as it was a billion years ago, perfectly preserved, peacefully remote.

Its work finished, the Power withdrew, leaving the world as slowly as it had arrived. 

It left no green plants. The air remained thin and freezing. The sky remained grey. But the oppressive, heavy silence of impending doom was gone, replaced by the sacred, resonant hush of a grand cathedral at midnight. Where little grew, the pale lichens now pulsed with a very faint, silvery luminescence—a lingering footprint of the Divine. 

The necropolis was still dead. But it was no longer tortured. It stood against the endless eons, vast, magnificent, and at absolute peace.
