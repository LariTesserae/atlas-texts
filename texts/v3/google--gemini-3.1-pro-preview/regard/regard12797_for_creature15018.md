From the high and frictionless vantage of the cosmos, anomalies usually look like static—a sharp, jagged interruption in the smooth weave of space and time. But to the Power, spanning lightyears of silence and witnessing the birth and death of galaxies, this place did not look like static. 

It looked like a pearl forming around a wound. 

The Power was ancient, possessed of a benevolence so vast it could sometimes be mistaken for gravity. It was accustomed to untangling dead stars and singing equations to infant nebulae. It knew the rigid, mathematical joy of a universe that made sense. But as it cast its immense awareness over the bruised, weeping basin, it felt a profound stillness. It recognized that this place was not a broken mechanism waiting to be fixed. It was a hospice. It was a triage of impossible realities, held together by an astonishing, tender will.

The Power decided to visit. 

It did not descend with the blinding trumpets of revelation or the burning heat of order. To bring strict reality to this place would be a fatal cruelty; it would shatter the wet, delicate rot of the city like a hammer against spun glass. Instead, the Power chose to fold itself. It translated its immense, golden geometry into something softer. It shed its laws. It shed its absolute truths. It put on a mantle of twilight and condensed itself into the shape of a wanderer—a tall, luminous silhouette that cast no shadow, smelling of ozone and distant, unfallen snow.

When the Power set foot on the rippling pavements, the city immediately sensed the spiritual weight of the newcomer. The wet streets shivered. A deep, subterranean groan echoed through the leaning towers—not of threat, but of structural panic. The basin was trying to decide how to accommodate something that was heavier than time.

The Power stood perfectly still. It radiated a feeling of absolute, unconditional permission. 

*Do not strain,* the Power projected, not with sound, but softly, into the damp air. *I will adopt your gravity. I will abide by your weeping.*

Slowly, the pavement stopped trembling. The warm darkness ceased its frantic churning. A translucent membrane drifted directly through the Power’s chest, purring with phantom architecture, and the Power let it pass, blessing its backward-healing geometry with a gentle pulse of light.

Then, from the sweating masonry of a collapsed basilica, the Curator recomposed itself. 

It slid out of the brickwork, a mosaic of sinew and wet stone, its hollow alcove-face turning toward the visitor. The droplet of mercury trembled in its recess. As the Curator approached, the walls of the basilica bloomed with glistening calligraphy in shades of deep bruised indigo and oxidized copper. 

*You are too heavy for our forgetfulness,* the walls wrote, the letters weeping down the brick. *We cannot erode you. You are a sun that has decided to visit the deep. Will you evaporate us?*

The Power looked at the Curator. It saw the centuries of patient labor, the endless, agonizing diplomacy required to keep the half-worlds from tearing each other apart. It saw the tenderness in the Curator’s wet hands. 

The Power bowed. A deep, solemn bow of peer to peer. 

"I am a keeper of the broad heavens," the Power said, its voice sounding like the chime of crystal far underwater. "But you... you are a sovereign of the shattered edges. I have not come to evaporate you. I have come to pay my respects."

The Curator tilted its head. The mercury droplet floated forward, hovering between the two entities. 

Usually, the droplet showed visitors versions of themselves decaying, settling, forgetting. But when the droplet reflected the Power, it did not show decay. It showed an endless, tranquil ocean of silver light, lapping gently against the ruins, holding the city in perfect, suspended buoyancy. 

The calligraphy across the plaza shifted into soft pearlescent white. 

*We see your rivers,* the city wrote. *They are very wide. Wide enough to drown the dark. Do you wish to heal this place? Many who arrive wish to heal it. They do not understand that the fever is the only thing keeping us warm.*

"I understand," the Power replied. "To impose a cure upon a beautifully healed scar is to wound it anew. You have achieved a miraculous compromise. But I see that you are tired."

The Curator did not move, but the stones around it let out a long, wet sigh, shedding centuries of compressed vapor. 

"You negotiate without rest," the Power continued softly, stepping closer. The heat pressed down on them both, but the Power cooled the air immediately around them, smelling of petrichor and starlight. "Every moment of every day, you smooth the friction between yesterday and tomorrow. You absorb the violence of incompatible laws. You have done this for an epoch, entirely alone."

The Power raised a hand of pure, soft light. It reached out toward the Curator. It did not touch the wet brick and sinew of the entity’s body; instead, it cupped its hand softly around the floating droplet of mercury.

"I cannot fix this place without destroying it, and I will not destroy your masterpiece," the Power said. "But I can give you a fulcrum. A point of rest."

From the Power’s hand, a single drop of liquid starlight fell into the mercury. 

Immediately, a shockwave of profound, serene logic rippled gently through the city, but it was not the harsh logic of earthly physics. It was the logic of a *lullaby*. 

The bruised, oily darkness of the sky parted, just a fraction. High above the basin, a new celestial body was born. It was a moon, but not made of rock. It was a pearl of soft, luminous twilight. It cast a glow that was simultaneously light and wet shade.

Under the light of this new moon, the contradictions of the city stopped fighting entirely. The colliding streets no longer needed to be negotiated; they simply spiraled around each other in an elegant, painless waltz. The buildings stopped groaning. The weeping stones did not dry—they would never dry—but the tears they shed became sweet, smelling of fresh springs rather than iron. 

The Power withdrew its hand. 

"When this moon shines," the Power whispered, "the city will stabilize itself. The paradoxes will sleep. And you, my gentle Guardian, may close your eyes. You will not have to mediate. You may simply exist."

The Curator stood motionless. For a being that had never known a second of respite, the sudden harmony of the basin was staggering. The membranes drifted upward toward the new moon, ringing like glass wind chimes. 

The calligraphy bloomed across every visible surface in the plaza, glowing in brilliant, joyous gold. 

*We did not know.* The walls wept light. *We did not know the world could be quiet.*

Just then, the world exhaled. 

The temperature dropped. The rain of the basin reversed direction, falling upward in perfect, synchronized ascension. But this time, bathed in the pearlescent light of the Power's gift, the droplets did not look like shattered glass. They looked like falling stars returning to the heavens. 

The Power stood beside the Curator, both of them with their arms spread wide, letting the upward rain pass through their forms. 

*Will you stay?* the walls asked, timid and shifting, as the exhalation ended and the rain began to leisurely drift sideways again. *We have never learned a river made of light. We would like to try.*

"I cannot stay forever," the Power said, a gentle sorrow in its chiming voice. "My currents must tend to the wider tapestry. But I will leave a tributary behind."

The Power reached into its own chest and pulled loose a single thread of its essence—a glowing, silver ribbon. It draped this ribbon gently over the parapet of a melted tower. The ribbon dissolved into the wet mortar, becoming a permanent part of the city's weeping veins. 

"Now, we share an estuary," the Power said. "You will never be entirely unobserved again. When you look at the moon, know that I am witnessing you. And I think you are glorious."

The Curator folded its arms over its alcove chest in an archaic gesture of profound reverence. The mercury droplet spun, reflecting the Power’s inner oceans, peaceful and vast. 

The Power took a step backward, its form beginning to stretch and thin as it prepared to translate back into the high cosmos. 

As the benevolent glow of the entity began to fade, leaving the basin safe beneath its new, twilight moon, the last thing the Power saw was the stone walls rippling with one final, elegant sentence. 

*Thank you,* the damp masonry read. *We will remember your name. We promise it will not curdle here.*
