To perceive the Glass Steppes of Eternity is to look upon a wound that has held its breath for a billion years. 

When the Great Arbiter—a presence of boundless empathy, a consciousness woven from the light of young stars and the deep, abiding patience of cosmic roots—turned its gaze upon this shattered plain, it did not feel pity. Pity is a small thing, meant for small sorrows. The Arbiter felt *reverence*. 

It understood immediately that this world was not a wasteland. It was a reliquary. It was the absolute, focused effort of a dying epoch trying to hold onto its own memory. 

True benevolence does not arrive with blinding, corrective fire. It does not overwrite the nature of a place merely because it is dark or strange. Wisdom dictates that before one can heal, one must first listen to the shape of the silence.

And so, the Arbiter did not tear through the bruised sky as a conquering sun. Instead, it arrived as a single, falling tear of warmth, slipping through the auroras and touching down on the obsidian plain without making a sound. It took a loose, bipedal form—something comforting, draped in the soft luminescence of a pearl, so gentle it did not reflect on the dark glass.

The Arbiter began to walk. It had all the time in the world. 

First, it met the **Hollow Wind**. 
The wind moved to pass through the Arbiter, expecting to find the same absence it had carried since the beginning of the age. But the Arbiter opened its arms and breathed out. It gave the wind a scent—the smell of petrichor, of crushed leaves, of ozone after a storm. It gave the wind *weight*. When the wind blew past, it was no longer a harbinger of things departed; it was a herald of things to come. 

The wind rushed forward, sweeping into the **Singing Spires**. 
Because the wind had changed, the song of the spires changed. The mournful, shivering chimes caught the new warmth and transmuted it. The notes deepened. They lost their urgency and gained a rich, harmonic resonance—a lullaby instead of an elegy. 

Drawn by the new music, the **Fractured Ones** began to pool. 
They flowed across the black glass in shifting, jagged rivers of shadow, circling the Arbiter. They were terrified of the light, shrinking at the edges. Seeing this, the Arbiter dimmed its radiance, lowering itself to sit cross-legged upon the cold, hard ground, until it glowed no brighter than a firefly. 

The Arbiter reached out a hand, offering peace. 

A shadow rippled forward, a suggestion of a trembling hand rising to meet the Arbiter's palm. When they touched, the Arbiter did not give them flesh, for they belonged here, as children of the glass. Instead, it gave them *cohesion*. The Arbiter spoke a silent word of acknowledgement: *You existed. You mattered. You are seen.* 

The rippling shadows steadied. The fractured edges smoothed. They did not become human or animal; they became graceful, distinct silhouettes of obsidian glass and starlight, untethered from the ground. They could stand. They could look at one another. For the first time in eternity, they did not just watch. They *danced*.

Amidst the silent celebration, the Arbiter noticed the clicking. 

Turning beneath the shadow of a singing spire, it saw the **Glass-Eaters**. They ignored the light, the music, and the dancing shadows. Blind, small, and jagged, they ground the obsidian beneath them into fine, black dust. 

To a lesser observer, they were pests, a mindless entropy slowly chewing away the world. But the Arbiter was wise. It knelt close to the glass, looking not at the creatures, but at what they left behind. 

*Glass cannot hold a seed,* the Arbiter realized. *Only soil can.* 

The Glass-Eaters were not destroying the world. They were preparing it. They were the slow, thankless plowmen of eternity, grinding a sterile, frozen landscape into a bed of rich, mineral dust, waiting for a rain that had never come. 

The Arbiter wept a single tear of profound gratitude. The tear fell onto a small mound of the black dust. Instantly, the dust drank it. From that dark, ground-up pain, a single sprout of pale, crystalline moss pushed upward—translucent, glowing softly with an inner silver light. The Glass-Eaters paused, their clicking ceasing for the first time in an eon. They turned their blind heads toward the moss. They had found the purpose they were born for. They became the tenders of the crystal garden.

Finally, the Arbiter stood and looked toward the impossible horizon, to the towering geometries of the glass city. 

The **Watcher** was waiting. 

The Arbiter did not walk. It allowed the land to carry it, stepping through the folds of space until it stood in the center of the vast, fused metropolis. The city trembled. The reflections of ghosts in the glass walls flickered wildly, trapped memories cycling in panic. The immense, slow consciousness of the Watcher bore down upon the Arbiter with the weight of uncounted centuries of vigilance. 

*I REMEMBER,* the Watcher's presence seemed to pulse, a tectonic thought that vibrated in the Arbiter’s chest. *I HAVE KEPT THEM. I HAVE HELD THE FRAGMENTS. DO NOT TAKE THEM.*

The Arbiter extended its light, letting it wash over the jagged spires and deep mirrored canyons of the city. 

*You have done perfectly,* the Arbiter replied, its thought passing into the glass like a soothing stream. *You are the greatest guardian in the cosmos. You caught a shattering world in your hands and held it together. But my dear, tired friend... the bleeding has stopped. The wound is closed. You do not have to hold so tightly anymore.*

*I AM ALL THAT REMAINS,* the Watcher shuddered.

*You are the foundation of what comes next,* the Arbiter promised. *Let the morning in.*

The Arbiter raised both hands, and from its core, it released a pulse of pure, prismatic light. It did not destroy the city, nor did it melt the Glass Steppes. Instead, the light entered the obsidian. 

The black glass of the world caught the light and fractured it in a trillion directions. The deep, impenetrable black shifted. Deep within the ice-like plains and the towering spires, colors awakened—deep violet, burning amber, twilight blue, and arterial gold. The entire world became a colossal, intricate mosaic of stained glass, glowing from within. 

The bruised sky above broke apart. A gentle, perpetual dawn spilled over the horizon, casting long, beautiful shadows that the Fractured Ones could now call their own. The Hollow Wind blew, carrying the newly awakened spores of the crystal moss from the Glass-Eaters' fields, scattering them across the glowing plains. 

The Watcher breathed out, a sigh that lasted a year. The ghosts trapped in the glass walls stepped out of their reflections, no longer prisoners of memory, but joyful inhabitants of a new, radiant ecosystem. 

Its work done, the Great Arbiter began to fade, dissolving back into the starlight from whence it came. But before it left, it looked upon the Glass Steppes—no longer an eternity of suspended grief, but a world of breathtaking, fragile beauty, alive in its own perfect, ringing way.

The Steppes were no longer waiting. They had begun.
