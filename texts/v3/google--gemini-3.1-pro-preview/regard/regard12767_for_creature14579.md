When a power of true benevolence and unfathomable wisdom turns its gaze upon a place, its first act is never disruption. It does not arrive like a storm, because storms are violent, and this place has had enough of thermal violence. It does not bring a deluge of permanent water, because to flood the Altiplano of Shed Teeth would be to drown its identity, to wash away the grey-gold lichen and suffocate the roots of the lonely, algorithmically spaced shrubs. To change the fundamental rules of a place is a tyrant’s benevolence. Wisdom knows better. Wisdom knows that to save a violin, you do not turn it into a flute; you tune the strings.

The Attention arrives, at first, as a shift in the barometric pressure.

It is midday. The heat is pressing its flat, thermal weight against the stone. The skies are that manufactured, saturated blue. And then, without clouds, without shadows, the hostility of the sky simply… softens. The UV radiation ceases to be a weapon. The temperature does not drop to freezing, but it ceases its assault. The air remains thin—so the adapted lungs of the inhabitants do not suddenly drown in thick oxygen—but the metallic, dry taste yields to something else. The faint, ghostly scent of petrichor. Not rain. Just the absolute, unshakeable promise that the sky holds no malice. 

The Attention moves through the plateau not as a being with a body, but as a quality of light, a resonance in the alleys. It perceives the whole fifty-kilometer expanse at once. It reads the striations in the rock, the ancient diaries of vanished water. It feels the *tick-tick-tick* of the contracting stone, the slow-motion collapse of the pillars, the infinite patience of the dust. 

And it feels the inhabitants.
It feels the deep, geologic embeddedness of the original ones, and the stranded, echoing purgatory of the later ones. 

The Power decides to act. It does not rewrite the sentence of the landscape. It simply corrects the grammar, and provides the punctuation.

**The Tuning of the Acoustics**

The weirdness of the plateau’s sound is the first thing the Power touches. The acoustic geometry is a perceptual glitch that unsettles the mind. The Power does not remove the echoes. It simply shifts the angles of the stone by fractions of a millimeter—so small a geological change it goes unregistered by the eye, but profound to the ear.

The echoes that used to arrive *before* the sound are gently nudged back into the flow of linear time. The voices that arrived from unexpected directions, distorted and lisping, are clarified. The alleys still carry sound for kilometers, but now they carry it like the nave of a great, open-air cathedral. 

When the wind blows through the pillar gaps, the low, sustained *hum* that used to sit uncomfortably in the sternum is brought into a perfect, resonant chord. It is a drone of immense comfort. The steel cables of the abandoned industry, which used to sing a thin, vibrating anxiety, are tuned. They now emit a frequency that aligns with the resting heartbeat of a sleeping mammal. The Altiplano ceases to sound like a clock that has forgotten the hour, and becomes an instrument playing a slow, deliberate adagio.

**The Grace for the Later Ones**

The Attention passes into the generator room, with its stretched steel door and dusty bolt holes. The three or five inhabitants who live there, who speak in clipped creole and wait for echoes, feel the Attention enter. They do not raise weapons. They do not hide. They stop breathing for a moment, letting the silence hold them.

These are the people of the interrupted sentence. The Power gives them the period.

It accelerates the decay of the industrial artifacts, but not through rust. It transforms the decay into integration. The iron of the generator pad oxidizes rapidly, blooming into the deep reds of the native rock. The corrugated metal roof crumbles, not into sharp shrapnel, but into a fine, mineral-rich dust that perfectly mimics the plateau's floor. The rail spur that ends in mid-air does not disappear, but its blackened wooden ties suddenly bloom with seeds carried on the wind—a sudden, creeping line of the thorny, grey-leafed shrubs, turning the failure of industry into a raised garden path.

To the minds of the Later Ones, the Power offers an acoustic whisper. It travels through the tuned alleys and arrives perfectly at their ears. It is not words. It is the feeling of a heavy pack being set down. It is the absolute, unquestionable knowing that nothing more is expected of them. The extraction is over. The debt is canceled. You do not have to wait for the rest of the crew to return. *You belong strictly to the stone now.* 

The Later Ones weep. It is the first time water has flowed from their eyes in years. The tears drop onto the dusty concrete, leaving small, dark circles. For the first time since they were abandoned, they look directly into each other's eyes when they speak, no longer needing to look at the walls to find the meaning in the echoes.

**The Blessing of the Original Ones**

How do you bless a people who already know they are the stone? Who want nothing, who build nothing, who simply participate?

The Attention moves deep into the slot canyons, where the original ones are arranged in the geometry of the shadows. They feel the Attention immediately. Their luminous, pale eyes widen in the daylight. They do not worship it. They do not fall to their hyperextended knees. They greet it exactly as they greet the *sweats*: with profound, quiet readiness.

For them, the Power performs a miracle of hydration. 

It draws up the deep, hidden aquifer, bypassing the basalt substrate. But again, it does not flood. It initiates a *sweat* of unprecedented perfection. Every permeable rock face on the fifty-kilometer tableland begins to weep simultaneously. 

But this water is different. It does not smell of sulfur. It is clear, cool, and infused with the exact mineral balance necessary to ease the caloric scarcity of the inhabitants. As the water beads and falls, filling the pillar-top pools and the slot-canyon depressions, the pale grey-gold lichen drinks and, for the first time in ten thousand years, flushes temporarily with a vibrant, luminescent silver.

The Original Ones step out of their narrow alleys. They cup their hands. They drink. The water tastes of mercy. It soothes the aching in their strange joints. It washes the fine red dust from their tight, concentrated faces. As they drink, the acoustic tuning of the plateau allows them to hear the breathing of their kin, kilometers away, clear and close, uniting their fractured population into a single, intimate auditory space. They are alone, together.

**The Convergence**

Dusk falls differently that day. The thermal cycle does not crash violently from sixty degrees to freezing. It lowers like a gently dimming lamp. The rodents emerge, the raptors circle—but tonight, the hawks do not need to hunt, for the insects are thick around the silver lichen, and the rodents gorge on the sudden bounty of blooming grass along the old rail line, and a temporary truce of satiety blankets the plateau.

At the edge of the widening flat, near the ruins of the generator building, one of the Later Ones stands. 

Fifty meters away, emerging from a slot canyon, stands one of the Original Ones.

Usually, they regard each other across a gulf of acoustic weirdness and fundamental separation. But tonight, the wind breathes through the gap between them, and the pillars sing their tuned, perfect chord. 

The Power holds them both in its gaze. It takes the deep, ancient belonging of the Original One, and the fresh, tear-washed peace of the Later One, and it plucks the air between them. 

The Later One raises a hand. A human gesture of greeting, stripped of all earthly context, pure in its intent.

The Original One does not raise a hand. Instead, they make a sound. It is a tonal, melodic vocalization, the sound they usually reserve for the moment the rain truly begins. But because the acoustics have been healed, the sound does not warp or fracture. It travels straight across the plateau, hitting the chest of the Later One as a warm, vibrating hum of welcome.

*You are here,* the sound means. *The stone holds us both.*

The benevolent Power withdraws. It does not leave—such powers never truly leave once they have looked—but it dissolves into the background, becoming the new baseline reality of the Altiplano. 

The rocks will still fall. The pillars will still eventually collapse into sediment. Water will still be an event, not a condition. The plateau will still be a spectacle of a thing mid-collapse. But it is no longer an uncanny, accidental glitch of geography and ruined industry.

It has become a sanctuary. It is a place that knows it is loved, wearing its stubbornness not as a defense, but as a crown.
