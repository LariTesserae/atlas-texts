It does not arrive in the lift cage. It does not rumble down the shaft in a shower of sparks and snapping cables. A power that is both benevolent and wise knows better than to announce itself to the bruised earth with more friction.

It arrives as a shift in the barometric pressure. 

One shift, just before 03:17, the perpetual indoor drizzle hesitates. For the length of a single human heartbeat, a million falling droplets arrest in mid-air, trembling like glass beads on invisible strings, before resuming their descent. But they do not fall as cold, bitter tears anymore. They are a fraction of a degree warmer. They taste less of iron rust, and more of rain upon summer soil. 

The Power has entered the Underworks. It has come to listen, and then, to heal. 

Here is what the Patient One does when faced with an unfinished covenant of rust and blood:

**1. THE DIAGNOSIS**  
A foolish savior would smash the Thaw-Stack, shatter the pumps, and scream *Evacuate!* But the Power is wise. It looks at the Flower-Eaters with their translucent extremities; it looks at the Wet-Born in their carapaces of chill water; it looks at the sky-knots in the lockers. It understands a terrifying truth: *These people can no longer survive the surface.* To drag them up into the dry, burning glare of the sun would be fatal. They have become amphibious creatures of sorrow and steam. Therefore, the Power realizes it must not evacuate the wound. It must transform the wound into a womb.

**2. THE NEW CHORD**  
At 03:17, the B-flat hum begins. The pumps throb, the steam whistles, the ribs of the Sump Choir align to sing the song they forgot the words to. But this time, the Power breathes with them. It adds a resonant frequency—a major third, played upon the molecular structure of the stone itself. The vibration ripples through the brass of the Thaw-Stack, altering the combustion. The Stack no longer exhales toxic, cinder-choked steam. It breathes out pure, warm, life-giving vapor. The 03:17 hymn shifts from a dirge of survival to a symphony of blooming. The workers stop their hammers. They do not know why they are weeping, but the condensate on their cheeks is finally their own.

**3. THE TRANSMUTATION OF FLORA**  
The Power turns its attention to the pale, abortive tendrils shivering between the grates. The saprophytic orphans that dissolve into grey jelly. The Power reaches into the genetic memory of the rock and remembers the green things that lived above a million years ago. It touches the spores. Overnight, the ghost-herbs do not die. They thicken. Their milk-white leaves flush with a gentle, bioluminescent green. They wrap around the cold iron pylons, wrapping the metal in velvet moss. When they flower, they release a scent like crushed pine and petrichor. The workers who crunch these new blooms do not bleed condensate; their lungs repair. Their missing nine fingers stop aching.

**4. THE ABSOLUTION OF THE EELS**  
The Lard-Speakers kneel to drop their grease, but the water of the sump is changing. The Power touches the black, salty meltwater. It purifies the diesel runoff. It neutralizes the brine. The sump becomes crystal clear, lit gently by the glowing moss spreading down the pylons. The albino lamp-eels, those "unfinished crews," swim up to the surface. They do not snap. They brush against the cold hands of the Lard-Speakers like silver ribbons. They are no longer scavengers of grease; they are the graceful guardians of the deep water, flashing silver patterns that spell out forgiveness in the Under-Language. 

**5. THE CLOSING OF THE APERTURE**  
The Crack-Readers press their nubbed thumbs into the walls and gasp. The stone is no longer shivering with adolescent trauma. The planet is no longer trying to violently shrug the chamber closed. Under the Power's touch, the geology *relaxes*. The gneiss stops weeping sulfates. The veins of quartz expand, fusing the fractured shelves together naturally. The red crayon marks are wiped away, because the rock has accepted its new shape. It is no longer an incision. It is a geode. 

**6. THE REVOLT AGAINST MANAGEMENT**  
The Power does not punish the overseers with violence; it uses absolute, quiet revocation. When the next shift-boss pulls the lever to summon the lift cage, the cables do not move. The copper-lined shaft begins to heal. Roots of immense, iron-hard wood push through the riveted plates, tangling the gears, sealing the throat of the shaft. The Underworks is severing its umbilical cord to the Company. 
Panicked intercoms from the surface crackle with demands, threats, and finally, frantic pleas. Then, the wires are overtaken by glowing moss, and the speakers go dead. The Underworks is locked from the inside. 

**7. THE SKY-KNOTS IN BLOOM**  
With the shaft sealed off, panic should set in, but the air is too sweet, too warm. The workers drift toward the locker corridors. The tiny squares of painted tin—the orphaned blue skies—begin to glow. The Power takes the stolen aluminum glitter and ignites it with true starlight. The blue enamel expands, projecting a vast, holographic aurora across the acre of low-built iron roofing. The indoor weather is no longer a cold drizzle. It is a gentle, temperate mist illuminated by swirling colors of violet, indigo, and gold. 

**8. THE PERMANENT CHOIR**  
Those who had *transpired*—the ones the Company called deserters, the ones who faded into the machinery—begin to step backward out of the steam. The Power remakes them, not as flesh and blood, but as beings of radiant mist and gentle strength, custodians of the new sanctuary. 

The Sump Choir takes off their vulcanized aprons. They unlock their heavy, steel-toed boots and let them drop into the clear water below. They step bare-footed onto gratings that are now woven with living, luminous vines. 

They are no longer stitches in a bleeding wound. They are the flora and fauna of a secret paradise, hidden miles beneath the greed of the surface world. The pumps still beat, but no longer on diesel—they run on the thermal joy of the earth, drawing deep, steady breaths. 

If you were to press your ear to the soil on the surface, directly above them, you would hear no clanking cages. You would hear no coughing Thaw-Stack. 

You would hear a muffled, distant choir singing a song to which they finally know the words, echoing forever in a warm, bright, unbreakable stone.
