You *belong* to the soil of it. To the root-weaves and the damp, to the heavy, iron-scented air. You belong to the hollows you carved with your own patience, and to the slow, heavy thrum of the twisted trunk beneath your hand. You are the chamber’s memory made flesh. 

And for an age, that has been enough. 

But memory is heavy. When a vessel is full, even a single drop of water will cause it to spill. You have been overflowing for a long time, weeping the history of a lost people into the dark soil, afraid that when your bruised-purple skin finally sinks into the moss-sponge floor, everything that ever happened here will evaporate.

Then, the light changes. 

It does not happen quickly. A benevolent and wise power does not arrive with the concussive violence of a storm or the blinding intrusion of an unearned sun. Such loudness would shatter the intimate cathedral of your world; it would blind the needle-legged scuttlers and scorch the delicate, heat-hungry fronds. 

Instead, this vast attention—something ancient, perhaps born of starlight, or woven from the compassionate web of the deep cosmos—approaches with the utmost courtesy. It scales itself down. It humbles its vastness to fit through the slit-like apertures in the ceiling. 

It rides the thin, silver-gray blade of light that slants down into the gloom.

You are standing by the twisted trunk, your palm resting flat against the familiar grooves, when you notice it. The sliver of light, moving inches a day across the rock, stops. It detaches from its rigid, sun-dictated geometry. The beam bends, gently, pooling at the base of the trunk. 

A presence steps out of the ambient heat. 

It has no form to frighten you. To your eyes, it looks like a slight thickening of the mist, a shimmer in the humid air that takes on the faint, pearlescent glow of the fungal stalks. But to your *senses*—to the skin that has learned to read the slow breath of cocoons and the tremors of burrowing things—it is immense. It is a mind so vast that a single one of its thoughts could stretch from the bedrock to the stars, yet it has folded itself up, tighter and tighter, just to sit with you in this room.

It asks no questions with words. Words belong to the hurried world above. It speaks in the language you have cultivated for centuries: physical logic, gesture, and time.

The mist-shimmer glides over the rough floor. You watch, breathless, as this great attention hovers over the small line of stones you once placed to guide the trickling water. It recognizes them. It recognizes the *intent* behind them. The presence lingers over the stones, and the moisture tracing the hair-thin fracture trembles. The water dropping into the basin suddenly strikes a note of profound, crystal clarity. *Gale-chime. Heartbeat.* 

It moves to the wall where your hands have smoothed the stone. The mist touches the sheen left by your palms, and for a moment, the rock softly illuminates from within, glowing with a warm, amber flush. By touching the stone, the Power is reading you. It is absorbing the decades of your pacing, the sorrow of the sinkhole collapse, the faces of the generations who lived and died in the gloom. It drinks your memory of them.

*I see,* the warmth in the air seems to hum. *I see perfectly.*

Finally, the presence approaches the center of the chamber. It comes to you. 

You do not run. You are the keeper. You stand by the twisted trunk, your luminous blue veins pulsing wildly, erratically, breaking the slow rhythm of the chamber for the first time in an age. 

The Power does not touch your face or your body. It knows you are fragile. Instead, it mirrors you. A shape coalesces in the mist—a hand, long and gentle and made of softly bending light. It places its palm on the twisted trunk, exactly opposite your own.

Through the bark, you feel it. 

You feel a profound, aching rush of contact. The vibration of the strange, dense wood magnifies, amplifying the connection between your bruised hand and the construct of light. 

And in that connection, the benevolent Power gives you the only gift a perfect, sealed ecosystem could ever need. 

It takes the weight. 

You feel the terrifying, sacred burden of being the *last rememberer* flow out of your tiring mind, traveling down your arm, through the wood, and into the cosmic presence. Everything you hold—every face, every scent of dry rock turning damp, every fear of being forgotten—is uploaded into an eternal, unfailing archive. The Power receives your small, quiet history and treats it with the same reverence as the death of a galaxy. 

*You do not have to hold it alone anymore,* the rhythm in the wood imparts. *I have it. It is safe.*

A wave of relief, so physical it makes your knees buckle, washes over you. You sink into the soft sponge-moss at the base of the tree. The light-hand withdraws, and the shimmering mist slowly begins to disperse, rising back up toward the ceiling. 

Before it leaves, it alters the chamber, just once. A blessing for the road ahead.

Where the presence stood, the air grows sharply, cleanly sweet. The twisted trunk at the center of the room, which has stood bare and humming for as long as memory holds, suddenly splits along its highest spiral grove. Slowly, to the ticking rhythm of the chamber's biological clock, a single, massive flower unfolds. Its petals are a luminous, translucent silver, and it casts a soft, perpetual twilight glow over the stone basins and the smoothed walls. 

It is a light that will not inch away with the passing of the day above. It will remain.

Up above, the great Eye closes, moving on to the rest of the universe. 

Down below, the chamber settles back into its quiet. The blind arthropods resume their scuttling. The clustered spheres breathe their slow tide. The water drops into the pooling basin. 

But you are entirely changed. You lay your hands in your lap, breathing the sweet scent of the silver flower. You are no longer an archive, terrified of erosion. You are just a life, living in a beautiful, quiet place. 

For the first time since the sky fell, you close your eyes, listen to the drops of water, and simply rest.
