To perceive Ashkar as this Entity perceives it, one must first unlearn the haste of biological time. 

The Power that turns its attention to the Kharak Plateau is old—so old that its thoughts are braided into the magnetic field of the planet, its memories written in the slow overturning of the mantle. It watches the continents drift like lily pads on a sluggish pond. It has seen mountain ranges rise in jagged arrogance, only to be ground to powder by rain and wind over hundreds of millions of years. To this Entity, mountains are not places; they are events, brief and violently beautiful.

And the Kharak Plateau is a very new event. A sudden, ten-thousand-year spasm of the crust. 

But as the Entity’s vast, diffuse awareness draws over the tectonic buckling, it notices an anomaly. A fever. A glowing, rhythmic pulse of heat trapped in the freshest fractures of the rock.

It focuses its immense attention. It sees the Ashkari.

It sees the mammalian heat-bloom of the ice-oxen. It sees the delicate, frantic beating of a million stone-swift hearts. And it sees the humans, driven like wedges into the very cracks of the world, subsisting on the edge of a knife. The Entity traces the flow of their blood—thick, hot, desperate for oxygen. It feels the vibration of their sub-vocal language humming into the basalt. It tastes the limestone mortar made of their ground, pulverized ancestors.

With perception comes something the Entity has not felt in an eon: *tenderness*.

It is moved by their audacity. It is humbled by their absolute refusal to die. They have not conquered the stone, nor have they submitted to it; they have simply woven themselves into its open wounds. 

And so, the Entity decides to act.

A lesser power—a god of human invention, perhaps, flush with ego and miracle—might have leveled the peaks for them. A foolish power might have smoothed the gneiss, thickened the air, brought up rivers from the deep aquifers, and planted warm, green valleys of grain. 

But this Entity is profoundly wise. It understands the fundamental law of Ashkar: *the struggle is the blueprint*. 

To give the Ashkari a warm valley would be to unmake them. Their wide, deep ribcages would become vestigial. Their brilliant, shadow-tracing eyes would go dull in the bright lowland sun. Their silent, tactile language of wrist-angles and fingertip-presses would be drowned out by the noise of the open air. The intense, telepathic intimacy of their communal sleep—the beautiful, desperate warmth of the *rinek*—would shatter into the lonely individualism of the plains. To "save" them from their environment would be an act of genocide. 

Therefore, the Entity's benevolence will not be an erasure of their reality, but an apotheosis of it. It will not break the rules of the Kharak Plateau; it will perfect them.

The Entity begins its work in the deep rock. 

The first change comes to the *fault-listeners*. The oldest elders and the youngest children, pressing their ears to the freezing bedrock, suddenly notice a shift in the infrasound of the earth. For thousands of years, the plateau’s growth has sounded like a chaotic, monstrous grinding—a sleeper thrashing in agony. 

But now, the noise resolves into a rhythm. A music. 

The Entity aligns the crystalline lattice of the rising continental shelf. It cannot stop the mountain from growing—the tectonic forces must have their way—but it can choreograph the shear. When a new fault is destined to open, the Entity sends a deep, resonant hum through the stone, days in advance. The mountain no longer snaps without warning. It sings before it moves. The fault-listeners weep with the terrifying beauty of it, warning the city with absolute precision. No family is ever again crushed in their sleep by a sudden settling of the walls.

Second, the Entity touches the moisture. 

It feels the warm exhalations of the thousands of Ashkari, the breath-fog rising, condensing, freezing. The Entity infuses the supercooled air with a subtle, organizing energy. The rime ice begins to freeze differently. Instead of opaque, unpredictable blooms, the structural ice of Ashkar crystallizes in formations of flawless, load-bearing geometry. 

The *breath-ice* specialists notice it first. The ice-bridges they construct to span the galleries no longer sag or splinter. They harden like diamond. The condensation forms perfect, transparent membranes across the outer crevices, letting in the blinding, surgical light of the sun while sealing out the fatal drafts of the static-storms. The exhalations of the Ashkari literally build unbreakable cathedrals of glass-clear frost around them. Their breath becomes their permanent fortress.

Third, the Entity goes to the *crack-widows*. 

During a major tectonic shift, an elder woman, a crack-widow whose family long ago fell to the deep crevices, lowers herself into a fresh, bleeding fracture in the rock. She takes her obsidian chisel and her bone lever, preparing for the grueling, month-long labor of nursing the sharp edges of the stone into a smooth gallery for the next generation. 

She places her chisel against the basalt. She raises her stone hammer over her head.

The Entity reaches up from the mantle and meets her at the point of impact. 

When the widow strikes the rock, it does not shatter into dangerous, razor-sharp shrapnel. Instead, guided by the Entity's vast manipulation of the stone's atomic fault lines, the basalt cleaves away in perfect, sheer, sweeping planes. With a few taps of her hammer, a space opens before her—flawless, arched, ergonomically fitted to the curvature of the Ashkari spine. The rock yields as if it has been waiting millions of years for her chisel. The mountain becomes a willing participant in the midwifery of its own flesh.

Finally, the Entity offers a gift to the dead. 

Since the Ashkari dead become the mortar of the Commonwealth, their bones ground and mixed with ice, the Entity blesses the very walls of the city. It allows a residual echo of its own vast consciousness to pool in the mortar. When the living press themselves against the walls of their crevices to sleep, soaking up the heat of their neighbors, they feel something new coming from the stone at their backs. 

It is a feeling of being held. 

The absolute, terrifying silence of the high plateau remains, but it is no longer empty. The acoustics of the snow and stone still swallow sound, but into that quiet pours a deep, geothermal comfort. The Ashkari dream in unison—dreams not of falling, or of freezing, or of suffocating, but dreams of deep, ancient roots, of being an essential nervous system of the world.

The Entity does not reveal itself entirely. It leaves no scripture, demands no worship, raises no prophets. The Ashkari do not suddenly invent metal or conquer the lowlands. They remain a people of the in-between, compact, breathless, blued with cold, living their brief, hot lives in layers of mammoth hair and guano-tanned leather. 

But they walk their vertical world with a new grace. The fear is gone. 

To stand on the outermost precipice of Ashkar now is to witness a miracle of symbiosis. The mountains are still young and violent; the city is still dense and confined. But the hostility between the two has ceased. Between the absolute cold of the sky and the relentless upward shove of the rock, the Vertical Commonwealth rests perfectly secure, curled in the cupped, stony hand of a God that knows just how tightly to hold them.
