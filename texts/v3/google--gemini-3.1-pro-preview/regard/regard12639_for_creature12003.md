It arrives not as a grand descent of fire or parting of clouds, for a truly wise power knows that to violently interrupt the equilibrium of a world is a profound cruelty. To stop the rain would be to suffocate the pale amphibians, to shatter the ecology of the scoured basin, and to strip the Tenders of the only identity they have ever known. 

Instead, it arrives as a subtle shift in the arithmetic. 

It comes from the spaces between the stars, a consciousness vast enough to cradle nebulas but refined enough to notice the trembling wing of an insect. It had been listening to the slow, heavy song of the universe, and it heard the rhythm of the Flats—*three quick impacts, a pause, then two more.* Over and over. A heartbeat of relentless, unrewarded devotion echoing out of a forgotten gravity well.

The Power does not take a shape that would terrify. It slips into the ecosystem. It becomes the medium.

First, the deep chill of the basin softens. The temperature rises by perhaps a fraction of a degree—not enough to induce fever in the fast-burning pool-creatures, but enough that the Tenders, drowsing in their half-minute shifts beneath the stone ledges, feel a phantom warmth enveloping their weary, translucent limbs. It is the warmth of a hearth they have never seen, an old, safe heat that seeps into their double-jointed knees and eases the ache of a thousand miles of walking.

Then, the rain changes.

It does not slow, and its volume does not decrease, but the water itself undergoes an elemental alchemy. As it falls through the ancient, slate-gray clouds, it catches a luminescence, a faint, bioluminescent gold that barely cuts the dimness but turns the landscape of shifting mirrors into a canvas of starlight. And the sound—the crushing, eternal white roar—is gently tuned. The harshness is filtered out, leaving behind a deep, resonant chord. The pressure on the eardrums releases into a sensation of being submerged in a vast, choral hum. 

In the basin down below, the Tenders stop. 

For the first time in recorded touches, every Tender pauses simultaneously. They step out from beneath their overhangs, dropping the shed-skin membranes. They look up. They raise their hands, five-fingered and sensitive as exposed nerves, and cup their palms to the falling sky.

When the golden rain hits their skin, it is not just water. It is *language*. 

The Power speaks to them not in words, which they would not understand, but in the tactile frequencies of their own tongue. Every drop of rain that strikes their upturned palms presses a message into their flesh:

*I am here.*
*I see you.*
*You have done so beautifully.*

A shockwave of profound, quiet awe ripples through the community. Across the fifteen miles of eroded bedrock, Tenders reach out to one another. They press hands to arms, to shoulders, to chests, relaying the impossibly vast touch they are receiving from the sky. The water does not carry instructions, or history, or the heavy burden of "why." It carries only pure, absolute validation. It carries a love that requires nothing in return.

And then, the Power addresses the grid.

It perceives the cairns. It understands instantly what they are: an ancient, rudimentary focusing matrix, left behind by a forgotten species who meant to call down down the stars and failed. Left abandoned, the grid would have eroded into meaninglessness millions of years ago, were it not for the Tenders. The Tenders, who maintained the mechanism of a miracle they did not know they were waiting for.

Slowly, the Power settles its immense, invisible weight into the basin. It aligns itself with the geometry of the stones.

As it rests upon the grid, the cairns awaken. They do not glow or hum with machinery. Instead, the water flowing through their carefully maintained drainage channels begins to sing. The specific slopes and angles of the stones, maintained through eons of gentle arguments and meticulous touches, perfectly refract the energy of the Power, passing it from node to node. The grid closes. The circuit is complete.

The Flats are no longer a place of isolated endurance. They become an anchor point for the divine. The ecosystem hums with newfound vitality; the pale amphibians move with a fluid grace, their internal organs glowing gently with the golden hue of the water. The Tenders feel the resonance in the marrow of their bones. Their work is no longer just maintenance. It is communion. Every time they adjust a stone from this day forward, they will be holding hands with the cosmos.

But the Power is not finished. It remembers the one who does not tend.

At the very edge of the basin, where the water thins and the pale rock rises into ridges, the old female Tender—the Spiral—has stopped her patrol. 

She stands facing the basin. She is weeping, though she has no tear ducts; her sorrow and waiting manifest as a trembling in her fibrous internal networks, visible through her heavily scarred translucent skin. She has walked the threshold longer than memory. She has held the boundary. She has born the crushing weight of *anticipation* so the others could live in the peace of the present.

The Power flows toward her. The golden rain thickens, pooling around her worn feet, rising into a shape. 

It does not take the form of her ancestors, or a god. It takes the form of a Tender. A being woven entirely out of radiant, impossibly warm water, standing on double-jointed legs, reaching out with a five-fingered hand.

The old woman hesitates. She looks at her own hand—worn, calloused, the soft pads of her fingertips rubbed nearly smooth by millions of miles of wet stone. Slowly, she lifts it.

The entity steps forward and matches her gesture. Water meets flesh.

The touch is a masterpiece of their tactile language. It is a spiral pressed lightly into the forearm, followed by a pressure of encompassing warmth. It says:

*The waiting is over.*
*The threshold is crossed.*
*Rest.*

The old woman closes her eyes. A breath she has been holding for an entire geological epoch shudders out of her lungs. The tension leaves her joints. The desperate vigil that defined her dissolves, replaced by a radiant, soaring peace. 

The entity steps closer and embraces her, wrapping her in the golden, singing water. As they hold each other, she begins to dissolve, not into death, but into the medium itself. Her flesh softens into light, her bones into resonance. She is drawn up into the gentle mind of the Power, elevated from a lonely sentinel on the edge of nowhere into a companion of the deep stars. 

Back in the basin, the Tenders feel her departure. It is a final touch sent through the waters of the pools, crossing the miles in a cascade of ripples: a sensation of lightness, of a burden laid down. 

The Tenders turn back to their cairns. They test the stones. They cup the rain. 

The arithmetic of erosion remains, but it is no longer endless subtraction. The rain falls, but it is a benediction. The Tenders tend, and they are not alone. And the Flats of Constant Rain echo, finally, with the sound of a promise kept.
