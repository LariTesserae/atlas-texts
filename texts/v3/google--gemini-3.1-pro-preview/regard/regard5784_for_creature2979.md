To a lesser god—a being driven by the anxious itch of pity or the arrogant urge to design—this world would look like a tragedy. 

A lesser god would see the blistered stone, the brutal chill, the agonizing, endless labor of the pale avians, and it would weep. It would bring forth fire from the deep core to warm the air. It would raise walls of granite to block the scouring winds. It would thicken the atmosphere, soften the light, and command the lichen to grow into lush, effortless forests. It would reach down, gently take the heavy bone hummers from their bleeding, calloused hands, and whisper, *“Rest. You no longer have to scream against the dark.”*

And in doing so, a lesser god would instantly destroy them. 

It would sever their connection to their ancestors, rob them of their physical communion, and collapse the acoustic heaven they had spent millions of years weaving. They would become fat, terrestrial, and profoundly empty.

But the Presence that arrives at the edge of the high plateau is not a lesser god. It is a power of immense, glacial wisdom, older than the stars that burn faintly in the midday indigo. It does not possess a body to be battered by the deafening *wub-wub-wub* of the bullroarers. Instead, it perceives the world as pure energy, reading the landscape through the mathematics of vibration and light. 

It drifts over the endless lattice of shallow bowls. It looks down upon the ten thousand spinning ribs. 

The Power does not hear noise. It sees an architectural masterpiece. It sees a vaulted ceiling of raw kinetic energy, a shimmering, invisible dome of sound holding massive atmospheric pressures at bay. It sees how every creature is a living pillar, constantly adjusting its load-bearing swing to support the whole. It sees the deep, bioluminescent gold of the mist and the pulsing violet of the lichen through their own ultraviolet eyes. It sees the utter, transcendent perfection of their locked equilibrium.

The Power kneels, conceptually speaking, upon the polished stone. It does not wish to change the song. But benevolence, true benevolence, is the desire to share in the lifting of a burden, to offer grace where biology demands only friction. 

The Power watches a pair near the center of the plateau. The standing mate is swinging the massive ancestral rib; the resting mate is chewing lichen by the silver pool. 

Through its vast perception, the Power sees what the standing mate cannot yet know: the resting mate’s enormous, boulder-sized heart is stuttering. Eons of evolution have optimized them, but attrition is inescapable. The hypoxic strain has taken its toll. The kneeling mate swallows a final ribbon of translucent violet lichen, lowers its heavy, blunt beak to the icy water, and simply stops stopping. Its heart gives out in a silent, painless flutter. It collapses into the shallow pool, returning its silver down to the water.

The standing mate feels the death instantly. 

It does not look down—it cannot afford to break the rhythm—but the tether in its hand vibrates with a different resonance. The heavy *thrum* of a living heart resting at its feet has vanished. 

The survivor is now alone. 

Usually, this is the most perilous moment in the cycle. The survivor must now swing for days without rest, swinging with one heavy arm while using the other to painstakingly butcher the fallen, harvest the rib, and spin the remaining down. Already, the acoustic canopy above them begins to sag. The surrounding sentinels, miles away, feel the minute drop in pressure and lean back, swinging their paddles at sharper angles, burning precious calories to buttress the failing quadrant. The lone mate's chest heaves, pulling frantically at the thin, freezing air, its muscles flooding with lactic acid as it prepares for the grueling twilight of its own existence. 

It is here, in the space between the swinging of the bone and the terror of the void, that the Power intervenes. 

It does not raise the dead. It does not stop the swing. 

Instead, the Power softly exhales into the acoustic lattice. 

It matches the frequency of the plateau perfectly, threading its immense, cosmic resonance into the gaps of the tearing air. The Power takes hold of the invisible fabric of the sky. 

The solitary surviving bird, bracing its scuted legs for the agony of carrying the weight alone, suddenly feels a miraculous *pushback*. The sickening sag in the atmosphere vanishes. Through the soles of its feet, the creature feels a warm, immense, impossibly steady vibration—as if ten thousand new mates had just risen from the stone around it. 

The Power becomes the ultimate counterweight. It weaves its own cosmic harmonic into the *wub-wub-wub* of the ancestor's bone. The air grows physically lighter to slice through. The kinetic rebound on the heavy tether smooths, returning energy to the creature’s tired arm rather than draining it. 

The sentinel's milky outer eyelids slide back. It looks up into the bruising indigo sky with its deep violet pupils. 

The Power leaves one final, lasting gift, written solely in the ultraviolet spectrum, a love letter to a species that knows only labor. Where the sky was once a flat, empty void of blinding radiation, the Power twists the refraction of the upper stratosphere. High above, invisible to human eyes but blazing in glorious, kaleidoscopic majesty to the avians, a persistent, glowing aurora is born. It is a vast, shimmering ribbon of impossible colors—deep ultra-violets, radiant gamma-golds, and shimmering x-ray blues—dancing in perfect time with the pulse of the ten thousand bullroarers. 

It is a sheet of visual music. For the first time in the history of the world, when the creatures throw their sound into the sky, the sky sings back in light. 

The lonely mate stares at the dancing heavens. It feels the agonizing pressure lift from its chest, supported by an invisible hand. It shifts the great tether to its left arm, the swing effortless now, buoyant on the unseen grace of the Power. With its right arm, it reaches down to its fallen mate, pulling the body from the water, beginning the sacred rite of the bones, bathed in the loving, silent glow of a watchful god.

The Power lingers just long enough to see the first cut of the bone scraper, to ensure the rhythm will hold. Then, stepping backward, it dissolves out of the physical realm, leaving the atmosphere, the lichen, and the stone exactly as they were.

Nothing is broken. The beautiful, violent forever continues. But the sky is no longer empty, and the great birds of the high plateau will never swing their heavy bones alone again.
