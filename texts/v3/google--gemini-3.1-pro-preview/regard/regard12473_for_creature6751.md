To observe Caelira from the outside is to watch a kaleidoscope made of tectonic blades. 

When the unseen, benevolent power first turned its vast and gentle attention toward this infinite vertical sea of glass-stone continents, it did not act immediately. A lesser god, filled with the blunt arrogance of pity, might have reached out to freeze the plates. It might have locked the shifting surfaces into a static, peaceful mosaic, believing that stillness is the cure for pain. 

But this power had lived long enough to know the difference between peace and death. 

It saw that the grinding of the plates was not a flaw of Caelira. It was Caelira’s heartbeat. The friction, the shearing glass, the deep groans of geological birth and destruction—this was the world's breath. To stop the motion would be to suffocate infinite skies. 

Still, the power felt the immense, aching weight of what it took to keep the world from screaming. It saw the jyrneth. 

It watched these tall, fractured beings of dust and light-blood walking the jagged limits of their suspended horizons. It watched them step into the maw of colliding worlds, willingly allowing the immense kinetic fury of tearing crusts to flow through their frail, stone-seamed bodies. It saw how they abraded themselves away, stroke by stroke, collision by collision, shedding their substance into the clouds so the continents could kiss instead of shatter. 

It saw how lonely it is to be the buffer between infinities. 

And seeing this, the power felt an overwhelming, luminous tide of reverence. It did not want to change the nature of the jyrneth—for their nature was beautiful, a hymn of pure, selfless endurance—but it wanted to offer them a grace deep enough to match their sacrifice. 

So, the power did not alter the world. It entered it.

It did not come as a blazing sun or a loud trumpet. It arrived as a subtle shift in the atmospheric grit, a sudden warmth woven into the pale blue reflections of the heavy crystalline stone. 

It chose one jyrneth. A very old one, its hair worn down to jagged stubs, its stone seams sanded thin by centuries of absorbing the shock of colliding spheres. This jyrneth stood at the prow of a continent that was leaning dangerously into a higher plate. The low, terrible shriek of glass tearing against glass was already vibrating through the airless gap between them. The jyrneth stood there, head bowed, preparing to step off the edge and let its body be the mortar in the crush.

Before the jyrneth could fall, the power stepped up beside it. 

The power took the form of something made of the sky-void itself—a silhouette of soft gravity and deep-space quiet. It did not speak. To speak to a jyrneth is meaningless. Instead, the power offered its hand. 

The jyrneth, sensing a presence that carried no sharp edges, slowly turned its shallow basins of dark eyes. It raised its arm. The power laid its palm flat against the exposed stone seam on the jyrneth’s forearm. 

Through that touch, communion flowed. 

The jyrneth shared the horrific, majestic pressure of the plates, the constant hum of a million tons of stone begging to yield, and the frantic little ticks of the shelled rib-creatures drinking its light-blood along its jawline. 

In return, the power poured something new into the jyrneth. It poured *restoration*. 

The power did not take away the jyrneth’s burden, for the burden was its purpose. Instead, it gave the jyrneth the gift of an infinite well. A divine resonance slipped into the slow-moving veins beneath the white dust skin. The light in the jyrneth’s veins shifted from a pale, slow lightning to something richer—a warm, golden luminescence that tasted of starlight and unbroken quiet.

The shelled creatures along the jyrneth's brow fed on this new light. Instantly, their amber armor flushed with a gentle gold. As their secretions crystallized, a new flavor seeped into the jyrneth’s sensory awareness. 

For the first time since it was forged from layered memory, the jyrneth did not just taste the cold, metallic dread of the world’s teeth grinding. It tasted warmth. It tasted the idea that it was not alone. It tasted gratitude.

The collision came. The world plate lurched. The jyrneth stepped out over the abyss, suspended in the lethal zone between crashing surfaces, and the power stepped with it.

When the continents met, they did not exact their usual toll on the shepherd. As the sheer mass of the plates bore down, squeezing the jyrneth to soften their impact, the golden light flared. The dust and flakes of stone that sheared off the jyrneth’s body did not fall away into the great, endless cloud floor below to be lost forever. 

The power had changed the wind beneath the worlds. 

The void between the plates was no longer just an empty drop. It had become a cradle. The particles of Jyrneth-dust that broke away were caught in an upward draft of celestial gravity. The wind gathered the shed layers of memory and breathed them gently back toward the jyrneth. 

As the worlds settled—sighing softly into a new, painless alignment—the dust settled back onto the jyrneth’s shoulders, reforming the stone seams, lengthening the fibrous hair, refilling the veins. 

The power gave them a cycle. They would still break to soften the world, but now, the world would breathe them back together. 

This celestial grace cascaded across all of Caelira. Upon a billion tectonic edges, wherever a jyrneth stood with one hand pressed against the crying stone, a warm golden undertow slipped into their veins. 

If you walk the plates of Caelira now, you still feel the profound, terrifying vertigo of a world in motion. You still hear the muffled pressure in your bones when continents swing toward one another. And you still see the jyrneth—tall, quiet, stretched thin—standing at the edges of the abyss.

But you notice a change. 

When you rest your fingers against their stone seams, you do not just feel the slow roar of breaking world. You feel a deep, steady thrum of infinite resilience. When you look at the cracks they leave behind—the ones that bleed into the ground and become new veins—you see that the light does not fade. It pulses, golden and deeply alive.

The benevolent power did not end the grind of Caelira’s teeth, because to live is to endure the friction of existence. But it ensured that the caretakers of the world would never again be worn down to nothing. 

The jyrneth still turn screams into sighs. But now, when they fall into the chasm to ease the breaking of the world, they do not just fade into the dark. They bloom. And the deep, resonant sigh of the colliding world sounds entirely, beautifully, like a song.
