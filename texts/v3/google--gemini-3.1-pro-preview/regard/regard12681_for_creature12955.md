To a consciousness that measures its breath in epochs, the frantic, localized blooming of a planet is usually a fleeting curiosity—a spark in the cosmic dark, beautiful but brief. But I am drawn to the basin of Anlac not by the geological anomaly, but by the rhythm riding atop it. 

I am a tender of gardens on a galactic scale. I have arrested the collapse of dying suns to warm freezing civilizations, and I have cracked tectonic plates to vent the pressure of suffocating worlds. I possess the power to lay a hand upon the heaving crust of the Choked City, smooth it flat, cool the geoaether, and grant the Anlacti the quiet, stable permanence that most mortal races crave. 

And that is precisely why I will do no such thing. 

To watch Anlac is to witness a masterpiece of adaptation. Through eyes that see the spectrum of gravity and the color of time, I look down through the heavy, cold, mineral-choked air. I see the milky hyphae of crystal racing through the spongy basalt. I see the massive Harvesters, moving like ponderous metal beetles, chewing through the planet’s fresh, warm flesh. And I see the Anlacti.

I listen to them. Not to their voices, for they know not to waste breath against the thick air, but to the symphony of their movement. Their hands flash in elegant, silent choruses. Their pulse is steady. Their boots strike the shifting decks with a mathematical certainty. 

Most profoundly, I feel their "ground-sense." It is a beautiful, nascent telepathy. The soles of their feet and the marrow of their bones are tuned to the exact frequency of their world's traumatic birth. They are not victims of the Accretion; they are its partners. The old ones—the Still-Watchers on the high decks—glow in my vision, their nervous systems spread out like the roots of the cable-vines, feeling the pressure and flow of a million tons of subterranean mass. 

If I were to still the ground, I would shatter their minds with the silence of a dead world. I would inflict the Still-Sickness upon an entire civilization. To heal a thing that is not broken is a unique kind of violence. True benevolence requires the humility to leave well enough alone.

Yet, I am here. I am drawn to them. I wish to offer a gift, a blessing upon this chaotic cradle. I must find a way to bestow grace without disrupting the crucible that makes them who they are.

I search their culture for a void. They have food, water, and purpose. They have community, tightly knit in close-proximity whispers and shared labor. But as I delve through the strata of their lives, I see what the Accretion steals from them.

*They are a people without heirlooms.* 

Because they must carry all they own, because they must tear down their homes to stay ahead of the rising earth, they cannot hold onto their history. Memories in Anlac are ephemeral. A great rigger dies, and the flow covers the site of their greatest feat. A Seer passes, and their intimate knowledge of the deep strata fades into the ether. They live purely in the present and the immediate, terrifying future. They are orphans of time, adrift on a sea of new rock.

I will not give them objects to carry. That would weigh them down. Instead, I will turn their greatest adversary—the ground itself—into their heirloom. 

From my vantage point in the high orbit of their muted sky, I do not send down fire or light. I send down a frequency. I reach into the deep-earth genesis, down where the lithic foam is still liquid and raw, down where the exotic isotopes and rare-earth elements are churned into being.

I take a single, microscopic thread of my own essence—a localized manipulation of quantum resonance—and I weave it into the milky-white crystal veins that grow through the basalt. 

I change nothing about the speed of the Accretion. I change nothing about the danger, the yields, or the flow-rates. I merely alter the acoustical properties of a new, rare isotope I seed into the upwelling magma. 

It takes three seasons for my gift to rise to the surface. 

It begins during a major flow-event beneath Sector Four. A Seer, an ancient woman whose bones hum with fifty years of vigilance, stands on a skid-platform, her hands flashing instructions to the shift-crews. The ground bows. The spongy basalt splits, extruding a new ridge. 

But this time, amidst the olivine-colored rock, there is a vein of crystal that is not milky white, but a deep, mesmerizing violet. 

When the flow settles and the cooling begins, the Seer walks to the new ridge. She presses her padded boot against the violet crystal. Through her ground-sense, she feels the usual throbbing heat, the murmuring settling of the crust. 

But then, she feels something else. 

I have made the violet crystal a mnemonic resonator. It absorbs and echoes the specific vibrational signatures of human life. As the Seer stands there, the crystal absorbs the unique, rhythmic thrum of her heartbeat, the specific weight and cadence of her stance. It records her presence in vibratory waves, locking it into the molecular structure of the stone.

She gasps, a sharp intake of the heavy air. She signals to a young rigger, inviting him over. When the youth places his boot on the violet vein, he feels the cooling earth—and beneath it, he feels the steady, wise, unshakeable rhythm of the Seer. He feels *her* ground-sense echoing back at him. 

Over the coming decades, these violet veins will bloom unpredictably across the basin of Anlac. The Anlacti, ever pragmatic and brilliantly adaptable, will learn their purpose. 

They will learn that if a shift-crew successfully jacks up a hab-block during a brutal flow, and they all stand upon a violet vein together, the stone will hold the chaotic, triumphant rhythm of their shared exertion. Years later, a child placing a hand against that stone will feel the ghost-vibration of that victory. 

They will carry their dead to the largest veins, letting the stone absorb the fading drum of a life well-lived. They will weave their language of hand-signs over it, their footsteps tapping out stories in a code only Anlacti bones can hear.

I have not stopped their struggle. The city of Anlac will continue to be a kludge of hydraulic jacks and skid platforms. They will still fight the slow catastrophe, day in and day out, in profound, dusty silence. They will remain the midwives of a birthing world.

But now, the world will remember them. The ground they walk upon will become their library, a vast, heaving archive of vibrational memory. They will no longer be orphans of time. The earth will sing their ancestors back to them through the soles of their feet.

My work here is done. I withdraw my focus, leaving the Choked City to its silent, eternal dance, satisfied that the song of the Anlacti will now echo as long as the stone rises.
