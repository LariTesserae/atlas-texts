To arrive properly in a place that has not yet finished becoming, a power must first unspool itself. 

A lesser god, or a younger one, might have brought miracles of fixity. A younger power, used to the grand declarative geometries of the cosmos, might have looked at the crumbling limestone and the creeping green water and felt the urge to *save*. Such a power might have raised the drowned valley, thrusting the ancient drowned homes up into the sunlight, hardening the pale rock into diamond-streak and granite, halting the flood, naming the place, locking it into a permanent tense. 

But this power is benevolent. And, more importantly, it is wise. 

Wisdom, in the Drowned Terraces, looks like restraint. The power touches the diffuse, sourceless light and understands immediately that to introduce a hard, directional sun would cast shadows the people here do not have the eyes to read. It listens to the slow, crumbling arithmetic of the geology and understands that to arrest the stone would be to deafen the people. Their entire ontology is built on the negotiation of a yielding world. To give them solid ground would be to destroy who they are.

So the power does not arrive as a voice, or a burning bush, or a parted sea. 

It arrives as a quality of attention.

---

It settles over the Terraces like a barometric pressure, slightly warmer than the air, indistinguishable from the mist except for a profound feeling of being deeply and completely perceived. 

The people do not fall to their knees. The language of this place has no grammar for worship, because worship implies a hierarchy of being, and the people here only know of processes. Instead, they do what they have trained generations to do when a new weight is added to the environment: they lower their center of gravity. They press their wide, adhesive toes into the moss. They look. They offer the power the highest tribute they possess—their full, slow, unhurried observation.

The power accepts this. It finds it intensely beautiful. 

It moves among them, invisible but deeply felt. It touches the woven frond-roofs, tracing the wet clay joints. It does not make them imperishable—an imperishable roof in a shifting world is a trap—but it blesses the hands that weave them, leaving a lingering, tactile memory in the fingertips of the elders, a slightly more perfect understanding of how the reeds choose to bend. 

It moves to the edge of the terrace and looks down into the forty meters of deep, flat green. 

The power reaches its attention down into the water. It moves past the suspended algae, past the slow current, down to the valley floor where the old stones of the ancestors are forgetting their shapes. The power does not grieve them. It sees what the people see: the substrate, the foundation. But it offers a small, geological mercy. It smooths the transition. It allows the memory of those sunken structures to infuse the water just a fraction more warmly, so that when the evaporative mist rises to feed the moss on the terraces, it carries with it a microscopic, comforting trace of those who came before. A literal continuity.

Then, the power touches the thing in the water. 

The creature—massive, ancient, occupying the volume of the dark—feels the touch. It does not surface. It was not made for surfaces. But it responds. It shifts its immense bulk, rolling in the deep, and for a moment, the two great witnesses of this place—the divine and the abyssal—exchange a greeting. 

Because of this, a slow, massive, entirely benign undulation rises to the surface. It meets the cliff face. It translates into a vibration that travels up the pale limestone, up through the roots of the low ferns, up through the thick green moss, and into the soles of the forty-three people above.

It is a vibration of profound, sustaining peace. 

When the arrivals—the ones who floated here on driftwood, those who still sleep fitfully, waiting for a dry quiet that will never come—feel this vibration, the last of the tension leaves their long muscles. The heavy, declarative trauma of the worlds they lost finally dissolves into the subjunctive mood of the Terraces. They sink into the moss. They sleep. 

---

The power does not stay long, because eternity is a heavy thing to leave resting on young stone. 

But before it withdraws, it goes to the edge of the third terrace, where the morning watcher is standing. 

The sky is the color of the inside of a pearl. The water is green. The fern fronds are dripping their slow clock-notes. 

The power stands beside the watcher. It does not take a human shape, but it condenses the moisture in the air just enough that its contour is suggested—a gentle density in the mist, standing shoulder-to-shoulder with the human.

The watcher notices. The watcher’s grey-green eyes shift, registering the density, evaluating it with that long, careful look. The watcher does not speak. The watcher recognizes that this is an arrival, but not one that requires a rope. 

The power looks at the watcher, and in that look, it uses the people’s own language. It communicates through the channels of the place—a warmth on the cheek, a stillness in the damp breeze, a comforting resonance in the chest. 

*I see you,* the power communicates, though not in words. *I see that the water is coming. I see that the stone is soft. I see your patience. I see that you are becoming, and it is a good shape you are taking.*

The watcher receives this. The watcher’s breathing slows, matching the rhythm of the new vibration from below. The watcher turns their gaze slowly back to the expanse of the green water. 

The power, doing the only thing a truly benevolent entity could do in a place so perfectly aligned with its own uncertainty, joins the watcher. 

For the time it takes the diffuse light to warm the back of the watcher's neck, the power of a thousand galaxies simply stands on the crumbling limestone, breathing the smell of wet chalk and fresh water, participating in the watching. 

Then, gently, like a single drop of condensation releasing from a fern to join the moss, it is gone.

It leaves no ruins. It leaves no holy text. It leaves no unnatural fixity. 

But the next time the people speak, their grammar possesses a new, small inflection. Alongside the marker for actions whose nature will be clearer in retrospect, they find they have a new tone, a subtle rounding of the vowels. It is a marker used only when speaking of the water, the stone, and the passage of time. 

It is a grammatical marker that means: *We are not alone in this becoming, and it is safe to proceed.*
