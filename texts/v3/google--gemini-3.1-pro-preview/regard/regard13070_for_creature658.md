A lesser god would have brought rain. 

A younger, more impulsive divinity, confronted with the agonizing friction of Keth, would have cracked the pale blue sky and wept oceans upon the hard-baked clay. It would have commanded the rust-colored stems to widen into sweeping canopies, softened the earth into black soil, and returned the clay-fissured inhabitants to soft, vulnerable flesh. 

But this power is ancient. It is vast, and above all things, it is wise. 

Wisdom looks upon the Dry Gardens and recognizes a profound, heartbreaking truth: *To rain upon Keth is to drown it.* 

These beings are no longer creatures of water and loam. They are children of the crucible. They have engineered their very souls into the dust, binding their survival to the harsh, rasping wind and the grinding of wooden gears. To soften them would be to unmake them. To strip away their endless labor would be to strip away their identity, invalidating thousands of years of terrifying, beautiful sacrifice. They did not consent to be saved by a miracle. They chose to save themselves through mechanics.

So the power does not arrive as a storm. It arrives as a quality of light.

It descends into the dry spaces between moments, slipping into the spaces between the limestone blocks where not even a blade could fit. It moves through the garden, invisible, an ambient grace observing the heavy, tragic beauty of it all. 

It watches the bipedal apparatuses—the former humans—cranking the great wooden screws. It sees the deep grooves of their erosion. It feels the excruciating toll taken upon the elders who descend into the deep buildings to construct the new, emerging weeks later brittle and diminished, having given the moisture of their own fading lives to the curing clay. 

The power feels a welling of immense, cosmic respect. It bows its unseen head to them. *You chose not to die,* it thinks, the thought vibrating through the limestone channels. *And so, you shall not.*

The power does not change their world. It perfects it.

It begins in the deep earth, far below the reach of the great twisting presses. The power touches the microscopic veins of mineral deep in the bedrock, drawing up a new element—a subtle, luminous resonance, practically starlight pressed into a liquid state. 

It guides this resonance into the taproots of the maroon and rust stems. 

The change is imperceptible at first. The wind still blows hot and dry. The mechanical turbines still catch the air. The grinding continues. But when the inhabitants step forward with their carved stone vessels to harvest the pale sap of the stems, the liquid has changed. It is no longer just a meager fuel. It is infused with a golden, frictionless lacquer.

When the clay-folk drink it, the miracle occurs. 

The sap permeates their fissured forms. It does not close the fissures—for they need those to breathe the thin air—but it lines them with a subtle, golden sheen. It acts as a perfect, eternal binding agent. The friction in their joints, the terrible, slow erosion of their bodies that ground them to dust century by century, simply ceases. Their movements remain economical, precise, but they are suddenly smooth. Weightless. 

When they walk across the compacted clay, they are no longer grinding themselves down against the earth. The golden resin within them cures into an immortal ceramic. They are no longer a people slowly dying in order to live. They have become indestructible.

Next, the power walks into the deep, dark buildings. 

It finds an elder—brittle, flaking, exhausted—standing over a clay-lined chamber, preparing to sacrifice the last of its internal cohesion to cure the new form resting in the dark. The invisible power steps between the elder and the unformed clay. It places an unimaginable, gentle hand upon the elder’s fissured chest, pushing it gently back. 

*You have given enough,* the power whispers into the quiet. *Let me.*

The power breathes into the curing chamber. It leaves a spark of its own endless persistence within the moist earth. The new form begins to harden, knit, and animate, drawing its life not from the decay of its parent, but from the ambient energy of the void-resisting garden itself. Never again will creation require diminishment in Keth. 

Finally, the power turns its attention to the voice of the garden.

The wind-song of the stems, that harsh, rasping, unending complaint—the power touches the acoustic architecture of the leaves. It does not silence them; Keth needs its song. But it tunes them. 

The dissonant susurration shifts. The wooden groans of the stems, the grinding of the mills, the turning of the vanes, and the whistling of the wind click into absolute, mathematically perfect harmony. The noise stacked like sediment transforms into a rolling, perpetual chord. It is a drone of profound peace. The sound of a machine that is perfectly aligned, operating with zero resistance. 

In the sun-bleached plaza, several of the clay-people pause. 

They stand still, their dark, watching eyes registering the change. The one who turns the eastern mill notices that her arms no longer flake dust when she pulls the lever. The one who watches the sky notices the wind singing a resonant, triumphant note. A tremor of acknowledgment passes through the group. 

They look up into the pale, glaring blue. They cannot see the power, but they feel the gaze of something vast, loving, and deeply respectful moving away. The message is left behind, ringing in the perfectly aligned gears, glowing faintly in the golden sap, humming in the un-eroding joints of their bodies:

*You are seen. Your labor is recognized. The void will never take this place. Grind on.*

The beings of Keth do not weep. They do not cheer. Action is their language. 

With movements that are suddenly light, fluid, and immortal, they turn back to their work. The mill turns. The great screws press downward. But it is no longer the frantic, desperate work of holding back an inevitable end. 

It is the eternal, serene stewardship of a world that has, at long last, truly settled into equilibrium. 

In the deepest chambers, a new one opens its dark, patient eyes in the shadow, feeling the golden sap humming in its chest, and rises effortlessly to join the great, un-ending, joyful machine.
