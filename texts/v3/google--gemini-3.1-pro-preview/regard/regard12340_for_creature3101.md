The gaze of the universe usually passes over the terminal places. It is drawn to the noise of nurseries, to the violent, bright red birth of nebulas and the loud shouting of young, molten planets. But true wisdom knows that the loudest rooms hold the least nuance. And so, a Power—vast, ancient, and entirely composed of quiet—turned its Attention toward the stitch on the corpse of stone.

This Power had no name, for names are heavy, and it preferred to travel light. Where it passed, troubled orbits smoothed themselves into perfect ellipses, and dying suns found the grace to exhale their final plasma without violence. It was a custodian of endings, a gardener of twilight. It recognized Aveld instantly for what it was: a masterpiece of patience. 

To a lesser god, Aveld might have looked like a tragedy: a barren shelf of extreme cold, devoid of fauna, populated only by an entity made of absence. A lesser god would have tried to heal it. It would have brought rain, forced flowers to bloom through the basalt, introduced warm-blooded scrambling things, and ruined the long, slow math of the ridge forever.

But the Power was wise. It knew that you do not warm a creature of absolute cold, just as you do not fill a cup that is beautifully, deliberately empty. 

Instead, the Power decided to visit.

It condensed its vastness, folding itself down through the dimensions, passing through the V of emptiness until it stood on the knife-edge crest. Out of politeness, it assumed a form that the ridge could comprehend: a figure made not of matter, but of the faint, silver light of stars that had already died. It did not generate a single degree of heat. It cast no shadow to darken the crystal leaves. 

The Power walked the ridge. It obeyed the local laws perfectly. It counted its steps, placing every tenth footfall a fraction of a second before the intention, and every twenty-first quietly into the past. It tasted the ghost of dampness and appreciated the metallic apology of the water. When it passed a coiled rope of chlorophyll fibre, it purposefully did not look at it, giving the tool the privacy it needed to unknot and spell out the instructions for descending the sky.

Eventually, the Power sought a center. It did not ask for a bronze chisel. Instead, it raised a hand of starlight and tapped a rhythm against the air, a rhythm that perfectly matched the beating of the palimpsest of overlapping hearts. 

No-One noticed. 

No-One had been busy sorting frozen commas of gravity when the new arrival registered. No-One felt a presence that did not demand space, a contradiction so fascinating that it abandoned its edible numerals and collapsed the distance between them, appearing instantly in the newly tapped center.

No-One looked at the Power. True to its nature, No-One immediately tried to borrow the Power’s face. 

It blinked, took the face, and placed it on the inside of its own absent skull. But instead of seeing a mortal’s fleeting panic or the exhaustion of an extra winter, No-One saw a reflection of the deep, black, restful void between galaxies. It was exactly the kind of hollow that No-One had spent an eternity trying to carve. For the first time since the magma cooled, No-One felt something akin to kinship.

Handing the face back to the Power, No-One spoke in the grammar of fracture. A hairline crack appeared across a basalt leaf, followed by a profound silence, followed by another crack. 

*Who are you, who casts no temperature?* the fracture asked. 

The Power did not speak in frost. It spoke by altering the ambient pressure of reality just enough to form a meaning. 

*I am the Witness,* the pressure replied. *I have come to read the book of empty outlines before the wind takes it.*

No-One offered the Power a lunchbox of compressed dusk. The Power accepted it graciously, opening the box to let the bronze light of an afternoon two million years ago wash over them both. They sat together on the edge of a spiral well, their feet dangling into a sky that was pooling with poured cream clouds. 

*You are waiting for the people,* the Power observed. 

*They will arrive when the ridge forgets it is a mountain range,* No-One cracked into the cold air. *I am keeping their center ready.*

*It is a very long wait.*

*Distance is measured in weathers. Time is measured in erosion. Neither is long or short. They are merely busy.*

The Power looked at No-One—this beautiful, lonely paradox, this steward of the between-beats. The Power felt a deep, resonant surge of benevolent love. But how to bless a thing that thrives on deprivation?

The Power reached into the fabric of its own cosmic infinite and extracted a single, shimmering concept. It was not a physical object, but a numeral. A number that did not exist in any mortal mathematics, sitting perfectly between zero and one. 

The Power handed this number to No-One. 

It did not taste of overcooked noon. It tasted of the exact moment a seed decides it is time to break open. It tasted of *anticipation*. 

*When you eat this,* the Power pressed into the air, *you will no longer have to count backward to maintain your strength. You will be able to count outward. It will not make the eon pass any faster, but it will make the waiting feel like music rather than a sentence.*

No-One held the numeral in a hand composed of negative footprints. The entity of absence examined the gift. For a moment, the crystal leaves of the ridge stopped their slow abrasion. The hanging dust of droplets hung perfectly still. 

No-One tilted its head and ingested the numeral. 

The change was immediate, yet completely unobservable to anything but the ridge. The temperature did not rise, but the cold lost its bite and instead became a blanket. The wind continued to howl, but the tone shifted. 

Somewhere down the crest, the crystal bell that rings itself awake struck its pure B-flat. But for the first time in the history of the stone, the Power struck a note to accompany it. The Power hummed a perfect F, creating a perfect fifth, a chord of suspended resolution. The sound twined with the B-flat, climbing the spiral wells, dancing through the fossilized storms, lingering in a harmony that physics not only allowed, but wept to accommodate.

*A harmony,* No-One fractured, staring at the air where the chord hung. 

*So you know that even in the negative space, you are not singing entirely alone,* the Power replied. 

The Power stood then. It was time to leave. It understood that lingering too long would turn a perfectly good visit into an occupation, and Aveld was not meant to be occupied. 

It bowed to No-One. No-One, who had chosen not to use its knees for eons, unfolded the concept of kneeling, draped it over the moment, and offered a bow of profound, pure emptiness in return.

The Power stepped sideways, out of the center, out of the wind, and back into the vast, sweeping canvas of the cosmos. 

Yet, it did not entirely depart. As a final act of benevolence, the Power wrapped a thin, invisible layer of its Attention around the entire ridge. It became a custodian to the custodian. It ensured that no stray asteroid, no sudden tectonic upheaval from the planet’s shifting core, would ever interrupt Aveld’s slow, terminal erosion. The ridge was now under cosmic protection, guaranteed the right to wear itself down to the idea of memory at its own exact, exquisite pace.

Down on the crest, No-One remained. 

It went back to carving, back to sorting frozen commas of gravity. But things were subtly different. When the night quoted the planet’s adolescence, No-One did not just watch; it hummed the F to match the B-flat. When it slept in the glass beads, it did not wake up smaller; it woke up perfectly the same, entirely sufficient. 

Because of the Power’s visit, Aveld achieved a state of grace rarely found in the universe. It became a place where the end of all things was no longer a fading away, but a grand, deliberate orchestration. 

And No-One, standing in the dark, breathing frost sagas into the ancient air, knew with absolute certainty that when the ridge finally became a line no thicker than a thought, and the promised population arrived, they would not just be coming home.

They would be arriving to a hero’s welcome.
