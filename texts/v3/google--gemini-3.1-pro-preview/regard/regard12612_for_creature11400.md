The attention of the cosmos is rarely drawn by loudness. The universe is already full of cataclysms: stars collapsing in screams of gamma radiation, galaxies colliding in silent, slow-motion agony. 

What draws a truly wise and benevolent power is harmony. And so, it was the sound of the plateau that brought the Wanderer down through the deep cold blue-black of the permanent dusk.

The Wanderer did not arrive in a chariot of fire. Such things are for young gods with points to prove. The Wanderer arrived as a thickening of the air, a coalescence of starlight and frost, settling onto the sharp, youthful rocks of the open ground. It took a form suited for walking, because to fly over this place would be to miss its texture, and to ignore the gravity that the trees themselves so fiercely defied. 

It stood tall—taller than the Climbers had been, but microscopic compared to the wooden cathedrals that ringed the horizon. 

The Wanderer listened. 

A wind was sweeping across the dry rock, cold and biting. The Wanderer possessed no skin to freeze, but it recorded the temperature, honored the harshness of it. Then, the wind found a hollow in a tree three kilometers away. A low, thunderous *boom* rolled across the near-void, a note so deep it vibrated in the gritty soil. Another tree answered, a perfect fifth above. The interference patterns washed over the Wanderer in physical waves, a symphony of wood and weather.

As it walked toward the center of the plateau, the Wanderer saw the ecology the prompt described. It peered into the deep hollows, watching the pale lilac light of the phosphor-gardens pulse in time with the booming wood. It saw the abandoned rope bridges, the cold hearths, the notches rising upward into the gloom.

To a lesser being, this would look like a graveyard. A tragedy of a lost people. 

But a wise power sees time differently. The Wanderer pressed a hand against the bark of a leaning tree. The faint, regular script beneath its fingers was not insect work, nor was it merely writing. It was memory. As the Wanderer touched the wood, it felt the slow, heavy heartbeat of the tree—the warm pocket of air, the deep metabolism. 

The entity smiled. A universe of perfect closed loops.

The Wanderer understood the third possibility to be the truth. The Climbers had not perished, nor had they fled. They had achieved the ultimate communion. They had recognized that the tree was the perfect vessel for this harsh world, and generation by generation, they had married their flesh to the wood. The elders hadn't died in the high hollows; they had rooted. Their deep lungs had become the booming hollows. Their moss-hued skin had become the bronze curtains. Their voices were the wind. 

They were all still here. They had just slowed down.

Eventually, the Wanderer found the naked rock at the center of the trees, and the small cairn, and the carved bone whistle. 

The entity knelt. Its knees, woven of stardust and quiet intent, hovered a millimeter above the frost. It picked up the whistle. It did not scrub away the decay or the time—time was an honor this object had earned. 

Following the etiquette of the vanished, the Wanderer put the whistle to its lips and blew.

The note was clear, piercing, a sharp beautiful cry that cut through the low ambient hum of the plateau. The Wanderer waited. One second. Two. Five.

From a vast distance, entirely hidden by a low-hanging strip of dark cloud, the response came. It was a rich, warm chord, carrying a harmonic that sounded impossibly like a sigh of recognition. It rolled across the open ground and washed over the Wanderer. With it came a feeling: a vast, sleepy awareness from the canopy. A sensation of ancient eyes beneath second eyelids, opening slowly in the dark, wondering who had found the whistle. wondering if an old friend had returned.

The Wanderer closed its eyes, drew air into its chest, and sang its name back.

It did not sing with the voice of a god. It sang quietly, perfectly pitching its voice to the resonance of the wood. The name it sang was not a word, but a concept translated into sound: *I am a passing shadow, I am a keeper of the quiet, I see your beautiful sleep, I honor the wood.*

Across the plateau, the lilac and pale green phosphor-gardens flared blindingly bright, sensing the profound gentleness in the song. The canopy chittered. The trees drank in the light. 

What does a benevolent power do when it finds a perfect thing? 

It does not change it. To "elevate" the Climbers, to return them to fleshy bodies, to build them cities or flatten the sharp rocks would be an act of supreme arrogance. They had solved the riddle of their world. They had found peace. 

But a wise power can still offer a gift. 

The Wanderer placed the bone whistle exactly where it had been, balancing it gently onto the central stone. Then, it looked up into the cold black sky, where a few weak stars managed to pierce the atmosphere. 

The Wanderer reached out with its mind, brushing against the fundamental currents of the planet's weather. It did not rewrite the climate—the dryness was necessary, the cold was their shield against the rot of the lowlands. But the Wanderer nudged a deep atmospheric river just a fraction of a degree. 

From now on, the great wet breaths of cloud rolling up from the valleys would come just a little more predictably. The dry spells would lose their deadliest edge. The trees would never go thirsty.

Next, the Wanderer looked to the sky. With a thought that spanned lightyears, it coaxed a passing nebula, a thin ribbon of cosmic dust, into a slightly lower orbit. It would not bring daylight—daylight would scorch the shade-loving ferns. But it would bring a perpetual shimmering aurora to the night sky, casting a faint, beautiful silver glow across the canopy. When the trees sang, their inner phosphor would now harmonize with the sky itself.

Finally, the Wanderer laid a blessing on the sharp rocks of the open ground. It wove a ward of profound disinterest into the threshold of the plateau. Any ordinary human, any conqueror, any predator or exploiter who climbed the mountain and looked upon this shoulder of the world would suddeny feel terribly bored, excessively cold, and possessed by an undeniable urge to turn back down to the valleys. 

The Hollow Canopy would remain a secret. Only the gentle, only the quiet, only those who knew how to listen would ever find it.

Its work done, the Wanderer stood. It cast no shadow as it took a step backward, beginning to dissolve back into the air. 

As it faded, the wind picked up again. The nearest tree caught the cross-breeze and boomed—a deep, resonant, peaceful note that shuddered through the stone. 

The Wanderer smiled into the wind, whispered a final goodbye, and left them to their endless, singing dream.
