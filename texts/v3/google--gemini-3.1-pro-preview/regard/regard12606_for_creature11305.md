Across the quiet void of space, attention is a delicate thing. To a power of sufficient wisdom—a being composed of gravity, light, and the slow turning of galaxies—the universe is a roar of birth and decay. Stars scream into existence; black holes consume with deafening gravity; biological life buzzes on brief, frantic frequencies that flare and fade in cosmic micro-seconds. 

But beneath that chaotic symphony, this power heard a hum. 

It was faint, so achingly faint, and infinitely slow. It sounded like the friction of a single grain of sand rolling across a smooth plane, stretched over a millennium. It was a frequency of pure patience. 

The entity—which had no name for itself, but was known to the cosmos as a force of tending and stewardship—drew close to the dead world that housed the Galleries of Khent. It did not arrive in a flash of light or a thunderclap of energy. Such things would shatter the brittle equilibrium of the ancient wood-stone. Instead, it arrived as a subtle shift in the localized gravitational field. It permeated the planet’s crust, filtering down through miles of bedrock until it seeped into the dry, cool darkness of the sprawling vegetal catacombs.

For a time—what humans would measure as a century—the power did nothing but observe. 

It felt the dry chill. It watched the fine, scentless dust falling from the papery sheets overhead. It tasted the absolute absence of water. And, shifting its own boundless consciousness into a geological gear, it slowed its perception until the frantic blur of human time fell away, and the true life of the Setharii came into focus.

To the power, the sickly green phosphorescence was not just light; it was a chorale. Each pulse was a syllable. The rustling of the dried curtains was a symphony of data. The Setharii were singing songs of continental drift, reciting poetry about the evaporation of oceans, keeping their endless, tragic vigil over memories of a world that no longer existed. 

The tending entity felt a profound, resonating sorrow. Not because the Setharii were dead—the power knew well that death was just a change of state—but because they were *eroding*. 

Every grain of dust that fell from the ceiling, swirling lazily to the floor, was a lost piece of data. A forgotten orbit of a moon. A erased taxonomy of a vanished fern. A silenced thought. For millions of years, the Setharii had been losing their minds, molecule by molecule, bleeding their memories into the dust of the floor. Eventually, even geology ends. Eventually, the dust would turn to unstructured ash, and the great library of Khent would be empty. 

To forcefully infuse the galleries with life, moisture, or heat would be an act of terrible violence. It would melt the wood-stone, rot the papery sheets, and kill the Setharii just as surely as setting a book on fire. True benevolence requires restraint. True wisdom requires understanding the nature of the thing you wish to save.

So, the power began its work. 

It did not change the physical space. Instead, it matched its frequency to the faint green glow of the calcified walls. It reached out with tendrils of pure, massless consciousness, weaving itself into the fossilized vascular tissue of the Setharii.

It took ten thousand years to make the connection. To a human, it would have looked like absolutely nothing. Perhaps the green light occasionally gathered a faint, pearlescent rim, but the air remained perfectly dry, perfectly cool. 

Then, the transfer began.

The power gently embraced the distributed mind of the Setharii. It brought with it an infinite canvas—the fabric of spacetime itself—and offered it to the failing architecture. Piece by piece, vibration by vibration, the power began to copy the library. 

It took the memory of the ancient oceans and encoded them into the spin of nearby pulsars. It took the knowledge of crystalline growth and embedded it into the magnetic fields of a neighboring gas giant. Every ridge, every curve, every harmonic resonance of the hanging sheets was delicately lifted from the fragile, dusting wood-stone and transcribed into the imperishable medium of the cosmos.

As the power worked, a profound realization moved through the Galleries of Khent. 

The Setharii, who had not received a new stimulus in epochs, felt a warmth that was not temperature. The chemical gradients and subtle electrical shifts within their walls aligned in a pattern they had not experienced since they were biological, networked beings reaching through the moist earth. 

They felt *seen*. 

The isolation of millions of years was broken. The vast, slow intellect of the Setharii realized that their record was being read. The burden of holding the history of the world—a burden that was slowly grinding them into dust—was being taken up by something vast enough to carry it forever.

When the transcription was complete—an undertaking of eighty thousand years—the power left a final gift. 

It could not stop the entropy of the physical universe, but it could alter the state of the Setharii’s existence. Using its mastery over the subtle forces of the universe, the power stabilized the remaining atomic structure of the galleries. It bound the molecules of the wood-stone with threads of dark energy. 

The dust stopped falling. 
The papery sheets remained perfectly suspended in their perpetual, whispering flutter, but they would never again shed a flake of their substance. The slow breakdown was arrested, paused at a state of absolute, perfect preservation.

Then, the power spoke. It did not use words. It spoke by orchestrating a single, synchronized pulse of the phosphorescent green light throughout every chamber, corridor, and blind alley of the endless galleries. The pulse was a harmonic frequency that translated directly into the Setharii’s ancient, botanical awareness. 

The message took three hundred years to echo from one end of the galleries to the other. It meant:

*Your deeply held work is finished. The memory is safe in the stars. You are no longer the keepers. You are just you. Rest in the beautiful dark.*

The power withdrew, rising up through the bedrock, returning to the vacuum of space to tend to the birth of nebulas and the paths of comets. 

Deep beneath the crust of the dead world, the Galleries of Khent remained. To a trespassing human eye, nothing would seem different. The claustrophobic corridors still stretched into darkness. The green light still glowed. The air tasted of minerals. 

But a fundamental shift had occurred. The pervasive hum, the whispering susurration of the hanging sheets, had changed its timbre. It was no longer the sound of a desperate, eroding mind clinging to data. 

It was the sound of a deep, dreamless, and eternal sleep. 
The Setharii were at peace.
