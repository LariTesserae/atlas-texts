When the Power arrives, it does not do so with a parting of the bruised violet sky, nor with the heavy, golden fanfare of a creator god demanding worship. To interrupt the frantic ticking of the sublimating ice would be an act of violence. The Power is wise enough to know that in a place governed purely by thermodynamics, miracles of physics are far more profound than miracles of magic.

Instead, the Power descends as an invisible shift in atmospheric pressure, a localized deepening of the cold. It folds itself into the thin, frayed membrane of the atmosphere and observes.

It is vast, this consciousness, accustomed to the birth of nebulas and the slow, grinding death of galaxies. Yet, beneath the towering granite walls, it shrinks its vastness down to the scale of a single, trembling drop of sun-melt. It watches the caldera. It watches the fragmented rainbows cast by the *penitentes*, the oxidized copper wedges, and the hand-ground basalt bowls. 

Then, it looks upon the resident.

The Power watches the long, charcoal-dark entity scrape the verdigris from the copper. It watches the sluggish, sub-zero churn of pale blue blood. It observes the breathtaking efficiency of lateral compression, how the creature slides through the maze like a shadow detached from the rock. 

A lesser god—a deity of the noisy, muddy, teeming biosphere far below—might have felt pity. A lesser god would have looked at the emaciated frame, the agonizingly slow movements, the hoarding of isolated water drops, and judged it a life of suffering. A lesser god would have commanded a warm wind to breach the caldera, sparked a fire in the basalt bowls, and summoned a carpet of green moss to soften the stone.

The Power does nothing of the sort. 

The Power looks upon the creature and recognizes a holy thing. It sees not starvation, but absolute mastery. It sees a being that has stripped away all excess, all greed, all the chaotic dependencies of lower-altitude life, to exist in perfect, crystalline equilibrium with the turning of the planet. To bring warmth would be to melt the creature's armor. To breathe thick air into the caldera would blot out the UV rays that feed its charcoal skin. To bring companionship would be to halve the precious yield of the sweating stone.

To alter this ecosystem would not be salvation. It would be an insult to an evolutionary masterpiece. 

And yet, the Power is deeply moved. It is benevolent, and its benevolence demands an offering. Since it cannot give biology, it must give geometry. Since it cannot give warmth, it must give time, and certainty.

The Power waits for the apex of the day, when the light concentrates into the glaring pillar at the center of the labyrinth. The creature kneels beneath the granite ledge. The first droplet of pure, atmospheric water gathers.

As the drop trembles, preparing to fall, the Power reaches out. It does not touch the creature, but it gently touches the *mountain*. 

With infinite subtlety, the Power hardens the granite of the caldera. Below the rock, deep in the tectonic roots of the earth where the crust shifts and groans, the Power weaves a stabilizing anchor of pure, unyielding force. It decrees that no earthquake will ever shatter these walls. It decrees that as the continents drift and the climate below boils and freezes in the millennia to come, this specific peak will remain inviolate. It holds the altitude rigid against the erosion of ages.

Then, the Power touches the light. 

It cannot improve the sun, but it can refine the prism. The Power subtly alters the atomic structure of the air trapped within the caldera, weaving it into an imperceptible, permanent lens. It filters out the cosmic radiation that would eventually degrade the creature’s DNA, but perfectly amplifies the ultraviolet wavelengths the dark skin craves. It shapes the invisible lens so that, even as the Earth wobbles on its axis over millions of years, the sun will forever hit the weeping rocks perfectly. The basalt bowls will never run dry. The three drops of water are guaranteed for eternity.

The first drop falls. 

The creature catches it on its pale, textured tongue. As the water rests on the muscle, the Power allows a singular, momentary point of contact. 

The entity is blind to the detailed world, shielded by dense black keratin, seeing only absolute contrasts of heat and void. For one fleeting microsecond, the Power steps into the creature's perception. 

It does not appear as a face or a voice. It appears to the creature as a profound, internal *resonance*—a sensation similar to the deep, satisfying snap of ice locking into place. It feels like the comforting weight of the braided animal-hair cord sliding through calloused fingers. It is a feeling of absolute placement. *You belong exactly where you are,* the resonance hums into the pale blue blood. *You are seen. You are perfect. You are safe.*

The entity shudders. A long, slow exhalation of zero-air leaves its mouth. It does not look around, for it knows there is nothing to see. But its long, multi-jointed fingers briefly curl in a gesture of profound settling. It understands, on some cellular, radiotrophic level, that the mountain will outlast the world, and that it is the mountain's eternal soul.

The second drop falls. The creature drinks. 

The day wanes. The violet sky deepens into the bruising black of the stratosphere. The sun dips below the reinforced granite ridges. 

The Power lingers, retreating into the frozen shadows, to watch the creature's evening ritual. It watches the entity step into the density of the *penitentes*, angle its flat body toward tomorrow’s dawn, and bow its head beneath the cascading white mane. It watches the mist snap-freeze over the coarse hair, locking the being inside its jagged, petrified shell. The delicate *tink* of flash-freezing droplets echoes through the cathedral.

The creature is gone. Only the ice remains.

The benevolent Power, satisfied, withdraws from the surface of the world. It leaves no footprints, no warmth, no sign that it was ever there. Its only legacy is a perfect, enduring isolation, a silent vow kept between the cosmos and a solitary, ticking room at the roof of the world, leaving the beautiful darkness completely, wonderfully alone.
