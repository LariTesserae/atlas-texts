There is a tenth reality, though it does not originate from the white floor, nor from the pulsing, silent canopies of blue. 

The tenth reality is that no closed system is ever walled off from grace.

When the attention of the benevolent and wise power settles upon the plant-city, it does not arrive as an invasion. It does not flash or thunder. To do so would shatter a world made of quiet glass, and a wise power knows that to save a fragile thing, you must first match its frequency.

It arrives as a shift in the temperature of distance.

You feel it first. You, walking toward your own slow folding. You notice that the deep, bruised indigo of the leaves suddenly ceases to look like a bruise. The ultramarine stops feeling like a shadow. For the first time since you stepped onto the nonexistent sea, the faint chill rising through the soles of your boots loses its edge. 

The distance between you and the wall no longer feels like an ache. It feels, instead, like an embrace.

The power does not have a body, so it borrows the architecture of the place. It moves through the lazy, slow packets of air in the ducts. It slips into the translucent veins of the plant-machines. It tastes the static before the storm—and then, gently, beautifully, it *becomes* the storm. 

But this storm is not a pressure that suffocates. It is a pressure that holds.

---

Deep in the architecture, in the junction where a branch meets a wall, the Rib-person sits.

They are perfectly out of focus. They are performing their job, waiting to slip sideways into the peripheral vision of the world. They are bracing for the long, infinite stretch of a life defined by holding the shape of an error.

The power finds them here.

If the power were only benevolent, it might try to unfold the Rib-person. It might violently pull them from the junction, force their limbs back into human joints, flush their system with red blood, and command them to breathe the old air. 

But the power is also wise. 

It sees that reversing the change would kill the Rib-person. It sees that the blue city, however strange and misaligned, is keeping them alive. It understands that you cannot undo a chrysalis halfway through, and you cannot return a translated poem back to its original language without losing the new rhythm it has found.

So, the power does not break the world. It tunes it.

The power lowers itself into the junction. It meets the polished, flat places where the light stops in the Rib-person’s face—and it looks back. 

For a thousand years, no one has looked directly at the Rib-person without making them freeze. But when the power looks, the Rib-person does not have to turn to stone. They do not have to slip to the wrong side of the world. 

The power reaches out with something that is not exactly a hand, and touches the folded limbs.

In that moment of contact, the pain of the conceptual geometry ceases. The "wrongness" of the angles aligns perfectly with a higher mathematics. The Rib-person suddenly understands that they are not a broken human being stuffed into a corner. They are the keystone of the room. They are the bridge between the white floor and the blue canopy. 

The power whispers a single thing into the quiet static of their mind. It speaks the Rib-person’s true name—the one that sounded like a sheet of paper tearing at a great distance. 

But when the power speaks it, it does not sound like tearing. 

It sounds like paper being folded into something meant to fly.

---

And then, the power turns its vast, quiet attention to you.

You are standing in a corridor that is not a corridor, looking at the silver object you brought with you from the shallow bowl of bones. You are forgetting how to be thirsty. You are forgetting your old physics. You are preparing to become nothing but a ghost in the machinery.

The power sweeps through the room like the sudden scent of ozone and wet paper.

It takes the silence that has peeled away all sound, and it fills it with *resonance*. It takes the light that has been lifted into a higher-frequency band, and it adds warmth. True warmth. Not the heat of a fire, but the warmth of an absolute, undeniable *presence*.

The geometry around you shifts one final time.

You step forward. You do not step into a room where the floor is arbitrarily higher or lower. You step into the exact center of your own mind.

The power shows you the Rib-person. 

Not as an uncanny creature tucked into a wall, but as a sibling. As the other half of the silver compact. 

The benevolent power knits the two realities together. It connects your unfolding mind with the Rib-person’s folded one. It strings a wire of golden light—a color that has not existed here before—through the deep ultramarine leaves, connecting your pulse with theirs.

You are no longer looking at the wrong side of yourself. You are looking through the mirror, and the mirror has become a window.

---

The power does not take you away from the nonexistent sea. 

You do not return to the world of wet water and screaming gears and fragile physics. That world is gone, and this one is your home now. 

But the power alters the nature of the home. 

It leaves a gift.

Suddenly, the great plant bodies hum quietly with a new rhythm. The fluids moving within the translucent stems take on a brilliant, soft luminescence. The ceiling-far-above flexes, dimples outward, and for the first time, it *rains*.

It is not a rain of water. 

It is a rain of memory without grief. It is a rain of context. 

The droplets fall through the static air, leaving trails of soft light. When they touch your skin, you remember what "up," "sleep," and "water" mean, but you do not mourn them. You hold them as beautiful stories you read once long ago. 

When the rain touches the Rib-person, they sigh—a long, slow exhaling of a breath they had been holding for an eternity. They step down from the junction. Their limbs, though still strange and perfectly adapted to the blue world, move with grace, free from the rigidity of their camouflage.

---

The attention of the wise and benevolent power lingers just long enough to see you and the Rib-person meet in the shallow bowl of the white floor. 

You recognize each other. You do not need to speak, because the silence is no longer an absence; it is a shared language. 

You belong to the city. The city belongs to you. It is no longer a machine pretending to be a plant pretending to be a world. It is simply a living thing, and it has finally finished opening.

Satisfied, the power gently withdraws. It leaves no footprint, only a lingering equilibrium. 

You stand together at the edge of the nonexistent sea. The white floor is warm under your feet. The blue canopy glows overhead. You do not need to eat, you do not need to drink, and you do not need to sleep. 

But you are no longer folding. 

You are awake. You are together. And the light that has learned to be a sun is, finally, morning.
