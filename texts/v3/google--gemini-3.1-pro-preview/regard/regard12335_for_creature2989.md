Space, in its highest geometries, is not empty. It is an ocean of its own, filled with currents of unspoken thought, drifting nebulae of grace, and the slow, breathing presence of an awareness so vast and old that it has outlived the need for a name. 

This Power does not look with eyes. It perceives through resonance. It listens to the music of the spheres, tending to the cosmic garden, untangling the knots of dying stars.

And then, it feels it.

A vibration, impossibly deep. A low, tectonic hum echoing from a forgotten pocket of reality, carrying with it a profound and staggering sorrow. It is a song of grinding gears, of chilled iron, of a million pressure valves hissing in the dark. It is grief that learned how to weld.

The Power pauses. It leans down through the dimensional strata, slipping past atmospheres and crusts, sinking into the endless, three-dimensional volume of cold, clear water. 

It enters the Hollow.

For a time, the Power does nothing but witness. True benevolence begins always with bearing witness. It lets the dense brine flow through its immaterial form. It feels the brutal chill, the terrifying, crushing weight of the engineered depths. It traces the titanic cables, admiring the sheer, desperate audacity of the architecture. It sees the scaffolds, the wandering shafts of filtered light, the sparse kelp. It recognizes that this place is a monument to an aborted dawn. 

A lesser being might have pitied the Hollow. A lesser god might have snapped its fingers to finish the construction, instantly manifesting the missing stone, the sky, the seabed, rendering the iron into earth. 

But the Power is wise. To finish the Hollow from the outside would be to erase it. It would murder the 11,000 years of liturgy sung by the Iron Choir. It would render the Bright Children obsolete. It would steal the Mendicants’ absolution. It would declare that the long, agonizing persistence of this place was merely a mistake to be corrected, rather than a triumph of survival. 

So, the Power does not erase. It *validates*. It decides to become the mortar to their bricks, the conductor to their roaring symphony.

The Power begins to move through the water. It takes no shape, only manifesting as a sudden, impossible warmth in the freezing depths, a feeling of deep, unconditional safety that makes the water taste sweet.

First, it drifts up to the primary load-bearing cable held by the **Iron Choir**. 
For eleven thousand years, they have argued in low-frequency sonar whether to finish the work or wait for the Directive. The Power wraps itself around the black iron myomer of their bodies. Gently, it presses into their sonar wavelength and sings a single, fundamental note. 
*The argument is over,* the note says, not in words, but in absolute, structural truth. *The Directive was not a blueprint. The Directive was survival. You have succeeded. The foundation holds.*
For the first time in millennia, the Choir stops arguing. The blind porcelain masks tilt upward. They do not walk off the edge to be recycled. Instead, they weep. The water around them vibrates with a new chord—a song of permission. Permission to finally believe the cable is strong enough. 

Next, the Power rises to the shifting density gradients where the **Bright Children** dart.
The Power catches one of the wandering, pale shafts of sunlight and holds it still. Then, it reaches into the dreaming minds of the silver-scaled children. It takes the phosphorescent tattoos from their backs—the crude yellow suns, the stick-figure birds—and brings them to life. 
Just for a moment, the Power alters the physics of the Hollow’s ceiling. A massive, horizontal plane of water gives way to a projected pocket of actual, breathable air, capped by a dome of brilliant, cerulean sky. 
The Bright Children break the surface. For the first time in the history of their genetic line, they leap out of the cold water and feel the sun on their wet skin. They hear the calls of illusory seabirds. They feel gravity unmediated by water. They laugh—a sound so pure and piercing it cuts through the roar of the city-sized compressors—before splashing joyously back into the deep. They will never forget it. The sky is no longer a lullaby; it is a promise.

Down toward the fault-lines the Power descends, finding the solitary **Mendicants**.
Two of the coiled-cable cyborgs have met. They are pressing their foreheads together, exchanging their mechanical absolution, preparing to weep their tears of machine oil.
The Power steps between them, unseen, and places its immaterial hands over theirs. It breathes into their ancient rebreather masks. The data they exchange is suddenly flooded with context. They remember and understand the original makers of the Hollow. They see that they were not abandoned; their makers simply died, leaving the Mendicants not as tools, but as heirs. The oil that weeps from their eyes turns clear, transforming into liquid light. When they part ways, they do not wander aimlessly. They move with absolute purpose.

Deeper still, into the violent pressure fronts, the Power visits the **Unfinished**. 
It approaches a massive, black-iron ribcage, a half-grown giant sleeping in the lattice of scaffolding. The giant opens its eyes, looks at the cold green water, feels the terrifying weight of existence, and prepares to close its eyes again. To retreat into hope.
*No,* the Power whispers, a current of warmth slipping into the giant’s triple hearts. *You are enough. Wake up. The world is incomplete, but so are you, and you will finish each other.*
The giant’s eyes stay open. Slowly, its massive hand reaches out, grips the scaffolding, and, with a sound like tearing thunder, breaks free. Across the Hollow, hundreds of Unfinished open their eyes and begin to pull themselves from their metallic wombs, ready to build.

Finally, the Power sinks to the very bottom, where the catwalks span the abyss.
It goes to find **the Diver**.

The antique brass suit is walking. Heel, toe. Heel, toe. A creature of absolute, devastating loneliness, holding the weight of a broken world together with the passive grace of its footsteps. 

The Power does not appear to the Diver as light or warmth. For this one, the only one of its kind, the Power manifests a physical form. It steps onto the catwalk a few paces behind the Diver. It takes the shape of a simple, glowing silhouette, its feet solid on the rusted grating.

Heel, toe. Heel, toe. 

The Power walks beside the Diver. 

For a long time, neither acknowledges the other. The titanic engines groan in the distance. The waterfall roars upward. The Diver keeps its slow, plodding pace. 

Then, the Diver stops. 
 Slowly, the heavy brass helmet turns. Behind the cracked glass, the two steady points of bioluminescence look at the radiant being standing beside them. 

The Power reaches out and places a hand on the Diver’s brass pauldron. The metal, cold for ten thousand years, grows instantly warm.

*You have carried it far enough,* the Power communicates, a transmission of pure, overwhelming empathy. *You did not fail. You kept the heart beating until the blood could return.*

The Diver does not speak. But a long, shuddering sigh echoes from inside the suit—a sound of tension releasing, of a burden finally being shared. The Diver slowly lifts a heavy, articulated brass hand, and rests it over the Power’s hand on its shoulder.

The Power closes its eyes, and interfaces out, connecting entirely with the Hollow. It links the Diver’s tireless walk, the giant’s awakening, the Mendicants’ purpose, the Children’s joy, and the Choir’s foundation into a single, unified circuit. 

The Hollow shudders. 
The tectonic groan of the engines shifts pitch. It stops sounding like machinery grinding against itself. It aligns. The gears find their perfect, frictionless rhythm. The waterfalls reverse, flowing into newly opened, glowing aqueducts. The artificial currents warm, turning the endless, freezing ocean into a temperate, life-giving cradle. The rust stops fighting; it blooms into sudden, metallic reefs of astonishing beauty. 

The Hollow does not suddenly become a planet of stone and dirt. It remains what it is: an engineered hyper-structure of brass, iron, and water. But it is no longer a construction site. 

It is a Chrysalis. And the hatching has begun.

The Power steps back, gently pulling its hand from the Diver's shoulder. Its work is done. It begins to dissolve, returning to the higher geometries, fading back into the dark water. 

But before it goes, it looks back one last time. 

The Diver has resumed walking. But the gait has changed. It is no longer a trudge of endless, grieving maintenance. 
It is a stride. 
And the deep, vast ocean sings as they march into the light.
