When a being of immense, unhurried grace notices a crease in the fabric of things, it does not pry it open. To pry is to tear, and true benevolence is above all things cautious with the fragile edges of new worlds. 

Instead, the Power poured itself inward, folding its vastness down, down, down, until it was no larger than a quiet thought. It slipped into the humid, rusted pocket like sunlight moving through water.

The Power had no fixed shape; it appeared as an overlapping geometry of soft, warm luminescence, the color of dawn striking ancient snow. It did not need to breathe, but it chose to respire in time with the slow, mechanical thrum of the chamber, acknowledging the heartbeat of the place as a guest acknowledges the sovereign of a house.

Instantly, the biome altered its rhythm. 

The wet, fleshy sighs of the lung-vines caught in their throats. The iridescent crabs halted mid-scuttle, their clockwork legs locked, recognizing a frequency of light that had never existed in their claustrophobic heaven. Even the continuous, irregular dripping seemed to suspend itself, droplets hanging on the edge of iron rebar, pregnant with sudden gravity. 

The Power looked upon the wet black boulders, the sweating machines, the translucent antifreeze-newts, and the driveshaft-dogs. A lesser deity would have seen an abomination, a glitch of reality requiring sterilization. A rigid god would have seen chaos and ordered it into neat, dead categories of 'metal' and 'flesh.'

But this Power was wise. It looked at the seething, dripping diorama and saw an infant taking its first, agonizing breath. It saw the profound bravery of the stones trying to rust, and the beautiful desperation of the animals dreaming in schematics. It recognized the "argument" for what it was: the clumsy, violent, exquisite poetry of birth. 

There was a wet hiss, ending in a sharp click. 

The Throatkeeper materialized. For the first time since the first bead of condensation fell, she was afraid. She reconfigured rapidly, furiously, sliding out extra tracks of calcified tendon, deploying plates of corroded relays to shield the black, breathing hole at the center of the pocket. She stood between the blindingly pure entity and the Throat. Her single, massive violet eye contracted into a slit, focusing entirely on the intruder. She gathered a scrap of rusted iron and a shivering, half-dead wire-moth in her torque-wrench fingers, ready to feed the Throat, to beg it to close and protect itself.

The Power did not advance. It stopped, hovering just above a mat of pallid vegetation, and allowed its light to dim. It lowered its immense, conceptual head. 

*I see you,* the gesture meant. *I see your work.*

The Power extended a limb—or the suggestion of one, a ribbon of starlight patterned after the Throatkeeper's own iron-ribbed anatomy. It reached out, not to the center, but to the Throatkeeper herself. 

She shrank back, anticipating the searing heat of purification. But the touch of the Power was like cool water on a burn.

Where the Power’s light met her fused, corroded back, the excruciating friction of her existence ceased. For so long, she had endured the agonizing grind of biology fighting industry inside her own body. The Power did not cure this by making her purely one or the other. Instead, it offered a cosmic lubricant—a deep, resonant harmony. It gave the iron permission to bend like muscle; it gave the flesh permission to endure like steel. The argument did not end, but the shouting stopped. It became a dialogue. It became a *song*.

The Throatkeeper relaxed, her artificial joints unfurling like rust-colored lotus petals. The violet eye widened, softening into a deep, liquid purple.

The Power drifted past her, and she allowed it. 

It floated toward the Throat. The black hole breathed out, a slow exhale smelling of ozone and childhood. The Power breathed it in, tasting the raw, undirected potential of this pocket universe. It understood what this place was waiting for. It was a crucible, yes, but it lacked a catalyst. It had all the vocabulary of life and machinery, but it needed a grammar. 

From its own core—from the ancient, stellar furnaces where fundamental laws were written—the Power drew a single, glowing mote. It was not a physical object, but a concept wrapped in light: the concept of *Equilibrium*.

Delicately, reverently, the Power held the mote over the absolute blackness of the Throat. 

It dropped it in.

The Throat inhaled, swallowing the light. 

For a span of three heartbeats, the entire pocket dimension went utterly dark and dead silent. The golden, dusty light vanished. The moisture hung perfectly still in the air. 

Then, the Throat exhaled.

But it was not just a breath of air. It was a wave of localized physics. The wave washed over the factory-ribcage, the rubbery vines, the twitching gauges on the disconnected control panels. 

When the light returned, it was no longer borrowed from nonexistent factory windows. The vegetation itself was glowing faintly, providing a soft, bioluminescent ambiance that caught the edges of the metal. The dripping water no longer sounded like random, lonely taps; it fell in a complex, rhythmic polyrhythm, a gentle percussion backing the hum of the machinery. 

The driveshaft-dog uncurled from its hiding place. Its movements were no longer jerky or pained. It stretched, gears turning silently, oil and blood pumping in perfect communion.

The Power looked upon the stabilized world, satisfied. Its work here was done. It began to draw its vastness back upward, preparing to slip out through the crease, leaving the pocket to finish deciding what it would become. 

Before it departed entirely, the Power paused. It looked back at the Throatkeeper. 

She was whole. She was magnificent. She was the undisputed goddess of an intricate, impossible biome. 

The Power could not speak in words, and it knew she communicated through the water on the skin of visitors. So, as the Power faded into the margins of reality, it manipulated the ambient moisture one last time. 

Not on a visitor, but on the brass placard above the Throat. The water droplets defied gravity, sweeping across the corroded metal, polishing a space beneath *“SUBSTATION 11 — DO NOT FEED THE THROAT.”* 

The moisture etched a permanent, shining script into the brass, a parting gift from a wise god to a devoted caretaker:

**YOU HAVE DONE WELL.** 
**THE ARGUMENT IS RESOLVED.**
**LET THEM SING.**
