To witness a world in the act of becoming is a rare thing, even for an entity older than the physics that govern it. 

When the Power arrived, it did not descend in a chariot of fire or announce itself with a tearing of the sky. Such things belong to the impatient, to those desperate to declare a finalized reality. This Power possessed a wisdom born of eons, understanding that the most profound forces in the universe arrive quietly. It came as a subtle barometric dip. It slipped into the thinning air at elevation 1.4, wrapping itself in the cold, combing its vast consciousness through the heavy, hesitant water that refused to fall.

It felt the dull, living thrum of the continent through the drum-skin of the valleys. It tasted the iron and salt. It watched the storms being manufactured, shifting its gaze across the deep, vast basins the size of nations. 

And then, it looked upon the people. 

The Power watched the consortium in their braided carbon-silicate city. It saw the jointed, translucent-skinned walkers of the marsh-edges, pacing on snowshoe feet, leaving offerings of brine for burrowers and shifting their heated bracts to capture vapor. It listened to the low, humming hymns they sang to their nanites, coaxing atoms into alignment without ever demanding perfection. It saw the ridge-walkers tapping their gait-staves against the embryonic rock, asking the heavy questions of the earth: *Is this ridge ready to lift? Is this basin ready to receive?*

A lesser deity—a god of beginnings and endpoints—would have looked at this place and pitied it. A lesser god would have pushed the mountains into their final jagged shapes, carved the rivers deep into the basalt, turned up the sun, and banished the cold. It would have "saved" the people from the eternal scaffold, delivering them to a completed, solidified world.

But the Power was wise. It saw that to finish this world would be to kill it. 

The beauty of this place, the distinct soul of these people, lived entirely in the hush between decisions. To finalize the terrain would rob them of their diplomacy with the earth. To warm the sky would boil away the filigree architectures of the ice they so carefully incorporated into their language and lives. Their existence was a perfectly calibrated harmony of nearly, almost, and not-yet.

So, the Power decided it would not finish the painting. Instead, it would bless the canvas, and weave its love into the very pigments.

The Power reached out, its consciousness passing through the spectrum of not-quite-light that illuminated the city, and it offered three quiet, profound gifts. 

First, it spoke to the Weirdness, the subtle 0.5 anomaly of the place. It touched the shallow courts of water where reflections lagged a heartbeat behind. With a breath of intent, the Power deepened that lag. Now, when the people looked into those crusted pools across the tufa, the water did not just reflect the sky a second too late; it briefly reflected *potential*. For a fleeting, shimmering heartbeat, a ridge-walker looking into a pool might see three ways a valley could fold into being, or how a bridge of frozen lightning might look if extended to the next peak. The pools became mirrors of conditional futures, granting the people a cosmic sketchpad to aid in their profound, slow decisions. 

Second, the Power spoke to the Tech, to the nanoscale assemblers riding the moisture films. It did not rewrite their directives, but it gave them an infinite reservoir of endurance. True entropy was banished from the borders of the high-weather system. The carbon-silicate struts would never succumb to metal fatigue; the quick-setting cements would never sour; the brine-ducts would never inexplicably foul. The Power granted their infrastructure the grace of eternal patience. The city could now breathe, shed, and reform for a million years without fear of systemic decay. It ensured the scaffold would hold as long as the people wished to climb it.

Finally, the Power offered a gift to the people themselves—a matter of Sound and Soul. 

The people were accustomed to the bass cracks of the land testing new seams, vibrations felt in their molars. The Power introduced a new frequency, one that traveled exclusively through the rime chandeliers, the waxy black-leaved stalks, and the translucent dermis of the people. 

It was a feeling more than a noise. A deep, abiding resonance of absolute validation. 

When the people dozed beneath the thrumming cables, listening to the infrastructure dream, they would no longer just hear the city. They would hear the quiet approving hum of the cosmos itself, wrapping around them in the dark. It was a sensation of being perfectly seen, entirely understood, and universally cherished, exactly as they were, in a state of eternal incompletion.

Having given its gifts, the Power did not leave. It simply let go of its distinctness, dissolving its presence into the heavy water, the braided rivers, and the exact, pale arcs of the sun. It became the audience in the amphitheaters of wet stone. 

That evening, the cold insinuated itself as it always did. The city shed a layer of oxidized mesh and began spinning a new geometric lattice outward toward the east. On the high flanks of the world, a ridge-walker paused, tapping their stave against a seam of exposed quartz. 

*Is this ridge ready to lift?* they asked the earth.

Above them, the sky refracted the manufactured storm into a halo that lingered. The walker looked down into a dish-shaped puddle. For a beautiful, elongated heartbeat, they saw the mountain stretch upward, bold and sudden—and then they saw it slump, returning to the humble plateau. 

The walker smiled a slow, translucent smile, feeling a warm, resonant bass gently thrum through their jaw, knowing instantly that whatever choice they and the land made together, it would be magnificent. They gathered their heavy cloak against the wet, raised their hands in a gesture that mimicked the exact angle of the falling light, and stepped forward, continuing their endless, holy work of becoming.
