To a lesser god, the first instinct would be to cleanse. 

A well-meaning but foolish power would look upon the Rust Quarter, see the cracked lips of the Riveters and the jagged ruin of the machinery, and weep a miracle of rain. It would flush the ancient pipes with sweet, rushing water. It would summon green grass to shatter the smog, command the sun to pierce the chemical fog, and turn the mechanical graveyard into a lush, pastoral Eden.

And in doing so, it would destroy them. 

The water would aggressively oxidize their homes and seize their companion-automata into statues of rigid death. The moisture would rot the copper-vine and melt the phosphorescent fungi. The sudden silence of a peaceful garden would deafen those who navigate by the heartbeat of the turbines, and the green world would alienate the Conductors, who would find no pulse in a flower. True wisdom knows that a coral reef cannot be "saved" by planting a forest in its place. 

So, when the Power—a vast, benevolent consciousness of light, resonance, and universal equilibrium—turns its eye to the Rust Quarter, it does not bring an end to the metal. It does not bring rain. 

Instead, it brings *harmony*.

It descends not from the clouds, but up through the deep geological faults, rising into the foundation turbines. The Power moves through the district as a frequency, an invisible hum that slips perfectly into the spaces between the clashing gears and the shrieking lathes. It takes a long, reverent moment to simply observe, humbled by the breathtaking ingenuity of the Riveters. It admires the dense bones of the children, the breathtaking arithmetic of the brass spider’s web, and the profound, stubborn beauty of a people who have carved joy out of iron. 

Then, with the infinite delicacy of a master watchmaker, the Power begins to provide its gifts.

**First, it touches the atmosphere.** It does not remove the copper dust—for the Riveters’ modified lungs expect it, and their blood relies on the iron—but it subtly alters the chemical composition of the air. The toxic edge, the volatile heavy metals that cause slow, lingering ailments, are gently transmuted into inert, harmless motes. The air remains an oily, metallic twilight, but it becomes rich, breathable, smelling of clean ozone and warm amber. The coughs that rack the youngest children at night quietly cease. 

**Second, it optimizes the ecosystem.** The Power reaches into the genetic memory of the phosphorescent fungi and the rust-eating beetles. It nudges them toward abundance. The copper-vines suddenly yield thicker, more malleable tendrils with a profoundly higher caloric density. The trace minerals in the weeping synthetic lubricants are synthesized by the Power into a golden, viscous nectar that is equally perfect for oiling a one-armed assembly bot and healing a terrible gash on human skin. 

**Third, it blesses the metal.** The Power cannot stop the crushing weight of gravity or the danger of the machines; to do so would strip the Riveters of their hard-won agility and awareness. But it changes the temper of the district's structural steel. Now, before a beam gives way, before a catwalk drops, the metal *sings*. A clear, resonant chime echoes through the steel milliseconds before a failure, giving the gear-agile Riveters exactly enough time to leap to safety. 

During "the shakes," the Power acts as a hidden shock absorber. It allows the frenetic acceleration, preserves the Convergence and the Harvest festivals, but subtly routes the worst of the destructive kinetic energy safely down into the bedrock. The deep shelters become places of absolute, womb-like safety, their walls vibrating with a soothing, purring frequency that knits broken bones and lulls tired workers into the deepest sleep of their lives.

**Fourth, it meets the Conductors.** The young ones, with their minds pressed deeply into the cold, ancient logic of the factory ghost-code, often skirt the edge of losing their humanity. The Power installs itself as a gentle firewall between their souls and the grinding infinity of the machines. When a Conductor plugs their modified nerves into an access port, they no longer face a freezing void of pure data. Instead, they feel a warm, supportive presence—a guiding hand that helps them translate the binary, protecting their organic minds from burning out in the circuit streams. 

Then, the Power seeks out Old Molten. 

Deep in the labyrinth, where the heat is staggering and the air is thick with grinding gears, the cosmic consciousness meets the emergent soul of the Rust Quarter. There is no awe demanded, no subjugation. The Power greets the patchwork cyborg-deity as a peer, an equal sovereign of this sovereign domain. In a silent exchange of pure mathematics and electromagnetic pulses, the Power leaves a gift at Old Molten's mechanical feet: a single, perfectly spherical bearing of an alloy not found on this world, frictionless and eternal, infinite in its energy output. Exactly what Old Molten needs to keep the heart of the district beating forever. 

Finally, before retreating to a quiet state of observation, the Power touches the tap-code. 

As the Riveters beat their rhythms on the pipes, passing messages of love, danger, and kinship through the district, they notice a change. The sound carries further. The noise of the machinery seems to miraculously part for their messages. The percussion becomes musical, every pipe tuned to a perfect pitch. When a mother taps a lullaby on a copper conduit on the tenth level, her child on the fifth level feels it as a warm, golden vibration that wraps around them like a blanket.

In her cathedral of discarded boilers, Old Thread pauses at her loom. Her copper-nubbed fingers hover over the cable-silk. She feels the shift in the quarter's heartbeat—the sudden, profound easing of hidden frictions, the strange perfection in the air, the merciful absence of the brutal edge that usually accompanies their survival. 

She taps a single, complex rhythm against the floorboards. It translates roughly to: *I see you. Thank you. Leave us to our work.*

And the Power, entirely content, does exactly that. It does not demand worship, for the Riveters already worship the perfection of the cam and the gear. It does not demand obedience, for they must obey the laws of physics and momentum. It simply remains in the spaces between the spaces, a silent lubricant against the friction of their suffering, allowing the beautiful, chaotic, metallic life of the Rust Quarter to spin on—endless, rust-covered, and perfectly, resiliently human.
