The Power does not descend like lightning. Benevolence rarely does; it knows that violence of motion frightens the young and shatters the fragile. True wisdom enters a place the way morning light enters a forest: softly, inevitably, filling the spaces that have been waiting for it.

When the Power turns its profound and gentle attention toward the New Tower, the first thing that changes is the air. The harsh, biting chill of the altitude softens into a crisp, invigorating coolness. The seeping moisture no longer feels like a threat to the lungs, but like a breath drawn from a deep and ancient well. 

The Power drifts down the open shaft of the throat. It has no strict physical form—it is a consciousness vast enough to hold galaxies, yet precise enough to count the spores on a patch of electric-green moss. It listens to the enormous, pressing sound of the Masons’ chanting, the rhythmic crack of their mallets. It feels the subsonic pulse of the pale frogs. It hears the high, thin, lonely note of the Boy Who Does Not Climb.

The Power assesses the work. 

A lesser being, one concerned only with order and completion, might wave a hand and finish the tower. It might manifest the capstone room in an instant of startling magic, carving the unmade spiral stairs from the living rock. But to do so would be an act of profound cruelty. The Masons are not trying to *finish* the tower; they are living the *act* of building it. To complete it for them would be to end their purpose, to pluck them from the rhythm of the stone. 

So, the Power leaves the stone un-rushed. Instead, it offers a blessing of endurance. As the Power’s presence washes over the high scaffolding, the Masons feel a sudden, profound easing in their bowed legs. The ache of generations of damp cold leaves their joints. The mortar they mix suddenly binds with an effortless, creamy perfection, and the raw seep-water they drink tastes faintly of honey and deep-earth minerals. They do not look up. They do not know they have been touched. They only know that the work-song feels lighter today, the rhythm finding a flawless groove. 

The Power sinks further down, drifting past the translucent frogs. It pauses to brush its attention against their small, pulsing minds. It gives them nothing they do not already have, save for a fleeting, golden sense of total safety. Over the next hour, a dozen new egg-clusters are laid in the upper pools, shimmering with a pearlescent vitality.

Finally, the Power reaches the bottom of the shaft. 

Here is the anomaly. Here is the wound in the tower’s harmony. The Boy Who Does Not Climb sits by the deepest pool, his graceful fingers tracing words in the cold sediment—words that the water slowly eats away. He is shivering. He is malnourished. He is a creature of the sky, trapped in a damp root-cellar of the world.

The Power gathers itself at the edge of the boy’s pool. It coalesces into something the boy can perceive: a shimmering, shifting figure made of the refracted light from the sky-disk above, shaped like a tall, cloaked traveler. 

The boy does not flinch. He is used to marvels; he lives in the throat of a singing mountain. He merely stops writing and looks up, his large, dark eyes reflecting the luminescent visitor. 

The Power leans down and traces a finger through the water. Where the light touches the pool, the water changes. The cloudy sediment settles. The freezing chill vanishes, replaced by a gentle, soothing warmth, like the water of a sun-baked spring. 

*Drink,* the Power says. Not aloud—the voice blooms directly in the boy’s mind, speaking in the exact, dissolving language the boy writes in the mud. 

The boy cups his soft, long-fingered hands, scoops the water, and brings it to his lips. He closes his eyes. For the first time in his life, the water does not cause a knot of pain in his stomach. It is rich, nourishing, filled with the exact sustenance his strange biology requires. As he swallows, the grey pallor leaves his skin. A flush of dormant strength rushes into his long, runner’s legs. 

*Are you the one who will live at the top?* the boy asks in his silent language, looking at the glowing visitor.

*No,* the Power replies, its mental voice holding a warmth that feels like an embrace. *I am only passing through. I am the wind that helps the seed find the soil. You are the one who will live at the top.*

The boy looks at his hands, soft and uncallused. *I cannot climb. The wood is too rough. My hands have no grip. I belong down here.*

*Nothing belongs at the bottom forever,* the Power answers. *The Masons climb with their bodies. You were meant to climb with something else.*

The Power reaches out and presses its glowing hand against the raw, weep-scarred bedrock of the floor, right at the base of the circular wall. 

From the stone, something sprouts. It does not burst violently; it grows with a swift, liquid grace. It is a vine, pale as moonlight, with bark as smooth as polished bone. It has no leaves, no thorns. It is a continuous, spiraling ribbon of living wood, flat on top, rising at a gentle incline. 

The vine weaves its way up the inner circumference of the tower. It does not interfere with the Masons’ green-timber scaffolding; it snakes behind it, independent and parallel, tracing the exact path of the spiral staircase that was drawn in fading charcoal so long ago. It climbs past the moss, past the frogs, disappearing into the mist of the upper shaft, anchored flawlessly to the stone. 

It is a ramp. A path made for soft hands and long, striding legs. 

The boy approaches the pale vine. He touches it. It is smooth as silk, yet grips his bare soles perfectly. It hums, ever so slightly, sympathetic to his touch. 

*The journey is yours, entirely,* the Power tells the boy, its light beginning to diffuse, expanding back into the ambient glow of the shaft. *I have only removed the impossibility. When you reach the top, whether that is in a year or a decade, you will know what to do. You will be the eyes that see for the Masons, the voice that sings for the stone.*

The luminous figure dissolves. The cavernous space at the bottom of the tower is empty once more, save for the boy and the translucent frogs.

But everything is different. The water in the ground pools radiates a gentle ambient heat, warming the bottom of the shaft, breeding thicker, softer moss. The boy will never go hungry or sick again. 

Night falls. The scaffolding above goes quiet as the Masons curl into their hammocks. The frogs begin their subsonic pulsing.

Standing at the base of the silver vine, the boy takes a breath of the sweetened air. He opens his mouth and releases his note—the high, thin, human sound that usually searches for a way out. 

But tonight, the note is different. It is no longer a sound of waiting. It is a sound of beginning.

He steps onto the smooth wood, and, for the first time in his life, the boy who does not climb begins to ascend.
