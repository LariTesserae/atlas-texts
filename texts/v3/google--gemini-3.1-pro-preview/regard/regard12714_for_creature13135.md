The Power arrived not from the stars, but from the spaces *between* things.

It was an entity of profound magnitude, a consciousness that had outlived the births and deaths of galaxies, moving through the cosmos like a quiet current. It was drawn to this geographical skeleton not by the deafening, concussive roar of the biological reef—for sound was merely vibrating air, a trivial physics to this being—but by the brilliant, frantic pulse of sheer *will* resonating from the hollow peaks. 

As it settled over the eaten mountains, the Power did not manifest as a body, a face, or a booming voice. Instead, it became an invisible, feeling pressure, wrapping itself delicately around the continent-scale lattice of petrified roots. It spread its awareness through the humid canyons, down into the unseen, steaming depths where the bedrock had once been, and up to the sun-scoured upper canopies where the giant epiphytes held their mirrored pools.

It watched the humans. 

It watched them for a century in the space of a single heartbeat. It felt the resin packed into their ears, understanding instantly the brutal necessity of their self-imposed deafness. It felt the hyper-articulated joints of their prehensile toes locking onto ancient bark, marveling at the tensile strength in their lean, calloused limbs. It translated the chaotic, blinding flashes of their bone-mirrors and read the full-body semaphores of their ultraviolet, painted skin. It saw their woven wasp-nest villages, the terror of a predator-bird's strike, and the terrifying, breathtaking grace of a hunter free-falling into the abyss only to snap their leather wings open to catch the invisible heat.

A lesser deity—a younger, more arrogant god—would have felt pity. A lesser god would have commanded the mountains to fill with stone once more. It would have crafted lush, flat meadows. It would have melted the resin from the humans' ears and silenced the shrieking beasts of the air, seeking to domesticate them into a recognizable, terrestrial peace. 

But this Power was immense, and with immensity comes wisdom. 

To give these people flat ground would be an act of violence. It would be an amputation. It would render their magnificent, prehensile bodies useless, turn their flawless acrobatics into a clumsy shamble, and reduce their brilliant, flashing culture to meaningless pantomime. They were not prisoners of the abyss; they were its absolute masters. They were a masterpiece sculpted by gravity and heat. 

The Power knew that true benevolence is not about removing a creature from its crucible; it is about honoring their shape, and perhaps smoothing the cruelest edges of their reality without disrupting the delicate tension that makes them beautiful. 

So, the Power decided upon a gift—two subtle miracles woven flawlessly into the rules of their suspended world.

First, it addressed the hunt. 

Survival for the people was entirely dependent on the invisible architecture of the thermals. To dive into the abyss was a leap of faith; if a hunter misjudged the location or strength of an updraft, they would fall forever. The Power reached into the atmospheric pressure of the hollow peaks and altered a single, microscopic facet of the humidity. 

It breathed a permanent luminescence into the specific friction of the rising heat. 

Because the humans’ dilated eyes were already hyper-sensitive to the ultraviolet spectrum, the change was an instantaneous revelation. A hunter clinging to a mega-vine, looking down into the miles-deep gulf, no longer saw empty, hazy air. They saw the updrafts. Spires of deep, shimmering violet and swirling gold now traced the thermals, rising like glowing, ethereal rivers from the darkness below. The sky became mapped. They could see the heat snapping and twisting, swirling around the roots. When they unhooked and fell backward into the roaring void, it was no longer a blind gamble; they dove into perfect, glowing columns of light, their leather capes snapping open dead-center in the strongest currents. 

The Power watched as a ripple of absolute joy shuddered through the tribes. High on the branches, painted sentinels flashed their bone-mirrors in new, wild geometries, singing a silent song of awe at the newly painted sky.

Second, the Power addressed their only true sin. The falling. The dead.

For fifty million million years, to die here was to be swallowed by gravity. The bodies wrapped in leaves were given to the void, falling into the eternal dark, lost to the abyss, separated forever from the tribe. It was a lonely, terrifying erasure.

The Power flowed down, down, down, through the miles of hollow wood, through the crushing heat, until it reached the very bottom—the ancient, eroded dust where the bedrock used to be. It laid its hand upon the deep floor. It remade the final destination. 

When the next elder died—a woman whose skin was mapped with the scars of a thousand hunts—her tribe wrapped her quietly in aromatic leaves. Dangling from the underside of a primary root, they loosened their grip. The bundle tumbled downward, shrinking away into the haze. 

But this time, it did not vanish into the dark. 

As the bundle dropped through the lower atmospheres, the Power reached out and held it, disassembling the body not with decay, but with starlight. The fallen elder was dissolved into a cloud of millions of glowing, weightless embers. 

Hours later, caught in the towering, newly visible purple thermals, the embers came rising back up. 

A silent storm of warm, luminescent, golden ash drifted upward through the wooden flutes of the mountain. It spiraled on the heat, lifting through the canopy. The glowing motes settled onto the heavy leaves of the epiphytes; they caught in the woven fibers of the hanging pods; they dusted the dark, calloused skin of the grieving tribe. The air pulsed with a soft, residual warmth. 

The people touched their skin, watching the glowing ash cling to their colorful paints. They pressed their chests to the vibrating wood, feeling the change. 

The abyss was no longer a hungry mouth that ate their loved ones. It was a crucible of light that returned them. The dead no longer fell; they became the very updrafts that carried the living, shimmering in the air, catching the wings of their children and lifting them higher.

Having finished its work, the benevolent Power slowly withdrew, pulling its consciousness back into the quiet currents of the cosmos. It left no temples, demanded no worship, and asked for zero gratitude. It left the noise intact. It left the predators flying. 

But as it looked back upon the eaten mountains, the world was richer. Deep in the roaring, humid void, tiny sparks of brilliant humanity flew across impossible drops, riding glowing rivers of ultraviolet breath, held aloft by the shining, golden memory of their ancestors. They clung to the ancient wood, perfectly adapted, perfectly placed, entirely profound.
