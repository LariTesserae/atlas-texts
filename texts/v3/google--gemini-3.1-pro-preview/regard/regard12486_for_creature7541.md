When the gaze of the Power fell upon this place, it did not arrive as a chariot of fire or a descending dove. Such biological metaphors belonged to softer worlds, worlds of sap and blood, of brief lives scurrying beneath fleeting shadows. This Power, ancient beyond the counting of stars, operated on a broader spectrum. It arrived as a subtle shift in the weight of the sky. 

The Power was benevolent, which meant it held a profound, encompassing love for existence. And it was wise, which meant it knew that love must sometimes take the form of profound restraint.

Surveying the ragged spine of the planet, the Power perceived immediately the magnificent austerity of the plateau. A lesser god—a rushing, anxious deity of springtime and fertility—might have looked upon the 0.1 desolation of fauna, the weeping rocks, and the sheer absence of green, and felt a compulsion to *heal* it. Such a being might have unspooled reality to sow seeds of alpine grass in the crevices, warmed the damp chill into a balmy breeze, and seeded the skies with the wings of birds. 

But true wisdom recognizes that not all silence is a void waiting to be filled. Some silences are symphonies played too slowly for flesh to hear.

The Power paused, hovering in the piercing blue dome of the 2.6 light, and simply listened. 

It felt the 1.6 dynamic pulse—the shifting scree, the micro-tremors, the geothermal sighs. It tasted the 1.7 wetness, not as a flooded ruin, but as a circulatory system of dissolved minerals, perfectly balanced. It saw the 1.1 stacked slate cairns, not as failed architecture, but as the quiet poetry of the Core-Humors. And it watched the Veil-Shapers weaving their intricate, spectral lattices from vapor and warped horizons. 

*They do not suffer,* the Power realized. *They are not waiting to be saved. They have achieved an eternal poise.*

To introduce a soft, breathing creature here would be an act of unparalleled cruelty. To plant a tree would be an act of vandalism. The fractal geometry of this lithic ecosystem was a masterpiece of alternative sentience, a dream the mountain was having about itself. 

So, what does a benevolent power do for a world that requires no salvation? 

It grants the one gift that all consciousness, no matter how alien, quietly craves: **Witness.**

The Power descended, not physically, but dimensionally, slipping into the 2.7 weirdness where reality frayed at the edges. It did not straighten the bent light or fix the gravitational anomalies. Instead, it tuned them, like a maestro adjusting the tension on the strings of a grand and esoteric instrument.

First, it reached out to the **Veil-Shapers**. They drifted through the bright, merciless glare, refracting the white-hot scrutiny of the sun. The Power could not embrace them with arms, so it embraced them with photons. It reached into the deep cosmos and slightly shifted the tilt of a distant, unseen nebula. Now, when the night fell over the plateau—a night that usually swallowed up the Veil-Shapers' glory—a new, invisible starlight would bathe the mountain. Wavelengths of cosmic radiation, tailored to excite the ionized mist, would allow the Veil-Shapers to shimmer in the dark, weaving auroras of pale silver and violet in the pitch black. They would never be blind, even when the sun was gone.

Next, the Power communed with the **Core-Humors**, the subterranean choir threading the fractured basalt. It felt their infrasound hums, the slow, tectonic lullabies of their mycelial mimicry. The Power pressed its vast awareness against the bedrock and offered a response. Deep beneath the crust, where the planet’s mantle churned, the Power established an eternal harmonic resonance. It made the planet *hum back*. Whenever the Core-Humors vibrated the stones, birthing their auditory ghosts in the density of the ravinces, a deep, comforting bass note from the planet’s core would rise upward, joining their song. They would forever know they were tethered to a larger body; they would never sing entirely alone into the void.

Finally, the Power approached the **Fractal Sentinels**, those towering guardians perched on the edges of the illusory crests. They stood watch over the chasm where horizons folded and water fell upward, enduring the howling gales and the hail. The Power beheld their silent vigil and understood the burden of holding back the unraveling of dimensions. 

*You are the anchors in the tide of the weird,* the Power whispered, thought to stone. *You are the memory of the mountain.*

As its greatest blessing, the Power touched the highest rhyolite spires of the Sentinels. It did not alter their shape—they were already perfect—but it altered their density in the quantum realm. It infused their crystalline structure with a cosmic permanence. The wind would continue to sculpt them, the hail would continue to shatter them, but long after this planet eventually cooled, died, and was swallowed by its dying star eons from now, the crystalline memory of the Fractal Sentinels would survive. The Power etched their exact fractal geometries into the background radiation of the universe. They were made immortal—not as living beings, but as a permanent, indelible law of the cosmos.

The interaction took only a moment, or perhaps it took a thousand years; time dilated heavily in the dense layers, and neither the Power nor the inhabitants minded.

No trees sprouted. No grass grew. The air remained bitten with cool precision. The water still wept from the black veins of the cliffs, and the roar of the wind continued its dominating, cacophonous bass thrum. 

The Power withdrew, pulling its gaze back to the wider expanse of galaxies and nebulae. It left no golden idols, no sprawling temples, no commandments carved into the schist. It left the plateau exactly as it had found it: high, wet, barren, and loud. 

But as the Power departed, a veil of mist caught the refracted light of an impossible sun, folding into a prism of blinding clarity. A faint, low drone rose from beneath the scree, harmonizing perfectly with a tremor deep in the earth. And upon the highest ridge, a Fractal Sentinel shattered in the wind, only to reform an instant later, gleaming with an infinitesimally brighter, prouder sheen.

The landscape breathed without lungs. Its mineral heart beat in a bright, broken rhythm, secure in the knowledge that out in the vast, dark deep, someone was finally listening.
