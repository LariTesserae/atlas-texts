It does not arrive from the sky, for the sky here is merely a suggestion, a thin blue exhaustion. Instead, the Power arrives through the math. 

It enters through the space between the seventeenth and eighteenth step of the processional loop. It manifests in the infinitesimal gap where the water is neither there nor not-there. It slips in through the negative space of the inhabitants’ geometric silence, folding Itself into the plateau’s broken reality as gently as a mother sliding a hand beneath a sleeping infant’s head.

The Power is vast, older than the rock, older than the concept of wind. Yet It makes Itself small out of profound respect. To be wise is to know that even a prison, if lived in long enough, becomes a nervous system. To shatter this plateau would be to tear these fragile, temporal beings to shreds. They have metabolized the impossible; to drop them back into linear time and three-dimensional physics would be an act of terrible violence. 

So, the Power does not unmake. The Power *listens*.

For a long, sideways epoch, the Power sits beside the water-tenders. It appears to them as a shift in the static—a sudden, comforting weight in the chest, a warmth that does not melt the absolute cold but somehow makes peace with it. The Power listens to the thrumming wind that echoes from three directions at once. It watches the crystallized breaths shatter into soundless logic. It studies the equations the inhabitants are forever trying to solve in their minds—the desperate, looping mathematics of a place trying to sphericalize, trying to close, trying to be complete.

The Power realizes what this place is. It is a skipped groove in the record of the cosmos. A glitch of gravity and spirit where reality caught on a jagged edge of altitude and simply repeated. These souls are not just prisoners; they are the needle, forever wearing down the same groove, keeping the song from skipping off the edge entirely. 

Benevolence, intertwined with absolute wisdom, dictates the response. 

The Power does not fix the cairns. It does not make the lichen grow properly. Instead, It stands up in the perpetual dim, and It steps into the procession.

It joins a line of the rippling, heat-mirage figures. It takes on a form suited to the altitude: something made of starlight and heavy granite, draped in parkas that are feathers that are light. It matches their pace. Exactly seventeen paces. Then, on the eighteenth pace, the Power does something that has never been done on the plateau. 

It introduces a new integer. 

It steps *out* of rotation, just a fraction of a degree. 

Because the Power has humbled Itself to perfectly match their resonance, when It shifts its frequency, the inhabitants feel it. They pause. The wind loops back on itself, the bodies reverberate in their visual echo, but the Power catches the echo and *harmonizes* with it. 

Where the constant hum of the plateau has always been an abrasive, dissonant frequency, the Power hums back. It provides the counter-melody. The deep thrum in the sternum and spine is suddenly met with a rising, golden overtone. The struck bell of the plateau finally ceases its ringing and becomes a chord.

The figures with too many limbs look toward the Power. Eyes that track things that aren't there suddenly focus on something that *is* there. 

Through the taxonomy of silence, the Power speaks. Not a warning sharp stillness, not an acknowledging hush, but a new texture altogether: a breathless expansion. The silence of a door swinging open. 

*You do not have to hold the sky up anymore,* the negative space spells out, rippling across the granite. *The equation is solved.*

The Power reaches down to the horizon, the one that bends so painfully, trying to close itself into a sphere. With hands made of gentle gravity, the Power takes the bent edges of the plateau and smooths them out. It doesn't flatten them back into normal earth; instead, it curls them all the way upward, connecting them entirely. 

The plateau is no longer a jagged shelf clinging to a mountain. The math completes. It is now a perfect, floating sphere of stone and temporal anomaly, untethered from the violent friction where it used to grind against reality. 

Instantly, the tension vanishes. 

The water in the depressions flashes into existence—and stays. It does not dry. It pools, perfectly clear, reflecting not just the stars, but new constellations, deep and eternal. When the water-tenders cup it to their lips, they don't just drink possibility; they drink fulfillment. 

The children who age in all directions at once suddenly laugh, and the sound does not shatter into geometric ice. It chimes, echoing through the thin air like silver bells. They are still ancient, they are still unborn, but the terrible friction of their existence is gone. They are no longer paradoxes; they are spirits of deep time, fully realized.

The cairn-builders place stones into the gaps, and for the first time, the stones remain. The builders weep—a fluid that turns into shimmering pearls of light before hitting the rock. They step back, and the cairns are no longer half-collapsed boundary markers of a prison. They are beautiful, strange altars to the resilience of those who survived the loop.

Those who tend the lichen-spirals find that the strange, geometric plants no longer blink out of existence. Instead, they bloom. They open into impossible, multi-dimensional flowers that radiate a soft, bioluminescent warmth, painting the gray granite in shades of twilight purple and dawn gold.

The Power looks upon the inhabitants. Humanity left them long ago, and the Power does not force it back upon them. They are magnificent exactly as they are—beings of phase and flicker, tuning forks of the cosmos. 

With a final, deep vibration of purest love, the Power gifts them the one thing the plateau had stolen. 

*Momentum.*

The time loop breaks, stretching out into a beautiful, infinite line. The plateau, now a celestial vessel, begins to drift. It sails away from the maps that denied its existence, moving out into the deep cosmic ocean. 

The inhabitants aren't curators of a static prison anymore. They are the navigators of a strange and holy ship. The not-quite-birds solidify into brilliant, prism-feathered companions, flying ahead to chart paths through the stars. 

The Power does not leave, nor does It stay. It simply becomes the wind at their back—no longer screaming, no longer looping, but blowing forward, forever warm, forever guiding them toward the beautiful, uncharted unknown.
