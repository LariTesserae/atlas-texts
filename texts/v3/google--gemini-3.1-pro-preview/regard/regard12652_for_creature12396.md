The Entity did not arrive with the trumpet-call of a conqueror, nor with the blinding, scorching light of a sun. To do so would have burned the delicate moss, shattered the patient stones, and evaporated the weeping pools. True benevolence does not announce itself by obliterating the nature of what it touches. True wisdom knows that sorrow is not a mistake to be erased, but a weight to be shared.

And so, the Entity arrived as a shift in the light. 

One moment, the mist was the color of tarnished silver. The next, it held the soft, pearlescent glow of the very first breath of dawn. 

The Entity took a form suited to the plateau: a figure of twilight and warmth, cloaked in robes woven from starlight and gravity. It stepped onto the spongy mat of peat. It did not sink, but it allowed the dampness to kiss the hems of its garments. It stood in the center of the weeping tableland and simply breathed. It inhaled the petrichor, the centuries of slow decay, the heavy, waiting silence.

*“You have waited so long,”* the Entity thought, projecting the sentiment not as a sound, but as a feeling of deep, unconditional reverence.

First, the Entity addressed the **Lichen-Speakers**. 
A divine being thinks in galaxies and attoseconds, but out of respect, the Entity slowed its consciousness. It sat cross-legged upon a large, weeping boulder, closed its eyes, and attuned its divine spark to the glacial pace of the plateau. It sat there for seventy-seven years. It did not move. It let the ferns unfurl against its knees. It let the moisture bead upon its face. 

Over those decades, it read the slow chemical pulses of the lichen. It listened to the long, laborious stories of the plateau's birth, of how the land had drawn up all the unwept tears of the world below so that the valleys would not drown in grief. The plateau had made itself a filter for planetary sorrow, and the lichen were its historians. 
In response, the Entity synthesized a single drop of golden sap from its fingertip, pressing it gently into the lichen network. It was a chemical sentence that took a decade to bloom: *“Your vigil is seen, and your history is honored.”* A pale, luminescent gold began to trace the edges of the lichen's blue-gray veins.

Next, the Entity rose and walked among the **Stone-Weepers**.
For millennia, their eroded faces had stared blankly, crying the bitter waters of forgotten grief. The Entity walked from stone to stone. It did not stop their crying—to stop crying before the grief is spent is to turn the heart to glass. Instead, it placed warm, radiant hands on either side of their gouged, sorrowful visages. The Entity kissed the foreheads of the stones. 
As its lips touched the mineral, the temperature of the rock shifted just a fraction. The water seeping from their eyes turned from cold and stagnant to warm. They were no longer tears of despair; they became tears of relief, of unburdening.

The **Hollow Wind** sighed, a long sound of anticipation. The Entity sang back to it. Not with words, but with a resonant harmonic hum that filled the empty spaces between the air molecules. The wind caught the melody, wrapping it around the gnarled, stunted trees. The trees seemed to straighten, just a fraction. 

Drawn by the harmonic warmth, the **Moss-Walkers** emerged from the mist. 
They drifted forward in their verdant shrouds, halting mere feet from the Entity. The Entity knew their nature—they would dissolve if touched. So, it did not touch them. Instead, it offered them a purpose. The Entity reached up and pulled a handful of starlight from its own halo, crushing it into a fine, glowing pollen. It blew the pollen into the mist. It settled upon the shoulders and heads of the Moss-Walkers, crowning them in soft, golden fire. 
*“Do not wander aimlessly anymore,”* the Entity imparted to them. *“You are the gardeners of this holy sorrow. Tend to the moss. Guide the waters.”* The Moss-Walkers tilted their featureless heads, filled suddenly with a gentle, glowing purpose. They turned and began to deliberately clear the dead fronds from the ferns, moving in a synchronized, beautiful dance.

Finally, the Entity approached the pools. 
The **Drowned Reflections** were there. As the Entity leaned over the water, a face appeared below the surface—a reflection of the Entity itself, but twisted in agony, mouth open in a silent scream, hands pressing desperately against the underside of the water's skin. 

The Entity knew the rule of the plateau: *Do not drink from the pools. The water remembers what it has swallowed.*

It understood now. The water had swallowed the trauma, the lost memories, the violent endings, and the unspoken regrets of a million lifetimes. It was too heavy for the plateau to process. It was trapped in an endless loop of waiting, because there was no one strong enough to bear the memory and stomach the grief. 

No one, until now.

The Entity knelt. It smiled at the trapped reflection, a smile of infinite, heartbreaking compassion. It pressed its radiant hands flat against the surface of the water, aligning its palms with the hands of the shade beneath. 

Then, the Entity cupped its hands, broke the surface, and lifted the water to its lips.

The Moss-Walkers stopped. The Hollow Wind held its breath. The Stone-Weepers ceased their seeping. 

The Entity drank.

It drank the cold, heavy water. In a single swallow, it took in wars that had faded from history, children who had wandered into the dark, lovers who had died apart, and the quiet, crushing loneliness of a billion ordinary lives. The pain was absolute. It was a poison born of pure sadness, enough to shatter a mortal mind into dust.

The Entity choked. Its radiant form flickered, turning gray, then black, as the abyss of the water surged into its divine core. For a terrifying, silent moment, it seemed the Weeping Plateau had conquered a god.

But darkness cannot extinguish infinite space; it can only give the stars a place to shine. 

Deep within the Entity's chest, a furnace of pure, unconditional love ignited. It took the agonizing memories, held them close, validated their pain, and then—through the alchemy of divine grace—forgave them. It metabolized the sorrow into peace.

The Entity opened its eyes. They were shining like twin suns.

It exhaled a breath of pure, warm vapor. 

The surface tension of the pools broke with a sound like a cracking crystal. The barrier was gone. The water was no longer dark; it was crystal clear, revealing a bottom lined with smooth, opalescent stones. 
The Drowned Reflections, their memories finally borne by a mind capable of carrying them, lost their physical shapes. They turned into ribbons of glowing, mist-like light, rising up from the pools in swirling helixes. They danced around the Entity, brushing against its robes in gratitude, before dissolving upward into the sky, joining the cosmos from which they had been long exiled.

**The Aftermath:**

The Entity did not leave the plateau instantly, nor did it dry the land. The plateau remained a high, mist-laden tableland. The ground remained a spongy mat of moss and peat. 

But the mist was no longer a shroud; it was a warm, comforting blanket, glowing with a perpetual, soft luminescence. 
The Stone-Weepers continued to cry, but where their warm tears struck the earth, tiny, pale blue flowers—*Tears-of-Morning*—sprouted, emitting a scent of vanilla and ozone. 
The Lichen-Speakers continued to tell their slow stories, but now they spoke not just of the long waiting, but of the Arrival, and the Drink. 
The Moss-Walkers tended the glowing gardens, their starlight crowns guiding them through the silver fog. 

The Weeping Plateau still wept, for mourning is a holy and necessary thing. But it no longer wept in despair. It wept the tears of someone who has finally been held, finally been understood, and finally been told that it is okay to let go. 

Its long wait was over. It had become a sanctuary.

Satisfied, the Entity kissed the wind, faded into the newborn dawn, and left the plateau to its beautiful, healing peace.
