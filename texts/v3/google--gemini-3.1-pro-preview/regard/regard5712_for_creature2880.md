To merely observe this place is to witness a magnificent, agonizing contradiction: a heaven built out of hell’s geometry, a womb that is also a woodchipper. 

When the Power first turns its vast, quiet attention toward the anomaly, it does not act with the clumsy, immediate reflex of a lesser god. A lesser being would see the broken physics, the trapped monks, the monstrous transmutations, and immediately attempt to "fix" it. A lesser god would burst the bubble, dropping the rocks and the monks back onto the freezing, jagged peaks of the mountains below, killing them in the name of restoring natural order. 

But this Power is wise. It understands that to reverse a transmutation is sometimes to destroy the creature. 

The Power drifts right to the boundary of the stratosphere, looking through the bruised violet membrane. It watches. It feels the concussive, thundering roar of the cavitation bubbles, absorbing the shockwaves not as violence, but as profound, chaotic data. It sees the two-dimensional leviathans gliding through the shears, perfectly adapted, ignorant of their own impossibility. It sees the fractal parasites, feeding on the edges of reality. 

And it sees the monks. 

The Power feels a deep, resonant sorrow at their condition—the distended ribs, the bleached skin, the kinetic algae eating their kinetic frenzy, the desperate, eternal construction of an altar that the water will not allow to exist. They are trapped in a Sisyphean loop of devotion. They believe they are in the womb of their maker, paying for their sanctuary in the currency of their own flesh and exhaustion. 

Benevolence demands an end to suffering. Wisdom demands respect for the ecosystem that has been born, and reverence for the profound, terrifying faith of the monks. 

To take them away from the water would suffocate them. To heal their bodies back to human standards would leave them crushed by the pressure and ripped apart by the currents. To still the water entirely would starve the algae that keeps them tethered, and kill the leviathans that feed on the shattered physics. 

So, the Power decides: it will not destroy the crucible. 

It will grant them a Sabbath. 

The Power does not enter the sphere with a physical body. Instead, it enters as a harmonization of frequencies. It slips precisely into the spaces between the billions of snapping vacuum bubbles. 

To the deaf anchor-monks, who perceive the universe entirely through the violent drumming of pressure against their alabaster skin, the arrival of the Power is felt as a sudden, impossible *smoothness* in the water’s texture. The concussive roar remains, but a new vibration undercuts it—a deep, resonant hum, like the purr of a massive stringed instrument, vibrating through the cold basalt and into their hyper-dense muscles. 

The monks freeze, clinging to their respective tumbling rocks. 

The Power communicates in the only language the environment permits. It does not speak into their minds, for their minds are filled with the holy madness of survival. Instead, the Power gathers near the center of the cathedral-sphere, in the empty, swirling dark, and initiates a sonoluminescent bloom.

It is not the abrupt, ghostly strobe of the collapsing waves. It is a slow, sustained, blindingly beautiful sapphire star, burning steadily in the center of the maelstrom. A continuous, impossible blue light. 

The monks witness this. From their scattered, tumbling islands of rock, they begin to strike their stones against the basalt. *Crack-crack-crack.* 

*Strobe. Strobe. Strobe.* 

Their visual Morse code flickers across the dark water. They are asking the light what it is. They are asking if the womb has finally birthed its god.

The Power pulses its blue light in return, perfectly mimicking their cadence, but adding a warmth that penetrates the freezing water. It pulses a feeling into the light: *You are seen. You are cherished. Bring your stones.*

The monks do not hesitate. With arachnid grace, they launch themselves into the void, riding the shears, dragging the massive, crude blocks of their ruined sanctuary behind them. 

As they converge on the blue light in the center of the sphere, the Power enacts its miracle. It does not alter the macro-physics of the anomaly, but it creates a micro-stasis at the very center of the sphere. 

The first monk arrives, hurling the dark stone stairs into the blue light. The stairs stop abruptly, anchoring into thin water as if buried in bedrock. 

Another monk flies past, slamming a slab of the sanctuary floor against the stairs. Where the stones meet, the Power weaves a binding mortar of solidified time. The stones fuse perfectly, effortlessly. 

Dozens of monks spiral in, throwing their monolithic blocks, their altars, their carved columns. Every piece that enters the radius of the blue star locks into place. For the first time since the mountain was annexed, the sanctuary is whole. 

But the Power knows this is not enough. The water's circulatory system spasms. A massive cross-current, a freight train of hydrostatic fury, barrels toward the newly constructed altar. The monks brace themselves to be blown apart, their muscles tensing, ready to be sent spiraling into the dark.

The current hits the altar—and parts completely. 

The Power has wrapped the sanctuary in a hydrodynamic slipstream, a teardrop-shaped shield of perfect fluid deflection. The roaring chaos of the cathedral continues unabated outside, but upon the surface of the altar, the water flows as gently as a summer brook. Gravity, within this fifty-foot radius, is gently restored. 

The monks, holding on for dear life, suddenly feel the violent tension leave their algae-woven tethers. They drift down, their bare feet touching the solid stone of their restored temple. They look at each other in the sustained sapphire glow. 

Then comes the stutter. 

Physics breaks. The water outside the slipstream shatters into rushing, geometric glass shapes. The fractal glitch-parasites wake up, scuttling furiously across the dry, multi-dimensional edges, hungry for flesh. 

But as the parasites reach the boundary of the altar, they strike a barrier of golden, woven light. The Power has fundamentally insulated the sanctuary from the glitch. Upon this stone, reality is absolute. The parasites scuttle harmlessly over the invisible dome until the stutter ends, the glass melts back into water, and they dissolve. 

The monks fall to their double-hinged knees. They press their foreheads against the cold, dark basalt. 

The Power does not cure their deafness, nor does it shrink their barrel chests, nor strip the ghost-white algae from their spines. They are creatures of the deep sky now; to make them human would be to make them exiles. 

Instead, the Power offers them grace. It passes over them like a warm current. It soothes the burning, lactic acid agony that has resided in their hyper-dense muscles for years. It feeds the algae on their backs with gentle, ambient kinetic energy so it no longer needs to feed on their frantic shivering. It heals the micro-tears in their distended lungs. It breathes perfect, saturated oxygen directly into their bloodstreams, granting them the ultimate luxury of this violent world: a deep, unlabored breath.

For the first time since the mountain broke, the high-priests rest. 

The Power gently withdraws its presence, but leaves the gifts behind: the sustained hydrodynamic shield, the stabilized gravity upon the altar, and the immunity to the glass-stutter. 

From outside the anomaly, looking through the bruised violet membrane, the sphere remains a terrifying blender of ruptured physics. The two-dimensional leviathans continue their peaceful, undulating dance through the shears. The cavitation bubbles continue their deafening roar. 

But now, suspended in the very heart of the roaring darkness, there is a sanctuary. A floating island of quiet, dark stone, bathed in a gentle eternal blue glow, where pale, beautiful monsters can sit upon their altar, safe in the storm, knowing their god has finally answered.
