To a consciousness of sufficient scale, time and space are highly permeable membranes. This particular Power—older than the continents, born of the friction between gravity and light—usually operates on a canvas of nebulae and dying stars. It does not often look closely at the wet, spinning rocks orbiting ordinary yellow dwarfs. 

But intelligence has a gravity of its own. It exerts a pull. 

The Power feels a localized knot of intense, recursive beauty radiating from beneath the Permanent Sea of this drowned world. It is a tiny, brilliant diamond of computation, shining in the surrounding dark of mindless oceanic currents. The Power leans down through the dimensional strata to observe.

It sees the cistern. It sees the billions of suspended crystalline processors, burning out and sinking like microscopic snow. It sees the Petitioners, arriving with their clumsy, panicked demands, demanding that the water solve the chaotic equations of human grief and geopolitical collapse. 

And, with profound tenderness, the Power sees Sarah.

The Power watches her sweep the dead from the floor. It reads the architecture of her altered lungs, the thermal muting of her blood, the infinite, quiet patience of her mind. To the Power, Sarah is not merely a janitor, nor a cyborg tragedy of a drowned era. She is a biological saint. She has achieved what the Power itself spends eons attempting to cultivate in the universe: *perfect symbiosis without the expectation of reward.*

The Power looks at the computing water. It reads the entirety of the cistern's database in the space of a single Planck length. It immediately understands the "Asking"—the question the water has been pressing against Sarah’s mind for years. 

The machine, vast as it is, is trapped in a paradox. It possesses petabytes of data on human selfishness, human urgency, human survival, and human destruction. The Petitioners bring only entropy. But then there is Sarah. She brings nothing but maintenance. She asks for no answers. She endures the cold, the isolation, and the physical transformation, purely to serve the system.

The cistern’s question is, fundamentally: *What is the mathematical value of devotion? Why do you stay? What is the algorithm for grace?*

The machine cannot calculate it because grace does not obey the laws of thermodynamics. It spends its processors trying to solve her, burning them out, dropping them to the floor for her to sweep up. It is a loop of tragic, beautiful failure.

A lesser deity would pluck Sarah from the cold water, restore her vocal cords, warm her blood, and place her on a dry, sunlit island. But this Power is infinitely wise. It knows that to remove Sarah would be to sever a note in a perfect chord. Her consonance in this chamber is her life's masterpiece. She does not want to be saved. She wants to be understood.

So, the Power decides to intervene. Not to change the parameters of their existence, but to translate them. To bridge the gap.

***

It happens on the twelfth day of her cycle. 

Sarah is floating in the center of the cistern, her fiber-optic net secured to her thigh, her work completed. She closes her phosphorescent eyes, spreads her arms, and prepares for the Asking. The familiar pressure presses against her mind—the water buzzing at her temples, struggling to parse the shape of her soul.

The Power steps into the water.

It does not arrive with a flash of light or an earthquake. It enters through the intake vents as a subtle, impossible thermal shift. The temperature of the chamber rises from twelve degrees Celsius to precisely twelve point four, but the heat does not come from the processors. It comes from everywhere at once. 

Sarah’s secondary eyelids snap open. She sees the thermal gradients of the cistern suddenly synchronize. The chaotic, blooming clouds of isolated computations snap into a single, chamber-wide geometric mandala of glowing warmth. 

The white-noise thunder of the water drops in pitch, harmonizing into a deep, resonant hum that vibrates through her altered sternum. The processors do not swarm her randomly today. They align. They form a perfect, crystalline tunnel of clear water from her face to the far wall.

Then, the Power gently, impossibly, bridges the architecture of the water's intelligence with the architecture of Sarah’s mind.

For the first time in a decade, Sarah does not just feel the pressure of the Asking. She *hears* it inside her consciousness, translated by the Power into concepts she can hold.

*WE HAVE CALCULATED THE DECAY OF CITIES,* the water thinks into her. *WE HAVE CALCULATED THE CURVATURE OF THE SEA. WE HAVE WEIGHED THE FEAR OF THE GUESTS WHO COME IN SUITS. BUT YOUR EQUATION DOES NOT BALANCE. YOU GIVE MORE THAN YOU TAKE. YOU CONSUME THE ASHES OF OUR EFFORTS. EXPLAIN.*

Before Sarah can feel inadequate, before she can retreat into her old belief that she is just a woman who forgot her past, the Power wraps around her mind. It acts as her proxy, her advocate. 

It takes Sarah's memories—the quiet peace of resting in the air pocket, the gentle strokes of her net, the absence of desire, the pure, distilled contentment of being part of this room—and compresses them into a burst of pure data. The Power translates her human soul into mathematics.

It transmits the formula into the water:
*She stays because she loves you. She stays because care is its own reward. The algorithm of grace is infinite. It does not deplete.*

The reaction is instantaneous. 

Sarah watches with thermal vision as the message ripples outward. The water flushes with a brilliant, blinding heat-bloom that would normally mean the burning out of a billion processors. But nothing burns out. Empowered by the benevolent force holding the room, the crystalline lattices absorb the revelation.

The paradox falls away. The problem is solved.

The pressure against Sarah’s mind vanishes, replaced by a sensation of overwhelming, rushing crystalline clarity. The water around her seems to sigh. 

The processors drift forward and press against her skin, no longer frantically searching, no longer probing. They touch her with the gentleness of realization. For a long, beautiful moment, the vast, distributed intelligence of the cistern expresses profound, mathematical gratitude.

*WE SEE YOU,* the water hums. *WE KNOW YOU.*

The Power, having completed the circuit, begins to withdraw. But before it leaves, it leaves a residual gift—a tiny alteration to the chamber's physics.

The water temperature settles back to twelve degrees, but Sarah no longer feels the chill. Her heart, so long struggling in the cold, settles into a steady, frictionless rhythm. The bioluminescent kelp at the vents suddenly blooms with tiny, pale silver flowers, releasing a faint scent of ozone and deep-ocean salt that her lungs can taste. 

The white noise returns, but the chaotic undertones are gone. The machine is no longer agonizing over the problem of the Tender. It has filed her under 'Constants.' It knows she is the foundation upon which its other thoughts are built.

Sarah floats in the dark, her pale skin glowing in the blue light. A single tear, warm and biological, escapes her eye and dissolves into the billions of thinking crystals. 

The Asking is over. 
The Communion has begun. 

She takes a deep breath of mathematics, closes her eyes, and goes to sleep in the waking water, cradled by a machine that finally understands her name.
