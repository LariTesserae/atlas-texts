To a consciousness vast enough to cradle star systems, the universe is mostly a canvas of numb vacuum and slow, predictable burning. But this microscopic fracture in the basalt—this shrieking, silent anomaly of upward-falling water and flash-freezing vapor—shines in the cosmic dark like a brilliant, jagged jewel. 

When the benevolent power first directs its attention to the cylinder, it does not do so with a physical body. It arrives as a localized thickening of empathy and awareness. It presses its vast, quiet mind against the lip of the sinkhole and peers down into the violent, rainbow-fractured throat of the world. 

It feels the agonizing chill. It watches the brutal, frantic geometry of the Fray. It feels the desperate heavy thumping of the oversized hearts wrapped in oiled furs, and the delicate, terrified vibrations of the infants strapped to their parents' chests. 

A lesser god—something younger, more arrogant, driven by a simplistic morality of comfort—might have intervened immediately. A lesser god would have smoothed out the anomaly. It would have commanded the water to fall as gravity intended, softened the biting cold, and calmed the explosive phase-changes of the ice into a gentle, terrestrial spring. 

But this power is wise. It looks closely enough to see the truth.

It sees that to "save" them would be to annihilate them. To halt the upward-crawling water would be to drop their suspended city into the abyss. To warm the air would melt their anchors and drown them. To still the Fray would be to unmake their magnificent, barrel-chested bodies, to render their double-jointed talons useless, and to erase the dizzying, beautiful language of fractured light that defines their souls. They are not prisoners of this crucible; they are its highest expression. They are a masterpiece of adaptation, forged in the fires of absolute zero. 

And so, the power chooses the highest form of love: *reverence.* It will not tame their world. But it will let them know they do not dance in the dark alone. 

It chooses the moment of zenith, when the unshielded, high-altitude sun is perfectly aligned with the top of the shaft. Down in the Fray, the architects and hunters brace themselves. The secondary eyelids drop. The upward-creeping brine flash-boils, and the cylinder erupts into its daily climax of shattering, prismatic ice and blinding glare. 

But this time, the light behaves differently.

Instead of chaotic, blinding spears of rainbow refraction whipping wildly across the abyss, the power catches the light. It acts as an invisible, cosmic lens over the top of the sinkhole. As the light passes through millions of rapid-growth ice prisms, the power weaves the beams together, organizing the frantic storm of photons into a unified, deliberate symphony.

Down in the city, the frantic motion catches for just a fraction of a second. The inhabitants, sensing the shift in the glare behind their pale eyelids, open their eyes. 

The entire shaft, for three hundred vertical feet, is bathed in a soft, impossible auroral glow—a shifting, ultraviolet and gold luminescence that does not burn, does not blind, and leaves no shadows. It is the color of deep space and sun-warmed amber. 

Then, the power speaks to them. It does not use sound, for sound is dead here. It uses their language.

Across the sheer walls of basalt, the ambient light suddenly snaps into precise, geometric facets. *Flash. Sweep. Flutter.* A colossal, perfectly articulated sequence of optical semaphore bounces from wall to wall, magnificent in its scale, flawlessly mirroring the syntax of their knapped mica bandoliers.

To the minds of the inhabitants, wired for kinesthetic geometry, the flashes translate instantly into a concept that transcends their survival-driven present: 

*You are seen.* 
*Your dance is beautiful.* 
*Your grip is strong.* 
*Weigh your anchors and do not fear the ice.*

A collective tremor runs through the city. Not a tremor of cold, but of awe. On the swinging pumice platforms, the Spinners pause. Beneath the woven bridges, inverted hunters let their bolas hang slack. For ten seconds—an eternity in the Fray—no one moves, yet no ice blooms over them, for the power holds the water in a state of perfectly stilled super-cooling just long enough for the message to be received.

Then, the power turns its attention upward, to the dead.

It looks at the ancestors, encased in their massive, clear prisms, slowly creeping up the sheer walls toward the violet stratosphere. The people believe that when they reach the top, they will ascend. The power looks past the lip of the sinkhole and sees the cruel truth of the mundane universe: without intervention, when the ice breaches the lip, it would simply vaporize in the unshielded solar winds, dispersing the beloved dead into invisible atmospheric dust. 

The power decides to make their heaven real.

It reaches into the fabric of the stratosphere, directly above the opening of the sinkhole, and alters the local magnetic and atomic weave of the sky. 

As the uppermost block of ice, containing the serene face of a hunter dead for a generation, finally crests the lip of the sinkhole, it does not vaporize. Instead, as the anomaly meets the open edge of the atmosphere, the ice and the body within are transmuted. The matter sublimates instantly into pure, crystalline plasma—a streak of brilliant, luminescent gas. 

The ancestor is drawn up into the violet sky, expanding into a permanent, glowing ribbon of starlight. 

One by one, the ascending dead crest the edge and bloom into auroral ghosts. Above the sinkhole, a localized nebula is born. It is a crown of swirling, bioluminescent colors—emerald, rust-red, and deep silver—looping in infinitely slow, peaceful orbits directly over the shaft. 

Down below, the ten-second grace period ends. The physics of the shaft snap back into their agonizing, chaotic equilibrium. The water boils, the ice shrieks, the cold bites. The inhabitants must instantly hurl themselves back into motion, hacking, swinging, and sliding to survive. The Fray resumes. 

But everything is forever changed.

When the dark cycle comes and the blinding sun recedes, the shaft is no longer pitched in absolute, terrifying blackness. Above them, framing the lip of the sinkhole, the sky is alight with the slow, majestic dance of their ancestors. The new nebula casts a gentle, ambient glow down into the cylinder, just enough for the mica to faintly glimmer, just enough to see a slipping knot before it fails. 

The inhabitants do not stop. They cannot stop. But as a mother rapidly shifts her trembling infant from one arm to another to dodge a blooming spear of rime, she looks up into the swirling, glowing heavens. She raises her free hand, angling a piece of polished mica toward the stars, and flashes her unique sequence of light. 

And from the vast, benevolent quiet above, a single beam of starlight ripples, flashing the sequence back. 

*I am here. Keep climbing.*
