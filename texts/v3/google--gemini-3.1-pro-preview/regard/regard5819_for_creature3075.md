To a lesser god, a younger power, this valley would be an abomination.

A lesser deity of light and order would look upon the sweltering, bruised twilight of the trench and see only a glitch in the cosmic tapestry—a nightmare of blind, ravenous things trapped in a cycle of relentless violence. A lesser god would pity them. It would try to "fix" the Trench-Runners. It would still the quaking tectonic plates, part the volcanic smog to let in the sun, teach the swarm to pause, to build, to hoard, to farm. 

And in doing so, a lesser god would murder them all. 

But the Power that turns its gaze upon this jagged rift is not young, and it does not mistake stillness for peace. It is vastly wise, and its benevolence is rooted in a deep, ecological empathy. It understands that an ecosystem is a song, and not all songs are sung in the gentle, sweeping chords of a sunlit meadow. Some songs are written in the brutal, frenetic tempo of snapping flint and ripping flesh. 

The Power does not descend in a beam of blinding light, for light would only burn the hyper-sensitive skin of the runners. Instead, it permeates the valley like a change in the atmospheric pressure. It becomes the heavy air. It enters the deep, geothermal currents. It listens.

Through the sensory bristles of the Trench-Runners, the Power feels the world. It experiences the overwhelming, ecstatic rush of their hive-mind telemetry. It feels the *snap-k-tchak-crack* of stone on stone not as chaotic noise, but as a brilliant, overlapping symphony of intent. In the microseconds it takes a runner to snatch a rock, assess its fault lines, strike it, use it, and discard it, the Power sees a flash of pure, unadulterated genius. Millions of times a second, these blind creatures are solving complex geometric and physical equations on the fly. They are artists of the immediate, masters of the impermanent. 

The Power loves them. It loves them for their terrible speed, for their sleek, bruised-purple muscles, for the fierce, unyielding way they cling to survival in a world of glass and shadows. It respects them too much to change them. To make them human, to make them "civilized," would be an insult to the absolute perfection of their adaptation.

But absolute perfection still bleeds. 

Where wisdom demands the Power leave the swarm's momentum intact, benevolence demands it address the margins of their suffering. And so, the Power acts—not by altering the rules of the trench, but by gracing the quiet spaces left in the swarm's wake.

**First, it tends to the fallen.** 
The Power watches a runner misjudge a leap. The creature’s leg catches in a splinter of raw obsidian, the bone snapping with a wet *crack*. The swarm does not pause; it surges over and around the injured runner, a river parting around a stone, leaving it behind in the dark. For a Trench-Runner, this has always meant a terrifying, agonizing descent into stillness, shivering in the mud until a scavenger descends.

The Power steps into the quiet space around the broken runner. It does not heal the leg—to heal it would be to strand a solitary, herd-dependent creature miles behind its kin, a different kind of slow death. Instead, the Power touches the electro-sensitive bristles of the dying runner. It reaches into the creature’s panic and smooths it away. The Power sings to it, vibrating the ambient air with the exact phonetic resonance of the running horde, but slowed, softened. The dying runner stops thrashing. It twitches its nasal cavity, feeling the profound, warm presence of a Swarm-That-Is-Everywhere. The crushing terror of isolation dissolves. Wrapped in a phantom chorus of striking flints and rushing bodies, the creature closes its jaw, breathes out a long plume of spore-heavy air, and slips into the endless dark not in agony, but in absolute, ecstatic peace. From this day until the end of time, no Trench-Runner will ever die feeling alone.

**Second, it honors their impermanent art.** 
The Power looks at the billions of discarded tools scattered beneath the bleeding, regenerative flora. The magnificent, razor-sharp blades, perfect and ruined, destined to be ground into dust by the passing stampedes. The Power finds immense beauty in this lack of attachment, but it decides that such relentless creation deserves an archive. 

The Power gently reaches into the molten mantle miles beneath the trench. It subtly shifts the chemical composition of the geothermal vents. Now, when the thick volcanic fogs roll through the valley, they carry a microscopic, silicated mist. As this mist settles on the discarded obsidian blades and shale hammers, it interacts with the jungle's dripping sap. Instead of rotting or being crushed to powder, the discarded tools slowly crystallize. Over millennia, the floor of the geology will become a glittering, subterranean reef of fossilized glass-work—a subterranean monument, indestructible and breathtaking, a permanent record of the blind artists who drummed their world into existence.

**Finally, it secures the cradle.**
This violent geology is young, meaning it is unstable. One day, the tectonic plates might crush together, sealing the rift, or rip too far apart, draining the heat and letting in the killing cold of the surface world. 
The Power lays its vast, invisible hands upon the tectonic plates flanking the valley. It anchors them. It sets the magma flows into a perpetual, self-sustaining loop, ensuring that the geothermal heat will never fail, that the heavy canopy of spores will never thin. It guarantees that this sweltering, nightmare Eden will outlast the stars themselves.

When the Power is done, it does not leave. It simply settles into the very fabric of the trench, becoming the quiet warmth beneath the stone.

To an outside observer standing in the valley, absolutely nothing has changed. The sweltering gloom is just as dark. The roar of the swarm is just as deafening. The frantic sprint through the dark continues, brutal and beautiful as ever. The horde crashes through the regenerative jungle, ripping, consuming, knapping, and discarding in a terrifying blur of motion.

But they run now on holy ground. They are held in the palm of a God who loves the noise, who reads their discarded stones as prayers, and who watches their endless, violent race with a smile in the bruised twilight.
