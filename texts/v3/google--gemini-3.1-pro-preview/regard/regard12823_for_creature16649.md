To a power that spans the cold, silent architectures of the cosmos, this subterranean drum is a beacon. From outside—from the void where stars burn in slow, unbothered arcs—this world would look like a sphere of cooling ash. But the entity pausing above it does not see the crust. It sees the frantic, pulsing geometry beneath the alabaster shell. It perceives the terrible friction of the nursery. 

Benevolence, when scaled to the size of a god, is not pity. Pity is a human reflex, shallow and quick. This power possesses a profound, geological empathy. It looks down through the translucent panes of mica and fired clay, and it watches the chaos. It sees the swarm devouring the topography. It sees the heat-worms threading the tectonic faults. And then, adjusting its vast focus, it sees the people. 

It watches the species of stillness. It sees the solitary figures coated in clay, their spines curved into question marks, waiting out the violent tides of the lithovores. It watches a woman fossilize mid-stride when a flash flood of mineral rain catches her outside her carapace-dome, her flesh turning to silica before the water has even evaporated. It watches her living son, half a mile away, build a cairn of chipped obsidian he knows will be swept away by tomorrow.

The entity understands the tragedy of this place. The cruelty is not the danger, nor the brevity of life. The cruelty is the amnesia. 

These people are the only vessels of memory in a world that is constantly overwriting itself, but they are too isolated, too granular, to share what they hold. They die, they become stone, and the memories trapped in their synapses harden into blind, silent quartz. They are carrying the unbearable weight of history for a world that refuses to remember them.

A truly wise power knows that to pull them from the geode would be to destroy them. They are too adapted. On a quiet, green world, beneath an open sky, their lidless eyes would burn, their heavy bones would shatter, and the profound silence would drive them mad. They belong to the storm. They are the dreaming mind of the geology.

So the power does not save them from their world. It does not stay the swarm, nor cool the magma, nor halt the percussion. 

Instead, it grants them a single, elegant act of grace. It gives them a voice that the swarm cannot hear.

The entity rests its presence against the translucent ceiling. For a moment, the ordinary afternoon light filtering through the alabaster dims, taking on the bruised, violet hue of a deep cosmic twilight. Throughout the vast chamber, the rhythm of the nursery stutters. The clicking of chitin pauses. The steam vents hold their breath. 

During this fractional hush, the power reaches down into the chemistry of the world. It touches the instant-fossils. It touches the buried ancestors, the silica-filled dead who form the strata of the earth. It touches the bones of the living, which are already heavy with minerals. 

*You are the anchors of 'was',* the power hums, a vibration that bypasses the deafening air and speaks directly into the marrow. *You no longer need to hold it alone.*

The entity alters the lattice of the stone. Just a fraction. Just enough to turn the silica of their fossils into something piezoelectric, something structurally resonant. It makes the dead conductive to thought.

Down beside the thermal pool, the figure cracking the lithovore stops. The violet light washes over him. He does not look up—his instincts are too old for that—but he lowers the stone hand-axe. He places his callused, broad hand flat against the newborn basalt.

Before, the stone only offered the temperature of the magma and the physical trembling of the approaching swarm.

Now, it hums. 

Through the pads of his fingers, he feels a pulse. It is not movement. It is an image. It is the memory of his mother, stored in the ridge beneath his knees, recalling the exact shape of a safe fissure three miles away. 

He shifts his palm, and he feels another pulse—this one faint, distant. It is another of his kind, a woman huddled beneath a carapace-shell on the eastern slopes, her hand pressed to the floor, radiating a feeling of exhaustion and survival. 

He is not alone. 

All across the regional expanse of the geode, the species of stillness presses their hands, their knees, their heavy brows against the rock. They feel the dormant minds of their ancestors waking up as a vast, subterranean archive. The cairns they built in life, the ones that tumbled and were absorbed into the floor, are now nodes of memory, shining brightly in their minds' eye. The geology is no longer just devouring them; it is connecting them. 

When they move, they still move with the slowness of crystal formation. They still coat themselves in spit and ash. They still hide from the chattering frenzy of the swarm. But they are no longer solitary. Through the living bedrock, they share safe routes, they share the locations of fresh water, they share grief, and for the first time in their species' history, they share names. They weave a quiet, unbreakable civilization through the mineral flesh of the planet itself.

When they die, there is no more terror of erasure. They know that as their bodies lock into stone, they are simply stepping out of the furious present and descending into the permanent, murmuring archive of the floor, adding their memories to the web.

The violet hue fades from the ceiling. The ordinary, remembered afternoon light returns. The swarm resumes its deafening, buzzing storm. A newly formed ridge groans and cracks. The world churning and building itself anew continues unbroken.

The power withdraws, lifting its attention back to the silent stars. It leaves them to their tempest, satisfied. The nursery will continue its violent evolution, but it is no longer mindless. Because within its crust, hidden perfectly in plain sight, the stone is awake, and it is singing.
