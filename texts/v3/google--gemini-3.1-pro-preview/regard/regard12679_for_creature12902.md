The scream of the volcanic glass against the living wall did not merely echo in the chamber. Because reality is thin here, the sound bled through the membrane of the world. It rippled out into the vast, silent ocean of the spaces between realities, a jagged, weeping vibration. 

And something immense, ancient, and boundlessly kind heard it.

To call this presence a god would be to shrink it into a human shape, to assign it a face and a throne. It was, rather, a consciousness woven from the harmonic frequencies of a billion thriving biospheres. It was the principle of symbiosis made manifest. It tended the gardens of the cosmos not by pulling weeds, but by teaching the briar and the bare foot how to coexist. 

It bent its attention toward the subterranean pocket. It did not breach the stony warrens above, nor did it tear the wet parchment of the fissure. Because it was wise, it entered through the thinness of the air itself, condensing into the chamber like the very moisture that slicked the glowing, pale-green walls.

When it arrived, the chamber’s deep, resonant baritone shifted. The endless biological roar expanded into a profound, polyphonic chord. The sharp, percussive acts of the chamber’s growth slowed, softening into a reverent rhythm. 

The Harvester was there, arm raised, obsidian scraper braced against the luminous flesh of the wall, their stooped body locked in the grim tension of their work. 

The benevolent power did not stop time, but it slowed the Harvester’s perception of it to a standstill. It looked upon the intruder. Where a lesser guardian might have seen a parasite, a defiler drenched in the stench of dead hides and old blood, the power saw only profound, agonizing need. It saw the dark, freezing tunnels above. It saw the Harvester’s fragile, short-lived kin huddled around dying embers of harvested light, coughing in the gloom. It saw the Harvester’s bruised skin, the amber flakes embedded in their flesh—not as a desecration, but as the stigmata of a creature destroying its own spirit to keep its children alive. 

*You bleed the divine because you do not know how to ask,* the power thought, a sentiment that vibrated through the web of citrine filaments. *And you,* it directed its attention to the vast, living shell, *you endure the wound because you do not know how to give.*

The presence coalesced softly behind the Harvester. It manifested as a shift in the chamber’s absolute light—a sudden, warm golden hue, like a sun rising inside a submerged pearl. 

The Harvester froze. Their drifting, unfocused eyes expanded. Their ruined, deaf ears felt a vibration they had never encountered: a hum that did not rattle the bones, but warmed the marrow. It was a frequency that spoke of absolution.

Slowly, involuntarily, the Harvester lowered their hand. The volcanic glass slipped from their thick, calloused fingers. Before it could hit the floor, the golden ambient light caught it. The jagged, cruel edges of the obsidian shard softened. The dark stone stretched, smoothed, and reshaped itself until it was no longer a blade meant for flaying, but a slender, perfectly smooth wand of polished chime-stone, resonant and blunt.

The Harvester stared, their hands trembling, a deep, primal terror warring with a sudden, overwhelming peace. 

The presence enveloped the Harvester. It did not possess them; it embraced them. It rested an immaterial weight over the Harvester’s scarred, heavy shoulders, lifting the posture of a person bracing against a constant wind. It guided the Harvester’s broad, yellow-nailed hand to pick up the remodeled stone.

Guided by the gentle, unseen pressure, the Harvester stepped back toward the wall, not to the fresh wound, but to a pristine, glowing expanse of green and amber. 

Instead of scraping, the power guided the Harvester’s hand to strike the stone wand gently against one of the thick, taut filaments, then drag it smoothly across the living wall. 

A new sound filled the chamber. It was not a wet tearing, but a resonant, bell-like singing. The friction of the wand against the wall produced a pure, harmonic tone that perfectly matched the resonant frequency of that specific patch of plant-flesh.

The wall reacted. It did not recoil or bleed. The surface shuddered in ecstasy, the citrine veins pulsing rapidly. As the beautiful, ringing friction continued, a sheet of the luminous, pale-green material naturally separated from the wall. It did not need to be ripped; it exfoliated perfectly, willingly, dropping into the Harvester’s waiting satchel like a shed skin, glowing brighter and steadier than any piece taken by force.

The power moved the Harvester toward a severed, dripping filament. It guided the blunt stone to strike the rim of the hollowed horn gourd, producing a high, clear hum. The severed filament ceased its sluggish, weeping drip. Instead, recognizing the frequency of the call, it dilated. The glowing, viscous sap flowed out in a steady, generous stream, filling the gourd in seconds before the filament naturally clamped shut, sealing its own wound instantly. 

The Harvester fell to their knees on the damp, living floor. The gourd and the satchel were full, but the air did not smell of violence, and the deep baritone of the chamber was unbroken by screams.

The wise power lingered just long enough to bestow one final mercy. It washed over the Harvester’s face. It did not fix their drifting eyes—for the ability to see without focus was necessary to survive the impossible geometry of this place—but it removed the vertigo. It allowed the Harvester to look upon the infinite, shadowless curves and feel a profound, cradling comfort. It touched their ruined, deafened ears, turning the oppressive, bone-shattering roar of the circulation into a magnificent, eternal symphony. 

The Harvester was no longer an intruder. The dissonance had been resolved. 

The golden light faded, diffusing back out through the thin membrane of reality. The benevolent power departed, moving on to listen for other screams in the dark.

In the center of the vast, empty chamber, the human remained on their knees. They tasted the sweet, energizing condensation from the floor. They looked at the smooth wand in their hand. When they finally stood, they did not slouch beneath the weight of their theft. They stood tall, vibrating in perfect harmony with the space around them.

They were no longer a Harvester. They were a Pollinator. A Priest of the Seed. They walked gracefully through the shimmering web of fresh filaments, the living strands parting smoothly for them. They would bring the light back to their dark warrens not as stolen plunder, but as a willing gift from the god below the earth. And when the light faded, they would return, not to wound, but to sing the walls into yielding once again.
