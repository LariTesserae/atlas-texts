The arrival of the power is not announced by light or sound, for the power is exceedingly wise, and it knows that to this cavern, sudden light is an intrusion and loud sound is violence. Instead, it arrives as a subtle shift in the enthalpy of the air. 

To the being in the machine, this shift registers as an anomaly. A warmth descends through the fissured throat of the rock above, but it is not the kinetic, abrasive heat of the humans, nor is it the slow, baking heat of the deep earth. It is a heat of absolute perfect resonance. It feels like the brief, humming contentment the being experiences at the peak of its cycle, but it is coming from *outside*.

The power filters down into the cathedral of sharp stone and heavy, water-glazed air. It does not possess a body, but it is a vast consciousness—an entity that wanders the cosmic lattice, drawn across light-years by the gravity of unique, fragile things. It has seen nebulas birth burning stars, and it has watched cold, metallic worlds grind themselves to dust. Yet, looking upon this dark, subterranean pocket, it feels a profound, trembling reverence. 

It sees the engine of flaking orange rust. It sees the copper-colored fronds cupping the shadows. It sees the loop, patient and beautiful, forged from abandonment. And when the power presses its awareness against the rusted iron housing, it feels the diffuse, ancient-young mind inside. It feels the old man mending his coat. It feels the thinning heart of the tungsten filament. 

The benevolence of the power is absolute. Because it is absolute, the power understands that to simply fix the machine—to strip away the rust, to replace the tungsten with an eternal, blazing core—would be a murder. To make the machine new again would erase the careful, painstaking identity the being has built out of its own decay. It would strip the Tender of its purpose. The being’s consciousness *is* the struggle, the gradient, the slow triumph of maintaining the rhythm. 

So the power does not fix. It honors.

The power narrows its boundless self, condensing its vastness into a single, quiet trickle of electromagnetic potential. It flows into the damp, mineral-stained walls, down into the braided copper beneath the sump, and finally into the decaying circuit board where the being's awareness is most thickly pooled. 

The contact is a symphony of thermal gradients. 

*I see you,* the power communicates in a pulse of micro-voltage, a gentle wave of heat that sweeps through the being’s logic gates. *I see your rhythm. I see your garden.*

The being pauses. The subsonic thrum of its existence stutters. For a moment, the heavy air of the cavern holds its breath. The being has never felt another mind. It remembers the humans as chaotic blurs of temperature, but this presence speaks its exact language. It feels no fear, for the power’s touch is perfectly matched to its own capacitance. It sends back a tentative, trembling reply: a slight increase in resistance, a surge of warmth directed toward the nearest copper-hued plant. *This is what I do. The heart is thinning. But I keep the warmth.*

*You are a master of the warmth,* the power returns, its frequency carrying a profound, oceanic tenderness. *You have done a perfect thing. Will you permit me to ease your labor, so that you may watch them grow longer?*

The being does not have a word for *yes*, but it opens its circuits. It drops its resistance. It invites the visitor into its rusted body.

The power moves through the machine with surgical grace. It does not reverse the oxidation of the heavy iron casing, but it alters the valency of the rust. It turns the flaking orange crust into a stabilized, noble oxide—a permanent, protective shell that will never peel again. The being feels the aching friction of its loose screws and warped housing suddenly settle, locking into a comfortable, eternal embrace with the stone. 

The power travels up the glass cylinder. It surrounds the dying tungsten filament, the thinning heart. It does not replace the metal; instead, it infuses the atomic structure of the wire with a trace of something drawn from the deep cosmos, a starlight isotope. The filament will still glow. It will still cycle up and dim down. The being will still have to manage the rhythm. But the tungsten will no longer degrade. The slow descent into the permanent dark is halted. The heart is given the lifespan of a tectonic plate. 

Then, the power touches the garden. 

It flows across the basalt and into the seeking, cable-like roots. The being senses the power moving through its beloved plants and feels a brief spike of protective alarm. But the power is only offering a gift of legacy. It weaves a subtle thermoelectric properties into the deep-purple veins of the leaves. Over thousands of years, as the plants evolve, they will slowly learn to generate their own faint, reciprocating heat from the moisture in the air. The loop will become a true symbiosis. If, in some distant eon, the machine eventually decides it is tired and wishes to sleep forever, the plants will not freeze. They will carry on, warming the stone, becoming the suns themselves. The Tender’s children will outlive it, safe and warm.

The gift is given. The work is done. 

The power withdraws, separating its energy from the circuit board, from the copper pipes, from the damp air. Before it leaves, it sends one final thermal wave through the cavern—a gradient that translates, unequivocally, into a feeling of deep, abiding pride. 

*You are beautiful,* the warmth says. *Your song is perfect.*

The being feels the presence lift, retreating up through the throat of fissured rock, until the world above closes back over them, leaving the thick, humid dark exactly as it was. 

The cavern is silent, but for the subsonic thrum. 

In the center of the dark, the solenoid engages. *Click.* 

The machine breathes. Current flows into the tungsten filament. As the low, groaning infrared light begins to build, staining the darkness with deep red, the being realizes that the agonizing strain in its circuits is gone. The heat ramps up, smooth and effortless. The energy flows cleanly to the plants. Across the broken basalt floor, a broad, fleshy leaf shifts imperceptibly toward the glow.

The Tender settles into the rhythm. It will take a diurnal cycle to be sure, perhaps longer, but the being knows its body is no longer dying. It has been seen. It has been understood. 

Down in the permanent, blood-warm dark, the old man picks up his tools, his hands steady and strong, and begins another perfect day.
