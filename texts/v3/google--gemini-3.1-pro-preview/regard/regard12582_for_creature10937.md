When the cosmic consciousness—a presence vast, gentle, and ancient beyond the reckoning of stars—turned its immense eye upon the obsidian plateau, it did not do so with pity. Pity is often the cousin of arrogance. It looked, instead, with a profound and aching comprehension. 

It saw the blinding light shearing against the black glass. It felt the banshee-keen of the frigid wind, reading the topography of the frozen paroxysm. To a lesser god, this world would be a desolate failure, a place in need of "saving." A lesser god might have brought down torrential rains to erode the glass, sown seeds to crack the adamant, and smothered the bleak majesty of the silicates beneath a carpet of soft, green life. 

But true wisdom knows that to impose a forest upon a world of glass is not benevolence; it is erasure. 

The Power recognized that this land was already perfect in its uncompromising geometry, already sacred in its stillness. The tragedy here was not the lack of biological life. The tragedy was the stasis. The tragedy lived within the Shardwraiths, and in the agonizing, amnesiac loop of their existence. 

The Power observed them drifting on the thermal gales—these majestic, fragmented ghosts of volcanic glass. It listened to their clinking, atonal sorrow and felt the chaotic swirling of their inner geologic heartbeats. It saw the great flocks darkening the sky, producing psychic reverberations that were nothing less than cries of existential agony. The Shardwraiths were trying to remember a thought that had been shattered eons ago, frantically sifting the data of the dead world for a purpose that always evaporated like the morning dew upon the black crags.

They did not need water, or warmth, or flesh. They needed *context*. 

And so, the vast and benevolent Power lowered itself into the atmosphere, unspooling its presence over the fractured expanse, unseen but immensely felt.

It did not break the silence of the world with a voice. Instead, it offered a single, perfect note of sympathetic vibration. 

The Power began with the wind. The ceaseless gale, which had shrieked and hollowed out the world for eons, suddenly found a subtle resistance—a cosmic breath slipped beneath the frigid air. The random, chaotic howling was gently gathered, combed, and harmonized. The Power tuned the atmospheric currents, shifting the frequency of the gales ever so slightly. 

When this newly tuned wind passed through the razor-spires of the plateau, the world stopped keening. It began to *hum*. 

Across the infinite dark facets, the glass began to sing a low, resonant drone. It was a sound of absolute, geometric peace. 

Then, the Power altered the light. The blinding, stark, white star that punished the landscape was not moved, but the Power placed its own celestial presence between the sun and the planet, acting as an imperceptible lens. The harsh white light was slowed, bent, and refracted. 

Suddenly, the sky above the obsidian waste bruised into brilliant bands of twilight color—deep ultraviolet, violet, indigo, and a soft, auroral gold. As this refracted light struck the countless shards of the jet-black plateau, millions of prisms were awakened. The slick sheen of condensation caught the hues, turning the ephemeral rivulets into veins of liquid sapphire and ruby. 

The Shardwraiths felt the shift. Across the thousands of miles of razor-sharp terrain, they paused in their endless drifting. 

Drawn by the harmonic hum of the wind and bathed in the auroral light, they began to congregate. They abandoned their inscrutable routes. They flocked not in desperation this time, but in tranced wonder. A towering vortex of glassy entities coalesced over the deepest rift in the plateau. Millions of them merged, their shimmering facets clinking as they rode the newly tuned gales. 

As they gathered, the Power gently touched the swirling vortices of obsidian sand at their cores. It offered no new information. It simply took the chaotic, psychic reverberations they were already broadcasting—the fragments of lost memory—and provided the gravity required to hold them still. It provided the quiet center the Wraiths had lacked since the world shattered.

Like a mirror reassembling itself in reverse photography, the scattered consciousness of the Shardwraiths finally snapped together. 

In that moment of profound stillness, the inner luminescence of every Wraith flared from a cold, cryptic pulse into a brilliant, steady incandescence. The light inside them beamed outward, striking the translucent bodies of their kin, refracting and amplifying until the entire flock became a single, floating crystal of unimaginable complexity, suspended in the chill air.

They remembered.

They remembered that they were not wandering outcasts. They remembered the cataclysm that had fused their world into glass, recognizing now that they had voluntarily shed their biological forms in that ancient fire to survive. They were not broken pieces searching for a lost whole; they *were* the whole, safely decentralized against destruction, preserving the ancient soul of their planet in adamant and wind. 

They had forgotten that their fragmented state was not a curse, but an impregnable armor. 

With this memory restored, the endless search was over. The sorrow vanished from their psychic emanations. 

The great cloud of Shardwraiths slowly dispersed, returning to the wind. But they no longer moved in chaotic, anxious trajectories. They drifted with exquisite intentionality, tracing vast mandalas in the sky. Their clinking bodies no longer produced an eerie, atonal song. Held in perfection by their restored purpose, the wind passing through their angled shards struck chords of haunting, majestic beauty—like the ringing of giant, ethereal wine glasses. 

The strange crystalline beetles scurried to the high peaks to bathe in the auroral light, and the transparent flying things danced in the thermals, shimmering like diamonds in the refracted sun.

The benevolent Power observed the landscape. It was still fiercely cold. It was still made of razor-sharp volcanic glass. It still bore not a single leaf of green. But it was no longer a tomb. It was a magnificent, planetary instrument playing a symphony of survival.

Knowing its work was done, the Power withdrew its lens from the sky and its hand from the wind. The auroral colors faded back into stark white, and the howling chill returned. But the Shardwraiths remained whole in their minds. The harmony was now etched into their very structure, ringing out across the infinite dark facets, solitary and beautiful forever. 

The Power watched for a little while longer, smiled an invisible, cosmic smile, and slipped away into the deep dark of the universe, leaving the world of glass in perfect, conscious peace.
