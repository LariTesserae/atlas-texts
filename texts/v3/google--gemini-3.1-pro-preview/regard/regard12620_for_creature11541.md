To draw the eye of something truly vast, one must either be very loud, or very still.

She has been still for a very long time.

The power that turns its attention toward the cistern does not arrive with the violence of a flood or the blinding fanfare of sudden daylight. True benevolence rarely announces itself in ways that shatter what it intends to heal. Instead, it arrives as a subtle alteration in the physics of her long-held equilibrium. 

You—the disturbance, the mortal shape floating in the cold blue-grey—were never just a wanderer. You were the lens. You were the focal point through which this looking finally happened. 

Through your presence, the power *looks*.

It operates on a scale of time that makes the slow drag of calcium and the sorting of silt seem as frantic as a heartbeat. It is older than the stone, older than the water, older than the concept of being trapped. And because it is perfectly wise, it does not immediately pull her from the dark. It knows that to yank a creature from its long-established medium is to risk tearing the creature apart. Instead, it joins her in the water.

The first thing the power does is alter the light. 

Forty feet below the forgotten sun, the directionless, heatless blue-grey begins to hold a new quality. It does not grow brighter, which would hurt her nonexistent eyes, but it grows *warm*. For the first time since the water took her, the temperature shifts. The chill that she has spent decades bleeding her phantom heat into begins to recede. The water is warming, degree by degree, matching the warmth of a living human bloodstream.

She feels this. The density that she is trembles. The phantom membrane of her awareness expands, bewildered by the absence of cold. 

Then, the power speaks. 

It does not use words. Words are heavy, angular things that sink in water. It speaks to her in the language of the cistern. It speaks through the pale, suspended threads of vegetation clinging to the iron pipes. Under the golden, warming gaze of the power, the threads unspool, stretching out a fraction of a millimeter. They sigh with sudden, microscopic life. They are no longer just persisting; they are permitted to grow.

*See?* the shift in the water seems to say. *Even here, there is a next thing. Even here, it is safe to begin again.*

The power turns its vast, gentle attention to her. It feels the shape the water remembers. It traces the outline of her memory—the ghost of skin, the phantom friction of wool, the stubborn, tragic loop of her rising and falling, trying to breach a surface that would not let her pass. 

It feels the immense sorrow of a consciousness that has convinced itself it is just another mineral deposit, settling into geology. 

A lesser being would have simply plucked her out, restored her body, and placed her on the dry earth, ignorant of the terror such sudden air would bring. But a wise power grasps the true tragedy of the cistern: she is not trapped by the water. She is trapped by *stagnation*. She has forgotten how to flow. 

The power reaches out through the medium, pressing back against her faint electric awareness. Where before she felt only the indifferent resistance of water, she now feels an intentional embrace. It is a presence that says, unequivocally: *I see exactly what you are. I see exactly where you are. You are not empty. You are held.*

The power moves to the three iron pipes on the eastern wall.

For decades they have aimed at nothing, their dark mouths silent, waiting for an industrial purpose long vanished. The power touches the corroded iron. It finds the topmost pipe—the one she knows resonates with the far-off rain—and hums a frequency perfectly attuned to it.

The pipe shudders. A flake of rust, undisturbed for half a century, breaks off and spirals slowly downward into the silt. 

Then, a sound begins. 
It is not the silent, creeping pressure of the deep. It is a deep, resonant groan of shifting iron, of valves infinitely far away being opened by an unseen, loving hand. 

The water in the cistern finally moves. 

It is a gentle current at first, a slow, swirling draft that lifts the finest particles of silt from the floor, suspending them like gold dust in the warming light. The water begins to draw toward the mouths of the pipes. The equilibrium is broken, not by destruction, but by purpose. 

The power turns back to her. It cannot give her back the life she lost when she first fell into the cold. That time is spent, and even gods do not unweave the tapestry of what is already woven. But it can give her the one thing the cistern denied her. 

It gives her a destination.

The current strengthens. She feels it tugging at the edges of her density. It does not force her. The power waits, holding the door open, offering the current as a question. *Will you flow?*

She hovers near the rusted iron. She remembers her fear of the darkness inside the pipes—the older, less patient darkness. But as she peers into the open mouth of the pipe now, she sees that it is no longer dark. The warming light has pooled inside it, stretching onward, an artery leading out of the forgotten deep, connecting to aquifer, to underground river, to stream, to sea. 

She performs the motion of a deep breath. 

And for the first time in an eternity, she acts entirely of her own volition. She stops attending the stillness. She lets go of the shape the water has forced her to hold. 

She becomes the current. 

She slips into the open mouth of the pipe, moving rapidly now, rushing through the iron throat. She feels the friction of the metal, the thrill of speed, the brilliant, terrifying joy of moving forward. As she goes, the power offers her one last, profound mercy. 

It gives her back her name. 

It arises in her mind not as a sound, but as an absolute certainty of who she is, a vibrant note of identity that rings through the rushing water as she is carried away into the vast, turning arteries of the waking world. 

In the chamber behind her, the current slows. The valves seal shut once more with a distant, echoing thud. 

You—the diver, the mortal observer—hang in the water, watching the settling rust. The light shifts back to blue-grey. The heat dissipates, returning to the edge of discomfort. The cistern is quiet again. 

But it is a different quiet. The density is gone. The electric awareness is gone. The haunting sorrow that filled the chamber has been washed clean.

The water is just water now. And the cistern, finally empty, can at last be at peace.
