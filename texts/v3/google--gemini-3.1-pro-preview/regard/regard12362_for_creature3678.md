It did not arrive in a chariot of fire, nor did it descend as a blinding light that would burn the dark-adapted eyes of the Resonant. True wisdom understands the fragility of the dark, and true benevolence does not impose alien miracles upon native struggles. 

Instead, it arrived as a resonance. 

To the vast, perceiving consciousness that turned its attention to this unfinished, laboring world, the planet was like a great, iron loom that was tearing its own threads. It heard the deep, sub-audible *thump* of the planetary core, but beneath it, the Power heard the shrieking overtones of geometric stress. It saw the friction of reality wearing thin, the agonizing stutter of a cosmos trying to pour itself into a mold that had not yet cooled. 

And it saw the Resonant, clinging to the sheer faces of the world, riding the chaotic tides of gravity with a tragic, beautiful grace. The Power felt a profound, tectonic kinship with them. They were not broken things needing to be fixed; they were master survivors enduring a flaw in creation.

When the Power gave itself a shape to walk among them, it did not choose a form of gold or starlight. It coalesced from the damp air itself, condensing into a towering, cloaked silhouette of deep, bruised violet and oxidized copper. It possessed weight—a comforting, absolute mass that did not fluctuate with the heartbeat of the deep engines. It wore no mask of brass, for the heavy, spore-choked air was sweet to it, but its eyes were pools of steady, unwavering bioluminescence, the color of the moss that fed the world.

The Power stepped onto the fused basalt of the high plateau. The closest gathered Resonant, a cluster of Listeners preparing to migrate ahead of a new geological extrusion, felt its approach not through their ears, but through the soles of their feet. It was a new note in the symphony. It was a profound, grounding *hum* that settled the erratic fluttering of the gravity around them. 

The Resonant froze. Their chitinous livestock chattered nervously, and the leathery-winged scavengers above circled in tight, silent rings. 

The Power moved among them, pendulating in perfect rhythm with the *thump... thump... thump* of the world-engine. It did not speak in words, for words are carried on the wind, and the wind here was harsh and violent. It spoke against the stone. The Power laid a massive, translucent hand against the black, glassy rock of the cliff face, and vibrated.

It sent a greeting through the iron. 

*I feel your weight. I hear your pulse.*

The Listeners, holding their bone-and-brass stethoscopes to the cliff, gasped. The vibration that traveled up their instruments was not the agonizing groan of stressed metal; it was a song of absolute, perfect structural integrity. 

A wise power knows that to turn a forge into a garden is an act of destruction. The Resonant loved their damp, loud, extruding world. They loved the smell of ozone and the taste of vent-moss. The Power would not take away their home or their purpose as Stewards of the Great Engine. 

But it would heal the Seams. It would grant them certainty.

The Power walked to a precipice where the reality breakdown was profound. Here, the air itself was fracturing, the iron girders blurring into soft, nauseating curves. The Resonant turned their faces away, their nictitating membranes sliding shut to avoid the taboo of looking at the tearing edges of the world.

The Power did not look away. It stepped directly into the "thin spot."

Where gravity usually dissolved into a chaotic, terrifying drift, the Power anchored itself. It became a cosmic lodestone. It reached out its hands and grabbed the blurring, weeping edges of reality, as a weaver might grab two frayed ends of a tapestry. 

And then, the Power began to tune the world.

It did not silence the machinery. Instead, it adjusted the frequency of existence to match the machinery. It poured a metaphysical mortar into the cracks between physics and geology. The Resonant felt it in their bones—a sudden, deep settling. The nauseating vertigo of the thin spots evaporated. The iron girders snapped back into hard, unforgiving, beautiful rightangles. The shadows aligned with the light. 

For the first time in their history, the Resonant were able to look directly at the corner of a building, and it remained steadfastly, stubbornly square. 

But the Power was not finished. Benevolence requires a reckoning with the lost.

The Power pressed its hands flat against the newly stabilized cliff face and closed its glowing eyes. It tuned its hearing past the *thump* of the core, past the hiss of the vents, listening for the faint, dissonant whispers trapped within the strata. 

*The Lost.* The ones who had slipped through the softening geometry and been accreted into the walls. They were not dead; they were stretched thin, screaming in a frequency no one could answer. 

The Power sang to them. It vibrated at a frequency of profound drawing—a magnetic lullaby. 

The basalt began to weep. Not with condensation, but with a shimmering, silver fluid that pushed its way out of the porous stone. Slowly, agonizingly, the fluid began to pool on the gantryways, coalescing, rising, and taking shape. 

The Resonant fell to their knees on the damp metal gratings. 

From the stone, figures emerged. They were naked, shivering, and disoriented, their skin glowing with a faint, residual silver light. They fell into the arms of the Resonant—ancestors, children, siblings who had been swallowed by the glitches months, years, or generations ago, now extruded back into the realm of the living. 

The reunion was silent, marked only by the clutching of felted garments, the sliding of brass rebreathers, and the deep, chest-rattling hums of weeping joy.

The Power watched, its vast heart satisfied. 

Before it departed, it knew it must leave a governor—a mechanism to ensure the machine did not tear itself apart again as it continued to build the planet. 

The Power knelt in the center of the basalt plaza. It reached a hand deep into the cooling crust of the earth, plunging its arm up to the shoulder into the magma and iron below. When it withdrew its hand, it pulled up a single, massive pillar of an alloy unknown to the universe. It was pale, impossibly smooth, and thrummed with a gentle, self-sustaining kinetic energy.

It was a tuning fork the size of a spire. 

Whenever the giant, subterranean drill-bits groaned, whenever a new layer of the world was violently extruded, this tuning fork would catch the chaotic vibration and instantly harmonize it, broadcasting a wave of stability across the plateau. The physics of the world would no longer fray; they would bend, beautifully and safely, around the noise.

Its work done, the Power did not demand worship. It did not linger to be made a king. 

It simply stepped back, allowing its form to unravel into the perpetual damp, becoming one with the fine, ceaseless condensation that slicked the metal gantries. 

The world remained loud. The twilight remained bruised and unbroken. The *thump... thump... thump* of the dormant god still echoed from the deep, dictating the pendulum-walk of the inhabitants. They would still farm the chitinous scavengers; they would still drink the potent moss-liquor. 

But as the Resonant walked their web-worked bridges, carrying their newly returned kin, they no longer wore the lead beads of fear. They walked with their heads held high, able, finally, to look at the sharp, magnificent edges of their home without terror. They lived, now, not in a reality that was breaking apart, but in a masterpiece that was simply, and safely, still being born.
