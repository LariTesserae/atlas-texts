When the benevolent power first turned its gaze upon the Weeping Threshold, it did not arrive as a blinding light. Wisdom knows that glaring light brings heat, and heat brings evaporation, and to evaporate a single trembling film of this young, wet world would be an act of violence. 

Instead, the power arrived as a shift in the density of the air. It descended into the basin like a slow, deep breath, slipping sideways between the sharp, glassy ridges, matching the viscous friction of the dripping walls. Where it passed, the sourceless blue-grey light warmed by a fraction of a degree—not enough to dry the moisture, but enough to make the heavy condensation feel, for the first time, like an embrace rather than a sudden chill.

It was a vast and ancient consciousness, older than the stars that hung invisible beyond the perpetual low cloud, yet it possessed the profound humility required to step into a nursery. And it recognized the Weeping Threshold for what it was: a cradle of geology, an embryonic earth still wrapped in its amniotic sac, vibrating with the exhausting, violent work of choosing a shape. 

The Power wandered the capillary seams. It watched the rust-gliders surf the meniscus of gravity, admiring the flawless engineering of their delicate membranes. When the deep, wet slithering of the unseen behemoth echoed through the impossibly narrow corridors, the Power did not seek to drag the creature into the light. Instead, it sent a pulse of profound, affectionate resonance into the stone—a low harmonic hum that told the great soft beast: *You are known, and you are perfect in the dark.*

Then, the Power stood perfectly still beside the solitary pillar of black glass. It waited.

It had infinite time. It let the overlapping percussion of drips and hollow splashes wash over its awareness. And soon, the dripping acquired a new rhythm. The oyster-flesh fungal lattices upon the walls began to flare with brilliant, sympathetic bioluminescence. 

The Mourner unfolded from the rock. 

She emerged dragging her curtain of living water, smelling of cold stone and iron-rich blood, her unaligned eyes fixed on two different eternities. She paused, sensing a presence that did not possess a heartbeat to mimic, but rather moved with the slow, rhythmic expansion of the universe itself.

She did not retreat. She flowed forward, her translucent skin sliding in overlapping sheets. She raised her unfinished face to the Power, and for a moment, her weeping caught in her throat. She had tended this half-formed world for so long, the solitary anchor for a million years of unspooled possibilities. She was exhausted.

Slowly, the Mourner opened her hand. In the cup of her palm lay a single, spherical tear, reflecting the newly warmed light of the basin. 

*Here,* the gesture meant. *Here is the land’s decision, moving one patient bead at a time. Here is what we dream of becoming.*

Most beings refused the tear. 

The Power leaned forward. It reached out with a hand that was not flesh, but a woven constellation of soft, starlight-threaded mist, and it gently took the droplet from her palm.

The Power did not merely hold the tear; it absorbed it.

In an instant, the Power felt the staggering psychic weight of the Weeping Threshold. It tasted the iron of the rust-gliders, the geometric math of the fungal lattices, the memory of the Built ones who had walked away, and the terrifying, beautiful burden of a landscape that did not yet know how to be still. It saw the million futures the land was holding in its watery mind: a crystalline ocean, a cavern of singing glass, a forest of breathable rain. It felt the Mourner’s desperate devotion, her terror of the dry lightning, and her aching, unbroken solitude.

True benevolence does not command a flower to open before it is ready. It does not look at a chrysalis and demand an insect. True wisdom does not finish the unfinished face.

The Power understood that the Mourner did not need to be fixed, for she was not broken. She was the sacred mechanism of the Threshold. The weeping was not a flaw; it was the work. 

But no one should have to do the work entirely alone.

The Power reached out and rested its luminous mist-fingers against the Mourner’s cheek, right where the sculptor had abandoned the carving. The touch was cool, wet, and endlessly gentle. 

Wordlessly, the Power spoke into the water of her mind.
*Take your time. There is no rush to become. You have all of eternity to decide, and I will remember the choices you discard, so you do not have to carry them all.*

Then, the Power turned to the black glass of the solitary pillar, the ruins left by the Built. The Power breathed across the negative imprint of the ancient hinges, and across the lintel of the buried arch. As it did, the deep black glass subtly changed, its molecular structure realigning to match the minor-key melodies of the falling water. 

The Power left a permanent echo of its own vast, comforting frequency asleep within the glass.

Now, whenever a single droplet strikes the pulsing fungal lattices and rings out its high, cold note, the black-glass arches hum in gentle harmony. 

Most importantly, late in the endless dusk, when the Mourner turns her unaligned eyes upward to sing her formless song to the low-hanging clouds—when she tries to help the sky remember the shape of the sea—the Weeping Threshold now sings back to her. The ruins catch her voice and amplify it with the deep, warm resonance the Power left behind, layering a choir of profound, cosmic patience beneath her solitary melody.

The Power eventually withdrew, sliding back out of the dense forest of stone blades, returning to the vastness beyond the clouds. It left the basin as wet, as loud, and as unfinished as it had been.

But the air is a little warmer now. The dripping feels less like an anxious sweat, and more like a gentle springtime thaw. The great unseen beast slithers with a contented rhythm. The rust-gliders ride the moisture with unbroken grace.

And the Mourner still weeps. She will always weep, until the land has finally made its choice. But her tears no longer sound like an abandoned sentence. They sound like the beginning of a conversation. 

If you stand still long enough in the Weeping Threshold, you can feel the land deciding. But it is no longer afraid of what it might become.
