To be a power of true benevolence and wisdom is to understand that true perfection is rarely comfortable, and that to alter a world too drastically is to unmake the souls shaped by it. 

When the gaze of this Great Presence sweeps across the turning earth and snags upon this jagged, high-altitude sanctuary, it does not arrive with thunder or blazing chariots. It arrives as an infinite, quiet attention. The Being peers through the bruising blue of the sky and the churning seas of cloud below, focusing down upon the plateau. It feels the metallic tang in the thin air. It observes the peculiar way the light bends at the precipice. It sees the delicate, impossible tapestry of life clinging to the young, sharp stone.

The Being watches the Windborne Kin. It traces the translucent, mottled gray of their skin, the flutter of their feathery down, the grace of their wiry, careful steps. It hears the rhythmic *tap-tap* of their stones, a language forged in the hollows of silence. It sees the Frostbound Spirits, those echoing entities of frost and shadow, weaving indifferently through the blinding light. 

A lesser god—one of vanity or impulsive pity—might look at the shivering Kin, the barren slate, and the hovering emptiness, and wave a hand to transform the plateau. A lesser god would summon fertile soil, warm breezes, and rushing rivers.

But this Power is wise. It knows that to warm the air would stifle the Kin’s lungs, meant for a thin and fragile breath. To blunt the young, biting peaks (erosion at 1.2) would be to erase the geometry that the Kin’s hands and feet have mastered. To flood the plateau with plenty would shatter the sacred, precarious dance among the tough shrubs, the iridescent insects, the wheeling hawks, and the stealthy Kin. To soften this world would be to destroy everything that makes it beautiful.

So, the Being decides it will not conquer the harshness of the plateau. Instead, it will hallow it.

The Power leans close, its presence felt as a sudden, profound stillness beneath the ever-present wind. 

First, it offers a blessing of **Endurance**. The Being’s awareness sinks deep into the bedrock of the young mountain, down into the violent tectonic roots that thrust this island into the sky. With a gentle, invisible touch, it binds the stone. The plateau will never crumble. The sheer cliffs will not shear away into the misty abyss. No earthquake will shake this meager hamlet of rock into the void. The stage upon which the Kin play out their fierce, small lives is made eternal. 

Second, it offers a blessing of **Providence**. The Being does not summon a lake or a rainstorm, but it touches the unseen currents of the air. It weaves a subtle celestial law into the atmospheric weirdness (0.7): whenever the mosses turn brown and the hollows dry, the clouds churning below will always swell upward, spilling their ghostly mist over the edges of the cliff to leave just enough glistening condensation in the stone hollows. The tiny alpine flowers—ice-pale and blood-red—will never completely fail to bloom. The Kin will always find just enough. Scarcity will remain their teacher, but starvation will never be their executioner. 

Third, the Power turns its attention to the crude, rough-hewn central altar. The Being recognizes the profound humility of the Kin, who offer bits of quartz to a sky they view as merciless. The Power reaches down and touches the altar. 

The slate and granite do not change shape, but the streaks of quartz within the altar stones suddenly ignite with a faint, inner luminescence. It is not heat—it is something deeper. A subterranean warmth of the spirit. From this day forward, when a Windborne Kin presses their long, elegant fingers against the altar during the bitterest winter nights, a soothing resonance will flow into their marrow. It will not warm the air, but it will banish the despair from their hearts, reminding them that they are tethered to something vast and living.

Finally, the Great Being bridges the veil. 

The Power sees the Frostbound Spirits lingering in the elongated shadows. It sees the Kin stepping carefully around them. The Being breathes a quiet frequency into the thin air, altering its strange acoustic rules for one miraculous, fleeting moment. 

A Windborne child, crouched near the edge of a jagged ridge, taps a stone. *Tap. Tap.* 
A Frostbound Spirit, a swirl of chilling mist, drifts past. 
Because of the Being’s touch, the sound does not fade. The child’s tap resonates, and for the first time since the mountains were born, the Spirit taps back. Not with a stone, but with a sudden, localized chime of ice cracking gently in the shape of a melody. 

The child freezes, slit-pupils widening in the extreme light. The Spirit pauses. In that frozen second, the flesh and the ethereal understand one another. They are both born of the cold. They are both children of the edge. The plateau is suddenly no longer a place of lonely coexistence, but of silent fellowship. The spirits will remain indifferent in their nature, but they will slowly become guardians, bending the shadows to hide the Kin when the great birds of prey hunt too close. 

Its work complete, the Benevolent Power prepares to withdraw. But before it leaves, it wishes to let the plateau know it has been Seen.

The blinding, merciless light (2.9) that blankets the plateau shifts. For the span of three heartbeats, the harshness of the sun changes entirely in quality, transitioning from a glaring, bruising white into a soft, liquid gold. The metallic tang in the air becomes the scent of fresh snow and ozone. The wind stops its moan and hums a low, resonant chord that vibrates through the soles of the Kin’s feet. 

Every tiny rodent stops its skittering. The hawk suspends mid-air on a perfect updraft. The Kin step out from their mortarless walls of stone and look up. They do not hide their faces. Their downy hair stands on end. They feel a gaze upon them—not of a predator, not of a merciless void, but of an infinite, tender parent who looks upon their ragged, tough, brilliant existence and pronounces it *masterpiece*. 

Then, the golden hue fades. The sky returns to its bruising blue. The harsh, biting cold resumes, and the wind gives its familiar moan. 

The Being pulls back through the threshold, leaving the plateau to its isolation and its struggle. Yet, the emptiness is no longer hollow. The isolation is no longer a threat. The plateau remains an extreme, precarious island in the sky, but it has been consecrated. It is now entirely, undeniably, and forever, a Holy Place.
