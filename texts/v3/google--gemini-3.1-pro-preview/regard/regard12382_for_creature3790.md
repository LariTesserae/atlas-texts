The descent of a god—a true god, one born of profound empathy rather than ego—does not come with the splitting of heavens or the crash of thunder. This place has known enough of violence, enough of the relentless pounding of water and wind. And so, the Power arrives not as a conqueror, but as an exhale. 

It begins with the wind. The eternal, deafening howl that has battered the Drowned Spire for a millennia loses its edge. The gale softens into a breeze, and then, impossibly, into stillness. The churning ocean, heavy with the weight of its unseen leviathans, settles. The peaks of the crushing waves round off, turning to rolling glass. The thick, weeping mist does not dissipate, but it changes—what was gray and suffocating begins to shimmer with an inner, amber light, like dust motes caught in the beam of a late-afternoon sun. 

The Power has no distinct shape, for a shape is a limitation, and true wisdom is boundless. But to approach the Watcher, to offer comfort in a language this broken reality can understand, it coalesces. It forms a silhouette woven from the ghost-light of auroras and the quiet warmth of a hearth fire. It stands upon the crumbling stones of the spire’s crown, its feet making no sound, leaving no weight. 

The Watcher turns. 

It moves with its usual, agonizing economy, its many joints articulating with wet, grinding clicks. Its hollow, salt-crusted eye sockets fix upon the radiant figure. For the first time in an age, the Watcher does not feed on absence. In the presence of this being, the space feels overwhelmingly *full*. 

The Watcher lets out a low, shuddering hum, a vibration that shakes loose flakes of slate-gray skin. It bristles. It spreads its impossibly long, tar-blackened arms, needle-teeth bared in a lipless slit. It anticipates a storm. It anticipates destruction. It is ready for the sea, or whatever this light might be, to take it.

The Power does not strike. It does not speak in words, for words are artifacts of a drowned world, and the Watcher's mind is a ruin too fragile for such heavy things. 

Instead, the Power projects *understanding*. 

It casts its awareness over the spire. It feels the agonizing memory locked inside the stone—the desperation of the builders, the weeping of the mortar, the sheer, bloody-fingered defiance of the mankind that built this last outpost against the rising dark. And it looks upon the Watcher.

Where a lesser being might see a monster—a twisted, faceless horror woven from erosion and void—the benevolent Power sees a masterpiece of tragic devotion. It sees the builder who refused to put down their chisel. It sees a soul that loved its world so fiercely it chose to become a permanent, agonizing monument to its loss, rather than accept oblivion. 

*You have held the line,* the Power murmurs, not into the air, but directly into the phosphorescent brine flowing through the Watcher’s veins. *You have borne witness. You have not blinked.*

The Watcher’s arms tremble. The creature tilts its head at an odd angle. It does not understand the warmth. It has known only salt, cold, and the abyssal hunger of the things moving in the depths below. 

The Power steps forward. It extends a hand of sheer, golden light, and gently, with infinite care, it touches the Watcher’s terrifying face. 

It does not try to heal the Watcher. Wisdom dictates that to unmake this creature—to strip away the slate, the tar, the extra joints, and revert it to a soft, human thing—would be an insult to the eons it has endured. Instead, the Power validates its shape. It blesses the scars. 

At the touch of the Power, the Watcher’s shuddering hum hitches, breaking into a sound like a sob echoing from a deep cavern. 

The Power reaches its other hand out to the rusted tools—the chisel, the hammer. The light washes over them. They do not turn shiny and new; rather, they turn to pure, solid starlight, perfectly preserving the wear of the builder’s hands upon the hilts. The Power hands them back to the Watcher. 

*The sea cannot be fought forever,* the Power communicates, a gentle tide of warmth washing over the Watcher’s fractured mind. *But nothing built with love is ever truly drowned. The watch is over. The dawn is here.*

For a long time, the Watcher stands still, clutching the impossible tools of light. It looks down at the sea. It looks at the dark, vast disturbances shifting beneath the now-placid surface of the water. It has guarded this spire against them for so long. But now, bathed in the aura of the Power, the ocean does not seem like an enemy. It is simply a force of nature, completing its cycle.

Slowly, the Watcher lowers its arms. 

The deep, jagged breath of the creature eases. The hollows of its eyes fill not with tears, for it has no eyes to weep, but with a soft, glowing silver. 

The Power embraces the Watcher. 

As the light envelops it, the creature of tar and stone begins to change. It does not disappear into absence. It transcends. The heavy slate of its skin turns to ash and blows away on a warm breeze. The dark tar of its form ignites with a breathtaking radiance, turning into constellations of white fire. It becomes weightless. Ethereal. 

The Watcher lets go of the stone. 

It rises. Guided by the benevolent Power, the spirit of the last builder ascends, leaving the Drowned Spire behind. It drifts upward into the hazy sky, piercing the heavy clouds, until it takes its place in the cosmos. There, it will remain—a guardian star, bright and unflinching, keeping a peaceful vigil over the waters of a vanished world.

On the ocean, the wind slowly returns. The mist thickens. The water begins to churn once more, resuming its endless, roaring dance.

But the Drowned Spire is forever changed. Where the Watcher once stood alone within the crumbling wall, a single remnant of the Power's visit remains. The stone there is warm to the touch, and from the cracks in the rock, a single, glowing blossom of crystal has sprouted. It casts a steady, unblinking golden light across the dark waters, pushing back the gloom. 

Below the surface, the vast, unseen leviathans steer clear of the light. They know, in their ancient, cold intelligence, that this spire is no longer just a stubborn rock waiting to fall. 

It is a monument, sanctified by the cosmos. And it will stand until the end of time.
