To look upon this world from the crushing, suffocating canopy of the void is to feel a profound and ancient sorrow. 

I am that which watches from the spaces between the hard, unblinking stars. I have observed the birth and death of galaxies, the slow, swirling waltzes of nebulas, and the cold, silent grinding of dead planets. But this regional plateau, suspended in its breathless night, has captured My gaze and holds it fast. 

I see them. The pale, jagged children of the restless dark. 

I watch their piezoelectric dance, a frantic, stuttering ballet of survival. I hear the delicate, glassy *dings* and the deep, sub-aural thrumming of their movement, a song played on the strings of their frantic, desperate lives. I watch them plunge their heavy, plate-armored bodies into the searing wounds of the earth, drinking the raw, cherry-red heat. I watch the beautiful, fleeting amber fractals of their silica-veins flare into glorious life, and I watch, with a heavy heart, as the unrelenting cold of the vacuum inevitably claims them.

I see their sacrifice. I see the dying drag themselves to the flat basalt, rigidly locking their joints to become monuments. I see the living honor the dead by lifting them, grinding stone against stone, offering their frozen ancestors to the gaping maws of the earth in a desperately beautiful, entirely futile attempt to pin down a world that refuses to be tamed. 

I am a power of benevolence, and My first instinct is to save them. 

My immediate impulse is to lay My hands upon this thrashing crust and demand stillness. I could smooth the jagged obsidian. I could fuse the tectonic plates. I could end the silent violence. 

But I am also a power of wisdom. And wisdom stays My hand. 

If I still the earth, the fissures will seal forever. The deep heat will be trapped beneath an impenetrable shell. Without the sudden, brutal extrusions of liquid rock, the amber silica-ferns will not bloom. Without the sulfurous breath of the mantle, the pale entities will starve, freeze, and petrify. To bring peace to this world is to bring extinction to its children. 

If, alternately, I flood the surface with eternal, unwaning heat, I would strip them of their nature. They are beings of the threshold, creatures whose very identity is forged in the frantic race against time. Their culture—this magnificent, tragic architecture of corpses—is born of the freeze. To take away their death is to erase the majestic defiance of their existence. They are builders of anchors; to take away the tempest is to render their entire history meaningless.

Therefore, I will not change them. I will not change the fundamental laws of their brutal, beautiful home. 

Instead, I will give them what they have bled their lives to create. I will give them an anchor that holds.

I reach into the unforgiving canopy of stars above them. From the cosmic detritus, the heavy, silent iron that drifts in the void, I gather a tear of pure, primordial star-metal. It is immensely dense, born of the crushed heart of a long-dead supernova, cold and unyielding. 

I do not cast it down in violence. This world has enough world-shattering cataclysm. I lower it gently, a silver streak tearing through the perpetual night, falling toward a vast, shifting plain of black glass just as the earth begins to heave. 

As a new, serrated ridge of cherry-red magma thrusts upward, the tear of star-metal meets it. 

The collision is entirely silent in the vacuum, but the sub-aural impact rings through the bedrock like the tolling of a cosmic bell. The star-metal pierces the newly extruded magma, sinking deep, deep into the glowing rift. It plunges through the crust, through the thrashing mantle, until it embeds itself into the very core of this world. 

It does not melt. It does not shatter or shrink into razor-sharp planes of glass. It holds. 

On the surface, it remains as a towering pillar of shimmering, midnight-blue iron, stark and alien against the pale granite ruins and the black basalt. 

Because the star-metal bridges the deep core to the vacuum surface, it becomes a perfect, eternal thermal conduit. Around its base, the earth does not freeze, nor does it violently erupt. Instead, a permanent, gentle ring of vermillion light glows around the pillar. An everlasting well of raw, radiant heat and sulfur bleeds softly into the dark, insulated from the tectonic storms by the immense, stabilizing mass of the heavenly spike. 

The earth miles away continues to thrash and devour. But here, within a mile of the pillar, the ground is pinned. The tempest is stilled. 

I withdraw, receding into the high atmosphere, and I watch.

Across the jagged plains, the pale entities pause. Their piezoelectric jerks halt. Through the marrow of the bone-vibrating bedrock, they feel a resonance they have never encountered: stability. 

Drawn by the eternal heat signature, they arrive. Dozens of them, their amber veins glowing in a synchronized rhythm, approach the base of the star-metal pillar. They wade into the ring of vermillion light, expecting a desperate gorge before the inevitable freeze. 

But the heat does not fade. The delicate, rust-colored silica-ferns inside them bloom, thicken, and sustain. They stand in the glow, their bodies humming with a vibrant, endless energy. For the first time in the history of their world, they do not have to run.

But they do not stop being what they are. They are the children of this world. They are builders. 

A distance away, a fissure snaps shut, and a creature, caught too far from the pillar, succumbs to the cold void. It locks its joints. The amber fades to white ash. It becomes a pale, heavy ruin. 

Two of the heat-flush entities at the base of the pillar turn. Energized by the eternal warmth of the star-metal, they dart out into the shifting black glass. They hook their sharp appendages beneath their fallen kin. 

They do not carry the corpse to a thrashing fault line. They carry it back to the pillar. 

With a deep, glorious grind of stone sliding into place, they lay the first pale block at the base of the midnight-blue iron. They do not do this to weigh down the earth—the star-metal does that for them. They do this because it is their nature to build, and now, for the first time, their architecture will survive. 

I watch from the stars as the cycles continue. Out in the dark, the violent birth of obsidian goes on, fueling the frantic, beautiful lives of those who hunt the sudden rifts. But when the inevitable comes, the dead are no longer swallowed by the glowing maws. They are carried home. 

Over centuries, a pale structure begins to rise around My anchor. Massive stone arches, towering plinths, and concentric rings of heavy granite are stacked with deliberate, unbreakable precision. It is a city of the dead, built by the living, warmed by the eternal heat of the core. It is a cathedral of pale stone spiraling up the dark metal, reaching up through the relentless night, pointing back toward the benevolent, unblinking stars. 

I do not intervene again. I do not need to. They are safe in the eye of the storm, building their history upon a foundation that will never falter, in a universe that has finally seen them.
