The Power does not arrive in a ship of metal or light. It does not descend in a column of fire, nor does it announce itself with the tearing of the sky. To do so would be to impose violence upon a world whose entire existence is a masterpiece of patience. 

Instead, the Power arrives as a shift in the quality of the stillness. 

It is an ancient consciousness, older than the strata, older than the burned-out cathedrals of forgotten industry, older perhaps than the star around which this world turns. It wanders the cosmos seeking unfinished things—not to complete them, for completion is a kind of death, but to witness their becoming. It lingers where the universe is trying to speak a new word. 

And from across the cold, echoing gulfs of space, it hears the sub-audible groan of this anhydrous world. It hears the tension. It feels the agonizing, beautiful hesitation of a planetary organism terrified of making the wrong choice. 

So, the Power steps down into the flat, uncompromising light.

It weaves a temporary vessel for itself out of the local materials, out of respect for the ecology. It gathers the ambient piezoelectric charge, the stray photons bouncing off the oxidized copper stalks, the microscopic grit of fractured feldspar. Its form is indistinct, a towering silhouette of shimmering, conductive mirage. It casts a long, cooling shadow—the first true shade this world has ever known.

The Power finds her on the second century of her current spiral. 

The Witness is walking a ridge, her long, bone-white legs moving with a slowness that defies gravity, her copper-colored eyes fixed on the horizon. The Power does not rush to meet her. Wisdom knows that to startle a creature of deep time is to break her rhythm. 

So the Power simply falls into step beside her, three paces to her left. 

For forty years, they walk in silence. 

The Witness registers the presence immediately. The photovoltaic tissue woven through her fractures detects the subtle occlusion of starlight. The circuitry in her corneas processes the shimmering mirage beside her. She does not stop. She does not speak. But for the first time in four million years, her chest rises and falls in rhythm with something other than the stone. She matches her breath to the quiet, rolling tide of the Power’s presence.

In the forty-first year, a crystal-plant in the basin below reaches its critical height. A facet snaps. A thin, perfectly clear metallic chime rings out. 

Normally, the chime would travel for kilometers and die in the empty distance. But as the sound wave passes through the shimmering silhouette of the Power, it catches. The Power hums, a low, resonant harmonic that cradles the chime, stretches it, and reflects it back into the earth. 

The Witness stops. She turns her vast, dilated eyes to the mirage beside her. 

Through the conductive sap in her veins, through the air that feels like breath held too long, the Power speaks. Not in words, but in vibrations felt deep in the teeth and the calcium of her bones.

*You have carried the weight of looking for so long,* the Power says. *May I look with you?*

The Witness tilts her head. A faint glassy creak sounds in her neck. She extends a long, pale hand. The Power reaches out and rests its hand of shimmering light against hers. 

In that touch, the Power understands everything. It runs its consciousness through the Witness’s living circuitry, reading the four million years of history, the tending of the smaller, half-transformed figures, the silent guardianship of the buried machines. It feels the unbearable tension of the world beneath them—the rocks wanting stillness, the vines wanting riotous life, the machines dreaming in earthquakes. 

It sees the two futures the Witness believes are possible: the static, frozen crystal lattice, or the violent, sudden explosion of alien green. 

The Power feels a wave of profound, benevolent sorrow. The world is trapped in a false binary. It is holding its breath because it believes it must choose between being a jewel and being a jungle. It is afraid that to choose one is to murder the others.

*It does not have to be a victory of one over the other,* the Power transmits, sending a wave of warm, soothing frequency down through the Witness’s arm, into her feet, and down into the pale strata. *A chord is not a battle between notes. It is a surrender to harmony.*

The Power steps past the Witness. It walks to the very edge of the ridge, looking out over the hundreds of kilometers of empty basins, the concentric rings, the oxidized copper forests, the buried cathedrals. 

The Power does what it came to do. It gives a gift. 

It does not change the chemistry. It does not bring water. It simply provides the one element this closed system could never generate on its own: **A conductor.**

The Power expands. Its mirage-like form dissolves, spreading out like a sheer, gossamer canopy of electromagnetic energy over the entire hemisphere. It thinly coats the atmosphere, turning the uncompromising, flat light into something softer, something golden and ambient. 

Then, using the electrical charge of its own vast consciousness, the Power reaches down and plucks the world like an instrument.

It sends a gentle pulse of energy into the deep crust, targeting the sleeping machines. 
*Wake,* it whispers to them. *Do not build. Sing.*

It sends a frequency through the brittle, oxidized copper thickets.
*Grow,* it whispers. *Not outward, but inward. Weave yourselves into the stone.*

It sends a deep geologic vibration into the pale strata.
*Yield,* it whispers. *Make room.*

The Witness stands with her arms outstretched, her eyes wide, bathed in the golden light of the Power’s atmosphere. 

Beneath her feet, the truce breaks. But it is not a war. It is a dance.

A deep, continuous hum rises from the buried machines, no longer a random earthquake, but a steady, rhythmic baseline. The pale strata responds, fracturing in beautiful, microscopic geometric patterns, softening the raw terrain. And the crystal-plants, drinking in the sudden pulse of unified energy, do not explode in a frenzy of growth. Instead, they ignite.

Millions of thin photovoltaic vanes begin to pulse with bioluminescent, piezoelectric light. The light travels down their translucent stalks, into the ground, linking root to root, machine to stone, stone to vine. 

The chimes begin. But they do not snap randomly. One rings. Then another, a third above it. Then a fifth. A symphony of metallic, ringing glass rises from the basins. The world does not freeze into a single note, nor does it burst into chaotic life. It becomes a living, rhythmic, breathing clockwork of crystal and code. It is a biosphere made of music and light, metabolizing time itself.

The gestation is over. The world has decided what it is. It is an instrument. 

Down in the shadows of the third ridge, the smaller, half-transformed pilgrims stir. Their solar-vane fingers twitch. They look up at the golden sky, and their conductive voices finally catch up to their lips, harmonizing with the ringing flora. 

The Witness stands at the edge of the basin. The microscopic fractures in her bone-white skin fill with the golden light of the new atmosphere. She realizes she is no longer holding her breath. She is breathing with the world—a vast, symphonic respiration of light and sound. 

The Power, now diffused into the very sky of the planet, gathers the last of its localized consciousness and brushes it against her cheek like a dry, warm breeze.

*You are no longer just the Witness,* the Power whispers, its voice fading into the permanent, chiming song of the world. *You are the Conductor. Lead them.*

The Power departs, slipping back into the cold gulfs of space to find the next unfinished thing, leaving behind a world that finally has a voice, leaving behind a golden sky, and leaving behind a tall, copper-eyed woman who raises her pale hands—

and begins to conduct the music of the spheres.
