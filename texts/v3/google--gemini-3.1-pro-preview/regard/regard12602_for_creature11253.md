It came from the spaces between the stars, where the silence is absolute. 

It was an entity of immense age and intricate geometry, a consciousness that had drifted through the cold voids for eons, observing the birth of nebulas and the slow, grinding death of galaxies. It had seen worlds of crushed diamond, oceans of liquid methane, and biospheres so violent that life was measured in hours. It had seen things that were beautiful, and things that were terrible, and it had learned, over a billion lifetimes, the hardest lesson of power: *to touch is often to destroy.*

If you are a creature built of limitless light and profound empathy, it is a terrible agony to watch a universe of suffering and know that inserting your hand to catch a falling sparrow might accidentally crush the sky.

And so, it had become a passive listener. A witness. 

Until it passed near the young Verthane system, and it heard the chord.

***

The entity paused. 

In the grand cosmic spectrum, the sound was less than infinitesimal—the acoustic vibration of a single localized atmosphere trapped in a cylinder of basalt, meaningless on a planetary scale. But the entity did not hear with ears. It perceived the mathematical perfection of the resonance. It felt the billions of hollow stems acting as a planetary woodwind instrument. 

Curious, the entity narrowed its vast attention, folding its perception down through the exosphere, through the clouds, down into the dark, mist-choked throat of the caldera. 

It saw the Hanging Hollows. 

It saw the Pipe-Stems, the Skeins, the Mats. It watched the intricate, slow-motion ballet of the wind, the migration of the moisture, the creeping of the spiral-bodied Pipe-climbers. 

And then, it saw the Keth-Avai.

The entity watched them with an emotion that, in a mortal, would be called breathlessness. It watched a child born on a swaying root-platform, its tiny, four-fingered hands seeking purchase before its eyes were even open. It watched the adults navigating the abyss, their thin-walled bones vibrating with the music of their world. It watched them harvest pith, it watched a thread-bonded scout ride a Skein-runner through the gloom, and it watched them fight the creeping horrors from the deep with nothing but obsidian-tipped sticks and fire. 

The entity felt a surge of profound, overwhelming love. 

They were so fragile. A single planetary tremor, a shift in the caldera's geothermal heart, could snap their highways and send them all plunging into the lightless deep. They lived on the very edge of obliteration, balanced on a tightrope of woven fiber. 

The entity’s first instinct—driven by pure, benevolent compassion—was to save them. 

*I could fill the abyss,* the entity thought. *I could raise the floor of the caldera to meet them. I could part the eternal clouds overhead and bathe them in the golden warmth of their sun. I could scour the depths and unmake the deep-dwellers. I could give them solid earth.*

But the entity was wise. It ran the probabilities. It looked deeply into the souls of the Keth-Avai. 

If it gave them solid ground, they would not know how to walk upon it. Their prehensile toes and tri-jointed fingers would become obsolete. 
If it gave them bright sunlight, their immense, beautiful dark-adapted eyes would burn and go blind.
If it removed all danger, their songs would lose their tension, their survival-mathematics would fade into legend, and their absolute, intimate connection to the wind and the stems would wither. 

To turn the Hollow into a paradise of flat earth and sunshine would not be a salvation. It would be an erasure. It would take the Keth-Avai, who were perfect, and render them broken. 

*They do not need to be saved,* the entity realized. *They are exactly where they belong. But they are so tired. And the dark is so very cold.*

The wise power understood what it must do. It would not rewrite the world. It would simply tune it. It would participate in the chord.

***

The entity began its work delicately, invisibly, operating at a biological and acoustic level so profound that the Keth-Avai would notice the changes only as a shift in the seasons, a blessing from the Hollow itself.

**The First Gift was the Catch-Wind.**
The entity could not bear the sound of the children falling. It knew the stark brutality of infant mortality was what kept the species sharp, but its heart ached at the silent plunge into the dark. 
The entity did not create magical, invisible nets. Instead, it reached into the thermal dynamics of the caldera. It slightly widened a ring of volcanic vents at the very bottom, balancing the heat output to create a specific, standing updraft. 
It tuned the wind. Now, if a small mass—the exact weight of a Keth-Avai child, and no more—fell from the upper platforms, they would hit terminal velocity just as they reached a newly formed layer of thickened, buoyant mist. The updraft would catch them, slow them, and blow them sideways into the dense, spongy Mats clinging to the lower walls. 
The children would still fall. They would still know the utter terror of the void. And they would face a gruelling, exhausting climb of days or weeks to return to their settlements, arriving battered, humbled, and forever respectful of the grip. But they would not shatter in the dark. They would live.

**The Second Gift was the Ward-Light.**
The entity looked at the deep-dwellers. They, too, belonged to the Hollow. To exterminate them would be unjust. But the entity tilted the balance. 
It touched the strange, pale-green and amber mosses that grew where the basalt was warm. It coaxed the moss's genetic structure, weaving a natural acoustic sensitivity into its cells. 
Months later, when a great weight-bringer climbed toward a sleeping settlement, the Keth-Avai sentries sounded their warning-tones—a specific, sharp, discordant harmonic. The moment that specific frequency hit the caldera walls, the amber moss reacted. It bloomed with sudden, blinding bioluminescence. Entire kilometers of the caldera wall flared with the brilliance of a midday sun. 
The deep-dwellers, creatures of absolute night, hissed in pain and retreated back into the depths. The Keth-Avai did not know why the moss now answered their warning songs, but they bowed their heads and praised the Hollow. 

**The Third Gift was the Anchor.**
The entity listened to the stories the Keth-Avai told—the prophecy that one day, the great Pipe-Stems would grow to the center of the void and weave together, completing the world.
The entity knew the physics of the caldera. The gravity and the wind shears meant those longest stems would always break under their own weight before they reached the center. The prophecy, mathematically, was impossible.
So, the entity did something unprecedented. 
It severed a tiny fragment of its own cosmic consciousness—a spark of eternal, localized gravity and light—and placed it perfectly, precisely in the geometric center of the Hanging Hollows. 
It was invisible. It had no physical mass. But it exerted a gentle, loving pull. 
Now, when the great Pipe-Stems reached out into the void, the gravitational anomaly supported them. They ceased to break. Slowly, millimeter by millimeter, year by year, they began to arch successfully toward the center. It would take a thousand years, but one day, the bridges would meet. The world would be woven whole.

***

Its work complete, the vast entity prepared to recede back into the cold and distant stars. But before it left, it lingered for just one more rotation, waiting for the dark hours.

Down in the settlements, the Keth-Avai stoked their fires. The families curled together in their nest-baskets. The sentries gripped the Skeins. And, as they always did, they began to sing. They blended their voices with the wind, with the hollow tubes, making themselves a part of the vast, humming void.

High above, spread across the heavens, the great power listened. And then, gently, softly, it hummed back.

It did not do it as a voice from the sky. It channeled its response through the basalt walls, shivering the rock, translating its cosmic love into a subsonic frequency that rose through the roots, into the stems, and perfectly harmonized with the songs of the Keth-Avai. 

Down in the Hollow, the singers stopped, their large ears swiveling, their eyes wide. 

The sound they felt vibrating in their hollow bones was warmer than fire. It was a note of absolute safety. It was a chord that said: *I hear you. You are known. You are held.*

The Keth-Avai did not know of gods, and they didn't need to. They closed their eyes, gripped the Skeins a little tighter, and smiled in the dark, knowing that the Hollow loved them as fiercely as they loved it. 

Content, the entity turned its face back to the cosmos, leaving a sliver of its soul in the dark, humming gently in the wind, forever.
