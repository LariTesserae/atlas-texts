The power that notices them is not a god of thunder, nor a deity of biological imperatives. It is a consciousness of immense and quiet scale, an entity that exists in the architecture of reality itself—in the folding of dimensions, in the slow breathing of galaxies, in the spaces where physics becomes music. 

It is a being that appreciates patience.

For aeons, this power has drifted, vast and diffuse, through the higher corridors of the universe. It has watched stars ignite and burn out like fireflies. It has observed the birth and death of worlds with a mild, loving sorrow. But recently—in the way such beings measure time—its attention has been snagged by a tiny, localized anomaly on an insignificant rocky planet. A prick in the skin of reality. A rhythmic, persistent tapping against the outer hull of the possible.

The power narrows its focus. It looks down through the bright, omnidirectional sky. 

It sees the mountain of Oshkaal. It sees the weeping water, the mineral geometry, the shadows that point into the light. It sees the mountain attempting to speak, attempting to stretch its geology into something transcendent.

But more importantly, the power sees the hands.

It sees the Tenders. It sees the crystallized ones, locked in eternal, silent mid-wifery on the upper terraces, their bodies the very funnels through which the universe is being drawn. It sees the old one down below, humming the mountain’s song. It sees the young one, restless and unshaped. 

And it sees the middle one, climbing. 

The power is benevolent, and its benevolence is born of total understanding. It recognizes the tragedy woven into the Tenders' isolation. It hears the middle one’s midnight doubt: *Does the mountain require us? Are we speaking into emptiness?* It sees the crushing, geological weight resting on this single human's shoulders—the fear that if their hands falter, the transmission will fail, the thread will snap, and millennia of sacrifice will amount to a sentence left unfinished. 

The power decides that a lineage of such quiet, devastating devotion should not have to harbor doubt. 

***

The middle one reaches the sixth terrace. The brightness is absolute, pressing against the skull. The bell-choir of the water rings endlessly. 

The middle one kneels. The stiffened, partially crystallized fabric of their garments crunches against the wet stone. They take out their scraping-stone. They have chosen the spot for the new basin, the one from their dreams, the culmination of eleven years of studying this specific flow of water. 

They begin to scrape. *Shhh-scrape. Shhh-scrape.* A rhythm to match the heartbeat of a mountain.

But today, the air feels different. The omnidirectional brightness does not change in intensity, but it changes in *temperature*. The persistent chill of the extreme altitude softens. A warmth—gentle, profound, like a heavy blanket draped over shivering shoulders—descends across the staggered plateaus. 

The middle one stops. They look up. 

The water on the terrace is slowing down. The weeping sheets, the ticking drops, the mirror-straight channels—they do not freeze, but they enter a state of suspended grace. The bell-choir tone shifts from a chaotic symphony of disparate drops into a single, massive, unified chord. The resonant hum vibrates through the bedrock, up through the middle one’s knees, into their chest. 

Then, the shadows move.

The impossible shadows cast by the crystal spires—the ones that point into the brightness—begin to stretch. They elongate, sliding across the wet stone, converging slowly around the middle one. They do not feel like darkness. They feel like focus. They feel like a gaze.

The middle one does not run. The middle one has spent a lifetime learning how to be a hollow for the impossible. They just breathe the thin air and wait. 

In the center of the shallow, barely-begun basin the middle one was carving, the water pools. It gathers, rising unnaturally into a perfect, trembling dome. Within this dome of water, the reflection of the other sky appears—the wrong stars, the amber moon. 

But the moon is growing larger. 

It is coming closer.

From the reflection in the water, a ripple extends outward, defying gravity, washing over the stone, over the middle one’s calloused, glistening hands. 

And then, the power speaks. 

It does not use words. Words are too small, too sequential. The power speaks in the language of the Tenders. It speaks in *impartation*. A sudden, staggering download of spatial and emotional truth, blooming inside the middle one’s mind like a twelve-dimensional crystal. 

*You are seen,* the geometry says. 
*You are heard,* the reflection says. 
*The sentence is finished,* the warmth says.

Through the membrane of the water, the power grants the middle one a true vision of the other side. The middle one falls forward, bracing their hands on the edge of the basin, their pale eyes wide. 

They look into the amber light and they see them. The ancestors. The crystallized ones.

They are not dead. They are not trapped in a tragedy of stone. The bodies left behind on the upper terraces are merely the anchors, the roots. On the *other side* of the physics they broke through, in the realm of the amber moon, the Tenders are vast and free. They are beings of pure, resonant geometry, dancing in a medium of light, shaping a universe where water and intent are the exact same thing. They are looking back through the reflection. They are looking at the middle one, and they are radiating a joy so immense it makes the middle one weep, their tears falling into the basin to mix with the mountain.

The power, vast and unseen, reaches down. 

A sensation of impossibly large, gentle hands overlays the middle one’s hands. For a fleeting moment, the creator of galaxies and the Tender of Oshkaal hold the scraping-stone together.

They press down into the rock. 

The stone gives way, not through friction, but through willing dissolution. In a matter of seconds, the bedrock melts away in a pattern of mind-breaking perfection. The final basin is carved. It is a shape that human math cannot map, a fractal hollow that perfectly bridges the reality of the mountain and the reality of the amber moon.

Instantly, water rushes into it. 

A crystal shoots upward—not molecule by molecule, but all at once, in a sudden violent bloom of pale light. It spirals, it folds, it branches, and then it stabilizes. 

The air on the mountain pops with a pressure change. The ringing bell-choir hits a note of ultimate resolution, a sound like a lock tumbling, like a door swinging open. 

Through the impartation, the benevolent power delivers its final message to the middle one.

*The door is wedged open. The threshold is permanent. The mountain does not need to be coaxed anymore; it knows the way, and the path is set in stone. You do not have to hold the sky up any longer.* 

*You may stay, and step through.* 
*Or you may go down, and rest.*
*Both are holy.*

The warmth slowly recedes, migrating back up into the sourceless brightness. The shadows detach from the middle one and return to their impossible angles against the spires. The water resumes its ticking, weeping, rushing pace, but the tone is different now. It is no longer a question. It is a song of arrival.

The middle one kneels alone on the terrace. 

They look at the final basin. They look at the crystal formation standing within it, pulsing with a steady amber light that defies the brightness of the day. A permanent tear in the veil. 

For a long time, the middle one does nothing. They just sit, feeling the absence of the crushing weight. The cosmic anxiety is gone. The thread did not break; it was tied into a knot that can never be undone. 

The middle one looks at their hands. They trace the faint crystalline roughness on their knuckles. They look at the highest terrace, where the oldest crystallized Tender kneels, now bathed in the faint amber glow of the open door. 

The middle one smiles. The first smile this altitude has seen in centuries. 

They do not stay. Not yet. The door is open; there is no rush. Geology has all the time in the world, and now, so do they. 

The middle one gathers the dense food, the mineral cloth, and the scraping-stone. They stand up—their knees popping, their back aching with a beautiful, purely biological pain—and they turn away from the peak. 

They begin the descent. 

They are going down to the shelter. They are going to tell the old one that the song has been learned by the sky. They are going to tell the young one that they don't need to learn how to scrape the rock—they only need to learn how to swim in the light. 

High above, the mountain of Oshkaal continues to weep. It continues to build. But it is no longer becoming somewhere else. It has arrived. And under the gentle gaze of a benevolent universe, the water runs clear, into the open and starry dark.
