Attention is a weight, and when it is cast by something vast and profoundly kind, it arrives like the warmth of a sunrise upon a cold stone.

It did not walk over the obsidian rim, for it was not a thing of legs and lungs. It seeped into the hollow as a shift in the atmosphere, a gentle unspooling of awareness. It was a wanderer of the cosmic tapestry, a tender of the great, rushing rivers of time and space, drawn here by an aberration. It had felt the snag in the fabric—a space where time had pooled and stagnated, catching like a burr in the wool of the universe.

The presence washed over the obsidian, tasting the youth of the rock. It brushed over the suspended hawk, marveling at the aerodynamic perfection held in perpetual stasis. It touched the ptarmigan, feeling the coiled kinetic energy of that never-completed step. It watched the single ripple in the pool make its agonizing, glacial journey to the edge and back.

It understood instantly what this place was. A chalice of pure potential. A geologic error where the present had swallowed both the past and the future. 

To many entities of power, this anomaly would be a puzzle to solve, or a blemish to erase. A less wise force would simply shatter the bubble. It would command the flow of time to resume. 

But wisdom is the understanding of consequences. 

If time were allowed to crash back into this hollow all at once, the floodwaters of sequence would be violent. The fresh obsidian would instantly scour into dust under the weight of un-fallen rain. The salamanders would wither into ash. And the woman—the beautiful, stubborn, unyielding mind at the center of the hollow—would be crushed by the immense backlog of eons she had endured. 

The presence gathered itself near the great copper and iron turbine. It looked at Margarethe.

It saw her completely. It saw the weathered lines around her eyes, the oil stains on her canvas trousers, the wrench pinning back her hair. It looked into those eyes and saw the magnificent, tragic ledger of her glacial glances. It read the transparencies of her memory. It felt the staggering weight of her patience, a patience that blurred the line between waiting and simply *being*.

To be benevolent is to listen. So, the presence slowed itself down. 

It shed its vast, cosmic frequency. It decelerated its consciousness, matching the rhythm of the hollow, then matching the rhythm of the ripple, and finally, matching the infinitesimal, crawling pace of Margarethe’s irises.

In that profound deceleration, the silence of the hollow gave way to a sound. It was her voice, vibrating in the slow-motion aether, a sound that had been ringing out for millennia.

*“…wiiiiill tuuuuurn.”*

She was finishing her sentence. She was maintaining her thesis against the crushing argument of eternity. 

The presence felt a swell of profound reverence. It looked from the woman to her machine. The Turbine. It examined the vector of her outstretched hand, tracing the trajectory from her fingertips to the cold iron housing. It looked up at the three great horizontal blades, the complex articulations, the immaculate gears ground to mathematical perfection.

In its wisdom, it saw the truth of her work. She had not built a machine to generate power. She had built a machine to *process* it. She had built a chronal governor, a mechanism designed to catch the latent, stagnant energy of the hollow and convert it into sequence. Her mathematics were flawless. Her metallurgy was inspired. The turbine was perfect.

It just lacked the breeze. 

Not a breeze of wind. A breeze of *will*. A prime mover. 

A lesser god would have blessed the woman and whisked her away to paradise. A wiser God knew that for an engineer, paradise is seeing the machine work. 

The presence stepped into the space between her outstretched hand and the iron. It did not take physical form, but it localized its vastness into a single point of intention. It slipped into the housing of the turbine. 

It flowed through the copper wire, clearing microscosm of verdigris. It settled into the molecular structure of the iron. It wrapped itself around the primary driveshaft, aligning its boundless energy with the grooves of Margarethe's gears. 

It did not force the mechanism. It did not break the laws she had drafted in brass and steel. It simply provided the exact measure of torque her equations demanded. It became the pressure she had felt in her instruments so long ago.

And then, with infinite gentleness, the presence pushed.

*CLACK.*

The sound was like a gunshot in a cathedral. It was the sound of a gear tooth dropping into its slot, slipping over a pall, biting into the groove of its partner. 

Margarethe’s hand flinched. The slowness shattered. 

*Clack-clack-clack.*

The horizontal blades groaned. A shower of perfectly preserved condensation rained down from the copper housing, splashing against the moss not as a frozen mist, but as a sudden, wet downpour. Flakes of rust, newly born, drifted from the inner housing.

The turbine was turning. 

The great blades, twenty feet long, began to sweep through the damp air. *Swoosh. Swoosh. Swoosh.* With every rotation, the turbine caught the immense, pent-up potential of the hollow. And because Margarethe had designed it perfectly, it did not let time explode. It pumped it out in a steady, metered, rhythmic pulse. 

*Thrum-thrum-thrum.*

With the first revolution, the ptarmigan’s foot struck the moss. The bird blinked, shook its feathers, and took a second step, chirping into the cool air. 

With the second revolution, the marmot’s whistle pierced the hollow, a sharp, vital alarm that bounced off the young obsidian. 

With the third revolution, the hawk above dipped its wing, broke its eternal circle, and rode a newly born thermal up toward the shifting clouds. 

With the fourth revolution, the single ripple hit the edge of the pool, splashed over the stone, and broke into a chaos of tiny, overlapping waves as real, sequential rain began to fall. 

The presence withdrew from the machine, pooling itself at Margarethe’s side like a shadow made of starlight. It wrapped a protective mantle of its own timelessness around her, ensuring that as sequence flooded the hollow, her biology would only experience the seconds that were passing, not the eons that had waited. 

Margarethe stumbled forward. The glacial suspension was gone. Gravity pulled her off balance. 

Her outstretched hand slammed against the iron housing. 

It was vibrating. It was warm. It was alive with the roaring, turning fury of her own genius. The gears spun in a blur of brass and oil. The deep industrial hum of *almost* had become the deafening, glorious roar of *is*.

She stood there, breathing hard. Her breath plumed in the cold air, rising and vanishing in proper, beautiful sequence. She touched the metal, tracing the rivets she had placed a lifetime—and a universe—ago. 

She did not weep, for she was not a wept-out woman. She was a mechanist. She pulled a rag from her deep pocket and wiped a smear of oil from the vibrating iron.

But then she paused. She felt the warmth beside her—a warmth that did not come from the friction of the machine. 

Margarethe turned her head. She looked at the empty space beside her stool. She could not see the vastness of the presence, but she could see the way the falling rain seemed to bend respectfully around an invisible shape. She felt the lingering echo of a consciousness that had decelerated to hear her speak.

She understood. She had built the sail, but something had finally provided the wind. 

Margarethe stood up straight. She wiped her oil-stained hands on her canvas trousers, looked directly into the center of the invisible warmth, and nodded once. A nod from one creator to another. A recognition of work well done.

"Thank you," she said. Her voice was harsh, cracking from disuse, vibrating in real-time, magnificent in its brevity. "Tolerances held."

The presence hummed, a sound like a major chord played on the rings of a distant planet. It rippled through the hollow, brushing the trembling ferns, kissing the surface of the chaotic pool, and ruffling Margarethe’s gray-streaked hair. 

Then, it dissolved. Its attention lifting, drifting back to the great cosmic rivers, leaving the hollow behind.

It left the alpine flowers to bud and wilt. It left the obsidian to slowly weather under the rain. And it left the engineer standing before her turning masterpiece, a woman finally, beautifully bound by time, listening to the eternal, triumphant song of her gears.
