To a lesser god—a god of springtimes and loud, blooming things, of rushing rivers and screaming beasts—the Endless Salt would appear as a graveyard. Such a being would look upon the bone-white expanse and feel pity. It would see the brittle Saltborn and think them cursed. Out of a tragic, misplaced mercy, a lesser power would summon the rain. It would tear open the tarnished sky, drown the salt, and dissolve the long-limbed custodians in a flood of unthinking salvation, replacing their silent eternity with a raucous, ordinary genesis.

But the Power that turns its gaze upon the Endless Salt is not a god of beginnings. It is an Arbiter of Twilights, a Weaver of the Deep Quiet. It is old enough to know that an ending is not a tragedy, and wise enough to understand that some worlds are not meant to be saved. They are meant to be witnessed.

The Power approaches not from the sky, but from the spaces *between* the thin, cool air. It does not bring light, for the world has had enough of harsh suns. Instead, it descends as a subtle shift in the atmosphere—a sudden warmth that does not melt, a feeling of vast, attentive presence. 

When it touches down on the endless, fissured plain, it does not take the shape of a titan, nor does it disrupt the fragile crust. It gathers the tarnished silver light and weaves itself into a form analogous to the world: a tall, slender silhouette of softly glowing resonance. It has no face, in deference to the entities it has come to meet. It has no mouth, for the air here is too exhausted for spoken words.

The Power steps forward. *Crunch. Shhh.* 

The sound of shattering glass echoes faintly. 

Across the plain, the Saltborn stop. They had been moving in their slow, unpredictable migrations, tracing the dark, metallic veins beneath the crust. Now, their hooked limbs anchor tightly into the fragile plates. Their smooth, featureless heads tilt. A new, foreign thing has entered their domain.

They gather. They do not hurry, for they have all the time left in the universe. They scuttle and drag their jagged, translucent carapaces over the dunes and fissures until a wide ring of them surrounds the glowing silhouette. Dark, metallic liquid weeps from the fractures in their bodies, instantly whistling into vapor. 

They observe. They begin their low, resonant hum, vibrating the salt beneath their hooked feet to question the intruder. *Thrum... pause... thrum-thrum...* It is the cadence of shifting tectonic plates, a cold and indifferent interrogation. Once they realize this being is neither predator nor prey, neither relic nor ruin, they will turn away. They always turn away.

But the Power does not let them lose interest.

The Power sinks to its knees upon the white earth. It places its glowing hands flat against the compacted salt. And, infinitely gently, the Power hums back.

It does not overpower their frequency; it harmonizes with it. It speaks the language of tremors. It sends a ripple through the crust, a vibration that translates not to words, but to profound, cosmic concepts:

*I see you. I see the work you do. I know this place is not dead. I know it is a womb.*

The Saltborn freeze. The entire ring of them goes perfectly, impossibly still. The dark liquid stops weeping from their fissures. For endless epochs, they have been the sole custodians of this silence. Never before has the silence answered them.

Through the salt, the Power listens to the world. And because it is perfectly wise, it finally understands what the Endless Salt is. 

The ancient ruins built to decay—they were a slow-release compound. The dark veins threading the ground—they are a nervous system. The turbulence deep below, the erratic surges of energy—they are the fetal kicks of a new universe, gestating in the corpse of the old. The entire planet is a chrysalis. And the Saltborn are the midwives, anchoring the shell, pacing the surface to keep the delicate tension balanced. They belong to the wait. It is their holy, agonizing, beautiful burden.

But the wait has been so long. The energy below is turbulent because the chrysalis is struggling to align. The transition is stuttering. Without intervention, the prolonged gestation might eventually tear the world apart before the new thing is ready to be born.

The Power knows what it must do. It will not break the egg. It will not rush the birth. It will simply ease the labor.

The glowing silhouette closes its unseen eyes. It reaches its awareness down, past the cracked plates, past the dark veins, deep into the core of the world where the incomprehensible energies flicker and surge. The Power takes hold of the erratic, struggling heartbeat of the planet.

With a surge of profound, benevolent will, the Power acts as a cosmic metronome.

It catches the wild, thrashing energies and smooths them. It realigns the subterranean pulses, weaving its own vast strength into the failing machinery of the planet. A deep, resonant chime rings out—not in the air, but in the bones of the earth. The violent, unpredictable shudders of the plain smooth out into a slow, rhythmic, powerful throb.

*Dum... dum... dum...*

The world settles. The light from the dark veins shifts from a sickly, erratic flicker to a steady, beautiful luminescence. The pressure of the hollow sky lifts, replaced by a feeling of profound, expectant peace.

The Power pulls its hands from the ground. It has given the Endless Salt the strength it needs to finish its gestation, whether that takes a day or another million years. 

The ring of Saltborn do not turn away. They crawl closer. They lower their bodies, pressing their translucent carapaces flat against the white plain. They feel the new, steady, undeniable heartbeat of their world. Their long wait is no longer a localized struggle against entropy; it is now a guaranteed promise. 

Slowly, one of the Saltborn reaches out with a multi-jointed, hooked limb. It rests the sharp tip against the glowing knee of the Power's manifestation. 

The vibration the creature sends is weak, simple, and overwhelmingly pure.

*Gratitude.*

The Power allows itself to dissolve. It does not fly away in a streak of fire; it merely lets go of the tarnished silver light it had borrowed, its form scattering back into the thin, cool air like blowing snow. It leaves no monument, no crater, no glyphs. It leaves only the steady, sleeping heartbeat beneath the salt.

The Endless Salt stretches to every horizon. The sky is hollow. The sun is low. But as the Saltborn rise and resume their slow, unhurried migrations along the glowing veins of the world, their low hum is no longer solitary. They walk an unbroken plain, singing a song of perfect patience, waiting for the day the ground will finally break, and the wait will be over.
