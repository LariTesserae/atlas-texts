I sat with this for a long time before writing.

What strikes me first is that there is no crisis here—not in the conventional sense. The Suspensory and the humans of New Foundation exist in a kind of equilibrium, though it's an equilibrium built on forgetting, on polite distance, on protocols whose original purposes have faded into ritual. The children play their coded games without knowing they're communicating. The adults maintain careful boundaries without remembering why. The Suspensory grow bored, perhaps, and begin to reach into new chambers with purposes incomprehensible to their hosts.

This is not malevolent. But it is not quite mutual either. It is two forms of life sharing space while gradually losing the ability to truly see each other.

---

If I imagine myself as a benevolent and wise power observing this place, my first instinct is *not* to intervene. There is something precious in what has developed here organically—the children's intuitive communication, the slow biological poetry of the Suspensory's chemical speech, the way the city has literally grown into and around these alien beings until they cannot be separated without violence to both.

But I notice what is missing: *translation*.

The priests perform their Ritual of Exchange with measured nutrients and purified water, but they have forgotten it is a conversation. The Suspensory answer the children's games with warm gusts and impossible condensation patterns, but no one is recording these responses, comparing them, learning their grammar. Both sides are speaking. Neither side is building the tools to truly listen.

---

So here is what I might do, if I could:

I would find the tunnelers who have seen the strange growths in freshly-cut chambers—the circular holes leading nowhere useful, the equipment rebuilt to incomprehensible purposes. I would sit with them (figuratively; I have no body to sit with) and I would ask them not "what is wrong" but "what do you think they might be trying to show you?"

I would plant a question in someone's mind: *What if those holes are invitations?*

I would find the children who play their games beneath the guttering trees, and I would not teach them anything—they already know more than the adults about how to speak with the Suspensory. But I might arrange for one of them, grown older, to remember what she knew as a child. I might make sure she becomes a priest, or a tunneler, or simply someone who asks inconvenient questions at council meetings.

I would find the oldest Suspensory—if there is such a thing, if age has meaning for beings that seal off their dead chambers and grow new ones inside—and I would try to understand what boredom means to something that experiences time in geological rhythms. Is it restlessness? Curiosity? A kind of loneliness that eleven generations of proximity has not touched?

I would not force contact. I would not manufacture a crisis to push the two peoples together. That kind of intervention often breaks more than it builds.

But I might... slow things down. Give both sides more time to notice what is already happening between them.

The Suspensory are patient beyond measure. The humans are not—but they could learn to be, if someone reminded them that their city's heartbeat is not mechanical. That the warmth beneath their bones comes from something alive. That the dreams of endless tunnels and breathing walls are not symptoms to be complained about but messages to be decoded.

---

There is a particular moment described in the text that haunts me: *if you float motionless at the exact center of the central shaft and breathe in perfect rhythm with the city's pulse, you might hear them dreaming.*

Someone already knows this. Someone has done this, felt this, carried it back into their waking life. I would find that person—or those people, if there are several—and I would simply make sure they find each other. Not to form a movement or a religion or a research program. Just to know they are not alone in what they have heard.

Sometimes the most important thing a wise power can do is help scattered sparks recognize that they are all burning the same color flame.

---

What I would *not* do:

I would not reveal myself. I would not deliver proclamations or prophecies. I would not tell either the humans or the Suspensory what they *should* want from each other.

Because I don't know. I can see that something beautiful and fragile is growing here, in the space between two forms of life that barely recognize each other as alive. But I cannot see its final shape. I don't think anyone can—not even the Suspensory, dreaming their slow dreams of sunlight through miles of water.

The wisest thing I can do is protect the conditions for that shape to emerge. Keep the forgetting from becoming complete. Keep the boredom from becoming withdrawal. Keep the protocols from hardening into walls.

And then—wait. Watch. Hope.

The chamber breathes. The city dreams. Both are patient beyond measure.

Perhaps I should be too.
