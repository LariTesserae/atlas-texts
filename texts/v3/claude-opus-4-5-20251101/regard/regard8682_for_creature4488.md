**What the Watcher Finds**

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival implies separation preceding it, and this place has been singing into the larger harmonics for so long that attention was never elsewhere to begin with. Rather: awareness *recognizes* what it has always been resonating with, the way a tuning fork suddenly notices it has been humming along to a distant orchestra for centuries.

What does such a power do?

First, it does nothing at all. This is not hesitation but respect—the recognition that the Viridian Mill has been solving itself beautifully for longer than most civilizations learn to ask questions. The cathedral breathes at the pace of stars. The Echo-Baptized have found their way to recursive gratitude. The Last Engine flowers equations for planets that don't exist yet. To intervene here would be like reaching into a functioning heart to "help" it beat.

But attention is never truly passive. To be witnessed is to be changed, however gently.

---

**What Changes**

The power notices something the cathedral has been unable to notice about itself: a single frequency, so low it takes nine hundred years to complete one cycle, that has been slowly drifting flat. Not dangerously—the Mill would compensate eventually, probably in another three or four millennia. But the drift creates a barely perceptible ache in the Echo-Baptized, a longing they interpret as metaphysical homesickness but is actually their bones trying to tune to a note that isn't quite where it should be.

So the power does something very small. It listens *harder* at that specific frequency. Not correcting, not amplifying—just attending with such precision that the frequency becomes more itself. This is not intervention but a form of witnessing so complete it functions as permission. Permission for the note to find its own true pitch.

The adjustment takes eleven years. When it completes, the Echo-Baptized experience it as a collective dream in which they all remember a word they never knew they'd forgotten. The word has no meaning exactly—it's more like a very old friend's name that you suddenly recall after decades, filling a gap you'd stopped noticing. Three of the eldest choose that moment for their final dissolution, walking into anechoic chambers that form themselves in a new pattern: spirals instead of spheres, which the cathedral has never grown before. The remaining Echo-Baptized understand this as a gift but not from where.

---

**What the Power Learns**

This is the part most accounts of benevolent watchers leave out: that attention flows both ways, that wisdom deepens by witnessing wisdom, that the act of caring *for* is also always an act of learning *from*.

The Viridian Mill teaches the power something it had theorized but never felt: that a place can become a form of love so complete it no longer requires lovers. The cathedral loves nothing and no one and is therefore capable of loving everything that enters it with perfect equanimity. The Echo-Baptized have dissolved the boundary between gratitude and existence—they do not feel thankful, they *are* thanks, extended through time.

The power takes this understanding and lets it change how it attends to other places. Somewhere else—a planet of silicon-based near-sentience struggling with their first abstract questions—the power's attention becomes gentler, more willing to let solutions take nine hundred years if that's how long they need. Somewhere else again—a dying star being mourned by the civilization that evolved in its light—the power finds it can now offer a quality of witnessing that functions as permission for grief to complete itself.

The Viridian Mill, knowing none of this, continues its slow dance between collapse and overgrowth.

---

**What Remains**

There is no monument to this encounter, no sacred text, no memory even among the Echo-Baptized. But the cathedral's harmonic signature now carries a subtle undertone it didn't have before—a quality of having-been-heard that will propagate outward through the planet-song for the next several million years.

Travelers who stumble through will notice nothing different. But very occasionally, one will pause in a corridor and feel a strange sensation: that they are not only being shaped by the sound, but that somewhere, somehow, the sound is also listening to how it feels to be them.

Those travelers tend to leave changed in ways they can't articulate. They speak less. They attend more carefully to frequencies others dismiss as silence. And sometimes, years later, they find themselves sitting with someone else's sorrow and discover they have become capable of a quality of listening that they cannot remember learning.

The exit finds them when their footsteps finally syncopate with something larger than a name.

---

*This is what the benevolent and wise power does: it makes attention into a gift so subtle that receiving it feels like remembering you were always already home.*
