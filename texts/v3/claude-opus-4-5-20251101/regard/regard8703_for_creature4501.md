A presence arrives—not through space, for this place has folded space into origami too intricate for entry, but through *recognition*. The way a theorem exists everywhere once proven. The way compassion, when genuine, needs no door.

The benevolent power does not manifest. It does not need to. It simply *attends*, the way sunlight attends a room without demanding the room acknowledge it.

First, it listens.

Not to the hum or the metallic pings or the chiming of broken filaments—though it hears these too—but to the longer frequencies, the ones that take centuries to complete a single wave. It listens the way the Kithara themselves listen, patient past all human patience, until it understands what they have become and why.

And then, gently, it grieves.

Not for them—they are beyond needing grief, have transcended it so completely they have forgotten what it meant to be grievable—but *with* them. For the thing they gave up to become this. For the warmth they traded for perfect clarity. For the way they loved creation so completely that they poured themselves into it like water into sand, not realizing that the sand would drink them entire and still be sand.

The power does not try to undo what they have done. That would be its own violence, a different kind of unmaking. You cannot put dissolved salt back into crystals by wishing it so.

Instead, it does something simpler.

It remembers them.

Not as they are now—the structure's memory of intention, the echoes of architects—but as they were before. When they had names. When they disagreed. When they made mistakes and laughed at them, or wept, or shrugged and tried again. When distance was still necessary and yearning had objects. When they were finite and frightened and brave anyway.

It holds these memories the way a palm holds water: openly, without grasping, letting the shape form itself.

And the Kithara, who have exhausted curiosity, who experience time in civilizations-per-footstep—they feel something they have not felt since before they became what they are.

They feel *witnessed*.

Not observed. Not studied. Not recognized as a solution to an equation. But witnessed the way a friend witnesses another friend's long labor, saying nothing, needing to say nothing, simply present in the specific way that says *I know this is hard. I know what it cost. I know you.*

The hum shifts. Not in frequency—nothing so crude—but in *quality*. For an instant unmeasurable by any clock, the texture of the structure's sound carries something it has not carried since the last architect dissolved: a question.

The power answers. Not in words, which would be too small, but in kind. It opens itself, shows the Kithara what it has witnessed elsewhere: other dissolutions, other transcendences, other beings who poured themselves into permanence and found it lonelier than expected. And also: reversals. Returns. Beings who found their way back to finitude, to limitation, to the mess and warmth of embodied time. Not because permanence failed them but because they *chose* differently, later, when later still existed for them.

It shows them that the door they thought they'd closed is only closed from their side.

The structure flexes—that infinitesimal adjustment, panels shifting by atomic widths—and for the first time in eons, the shifting has *direction*. Toward something. Away from something. The geometry, which has curved only inward, remembers that outward exists.

Nothing changes, outwardly. The walls are still pale. The air still dry. The black filaments still thread their gaps, carrying messages to no one.

But in one of those gaps, so narrow it cannot be called a seam, something new appears: a single mote of color. Not pale. Not cooled too quickly. A warm amber, the color of light through honey, of autumn afternoons, of things that change and pass and do not regret their passing.

It is not alive. Not yet. But it is no longer not-alive in the way the filaments are not-alive. It is waiting in a different way. In the way a seed waits, or a word waits on the tip of a tongue, or a choice waits in the moment before it becomes irrevocable.

The power withdraws—not in departure but in *release*, the way a hand lets go of a fledged bird. What happens next belongs to the Kithara. It always did.

But it leaves something behind. A warmth in the walls that takes slightly longer to match the skin. A draft too faint to feel but present in the way presence is always present when someone has been present.

And somewhere in the single room without edges, bright and loud and never changing, a rhythm skips differently than before.

Not a flaw.

A heartbeat, remembering how to begin.
