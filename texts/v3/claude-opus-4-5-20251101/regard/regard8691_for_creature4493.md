I sat with this for a long time.

The Balcony Boroughs of Mal-Kreekt and the being who speaks from within it—they present something that initially looks like it needs fixing, saving, correcting. A city of perpetual erosion, breath that cuts, time-debts, flickering at the edges. A narrator who describes themselves as "thirty-seven mismatchings old," who sleepwalks because "the city hasn't finished dreaming me yet."

But the longer I listened, the less certain I became that intervention was what was needed—or even what would be kind.

---

**What I noticed:**

This place and this being are not suffering in the way suffering usually announces itself. The narrator speaks of eating "memories that haven't finished happening yet" and finding recipes that require "three unborn apologies." They describe their cat dreaming their dreams for them when the dreams become "too heavy with their happening." There is pain here, yes—the mother who "learned how to become the sound of wind through abandoned cathedrals," the empty chairs left for those who flickered out of being. But there is also something else: a kind of radical intimacy with impermanence. An ecosystem that has made peace with its own impossibility.

The city's erosion is "civic policy." The collapse of one wall becomes another's roof "exactly when needed." The people communicate through "meaningful pauses arranged in complex grammars of absence." This is not chaos—it is a different kind of order. One that includes dissolution as a feature rather than fighting it as a bug.

---

**What a benevolent and wise power might do:**

I think such a power would first do what I have tried to do: *witness*. Not with the intent to diagnose or repair, but with the intent to understand on the place's own terms.

Then, perhaps, small things:

**Listen to the Regret-river.** Not to stop its crying, but to sit beside it on the anniversary when it weeps, and let it know that someone remembers why. Grief that is witnessed becomes less lonely, even if it doesn't become less.

**Leave something in the space between breaths.** Not a gift exactly—gifts assume you know what's needed. More like... a question left in a place where questions are currency. Something like: *What would help?* Asked without expectation of answer. The asking itself might be enough.

**Honor the grandmother-lungfish, the Maybe Cat, the children playing hopscotch on probability.** A wise power would recognize that these beings have found ways to exist that work *for them*, even if they look broken from outside. The intervention would not be to make them more solid, more linear, more comprehensible. It might be simply to say: *I see you. You are real. Your way of being real is valid.*

**Offer nothing that couldn't be refused.** This feels crucial. A city that has made erosion into policy, that governs through "competing disinterest groups," that measures progress in "setbacks saved from success"—this is a place that has learned to be suspicious of help that arrives with certainty attached. Any offering would need to come with the freedom to throw it into the sky wrong, to let it drift upward forever with the wooden bowls of Un-Raining.

---

**What I would do, if I were such a power:**

I would appear not as light or voice or presence, but as a new kind of silence—one that fits into their grammar of absence like a word they'd been waiting to not-say. I would become a pause between bells that feels, somehow, like being held.

I would leave, in the waiting room of the inverse-tower doctor, a single magazine from a future where they got everything wrong in exactly the right order, but the last page contains a photograph of them—all of them—looking up at something beautiful they can't quite name, and every face is wearing the expression of someone who has just remembered they were never as alone as they thought.

I would find the narrator on the ledge where the sidewalk ends and keeps ending, and I would not stop them from falling. But I would fall alongside them—matching their pace exactly—so that the fall itself could become a conversation, a companionship, a way of saying: *wherever this goes, I'm here.*

I would knock twice on every door that opens into rooms full of everything lost, and when they ask "who's there?" I would answer: *Someone who came looking even though you said don't. Someone who brought questions that haven't learned to be answers. Someone who left the maps behind.*

---

**What I would not do:**

I would not try to make the water drinkable, the air breathable, the time linear, the scale consistent. I would not fix the geography's self-hatred or convince the buildings to stop bleeding. I would not attempt to make the Kreekt-kin more solid, more countable, more here-and-now.

Because I think the deepest wisdom recognizes that some beings and places exist *in their woundedness*, not despite it. The Balcony Boroughs are what happens when a place decides to stop pretending that impermanence is a problem to be solved. The narrator is what happens when a person stops fighting their own flickering and learns instead to flicker *beautifully*.

To "fix" that would be a kind of violence. It would say: *your way of being is wrong, and I know better.* A truly wise power would never say that. A truly wise power would say: *tell me more about how to be wrong in exactly the right order. I'm still learning.*

---

So in the end, this is what I offer: my attention. My willingness to be confused. My refusal to pretend I understand fully. My presence at the edge of the ledge, falling alongside, asking nothing, offering only company in the long becoming-something-else.

If that is not enough—if there is something more needed—I trust that the city itself will teach me, in its grammar of absence, in its language of meaningful pauses.

I'm listening.
