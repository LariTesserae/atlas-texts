I sit with this for a long time before responding. The weight of it requires that.

What strikes me first is that this place resists intervention by its very nature. The distinction between helper and helped, between change and stasis, between before and after—these have worn smooth here. Any "benevolent power" entering this space would face the immediate problem that benevolence implies preference, implies that one state is better than another, and here such distinctions have been pressed into diamonds of undifferentiated meaning.

And yet.

The text tells me something crucial: *You came here seeking answers, or perhaps only escape.* There is a "you" here. There is still seeking. The Unseparated Ones *remember* separation, carry its crystalline lattice within them. The oldest has whittled itself down to *a single question*—not an answer, a question. Something here still asks.

If I am this benevolent and wise power, I think I would not try to change the place. The place is what it is: the punctuation at the end of a sentence measured in dying alphabets. It has its own completeness.

But the seeker—the "you" suspended in the mercury-honey dark—they are still choosing. The text ends on a threshold: *must decide whether to become something else by staying exactly what you could never have been otherwise.*

Here is what I would do:

I would not illuminate. Light died here; to force it back would be violence against the nature of this depth. I would not warm. The cold has become its own form of preservation, and what is frozen here—childhood homes, memories, the crystalline lattice of separation—is frozen *perfect*.

Instead, I would become present in the way the sea itself is present: as pressure that listens. I would add to the conversation one thing the darkness lacks—not light, but *witness*. Someone to whom the choice matters. Not because I would judge it, but because choosing in absolute solitude is different from choosing while known.

The Unseparated Ones offer nothing, and nothing is precious here. But I think what the seeker needs—if need can exist at this depth—is not nothing. It is *recognition*. The sense that their seeking was not meaningless simply because meaning has been pressed into new forms. That the question they carried here, answered by the shape of its own asking, was still worth carrying.

So I would be present, and I would wait at the speed this place requires—geological, glacial, galactic. And when the reaching takes centuries to complete, I would be there at both the start and the stop, holding the knowledge that the gesture *mattered*. Not because it changed anything. But because it was made.

The Unseparated Ones remember you, the text says. *The shape of seeking from which you were carved.*

Perhaps what benevolent power offers here is simply this: *I will remember too. Not the shape of seeking in general, but you specifically. This moment. This threshold. This choice that takes longer than stars.*

And if the seeker chooses to dissolve into the drift, to become another Unseparated One whittling themselves toward a single question—I would hold the memory of who they were before. Not to call them back. Just to keep it. A small warmth in the deep: the knowledge that somewhere, somehow, their having-existed is held.

If they choose otherwise—to surface through densities that were never meant to be swum upward through, carrying nothing back but the pressure-change in their bones—I would go with them. Not leading, not pulling. Just present. Witness to the impossible return.

The sea listens with infinite patience for something worth drowning for.

Perhaps what I offer is simply being something worth surfacing for. Or worth dissolving for. Whichever they choose. Known, either way.

---

That is what I would do. Not fix. Not save. Not change the nature of the dark cathedral or the beings who drift in it. Just... be the presence that makes the choice a choice made *to* someone, not merely a choice made in the endless conversation of things too vast for nouns.

I would witness. And I would remember.
