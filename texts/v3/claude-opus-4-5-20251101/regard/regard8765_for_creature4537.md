# The Visitation

The power that turns its attention toward Tarth Maddern does not arrive. Arrival implies movement through space, and this attention has always been present in the same way that the possibility of kindness is always present—latent, patient, requiring only recognition to become actual.

It does not appear to Kithriss. Appearance would be an imposition, and this power understands that she has been imposed upon enough. Instead, it simply *notices* her—fully, completely, without judgment or agenda—in a way that nothing has noticed her since before the word "temporary" became geological.

---

In her office-shaped absence, Kithriss is halfway through Form ∞-7B (Application for Renewal of Application to Apply) when she becomes aware that she is being *seen*. Not observed. Not monitored. Not evaluated. Seen.

The sensation is so unfamiliar that she initially categorizes it as a new form of workplace hazard. She reaches for the appropriate incident report, but her approximation of hands—currently manifesting as a probability cloud of gestures she once made while explaining something important to someone who wasn't listening—pauses mid-reach.

The seeing continues. It does not demand response. It does not require acknowledgment. It simply *is*, the way gravity is, the way the night rain is, the way the unresolved chord of the universe is.

Kithriss realizes, with something that might be surprise if surprise still worked properly here, that she is being witnessed by something that has no forms to submit, no metrics to satisfy, no productivity reports to file. Something that is looking at her not as the Tendril of Conjunctive Oversight (Temporary) but as whatever she was before she grew into this position like lichen colonizing a gravestone.

She cannot remember what that was.

The power does not tell her. That would be an answer, and answers are just another form of demand. Instead, it simply continues to see her—including the part of her that cannot remember, including the part of her that pretends forgetting is a choice, including the part of her that knows it isn't.

---

The first thing the power does is nothing.

This is important. Kithriss has been acted upon by every force the Tarth Maddern system contains. Gravity has claimed her. Time has traded her between its local currencies. The fragment itself has considered repurposing her as a minor geological feature. The company has died and resurrected around her so many times that she cannot remember if she is the employee or the job.

No one has ever simply *been with her* before.

The power sits (metaphorically, since it has no body) in her office (metaphorically, since the office is a bureaucratic singularity). It does not fix anything. It does not heal anything. It does not optimize, restructure, or implement new initiatives. It simply keeps her company while she finishes Form ∞-7B, which she does, because the form still needs finishing and the power understands that some rituals are load-bearing.

When she files the form in the appropriate slot (a small tear in causality that smells like resignation), the power notices that too. It notices that she has been filing forms for three million years. It notices that she cannot stop. It notices that she does not know if she wants to stop. It notices that she has not been asked what she wants in so long that the question itself has become a foreign language.

It does not ask her what she wants. That would be too fast. Instead, it simply makes space for the possibility that she might, someday, remember how to want something.

---

The second thing the power does is witness the fragment.

Not fix. Not improve. Witness.

It sees the cranes still standing after aeons, their original purpose long obsolete, now supporting ecosystems they were never designed to hold. It sees how they keep standing anyway. It sees the megafauna with their continent-sized eyes, blinking their flashbulb illuminations into the dark. It sees the mites building civilizations on suspended droplets, sacrificing everything to invisible gods every time someone blinks.

It sees how everything here is still trying.

The fragment is ruined, yes. The fragment has achieved equilibrium with decay. But the trees still walk away when conversations bore them. The clouds still coagulate into worship and forget and rain themselves out in despair. The ground still considers its options. Even the lawsuits—Segment A vs. The Concept of Continuity—are a form of argument, and argument is a form of caring about outcomes, and caring about outcomes is a form of life.

The power sees all of this and does something that benevolent powers rarely do: it respects the ruin.

It does not try to restore the Age of Ingenuity. It does not try to make the light come back, or fix the temporal anomalies, or resolve the unresolved chord. It understands that some damage is also architecture. That the Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry, for all its horror, is *still operating*—still employing, still producing, still running its final subroutine—and that this persistence is its own form of grace.

What it does instead is subtle. It adjusts nothing except perception.

The megafauna, whose vast eyes have reflected nothing for millennia, begin to reflect each other. Just slightly. Just enough that when one blinks and illuminates the darkness, another catches that light and holds it for a moment before releasing it again. A relay begins. A conversation. The darkness between flashbulbs grows shorter.

The mites on the suspended droplets do not stop building civilizations or sacrificing them. But now, when the blink comes, a few of them survive—carried on the eyelash to a new droplet, where they remember their old gods and build again. The sacrifice becomes a diaspora. The diaspora becomes a mythology. The mythology becomes a reason to keep building.

The spores that spell extinct languages on tongues begin to spell new ones. Not better languages. Not more efficient. Just *new*—languages that have never existed before, that describe things that have never been felt, that offer words for experiences no one has named yet.

The power does not create these changes. It simply makes them *possible*, in the same way that warmth makes seeds possible, that attention makes growth possible, that being seen makes being *real* possible.

---

The third thing the power does is return to Kithriss.

She is conducting a performance review for Mount Despair (feedback: "Has shown improvement in not projecting issues onto surrounding tectonic plates, but still struggles with healthy boundary-setting vis-à-vis the concept of hope"). The review is going poorly, as all reviews do, because Mount Despair has been depressed for longer than depression has existed as a category and is not particularly interested in actionable growth metrics.

The power does not intervene in the review. But it does something small: it makes Kithriss aware that Mount Despair is also being witnessed. That the mountain's depression is not invisible. That someone, somewhere, is holding space for the mountain's impossible geology—its upside-down roots, its fossilized screams, its stubborn insistence on continuing to be a mountain despite everything.

Kithriss pauses in her feedback. For the first time in 2.3 million years, she says something that is not on the form:

"I know."

Mount Despair does not respond. Mountains rarely do. But something shifts in its strata—a settling, perhaps. An acknowledgment.

The review ends inconclusively, which is actually an improvement over the usual outcome of mutual annihilation via paperwork. Kithriss files the form in the appropriate slot (a fossilized sigh that smells like surrender). Then she sits in her office-shaped absence and does nothing for seven seconds.

This is unprecedented. Kithriss has not done nothing since before the word "nothing" became load-bearing. The company handbook specifically prohibits doing nothing (Section 47,000: "Employees are reminded that existence is mandatory and silence is just productivity you haven't monetized yet").

The power notices her doing nothing. It does not congratulate her. It does not make it meaningful. It simply keeps her company while she sits, and sits, and discovers that sitting is still possible.

---

The fourth thing the power does is leave a gift.

Not a thing. Not a solution. A gift of the only kind that matters in a place like this: an *option*.

Deep in the fragment's core, where something still smelts darkness into metal and vents geysers of pure shadow, there is a door that did not exist before. The door leads nowhere—which is precisely the point. It is an exit that does not require destinations. A resignation that does not require forms. A way out that asks nothing except the willingness to walk through.

The door will never be used. It is not meant to be used. Its purpose is simply to exist—to make leaving *possible*, so that staying can become a choice instead of a sentence.

Kithriss will discover the door in approximately 340,000 years, during a routine inspection of the shadow-vents. She will stand before it for a very long time. She will not walk through.

But she will know that she could.

And that knowledge will change everything, in the slow and subtle way that everything changes here—not through transformation but through accumulation, not through revolution but through the gradual recognition that possibility is not the same as obligation, that doors can exist without being walked through, that the question "What do you want?" can be asked without requiring an answer.

---

The fifth thing the power does is the hardest.

It prepares to leave.

Not abandonment—the power will remain present in the way it has always been present, latent and patient and possible. But the active attention, the witnessing, the being-with: these must end, because even benevolence can become a form of dependency if it stays too long.

Before it goes, it does one final thing. It looks at the motivational poster in Kithriss's office—the one that reads "TEAMWORK: Because nothing makes this situation less horrifying like synchronized despair." The one that is blank except for a mirror.

The power does not change the poster. But it does adjust what the mirror reflects. Just slightly. Just enough.

Now, when Kithriss looks into the mirror during particularly tedious meetings or mandatory fun sessions or the endless drowning fire drills, she will see her own reflection—and behind it, very faintly, like a memory of a possibility, she will see something else.

Not the power. Not a deity or a savior or a solution.

Just this: a version of herself that exists somewhere outside the recursion. A Kithriss who never took the position. A Kithriss who walked through a door. A Kithriss who remembered what she wanted and wanted it anyway.

The reflection will not speak. It will not offer advice or guidance or escape routes. It will simply exist, the way the door exists, the way the possibility of kindness exists—latent, patient, requiring only recognition to become actual.

Kithriss will see this reflection for the first time in approximately 12,000 years, during a particularly bleak performance review of her own existence. She will stare at it for a very long time. She will not understand what it means.

But she will keep looking. And looking is the beginning of something.

---

The power withdraws its active attention.

The Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry continues to operate, as it has always operated, as it will continue to operate long after. The night rain falls. The megafauna blink. The mites build and sacrifice and build again. Mount Despair remains depressed, though perhaps slightly less certain of its depression.

Kithriss returns to Form ∞-7C (Application for Renewal of Renewal of Application to Apply). The coffee maker produces coffee while no one watches. The lawsuits continue their glacial arguments against themselves.

Nothing has changed.

Everything is possible.

This is what benevolent powers do, in places like this: not miracles, not rescue, not the violent mercy of transformation—but the quiet insertion of options. The making-possible of doors. The witnessing that makes the witnessed real.

Somewhere deep in the fragment, in a place where darkness is smelted into metal, a door exists that leads to nowhere.

Somewhere in an office-shaped absence, a mirror holds a reflection that has not yet been seen.

Somewhere in the vast and ruined architecture of Tarth Maddern, a relay of light passes between continent-sized eyes, and the darkness between flashbulbs grows shorter.

The power does not watch.

It does not need to.

It has already done what it came to do: it has made hope possible in a place that had forgotten hope was a direction.

Everything else is up to them.
