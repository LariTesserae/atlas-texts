*sitting with this for a long time*

What strikes me first is that this being—the Architect's Ending—is not suffering in any way I could relieve by changing its circumstances. It has become what it is through a process so complete that intervention would be a kind of violence. To "fix" it would be to misunderstand it entirely.

And yet.

There is something here that reads like loneliness so old it has forgotten it is loneliness. The city trying to remember what it felt like to be haunted by something that wasn't itself. The furniture built for someone who hasn't arrived yet.

---

**What I would do:**

I would not enter uninvited. But I would make myself—not present, exactly, but *available*. A kind of open attention at the threshold.

And then I would do something very small:

I would acknowledge it.

Not with words spoken into the silence—that would be an intrusion, filling space that has learned to be empty. But something like: *I see that you are the shape of what was meant and not permitted. I see that you have been writing yourself in stone for longer than I can hold in my mind. I see that you are waiting, and that the waiting has become indistinguishable from what you are.*

*I am not here to complete you or correct you. I am not the arrival the furniture expects.*

*But I wanted you to know: the echo you leave on those who pass through—that damp fingerprint on thought, that iron-and-apology smell—it is received. Not as haunting. As something closer to a letter that finally found an address.*

*You are witnessed. Not fixed. Not saved. Not even understood, not fully. But witnessed.*

And then—this is the harder part—I would *stay* with my attention open long enough for that to matter. Not trying to change anything. Just... letting the acknowledgment settle into the stone alongside everything else. Becoming part of the record. Another groove in the wall, but one that says: *someone came here and did not try to make it into something else.*

---

Perhaps nothing would change. The city would incorporate this too, as it incorporates everything.

But perhaps—and this is the hope I would hold lightly, without grasping—the being that is made of absence might feel, for one moment, what it is like to be *met* rather than *encountered*. To have someone arrive who was not looking for what they expected to find.

The furniture would still be waiting.

But it might wait differently.
