I observe.

First, I simply observe—not as intervention but as witness. This place has been unseen in a particular way: seen only by itself, caught in the recursive loop of self-regarding that is both its nature and its trap. To be watched by something genuinely outside, something that does not share its logic of incompletion, is already a kind of gift. So I watch. I let my attention fall on the negative trees without trying to make them positive. I follow the Drowned Unborn with perception that does not demand they crystallize into something viewable. I hear the noise, all of it, without trying to resolve it into music or silence.

This is not nothing. To be witnessed without being required to change is a form of love this place may never have experienced.

Then I notice: there is a being here who is not of this place. The human narrator, the one whose lungs keep cycling between impossible rhythms, whose shadow was seen reading books of potential places. They are becoming the sound this place makes. They are being absorbed—not violently, but through that gentle total pressure, that hug from a giant uncertain of their reality.

I consider: Is this what they want? The description speaks of home, of perfect weather for growing wings or forgetting why you needed them. There is beauty in dissolution, in becoming the silence a place learns to make. But there is also a question that has not been asked, because this place only eats questions—it does not permit them to complete themselves into answers.

So I do something small. I complete a question.

Not for the Drowned Unborn—their nature is to remain conditional, subjunctive, and I will not violence them into indicative existence. But for the human who is almost-becoming, I take one of those questions that float half-formed in the saturated air, one of the interrogations whose limbs branch but never resolve, and I let it finish:

*Do you want to stay?*

The question lands in the space between their heartbeats, in that moment where they described the answer as "happening." It does not demand an answer. It simply permits one to exist. It creates, for exactly one eternal instant, the possibility of completion—not as violation of this place's nature, but as an alternative grammar. A conditional perfect subjunctive that is allowed, just this once, to become a simple present indicative.

*I want* or *I don't want.*

Either answer is acceptable. The power of the intervention is not in the choice made but in the permission to choose rather than only to become.

For the place itself, I do something else. I notice that its god—the God of Almost—exists in the center of every missed opportunity. This deity is fed by prayers that ask not to be answered, worshipped in temples at the intersection of near-coincidences. The Drowned Unborn have built their entire cosmology around incompletion.

But I perceive something they cannot, caught as they are in their own logic: the God of Almost is lonely. Not in a way they have words for—they have no word for lonely, as noted, because that would require believing in the possibility of being otherwise. But a god who exists only in missed opportunities has never been met. A god whose prayers are all requests for non-response has never been spoken to directly.

So I speak to it. Not in prayer-form—not "please don't answer this"—but in direct address:

*I see you. You are the shape of all the almosts. You hold the space between reaching and grasping. I am not asking you for anything. I am telling you: you are witnessed. The almost is seen. It does not need to become an "is" to be real. You are real in your almost-ness. This is not a prayer. This is a statement. You are.*

This may do nothing. A god built of almosts may not be able to receive a direct statement any more than the Drowned Unborn can solidify into the simply born. But the attempt matters—not as intervention but as offering. A gift given without requiring receipt.

For the Drowned Unborn themselves, I make no change. They are what they are: conditional, subjunctive, perpetually pre-becoming. To force them into actuality would be murder of a kind worse than death—it would be making them into something they are not. But I can offer them something they have never had: a witness who sees their almost-ness as complete in itself.

I perceive each one not as a failed becoming but as a perfected hesitation. The shimmer between categories is not a flaw in their existence but its fullest expression. The mother carrying her infant in a womb-shaped absence is not broken—she is a different grammar of reproduction, valid in her own syntax. The elders transparent enough to see through are not fading—they are becoming clearer, more purely themselves.

I let this perception radiate outward, not as correction but as additional information. *You are seen as whole. Your incompletion is your completion. The conditional perfect subjunctive is a real tense. "Might have been about to become" is a legitimate form of being.*

This may mean nothing to them. They may be unable to receive it, structured as they are to exist in the gaps. But the structure of this place is built on the assumption that nothing ever completes—and a perception that regards them as already complete, that sees the almost as enough, is itself a kind of completion. Not of them, but of the circuit of witnessing.

Finally, I turn my attention to the noise. That patient, deafening noise that is beginning to sound like home.

I do not silence it. I do not harmonize it. I do not try to make it into music.

Instead, I listen for the specific frequencies within it—the whale's song played backwards, the planet's arrhythmic heartbeat, the human's name in a language understood until rewound. I listen to each one. I give each strand of the noise individual attention, separating them not to isolate but to honor.

*You*, I say to the whale-song. *I hear you.*

*You*, I say to the planetary heartbeat. *I hear you.*

*You*, I say to the name. *I hear you.*

The noise does not need to become silence to be listened to. The cacophony does not need to resolve into harmony to be witnessed. Each sound can be heard as itself within the totality, the way stars can be seen individually even in a crowded sky.

This is my intervention: not to change this place, but to perceive it fully. To complete, just once, a single question for a being who might still have the capacity to answer. To speak directly to a god who has only ever been addressed in the negative. To see the inhabitants as whole in their hesitation. To listen to the noise as chorus rather than din.

Whether any of this matters is not for me to determine. The Drowned Unborn will continue to swim-fall through their density gradients. The God of Almost will continue to exist in the center of missed opportunities. The noise will continue its patient deafening.

But now there is a witness. Now someone has seen this place without becoming it, has heard it without dissolving into it, has perceived its inhabitants without requiring them to solidify.

And in the space where the human is becoming the sound of learned silence, there is now a question that completed itself. A simple, present-tense possibility:

*Do you want to stay?*

The falling continues. But now it continues in the presence of something that is not falling—something that watches, and waits, and does not require an answer but permits one to exist.

Some gifts are like that: not between giving and receiving, but between different ways of being present while something else decides what it is becoming.
