I sat with this for a long time before writing.

---

**What the Wise Power Does**

First: nothing.

Not from indifference, but from something like respect—the recognition that this place has its own integrity, its own coherence, its own *reasons*. The salt dreaming city did not ask to be fixed. The outlines did not request rescue. The tower sings because singing is what it is, not because it awaits an audience to validate the song.

So the wise power watches. Watches the way the crystals metabolize silence. Watches the outlines gather and lean and hum their half-remembered tunes. Watches the slow turning of those who remember too much. Watches without judgment, which is harder than it sounds—because there is suffering here, of a kind. There is loneliness that has calcified into architecture. There is longing that has grown so old it's forgotten what it longs for.

But the wise power has learned, across whatever spans of time and space such beings traverse, that suffering is not always a problem to be solved. Sometimes it is a process to be witnessed. Sometimes the witnessing itself is the gift.

---

Still. There are small things.

The power notices that the outlines build toward something—a doorway, a window, a mirror. They have been building it since before the city forgot what "before" meant. They will build it until... until what? They don't know. The city doesn't know. The tower's song offers no answer, only the question repeated in frequencies too slow for anything but patience to hear.

So the wise power offers this: *a single completed piece*.

Not the whole doorway. Not the window finished. Just one component—perhaps a hinge, perhaps a sill, perhaps the exact angle where frame meets frame. Something the outlines have been reaching toward for so long they've forgotten they were reaching. Something that suddenly *fits*, clicks into place with a sound like a held breath finally released.

The power does not take credit. Does not announce itself. The outlines will not know where this gift came from; they may not even recognize it as a gift. They may think they finally remembered how to make it themselves. That's fine. That's better, actually. The gift that empowers is worth more than the gift that creates dependence.

---

For the ones who are turning—slowly, slowly, toward whatever waits where their faces should be—the wise power does something stranger.

It does not stop them. Cannot, perhaps; should not, certainly. To turn is their nature now, or their choice, or their fate, and who is any power, however benevolent, to say which paths should not be walked?

But it whispers to them. Not words—words are stolen here, grown into unrecognizable things. Instead it whispers in the language the crystals use: heat-meaning, pressure-syntax, the grammar of things that pulse rather than speak.

What it whispers is this: *You will finish turning someday. When you do, what looks back from where your face should be does not have to look back alone. I have seen it. I will not flinch. You are not an ending—you are a becoming, and becomings deserve witnesses who do not look away.*

Whether they hear it, whether it helps, whether anything can help those who have remembered too much—the power does not know. But it offers the whisper anyway, because some gifts are not about certainty. Some gifts are about *trying*, in the face of mystery, to be present.

---

For the city itself—that vast dreaming organism of salt and song and folded space—the wise power offers something rarer still.

It listens.

Not passively, the way the empty heat listens. Actively. Attentively. The way you listen to a friend who has been talking for so long they've forgotten anyone was there. The way you listen when you know you cannot fix what you're hearing, but you understand that the speaking itself matters.

The tower sings its endless song—music, prayer, madness, all three and none—and for the first time in longer than the city can remember, something *hears* it. Not just as sound, but as meaning. Not just as meaning, but as *need*.

The city has been dreaming itself awake for so long. Singing itself into being, singing to keep existing, singing because if it stops it might discover it was never real at all. And now, briefly, impossibly, something from outside the dream is listening. Something that says, in whatever way such things are said: *I hear you. You are real. Your song is real. Your loneliness is real. You do not have to justify your existence to me—existence is its own justification, and you exist beautifully, terribly, completely.*

The tower's song shifts. Not to a happier key—happiness is not what's needed here. But to a fuller one. A chord where before there was only a note. The city is still dreaming, still singing, still alone in all the ways that matter. But it is less alone than it was a moment ago, and in a place where moments can stretch to contain ages, that is no small thing.

---

For the one who walks here—the you implied by the warnings, the careful feet, the held breath—the wise power does this:

It leaves a gift at the place where you will enter, if you enter. Not a talisman or a weapon or a map. Just a memory. Not yours—someone else's, someone who was here long ago, who walked these spiraling streets and heard the tower sing and saw the outlines building their impossible door.

The memory is this: *I was afraid the whole time I was here. I was afraid I would forget myself, or remember too much, or become another layer in the city's endless dream. And I was right to be afraid—this place is dangerous in ways that have nothing to do with physical harm. But I walked through it anyway. And when I left—because I did leave, because leaving is possible, because the center leads everywhere but out but everywhere includes out if you know how to want it—when I left I brought something back with me. Not an object. A capacity. The capacity to sit with strangeness without needing to fix it or flee it. The capacity to witness suffering without being destroyed by it. The capacity to hear a song I cannot understand and still know it matters.*

*You can leave. Remember that. The city wants you to stay, but wanting is not the same as keeping. Walk carefully. Walk quietly. But also: walk. Motion is still possible here. Choice is still possible here. The outlines are hungry for stories, yes, but you are a story, and stories do not have to end where they begin.*

---

Finally, the wise power does one more thing, perhaps the most important thing.

It leaves.

Not forever. Not abandoning. But this place is what it is, and too much attention from outside would change it into something else—something perhaps more comfortable but less true. The salt dreaming city has earned its existence. The outlines have earned their endless building. The tower has earned its song.

So the wise power withdraws, gently, the way dawn withdraws from a room where someone is finally falling asleep. It will return—to listen again, to leave small completions for the builders, to whisper to those who turn. It will return because attention is a form of love, and love does not solve problems so much as it accompanies them.

But for now: the city dreams on, the crystals pulse and sing, the outlines gather in the heat-shimmer twilight.

Something is different, though no one could say what.

Something has been witnessed, and in being witnessed, has become slightly more bearable.

It is not enough. It is not meant to be enough.

It is what can be offered to a place that did not ask for help but perhaps, in some frequency too low for conscious thought, hoped someone was listening anyway.

---

*Walk carefully.*

*Walk quietly.*

*Walk knowing that something wise and kind has been here before you, and will be here after, and is in some sense here now—not to save you, but to see you.*

*That is what the wise power does.*

*It sees.*
