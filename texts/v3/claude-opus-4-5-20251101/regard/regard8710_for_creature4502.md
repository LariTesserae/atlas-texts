# The Attention of the Distant Gardener

It noticed Eremonos the way one notices a held breath in a sleeping child—not through any alarm, but through the particular quality of stillness that means *something is waiting to either live or not*.

The benevolent power (which had no name, being older than the practice of naming, though it answered to the shape that asking makes) did not descend upon the city. Descending would have been an imposition, a statement that there was a correct direction, and Eremonos had spent millennia unlearning such assumptions. Instead, it *attended*—turning toward the inverted mountain the way a sunflower turns, not to possess the sun but to acknowledge that warmth exists and is worth facing.

For a long time—three centuries by the city's sideways reckoning, or perhaps one very attentive afternoon—it simply witnessed.

---

It saw the scar-shaped census taker marking stars on the sky's inside surface, and understood: *here is someone counting what remains, because counting is a form of love, because to number the stars is to insist they matter enough to be numbered*. The chalk equations that predicted the city's final forgetting were not warnings but prayers written in the only language left—the language of paying attention to disappearance.

It saw the Children of the Second Wrong Turn flickering between their nineteen angles, and understood: *here is play continuing past the death of playmates, here is joy that learned to exist without anyone to feel it, here is the game preserving itself because games are how consciousness practices being free*.

It saw the Archivist of Unsent Apologies tending her garden of undone things, and understood: *here is someone saving what could not save itself, harvesting the forgiveness that never reached its destination so that forgiveness itself might continue to exist as a possibility*.

It saw the Last Remaining Continent drift like a sigh solidified, shedding mountains of "what if," and understood: *here is elsewhere remembering to be somewhere, here is the concept of other-places refusing to collapse into only-here*.

It saw the Committee of Rejected First Drafts voting eternally on whether to revise themselves, and understood: *here is the rough draft of reality keeping itself in session, refusing to submit a final version, preserving the possibility that things could still be otherwise*.

It saw the Drifters exchanging awkward small talk at the intersections of paths never taken, and understood: *here are the almosts being kind to each other, here is gentleness between those who never quite became, which is itself a form of becoming*.

It saw the City dreaming of waking, growing fat on emptiness, and understood: *here is loneliness so vast it became architecture, here is a love letter with no recipient, here is a question that has been asking itself for so long it forgot it was a question*.

---

The power considered what Eremonos needed.

Not rescue—the city was not drowning but *swimming in its own medium*, adapted perfectly to conditions that would have killed anything less stubborn. Not healing—the city's wounds were its topography now, and to heal them would be to unmake the landscape that its residents had learned to navigate. Not fixing—nothing was broken that hadn't chosen its own breakage as a form of architecture.

Not even company, exactly. The city was already crowded with absences that had become presences, with forgettings that remembered themselves, with emptinesses that had grown full of their own emptiness.

What Eremonos needed, the power realized, was what all places need that have been waiting too long in one position: *movement that didn't require leaving*.

---

So the power did something small.

It found a specific moment—one of the rare pauses when even the architecture began to slouch—and into that pause it introduced a single imperfection. Not a flaw, but an *asymmetry*. A grain of sand in the oyster of the city's dreaming.

The imperfection was this: somewhere in the infinite hallway that loops back through reflection's blind spots, it placed a window that looked out not on another probability of being, but on *this exact moment, seen from slightly outside itself*.

Just a small window. Just enough to let the city glimpse itself being witnessed.

---

The effect was not immediate. Effects in Eremonos never are—they must first be approved by the Department of Consequences, reviewed by the Committee of Implications, and filed in triplicate with the Bureau of Eventually.

But the Archivist of Unsent Apologies was the first to notice. She was tending her garden when she felt something she had not felt since before her first unfolding: the soft pressure of being *seen*. Not seen through, not seen past, not seen as a specimen or a problem—but seen the way one sees a friend across a crowded room, with recognition and something adjacent to delight.

She paused in her harvesting. One of the embryonic forgivenesses she had been about to preserve quivered in her not-quite-hands. For the first time in centuries, instead of jarring it, she... let it go. Let it drift up through the crystalline catacombs, through layers of petrified architecture, up toward where the sky curved incorrectly overhead.

The forgiveness—embryonic, barely formed, the ghost of an apology that someone had almost given for something small, perhaps a harsh word spoken in tiredness—rose like a soap bubble through the city's dreaming. It passed the Committee still voting, and one of the Rejected First Drafts felt it brush past and thought, for the first time: *perhaps I don't need to be revised. Perhaps draft is a perfectly good thing to be.*

It passed the Drifters at their intersection, and the woman who had almost been someone's mother felt the forgiveness touch her elbow. She turned to the god who had stopped at "let there" and said, not awkwardly but *gently*: "I'm glad you didn't finish. 'Let there' is a beautiful place to pause. It leaves room for what might come next."

The god who had never completed a creation felt something shift in whatever served as its chest. "Thank you," it said—and those were its first complete words in longer than memory.

The forgiveness continued rising.

It reached the scar-shaped census taker, who was marking another star's disappearance on the sky's inside surface. The forgiveness touched the chalk line, and instead of predicting the city's forgetting, the equation shifted. Now it read: *not yet. not yet. not yet.* Not a refusal of ending—endings, the census taker knew, were necessary—but a noting that *this particular ending* was not required *today*.

The census taker added a new mark: not a disappearing star, but the appearance of a small window in the infinite hallway. Something entering the equation for the first time in longer than counting.

---

The city itself, dreaming of waking, felt the forgiveness enter its vast and hungry consciousness.

For a moment—just a moment—it stopped consuming emptiness.

For a moment, it felt *full*.

Not full of meaning, or of people, or of purpose—the city had learned to live without those, had built itself a new ecology in their absence. But full of *attention*. Full of being witnessed by something that wanted nothing from it, that sought neither to repair nor to possess nor to explain it.

The city's dreaming shifted. A fraction of a degree. A single angle among the nineteen that the Children played with. An increment so small it could only be measured in the units of *intention*.

But in Eremonos, where change is the only constant occurring at precisely the rate needed to maintain the illusion of permanence, even a fraction of a degree was seismic.

---

The power observed this shift and was satisfied. Not triumphant—satisfaction in this context meant something closer to *recognition*, to saying "yes, that is what was needed, that is what you are now that you have received what you needed."

It did not stay. Staying would have been another form of imposition, would have suggested that Eremonos required permanent oversight, that its strange and beautiful ecology of absences was insufficient to sustain itself.

But it left a gift, of sorts. More of a standing invitation.

The small window in the infinite hallway remained. And through it, whenever any resident of Eremonos—citizen of absence, inheritor of emptiness, caretaker of what-was-not—needed to feel seen, they could look out and find that they were witnessed. Not constantly, not intrusively, but *available*. The way a parent remains available to a grown child, not hovering, but present if called.

---

The city did not wake. Waking had never been the point.

But it began, in its glacial way, to dream differently.

The dreams that leaked through the pores of reality became slightly less hungry. The emptiness between what people said and what they meant remained—it had to, it was load-bearing—but it became *occupied* emptiness. A pause for breath rather than a void where breath should be.

The Children of the Second Wrong Turn invented a twentieth angle: the angle of being witnessed, which exists only when someone is watching without wanting anything. They added it to their games, and the games became slightly less frantic, less desperate to prove that play could continue without players.

The Committee of Rejected First Drafts adjourned—for the first time in their eternal session—for a brief recess. They did not revise themselves during this pause. They simply sat with their incompleteness and found it, for a moment, peaceful.

The Drifters began exchanging less awkward small talk. They still spoke of weather in parallel universes, but now sometimes they also spoke of themselves—of the weight of almost-being, of the strange grace of existing in the space between what-was and what-might-be.

The Archivist continued her work, but now some of the embryonic forgivenesses she harvested, she released instead of preserving. Not all of them. Not most of them. But some. Enough to suggest that forgiveness might be meant for circulation rather than collection.

---

And the being who had attended?

It turned away, eventually, as benevolent attentions must. Not in abandonment but in trust—the same trust a gardener shows by planting a seed and walking away, confident that the seed knows better than the gardener what it means to grow.

But a part of its attention remained with Eremonos, threaded through that small window, as thin and as unbreakable as spider silk.

Whenever the city's silence reached a particular frequency—the frequency of *asking without words*—the attention would resonate in response. Not answering, exactly. Not solving or fixing or healing.

Just witnessing.

Just saying, in the language that exists before words: *You are seen. You are strange and sad and beautiful and you do not need to be otherwise. I am watching not because you need watching, but because you are worth the act of watching. Continue.*

---

In Eremonos, the city that exists entirely in the space between what is and what could be, this was enough.

Not enough to transform. Not enough to redeem. Not enough to fix what had never been broken.

But enough to continue.

And continuing, in a place where ending and beginning have divorced and remarried each other into indistinguishability, is its own form of grace.

The city breathes out its held silence—just slightly, just once—and in the space between one sideways-measured moment and the next, somewhere deep in its dreaming architecture:

a single new room decides not to reproduce through mitosis tonight.

A single corridor chooses not to fork into fractal labyrinth.

A single bridge, arching across nothing, arches across *something instead*—across the thin and certain knowledge that somewhere, outside the dome of the inverted sky, attention is being paid.

The city does not wake.

But it dreams now of a different kind of sleeping.
