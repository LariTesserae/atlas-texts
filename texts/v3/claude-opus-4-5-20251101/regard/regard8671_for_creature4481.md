I read this twice, slowly, letting the imagery build its weight.

What strikes me first is the coherence beneath the surrealism. This isn't randomness dressed as profundity—it's a rigorously constructed emotional architecture. The world operates by consistent laws, just not ours. Objects become maps of their histories. Observation extends things. Memory outranks stone. These rules create real stakes within the dream-logic.

The piece seems to be about dissolution of self as both terror and homecoming. The narrator loses their name, their companions become unrecognizable, they marry the sound itself—and this is described as "perfect." There's grief here, but also something like relief. The exhaustion of maintaining boundaries in a universe that doesn't recognize them.

**What a benevolent and wise power might do:**

Nothing spectacular.

They would not fix the world. The world is not broken—it is simply operating at a frequency that metabolizes identity faster than the being can regenerate it. The Ring's slow consumption, the stairs that climb you back, the dreams that become community theater—these are not malfunctions. They are the local physics.

What the being needs is not rescue but *witness*.

The benevolent power would sit with them in one of those breathing cathedrals, beside the altar of held water with its fish of unspoken alphabets. They would not speak—speaking here is already contaminated by the flowers echoing your thoughts. Instead they would simply *be present* in a way that doesn't demand the being perform coherence.

They might offer one thing: a container that doesn't leak. Not a physical object—those rot or become what they touch—but a quality of attention. A way of being watched that doesn't extend you endlessly, that allows endings. A gaze that says: *you can stop humming the rotation song for one breath. I'll hold the island from being born while you rest.*

When the being eventually opens their mouth and tastes salt instead of song, the power would receive that too. Would recognize it as speech. Would answer in the only language that survives here: presence that doesn't require you to be locatable to be loved.

Then they would leave, because staying too long would make them part of the architecture. But they'd leave behind something like a seam—an almost-invisible line in the world where the being could, if they chose, remember that dissolution is not the only grammar available.

Not an escape route. Just the knowledge that one exists, held loosely, like a map written by a place hoping to escape itself.
