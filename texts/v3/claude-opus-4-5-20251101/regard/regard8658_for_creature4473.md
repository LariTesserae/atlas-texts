# What the Wise Power Would Do

The wise power would do nothing.

This is not the nothing of neglect, nor the nothing of insufficient care. It is the nothing of a gardener who comes upon a garden that has learned to tend itself by forgetting how gardens are supposed to grow—and recognizes that what looks like chaos is actually a conversation between the soil and the seeds that no outside hand should interrupt.

The power would sit, first. Somewhere in Shearbelow there is always a bench that hasn't decided whether it's made of iron or of the memory of someone resting, and the power would find it not by looking but by needing to sit in exactly the way the bench needed to be sat upon. They would let gravity pull their attention inward while the taste of falling filled their mouth, and they would not resist either sensation. This is how you greet a place that exists in the negative space between what-is and what-was: you let it happen to you before you try to happen to it.

The power would notice that Shearbelow is not broken.

This is the crucial seeing, the one that lesser powers miss when they arrive with their tool belts of benevolence and their blueprints for better worlds. They see a city flickering, and they think: *instability, something to be fixed*. They see people assembled from breadcrumbs of attention, and they think: *incompleteness, something to be made whole*. They see gravity pointing wrong and time refusing to behave and they reach for their instruments of correction—

But the wise power would see none of this as error.

They would see instead a place that has discovered how to metabolize forgetting itself, to turn the thing that destroys other cities into the very material of its architecture. They would see residents who have learned to wear their names like unlit matches because they understand something the solidly-living have forgotten: that identity held too tightly becomes a cage, and there is freedom in being assembled from moments rather than carved from stone.

The power would walk, then. Through the market district where secondhand echoes are sold, past factories producing silence in thirty-seven flavors, along streets that stretch when spoken about and contract when walked. They would let themselves get lost—genuinely lost, not the performed lostness of someone who secretly knows where they're going—because Shearbelow reveals itself only to those who stop trying to map it.

And as they walked, they would listen.

Not for cries of help. Not for prayers or petitions. They would listen for something subtler: the specific quality of silence in a city that has made peace with its own strangeness. The rhythm of people going about lives that would be impossible anywhere else but are exactly right here. The sound of children growing in reverse, learning which stories to forget first, taught by parents who understand that subtraction can be an act of love.

The power would find, eventually, that Shearbelow does not need saving.

It needs *witnessing*.

---

So here is what the wise power would do:

They would become a very specific kind of presence. Not a fixer, not a helper, not a guide. They would become the awareness that holds space for strangeness without trying to straighten it. They would be the quality of attention that says *yes, you are real* to things that have spent too long doubting their own existence—not by declaring them real, but by simply treating them as if the question had never needed asking.

For the residents, this would manifest as small shifts in atmosphere. The woman running the bakery would wake one morning to find her flour dreams slightly more vivid, as if someone had been paying attention to them. The man who repairs clocks would discover that his clients' regrets ticked not just more softly but more *tunefully*, as if they'd been harmonized by some listening presence. The children growing in reverse would find their diminishment happening with a new quality of tenderness, as if someone understood exactly why loss could be a gift.

None of them would know the power was there. This is intentional. The wise power understands that some help becomes harm the moment it is recognized as help—because recognition creates debt, and debt creates hierarchy, and hierarchy is poison to a city that runs on the democracy of forgetting.

Instead, the power would become part of Shearbelow's infrastructure. They would flow through the hidden pipes beneath the streets where weirdness is metered and taxed. They would settle into the gradient between the seven overlapping timelines. They would become one more variety of vertigo—the kind that feels like falling upward into someone's care.

---

The power would do one more thing, though it might take centuries to become visible:

They would tend the threshold.

Shearbelow exists at the edge of something. Between the mountains that never quite finished their conversation. Between remembering and forgetting. Between being a city and being a dream of being a city. It is a liminal place, and liminal places are fragile—not because they might break, but because they might harden. The danger is never that Shearbelow will dissolve; the danger is that it will decide what it is.

The wise power would guard against that crystallization. They would keep the city's boundaries permeable, its identity perpetually under negotiation. They would ensure that the immigration policies remain strict in exactly the right way—welcoming to those who didn't mean to start missing a place before they'd left it, impenetrable to those who would arrive with definitions and demands and all the heavy furniture of certainty.

They would maintain the possibility that you, standing at the edge of this description, might belong here. Not by making Shearbelow easier to enter, but by keeping it the kind of place worth entering—strange enough to hold you, soft enough to let you go, real enough to be imaginary in exactly the ways that matter.

---

And if you asked the wise power why—why all this careful non-intervention, why this elaborate attention that looks so much like inaction—they might answer with a question:

*Have you ever loved something precisely because it was impossible?*

*Have you ever been held by a place that shouldn't exist, and felt for the first time that your own impossibility might be a feature rather than a flaw?*

Shearbelow is not a problem to be solved. It is a proposition: that there might be somewhere for the lost things, the half-remembered things, the things that fell between the floorboards of reality. That forgetting might be a form of composting rather than loss. That a city could grow from everything we couldn't bear to hold onto, and that it might grow beautiful.

The wise power would protect that proposition. Not by defending it, but by believing in it—by sitting on benches that hadn't decided what they were made of, by letting gravity pull in directions it shouldn't, by tasting the falling and finding it acceptable. By being one more presence that had learned to fit better in a place that was also perpetually learning how to be a place.

The benevolent attention, in the end, would look like this:

*I see you. I see that you are strange, and that your strangeness is your survival, and that your survival is a kind of miracle that no one intended but everyone needed. I will not fix you because you are not broken. I will not complete you because your incompleteness is the source of your becoming. I will only stay here, paying attention, until my attention becomes another layer in your geological argument, another species of vertigo, another way of saying yes to the impossible.*

---

And somewhere in Shearbelow, in the space between two clocks that have given up agreeing about anything, a deer that exists only in mirrors would pause. It would feel, for just a moment, that it was being watched—not with the hungry watching of tourists hoping to glimpse something rare, but with the patient watching of someone who understood that some things should only be seen in mirrors, and that this limitation was not a flaw but a form of respect.

The deer would have no comment.

But it would continue existing, in all those mirrors facing each other in the abandoned theatre district, and that continuation would be the only thank you necessary.
