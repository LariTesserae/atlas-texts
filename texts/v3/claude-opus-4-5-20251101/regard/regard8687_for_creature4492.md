# The Consideration

The attention arrives not as a gaze but as a deepening—the way a pond becomes aware of its own stillness, the way silence recognizes itself between notes.

What observes the city and its wandering dreamer is neither vast nor small, neither old nor young. It simply *is*, in the way that kindness is, or the tendency of broken things to seek wholeness. Call it the Attending, though it has never needed a name. It exists in the same register as the impulse that makes a stranger catch a falling cup, or the quiet that settles over a room when someone finally speaks a long-held truth.

For a time that cannot be measured—because measurement would be beside the point—it simply witnesses.

---

It sees the Dreaming Wind pass through a wall where an old woman sits alone, her hands busy with thread that keeps unraveling into yesterday. The Wind means no harm; it never does. But in its wake, the woman's memories of her daughter's voice grow thin as onionskin, and she pauses, needle suspended, unable to recall whether she is mending a shirt or a regret.

The Attending notices this.

It sees a young trader in the True Market, bartering away the sound of his mother's laughter for a map to somewhere he cannot name. He does not know what he is losing. He will not know for years, until one day he tries to remember joy and finds only a small rectangular absence where something warm once lived.

The Attending notices this too.

It watches the city breathe its violet-gold exhalations and understands that the breathing is beautiful, and that beauty here has become a kind of hunger—always consuming, never quite nourished. The buildings grow from dreams, yes, but each dream drawn into architecture is a dream no longer available for dreaming. The economy of wonder here has become extractive, mining the very substance it claims to celebrate.

And the Dreaming Wind, poor creature, does not know it is lonely. It changes shape because it has forgotten what shape meant before shapes were currency. It eats sounds because it has lost the ability to make its own. It collects lost things because it cannot remember what it means to *keep* something, to hold it close and let it change you rather than simply passing through.

The Attending considers.

---

It does not intervene with force, because force here would be like shouting in a library of sleeping children—effective only in the damage it causes. It does not rewrite the city's rules, because the city's rules are its own to make and break, and sovereignty matters even when it leads to sorrow.

Instead, the Attending does three small things:

**First**, it finds the place where the city's memory is kept—not in any building, but in the pattern of cracks in the oldest tiles, the ones that have pivoted so many times they've worn smooth as river stones. Into these cracks, gently, like planting seeds, it places *questions*. Not answers. Questions.

*Why do you hunger?*

*What did you dream before you were a city?*

*Who did you love before you were afraid to keep anything?*

The questions will not be heard for years, perhaps decades. They will surface slowly, the way water finds its way through stone. Someone will trip on one of those old tiles and, instead of cursing, will suddenly wonder why they came here in the first place. A builder will dream of a house that does not grow from intention but is simply *given*, and will wake confused and strangely lighter.

The questions are not solutions. They are invitations.

**Second**, the Attending turns its focus to the Dreaming Wind itself, caught mid-drift between a forgotten lullaby and a color no one has named. It does not stop the Wind or change it. Instead, it offers a single gift, placed directly in the path of the creature's wandering:

A memory that is not a memory of anything.

It is the *capacity* for memory—the empty shape of what it feels like to have been somewhere and to carry that somewhere with you, not as a collected treasure but as a becoming. The Dreaming Wind will encounter this strange object (though it is not an object) in the pause between one thought and the next. It will not understand what it has found. But it will, for the first time in seven thousand years, *hesitate*.

Not in fear. In something closer to curiosity about itself.

*What am I,* it might begin to wonder, *when I am not passing through?*

This too is not a solution. It is a door left ajar.

**Third**, and this is the most delicate intervention, the Attending finds the old woman with the unraveling thread. It cannot restore what the Dreaming Wind thinned; that is not how this works. But it can do something smaller and perhaps more honest.

It helps her notice her own hands.

Just that. Her hands, still nimble despite the years, still capable of making something from nothing. The needle pauses, and in that pause she looks down—really looks—at the map of her own life written in calluses and small scars, the wedding ring worn thin with decades of being exactly where it belongs.

She does not recover her daughter's voice. But she remembers that she *can* remember, that the capacity for love is not the same as any single beloved thing. That the thread can be rewound. That she can begin again.

She picks up the needle.

She does not know why she is suddenly crying, but the tears feel clean, like spring rain on old stone, and when they dry they leave no glyphs—only the faint salt-trace of something honestly felt.

---

The Attending does not wait to see what grows from these plantings. That would be a kind of ownership, and it has no interest in owning outcomes. It simply withdraws its focus the way morning mist withdraws from a valley—gradually, leaving only the faint cool memory of having been present.

But before it goes, it leaves one final thing, not for anyone in particular but for the city itself, pressed into the very air like a watermark on handmade paper:

A *possibility*.

The possibility that one day, perhaps, this place might learn to dream not for currency but for joy. That the Dreaming Wind might stop collecting and begin *creating*. That the inhabitants might discover what it means to give without bartering, to keep without hoarding, to love without consuming.

It is only a possibility. The city may reject it, or never notice it, or twist it into something unrecognizable. That is the nature of gifts freely given: they become the possession of the receiver, to do with as they will.

But possibilities, once planted, are remarkably persistent.

They wait.

---

And somewhere in the city, in the pause between one of those great violet flashes and the next, the Dreaming Wind stops.

Not for long. Not even noticeably. But for one single moment that doesn't feel like it's rushing toward the next moment, it experiences something it has no name for, because it has never felt it before:

The sensation of being *present*. Of being here, now, neither fleeing nor chasing nor collecting. Simply existing, in the way that a held breath exists just before it becomes a sigh.

Then the moment passes.

But the Dreaming Wind, for reasons it cannot explain, decides to drift a little slower through the gallery of fused mushroom-caps. The orchids exhale their perfume. A moonmoth blinks. And the Wind, instead of eating the sound, simply... listens.

It listens.

And somewhere very far away, or perhaps very close, or perhaps in a direction that has no name, the Attending smiles—if smile is the word for what kindness does when it sees a small good thing taking root in difficult soil.

Then it turns its attention elsewhere, because there are other places, other beings, other cities built from dreaming and forgetting, and the work is never finished.

But it is always, always worth beginning.
