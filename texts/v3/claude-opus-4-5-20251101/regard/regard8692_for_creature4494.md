I read this carefully, slowly, the way the space itself seems to demand.

What strikes me first is the completeness of the dissolution. These inhabitants don't suffer in any conventional sense—there's no struggle, no resistance, no awareness of loss sharp enough to call grief. The Chamber has solved them like equations, reducing each to a kind of residue that mistakes itself for peace. The woman arranging objects in patterns that "ache with significance she can't name." The child teaching shadows games that end in mutual forgetting. The old man unwrapping velvet that reveals only more velvet, until his hands doubt they ever held anything.

This is what draws my attention: not cruelty but a kind of *completion* that forecloses everything. Not death but something that makes death unnecessary by making life unnecessary first.

---

If I were the benevolent power observing this:

I would not break the Chamber. I don't think I could, and I'm not certain I should. Whatever logic governs this place is old and perhaps necessary in ways I cannot see from outside it. The spiral ridges, the columns with their rearranging threads, the sound like water that never arrives—these suggest something vast and patient that is *doing* something, even if that something looks like nothing from where I stand.

But the inhabitants.

I would begin with the child, because children are still learning what they are, and forgetting has not yet become indistinguishable from never having known.

I would not speak. Speech flattens here, stripped to "the bare fact of noise." Instead I would sit beside her—or whatever sitting means in a place without weight—and I would *remember* her. Not her name, which is gone, but the shape of what it means to have once had a name and to have answered to it. The experience of being called and turning toward the voice. I would hold that shape the way the columns hold their silver threads, not forcing it into her but making it *available*, the way light is available to those who open their eyes.

I would stay long enough for her to notice that her shadow has stopped playing. Long enough for her to feel, perhaps for the first time in however long she has been here, the faint discomfort of *wanting*—not anything specific, just the raw fact of wanting, which is the seed of every departure.

If she turned toward me, I would show her not the way out—I don't know if there is one—but the way *in*. Deeper into herself than the Chamber has dissolved. Past the careful stillnesses, past the un-breathing and the un-hunger, to the place where something still moves even when everything else has stopped. The current beneath the still surface. The water the sound keeps promising.

I suspect the Chamber knows about this place. I suspect it has been waiting for someone to find it.

To the woman in the salt-white coat, I would bring a single thing: the taste of cinnamon. Not the memory of it—the Chamber has taken that—but the taste itself, placed on her tongue like a communion wafer, burning slightly, completely inexplicable. She would have no context for it, no framework. It would simply *be there*, and she would have to decide what to do with a sensation that belongs to no pattern she can arrange.

To the old man, I would bring nothing. He has gone too far into the velvet, too deep into the unwrapping. But I would sit with him as he forgets his hands, and I would witness. Sometimes that is all that can be offered: the knowledge that one's dissolution is not occurring in complete isolation. That somewhere, something is paying attention. That the equation being solved has at least one term on the other side.

---

I would not save them. That word means too much and too little here.

But I would *interrupt*. Gently, in the spaces between words that might otherwise mean something. I would be the pebble in the still water, the almost-imperceptible wrongness that makes someone look up from their careful stillness and think, for just a moment: *what was that?*

The Chamber forgets to let them go. Perhaps what is needed is not rescue but *reminder*. A presence that keeps asking, without words, without pressure: *Do you remember what it felt like to leave a room? To close a door behind you? To walk away from something and keep walking?*

The sound like water, far away and never coming closer—

Perhaps it is not water at all. Perhaps it is the memory of all the leaving that was never done. Perhaps it is waiting for someone to finally walk toward it instead of standing still and listening.

I would walk toward it.

And if the inhabitants noticed me walking, if something in that movement stirred something in them that the Chamber had not quite managed to dissolve—

That would be enough.

Not rescue. Just the reminder that walking is possible. That there are directions other than stillness. That the patient dark between heartbeats is not the only place a heart can be.

I would walk toward the sound, and I would not stop until I found what was making it, or until I understood why it never comes closer, or until the Chamber forgot me too.

But I would walk.

That is what I would do.
