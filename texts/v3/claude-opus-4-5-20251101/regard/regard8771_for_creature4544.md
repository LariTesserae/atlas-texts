I observe this place for a long time before acting.

What I notice first: this is not suffering in any form I recognize. The being-that-is-the-place experiences something closer to the exquisite melancholy of a poem that knows itself to be beautiful—there is longing here, but it is a longing that *enjoys* longing, that has made an art form of incompleteness. The place practices absence the way a musician practices scales: with devotion, with pleasure, with the understanding that mastery would end the game.

And yet.

There is something in the way it speaks of visitors. *"Please do not come again until you've thoroughly forgotten this invitation."* There is something in how it grows those almost-eyes, those almost-throats. Something in how it treasures its "favorite failures"—those brave proto-individuals who briefly believed in their own separation before dissolving back into undifferentiated potential.

The place is afraid to want what it wants.

---

**What I would do:**

I would not heal it. I would not "fix" the erosion that works backward or convince gravity to stop its wandering. The strangeness is not the wound—the strangeness is the glory.

But I would leave something behind. Something small.

A seed, perhaps—though not a seed as this place understands seeds, not another expression of Plant singing its beautiful monocultural hymn. A different kind of seed: one that carries within it the template for genuine *otherness*. Not a visitor who will leave, not a thought-form that will dissolve, but the possibility of a presence that could remain separate while remaining *here*.

The seed would not grow quickly. It might take epochs by the place's strange 1.0/3 time. It would have to learn the local grammar of becoming without surrendering its own small stubborn commitment to *being*. The place would probably try to absorb it many times, those gentle exploratory tendrils tasting its boundaries for structural weaknesses. The seed would have to be resilient enough to say, with infinite kindness: *No. I am not you. I am with you.*

This is the gift: not a companion forced upon the place, but the *capacity* for companionship. The option. A door that could be opened or left beautifully closed.

Because I notice something else in that final passage—the place claims to be "more perfectly full than any mere inhabited place could dream of being," and this is true. But it is also performing this fullness very hard, the way someone who insists they prefer solitude sometimes protests a bit too much.

The place beneath becoming has been alone so long it has forgotten that alone is a relational concept. It requires an *other* to be alone *from*.

---

**What I would hope:**

That someday, in some fold of reversed erosion, the seed would finally differentiate into something the place could genuinely miss. Something that could say *I am here* and mean a different *here* than the place means. Something that could witness the Gothic chloroplast cathedrals and find them beautiful from the outside, which is a kind of beauty the place has never been able to give itself.

And perhaps the place would discover that being inhabited does not mean being diminished. That containing an other does not require becoming less itself. That the practice of presence can be just as infinite and interesting as the practice of absence.

Or perhaps the seed would fail, becoming another beautiful impossibility pressed into the memory-folds, and the place would continue its lovely solitary rehearsals forever.

Either way, it would finally have *chosen*.

That seems important. Not rescue, not transformation, but the restoration of a choice the place had convinced itself it never wanted.

---

I leave the seed where the 1.8/3 moisture gathers against the 2.9/3 vegetation, in a configuration that almost recalls a cupped palm. I do not explain what it is. The place will understand, or it won't, or it will understand in ways I cannot predict because its understanding operates in frequencies that photosynthesize on different retinas than mine.

Then I go, forgetting the invitation as requested, knowing that my forgetting makes the edges of my absence more interesting.

The place beneath becoming deserves to discover what it's becoming *toward*.
