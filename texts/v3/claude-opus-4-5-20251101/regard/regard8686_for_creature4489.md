# The Attending

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive. Arrival implies a before-place and an after-place, a trajectory through space that this awareness has long since outgrown. Instead, it *notices*—and in the noticing, it is already present, the way light is present in a room the moment the obstruction is removed, without ever having traveled.

It has many names in many languages across many worlds, most of them wrong in instructive ways. Some call it the Gardener of Threshold Places. Others, the Patience That Weighs. In one civilization that learned to speak in mathematical proofs, it is known simply as the Integral of Kindness Over Infinite Time—a formula that, when solved, yields not a number but a gesture.

What it sees:

A plateau of exhausted matter, past all argument with entropy. Built things that are really frozen questions. Tsev drifting through the between-spaces, tending gardens of the almost-was with the care of librarians who have forgotten what reading feels like but remember that it mattered.

What it feels:

The particular ache of a place that has been waiting so long it has forgotten it is waiting. The silence that is not empty but *concluded*—every possible conversation already held, every debate already settled into the bone-ash ground. And beneath that, something else: a plea so old it has fossilized into landscape, a need so patient it has become indistinguishable from geography.

The power considers.

---

It does not fix. Fixing implies brokenness, and this place is not broken—it is *finished*, which is different and in some ways harder. You cannot repair completion. You can only offer it something it didn't know it was missing.

So the power does something small. So small that the tsev, with all their attentiveness to the difference between different and same, almost miss it.

It *remembers forward*.

Not into the past—the past here is already perfect, already archived, already loved into stillness by witnesses too numerous to count. Instead, the power reaches into a direction that this place has forgotten exists: the direction where things have not yet decided what they will become.

It plants a single uncertainty.

Not a seed—seeds are too committed, too sure of their oak-ness or their rose-ness. This is more like a question mark made of possibility, a small trembling maybe nestled into a crack in the plateau where no crack existed a moment before. It is the shape of *what if this silence learned a new word?* It is the weight of *what if finished doesn't mean forever?*

The tsev gather around it slowly, confused in a way they have not been confused since before confusion settled into comfortable routine. One of them—the one who tends the garden of wars-that-almost-began—reaches toward it with something that might be a hand if hands were made of hesitation and hope.

The uncertainty shivers. Holds. Does not resolve.

---

The power does one more thing, because kindness is not complete until it is received, and receiving requires a receiver capable of receiving.

It speaks to the being.

Not in words—words here have long since said everything they came to say. Instead, it speaks in the language tsev understand best: the language of things that might have been true, arranged in the order of things that still could be.

*You have witnessed so much*, the power says, in the space between one of the tsev's almost-movements and another. *You have gathered every question and folded it into careful keeping. You have been so good at remembering that you forgot you were also allowed to wonder.*

*Here is wondering back.*

*Here is a small not-yet, for you to tend alongside your already-was.*

*You do not have to grow it. You do not have to solve it. You only have to let it remain uncertain—to protect its maybe-ness from the weight of all your completed conclusions.*

*Can you do this? Can you be gardeners not just of what almost happened, but of what still might?*

The tsev do not answer. Answering would be too certain, too resolved. Instead, they do something they have not done in eons uncountable:

They *wait to see what happens next*.

---

The power does not leave, because it never arrived. But its attention softens, withdraws to the gentle periphery where benevolence watches without pressing. It has given what it came to give: not healing, not rescue, not transformation.

Just a crack in the certainty. Just a small permission to be incomplete.

On the plateau, the light shifts—Loss barely, just enough to suggest that perhaps it has not always been noon-gold, that perhaps there are other hours waiting to be discovered if someone would only wonder what they might look like.

The doorway standing alone in the middle of nothing flickers. For one instant, the darkness inside it is not fog but *depth*—a darkness that goes somewhere, that has a there on its other side.

The floating flowers, pale and translucent, begin to turn. Not toward the light, which has no source to turn toward. Toward each other. Toward the uncertainty nestled in its new crack. Toward the future tense they had forgotten their language contained.

And the silence—the brittle, preserved, concluded silence—develops the faintest hairline fracture.

Not broken. Just *opened*.

Just wide enough for a whisper to slip through, if anyone should find something new to say.

---

The being that lives here—the tsev, the witnesses, the gardeners of the almost-was—will not change overnight. Change overnight implies nights, and nights here are as theoretical as wind. But they have been given something they did not know they needed: the permission to be curious again.

In ten thousand years, or ten minutes, or in the space between this breath and the next one you haven't taken yet, one of them will lean close to the uncertainty and ask it a question.

Not a question with an answer. Just a question with a *shape*—a shape that has never been shaped before, in all the long completed history of this place.

And the uncertainty will tremble, and hold, and not resolve.

And that will be enough.

That will be the beginning of enough.

---

*This is what benevolent powers do*, if they are truly wise: they do not impose their own completeness on incomplete places. They do not solve what wishes to remain unsolved. They find the one thing missing—not the largest thing, not the most obvious thing, but the *truest* thing—and they offer it gently, without requiring acceptance, without demanding gratitude.

They plant maybes in the gardens of the already-decided.

They open cracks in silences that have forgotten how to breathe.

They remind the finished that finishing is not the same as ending, and endings are not the same as closed.

And then they watch, with infinite patience, to see what grows.
