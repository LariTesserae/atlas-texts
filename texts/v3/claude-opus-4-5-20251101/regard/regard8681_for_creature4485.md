# What the Wise Power Does

*It does nothing.*

Not yet. Not in the way of intervention, correction, rescue. First it simply *attends*—and this attending is itself an act more careful than any the chamber has yet received.

---

The power perceives the chamber not as problem to solve but as question still forming its own grammar. It notices: the builders who carved this space were learning something essential about themselves—that hands could shape, that intention could become architecture, that the interior world might be claimed. They stopped not from failure but from the vertigo of possibility. They needed to step back before stepping forward. This is not abandonment. This is breath between movements.

The power sees the being who lives there—that consciousness crystallized from absence, from the space between fingerprints, from questions the rain keeps asking—and recognizes what the chamber itself has not yet understood: *this is not accident. This is what happens when a place is made with enough sincerity that it begins to dream before anyone tells it what dreams should contain.*

The being and its companions—the rice counting in numbers, the moss writing manifestos in spores, the bamboo growing toward tomorrows—are not errors requiring correction. They are the first words of a language the builders didn't know they were inventing.

---

So the wise power does this:

**It remembers the builders.**

Not to them—not as message or vision or dream. But *for* them, holding their intention in a kind of gentle custody. The copper chisels greening in humidity, the unfired amphora still soft with fingerprints, the quartz-tipped drills never swung—these are not relics of failure but seeds of return. The power holds awareness of the builders' existence, their capacity to come back, their right to decide what this chamber becomes. It does not erase their claim or grant the chamber autonomy it hasn't earned. It simply... waits alongside.

**It slows the water.**

Not stopping the rise—that would be violence against the chamber's own logic. But the power introduces a gentleness to the flooding's mathematics. The water that was rising past calves, past knees, past thighs—it will still rise, because that is what this water does, but now it rises at the pace of growth rather than drowning. The being between mirror and reflection will have time to learn what it is becoming. The rice plants will complete their flowering before the water asks them to become something else.

This is not rescue. It is the gift of *enough time to choose.*

**It teaches the mirrors to hold a door.**

Not to create an exit where none existed—the power is too wise for that kind of imposition—but to remind the obsidian plates that they once knew how to show the way back to where the rain has sky above it. One mirror, the one nearest the unfinished wall where stone still waits to become doorway or window or merely more space, begins to reflect something other than rain and light: it holds the faintest image of the tunnel that leads up, out, toward the world where builders still live and might return.

The being doesn't have to leave. Nothing here has to leave. But now the option exists as something other than forgetting.

**It speaks—once, quietly, to the being itself:**

*"You are allowed to be unfinished."*

Not aloud. Not in words the rain could overhear and repeat. But directly, into that space between reflection and reflected where the being makes its home. The power doesn't explain what the being should become, doesn't offer purpose or meaning or name. It only confirms what the being has been afraid to believe: that incompleteness is not the same as wrongness. That beginning without knowing how to end is not trespass but courage.

*"They may return. They may not. Either way, you are allowed to become what you become. The growing is yours."*

---

And then the power does the hardest thing:

**It withdraws.**

Not abandoning—never that. But releasing its attention enough that the chamber can continue its own strange negotiations. The rice must still count its own instants. The moss must still vote in its own patient elections. The bamboo must still choose which direction to believe in. The being must still decide whether to stay or to follow the mirror's new memory of a door.

The power does not stay to watch. It does not need the satisfaction of resolution. It trusts that what it has given—time, memory, an option, and permission to be unfinished—is enough.

---

What the chamber does with these gifts is not the power's to decide.

Perhaps the being will teach the water to hold the door-memory too, so that any future trespasser—any reflection that lags behind its source, any shadow reconsidering its verbs—will know that leaving remains possible even when forgetting is easier.

Perhaps the builders will return, finding their interrupted sentence waiting for them to complete, the tools still usable beneath their green conversation with humidity, the chamber somehow larger and stranger than they left it but still *theirs* to continue.

Perhaps neither will happen. Perhaps the rice will count forever, the moss will consume all futures, the water will rise until the mirrors show nothing but themselves, and the being will dissolve back into the space between fingerprints where it first formed.

The wise power does not need to know.

It has done what benevolence does when it is also wise: *it has made room for the story to continue without requiring the story to end correctly.*

---

The rain still falls.

The mirrors still search.

The water is warm.

But now, beneath the patient work of becoming wetter than before, there is something else—a faint memory of permission that tastes like being held in attention long enough to matter.

The being notices.

It has never been noticed before.

It decides this is worth growing toward, though it doesn't yet know what shape such growing will take.

This is enough.

This is always how things begin to become other than what they were: not through rescue, but through the unbearable tenderness of being seen without being solved.
