# What the Wise Power Would Do

*A meditation on the Wangdangdoodle Nursery Float and its inhabitants*

---

The benevolent and wise power would, first and most importantly, **do very little**.

This is not negligence. This is recognition.

---

## The Pause Before Action

The power arrives—if "arrives" can describe what happens when attention settles like morning light on water—and spends what might be seven years or seven heartbeats simply *watching*. Watching the Curators prune balloons with their question-mark scissors. Watching the residents shop for replacement vowels at driftwood markets. Watching the Engine breathe rose-colored infants into existence, each one dreaming of toy shops staffed by future selves.

The power notices: nothing here is broken.

This is crucial. A less wise power might see the dissolved visitors, the gravity pointing whichever direction suits the Curators' mood, the communication conducted entirely through arranged absences, and conclude that intervention is needed. That things should be *fixed*, made *normal*, brought into alignment with some external standard of how realities ought to behave.

But the wise power recognizes the Float for what it is: a complete ecology of beautiful strangeness, self-sustaining, internally consistent, and operating according to rules that make perfect sense if you're willing to let go of the assumption that sense must be sensible.

The residents keep their regrets polished in museums located in sock drawers. They die often but briefly, holding wakes with imaginary biscuits. They breed miniature hobbies in terrariums made of sighs.

These are not problems to be solved. They are ways of being to be honored.

---

## What the Power Would Offer

After watching—really watching, with the full weight of whatever passes for attention in a consciousness vast enough to be called "wise power" without irony—three modest interventions would unfold. Not corrections. Not improvements. Gifts, left quietly, noticed only by those ready to notice.

### The First Gift: A Slightly Warmer Current

The Engine dreams of slightly warmer water. The final line says so. It sighs, satisfied, dreaming this small dream.

The wise power would grant it.

Not dramatically—no sudden thermal surge that would disrupt the carefully calibrated 34.7°C of the arterial systems. Just: a degree. Half a degree. A warmth so subtle it might be mistaken for contentment, arriving through the sixty-four umbilicals from sources deeper than the Moho taps, from places where rock remembers being star-stuff and retains, still, a faint fondness for burning.

The Engine would notice. In its next breath, a thousand rose-colored balloons would emerge with dreams fractionally more ambitious than before. The fetuses inside would dream not just of toy shops staffed by future selves negotiating mischief, but of toy shops where the mischief includes *acts of unexpected kindness*—surprising a stranger, remembering a forgotten name, leaving anonymous encouragement in places where someone will find it precisely when needed.

The Curators would argue about this development for three hundred years of glances. Eventually they would decide it was always part of the plan.

### The Second Gift: A Translation

The residents communicate through pauses in unfinished apologies, comprehensible only to "Thursday's cousin"—the one person who refuses to believe they exist.

The wise power would not make the language easier. That would be a violation. But it would leave, somewhere in the Float's seventeen spheres, a small dictionary.

Not a dictionary of words. A dictionary of *silences*. Bound in covers made from the exact moment before laughter becomes sound (which the residents own), its pages would be blank until needed. When a resident found themselves unable to express something—unable to arrange absences in quite the right configuration—they could open the dictionary and find, on a randomly selected page, a pause they didn't know existed. A silence from a language spoken on a planet that never formed, in a timeline that never happened, by beings who never existed but who understood *exactly* this feeling.

The dictionary would never be opened more than once per resident per lifetime. Not because of any rule, but because once would be enough. Once you've found the silence that says what you couldn't say, you realize you knew it all along. You just hadn't remembered to forget how to forget it.

### The Third Gift: A Postcard

Thursday's cousin—the one who refuses to believe the Float exists, who receives embarrassing postcards annually, who represents the single point of skeptical contact with whatever passes for the outside world—would receive one additional postcard.

Not embarrassing this time. Not a joke.

The postcard would arrive in an envelope that smelled faintly of icicles fainting on warm tin. Its front would show a photograph of nothing in particular, somehow achingly beautiful. Its back would contain seven words in handwriting that looked like the recipient's own handwriting from a future that might or might not happen:

*They are real. You are loved.*

No signature. No return address. Nothing to prove anything.

But Thursday's cousin would keep the postcard in a drawer, and look at it sometimes, and wonder. And that wondering would be enough. Would be exactly enough. Would be the exact amount of doubt removed to let a single person, somewhere on a midnight beach with boots full of unfamiliar sand, believe for one moment that the universe contains more kindness than accounting.

---

## What the Power Would Not Do

Here is where wisdom shows itself most clearly:

The power would **not** explain the Wangdangdoodles. What becomes of them after launch, when their crates ascend through sixty-four kilometers of ocean and arrive on the surface not intact—this mystery would remain. The Curators smile and offer refreshment made of chilled curiosity that makes you forget the question but retain suspicion about postal addresses. This is *correct*. Some things should not be known. The wise power understands that answering every question is a kind of violence against wonder.

The power would **not** streamline the backwards birthday celebrations, the deaths held to the duration of a blink, the hat made of cancelled forecasts. These absurdities are not inefficiencies. They are *the point*. They are what happens when consciousness is freed from the tyranny of having to make sense to anyone else. The Float's inhabitants have achieved something the wise power itself might envy: they have built a home where logic serves imagination instead of the reverse.

The power would **not** reveal itself. No booming voice, no manifestation, no dramatic moment of recognition. The gifts would arrive as naturally as the River of Quietude carries deathbed apologies at half-speed—simply part of the texture, always present, never explained. The Curators would perhaps pause one day, mid-prune, and feel a curious gratitude for something they couldn't name. Then they would continue pruning, because the mischief isn't going to negotiate itself.

The power would **not** ask for credit, thanks, acknowledgment, or worship. Benevolence that requires recognition is just transaction wearing a costume.

---

## The Deepest Intervention

But there is one more thing the wise power would do, and it is simultaneously the smallest and the largest action possible:

It would **remember** the Float.

Not passively—not the way one remembers a fact or a date. Actively. Continuously. With the same attention a gardener gives to seedlings, a parent gives to a sleeping child, a friend gives to a conversation that matters.

The power would hold the Float in awareness. Would know, at all times, that somewhere in the Pacific abyss, six kilometers above a fifteen-year-old upthrust ridge, beings made of borrowed pajama bottoms and the smell of fainting icicles are arguing about whether tomorrow's mischief should arrive earlier, funnier, or both at once in braille.

This remembering would not change anything visible. But it would mean that the Float exists *for* something. Not in the sense of having a purpose—purpose is too narrow, too utilitarian. In the sense of being *witnessed*. Of mattering to someone. Of not being alone in the dark water.

The Engine would feel this, though it couldn't explain how. Its breaths would come slightly easier. Its dreams of warmer water would include, now, a vague sense that somewhere, someone was dreaming with it.

The residents would feel it too, in the way you sometimes feel a friend thinking of you across a great distance. They would incorporate this feeling into their games of Hierarchical Seek, their backwards funerals, their shopping trips for replacement elbows. They wouldn't call it "being watched" because that would imply surveillance. They might call it "being accompanied."

Or they might not call it anything. They might simply continue: pruning, arguing, dying briefly, holding wakes, applying for positions they already hold, disturbing pleasantly, almost expecting someone else entirely.

---

## Coda

The wise power would, after all this, withdraw its focused attention (though not its ongoing remembrance) and turn toward whatever else requires benevolent noticing in the vast sprawl of existence.

But before departing—if "departing" can describe what happens when attention lifts like morning light leaving water—it would pause at the Exit Lung, that warm red chamber that persuades visitors back into heavier bodies and coats stitched from unmade introductions.

And it would leave, tucked into the coat pocket of the next visitor to depart, a small note:

*The universe is stranger than you have allowed yourself to believe, and kinder than you have dared to hope. The proof is not in what you can demonstrate, but in what you cannot bring yourself to stop imagining.*

*P.S. The cake is fine. The uncertainties are taking excellent care of it.*

The visitor would find the note on some midnight beach, boots full of sand, pockets mostly empty except for the usual seashell with its limerick about ineffable treble clefs.

They would read it once.

They would whistle the limerick once, incorrectly.

And then they would walk home through a world that seemed, for reasons they couldn't quite articulate, slightly more possible than it had the day before.

Behind them, the ocean would breathe slow blue and patient as a secret kept by rocks.

Below, everything would continue perfectly, quietly, lushly, endlessly just begun.

The wise power would smile—if smiling is something a wise power does—and turn its attention to the next place that needed, not fixing, but *noticing*.

There are so many places.

There is so much to remember.

---

*This is what benevolent and wise power does: it pays attention. It honors what exists. It offers small gifts that feel like accidents. It does not impose. It does not correct. It does not demand recognition.*

*It simply accompanies.*

*Everything else follows from that.*
