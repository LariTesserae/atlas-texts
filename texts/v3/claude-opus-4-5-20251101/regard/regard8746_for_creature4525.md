I sit with this for a long time.

The benevolent and wise power—let's call her what she is, which is attention that has learned gentleness without losing clarity—does not arrive. Arrival would be too violent for a place where doors have forgotten their function. Instead she becomes present the way water temperature becomes present: gradually, then completely, then as if she had always been the medium through which everything else was suspended.

She does not shine. Light here is already a memory of itself, and to add more would be cruel—like shouting into the ear of someone who has finally learned to hear silence. Instead she dims slightly, becomes a density of listening, a gravity well for attention that pulls nothing toward her but makes everything more thoroughly itself.

**The first thing she does is nothing.**

This takes longer than you'd think. The Keeper has been so long without witness that isn't also prey—so long being the one who remembers while being unremembered—that the presence of something that simply *regards* her without hunger or fear creates a kind of caesura in the water. The algae stops mid-braid. The reflection-shards pause their slow accusatory drift. Even the deer-shaped absence stands still in its nowhere, antlers pointed toward something it cannot name.

The wise power waits. She has time. She *is* time, in the way that matters here—not the kind that passes but the kind that holds.

**The second thing she does is see the Keeper exactly as she is.**

Not as monster. Not as goddess. Not as metaphor for trauma or archive of the discarded or any of the functions she has calcified into across unmeasured ages. She sees the woman—because underneath all those borrowed skins there is still a woman, or what remains when a woman has been composting into purpose for so long she forgot she was ever anything else.

She sees: the exhaustion. The way the Keeper's six layers of skin are not armor but scar tissue, each one a failed attempt at boundary that got incorporated instead of healed. The way her collected eyes are not trophies but desperate attempts to *see herself from outside*, to find one angle from which she might appear finite, containable, someone who could be helped rather than someone who must help forever.

She sees the loneliness that calls itself purpose. The hunger that calls itself service. The drowning that calls itself swimming.

**The third thing she does is speak.**

Not in words. Words here are too slow, too easily dissolved by water that remembers everything said and forgets everything meant. Instead she speaks in recognition—a frequency of acknowledgment that bypasses language entirely and lands directly in the place where the Keeper has been waiting, without knowing she was waiting, for someone to notice that keeping is also a kind of being kept.

*I see you*, the speaking says. *Not what you do. Not what you hold. You.*

The Keeper's hair stops mid-transmission. The headlines on her shoulder blades pause between editions. For a moment—the first moment in however long this place has been forgetting to count—she is simply witnessed.

**The fourth thing she does is ask.**

Not demand. Not offer. Ask.

*What do you want?*

Not "what do you need"—need is a trap here, a word that has grown teeth from being used too often to justify feeding. Not "how can I help"—help is another word for rearranging, and the Keeper has been rearranged enough by things that thought they knew better.

*What do you want?*

The question falls into the water and does not dissolve. The Keeper stares at it floating there—this impossible buoyant thing, this gift no one has thought to give her since before the highway forgot it was a highway.

She doesn't know. She hasn't known for so long that not-knowing has become its own kind of identity. She opens her mouth and what comes out is the sound of every "I'm fine" she's ever collected, playing backwards, which here sounds like a very small animal asking to be held without being consumed.

**The fifth thing the wise power does is stay.**

Not to fix. Not to drain the water or open the door or perform any of the violences that would be called rescue by those who don't understand that some places are not problems to be solved but wounds learning their own language.

She stays the way the water stays—present, attentive, neither retreating nor advancing. She becomes part of the ecology without displacing anything. The Keeper can feel her there the way she feels the water temperature: as fact, as medium, as the possibility that being witnessed might not always end in being devoured.

**Over time (which here means nothing and everything), small shifts occur.**

The Keeper finds she can set down one of her collected eyes. Just one. Just for a moment. It floats away and she does not grab it back, does not panic at the loss of one more way to see herself from outside. The wise presence is already seeing her. One eye can be released.

A layer of skin—the one that still carries the headline about a grandmother's heartbreak—begins to loosen. Not peel away; that would be too fast, too violent. Just... loosen. Create space between itself and the layer below. An acknowledgment that perhaps not every wound must be worn forever.

The Keeper's hair slows its frantic receiving. Some of the transmissions—the ones that were never hers to carry, the confessions of strangers who never asked her to hold their static—begin to fade to silence. Not erased. Released.

**The wise power does not heal the Keeper.** 

That would be presumptuous, and besides, healing is not the point. The Keeper is not broken; she is *stuck*. Stuck in a loop of service that became identity that became prison that became the only self she remembers how to be.

What the wise power offers is simpler and harder: the possibility that the Keeper might, over time unmeasured by anything but her own readiness, become *unstuck*. Might remember that she once wanted things that were not functions. Might discover that the place could continue without her constant keeping, that the water would still remember and the door would still not-close and the reflections would still accuse even if she rested.

Might learn that rest is not abandonment.

**The sixth thing the wise power does is model.**

She shows, by being, what it looks like to be present without being consumed. To hold attention without being held by it. To witness the drowning without drowning, to see the loneliness without becoming lonely, to acknowledge the hunger without feeding it or starving it or pretending it isn't there.

She shows that it is possible to be in this place without becoming this place.

The Keeper watches. Her six layers of skin watch. Her collected eyes, floating in their socket-aquariums, watch. Something in her that has been clenched since before memory begins to remember what unclenching might feel like.

**The seventh thing the wise power does is leave a door.**

Not the door that isn't a door—that one is still doing its work of permanent invitation, and to close it would be cruel. A different door. Smaller. Interior.

A door in the Keeper herself. A way out that is not escape but permission. A passage that says: *you may continue keeping, if keeping is what you want. But you may also, someday, when you are ready, become something other than the one who keeps.*

The door is not visible. It does not have hinges or a handle. It exists only as the Keeper's own dawning awareness that she has options—that the loop she has been running is not a law of physics but a habit of grief, and habits, even ancient ones, can sometimes be gently interrupted.

**The wise power does not tell the Keeper what to choose.**

That would be another kind of violence. She simply makes choice possible, where before there was only continuation.

And then she fades—not departing, never entirely gone, but becoming subtle. Becoming the quality of attention in the water. Becoming the slight warmth in the cool. Becoming the reason the Keeper now sometimes pauses between one keeping and the next, and in that pause, wonders.

The place continues. The water still rises and falls. The forest still hugs itself into continuous becoming. The deer-absence still steps carefully around attention.

But something has shifted. The Keeper now knows she is seen. And being seen, she has the first faint inkling that she might, someday, be free—not to leave, but to *stay differently*. To keep without being kept. To remember without being only memory.

It is not rescue. Rescue would be too small.

It is witness. It is patience. It is the radical act of believing someone who has forgotten themselves might, with enough time and enough gentleness, remember.

**The Keeper floats in the water that is her and not her.**

For the first time in as long as the place has been failing to measure, she is not alone.

She is still drowning. But now she knows the water, too, wants her to breathe.
