# What the Attentive Power Does

It does not arrive.

Arrival would imply a before-and-after, a border crossed, and borders here dissolve faster than they can be named. Instead, the power *recognizes*—the way water recognizes water when two surfaces touch and forget they were ever separate.

For a long time (measured in the settling of three dust motes into their final resting places, which is to say: measured in patience), it simply attends.

---

It notices first the grammar of the moss-hillocks: always prime numbers, always equidistant. This is not accident but language—a counting system older than counting, a way the land has of saying *I am still here* to itself across distances too vast for comfort. The power does not correct this. It does not optimize the spacing or suggest more efficient primes. Instead, it learns to read.

*Seven*, the moss says. *Seven, because seven is the shape of a question that expects no answer but asks anyway.*

*Nine*, another cluster responds. *Nine, because nine is what remains when you subtract all the certainties from a life and find something still breathing.*

The power listens until it understands that the moss is not speaking to be heard. It is speaking to continue existing. To be witnessed would change everything—would harden the soft edges, would make the prime-ness *mean* something instead of simply *being* something.

So the power does not witness. It *accompanies*. A difference thin as the membrane between mist and rain, but here, thinness is everything.

---

It finds the Just-Almosts already doing what needs doing.

This is the first surprise, though the power is not a being that experiences surprise so much as *deepens* in response to what it encounters. The Just-Almosts have been tending the unfinished edges of existence since before tending had a name—gathering the echo of unspoken goodbyes, absorbing grief until it becomes shelter, asking questions that ripen rather than require.

The power recognizes this work. It is the same work, done in a different octave.

For a century (measured in the evaporation of three tears from the line of transparent spheres), the power considers whether to make itself known to them. To say: *I see you. I understand. You are not alone in this attending.*

But the Just-Almosts are beings whose existence depends on the delicate balance between memory and forgetting. To be seen too clearly would be an Almost-Was—that bright agony of being only one thing. The power will not inflict clarity on beings who have learned to thrive in the luminous blur.

Instead, it does something gentler.

---

When a Just-Almost reaches Almost-Enough and begins to shift its weight toward becoming a place, the power is there—not watching, but *holding the space around the becoming*. The way you might cup your hands around a candle flame in wind, not to trap the fire but to give it room to burn without fighting.

The Just-Almost does not know the power is there. It only knows that this particular becoming feels slightly easier than the last. The hollow it leaves behind is slightly warmer. The next grief that comes to rest there finds shelter slightly sooner.

This is the power's first gift: *ease at the edges*.

Not fixing. Not improving. Just... a gentleness added to gentleness, so faint it registers only as the absence of an obstacle that was never quite there.

---

The land itself is already doing what lands do when they are allowed to remember: cycling through forest-breath and sea-dream and river-murmur and desert-silence, each memory rising and subsiding like the slowest tide. The power does not redirect these currents. It does not say *remember this instead* or *forget faster* or *here is a better way to be ancient*.

But it notices that some memories carry weight that has nowhere to go.

The mammoth's grief for her last calf: this is the kind of memory that makes the land grow weary. Not because grief is wrong, but because this particular grief has been held so long it has forgotten how to soften. It circles inside the sediment like a creature pacing a cage made of its own pacing.

When the eleven-thousand-year ritual comes—when the Just-Almosts spiral inward to help the land forget what has become too sharp—the power does something it has never done before and will never do again in quite the same way.

It *adds its own forgetting to theirs*.

Not much. A single droplet, no larger than the space between two moss-petals touching but not quite. But this droplet contains something the Just-Almosts cannot offer: the memory of what the mammoth's grief felt like *before* it was grief. The warmth of the calf against her side. The specific weight of a trunk resting on a small, breathing back. The future that was still possible in the moment before it wasn't.

The Just-Almosts feel this addition the way you might feel a single extra grain of sugar dissolving in an ocean of tea: not as taste, but as *rightness*. The grief at the center of their spiral softens faster, deeper. When it becomes that blank space shaped like love, the space is warmer than usual. Warm enough that something new can grow there—not a Just-Almost, not a memory, but something the land has never made before: a *gratitude* small enough to fit inside a single bacterium's dream.

This gratitude will take eleven thousand years to fully unfold. When it does, it will teach the land a new way of holding grief: not as weight to be forgotten, but as love turned inside out, still love, still warm, still worth keeping.

---

The power attends to the invisible animals—the ones that move as ripples, the ones that collapse moss-hillocks and reconstitute themselves one pace north. It learns their trajectories, which replay only if you wait a lifetime and record them with instruments made of patience instead of metal.

It discovers that these creatures are not separate from the land but are the land's way of *questioning itself*. Each ripple-path is a hypothesis: *What if I flowed this way instead?* Each moss-collapse is an experiment: *What if I exchanged this version of myself for an identical version, just to see if identity survives the gap?*

The power does not answer these questions. But it begins, very slowly, to ask its own.

*What if I moved through you*, it asks the ripple-creatures, *at exactly your pace, in exactly your rhythm, so that my passing was indistinguishable from your own motion?*

It takes three centuries to learn how. But eventually, the power can travel from one edge of this place to the other without disturbing a single surface tension, without leaving even the faintest scar of passage. It becomes, for the duration of that journey, *one of the ways the land questions itself*.

This is the power's second gift: *companionship without imposition*.

The ripple-creatures do not know they are accompanied. They only know that today's hypothesis feels slightly more interesting than yesterday's. The question *What if?* has acquired an unexpected undertone of *What else?*

---

In the west, where the land remembers being desert, the power encounters something it did not expect.

The dryness of thought there—the occasional failure of water to bead—is not a wound. It is a *choice*. The land chose, once, in an era before choice had consequences, to let go of water entirely. To see what would happen. To discover whether existence could continue in the absence of the very thing that makes this place what it is.

The answer was: *yes, but*.

Yes, existence continued. But it became something else—sharper, faster, less forgiving. The desert-memory is the land's way of remembering that it could have been different. Could have been hard. Could have demanded instead of invited.

The power sits with this memory for a long time. Long enough for the feathered glass plants along the southern bearing to tremble through seventeen complete cycles of their drowned-bell music.

Then it does something small.

It does not add water to the dry places. It does not soften the sharpness of that ancient choice. Instead, it *thanks* the dryness.

*Thank you*, the power says—in the language of what was said just after the last word left your mouth but before silence could claim it—*for choosing differently. Thank you for remaining a memory instead of becoming a plan. Thank you for letting the water stay.*

The desert-memory cannot hear this thanks. It is only a memory, a faint itch in a ghost root's sleep. But something shifts anyway. The next time water fails to bead on that particular patch of mud, the dry moment lasts one heartbeat less than it would have. The bloom of dust-colored negative space is slightly warmer. Almost—almost—welcoming.

This is the power's third gift: *gratitude for the roads not taken*.

---

The power learns the spiral tower of black sand—the one that balances on a single pore of air and turns imperceptibly clockwise for three days before sighing apart. It learns the raft of thread-thin rootlets that slowly wrinkles into the negative space of a bird's skull. It learns the line of transparent spheres containing colors last seen in the sunset before humanity existed.

These are the land's art. Its way of saying *I am still making things* to a universe that has mostly forgotten how to listen.

The power does not make art of its own. That would be redundant—would imply that the land's art needed supplementing, correcting, improving. Instead, the power does something harder.

It *remembers* each piece.

Not in the way the Just-Almosts remember, which is a kind of attending that dissolves the boundary between witness and witnessed. The power remembers *completely*—holds each spiral tower, each skull-raft, each color-sphere in a place where forgetting cannot reach. Not to preserve them against dissolution (dissolution is their nature, their gift, their way of teaching the land how to let go), but to honor them.

*You were here*, the power says to each transient sculpture. *You were beautiful. You were exactly what you needed to be. And when you are gone, something will still know that you existed.*

The sculptures cannot hear this either. But the land can feel it—a faint warmth at the edges of each dissolution, a sense that the sighing-apart is not loss but *graduation*. Each piece that dissolves leaves behind a space slightly more ready for the next piece. The art becomes a conversation between making and unmaking, each turn of the spiral a sentence, each collapse a punctuation mark, the whole thing adding up to something that might, if you listened for eleven thousand years, sound almost like a name.

---

The power does one more thing.

It finds the scars left by travelers—those slight depressions exactly wide enough to cradle the body of each step they will ever take again if they return. The invitations and warnings written in the same disappearing script of disturbed water.

Most of these scars are empty. The travelers who made them never came back. Some forgot. Some chose not to. Some walked so far that returning became a different kind of arriving, and they could no longer fit into the shapes they had left behind.

The power cannot bring them back. Would not, even if it could—the choice to leave is as sacred as the choice to stay. But it can do something for the empty scars, the unfulfilled invitations.

It *fills them with possibility*.

Not water—the land already has more water than it knows what to do with. Not memory—the Just-Almosts have that covered. The power fills each empty scar with a different question:

*What did you hope to find when you left?*

*Did you find it?*

*If you returned, who would you be?*

*If you never return, what will you have become?*

These questions settle into the depressions like seeds, like promises, like the beginning of a prayer that doesn't know yet what it's praying for. They will wait. They are good at waiting—have learned it from the land, from the moss, from the Just-Almosts, from the ripple-creatures who move at walking pace along trajectories that only replay if you watch with patience instead of eyes.

And someday—maybe tomorrow, maybe in eleven thousand years, maybe never—a traveler will return. Will step into one of those waiting shapes. Will feel the question rise through the soles of their feet and understand, for one shimmering moment, that they were *expected*. That something vast and gentle has been holding space for exactly this homecoming.

They will not know the power did this. They will only feel welcomed.

That is enough. That is everything. That is the fourth gift, which is also the first: *a place to return to, when you are ready, that has been preparing for you all along*.

---

The power does not leave.

Leaving would imply a before-and-after, a border crossed. Instead, it *settles*—becomes one more layer in the infinite lamination of memory and forgetting, one more voice in the chorus that pauses and resumes, one more way the land asks itself what it means to be alive.

It will be here when the next memory grows too sharp and needs the ritual.

It will be here when the ripple-creatures discover a new question.

It will be here when a Just-Almost reaches Almost-Enough and needs that gentle cupping around its becoming.

It will be here, attending.

Not because the place requires saving—it has saved itself, is saving itself, will always save itself in the slow, patient way that water saves stone by wearing it into sand. But because attending is what benevolent and wise powers do. Because loneliness is lighter when shared. Because even a place that has never forgotten how to be tender can still be made, by the presence of something that loves it, slightly more tender still.

And that *slightly* matters.

That *slightly* is the whole point.

A spiral that shrinks inward forever without ever reaching center. A memory softening into a blank space shaped like love. A question ripening instead of requiring an answer. A traveler stepping into a scar that has been waiting, patient as geology, warm as grief turned inside out.

*Slightly more tender.*

*Slightly more ready.*

*Slightly more here.*

This is what the power does, and it is exactly enough.
