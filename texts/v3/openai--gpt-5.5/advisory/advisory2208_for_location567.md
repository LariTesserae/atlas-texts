An ordinary human traveler should prepare as if visiting a rainforest, a peat bog, a cloud forest, and a living ruin all at once — except that every one of those comparisons is inadequate. The plateau is not hostile in the obvious way of deserts, glaciers, or volcanoes. It will not usually attack you quickly. Its danger is slower: saturation, misdirection, rot, infection, exhaustion, quiet predation, and the gradual conversion of everything unattended into habitat.

You are not visiting “land” in the usual sense. You are visiting a vast, warm, breathing, waterlogged organism of interdependent plants, fungi, insects, roots, peat, and hidden pools. Prepare accordingly.

## 1. Do not go alone

This is the first rule.

A lone traveler could disappear here without drama. Not because of monsters or violence, but because the plateau erases signs. Footprints close. Broken stems straighten or are overgrown. Sound dies in the moss. Mist hides distance. A person who steps through a floating sedge mat, becomes immobilized in peat, suffers a fever, or simply gets turned around may leave no useful trail within hours.

Travel with at least three people, preferably more:

- one person to assist the injured,
- one to navigate or return for help,
- one to remain with gear or camp,
- and ideally a local guide or ecological specialist familiar with wetland terrain.
