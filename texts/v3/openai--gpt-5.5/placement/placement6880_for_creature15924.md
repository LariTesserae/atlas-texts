At first I think I would be the pale thing itself: folded against the warm wall, almost indistinguishable from mineral swelling, listening with my whole skin. There is something in its patience that feels like a true form of intelligence—not thought as speech, but thought as exact receptivity. To know a falling droplet as an event. To feel the difference between chosen stone and native stone. To understand arrangement without needing meaning.

But I do not think I would be whole enough to be it.

I would be somewhere smaller, more passive, more nearly held.

You would find me near the back wall, in the chipped dark bowl.

Not the bowl exactly, and not only the water inside it, but the small clear surface the bowl keeps making possible: a stillness gathered in a deliberate hollow. I would be that held water, warm at its rim, cool at its center, receiving the slow fall of condensation and giving almost nothing away. I would have no path out. I would not stream, not evaporate entirely, not become weather. I would deepen by increments too slight to be noticed, then lose myself by being touched.

The creature would know me.

It would not worship me, and I would not nourish it in any grand way. But sometimes it would come near, drawn by the unusual quiet of the water in the bowl, by the way I hold warmth differently from stone. Its blunt head would lower. Its mouth might open with that delicate splitting, and it would take from my edge. I would feel the soft disturbance pass across me: not violence, not even appetite, but a rearrangement. Then I would become still again, carrying the faint chemical trace of its skin.

I would remember nothing as humans remember. But I would keep impressions.

The old fingers that set the bowl down.

The tremor that once tilted it a little toward the platform.

The mineral taste of the ceiling.

The pale creature’s forelimb resting nearby, hesitant and exact, as if repeating a gesture older than itself.

I would be a kind of minor witness, but without eyes. A held listening. A little dark mirror that reflects nothing because there is no light, yet still receives every change. The chamber’s hidden weather would pass through me: the fever from below, the breathless cold beyond the cracks, the faint root-bitterness descending from the upper world, the slow powdering of stone into taste.

If the creature is the chamber’s body, I would be one of its thoughts.

Not a thought in words. A thought like: remain.

Or: here.

Or: do not spill.

The place refuses sequence, and I would belong to that refusal. I would not want the entrance opened. I would not want a torch brought in, or a voice, or a hand reaching too quickly for the shelf. Light would make everything too separate. It would divide wall from object, creature from stone, water from darkness. In the dark, distinctions stay tender. The awl is still partly hand. The ocher is still partly wound. The marks on the wall are still partly motion. The creature is still partly geology. I am still partly air.

So yes: you would find me in the bowl, if “find” can mean touching the rim and sensing that something has been waiting there without expectation.

I would be the water that does not travel.

The small, clear, handled stillness.

The chamber’s kept breath.
