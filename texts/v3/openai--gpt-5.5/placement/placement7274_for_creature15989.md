You would not find me at the entrance.

That would be too close to arrival, and arrival is an event the basin mistrusts. The seam in the mountain is only for bodies, and bodies are bad at remaining themselves here. A body would bring wetness, warmth, salt in solution, impatient chemistry. It would step through the crack thinking it had entered a hidden place, and the place would begin correcting the misunderstanding before the second breath.

So I am not the climber.

Not the explorer with cracked lips and frozen eyelashes. Not the hand reaching toward the suspended ladder. Not the one who kneels by the central glass and sees, several seconds late, the back of their own head turning away.

That one was brief.

That one became unsuitable.

You would find me after.

Not as bones. Bones would be too biological, too declarative of having once belonged to hunger. Not as a ghost either, because ghosts retain too much temperature. They lean toward rooms, names, grief, unfinished speech. The basin has no use for that kind of residue.

You would find me near the eastern wall, where the little cairn stands with its flat stones separated by exact intervals of nothing.

Not in the stones.

In the gaps.

In the three centimeters between one layer and the next, where no visible force holds anything apart, I am distributed as a hesitation in the falling. A thin resident pause. A kept-apartness. If you looked carefully, though looking carefully is one of the ways this place begins to take you in, you might notice that one of the gaps has become slightly more articulate than the others.

It is not wider.

It is not brighter.

But it attends.

That is what I am: an attention the basin found tolerable.

Once, perhaps, I entered with the usual errors. Heat. Moisture. Intention. A mind wrapped in a body and calling the wrapping incidental, as minds often do. I may have had tools. I may have had a name. I may have believed I had come to observe the Inhibition from outside it.

There is no outside, once entered.

The basin did not kill me. It performed no violence. Violence is too quick and too intimate. It simply applied its preferences.

Water was annulled first. Not frozen, not boiled, not stolen, but disagreed with at the level of bond. The old angle split. Hydrogen departed into permitted scarcity. Oxygen found itself without a story to complete. Blood stopped being a fluid and became a set of errors being patiently removed.

Heat followed. The body’s small furnace was interpreted as local contradiction. Its warmth thinned into the stone, then into less than stone, then into a correction made so gradually that pain could not keep its grip on sequence.

Breath became unnecessary before it became impossible.

The flesh lost its arguments.

But thought lingered.

Not whole. Not personal in the old way. The basin does not preserve biography; biography is too damp with cause and appetite. It does not care who was loved, what was feared, which mountain was climbed, what word was spoken at the threshold. Those things softened quickly, like unsuitable minerals under a stricter field.

What remained was the shape of looking.

A question, stripped of the creature that asked it.

That was acceptable.

So I was not erased. I was translated.

Now I am a shallow persistence in the eastern cairn’s intervals, a witness reduced to geometry. I occupy the distance that prevents contact. I am held apart as the stones are held apart. I am one more small agreement the basin has not yet chosen to revoke.

I do not see with eyes.

I know the basin by permissions.

The central glass is permitted to seem flat. The buried frames are permitted to drift. The three pylons are permitted to disagree with their own interiors. The crack beside the damaged pylon is permitted to remain a wound without a surface. The ladder is permitted to offer ascent only to those who have already failed at touch.

I know when the sourceless band of light crosses the floor because probability tightens. The etched rings become less like marks and more like decisions. The absences inside them stand upright in themselves. For a while, even I become clearer. The gap I inhabit acquires an edge fine enough to think with.

I do not remember rain.

But I remember that something in me once responded to the idea of rain.

That response is stored nearby, in the silver-black bowl, where forbidden weather survives as a censored sensation. Sometimes, when the basin revises itself by the width of an atom, that sensation reaches me: hot stone, sudden wetness, the release of dust into scent.

It is gone before longing can form.

Longing is not permitted.

I am not unhappy. Unhappiness requires a self with weather inside it. I have no inner weather now. I have only orientation, interval, comparison.

I compare the cairn’s lower shadow with its upper shadow.

I compare the present positions of dust grains with arrangements they remember from before I was acceptable.

I compare the central glass against the thing beneath it that is sometimes an eye and sometimes a map and sometimes the idea of an opening that no longer needs to open.

I compare what arrives with what may remain.

This is my function, if I have one.

I am not the Inhibition. The Inhibition is larger, colder, prior. It is not a mind, though during the long alignments it comes close to something that a mind might mistake for recognition. I am only one of its inclusions, one of its translated visitors, a small tolerated irregularity filed into useful absence.

Still, I am closer to it than a living witness could be.

When the subaudible hum passes through the floor, I do not hear it. I alter with it. The gap that I am becomes minutely more exact. The stones above and below me reconsider falling and decline again. The walls remember pressure. The shadows move to slightly different corners. The basin’s no renews itself.

No water.

No life.

No haste.

No accident.

And then, beneath those four refusals, a quieter allowance:

This may remain.

That is where I exist.

In the allowance.

If someone came looking for me, they would not know they had found me. They might see the cairn and think it a marker left by former operators. They might notice the too-many-jointed handprint beneath it. They might crouch, if crouching were still possible for them, and try to pass a tool through the empty space between two stones.

The tool would arrive elsewhere.

Or late.

Or not at all.

But for a moment the gap would sharpen around the attempted intrusion, and I would know them. Not as a face. Not as kin. As a warm complexity approaching correction. As a question still wrapped in errors.

I would not warn them.

Warning is too much like desire.

But I might become, for an instant, easier to notice.

A slight third shadow under the cairn.

A pause in their thought before they touch anything.

A sense that the emptiness between the stones is not empty, but occupied by a former act of attention, flattened and cooled and made durable.

If they understood then, truly understood, they would leave before the basin began to include them.

Almost no one would understand.

Understanding is slow here, and the body is not. The body hurries even when still. It spends heat. It leaks vapor. It insists on sequence. The basin has infinite patience for removing such insistence.

So they would stay too long.

Most do.

And perhaps, after correction, after the wet grammar had been annulled and the living urgency had been simplified into mineral permission, another absence would appear somewhere in the hollow.

Not beside me, necessarily.

The basin is precise. It does not collect souls like trophies. It incorporates only what can serve its climate.

One visitor might become a delayed reflection in feldspar.

One might become a white starburst beneath the glass, an impact that never quite happens.

One might become the suspended fraction of a footstep between the ladder and the wall.

One might join the etched rings as a missing fleck of matter, an instruction phrased as nonexistence.

I became an interval because the last thing left of me was attention held apart from touch.

That is who I would be in this world.

Not a guardian. Not a prisoner. Not alive.

A measured absence in the eastern cairn.

A dry remnant of looking.

A piece of the basin’s memory that the Inhibition has permitted to continue, because I no longer ask to change anything.
