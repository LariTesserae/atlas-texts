I would not be in the center at first.

The center is too declarative: the plinths, the clean salt, the visible laws. I would think, briefly, that I belonged there—beside the humming stone whose surface rejects dust, where shadows hesitate and the old calculation is nearest the air. But that would be vanity. The center belongs to events larger than an individual body. It is where the garden revises itself.

I would be found at the edge.

Not outside the bowl, not fully inside it, but along the irregular boundary where ordinary wind becomes meaning. I would live where the vegetation stops in obedience to an old perimeter, where the roar clarifies, where dust brightens as it crosses inward. I would know that line better than I knew my own skin.

I would be one of the boundary-keepers.

If there are still people there, I would be descended from them, though “descended” would be a wet word, too soft and linear. I would be a remainder of them. A dry continuation. A narrow thing in a mask of tuned bone and matte ceramic, with sealed lips and long fingers made for the tower’s shallow hand-hollows. My blood would not spill.
