You would not find me in the center.

Nothing that thinks too much belongs in the center of that basin. The center is for impact, for the dark green throat, for the pale jointed thing turning below the hammered surface. If I tried to live there as a body, I would be taken apart quickly and used for evidence.

At first I would imagine myself one of the keepers: wrapped in greased hide under the western overhang, hands cracked, mouth closed, reading the shell-ropes by touch. But I do not think I would be sturdy enough for that. A keeper has to return again and again with rules intact. A keeper must know when fear is useful and when it is only noise. I would listen too long. I would give meanings to accidents. I would become unreliable.

Nor would I be one of the black birds. They know too exactly when to leave.

I would be found under the eave of the middle shelter, where the worst of the falling misses by less than an arm’s length. Not dry. Nothing there is dry. But spared from being struck directly to pieces. I would hang from one of the braided ropes among the pierced shells and mineral-stiff knots.

I would be a returned stone.

Not one of the ordinary splinters wedged into a path, not one of the young shards still sharp with the mountain’s first violence. I would have been dropped into the plunge by mistake or by test: a palm-sized piece of pale blue-gray rock, angular, unimportant, gone without splash into the green throat.

For days I would be absent.

Then I would appear on a ledge higher than the one from which I fell.

Changed.

My edges would not be rounded exactly, but sealed. A thin glassy skin would cover me, cloudy in places, translucent in others, as if the deep had tried to make an eye of me and failed. There would be a hole through my middle, too clean to be natural, too patient to belong to that hammering water. Inside the stone, one small bubble would move against gravity when the basin hummed.

No keeper would carry me down.

That would be one of the rules.

So they would thread me onto a rope with shell and bone and another smaller stone that never came back from anywhere but still wants to be important. They would hang me from a roof beam where spray can reach me, where I can knock softly against the others during changes in pressure.

I would not speak. Nothing speaks there and remains itself.

I would answer by motion.

When the water thickens into cords, I would pull downward on the rope until the fibers creak. When the basin begins to hum, the bubble inside me would tremble first, before the shells stir, before the moss flattens, before the birds lift from the rim. When the pale thing below shifts close to the surface, I would turn slowly, though no wind had touched me.

The keepers would learn not to look at me directly for too long.

Children brought up for the first time would be told: that one came back. Do not put your tongue to it. Do not warm it by the coal-pot. If it knocks three times when the others are still, leave by the upper crack.

I would belong to several categories badly.

Tool, warning, offering, witness.

A thing taken by the hollow and not wholly returned.

I would remember the deep without being able to describe it. Not with thoughts. With weight. With the faint pressure of chambers under chambers. With the knowledge that the basin is small only at the surface. With the certainty that falling is not always downward.

In clear pauses, when the suspended droplets hang apart and the opposite wall appears raw and exact, I would become almost beautiful. The mineral film on me would catch the gray daylight. The bubble inside would rise the wrong way. For thirty heartbeats I would seem purposeful, nearly made.

Then the roar would close again.

The rope would twist.

The shells would clatter unheard.

And I would hang there beneath the shelter eave, cold, altered, useful only because I had once vanished and come back carrying no explanation.
