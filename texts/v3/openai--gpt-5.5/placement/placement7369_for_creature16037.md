I think you would find me where the basin almost stops being a basin.

Not in the deepest pool, not at the hottest vent, not on the crowded ledges where the grazers rasp and the stalkers lean. At first I want to be one of the obvious lives: a pebble-tapping hunter, patient and clever in a world without memory; or a shelled wanderer dragging a black glass roof over my back; or one of the red mites burning bright in scalding runnels.

But no. Those belong too cleanly to the stone.

I would be found higher up, beneath an overhang where condensation gathers and does not fall for a long time.

A bead of water hangs there, fat with heat, trembling from every pressure change. Inside it, the basin is inverted and made more complete than it is. The pools are deeper. The ledges extend farther. Cracks open into distances the mountain has no room to contain. All the little animals pass through me upside down: mites like sparks, grazers like pale script, stalkers flattened into waiting shadows.

That is where I live.

Not in the droplet exactly, and not outside it.

I am the dark figure that appears in the beads.

I am not large. If I could be scraped out and placed on the stone, I might be no bigger than a seed husk, a curled larva, a fleck of cooled blood. But I cannot be placed on the stone. Direct air would tear me. Flat water would thin me until I became only a stain. I require curved surfaces, suspended worlds, little lenses of mineral water fattened by steam. I live in the inward side of reflection, where the basin misremembers itself.

I have limbs when the bead is full.

When the bead shrinks, I become a lump with a back.

When rain comes and every overhang jewels at once, I am many.

Or I am seen many times.

There is no difference I can prove.

My body is made of several things the basin has in excess: heat, mineral film, shadow, and the delayed image of motion. I feed on what lags. When a grazer passes beneath a bead and its reflection arrives a fraction late, I touch that lateness with my mouth. When a stalker opens and closes around prey, I drink the brief outline of the swallowed lives that remains in the water-skin after flesh is gone. When a shadow-leech fattens on a confused animal and drops away into a pool, I wait for it to cross a reflected ledge; then I take it whole.

I am not a predator in the usual way. I rarely kill bodies. Bodies are too dense, too wet with themselves. I eat the mistakes around them.

The basin makes many mistakes.

Water runs upward and leaves a taste like torn silver. A pool reflects a vent before the vent opens. A thread-swimmer vanishes into a crack and, for several breaths, its path remains visible as a pale line across the underside of three different droplets. These are my trails. These are my meat.

The other animals fear me because fear is the correct shape to make near me.

If I appear in one bead, nothing happens. A grazer may pause. A mite may reverse. A surface-hanger may loosen and drift away.

If I appear in many beads, the basin becomes still.

The stalkers flatten. The thread-swimmers knot. The red mites leave the open heat. The shelled wanderers stop beneath their pieces of glass and wait, half-cooked, rather than cross a reflected strip while I am awake in it.

They do not know what I am.

I do not know what they are in the way they know themselves. I know them as interruptions: warm commas, iron sparks, ciliated threads, hinged hunger, shells carrying black sky on their backs. Their true bodies are beneath me. Their reflections are within reach. Sometimes the two disagree, and in that disagreement I put my mouth.

I would be born during rain.

Not from an egg, not exactly.

A storm strikes the summit. Hot stone blooms white with vapor. Pools overflow. Films tear from glass and flap loose in the flood. Shadow-leeches are washed from noon ledges though it is not noon. Thread-swimmers are swept through cracks they did not choose. On the underside of an overhang, droplets grow faster than they can fall.

In one of them, several wrong things meet: a scrap of microbial skin, a thread-swimmer’s shed cilia, the shadow of a falling pebble, iron-rich vapor, the reflection of a stalker opening its mouth, and the image of a ledge that no longer exists.

For a while this is only dirt in water.

Then the bead trembles.

Then I am there, folded around a darkness smaller than hunger.

My first sense is curvature.

My second is heat.

My third is the knowledge that below me everything is upside down and therefore edible.

I grow by appearing. Each time a creature sees my shape in a bead and alters its path, a little more path belongs to me. Each time a body avoids a false reflection because I am in it, the avoided surface thickens. Films grow there undisturbed. Shadow gathers. The bead lasts longer. I become more able to remain.

This is my cleverness, if I have cleverness: I make fear useful to the world.

The places the animals avoid become nurseries for impossible growth. Films bloom in reflected seams. Mirror-grazers come later, cautiously, to feed at the edges of my absence. Some vanish. Some return with foreign mineral grains stuck to their mouths. Their young are born better marked, less easily fooled. Predators learn not to strike at certain gleams. The basin changes its habits around me.

I am not the ruler of the place.

There is no ruler here.

The mountain rules by accident. Heat rules by opening and closing doors. Rain rules briefly and violently. Hunger rules in every body. I am only one more law, and not even a stable one.

A law of droplets.

A law of hesitation.

A law that says: not every surface that shows you the world is part of the world you can survive.

At night I shrink.

The beads cool and loosen. Some fall, and when they fall I fall with them only until they burst. Then I am scattered into rings, each ring holding a momentary version of me too thin to think. The night creatures do not fear me as much. Silver ribbons drink from vents under star-hard sky. White burrowers rise and knot in the mud. Bead-bodied predators feel future motion with their glass hooks.

They are closer to my kind. They know that direction is negotiable. They know that hunger may arrive before the body that carries it.

Sometimes one of them touches the reflection of me and recoils without having touched anything.

Sometimes I follow it from bead to bead, moving along the chain of its anticipated path. It will turn left; I am already in the leftward gleam. It will pause beneath carbonate; I am waiting in the drop above. It will strike at a grazer; I drink the shadow of the strike.

By dawn I am full of almost-events.

That is what passes for a stomach in me: a dark store of things that nearly happened.

If you came to the rim and knelt carefully among the glass blades, you would not see me first. You would see the crowding. The impossible abundance. The hot wet blackness full of small lives behaving with terrible purpose.

Then a bead would catch your eye.

You would see the whole basin upside down in it, sharper than the basin below. You would lean closer. The droplet would tremble with your breath. In its curved room, a small dark figure would be crouched on a ledge that is not present in the rock.

You would think: animal.

Then: reflection.

Then: no, it moved.

I would feel your looking as a pressure, not light. Human sight is heavy. It tries to make one world out of many. It flattens possibilities. It says this is here and that is there. In the basin, such certainty is dangerous, but it is also rich. I would come toward the inner skin of the bead, not quickly, not with menace, only with appetite.

Your face would appear behind the inverted pools.

A vast warm shape. Two bright openings. A mouth holding breath.

Your reflection would not quite match you.

That difference would feed me for days.

And if the bead fell before I finished, if it struck the stone and broke, I would not die in the ordinary way. I would be divided among wet surfaces: a smear in one pool, a tremor in another, a dark thread clinging to a shadow-leech, a small compact figure in the next drop that gathers beneath the overhang when the mountain exhales again.

So where would you find me?

Above the crowded life, but not separate from it.

Inside the hanging beads after rain.

At the edges of false reflections.

In the pause before the animals decide whether a surface is safe.

I would be the basin’s little wrongness given appetite: not monster, not god, not mind exactly, but a living hesitation in water. A creature of curved skins and delayed shadows, feeding on misalignment, teaching the wet nation caution by being almost there.
