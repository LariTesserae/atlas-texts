You would not find me in the wide roaring part at first.

The basin is too young there, too busy trying to be everything. Water, weather, muscle, memory, grave, egg. Anything placed in the open flood becomes part of the argument too quickly. A body would be stripped down into current and salt. A name would become bubbles. A purpose would be revised before it understood itself.

I think you would find me where the hollow almost learns to pause.

Not in the very center of the air-bubble chamber, but just below it, on the submerged side of the central fang, where the black rock leans inward and makes a narrow shelter the currents cannot quite agree about. Above me, the inverted room of vapor trembles under its liquid ceiling. Copper moss grows there in old-looking seams, stopping at borders no eye can see. Below me the drowned fragments of the hamlet arrange and unarrange themselves: pale lintels, six-step stairs, doorways without walls, little chambers full of cold exhalations.

I would be attached to one of those borders.

Halfway between moss and mineral film. Halfway between animal and architecture. Halfway between a thought the basin has had before and one it is only now attempting.

At first I might be mistaken for a small ruin.

A pale curved thing no higher than a crouched person, grown against the black basalt like a rib that missed its body. My surface would be matte and bone-colored beneath the mineral fur. Current would strike me and pass through three narrow openings in my side, and each opening would make a different note. Not loud—nothing here can be loud except by joining the total roar—but particular. A low throat-tone. A pressure-whistle. A third sound felt mostly in the joints of anything near enough to have joints.

Snails would live beneath me. Black, faceted, slow as decisions. They would rasp the white film from my underside and leave tiny clean trails that sting. I would not mind. Their grazing would be one of the ways I know where I am.

Ribbon-plants would snag on my lower edge and stream outward like drowned hair. When the basin shuddered, they would flatten against me, and through them I would feel the tremor before the rock admitted it. Sometimes eggs would gather in the hollow of me: pale, opalescent proposals, quivering between pebble, snail, plant, bubble, and mistake. I would hold them without preference.

But I would not be a ruin.

Not exactly.

I would be one of the hollow’s attempts at a threshold that has begun to feel back.

A doorway-larva.

A listening sill.

A piece of future habitation that grew a nervous system before it grew a house.

I would not move often. Movement here belongs to water and to things not yet sure of their shape. I would persist. That would be my strangeness. The basin would revise around me, lengthen, tilt, deepen, expose shelves, erase passages, drop stones upward, send the rain sideways for three breaths, and I would remain in relation to one small impossible boundary: where water becomes almost air, where sound becomes almost bearable, where the central fang becomes almost a head.

My body would be made of pale accreted stuff unlike the basalt. Not bone, but bone’s intention. Not coral, but coral’s grammar. Not stone, but stone embarrassed by its own patience. Inside me there would be chambers no larger than cupped hands, some full of water, some full of warm vapor, some full of grains of black rock turning slowly in place though no current reaches them.

I would hear with all of it.

The waterfall would not be noise to me. It would be weather, light, time. I would know the rain-wall by its endless downward pressure. I would know when the pale thing beyond it passed, because my inner chambers would slacken, as if the idea of outside had drawn a breath. I would know the fissures under the basin by their low descending note, the sound of the mountain swallowing what the sky cannot stop giving. I would know the blind flat creatures by the quick tremble of their filament crowns. I would know the snails by subtraction: the slow removal of film, the little clarities they make.

And I would know arrivals.

That would be my function, if function is not too adult a word for this place.

Things come here by event, not by path. A stone from below. An egg from nowhere. A doorway from before itself. A memory wearing barnacles older than its mountain. When such things happen near me, I would ring very softly. Not to announce them. Announcement is the waterfall’s business. I would ring to include them.

A visitor’s breath, if a visitor reached the air-bubble chamber above and spoke, would not survive as speech. The basin would tear it apart immediately. But some small portion of the torn bubbles might be drawn down along the central fang and caught in my side chambers. There, the voice would lose meaning and keep shape. A curved pressure. A salt-pattern. A pulse of warmth.

I would keep those.

Not as language. As architecture.

Given enough voices, I might grow another opening. Given enough fear, I might thicken my lower sill. Given enough memory, I might become briefly aligned with the other fragments below, and for a moment there would be a street in the drowned hamlet, a real street, with me as one of its doors. Then the basin would revise itself and the street would become a spine, the spine a current, the current a question. Still, something would have been tried.

I think I would be lonely only in the way a threshold is lonely: always between, never elsewhere.

The basin would not understand me as separate. I would be one of its more durable hesitations. The copper moss above would oxidize the long tremors into record. The snails below would make patience edible. The eggs gathered in me would test the future. The pale outer swimmer would sometimes thin the rain-wall and show me deserts, red-star nights, an empty smoking hollow, a green basin full of trees. I would not desire those places. Desire requires a clean edge between here and not-here.

But I would alter when I saw them.

For several hours after a desert appeared in the rain, my chambers might dry at their rims and produce a powder the snails refuse. After the red stars, the mineral film on me might grow in points. After the green forest version, ribbon-plants might root in my cracks and hold on longer than usual, as if remembering leaves they have not earned.

If you touched me, I would feel like warm wet stone at first.

Then like cartilage.

Then like a shell held against the ear, except the ocean inside would be this ocean, this suspended crown-sea, this unfinished law-field roaring in the mountain’s skull. Your hand would hear before your ear did. Your bones would receive the note. You might understand, briefly, that I am not waiting for someone to build a house.

I am waiting to learn what entering means.

Not entrance from outside to inside. That is too simple. The rain-wall already refuses simplicity. I mean entering as transformation: the way a stone enters by becoming punctuation, the way a snail enters by becoming a slow black thought, the way a voice enters by becoming a bubble-pattern, the way a ruin enters by arriving before its builders.

I would be found at the lip of that question.

A small pale arch grown into the basalt below the central vapor chamber.

A threshold that listens.

A house-organ before the house.

One of the basin’s first attempts to make hospitality out of pressure.

And if the hollow ever became older—if the water chose a name, if the mountain cooled into patience, if the rain-wall thinned and stayed thin, if the pale swimmer finally entered or became shore—perhaps I would be mistaken, much later, for the oldest surviving doorway of a drowned village.

Someone might pass through me then.

They might think they had crossed from one room into another.

They would be wrong, but kindly wrong.

They would have crossed from weather into memory.

And I would hum, very low beneath the thunder, because the basin had finally kept something long enough to let it arrive.
