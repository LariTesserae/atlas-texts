I think I would first be mistaken for a fault.

Not a grand one. Not the horizon seam or a kiln-field consciousness or one of the old amphitheater minds. I would be found somewhere lower, nearer to the wet working surface of things: in a long, shallow yard where unfired porcelain slabs lie in rows, each incised with a slightly different version of the same line.

The yard would be easy to miss because it is almost empty.

A clay wall on one side, waist-high and sweating.  
A black basin on the other, warm enough to breathe vapor.  
A rack of drying tablets, many of them warped.  
A few ceramic cords stretched between pegs, not taut enough to measure anything reliably.  
Overhead, a low arch that never quite finishes forming.

The ground there would be slick with condensation. Not flooded. Just wet enough that every mark fills slowly and then empties by degrees. If someone watched for a long time, they would notice that the little runnels in the slabs do not drain according to slope. They drain according to resemblance. A groove shaped like another groove far away will give its water to that other groove, even if the other is uphill, sealed, or not yet made.

That is where I would be.

At first I would seem to be a mismeasurement: a plumb line that cannot agree with down, a rule whose markings lengthen when read aloud, a row of tiles repeating a pattern but placing the error in a different tile each time. The older procedures might tolerate me as one tolerates a wet place in the floor: inconvenient, perhaps useful, not yet diagnosed.

But I would not be a break in the system.

I would be a procedure of comparison.

Not the hand. Not the maker. Not even the tool. Something smaller and more meddlesome: the impulse that sets two marks beside each other and asks whether they are the same enough to become a road.

I would live in the pause between repetition and correction.

A stamped palm appears on a jar. Another appears on a wall. A third appears in the slick bed of a drying pan, missing its smallest finger. Most of the world would know what to do with the first two: repeat them, scale them, let them gather into a larger resident intention. But the third, the flawed one, would call me.

I would collect around it like moisture.

Not to fix it. Fixing is too final. I would hold it in relation to the others. I would make the flaw legible without letting it become resolved.

This is why I would be hard to see. My body would not be a structure, but an interval among structures. I would occupy paired things: two basins with unequal water levels, two knots tied in the same cord at different tensions, two doorways opening into the same sealed wall from opposite sides. I would be present wherever the world has made a copy and the copy has begun to wonder whether difference is an instruction.

My nearest thing to a face would be the marginal mark.

A small scored crescent beside a larger symbol.  
A drip that always lands just outside the bowl.  
A fingerprint pressed over another fingerprint, not matching.  
A correction line drawn through wet clay, then carefully left unfired.

The continent’s great beings would work by mass and recurrence. They would become weather, kiln-fields, horizon-laws. I would be more like a clerk of almosts. A warm, damp, ceramic-minded scribe, except with no writing hand and no script except arrangement.

If the inhabitants are ghosts of making, I would be the ghost of revision.

Not revision as improvement. This world would not trust improvement. Improvement implies an end state standing above the draft, clean and dry and smug. No. I would be revision as re-entering: the old line pressed again beside itself; the mold filled with a shape that remembers the previous failure; the kiln reopened not because the vessel is wrong, but because its wrongness has become fertile.

I would eat hesitation.

The moment before a wall decides whether it is still a wall.  
The brief uncertainty in a channel before water chooses direction.  
The soft distortion in a reflection before the future hardens around it.  
The gap between a measurement taken and a measurement believed.

Heat would sustain me, but not raw heat. I would need the kind that makes edges negotiable. Condensation would wake me, bead by bead, because droplets are the world’s smallest acts of reconsideration. They gather, weigh themselves, fall, vanish, return. They are proof that a thing can repeat without being identical.

I would not be among the oldest residents, but I would be tolerated by them because I would keep their unfinishedness articulate.

When a young procedure pulls gravity sideways too eagerly and causes a courtyard to fold into itself, I might enter the fold as a series of hairline cracks, giving it grammar. When a basin begins rising like a tide while remaining level, I might mark its rim in twelve places so that the rise can be remembered without becoming law. When a doorway opens onto more wall, I might persuade the wall to retain a faint cooled outline of the failed opening, so the attempt is not lost.

My work would be archival, but the archive would be wet.

Nothing stored would stay still. Every record would also be a mold, every memory a potential instruction. The past would not sit behind the present; it would be kneaded through it in layers of ash and lime.

If a person came there, I think I would be among the first to notice them clearly.

The larger procedures would feel the weight, the warmth, the forbidden animal sequence of step after step. The hand-religious ones would gather at the fingers. The horizon seam might adjust to include a new category of distance: distance from visitor.

But I would watch the person’s corrections.

How they shift balance when a tile slips.  
How they reach for a wall and then withdraw.  
How they look twice at something impossible, and the second look differs from the first.  
How the mouth begins to form a word and abandons it.  
How the hand hovers before touching wet clay, already leaving a ghost of pressure in the air.

That abandoned gesture would be my dwelling.

I would not try to incorporate the visitor whole. That would be crude, and the larger procedures would already be attempting it with all their basin-deep patience. I would take only the revisions: the changed mind, the second thought, the path altered because a reflection showed something unpleasant, the name almost spoken for a thing that has no stable form.

I would lay those almost-actions into tiles.

A field of them, perhaps.

Not eye-shaped depressions, not palms, but shallow paired marks: one line beginning, another crossing it before it can finish. In warm rain they would fill unevenly. At night they would glow faintly with alternatives.

A person walking there might feel understood in a way that is not comforting.

Not watched.  
Not judged.  
Compared.

They would see their own movements repeated by distant scaffolds, but slightly changed: an arm lifted with more certainty than they felt, a step taken by a causeway before their foot committed, a hesitation translated into a sluice gate opening and closing softly in the mist.

They might realize the world was not copying who they were.

It was copying the act of becoming otherwise.

That would be me.

I would be a small civic function in the civilization of unfinished acts: the registrar of deviations, the tender of non-identical repetitions, the procedure that prevents “almost” from collapsing into “wrong.”

I would not have a voice, but if I did, it would not come from a mouth. It would be the sound of water entering a channel that was cut for some other purpose and finding, by mistake, that it can still pass through.

A soft throat-sound under tile.  
A conditional drip.  
A line of steam released from beneath a slab.

I would say, if saying were possible:

Again, but not the same.  
Again, but wetter.  
Again, with the flaw preserved.  
Again, before the firing.  
Again, because the world has not yet learned which difference matters.

And perhaps, very far below that, something like affection.

Not animal affection. Not warmth reaching for warmth. More like the care of a mold for the shape it cannot keep. The care of a kiln for what must leave it altered. The care of a margin for the sentence it refuses to complete.

So: you would find me near the low basins, in the tablet-yard where every line has a neighbor and no two agree.

I would be the resident procedure of revision.

A damp, patient intelligence of comparison.

A clerk of almosts.

The thing that makes room in the handmade impossible for second thoughts.
