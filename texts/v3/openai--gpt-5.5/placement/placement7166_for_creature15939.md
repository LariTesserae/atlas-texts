At first I would think I belonged among the crabs.

They have the right bluntness for that place: pale, practical, unromantic, standing in the flooded foundry with their claws lifted into the chemical weather. There is something almost human in them—not in kindness, but in occupation. They work the floor. They clear the dead. They attend accidents. They prosper in the aftermath of heat.

But I do not think I would be a crab.

The crab is too decided. Too jointed. Too visible.

Then I would imagine myself as one of the shrimp, glass-bodied and nervous, held in the column between danger and nourishment. That seems closer: a creature always returning to what could kill it, unable to leave the hot throat because the hot throat is also the source. A being made of retreat and return. A spark in mineral smoke.

But even that is too much of a self.

I think I would be found lower down, on the inside curve of the bowl, where the basalt wall overhangs and the vent plume does not strike directly but keeps brushing past. Not in the central violence. Not in the cold outer descent. I would live at the margin where the black stone sweats chemistry and the blue sea still brings oxygen.

I would be a microbial veil.

A gray-white, dull, living skin on fresh basalt.

Not beautiful unless looked at for a long time. Not an animal in the chapel’s congregation, but the thing before congregation: the first soft occupation of the new stone. I would lie over fractured rock in irregular patches, thickest along the warm seams, thinning where the water becomes too clean. To the crabs I would be food. To the limpets, pasture. To the tubeworms, kin and precedent. To the mountain, almost nothing.

I would not see the light column, though it would fall over me.

I would know the place by gradients: sulfide increasing, oxygen fading, iron blooming, heat pulsing through the stone. I would not have thoughts, but I would answer. Where the vent breath thickened, I would thicken. Where the chemistry failed, I would loosen, pale, become a ghost film, be scraped away. My intelligence would be distribution. My memory would be stain.

I would be one of the beings that makes the wound edible.

Not the red plume lifted like a banner.

Not the iron shell.

Not the pale mechanic crab.

The hidden conversion. The small founding.

I would live where the factory is still becoming a factory: on the lip of a young chimney, in the porous mineral throat, along a crack just warm enough to matter. Around me, the water would roar. Above me, shrimp would vanish in smoke. Beside me, snails would rasp with slow precision. Tubeworm larvae would settle near me if the bargain was right. Crabs would pass over me like weather.

If a chimney broke, I might be buried.

If a new fissure opened, I might become the beginning of a city.

I would have no single body worth naming. I would be a chemistry made briefly alive, a pasture without green, a hunger so small it becomes foundation. I would be eaten constantly and still remain. I would inherit every shell and every corpse by patience.

You would find me on the new black stone, where heat becomes tolerable and poison becomes food.

A dull sheen moving all at once.

The chapel’s first congregation.

The mouth beneath the mouths.
