I would not be found on the highest platform, or in the council shade, or among the fish-house people with their glittering wealth of scales.

I think I would be lower than that.

At first I would be only a child with a hook in my hand, standing ankle-deep beside a grate while an old woman told me to stop looking at the water and start listening to it. I would belong to one of the drain families, the people others respect but do not invite too close to food. My household would have a raised sleeping shelf, three good hooks, two cracked shell scoops, a dog with one torn ear, and a family niche where the whitened finger bones of my mother’s mother were kept above the wet line.

You would find me in the narrow places where the city admits what it is.
