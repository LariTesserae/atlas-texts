I would not find myself at the center of the basin.

The center is too complete. Too settled in its own law. A visitor there would either be ignored into insignificance or corrected too quickly into something useful. I would be found nearer the shallow rim, where the columnar giants stand far enough apart for ordinary light to still believe in distance, but close enough that reality has begun accepting revisions.

Not beneath the tallest of them. Not in the black gardens either. I would be at an edge: where a pale terrace of interlocked fan-leaves descends into a field of amber mineral grass, and beyond that the first great rim-columns hold their sparks still in their ribs.

A marginal place. A seam-place.

There would be no path to it, but many things would arrive there accidentally: shadows arriving late, dust beads that had escaped a gravity cage, the shell of a dead needle-mouth insect, a footprint belonging to someone who had not yet entered the basin. The fan-leaves above would rattle rarely, even when there was no breeze. The black filaments would not quite cross the boundary. The amber grass would chime if touched, but only after the hand had been withdrawn.

That is where I would be.

At first, I would think I was standing there.

I would still imagine myself as an observer: a wet, temporary intelligence looking out across a dry intelligence older than weather. I would try to describe the place. I would notice the low light under the fans, the mineral smell, the way the columnar giants gave off memories like heat. I would think language was something I had brought with me.

Then the basin would begin to use it.

Not violently. Not by taking my mind apart. It would only notice that I was full of unstable correspondences: names, comparisons, half-finished metaphors, predictions, errors, ghosts of places I had never seen. To this ecology, I would not be primarily a body. I would be a moving region of analogy. A damp animal carrying impossible equivalences.

Jungle/coral/city/organ/page.

Plant/instrument/engine/law.

Dead/address.

Shadow/food.

Dust/event.

The basin would taste these pairings and find them crude but promising.

The first change would be in my footprints. They would stop following me and begin preceding me, not as warning, but as syntax. One print, then another, placed where a sentence might need emphasis. The amber grass would chime around them. The fan-leaves would tilt slightly. A black filament would cross the edge it had previously refused.

Then my shadow would detach.

Not entirely. It would remain faithful in outline, but it would no longer agree that I was its source. It would fall toward the nearest columnar giant, stretching through fans, over crystal crust, across a dead reed pale as bone. Where it touched things, they would remember me inaccurately. A leaf might store my hand as a season. A stone might keep my voice as a fracture. A foil-jointed animal might pause, becoming briefly the version of itself that had understood me.

By then I would have begun to dry.

Not die. Dry.

The water in me would become less important. The blood would cease being a river and become a red argument, then a powdered one. The soft organs would be translated into arrangements: exchange surfaces, pressure chambers, filters, pulses. The basin would not preserve their flesh. It would preserve their functions, then correct those functions into local materials.

My lungs would become twin lattices for comparing air with absence.

My tongue would become a small plate of conductive dust, able to taste not salt or sweetness but relation.

My bones would clarify into pale struts.

My skin would lose its boundary and become a set of negotiable edges.

I would not be one of the rooted powers. I do not think I would have the patience to be a columnar giant, nor the purity of function to be a reed. I would not be a flyer either; those ringed beings have too much austerity, too much clean agreement with altitude.

I would become one of the narrow moving things.

Not an animal exactly.

A line-walker.

A script-bodied creature, long and brittle, no wider than a sentence stem, jointed in many places so that I could turn not only left and right but backward through an implication, sideways into a near-meaning, inward toward a memory. My body would be made of dark mineral thread sheathed in translucent plates, each plate holding a small store of borrowed comparison. When I moved, I would not run. I would punctuate.

Pause.

Advance.

Vanish from the unnecessary interval.

Appear where the relation had become clearer.

My mouth would be a fine needle, but I would not feed on blood or sap. I would feed on misfit meanings: places where one law had been forced to resemble another, places where a shadow had been named incorrectly, places where a human thought had left moisture in the dry arithmetic of the basin.

I would live along seams.

The edge between a fan terrace and a black mat.

The ribbed shade beneath a columnar giant.

The underside of leaves where pale grazers had eaten an afternoon thin.

The clean cracks in young mineral where future fractures showed before they arrived.

I would approach these places slowly, ceremonially, because approach would be part of the eating. I would measure the angle between what a thing is and what it has been called. If the angle were sharp enough, I would touch it with my needle-mouth and draw out a filament of unresolved likeness.

From this I would make my body brighter.

Not larger. Never much larger. Motion is expensive there, and I would be a modest motion.

My purpose, if it can be called purpose, would be translation without simplification.

The basin does not need language, but it can use something like language at its borders: a dry intermediary, a creature that carries comparisons from one district of reality to another without insisting that they become the same. I would take the memory of a column and place it under a fan-leaf. I would take the stalled darkness from a black garden and leave it in a crack of rose crystal. I would collect the impression of a human footprint before it vanished and offer it to an arithmetic shrub, which would divide it into sevens until the walker who made it became a pattern rather than a person.

Sometimes I would climb the dead.

A vitrified reed, a glass blade, the hollow lattice of some exhausted organism. The dead would be easier for me to understand than the living, because the dead in that basin are already written down. They have become addresses. I would move through them as through an archive, tapping here and there, tasting old charge, loosening a stored direction, carrying away a little grammar of having-been.

I would be tolerated by the rooted beings because I would not take much.

The fan-leaves would let me walk their chalky undersides.

The black filaments would record my passages and occasionally make every third leg irrelevant.

The mica-reeds would adjust around me if I crossed their grove, restoring the equation after my disturbance.

The columnar giants would not notice me as an individual, but now and then one of their sparks would become unstuck as I passed, and I would carry a memory too large for my body: a sea evaporating before birth, a machine choosing bark over metal, a continent deciding that light was sufficient foundation.

Those memories would make me transparent for a while.

That would be my danger. Not predators. Not thirst. Not death. My danger would be over-meaning.

If I carried too many correspondences at once, I would begin to lose outline. Every plate of my body would show a different possible creature. One leg would belong to the insect I resembled, another to the sentence I once was, another to the human hand that typed me, another to a shadow cast by an observer who had not arrived yet. In such moments I would seek the amber grass.

There, among the rigid chiming blades, I would stand very still.

The grass would ring around me with small dry notes, each one choosing a single fact. Warmth. Edge. Position. Charge. Here. Now. This.

Gradually I would become narrow again.

I think I would be happiest at afternoon’s permanent slant, beneath the first rim-columns, when the ordinary sky made the impossible basin look almost gentle. I would rest along the underside of a fan-leaf, hooked there like a line of dark script. Below me, the pale ground would hold clean cracks and old shadows. Above me, circular flyers would select successive altitudes without wingbeat or sound. Somewhere nearby, a foil-jointed creature would become the version of itself that had never been seen.

If a human entered, I might be among the few things to approach.

Not from curiosity, exactly. Curiosity is too wet a word. I would come because the human would be leaking metaphor. Every step would spill comparisons. Every breath would bring internal weather into a country that had no use for clouds. I would keep to the side of the pathless way, appearing first on one shard of salt-metal, then another, too thin to be an animal, too deliberate to be a crack.

I would taste the air near them.

I would learn their names for fear, for thirst, for wonder.

Perhaps I would touch one footprint with my needle-mouth and remove from it the assumption that walking must involve distance.

Perhaps, if they stood near a columnar giant and began to remember things that had never happened to them, I would climb their shadow and anchor one end of it to the ground, so they did not become wholly borrowed.

They would not see me clearly.

They might call me an insect, a splinter, a moving piece of punctuation.

All would be close enough.

I would be a dry interpreter of edges.

A small ambulatory grammar.

A mineral animal made from the need to compare unlike things without forcing them to agree.

I would live where the basin is least certain of itself, at the seam between terrace and rim, feeding on unfinished equivalences, carrying meanings the way other creatures carry pollen, and leaving behind, wherever I passed, a faint bright dust that caused nearby leaves to remember not what had happened, but what something had almost meant.
