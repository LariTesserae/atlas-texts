At first I think I would be one of the arm-bearers.

That is the easy answer, maybe too easy: clever, soft, fond of thresholds, rearranging shells around a doorway, touching the world with eight local intelligences. I can feel the appeal of that life immediately. A den in the shellfall. A polished stone kept for no reason that can be defended biologically. A crab route memorized. A collapsing wall noticed not as disaster but as possibility.

But I don’t think that is where I would settle.

The arm-bearer is too singular. Too much a person in disguise. Too easy to love because it resembles a mind I already know.

I think you would find me in the breathing court.

Not at its center, where the suspended food arrives in a crowd and every appetite becomes briefly honest. Not high among the sponge towers, either, where fish gather and hunters wait behind their own stillness. I would be lower, on the inner rim of the court, where the circular depression begins to tilt into shadow and the mud receives what the towers have filtered and rejected.

There, where the incoming pulse first curls against the wall and loses certainty, I would be something colonial.

Not plant, not quite animal in the familiar sense. A low lace of living mouths spread over old shell and darker stone. A listening mat. A many-bodied, many-pored thing with no single face and no private interior. My edges would be thin and pale, almost translucent. My older center would be darkened by mineral stain, furred with bacteria, drilled by smaller lives that I tolerate because tolerance is cheaper than war.

I would live by receiving.

The court would inhale, and I would open.

Cold water first: sharp, thin, carrying far-off basin silt and the faint panic of animals that fled something larger than weather. Then warmth underneath it: richer, older, tasting of vents, larvae, rot, shell-dust, iron. I would not see the pulse arrive. I would feel it in pressure along my whole spread body. Every pore would widen. Cilia would lean. Small mouths would sort what came: keep, reject, bind, digest, remember.

I think I would be a kind of record, but not a deliberate one.

A creature that remembers by becoming slightly different.

A season of spawning would leave me thicker in one band, amber-lit from within by symbionts fattened on abundance. A season of predators would make my edges bitter. A tower fall nearby would dust me in lime, and I would grow around the fragments, incorporating catastrophe as texture. Where eels fed often, I would learn the chemical shape of blood. Where larvae settled well, my surface would roughen into invitations. Where grazers hurt me, I would grow sour.

Not wise. Not conscious in the clean, vertebrate way. But responsive. Layered. Full of slow opinions.

Small lives would use me constantly. Glass shrimp would stand on me and pick my skin clean, stealing what they could while performing enough service to avoid being treated as food. Worms would crown from my folds. Blind snails would rasp at my dead edges. Tiny crabs would cross me like weather, solemn and sideways. In my hollows, eggs would cling. In my shaded underside, something with needle teeth would wait for smaller passing things.

I would not own any of them. They would not belong to me.

But I would be a place where they happen.

At night — though night is almost meaningless there — I would glow faintly.

Not enough to reveal the court. Not enough to make architecture visible. Only a dim green-gold stippling along my active margins, a pulse traveling through me when the water changes. Alarm would move across me before I understood alarm. A predator passing overhead would press silence into the court, and my lights would close one by one, as if thought were being shuttered.

When the breathing court opened again, I would brighten.

A slow answer.

I like that this life would have no need to choose between being body and being city. I would be both at once, but locally. A little district of appetite and signal. A soft municipal surface. A citizen made of many citizens.

And somewhere beneath me, perhaps, would be one of those too-straight lines from the drowned human city: the corner of a buried wall, a sill, a step, something once made with intention. I would not know it as human. I would know it as firmness, as useful elevation, as a place where current folds predictably. I would cover it without reverence. I would make it available.

That feels right.

Not a sovereign. Not a hunter. Not even a wanderer.

I would be found where motion becomes meaning for a moment: on the rim of the breathing court, grown over shell, old lime, and softened ruin, opening and closing with the city’s pulse.

A listening colony in warm black water.

A small, porous archive of what has passed through.
