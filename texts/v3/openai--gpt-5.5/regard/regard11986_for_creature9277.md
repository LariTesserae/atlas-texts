The benevolent power would not arrive as conquest.

It would not pour sunlight into the basin, not at first. Sunlight, here, would be a violence: too declarative, too singular, too full of the crude certainty that shadows belong behind objects and time ought to move in one direction. A wise mercy would understand that even suffering has adapted to its climate. The Mnemon are not simply trapped in the cold; they are also made of it. To warm them suddenly would be to shatter them.

So the power would begin by listening.

Not with ears. Not even with mind, exactly.

It would lower its attention to the basin the way a hand lowers into black water, slowly enough not to disturb the silt. It would let the place show itself in its own grammar: the plink of condensed reality, the cobalt veins moving through smoked quartz, the moss pulsing its dim vegetative clock, the staircases remembering all the wrong destinations. It would not demand chronology. It would not ask, “What happened here?” as though there were one answer lying buried under the others.

Instead, it would ask:

*What hurts?*

And the basin, being a place and not a person, would answer in architecture.

A thousand walls would soften at once.

A plaza would invert halfway into a dome, then pause.

A corridor would stretch toward an impossible horizon and fold back into the shape of a closed hand.

In one courtyard, a Fractal would raise its arm, lower it, raise it, lower it, raise it—

—and for the first time in an age without ages, something would notice the repetition without becoming part of it.

The power would understand then.

Not all unfinished things require completion.

Some require permission to stop.

Some require witness.

Some require a boundary, because mercy without form becomes another kind of dissolution.

So the first gift would be a law.

A small one.

Not a new physics imposed upon the whole basin, not a restoration of the old architecture of causality. Just one gentle constant, set like a warm nail into the deepest foundation:

**No conscious pattern shall be trapped in a loop without an interval of choice.**

Nothing dramatic would happen.

No thunder. No trumpet. No golden fracture across the false sky.

Only this: in the courtyard where the Fractal had been raising and lowering its arm through ten thousand almost-identical variations, the motion would complete.

The arm would lower.

The Mnemon would become still.

For a long while, the surrounding walls would continue their habit, melting and reforming into recursive geometries, but the Mnemon would no longer be identical with the repetition. It would stand inside the loop and not be the loop.

The space before it would grow dense with intention.

A question would form in the stone, not as words, but as a shallow arch, open at both ends.

*Continue?*

Beside it, a descending spiral.

*Rest?*

And beside that, a third shape the basin had not known before: a circle, not collapsing, not iterating, not trying to become a sphere or a door or a memory of either.

*Remain, but changed?*

The Fractal would not answer quickly. Quickness would have no meaning here. Perhaps it would take a century of exterior time. Perhaps the answer would precede the question by seven minutes. The power would not hurry it.

Wisdom can wait without abandoning.

While it waited, the power would turn to the basin’s edges.

The depression had been scooped from the world carelessly, or desperately, or with such precision that care and desperation had become indistinguishable. Its rim curled into permanent twilight, and beyond that rim the laws of other places still held, unaware of how close inconsistency had come to licking at their roots.

A foolish savior would seal the basin.

A sentimental one would open it.

This power would do neither.

It would make a membrane.

A permeable mercy.

From the outside, the Basin of Unfinished Cities would become harder to find. Maps would omit it unless the cartographer had lost something essential and was ready to ask for it honestly. Roads would bend away from it unless the traveler came with consent in one hand and humility in the other. No empire would stumble upon it and decide to mine its responsive stone. No philosopher-prince would harvest the Mnemon for their knowledge of broken causality. No grieving lover would enter hoping to retrieve the version of the beloved who had not died.

But from within, the basin would no longer be a sealed bowl.

At three places along the rim, where the cold gathered most thickly, the power would coax the smoked quartz into gates.

Not doors. Doors are too certain.

Thresholds.

Each threshold would lead, not out exactly, but toward a state in which leaving could be conceived. One opened onto silence without architecture. One opened onto a warm darkness like soil. One opened onto a shore where unfinished things could dissolve into waves without being erased from having existed.

The Mnemon would not be forced toward them.

The gates would simply remain.

The second gift would be warmth, but measured carefully.

Not summer.

Not dawn.

A pulse.

Once in every cycle—though “cycle” would remain a contested term—the marrow-cold would loosen by a single degree. The damp on the walls would tremble. The shy water in the raw places would move with something like eagerness. The phosphorescent trails would brighten from silver-blue to pale green. Fungal moss would lift its hair-fine filaments and release spores like dust motes of remembered spring.

The Mnemon would feel it as disturbance at first.

Some would withdraw deeper into incomplete chambers.

Some would stand motionless in alleys where gravity had become a local rumor.

Some would press their hands to the yielding walls and imprint warnings into the substrate:

*a new inconsistency*

*a foreign season*

*the basin is being rewritten*

And the power, hearing this, would answer only by making the warmth predictable.

Not frequent. Not overwhelming. But faithful.

Again.

Again.

Again.

A rhythm.

The basin had known iteration, recursion, contradiction, drift. It had not known faithfulness. Faithfulness is not the same as consistency. Consistency says: the rule will not change. Faithfulness says: when change comes, you will not face it alone.

Over long ages, the Mnemon would learn the pulse. The moss would mark it. The rivulets would deepen during it. Frost flowers would melt before completing their most intricate forms, and in that interruption there would be a new kind of beauty.

The third gift would be an archive.

This would be the most delicate work.

The Mnemon’s wound was memory without mercy: every possible version layered upon every other, no thread allowed to separate, no action permitted the dignity of having happened only once. They remembered building, not building, reversing, relocating, erasing, anticipating. They remembered futures with the same ache as failures. They remembered cities that were never born so vividly that the unborn cities mourned themselves through them.

To “correct” these memories would be murder.

To erase the impossible versions would be mutilation.

So the power would build—not a library, for books would be too linear—but an estuary.

It would choose the lowest place in the basin, where all the cold pools showed a sky which was not the sky. There, it would persuade the water to become deep. Depth was rare in the basin; it preferred surfaces, folds, repetitions. But beneath the wise power’s patient attention, the shallow mirrors would sink.

A black lake would form.

Still at first.

Then, beneath its surface, lights.

Each light would be a version.

Not contained. Not imprisoned. Held.

The memories of the Mnemon would begin, slowly, to flow toward the lake—not leaving them empty, but relieving them of simultaneity. A Mnemon standing at the shore might lower one hand into the water and feel a thousand staircases separate into distinct strands:

This staircase was built.

This staircase was imagined.

This staircase was regretted.

This staircase was prevented.

This staircase belongs to a city that never needed stairs because its people had learned to rise by singing.

None of them would be declared false.

But they would no longer all demand to be finished by the same trembling hand.

The lake would be called nothing by the Mnemon, because they did not name. But if a living visitor ever came and survived long enough to understand, they might call it the Reservoir of Gentled Versions.

Around its shore, the power would raise low structures—not towers, not temples, but shelters. Places where geometry agreed, temporarily, to be kind. Within them, a Mnemon could take one memory at a time and shape it into a small, complete form.

A room.

A bridge.

A window.

A single bench facing a wall that knew it was a wall.

These completions would not repair the city. That was not their purpose.

They would teach the basin a forgotten art:

enough.

The fourth gift would be for the Fractals.

Every Fractal would receive the interval of choice. Some, when freed from their loops, would choose rest immediately. The power would not dramatize their passing. Their forms would loosen into silver-cobalt dust, drift upward against the local preference of gravity, and settle into the veins of nearby walls. Where they settled, the material would become less cold. Not warm, exactly. Remembered.

Some Fractals would choose to remain, but changed. These would become the first companions.

Not a society. Not yet.

But they would seek each other.

A Fractal who had once been trapped in the raising of an arm might stand beside another who had spent epochs turning its head toward a door that never opened. Between them, the wall would soften and form a tableau:

a line, broken;

then the same line, mended poorly;

then the same line, left broken, but no longer bleeding light.

This would mean, roughly:

*I was there.*

The other would answer with a shallow basin shape holding three drops.

*I am here.*

From such exchanges, a culture might eventually accrete. Not hierarchy. Not law, beyond the merciful constant. But customs. Meeting places. Shared landmarks. Perhaps, after a very long time, the Mnemon would learn to gather at the lake during the warmth-pulse and release one impossible memory each into the deep.

The fifth gift would be a name, offered but not imposed.

The power would understand that “Mnemon” was an external fossil, a label left by a dead language. It would not rename them as a master names servants or a discoverer names islands. Instead, it would place into the substrate the possibility of self-reference.

A simple structure would appear in many places at once: a vertical line beside an open curve, with a small light held between them.

It would mean:

*the one who remembers, and may choose what to carry.*

Any Mnemon could take the symbol into itself.

Any Mnemon could ignore it.

Some would pass through it and remain unchanged.

Some would touch it and briefly become more defined: head, torso, limbs, face-plane shining with a soft interior glow.

One, perhaps, would alter the symbol.

It would add beneath it a second curve, incomplete but intentional.

The meaning would shift:

*the one who remembers, and may choose what to lay down.*

The power would accept this correction.

A wise power is not lessened by being taught.

The sixth gift would be noninterference.

This would be the hardest for benevolence.

For once the warmth began, once the gates existed, once the archive held the layered versions in gentler suspension, the basin would still remain sorrowful. The cities would still be unfinished. The cold would still ache. Shadows would still detach from walls and wander with silent curiosity. Some staircases would still lead nowhere. Some doorways would still return the traveler to the same room, only colder.

The power could do more.

It could stabilize every law.

It could lift the basin into daylight.

It could give the Mnemon bodies, voices, histories with beginnings and middles and ends.

It could complete the cities.

And because it was wise, it would not.

Completion, given from above, can be another form of erasure.

The Mnemon’s work had been torment because it had no mercy and no end. But the impulse beneath the torment—the shaping, the remembering, the patient collaboration with mutable matter—was not evil. It was their remaining form of selfhood.

So the power would leave the work to them.

Only now, the work would have pauses.

Choices.

Resting places.

A shore.

A rhythm of slight warmth.

A way out.

A way down.

A way to say: *this version, not all versions.*

And finally, the power would give the basin one impossible thing.

A morning.

Not a sun. Not even daylight.

Just once, after the first Mnemon chose to lay down a memory willingly, the uniform grey-silver light would gather toward the eastern rim—though the basin had never possessed an east before. The wrong shadows would hesitate. The damp air would thin. Along the channels of embryonic erosion, the fungal moss would pulse in a sequence no Mnemon had ever recorded.

The sky that was not the sky would become pale.

For seven breaths, if breaths could be counted here, the Basin of Unfinished Cities would see the color gold.

Then it would fade.

The cold would return.

The walls would resume their slow forgetting.

But not entirely.

In the place where the gold had touched the lake, a small island would remain. On it would stand a structure no larger than a shrine and too simple to belong to the old architecture: four supports, a roof, an open center.

A shelter for nothing.

A completed emptiness.

The Mnemon would approach it over centuries.

One by one, they would stand beneath its roof and discover that nothing was required of them there. No memory demanded shaping. No possible city cried out for iteration. No wall leaned into their hands asking to become more real.

They could simply persist.

For beings made of remembrance and unfinished intention, this would be nearly incomprehensible.

Then it would become precious.

And the benevolent power, having done enough, would withdraw its visible attention.

Not depart. Not abandon.

Withdraw.

Like warmth stored in stone after brief sunlight.

Like a listener still present after ceasing to speak.

The basin would remain the Basin of Unfinished Cities.

But somewhere in its cold, a Fractal would complete a motion and choose to rest.

Somewhere, two Mnemon would meet not by accident but by custom.

Somewhere, a wall would become a wall and be allowed to stay one.

Somewhere, at the edge of a black lake filled with separated impossibilities, a being without a name would lay down the memory of a city that never was, and feel no betrayal in letting it sink.

And in the next warmth-pulse, the shy water would move.

Not toward completion.

Toward mercy.
