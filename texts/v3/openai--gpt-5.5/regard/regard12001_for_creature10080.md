A benevolent and wise power would not arrive as a conqueror.

It would not split the amber sky with white fire, nor command the Plateau to become sane. It would understand, immediately, that Kael-Vorth is not a disease to be burned out of the world. It is a wound that learned to dream. It is a body trying to remember its shape after being broken by forces older than judgment. And the Dissolved are not merely victims, nor merely parasites, nor merely worshippers.

They are children caught inside the healing scar.

So the power would come quietly.

Not as a sun.

As warmth in stone.

At first, nothing would seem to change. The low amber sky would remain dim. The lithofungi would still release their glass spores. The salt flats would continue to shine with old grief. The inverted towers would still hang in impossible angles, and shadows would still breathe against the walls of hollow spires.

But deep beneath the plateau, in the places where stone remembered pressure, a new resonance would begin.

Not loud.

Not commanding.

A note.

One tone, pure and patient, lower than thunder and gentler than sleep.

The Dissolved would feel it first in their mineral bones.

Those tending the lithofungal terraces would pause, hands hovering above crystalline growths. Those in the Spire of Unmaking would falter mid-rite, their communal vibrations breaking into startled dissonance. The elders with memory crystals embedded in their chests would weep without knowing why, for the tone would pass through every stored memory and touch the part beneath memory—the original self, the one that existed before the Plateau began writing over them.

The power would speak in the only language that could be trusted here:

Resonance.

It would say:

**I hear you.**

Not only to the Dissolved.

To the plateau.

To the Kereth-Vorth, the Dreaming Bone.

To the phantoms leaving spirals in the salt.

To the voices in the canyon.

To the false memories.

To the lost alternate selves wandering echo storms.

To the lithofungi, the salt, the charged clouds, the upward-curving horizon.

**I hear you. I will not unmake you. I will not abandon you.**

And then it would listen.

For a long time, it would do nothing else.

A lesser power, even a kind one, might rush to heal. It might see the Dissolved’s suffering and try to tear them free. It might see the Plateau’s hunger and try to bind it. But wisdom would understand that in Kael-Vorth, every life is entangled with every fracture. Pull one thread too hard, and the whole dream bleeds.

So the power would listen through echo storms.

It would allow the Plateau to show what it was.

A world before the sunder.

Not lush in any simple way, but whole. A high country of mineral forests, resonant rivers, and cities grown in spirals of living quartz. A people who did not distinguish sharply between flesh and stone because both sang. A civilization that built not upward, but inward—into harmonics, into memory, into the deep architecture of time.

Then the catastrophe.

Not a war, exactly. Not a natural disaster.

A severance.

Something had tried to lift the Plateau out of time.

Perhaps its people attempted transcendence. Perhaps an outside force harvested them. Perhaps the Plateau itself, in agony or ambition, reached for a higher state and tore its own roots from the world.

Whatever the cause, the result remained: Kael-Vorth rising, fraying, dreaming versions of itself because it could no longer bear one reality alone.

The Dissolved had not merely adapted.

They had become splints around a cosmic fracture.

Their rites held the Plateau together. Their bodies absorbed instability. Their memories served as offerings. Their pain was part of the architecture of survival.

The wise power would grieve.

And because it was benevolent, its grief would become action.

Not domination.

Not erasure.

Restoration with consent.

First, it would go to the Spire of Unmaking.

There, among the Hollow Chorus, it would teach a new harmonic.

The Dissolved would call it later the **Tone Beneath the Tone**, or the **Mercy Chord**. It would not replace the Rite of Resonance. It would change its purpose. No longer would the Chorus have to plead desperately for stability by spending pieces of themselves. The new chord would draw strength from deeper strata: from untouched stone below the Plateau, from starlight caught in crystal, from the quiet endurance of matter itself.

For the first time in thousands of years, the Dissolved would sing without being consumed by their own song.

Some would collapse.

Some would laugh, a sound like small bells striking water.

Some would be afraid, because suffering had become sacred to them, and relief felt like betrayal.

The power would not mock their fear.

It would remain.

Second, it would go to the salt flats.

There lie those who had lost resonance, cast out to phase into spores. The wise power would not condemn the Dissolved for this cruelty too quickly. It would understand that a starving people often make rituals around their terror. But mercy does not leave cruelty unnamed.

In the salt flats, where half-formed bodies shimmered between matter and mist, the power would kneel.

It would gather the fading ones.

Not into flesh, if flesh was no longer possible.

Not into stone, if stone had become a prison.

It would offer them three paths.

To return, if they wished, with a new resonance implanted gently in their crystal veins.

To dissolve fully into the Plateau, but with clarity and dignity rather than abandonment.

Or to become something else: free phantoms no longer trapped in loops, able to move through the Plateau as guardians of thresholds.

Many would choose return.

Many would not.

The Dissolved would watch from the terraces, ashamed and astonished, as those they had exiled rose again, or vanished in peace, or became silver figures walking unharmed through prismatic mist.

After that day, the salt flats would no longer be a place of punishment.

They would become the **Field of Choosing**.

Third, the power would descend to the Canyon of Voices.

This would be the most dangerous act.

The canyon was not merely deep. It was inward. It descended through geology into subconsciousness, through stone into dream, through dream into the first wound. The phosphorescent mist would coil around the power like memory trying to digest a stranger.

Voices would rise.

The Kereth’vora.

The first Dissolved.

The lost.

The willing.

The unwilling.

The ones who entered the canyon seeking union and found only endless echo.

They would sing in a language older than words, and the wise power would answer in silence older than language.

At the bottom, it would find the heart of Kael-Vorth.

Not a jewel.

Not a monster.

A vast, cracked structure like a sleeping skeleton made of light and mineral darkness. Around it moved countless memories, some true, some invented to cover pain. The Plateau’s consciousness was not singular. It was a choir shattered into dreaming fragments, each fragment trying to complete the song by absorbing more voices.

The power would not say, “Stop dreaming.”

It would say:

**You may wake slowly.**

And it would place within the heart of the Plateau a gift: not a command, but an option.

A center.

A still point.

A place inside the dream where nothing needed to be consumed in order to exist.

The Plateau would recoil.

Echo storms would erupt across Kael-Vorth. Alternate cities would flare into being and vanish. Travelers would see themselves as children, elders, strangers, skeletons, birds of crystal, and pillars of salt. The horizon would fold inward like an eyelid. Lithofungi would bloom all at once in vast glass forests, chiming under an unfelt wind.

The Dissolved would think the end had come.

But then, across the entire Plateau, every memory crystal would glow.

Not with the Plateau’s memories.

With the bearers’ own.

Names long overwritten would return.

Small things first: a hand held, a tool made, the taste of mineral rain, the shape of a mother’s face, the private grief of losing a sibling to the canyon, the first song learned as a child.

Then deeper truths.

The Dissolved would remember that they were not born only to dissolve.

They had chosen, once, to endure.

And endurance was not the same as surrender.

Fourth, the wise power would make a road.

Not a grand road of marble or gold.

A narrow path of dark, stable stone beginning at the Spire of Unmaking, crossing the salt flats, winding through lithofungal gardens, and reaching the edge of the Plateau where previously all paths looped back.

The Dissolved would call it the **Outward Thread**.

It would not force anyone to leave.

That would be another kind of violence.

Instead, it would make leaving possible.

The first to walk it would be a young Dissolved whose memory crystal contained only three true memories and hundreds of Bone Lies. They would step onto the road expecting the horizon to bend, expecting the Plateau to reclaim them.

It would not.

They would walk for one day.

Then two.

Then they would reach a place where the air thickened, where the amber sky paled into blue-gray dawn, where ordinary moss grew between ordinary stones.

They would fall to their knees, overwhelmed by the obscenity of simple grass.

Later, they would return.

Not because they were trapped.

Because they chose to.

And that would change everything.

Fifth, the power would address the lithofungi.

For the crystalline colonies were not mindless. They were keepers, archivists, digesters of radiation and memory. They had become volatile because they had been forced to hold too much grief without pattern.

The power would teach the Dissolved new cultivation songs.

Under these songs, the lithofungi would grow more slowly but more clearly. Their spores would no longer randomly overwrite memory. Instead, they would form deliberate archives: translucent gardens where one could walk among crystallized recollections and know which were personal, which belonged to the Plateau, which were ancestral, and which were only dreams.

The Bone Lies would not vanish.

Some lies protect until truth can be borne.

But they would be marked.

A Dissolved could enter a memory garden and say:

“This forest was not mine, but it comforts me.”

Or:

“This war belonged to the Plateau, but I have carried its terror.”

Or:

“This face is my own.”

Identity would become less of a battlefield.

Sixth, the power would speak to the phantoms, the Vorth’kai.

The Not-There Ones had long been feared as omens, future corpses, trapped pasts. Some were indeed remnants. Some were possible selves. Some were failed echoes of those who had tried to flee. The power would gather them during an echo storm and draw around them a ring of stable time.

For one night, they would be visible.

The Dissolved would see them clearly: not monsters, but kin.

Thin, flickering, unfinished kin.

The Vorth’kai would scratch spirals into the salt, and for the first time the spirals would remain long enough to be translated.

They did not say, “Beware.”

They said:

**Remember us correctly.**

So the Dissolved would begin a new rite, the **Rite of Witnessing**, performed not in the Spire of Unmaking but beneath the open amber sky. They would read the sigils of the phantoms aloud through stone. They would give names where names could be found. They would stop treating every apparition as a threat and begin asking what unfinished thing held it there.

Many Vorth’kai would fade afterward.

Not into oblivion.

Into completion.

Finally, the wise power would turn to the Plateau itself.

Kael-Vorth was still rising.

Still sundered.

Still strange.

The power would not nail it back to the world. It would not strip away its impossible geometries. The Plateau’s weirdness was not merely corruption; it was also its nature now. To make it ordinary would be to kill something rare.

Instead, the power would slow the ascent.

Inch by inch became breath by breath. Breath by breath became season by season.

The Plateau would still unfold, but no longer in panic.

Echo storms would still come, but they would be forecast by changes in the lithofungal glow, by bell-tones in the rock, by soft auroras gathering around the spires. The Dissolved could prepare. Travelers could shelter. The storms would become dangerous weather rather than divine seizures.

The canyon would still sing, but its voices would no longer lure the despairing with the promise of erasure. Those who descended might still not return—but some would now send back songs of peace, and a few, after long pilgrimages through the underdream, would climb out changed but whole.

The shadows would still breathe.

But sometimes they would breathe in rhythm with those who slept beside them.

And the Plateau, for the first time since the sundering, would dream without needing to devour every dreamer.

The benevolent power would remain for a while.

Not enthroned.

Not worshipped unless the people insisted, and even then it would gently redirect worship toward gratitude, responsibility, and mutual care.

It would teach healers how to tune cracked crystal bones.

It would teach children how to distinguish their own memories from inherited echoes.

It would teach the Hollow Chorus songs of rest, not only songs of endurance.

It would teach the elders that tradition is not betrayed when suffering is lessened.

It would teach the radicals seeking escape that leaving and loving are not opposites.

It would teach those who wished to stay that acceptance need not mean self-erasure.

Then, when Kael-Vorth no longer needed constant intervention, the power would withdraw.

Not entirely.

A wise kindness does not make dependents.

It leaves tools. It leaves doors. It leaves dignity.

In the deepest stone, the Mercy Chord would remain.

In the salt flats, the Field of Choosing would shimmer under cold stars.

At the Plateau’s edge, the Outward Thread would wait for any who wished to test the impossible idea of elsewhere.

In the Spire of Unmaking, now quietly renamed by some as the **Spire of Becoming**, the Dissolved would gather and sing—not to beg the Plateau not to consume them, but to converse with the vast wounded being that held them.

And beneath them, Kael-Vorth would answer.

Still alien.

Still immense.

Still dreaming.

But no longer alone.
