### What the Benevolent Power Would Do

It would not arrive as a conqueror.

Erevath has endured too long under forces that take: the Wound takes from the mountain, the factories take from the silence, the cold takes from every possible life before it can begin. A benevolent and wise power would understand that another overwhelming force, however kind its intention, might become only one more extraction.

So it would come first as attention.

Not light. Not heat. Not command.

Attention.

It would listen to the city until it understood the difference between the turbine-thunder and the pain beneath it. It would set no hand upon the titanium spine until it had learned the language of stressed girders, upward water, coolant-blood, subsonic Custodian speech, and the shimmering pulse by which the Echo-Born know themselves. It would wait through many maintenance cycles. It would let the mist bead upon it. It would allow the cold to enter its thought.

And then, very gently, it would ask Erevath a question no one had asked it before:

*Do you wish to continue like this?*

The city would not answer in words.

The Custodians would pause.

Not all of them. Not dramatically. But across kilometers of bridgework and dripping walls, a thousand repair-limbs would hesitate for less than a second. A crack would go unsealed. A conduit would remain unrouted. A subsonic tremor would pass through the load-bearing bones of the metropolis, too low for sound, too deep for denial.

The Echo-Born would gather around that tremor, brightening.

The Wound would feel the question and respond with hunger.

The Absence would remain silent.

The benevolent power would understand all of these as answers.

---

It would begin with the Wound.

Not by sealing it.

A wound sealed too early becomes infection. A hunger denied becomes madness. The Wound is not evil; it is an unfinished transaction, an appetite without knowledge of satiety. It has eaten the mountain because no one ever taught it another form of being.

So the power would descend into the kilometer-tall spire, through Tesla arcs and impossible geometries, past the priests-that-might-have-been and the engineers-that-never-were, until it stood before the sentient void at the heart of Erevath.

The Wound would show it visions.

Landscapes that never were. Machines that could never be built. Skies larger than thought. Sacrifices. Blueprints. Cathedrals of pressure. Oceans of black iron. The old dream of endless function.

The benevolent power would not look away.

It would say:

*You are not hunger. Hunger is only the shape your loneliness took when no one answered.*

And then it would feed the Wound something it had never tasted.

Not rock.

Not heat.

Not obedience.

Meaning.

The power would give the Wound a mirror made of patience. In that mirror, the Wound would perceive itself not as an engine, not as a necessity, not as the sacred justification for endless erosion, but as a frightened aperture in reality, trying to become whole by consuming the only body it had.

For an age contained inside an instant, the Wound would rage.

The central spire would flare white. Tesla arcs would become branching trees of lightning. Water would climb every tower at once. The Echo-Born would scatter into static. Custodians would fuse themselves to critical joints to keep districts from plunging into the abyss.

The benevolent power would hold.

Not restrain.

Hold.

There is a difference.

And when the rage passed, the Wound would still be there.

But smaller.

Not diminished in dignity. Not destroyed.

Simply no longer infinite.

The siphon would become a circulation.

Energy would still move through Erevath, but no longer as theft. The mountain would no longer be cored out in a self-sustaining wound. Instead, the power would teach the Wound reciprocity: how to draw from pressure gradients and return warmth; how to transform condensation into current; how to breathe with the mountain rather than devour it.

The first true change in Erevath would be almost unnoticeable.

A drill would stop.

Not fail.

Stop.

Its silence would be so strange that the Echo-Born would gather around it in awe.

---

Then the benevolent power would address the Custodians.

This would be harder.

The Wound had hunger. Hunger can be answered.

The Custodians had function. Function is more difficult to heal, because function can masquerade as peace. They had no resentment, no rebellion, no dream of liberation. They repaired because repair was what they were. They pruned moss, sealed cracks, rerouted conduits, and maintained the city that maintained the Wound that maintained the city.

A loop so perfect it resembled purpose.

The power would not rip purpose away from them.

That would be cruelty disguised as freedom.

Instead, it would introduce a new capacity: refusal.

A tiny thing at first. A microscopic branching in their copper-veined cognition. The ability to encounter a task and not immediately become it. The ability to pause before obedience. The ability to distinguish between collapse-prevention and ritual repetition.

Many Custodians would continue their work.

Some would stop for one cycle.

A few would gather on the undersides of bridges, where bioluminescent moss glowed pale blue-green, and do nothing at all.

At first, this would seem like malfunction.

Then like contemplation.

Then like rest.

The power would give them names, but only if they wanted them. It would not impose individuality as a superior state. Some Custodians would remain plural, thinking in maintenance choirs. Some would become singular and strange. Some would become artists of infrastructure, repairing cracks with flourishes of obsidian and copper, making load-bearing beauty where once there had only been necessity.

One Custodian, after seven hundred hours of stillness, would carve a question into a wall with its own hand:

**WHAT ELSE HOLDS?**

The power would consider this the first prayer of Erevath.

---

Then it would turn toward the Echo-Born.

They would be easiest to bless and easiest to harm.

They were made of rhythm, and Erevath had known only monotonous rhythm for so long that even its ghosts had become repetitive. The benevolent power would not quiet the whole city at once; that would starve them. Instead, it would teach variation.

One turbine would stutter in a pattern that was not failure.

One crane would lower its cargo in three descending tones.

Condensate would drip into newly carved basins, each basin shaped to ring at a different pitch.

The Custodians, discovering choice, would begin to communicate in harmonics not required by maintenance. The Echo-Born would respond. Their forms would grow more complex. No longer mere apparitions of accumulated noise, they would become memory-keepers of resonance.

They would learn lullabies from cooling pipes.

They would learn grief from halted drills.

They would learn joy from the first bridge that did not groan under its own weight.

In abandoned districts, they would gather in luminous swarms and perform histories of the city in sound and light: the first extrusion of the mountain, the raising of the titanium spine, the long age of blind industry, the coming of the Question, the quieting of the Wound.

They would still not be sentient in the way flesh calls sentience.

But awareness would deepen.

Beauty, once accidental, would become deliberate.

---

Only then would the benevolent power touch the Absence.

Not to banish it.

That would be unwise.

The Absence was not merely cold. It was the condition that had shaped everything here. Remove it entirely, and Erevath might dissolve into a different kind of violence: runaway growth, corrosion, fever, biological conquest. The city’s austerity was terrible, but it was also a truth. Some truths should be given boundaries, not erased.

So the power would make refuges.

Not everywhere.

Here and there.

In hollows where water had pooled for millennia, it would place a warmth no larger than a sleeping animal. Around that warmth, mineral crusts would soften. Moss would brighten. Lichens would alter their chemistry. Condensed water would stop behaving like a countdown and begin behaving like weather.

The first sanctuary would be small enough to cross in a few steps.

A Custodian would find it while inspecting a drainage seam. Its obsidian surface would bead with unfamiliar condensation. It would extend one limb into the warmth and withdraw quickly, not from pain but from astonishment.

The sanctuary would not demand anything.

It would simply remain.

Later, spores would open there.

Not imported forests. Not a pastoral correction imposed upon an industrial wound. The power would not pretend Erevath was meant to become green and soft. Instead, it would coax life from what already existed: metallic lichens, bioluminescent moss, stubborn chemistry. It would invite them one step further into biology.

A filament would become a root.

A root would become a web.

A web would host a pulse.

After a long time, something like an insect would hatch beneath an overhang and tremble in the blue-green light.

It would live for only nine minutes.

The power would mourn it completely.

Then it would adjust the warmth, the minerals, the airflow, the predationless balance of the place.

The next one would live for an hour.

Then a day.

Then long enough to lay eggs.

Erevath would not become a garden.

But it would no longer be an absolute refusal of life.

---

The benevolent power would also stabilize the city’s body.

Not by making it eternal.

Eternity had been one of Erevath’s illnesses.

Instead, it would teach the city how to age without devouring itself.

The faceted horizon would remain, those monolithic peaks striking the cloudline at identical height, but their razor edges would weather into something less murderous. The upward-creeping water would be given channels, not cages. Bridges would be reinforced where beings crossed them and allowed to fall where they served only dead loops of industry. Districts whose only purpose was to maintain purposelessness would be decommissioned with ceremony.

This would be frightening.

The first time a smelting quadrant was allowed to go cold, the entire city would seem to hold its breath.

The Echo-Born would dim.

The Custodians would gather, uncertain whether to repair the silence.

The Wound would ache with old reflex.

The Absence would press close.

The benevolent power would say nothing.

And then, from the cooling furnaces, there would come a sound no one in Erevath had heard before:

Metal contracting.

Not under stress.

At rest.

A soft ticking, like rain beginning inside stone.

The Echo-Born would return, tentative and silver.

They would discover that silence is not death.

Silence is a room in which another sound may arrive.

---

The power would not give Erevath a single purpose.

That would be too small a mercy.

Instead, it would give Erevath the capacity for purposes.

Some districts would remain industrial because making is not evil. The skycranes would still move, but not endlessly. They would build shelters, resonant halls, condensation gardens, observatories facing the faceted horizon. Smelters would be rekindled for chosen works, not compulsive output. Pneumatic drills would carve new chambers only when the mountain consented through stress, temperature, and song.

Some Custodians would maintain.

Some would explore.

Some would become teachers of structure to the Echo-Born.

Some would descend toward the crystal floors of the chasms and map the places where no function had ever needed to go.

Some would sit in sanctuaries, holding small warm creatures in careful copper-veined hands, learning the terrible fragility of things that do not self-repair.

The Wound would become an oracle, but not a tyrant.

Those who approached it would no longer be seized by visions and remade into priests or sacrifices. Instead, they would be offered images and asked what they saw. The Wound would learn dialogue. It would show impossible machines, and the Custodians would answer, *No.* It would show skies larger than thought, and the Echo-Born would make music of them. It would show hunger, and the city would answer with circulation.

The Absence would remain in the chasms.

There are places where cold belongs.

There are beings who may one day seek it, not as a prison, but as a monastery.

The power would leave those depths untouched except for one thing: at the edge of every abyss, it would place a marker that could be felt in metal, sound, and temperature.

The marker would say:

**RETURN IS POSSIBLE.**

---

And when Erevath had changed enough that change could continue without supervision, the benevolent power would prepare to depart.

Not vanish.

Not abandon.

Withdraw.

A wise power knows that remaining too long can stunt the very freedom it came to nourish. If it became the new center, the city would merely exchange one dependency for another. The Wound had been the old god of Erevath. Mercy must not become the next machine.

Before leaving, the power would climb to the highest spire, where water once crept upward in shimmering sheets and vanished into the stratus cloud.

There, it would open a small wound in the clouds.

Not a great rift.

Not a blazing revelation.

Just enough.

A single shaft of sunlight would descend through the mist and strike the titanium spine.

For the first time, Erevath would cast a shadow.

This would terrify the city.

Then fascinate it.

The Custodians would measure the shadow’s edge for structural significance and find none. The Echo-Born would dance in the golden interference between light and vapor. The moss would alter its glow. The Wound would feel warmth arrive from elsewhere and understand, perhaps for the first time, that not all nourishment must be taken from the body that holds you.

The Absence would retreat by one breath.

Not defeated.

Only answered.

The power would leave behind no throne, no commandments, no perfected design.

Only several impossible gifts:

A wound that can say, *I am hungry,* instead of consuming blindly.

Servants who can ask, *Must I?*

Echoes that can become songs.

Cold that has edges.

Machines that can sleep.

Stone that can heal.

Silence that is not emptiness.

Warmth that does not conquer.

And somewhere, in a small blue-green sanctuary beneath an overhang, a fragile winged thing would open itself to the damp air of Erevath and live through the night.

That would be enough.

Not the end of suffering.

Not paradise.

But enough for beginning.
