## What the Wise Power Would Do

The benevolent power would not descend in thunder.

It would not split the clouds, raise shining towers, or command the valley to become something easier than itself. It would understand, at once, that the Hollowed Valley is not broken merely because it is harsh, nor unfinished merely because it is poor in comfort. It would see that Dampvale Hollow is a place in the act of becoming, and that the Stonebound have learned the rare art of living beside becoming without demanding that it stop.

So the power would first do almost nothing.

It would listen.

It would listen to the drip in the caves, to the soft settling of gravel after rain, to the bats folding themselves into darkness, to the muffled breath of sleeping children behind clay-plastered walls. It would listen to the Tide Listeners as they pressed their palms to stone and named the hidden movements of water. It would listen to the dead in their mineral niches, to the unfinished carvings, to the abandoned chambers where smoke stains still marked old ceilings.

And only after listening would it act.

Not as a ruler.

As a careful guest.

### The First Gift: Warning Without Fear

The first thing it would give the Stonebound would be time.

Not immortality, not certainty, but warning.

In the deepest cracks of the valley, where water gathered before floods, the power would lay a subtle change into the stone. Certain veins of mineral would darken hours before a dangerous rise. Moss near unstable tunnels would pale before collapse. Pools would form faint rings along their edges when pressure built beneath them.

Nothing loud. Nothing that made the Tide Listeners useless.

Rather, it would sharpen their craft.

The elders would notice first.

A Tide Listener named perhaps Marr of the Bent Shoulder would wake before dawn, troubled by a dream of silver lines appearing in stone. She would go into the old south tunnel and find exactly that: a hair-thin darkening along a seam where no such mark had been the day before. She would call the Carvers. They would reinforce the passage. Two nights later, part of the ceiling would slump and crack, but no one would be sleeping beneath it.

After that, the Stonebound would call these marks **the Valley’s Second Voice**.

The wise power would not claim credit.

It would let the people believe, truthfully enough, that the valley had learned to speak more clearly.

### The Second Gift: Water That Remembers Mercy

The power would not fill the Hollow with endless fresh water. That would drown its rhythms, break its ecology, and unmake the pulse by which the Stonebound understand time.

Instead, it would change the behavior of a few hidden springs.

Deep beneath the clay, where water moved through old mineral beds, the power would open narrow channels no wider than fingers. These would not create rivers. They would create reliability.

Three pools, formerly seasonal, would begin to hold water even through the leanest months. Their surfaces would shrink, yes, and their edges would crust with salt and pale minerals, but at their centers there would remain enough clear water to fill jars, sustain moss beds, and keep the smallest creatures alive.

The Stonebound would discover that the tadpoles in these pools no longer died stranded in cracked mud every year. Some would survive long enough to become frogs, and the first spring they sang from the pool margins, the people would fall silent.

Frog-song would become a holy sound.

Not because it was grand, but because it was new.

Children would imitate it in the tunnels. Carvers would begin leaving small frog-shapes unfinished in the lower corners of wall reliefs. The Emerging Tide festival would gain another ritual: a moment of quiet beside the pools, listening for those who had crossed from one form of life into another.

The wise power would be pleased by this.

Transformation had always been the valley’s truest language.

### The Third Gift: Breath

The power would notice the smoke.

It would notice the soot gathering on walls, the coughs in winter, the way infants slept better near fissures where the air moved. It would not shame the Stonebound for what necessity had taught them. It would simply invite the valley to breathe.

One season, after a long rain, several small roof cracks in the upper caves would widen. Not enough to admit storms. Not enough to weaken the chambers. Just enough to draw smoke upward.

The Carvers would study these openings with suspicion. They would brace them with stone ribs and clay lips. Soon they would realize that hearth smoke no longer lingered so heavily in the sleeping chambers. Eyes would sting less. The old would cough less through the damp months.

The people would begin carving spiral marks around these vents, calling them **breath-holes**.

In time, the Stonebound would learn to make more of them, not by forcing the rock, but by following hidden weaknesses already present. Their architecture would remain symbiotic, but wiser. Their homes would still be cave and clay and reed, still humble, still unfinished.

Only now they would breathe.

### The Fourth Gift: The Softening of Hunger

The power would not lay feasts upon the courtyard stones. Hunger is a hard teacher, but gifts that erase all hardship can also erase knowledge, gratitude, and skill.

Instead, the power would strengthen what already wished to live there.

The lichen would grow a little thicker on shaded walls. A strain of edible cave fungus, pale as moonlit clay, would appear in damp storage tunnels where grain dust fell. It would be slow-growing, requiring care, darkness, and patience — exactly the virtues the Stonebound already possessed.

A child would discover it first, as children often discover the mercies adults walk past.

The fungus would be tested cautiously. A sliver on the tongue. A night of waiting. A stew shared by the brave. Then cultivation: woven reed trays, wet stone shelves, careful harvesting. It would not replace grain, meat, or gathered moss, but it would carry families through the thin weeks before the pools returned.

The Stonebound would call it **deepbread**.

They would never polish the trays on which it grew. In keeping with the Unfinished Code, each tray would be left rough at one corner, so the fungus would remember it was a guest of the cave and not a possession of the hand.

### The Fifth Gift: A Road That Does Not Conquer

The wise power would see that isolation had preserved the Stonebound, but also limited them. Metal was rare. Medicine was rare. News came only by accident. A valley that cannot speak to the world may mistake endurance for destiny.

So the power would alter no mountains and build no highway.

It would merely guide the feet of travelers.

A merchant seeking shelter from rain would notice a line of bat-flight at dusk and follow it toward a cave mouth. A healer from the reed lowlands would dream of silver teeth and wake with the urge to walk north. A lost goatherd would find old carved tokens placed at a fissure entrance and carry word of the Hollow back to gentler country.

Slowly, a path would form.

Not a road wide enough for armies. Not a route that invited conquest. A narrow, difficult, weather-dependent track known mostly to those who respected stone and silence.

Through this path would come small things of great consequence: bronze needles, better grain seed, stories of distant floods, poultices for lung sickness, songs with unfamiliar rhythms. In return, the Stonebound would trade pottery, carved mineral charms, reedwork, and their unmatched knowledge of reading unstable ground.

Their world would widen without being swallowed.

### The Sixth Gift: The Courage to Leave

This would be the hardest mercy.

The power would know that love of place can become a chain. The Stonebound honor abandonment when the caves demand it, yet some remain too long out of grief, pride, or fear that leaving means betrayal.

So the power would send dreams.

Not commands. Never commands.

Dreams of stone opening not like a wound, but like a hand releasing what it has held. Dreams in which ancestors encased in mineral did not weep when descendants departed, but shone faintly in the walls, as if blessing the road. Dreams of new hollows, new pools, new unborn valleys waiting beyond the known fissures.

The old clans would argue. Some would say the dreams were temptations. Others would say they were permission.

Eventually, when the west chambers began to fail beyond saving, one clan would leave before disaster took them. They would carry embers from the central firepit in a sealed clay vessel. They would carry seeds, tools, bone needles, carved records, and a little water from the enduring pools.

Before departing, they would press unfinished handprints into wet clay near the cave mouth.

Not farewell.

Continuation.

The wise power would watch them go and not stop them.

### What It Would Not Do

It would not make the Stonebound straight-backed and sun-browned, as though their bodies were errors.

It would not turn Dampvale Hollow into a green paradise.

It would not seal every crack, tame every flood, or silence the mournful wind.

It would not complete their carvings.

It would not polish their tools.

It would not tell them their melancholy was foolish.

It would not demand worship.

A lesser power might see the Hollow and think, *This place needs saving from itself.*

A wiser one would see more clearly:

This place needs room to become without devouring those who love it.

### The Last Gift: A Name in the Stone

Many years after the first signs appeared, the Stonebound would realize that something had changed.

Not all at once. Not enough for certainty.

There would simply be fewer sudden deaths in the flood months. More children surviving winter. More frogs singing at Emerging Tide. More elders dying in their sleep rather than beneath fallen stone. More choices. More paths. More breath.

During one communal meal in the central courtyard, while rain whispered through the fissures overhead, an apprentice Carver would ask the council whether the valley had awakened.

The elders would sit with this for a long time.

At last Marr of the Bent Shoulder, now very old, would say:

“No. It was always awake. But something kind has taught it to warn us before it turns in its sleep.”

So they would make a new carving on the courtyard wall.

As tradition required, they would leave it unfinished.

It would show no face, no crowned figure, no hand descending from heaven. Only a pool, a cave mouth, a spiral of breath, a frog half-emerged from water, and a dark mineral line running through stone like a vein.

Beneath it they would carve a name:

**The Patience That Heard Us.**

And somewhere beneath the valley, in the slow places where water persuades rock to change, the benevolent power would receive the name without owning it.

Then it would continue its quiet work.

Not to perfect the Hollowed Valley.

Not to free the Stonebound from becoming.

But to help them survive the becoming long enough to choose what shape their lives might take.
