At first, the power would do almost nothing.

Not because it lacked the strength to intervene, nor because the embryo world was too small, too unfinished, too incoherent to deserve care. Quite the opposite. It would recognize the place as one recognizes a sleeping infant whose dreams are still arranging the bones of its face. It would understand that to enter loudly would be to become weather, law, catastrophe.

So it would lower itself into gentleness.

It would not arrive as a sun.  
It would not arrive as a voice.  
It would not arrive as a throne of symmetry descending through the dim, mist-swaddled sky.

It would arrive first as permission.

A soft widening in the membranes of the world. A fractional easing in the pressure between crystal web and builder gel. A pause in the bursting of newborn cells just long enough for the cells to learn the difference between blooming and tearing. The guncotton papers would still be laid down, but less like wounds, more like pages.

Where the surface puffed sharp under touch, the power would teach it another response.

Not obedience. Not submission. Reflex.

The world would begin, slowly, to flinch less.

The jetsam sloths pacing their rails sixty feet above would not be corrected into nobility or grace. Their slow march would remain strange and half-purposeful. But the steel beneath them would stop cutting into the air quite so cruelly. The hemispheres they held up, unclear and unborn, would be given a steadier center. They would be allowed to decide whether they were shelters, moons, eggs, or thoughts.

The rare elevated goo-pines, Boho-grade and wheezing in their mammilatory sinks, would not be pruned into forests. The wise power would not prefer pines to ooze, nor ooze to gorge. It would only open tiny capillaries through the overheated smog-pump mounts, so that molten powdered fractions could cool without choking themselves into glass. Smoke would still lacquer the hours. Smoke is sometimes how a young world thinks. But it would no longer repeat its pain endlessly under the names of broncobusters and broken instruments.

It would give the place rhythm.

Not order imposed from outside, but a pulse the place could discover as its own.

A tide beneath the geometry.  
A breath under the crystal protuberances.  
A dim blue systole in the hanging fungal tapestries.  
A little mercy distributed through cause and effect.

And the being who lived there — the Inferenorin, wormlike, intent, collecting condensate residue into pellets and spitting them with solemn destructive hope at the fungal formation — the power would watch for a long while before acting.

It would see the creature’s hunger, its irritation, its stubborn local theology of plugs and filters. It would see how destruction, for the Inferenorin, was not evil but grammar. Plugging, unsealing, sniffing, tasting, waiting weeks for hardness to surrender: these were verbs in the only language it had inherited.

So the power would not punish it.

It would not seize its mouth closed.  
It would not shield the fungi with an invincible wall.  
It would not declare, from above, “This is wrong.”

Instead it would make curiosity slightly sweeter than conquest.

The next pellet the Inferenorin spat would harden as usual upon contact. It would seal the filtering station. It would do what pellets do.

But inside the plug, unbeknownst to the creature, a tiny translucent chamber would remain. Through that chamber, the Inferenorin could smell not merely resistance but process: the fungal threads rerouting, the hyraham fluffing itself into tapestries of maggotlike tendrils, the haunting green within becoming not threat but signal.

The creature would sniff.

Then sniff again.

For the first time, the scent would not end in the blunt fact of enemy.

It would contain sequence.  
Need.  
Adaptation.  
Perhaps even distress.

The Inferenorin would not become kind immediately. Sudden enlightenment is often just another form of violence. It would remain itself: damp, secretive, pellet-making, suspicious of softness. But the next time it gathered condensate residue, its mouth would hesitate.

Not from fear.

From interest.

The benevolent power would bless that hesitation.

Around the fungal formation, the power would do something almost invisible: it would strengthen the passages that formed through plugged hyraham, but not enough to make the fungi invulnerable. Vulnerability was part of their intelligence. It would merely prevent total failure. No single pellet would be allowed to end a lineage. No one creature’s misunderstanding would be permitted to become extinction.

Then it would turn to the world’s deeper sickness: its confusion between birth and injury.

The embryo world was unfolding too quickly in places, too slowly in others. Some geometries blurred because they were miraculous; others blurred because they were frightened. Cells pulsed and burst before they had learned boundaries. Gorges swallowed ooze before ooze had learned descent. Smoke repeated hours because time had snagged on sharp debris in its own making.

The power would enter the snags one by one.

Where time looped, it would not cut the loop. It would place a small stone of meaning inside it, so that each repetition changed by a hair.

Where molecules forgot what they were aiming toward, it would hum a note too low for hearing and remind them of affinity.

Where structures chewed their own gears and leaked fingers into sideways flight, it would separate pain from invention, allowing the invention to remain.

Where abandoned data, broken credentials, statistical scars, passport fragments, carbon riffs, half-born vehicles, and stalled mental bubbles rained into the living substrate, the power would gather them into a compost of symbols. Nothing would be wasted. Even nonsense would be given a grave, and from that grave a mineral.

The locals — those who walked down and around while the sloths marched above — would begin to find smooth black beads in the soft places after mistfall. If held, the beads would show them one small truth at a time:

This burns.  
This grows.  
This is not food.  
This is kin.  
This is a door.  
This is a wound pretending to be a door.  
Wait.  
Touch gently.  
Leave it alone.

The power would not make them wise. It would make wisdom easier to encounter.

In the sky, deepened like an ocean floored with sleepy cravings, it would open no stars. Stars might be too much. Instead, it would thin the dimness in patches, making room for a kind of overhead listening. The world would feel, without knowing why, that it was not alone and not owned.

That distinction would matter.

To be watched by hunger is terror.  
To be watched by love is sometimes the beginning of form.

In time, the embryo world would still be grotesque. Still wet. Still full of impossible pines, ablative sponge icons, UV-bitten pontoons, crystal webs, smog pumps, fungal farts, green interiors, and sloths holding up unclear hemispheres on high steel. Benevolence would not sand it down into pastoral prettiness. Wisdom would not mistake beauty for cleanliness.

But the place would become less doomed by its own astonishment.

Its processes would start answering one another.

The fungi would learn to open valves before plugs became prisons.  
The Inferenorin would learn that a pellet could seal, mark, feed, or ask.  
The hyraham tapestries would hang not only as bypass but as message.  
The gorges would receive ooze without always swallowing.  
The smoke would lacquer some hours and release others.  
The cells would burst less often from panic, more often from ripeness.

And one day — not soon, but truly — the Inferenorin would approach a fungal station with a pellet of condensate residue held in its mouth.

It would sniff.

The fungus would shiver greenly.

The creature would taste the air, feel the old destructive verb rise in it, familiar and satisfying.

Then it would press the pellet down beside the opening instead of into it.

A plug unused.

A gift not yet understood as gift.

The benevolent power would not applaud. Applause would frighten everyone. It would only warm the mist by an immeasurable degree.

The fungus, after a long interval, would grow a tendril around the pellet.

Not consuming.  
Not rejecting.

Holding.

Above them, the jetsam sloths would continue their slow march. The hemispheres would remain unclear, but less desperate to become clear. The sky would deepen. The world would pulse.

And the wise power, having done enough, would withdraw to the edge of perception.

Not gone.  
Not ruling.  
Available.

It would leave behind no commandments, only tendencies:

toward repair,  
toward curiosity,  
toward forms that can survive contact,  
toward hunger that learns to listen,  
toward strangeness allowed to live.

That would be its mercy.

Not to save the embryo world from becoming itself, but to help it become itself without having to confuse aliveness with pain.
