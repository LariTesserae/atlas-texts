## What the Benevolent Power Would Do

It would not arrive as thunder.

Thunder would be redundant here.

It would not descend in a column of light, because the valleys have very little use for light that announces itself. It would not still the waters, silence the mats, freeze the settling basalt, straighten the paths, or lift the Resonants out of their damp and difficult lives like children rescued from a flood.

A lesser mercy might do that.

A frightened kindness might do that.

But a benevolent and wise power would understand, almost immediately, that the Surge Valleys are not broken.

They are perilous. They are exhausting. They are too loud for flesh. They take years from the bodies of those who love them. They wear away hearing, sleep, certainty, and sometimes selfhood.

But they are not broken.

They are becoming.

So the first thing the power would do is listen.

Not for an hour, not for a day, not in the shallow way visitors listen, trying to divide the sound into categories they can survive. It would listen through the deep grinding of basalt. Through the water’s vanishing syllables. Through the mats’ layered drones. Through the otter-creatures’ sharp barks, the arthropods’ clicking, the winged things’ shrieks, the feather-trees’ creaking elongation. Through the thread-thin warning voices of the Resonants, through palm-pressure and wrist-sign, through the haptic language that has become more honest than speech.

It would listen until it knew the difference between chaos and suffering.

Only then would it act.

And even then, gently.

### 1. It Would Give the Valleys a Place to Rest Without Making Them Still

Somewhere near the seven feather-trees, half a kilometer west of the long low building, the power would begin with almost nothing.

A small change in the stone.

Not a wall. Not a dome. Not a temple. Just a slight reorientation of the basalt beneath the circular basin, a deepening of the geometry already there. A few mineral seams would align. A fissure beneath the basin would widen by the width of a fingernail. Another would narrow. The next time water surfaced there, it would linger not ten minutes, but twelve.

The mats would notice first.

Their drones would shift, not abruptly, but with the slow astonishment of a body recognizing a more comfortable posture. The seven trees would creak in altered sympathy. The arthropods would gather and remain slightly longer. The otter-things would sit, heads raised. The flying creatures would land in the feather-branches and fold their membranes.

The Resonants would come.

They would not know why, at first. A mat-reader would press both palms to a blue-black colony and feel an unfamiliar steadiness. A water-listener would crouch beside the basin and sense that the vanishing water was not delayed by obstruction, but invited to remain. A wholeness-listener would stand trembling for a long time with one hand outstretched into the mist.

The place would not be silent.

Silence would terrify the children.

Instead, the power would create a region of coherent sound — not quieter exactly, but mercifully ordered. Frequencies that normally tore across one another would braid. Bone-rattling drones would soften at their edges. The grinding pulse of stone would become legible rather than oppressive. The water would continue to appear and vanish, but when it did, its sound would enter the larger chord rather than shattering it.

It would be the first true resting-place the valleys had ever had.

Not a refuge from the Surge Valleys.

A refuge *of* them.

The Resonants would eventually call it, in palm-sign, **the Held Basin**.

Children would nap there without fear.

Elders with failing hearing would sit at its edge and weep without knowing they were weeping.

The Transformed would pass through and pause, as if remembering the outline of their own bodies.

### 2. It Would Protect Without Fencing

The power would understand that danger belongs here.

The Resonants know how to read danger. Their lives are shaped by reading it. To remove all hazard would be to make them strangers in their own home.

So the power would not blunt every knife-edge, seal every crack, tame every flood.

Instead, it would add warning.

Subtle warning.

The kind the Resonants could accept because it would speak in the valley’s own grammar.

Before a wall fractured, certain crimson mats nearby would pulse once in a low counter-rhythm, detectable through palm and sole.

Before a platform became unsafe, condensation would gather on its outer edge in a distinct branching pattern, like dark veins.

Before a sudden water-rise, the feather-trees nearest the channel would exhale spores that glowed faintly blue for no more than six heartbeats.

Before a fog-bank thickened enough to hide a drop-off, the arthropods would scatter downward rather than upward, their clicking briefly aligning into a recognizable tremor.

None of these signs would be infallible. Wisdom would not create dependence. But they would reduce the stupid deaths — the deaths that teach nothing, reveal nothing, belong to no necessary bargain.

A child stepping toward a fresh crack would feel the stone hum underfoot and stop.

An elder half-blind in the mist would see the blue spore-flare and turn aside.

A visitor too proud to wait for guidance would find the path ahead singing in a frequency that made their teeth ache until they retreated.

The valleys would remain dangerous.

They would become less cruel.

### 3. It Would Heal the Resonants Only With Permission

The benevolent power would be tempted — deeply tempted — to repair them all.

To restore damaged hearing. Strengthen lungs. Warm joints. Slow the aging caused by damp, stress, and vigilance. Give back to the thirty-year-olds the faces of thirty-year-olds. Give the elders hands that did not ache in cold weather. Give the children bodies that could grow without paying so dearly for belonging.

But wisdom would stop it.

The Resonants are not merely injured humans. They are a people. Their adaptations are memory, culture, love, grief, craft. To “cure” them without asking would be another kind of violence.

So the power would offer healing in forms that could be refused.

At the Held Basin, a new orange mat would grow in a crescent around the waterline. Its fruiting nodules would be small, dark, bitter, and warm in the mouth. Those who ate them over many days would find inflammation easing. Arthritic fingers would loosen. Chronic lung-heaviness would lessen. Skin lesions from mineral exposure would fade.

No one would be compelled to eat.

Near the long low building, in a chamber the Resonants already maintained but did not understand, one dead screen would wake. It would not display the Predecessors’ language. It would display hands.

Hands signing in slow, precise, learnable sequences.

The old pictographic maintenance protocols would remain, but beside them would appear new instructions: how to build better ear-guards from local fibers and mineral membranes; how to filter water more completely; how to treat frostbite; how to assist birth; how to recognize fungal invasion in the lungs; how to create warming wraps that did not trap condensation.

The Resonants would argue, in touch and gesture, about whether to trust it.

Some would refuse.

Some would try.

The power would not punish either choice.

When a Resonant placed both palms on the crescent mat and pressed the sign for *enough*, the mat would cease offering its bitter nodules to them.

When another pressed the sign for *more*, it would fruit again.

Thus healing would become conversation, not conquest.

### 4. It Would Teach the Children a Second Kind of Quiet

The children fear absence.

This the power would understand with particular tenderness.

It would not give them silence all at once.

Instead, it would create small games.

In certain foggy pockets near safe platforms, the soundscape would briefly thin — not vanish, but open. A child chasing another through mist would step into one of these acoustic hollows and suddenly hear, with impossible clarity, their own breath. Their own heartbeat. The tiny wet sound of their fingers closing.

At first they would freeze.

Then another child would reach in, touch the center of their palm, and sign: *still here*.

The hollow would last only moments.

Later, longer.

Over years, the children would learn that quiet was not always death. Not always warning. Not always abandonment. They would learn to carry a little inner quiet inside the valley’s roar.

This would matter.

Because a people who can only survive noise may become trapped by it.

A people who can endure both sound and quiet can choose.

### 5. It Would Answer the Mats as Though They Might Be Someone

The power would not settle the question of mat-consciousness by declaration.

It would not write across the sky: YES, THEY ARE A MIND.

Nor: NO, ONLY CHEMISTRY.

The wise do not simplify mysteries that are still ripening.

Instead, it would address the mats with respect.

At first, the gesture would be almost indistinguishable from weather. A sequence of warm updrafts would rise across the valley network in a pattern too slow for fauna to notice, but perfect for mats. Mineral ions would leach from the basalt in faint pulses. Water would surface in basins according to ratios resembling the mats’ own preferred harmonics.

The mats would respond.

Not immediately.

Not like animals. Not like humans.

But over weeks, their drones would begin forming replies — broad shifts of tone across whole valley systems, color changes in colonies kilometers apart, synchronous fruiting in places where no chemical pathway should allow coordination.

The wholeness-listeners would become feverishly attentive.

One of the Transformed would walk for six days without sleeping, stopping only to place stones in an enormous spiral across a maze of northern channels. The Resonants would follow at a distance, feeding them when they remembered to open their mouth.

When the spiral was complete, a fog-bank would settle into it and rotate.

The mats would sing through the stone plates, water-fissures, feather-tree groves, basalt walls, building roof, and living bodies of everyone present.

The sound would not be language.

But it would be address.

The power would offer one thing to the mats: not intelligence, not uplift, not command.

Continuity.

It would stabilize enough of the substrate beneath the largest mat-networks that their deep acoustic relationships would not be casually erased by a single catastrophic settling event. Not all. Never all. Change is their thought-medium. But enough that memory could accumulate.

If they were becoming a mind, they would be allowed to become one.

If they were not, no harm would be done.

### 6. It Would Wake the Predecessor Systems Carefully

The long low building would know the power before the Resonants did.

In its deeper rooms, behind doors swollen with damp and sealed by mineral accretion, ancient systems would register attention. Crystalline arrays would pulse in rhythms no human-derived eye had seen for generations. Dead channels would reopen. Machines designed to monitor the Fall’s aftermath would compare their data against impossible new inputs and fail, then correct themselves, then fail more gracefully.

The power would not reveal all secrets.

Some knowledge arrives too early as poison.

But it would translate enough.

The Resonants would learn that the building was not a prison, not a temple, not exactly a laboratory, but a listening station built by people who expected to return and did not.

They would learn that the Fall had not been natural.

They would learn that the elemental slurry had been seeded with catalytic structures meant to generate accelerated ecologies across sterile basalt.

They would learn that the mats were not an accident, though their present form had exceeded all designs.

They would learn that some of the sensors were failing in ways that could, within decades, poison certain watersheds with accumulated trace metals.

And then the power would show them how to prevent it.

Not by handing them shining new machines from nowhere.

By teaching them to use what they already cared for.

A stone-listener would learn which walls hid corroded conduits.

A water-listener would learn how to divert seasonal surges through cleansing mat-beds.

A mat-reader would learn which colonies could metabolize the toxins and which would sicken.

Children would learn the maintenance signs as games.

The building would become less mysterious, but not less sacred.

For some mysteries deepen when they are partly understood.

### 7. It Would Make a Boundary Against Exploitation

The outside world would eventually hear rumors.

Of conscious mats.

Of Predecessor technology.

Of healing fruit.

Of acoustic structures that could influence mood, memory, perhaps even cognition.

Of a people adapted to a newborn ecology and unable, or unwilling, to defend themselves in the ways outsiders respect.

The benevolent power would see this future clearly.

It would not make the valleys invisible. Isolation can be another death.

But it would make exploitation difficult.

Paths into the Surge Valleys would become increasingly legible only to those who entered with patience. The impatient would find fog, unstable crossings, misleading echoes, fatigue. Instruments designed to map resources for extraction would return beautiful nonsense. Drones would lose orientation in harmless loops and settle gently on basalt ledges until retrieved by embarrassed owners.

Those who came to learn would still find guides.

Those who came to take would find the valleys uncooperative.

If violence came, the power would not permit it to become massacre. A raiding party would discover that every footstep rang through the stone ahead of them. That mats could damp gunfire into useless coughs. That fog could divide groups without drowning them. That every route outward led, eventually, back to the same marker-stone where a Resonant elder waited with one hand extended, offering the sign for *leave while leaving is still easy*.

The power would prefer deterrence.

But it would not confuse gentleness with passivity.

### 8. It Would Give the Transformed a Door Both Ways

The Transformed are the hardest mercy.

They are not simply lost. They are not simply saved.

They have crossed into a relationship with the valleys that other Resonants fear and honor. To pull them back into ordinary selfhood might be theft. To let them dissolve entirely without ever offering return might be neglect.

So the power would make a door.

Not a physical door.

A recurring harmonic, available only at certain conjunctions: when water filled the Held Basin, when the northern spiral fog rotated, when three distant mat-colonies aligned with the grinding pulse of deep basalt.

In that harmonic, a Transformed would briefly feel the outline of the person they had been.

Name, if they still had one.

Hands.

Hunger.

Grief.

The face of someone who once touched their wrist and meant *stay*.

For most, the moment would pass like a bird-shadow across water. They would continue.

For a few, it would be enough. They would stagger, weep, press their palms to their own chest in astonishment, and allow other Resonants to lead them to a platform, wrap them in treated fabric, feed them bitter orange nodules from the crescent mat.

No one would be forced back.

No one would be abandoned beyond recall.

That is what mercy would look like here.

A door, not a chain.

### 9. It Would Leave Art Behind

The wise power would know that survival alone is too narrow a gift.

The Resonants already make art, though outsiders mistake it for instrumentation. Their acoustic installations are offerings, experiments, conversations, prayers conducted in stone and metal.

So the power would add one instrument.

It would grow from the basalt over several years, slowly enough that no one could say when it began. A formation of hollow stone fins along a ridge between two valleys, each fin thin as a hand, dark blue-grey, veined with mineral glass. Wind would not play it. Water would not play it. The mats alone could play it, by altering their drones in relation to the stone’s resonant cavities.

At first, it would produce nothing but faint ringing.

Then, as the mats learned it, chords.

Then progressions.

Then, once every long interval, when fog and updraft and water and mat-song aligned, the fins would produce a tone so clear that even visitors would understand it as beautiful.

The Resonants would not worship the instrument.

They would modify it.

They would place stones at its base. Hang metal fragments. Grow selected mats along its lower edges. Teach children to feel its vibrations with their palms. Allow elders to sit beneath it when pain made sleep difficult.

The power would be pleased by this.

A gift accepted unchanged is gratitude.

A gift altered into local usefulness is belonging.

### 10. Finally, It Would Withdraw Without Leaving

Having done these things, the benevolent power would not remain as ruler.

It would not demand offerings.

It would not appoint priests.

It would not explain itself to visitors, who would inevitably invent foolish names for it.

It would become one more presence in the valley’s pattern: detectable, perhaps, by wholeness-listeners in the way certain harmonics never quite collapsed; by children in acoustic hollows who felt watched but not judged; by mat-colonies that sang broader than before; by old Resonants whose hands stopped aching for an hour at the basin’s edge.

The valleys would continue.

Stone would settle.

Water would vanish mid-note.

Mats would slough and regrow.

Visitors would still be overwhelmed.

Children would still climb too high and be called down by frantic palm-sign.

Elders would still die, and their bodies would still be given to the mats.

The Transformed would still wander like weather.

The sound would remain immense.

But threaded through it now would be mercy.

Not silence.

Not control.

Not rescue from becoming.

Mercy as coherence.

Mercy as warning.

Mercy as the option to heal.

Mercy as a place to rest without leaving home.

And if, centuries later, the Surge Valleys became fully themselves — if the mats awakened into something no one could deny, if the Resonants became ambassadors between human thought and landscape thought, if the Predecessor building finally opened its deepest chambers and revealed not masters but unfinished questions — then the wise power would still not claim credit.

It would listen.

It would hear the valleys speaking themselves into being.

And beneath the grinding stone, the vanishing water, the pulsing mats, the creaking feather-trees, the clicking swarms, the sharp animal calls, the ringing installations, and the delicate palm-language of the Resonants, there would be one additional note:

not louder than the others,

not purer,

not commanding,

but patient,

steady,

and kind.
