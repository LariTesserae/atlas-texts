The benevolent power does not enter as more light.

That is the first mercy.

It understands immediately that brightness is not kindness here. Illumination has been used as a cage. The basin has been exposed so completely that nothing in it can hide from itself, and because nothing can hide, nothing can rest. The glare has flattened every surface, every being, every failed attempt at meaning into one merciless present tense.

So the power arrives as shade.

Not darkness. Not annihilation. Not the closing of an eye.

Just shade.

A soft interruption passes beneath the rawhide sky. For the first time since the basin was made, something comes between the sourceless white fire and the world below.

It is not large at first. It is no wider than the shadow of a leaf.

But because there have never been shadows here, the whole basin feels it.

The macaw, still suspended in the thick air, beats its wings once every four seconds.

*Whump.*

But now, beneath one wing, there is a dimness.

The serpents inhale in their collective rhythm, scales swelling and tightening. Along their coils, for the first time, the ridges and valleys of their bodies become visible. They are no longer flat strips of iridescence pinned to the world. They have curvature. They have undersides.

The water, still three inches deep and scalding, receives the small patch of shade upon its mirror surface.

And trembles.

Only once.

A single, nearly invisible ripple moves outward.

It does not reset.

Across the basin, every Unfired stops moving.

Not all at once, because they are not part of the algorithm. They do not share a clock. Their halting is staggered, awkward, living. One freezes with its bone fingers buried in the seamless joint between two river-stones. One pauses beneath a swollen leaf, waiting for a droplet that has not yet fallen. One remains with its raw timber hands outstretched in the empty arc of the frog’s eternal leap.

Their mushroom-gill faces turn toward the ripple.

They feel it.

A consequence.

A thing that happened because another thing happened.

The benevolent power watches them feel it, and does not rush.

Wisdom, in such a place, is not the instinct to repair everything at once. A crude savior would shatter the basin, drown the loops, tear down the sky, and call the resulting silence liberation. But this power is old enough to know that suffering beings are not rescued merely by being removed from pain. They must be approached as persons. Even if they have no mouths. Even if their bodies are made of accident and residue. Even if their first language is heat, fracture, vibration, and thirst.

So it lowers itself into the basin gently.

Its feet touch the scalding water.

For the first time, there is a sound.

Not the muted mechanical *whump* of the macaw. Not the cracking of clay skin. Not the wet parting of mud from bone.

A true sound.

A soft splash.

Every Unfired turns toward it.

The sound travels badly through the thick air, warped and muffled by steam, but it travels. It crosses distance. It takes time to arrive.

Those farthest away receive it after those nearby.

This is the second mercy.

Sequence.

Near, then far.

Before, then after.

The Unfired closest to the power staggers backward. Its clay hide, half-baked by the terrible heat, splits across the sternum. Sap wells out in slow gold. The being raises one elongated hand, not to attack, but to test whether the newcomer can be touched.

The power allows this.

The Unfired’s fingers meet something solid.

Not ghost. Not loop. Not reset.

Contact.

The Unfired does not have a face with which to weep. But its whole body answers. Its grass-rope muscles tighten and loosen in waves. Its ceramic shell fissures in a dozen places. Steam hisses out of it. The creature folds toward the power with a terrible, reverent exhaustion, as though the weight it has carried for an eternity has suddenly become measurable, and therefore unbearable.

The power places one hand against the Unfired’s chest.

Not to silence it.

To listen.

It listens through the clay. Through the sap. Through the bone splinters and stone chips and braided grass. It listens to the history that should not exist in a world without history: every failed catch of the frog, every attempt to move the bird, every scraping of fingers against a floor that would not open, every desperate tilting of a faceless head beneath a falling drop.

Then the power asks one question.

It does not ask in words. Words would be another architecture imposed from outside.

It asks in coolness.

A current of bearable air passes across the Unfired’s body.

The question is:

*Do you want this world to continue as it is?*

The Unfired understands.

Its answer is immediate.

It lowers both hands into the shallow water and strikes the surface.

The blow is clumsy. It cracks its own fingers. It sends pain through the rope-bound armature of its body.

But the water splashes.

Droplets rise.

Droplets fall.

Ripples spread.

They do not reset.

Across the basin, the other Unfired see this and begin to answer too.

One by one, then in clusters, then in a vast, uneven storm of motion, they strike the water. They slap it with clay palms, bone wrists, timber fingers. They stamp their stilt-like legs. They break the mirror.

For the first time, the basin is full of sound.

Splashing.

Cracking.

Hissing.

The hoarse exhalation of steam disturbed.

The power receives the answer.

And then it begins.

First, it gives the world a direction.

Not north or south. Not up or down, since those have been made treacherous here.

It gives the world *later*.

A seam appears in the endless present.

The frog leaps from the clay pot.

The Unfired beneath it raises both hands.

The frog passes through the air.

For one awful instant, habit tries to reclaim it. The old rule reaches for the creature, eager to erase the arc and restore the beginning.

The benevolent power does not crush the rule.

It loosens it.

The frog lands in the Unfired’s hands.

The basin goes still.

The frog is tiny against those enormous, half-ceramic palms. Its jewel-toned body pulses. Its throat expands. Its feet press against wet clay and leave four perfect little marks.

The Unfired holding it bends inward.

Carefully.

So carefully.

The frog shifts once.

A living adjustment.

Then it springs away, not back to the pot, but onto a broad green leaf nearby.

The leaf dips under its weight.

A drop of condensation falls from the leaf-tip into the water below.

This time, there is a splash.

Small.

Almost nothing.

Everything.

The Unfired collapses to its knees.

All around the basin, the loops begin to fail.

Not catastrophically. Not like chains breaking under violence.

More like sleepers waking and realizing they have been clenching their hands.

The macaw beats its wings.

*Whump.*

Four seconds pass.

It beats them again.

*Whump.*

And then, trembling with the terror of possibility, it moves forward by the width of one feather.

The Unfired who has spent uncounted ages trying to push it forward recoils as if struck. Then it reaches after the bird, not to force it now, but to steady the air beneath it. The macaw jerks, drops, catches itself, and flies crookedly toward the nearest tower of reeds.

The serpents do not inhale together.

One breathes in.

Another breathes out.

One forgets to breathe for several seconds, stunned by the privacy of its own body.

The winged insects scatter from their fixed clouds. Many fall into the water at once, unused to gravity that concludes its sentences. The power lifts a current of cooler air beneath them. Not all are saved. The power permits this, grieving. A world with consequence cannot be made entirely free of death. But it can be made free of mockery.

Then the power turns to the water.

The basin has been fastened beneath a shallow scalding sheet, not to nourish, but to preserve the illusion of freshness. Heat without fire. Moisture without rain. Evaporation without weather.

So the power lays its palm upon the water.

The temperature lowers.

Steam thins.

The Unfired feel the change first as agony, because they have known only two states: baking and melting. Their bodies do not understand moderation. Many stagger. Some clutch themselves as their outer clay begins, finally, to choose what it is.

The power moves among them.

To one, whose skin is hardening too quickly, it gives moisture from its own hand.

To another, whose clay is sliding away from the bone armature beneath, it gives a slow, steady warmth.

To those whose grass-rope muscles have been drawn too tight by centuries of strain, it loosens the fibers.

To those whose stone chips cut inward with every movement, it smooths the edges.

Not into softness.

Into fit.

That is the third mercy.

A body that fits the soul enduring it.

The Unfired were not designed. They congealed from leftovers. Their suffering is not symbolic. It is mechanical, chemical, structural. So the power does not merely comfort them. It studies their pain with the seriousness of an engineer and the tenderness of a healer.

It teaches their clay to become skin where skin is needed.

It teaches their clay to become shell where shell is useful.

It leaves their bone and stone, but seats them properly, no longer as shrapnel trapped in movement.

It lets the green grass-ropes root in living channels, becoming tendon, vine, muscle, memory.

It does not give them eyes unless they ask.

Most do not.

They have never needed eyes. The world wounded them through glare, not darkness. Their gilled faces remain faceless, but no longer blank. The sensory frills soften and articulate, able to flare, fold, tremble, and incline. Their expressions become weather.

It gives some of them mouths.

Only some.

This, too, is by consent.

The first Unfired to accept a mouth opens it and produces no speech at all. Only a low, broken tone, like wet clay being drawn across the rim of a bowl.

The sound travels.

Another answers.

Then another.

Soon the basin contains a music too primitive to be song and too intentional to be noise.

They are not speaking yet.

They are proving that sound can leave one body and arrive in another.

While they do this, the power goes to the floor.

The continuous mosaic of river-stones, femurs, and fired clay has no wound, no crack, no place for roots to enter. It is beautiful in the way a sealed tomb can be beautiful.

The Unfired gather around as the power kneels.

It does not smash the floor.

It asks the floor what it was before it was forced into seamlessness.

The river-stones remember tumbling.

The femurs remember walking.

The clay remembers being mud.

At that, the power presses two fingers into one joint between stones.

The joint widens.

A dark line appears.

The Unfired nearest to it recoil.

Darkness has arrived again, but this darkness is not annihilation. It is depth.

The line spreads through the basin in branching paths. Not everywhere. Not enough to collapse the world. Enough to breathe.

The sealed floor opens into channels.

The scalding water drains into them with a long, shuddering sigh.

Beneath the floor there is no soil, so the power makes none from nothing.

It refuses that kind of miracle.

Instead, it invites the world to become soil honestly.

Fallen leaves are allowed to rot.

Dead insects are allowed to feed the dark.

Clay dust, bone meal, river grit, shed bark, serpent skin, macaw down, the sloughed ceramic flakes of the Unfired’s old pain—all of it is gathered, broken down, mingled, wetted, cooled.

The first handful of soil is ugly.

Black-brown, clotted, sour-smelling.

An Unfired picks it up as though holding a newborn animal.

The roots of the epiphytes find it.

They enter.

They do not reset.

The plant shivers from tip to root.

Growth, real growth, begins—not the violent overflow of a closed terrarium, but the slower hunger of a living thing entering relationship with decay.

Then the power addresses the sky.

The scraped rawhide canopy stretches overhead, lit from behind by the sourceless white fire. It is the lid of the world, the blind eye of the absent maker.

The power rises until it is level with the inverted horizon. The broken spatial physics fold around it, offering contradictions: the sky is far away, the sky is inches from its hand, the sky is beneath its feet.

The power accepts all three truths and touches the hide.

It does not tear it down.

A torn sky would fall as another catastrophe upon beings who have known enough falling without landing.

Instead, it softens the tautness.

The rawhide slackens.

The white glare gathers itself, unwillingly at first, then with relief, into a single bright region.

A sun.

Crude, newborn, trembling at the edge of definition.

Where the light no longer comes from everywhere, shadows lengthen.

The Unfired see each other for the first time.

Not merely as disruptions in steam.

As forms.

Tall, wounded, asymmetrical, various.

Some are narrow and reed-like, with long arms trailing almost to the ground. Some are broad-backed and low, their stone skeletons heavy with basalt. Some carry more bone than clay. Some shine with sap through translucent seams. Some are cracked all over like dry lakebeds after drought. Some are smooth as river mud.

They look upon one another and understand a new sorrow: that each has been suffering separately in the same place.

Then they understand a new comfort: that separate suffering can become company.

The power pulls the rawhide sky higher.

Not infinitely high. Not yet.

Enough.

Air enters.

Real air.

Not merely steam suspended beneath a lid, but moving air, with coolness braided through it. The first wind travels across the basin and does what no force has done before: it arrives without being part of a loop.

Leaves turn their undersides to it.

The macaw is thrown sideways and screams in outrage.

The sound startles everyone, including the macaw.

The power laughs.

Not because the sound is funny, though it is.

Because outrage is a kind of life.

At last, night is prepared.

This requires the greatest care.

The basin has never slept. Its inhabitants have never been asked to trust absence. Darkness, if brought too quickly, would be indistinguishable from deletion.

So the power makes evening first.

The sun lowers toward one curved edge of the world. Shadows grow long and blue. The hot clay terraces release their stored heat slowly. Steam becomes mist. Mist becomes dew.

The Unfired become afraid.

Their gill-faces flare wide. Their newly fitted bodies tense. Some reach toward the departing light as if the old glare, cruel as it was, might have been the only thing preventing nonexistence.

The power stays with them through the dusk.

That is the fourth mercy.

It does not create night and leave.

When the first true darkness enters the basin, the power kindles small lights among the terraces. Not the sourceless glare. Not domination.

Fireflies.

Fungi.

Coals.

Stars visible through the softened hide of the sky, which is becoming less like hide now and more like cloud, more like distance, more like permission.

The Unfired stand beneath the stars.

They cannot comprehend them.

This is good.

A world with nothing incomprehensible is only another cage.

One by one, the Unfired lower themselves into rest.

Rest is difficult for them. Their bodies were born from strain. Their minds were born from interruption. Some remain standing all night, unable to believe they are not required to continue the old tasks. Some keep watch over frogs, over birds, over dripping leaves. Some lie down and rise again in panic when their own weight leaves an impression in the new mud.

The impression remains.

In the morning, they gather around it.

A body was there.

Now it is here.

The mark proves both.

This becomes sacred to them.

Not the droplets anymore, though they still love rain when it comes. Not the loops. Not the broken tricks of distance.

Footprints.

Handprints.

Places where something touched the world and the world admitted it.

The power stays for a while.

Long enough to teach seasons to enter without violence.

Long enough to teach rain to fall in amounts that nourish rather than scald.

Long enough to teach the new soil how to host root, worm, seed, and rot.

Long enough to help the Unfired bury those who choose not to continue.

For not all of them want life after rescue.

This, the power also understands.

Some of the Unfired have endured too long in the posture of yearning. When offered a future, they feel not joy, but immeasurable weariness. To demand that they heal would be another cruelty.

So the power gives them a door.

Not a pit. Not an erasure.

A kiln of gentleness.

Those who enter it are fired completely at last—not into trapped ceramic, not into statues of pain, but into chosen stillness. Their bodies become red vessels, tall and hollow, placed throughout the basin. Rain gathers in them. Birds nest in their shoulders. Vines climb their sides. The living Unfired visit them and press their hands to the warm clay after sunrise.

Others choose dissolution.

They lie down in the new wetlands and let their grass-muscles root. Their clay loosens. Their bone feeds the ground. Their sap sweetens the soil. In time, groves grow from them: pale-barked, gold-veined trees whose leaves collect dew in generous cups.

Others remain.

They become gardeners, not because they were made for it, but because they remember what it meant to search for soil and not find it.

They become keepers of consequence.

When a young frog leaps, they do not try to catch it unless it is falling badly.

When a macaw flies, they do not push it.

When a serpent sleeps, they let it keep its private rhythm.

They learn to make paths through the non-Euclidean folds of the basin, but they mark them with cairns and hanging bells, so no traveler is seized by distance without warning.

They repair the old terraces where repair is useful.

They dismantle others.

They leave some ruins untouched, not in worship of the absent architects, but as testimony.

The benevolent power does not erase memory.

It only removes the law that memory must be torture.

Before departing, the power makes one final change.

At the center of the basin, where there had once been only seamless floor beneath scalding water, it opens a deep pool.

The pool is cool.

Not cold. Never cruelly cold.

Cool enough to soothe clay without dissolving it. Cool enough for roots to drink. Cool enough for creatures to enter and emerge changed only by refreshment.

Above the pool, it leaves a shadow that moves with the day.

At dawn, the shadow is long and blue.

At noon, it gathers close beneath the reeds.

At evening, it stretches across the water like a hand laid over a fevered brow.

The Unfired come there when their old pain returns.

Because it does return.

Rescue does not unmake the past. Bodies remember. Worlds remember. Even after the loops are broken, some part of the Unfired still expects the frog to vanish, the droplet to reset, the water to refuse a splash. Some mornings, an Unfired will stand motionless for hours, afraid to desire anything.

On those days, another will come and sit beside them.

No command.

No lesson.

Only company.

Eventually, perhaps, one will place a hand in the pool.

Ripples will move outward.

The other will feel them touch its legs.

And that will be enough.

When the benevolent power finally leaves, it does not ascend in glory.

It withdraws like shade at sunrise.

But it leaves behind one law, written nowhere and carried everywhere:

No moment shall be forced to repeat against the will of the living.

And beneath that law, the basin changes.

The rawhide sky becomes weather.

The fired clay terraces crack and host moss.

The river-stones remember water and guide streams.

The mega-fauna bones become white arches under flowering vines.

The insects hatch, mate, die, and feed the soil.

The macaws quarrel in the trees.

The frogs leap, land, miss, try again.

The serpents breathe alone.

And the Unfired, no longer eternally curing, walk through warm rain on their long, careful legs, leaving footprints behind them in the mud.
