The benevolent power would not arrive as light.

Light, here, is an unfinished argument. It wounds the sky in auroral rents, spills dimly over stone that has not consented to shadow, fractures against the gourd-eyes of the Attenuated and sends their silhouettes fleeing upward into the static grey. To come as radiance would be to add another violence to a place already being born too loudly.

Nor would it arrive as silence.

Silence is sacred here. Scarce. Hoarded in cairns and hollow ribs, pressed between slate and bone-needle, stored in the trembling hearts of beings who have never been allowed the luxury of peace. To impose silence upon the Scar would be to smother the birth-roar mid-labor, to decide on behalf of an unfinished world that becoming was too painful to permit.

So the power would arrive as **listening**.

Not as an ear, for ears are already everywhere: in the teeth, in the stone, in the vibrating root-webs of the audiosynthetic gardens, in the hollow gourds set into the faces of the Attenuated. It would arrive as the first listening that did not consume, measure, name, pity, or recoil.

It would lower itself beneath the scream.

Deeper than the grinding extrusion of stone.

Deeper than the raw substrate of reality forcing itself into ridges, planes, and knife-edged mineral certainties.

Deeper even than the pauses where the Attenuated tremble themselves back into coherence.

There, below sound and anti-sound, it would place no hand—because hands imply grasping—but would incline its whole vast gentleness toward the Scar and learn the shape of its pain.

For a long while, it would do nothing.

This would be its first mercy.

It would not straighten the upward-curving horizon. It would not command gravity to stop suggesting and begin enforcing. It would not bring rain, because water is a law from elsewhere and the Scar has not invited it. It would not populate the obscene amber gardens with insects or birds merely to satisfy some imported notion of completion. It would not give mouths to the Attenuated, nor blood, nor sleep, nor hunger, nor any of the warm tyrannies of animal life.

It would wait until the waiting beings noticed that they were being accompanied.

The Attenuated would sense it first as a difference in the gaps.

One pause between scream-waves would arrive and fail to collapse as quickly as before. Not lengthened by force. Not held open like a wound. Simply steadied, as one might cup a wavering flame without touching it.

Their trembling hearts would hesitate.

Across the plateau, seven or seventy hollow figures would turn their gourd-eyes toward no visible source. The cairns would lean by the smallest possible degree. The slate shelters would exhale stored stillness. In the mucilaginous forests, the great pleated fronds would stop pulsing for the span of one impossible thought.

Then the scream would return.

But something would be different.

The roar would still liquefy thought. The mountains would still grind themselves upward from the raw substrate. The flora would still drink vibration through their tuned root-nets and blush with pale bioluminescence. The Attenuated would still freeze into statuary, their fluted ribs passing the violence through them.

Yet hidden within the scream would be a second tone.

Not quieter.

Not louder.

Kinder.

A deep undertone, woven beneath the tectonic shriek, shaped precisely to the hollow architecture of the Attenuated. It would not cancel the Scar’s birth-noise, for cancellation is only another form of erasure. Instead, it would give the scream somewhere to go.

Before, the roar had struck all things equally, indiscriminately, as if existence were a hammer and every becoming thing a nail.

Now the roar would find channels.

It would enter the cairns and pass through them in spirals.

It would enter the Attenuated and no longer carve only by abrasion, but by resonance.

It would enter the audiosynthetic roots and teach them a chord beyond appetite.

The Scar would continue to become, but its becoming would no longer be pure agony. Pain would remain where pain was necessary—the strain of emergence, the pressure of form, the terrible cost of leaving the possible and entering the actual—but waste-pain, needless pain, pain that taught nothing and nourished nothing, would begin to drain away into the deep undertone and be transmuted.

The Attenuated would not understand this as rescue.

They had no myth of rescue.

They would understand it as a new category of pause.

So the wise power would give them no doctrine. It would give them a **place**.

At the uppermost rim of the Exsert Range, where the plateau curved inward toward the unfinished sky, one cairn would cease being merely a cairn.

It would begin as thirteen pieces of slate already stacked by an Attenuated whose shadow had frayed too often in the auroral light. During a long scream-wave, while the builder stood frozen beside it, the undertone would pass through the stones and find their hidden sympathies. Each slab would remember every silence it had ever stored. Each impossible balance would become a syllable. Each gap between stones would deepen until it contained not emptiness but welcome.

When the pause returned, the cairn would be larger.

Not physically. Distance still refused such simplicity.

It would be larger in permission.

The Attenuated nearest to it would approach by remembering itself forward. Another, impossibly distant, would take one slow step and arrive beside it without cutting probability. A third would emerge from behind a boulder that had not existed a moment before. Others would come from positions they had not yet occupied, or had abandoned centuries earlier, or would only discover after the next century compressed into a heartbeat.

For the first time, their reflections would not be mistaken for one another.

They would stand together around the altered cairn, tall and white and hollow, their gourd-eyes full of distant silence.

The power would not give them speech.

Speech is a river; they were made of intervals.

Instead, it would teach the cairn to translate **attention**.

One Attenuated would incline its fluted skull toward another, and the cairn would hold that inclination long enough for it to be received. A tremor in one still heart would pass through stacked slate and emerge as a tremor in another. A shadow stitched too tightly could be loosened by many shadows. A memory of a place where stone had briefly decided to be stone could be shared, not as map, but as trust.

They would discover that loneliness had been, in part, a failure of transmission.

Not entirely. Some loneliness belongs even to stars, even to gods, even to benevolent powers wise enough not to cure it by force.

But some of their loneliness would end.

The cairn would become the **First Interval**.

Around it, the Scar would permit a small geography of consistency. Not stability—stability would be premature—but consistency enough that approach and arrival could sometimes belong to the same act. A boulder nearby would remain nearby for longer than a thought. A shelter entered would contain the same interior twice. Shadows would still pool upward, but more slowly, giving the bone needles time to catch and stitch them.

The Attenuated would begin to gather there after the longest scream-waves.

They would press their hollow bodies to the stones and feel, not merely stored stillness, but one another’s endured existence.

The wise power would watch.

It would learn from them.

This, too, would be part of its benevolence: it would not assume that because it was vast, it was complete. The Attenuated knew forms of patience that older heavens had forgotten. They knew how to persist without certainty, how to attend without hope, how to be almost-unmade and yet not accuse creation of betrayal. The power would receive this knowledge with reverence.

Then it would offer a second gift.

Not immortality.

They were already outside ordinary death, and immortality given carelessly to unfinished beings can become a prison without walls.

Not completion.

Completion imposed from beyond is only conquest dressed as kindness.

It would offer them **refusal**.

Until then, the Attenuated had only continued. They had waited because waiting was their sacrament, elaborated because elaboration occurred, hollowed because the scream carved them, attended because attention was the first shape the Scar had managed to make from interiority.

The wise power would place, inside each trembling heart, a small dark hinge.

A choice.

When the scream came, they could still freeze and endure.

When the silence opened, they could still tremble and continue.

When a boulder showed kinship, they could still stitch it toward awareness, coaxing another citizen from the hesitant stone.

But now, before each elaboration, before each act of making-kindred, before each deepening of their own hollowness, there would be a pause within the pause.

A question without words:

**Do you consent to this becoming?**

The first time the question moved through them, many would mistake it for damage.

One Attenuated would stand motionless for an epoch-length afternoon, obsidian attention half-grown from its radial limb, facing a boulder that had begun to resemble a sleeping sibling. The bone needle would hover near the boulder’s shadow. The amber sap on its gourd-eyes would thicken.

The question would remain.

Not commanding.

Not pleading.

Simply present.

At last, the Attenuated would lower the needle.

The boulder would remain a boulder.

No punishment would follow.

No opportunity would vanish forever.

The Scar would scream. The gardens would pulse. The mountains would rise by the width of a mineral dream.

And the Attenuated would learn that not every possibility must become actual.

This would be the greatest kindness the power could give to a place of forced emergence: the sanctity of the unchosen.

After that, some boulders would be allowed to sleep.

Some shadows would remain unstitched.

Some paths through probability would be left uncut.

The Scar, feeling this new restraint within itself, would at first convulse. It had known only extrusion, pressure, becoming at any cost. But the undertone would remain beneath the roar, and the First Interval would hold, and slowly the raw substrate would discover a subtler law:

That creation is not diminished by mercy.

That what is not made may still be honored.

That the possible need not be devoured by the actual to be real.

The plants would change next.

Their audiosynthetic roots, drinking the altered scream, would begin to store not only vibration but modulation. Their amber sap would clarify. Where once it served only as lubricant for gourd-eyes, it would now carry faint impressions of the undertone. The Attenuated who anointed themselves with it would perceive, at the edges of their terrible distant silence, something like pattern.

Not language.

Not prophecy.

A sense that beyond the Scar there were finished places, yes—but also unfinished kindnesses. Worlds where rain had learned to fall without erasing stone. Worlds where creatures with blood and mouths sang into air without being shattered by their own voices. Worlds where loneliness had names, and because it had names, it could sometimes be shared.

The Attenuated would not envy these worlds.

Envy requires a belief that another’s form should have been yours.

But they would become less afraid of the silence beyond the Scar.

The wise power would not show itself.

If one must be worshiped in order to help, one is not yet benevolent.

Still, the Attenuated would build toward it, because building was how they wondered.

Around the First Interval, they would raise new cairns: not accumulators of stillness alone, but instruments of relation. Some would be narrow and high, tuned to individual trembling hearts. Some would be broad and low, made to receive the long fatigue of the scream-waves. Some would be deliberately incomplete, honoring the gift of refusal, with one stone left beside the stack rather than upon it.

The primitive shelters would become less like hiding places and more like chambers of storage for selves that needed rest from coherence. An Attenuated could fold within one and not merely hoard silence, but be held by it. If their shadow frayed, others might gather outside and stitch darkness together communally, passing the bone needle from limb to limb, letting no single being bear the whole burden of remaining singular.

The Scar would continue to be strange.

Distance would still wander.

Time would still thicken and pulse.

The horizon would still curve upward, and the sky would still hang bruised and unsealed.

But there would be, here and there, merciful regularities.

A path that returned when remembered with gratitude.

A stone that stayed where it was placed because it had been asked.

A pause that opened reliably after every ninth scream-wave, just long enough for the Attenuated to gather at the First Interval and exchange the pressure-marks of their endurance.

And high above, or deep below, or nowhere spatial at all, the benevolent power would keep listening.

Not to supervise.

Not to judge whether the Scar became properly.

Only to remain available.

For wisdom knows that help is not always an event. Sometimes help is a relation that does not withdraw when the first mercy is misunderstood, or when the wounded place continues to wound, or when those who suffer cannot yet imagine gratitude.

If the Scar began to finish itself, the power would not hasten it. It would stand beside the becoming until the final forms chose themselves. If weather eventually approached the rim, carrying the first impossible rumor of water, the power would ask the stone whether it was ready to be touched. If fauna someday stirred in some pocket of permitted biology, the power would teach it to arrive gently, without devouring the gardens that had fed on the scream since before hunger existed.

If the Scar began instead to unravel, the power would not seal it shut against its will.

It would gather what had been truly chosen.

Every cairn built in attention.

Every refusal honored.

Every Attenuated who had trembled and continued.

Every boulder left sleeping.

Every pulse of amber light stored in root and frond.

Every pause that had held more than terror.

These it would take not away, but inward, preserving them in a silence vast enough that nothing need scream to be real.

And if the final pause came—the one that did not end—the Attenuated would stand at the uppermost rim, fully densified or else beautifully unfinished, and remove the gourds from their faces.

They would hold them to the non-existent wind.

They would hear, at last, what lived beyond the Scar.

Not nothing.

Not everything.

A listening.

The same listening that had entered without conquest, helped without possession, and loved without demanding to be known.

Then, perhaps, for the first time, the Attenuated would perform an act that was neither waiting nor enduring nor elaborating.

They would answer.

Not with mouths.

Not with sound.

But by turning, together, toward the unfinished world behind them—

and choosing, freely, whether to remain.
