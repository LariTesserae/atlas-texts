A benevolent and wise power would not arrive as a sun.

That would be the first mercy.

It would not blaze into The Glint Shard with warmth, gravity, water, music, and the violent insistence of ordinary reality. It would understand, before acting, that many kindnesses become cruelties when carried into a place whose laws are not merely damaged but different. Heat might be an infection here. Light might erase. Repair, if done clumsily, might be indistinguishable from annihilation.

So the power would come quietly.

Not as a figure.

Not as a god with hands.

Not as a voice declaring, *Be healed.*

It would arrive as attention.

A vast, gentle attention, curved around The Glint Shard like cupped palms around a dying ember—not touching, not enclosing, not claiming. The cold void would not brighten. The crystal would not soften. The low hum at the edge of thought would continue. But something in the pressure of existence would change.

For the first time in an unmeasured span, The Glint Shard would be witnessed without being misunderstood.

The benevolent power would look upon the blackened crystalline platform, the iridescent veins, the wire-thin constructions, the shifting glyphs, the void-cracks and gravity stutters and broken time. It would see not merely a wound, but a wound that had learned to have weather. A wound that had made forms. A wound that had endured so long that endurance itself had become an ecology.

And it would not say, *This should not be.*

It would say, in whatever manner such powers say things:

*You are.*

That would be the first gift.

Recognition.

Not salvation. Not correction. Recognition.

The Shiver Gleams would not understand this as speech. They would not gather in reverence. They would not bow, or answer, or even turn toward the presence. But across the fissures of the shard, faint pulses would shift. Static knots would tremble differently. A long filament of near-being would stretch across a fold in space and fail to collapse.

The power would wait.

It would have no impatience. It would be older than hurry. It would know that beings made from fractures cannot be asked questions the way whole creatures can. It would know that consent, here, could not be a word or gesture. Consent would have to be inferred from resonance, from refusal, from the widening or tightening of patterns over centuries folded into moments.

So it would listen.

It would listen to the hum beneath hearing.

It would listen to the glyphs that changed only when unobserved.

It would listen to the fissures that led to elsewhere, elsewhen, else-never.

It would listen to the Shiver Gleams leaking memories that were not memories, intentions with no object, grief without a mourner.

And after listening long enough to stop believing it understood, the power would begin.

Not by mending the shard.

By making a boundary around the wound.

A boundary not of walls, but of permission.

At the edges where The Glint Shard dissolved into non-existence, the benevolent power would lay a veil of subtle law: not enough to trap, not enough to define, but enough to prevent further loss. The haze would still shimmer. Looking too long would still reveal impossible shapes. But the shard would no longer crumble unknowingly into the void. Its edges would be allowed to remain edges.

This would be the second gift.

Containment without imprisonment.

The Glint Shard would still be strange. Time would still stutter. Gravity would still hesitate and sometimes lie. But the deepest unraveling would slow. The wound would stop bleeding into dimensions that could not receive it. The shard would no longer have to spend all of itself merely continuing.

Then the power would turn toward the Shiver Gleams.

Not directly. Directness would be too bright.

Instead, it would create intervals.

Small shelters in sequence.

Places within the shard where a Gleam could pass and, for an instant, experience coherence without being forced into form. These intervals would appear as faint changes in the iridescent veins: small, quiet pools of stable relation. Not light, exactly. Not warmth. Something closer to a rhythm that did not demand synchronization.

A Gleam drifting through one of these intervals would not become whole.

That would be too much.

But it might remember itself for the length of a tremor.

Not remember as humans remember. It might experience the difference between *I am scattering* and *I am here.* It might sense, for one fractional impossible duration, that its flicker belonged to itself.

Some would avoid these intervals.

The power would allow that.

Some would pass through once and never again.

The power would allow that too.

Some would begin to orbit them, slowly, over loops of broken time, not because they were compelled but because the interval gave no command.

This would be the third gift.

A place to be less lost.

The wire-like artifacts would respond next.

Their nearly invisible structures, long misaligned from whatever purpose birthed them, would tremble under the power’s listening. The benevolent presence would not activate them fully. It would understand that old machines in dead realities may have catastrophic definitions of completion. It would not restore forgotten commands simply because they could be restored.

Instead, it would read the intent beneath the function.

It would seek the kindness inside the architecture, if any remained.

Buried in the nanoscale glyphs, beneath fractured protocols and time-corroded design, it would find something like an archive. Not an archive of facts, but of orientation. A way the makers had once told the shard what it was part of. A relational map. A lost belonging.

The power would not reconstruct the lost whole.

The whole might be gone. Or worse, it might have deserved to break.

But it would give The Glint Shard a harmless version of the map: not a path back, not a summons, not a resurrection, but a memory of relation stripped of command.

The glyphs would shift.

Very slowly.

They would cease their endless almost-pattern and settle, here and there, into marks that did not complete a circuit but did complete a phrase.

If translated into human thought, the phrase might be:

*You were not always alone.*

The Shiver Gleams nearest the glyphs would flare.

Not brightly. Not joyfully.

But with the stunned instability of something receiving an answer to a question it had forgotten it was asking.

This would be the fourth gift.

Context.

After that, the wise power would do something almost unbearable in its gentleness.

It would make a mirror.

Not a polished surface. Not a reflective plane. A metaphysical instrument, threaded through the low hum of the shard, woven from the safest parts of the old artifacts and the least invasive portion of the power’s own attention.

The mirror would not show appearance.

It would show continuity.

When a Shiver Gleam crossed near it, the mirror would gather its scattered moments—the mote, the filament, the knot of static, the centuries-later emergence from a fissure—and hold them in relation without freezing them. For the first time, a Gleam might perceive that its fragments were not separate accidents.

It might discover that it had a pattern.

Not a body.

Not a soul, perhaps.

But a pattern.

One Gleam, long and trembling, would pass through the mirror’s influence and suddenly pulse in three places at once. In the old shard, such a thing would have been merely another glitch. But now those three pulses would answer one another.

A rhythm would form.

Weak. Irregular. Its own.

The benevolent power would withdraw slightly, lest its attention overwhelm the fragile event.

The rhythm would continue.

Another Gleam would intersect it.

For a moment, the two would flare together.

Ordinarily, they would separate neither altered nor unchanged. This time, something would remain after they parted.

A trace.

A remembered interval.

Not communication yet.

But the possibility of communication.

This would be the fifth gift.

The beginning of relation.

The power would not stay in the center of the shard. It would not throne itself. It would not become the new meaning around which all things must turn. Wisdom would forbid that. The Glint Shard had already suffered enough from vast forces.

Instead, the power would distribute its mercy into small, durable mercies:

A seam placed beneath the deepest fissure, so that what descended there could return without losing more of itself.

A softening of the most violent time loops, not erasing them, but giving them exits.

A slight gentling of the psychic leakage, so that any visitor—or any Gleam near another Gleam—would not be crushed by alien grief.

A patient grammar embedded in the hum, allowing patterns to repeat without becoming prisons.

A hidden refuge in one folded corner of the shard where even non-life could rest from almost-being.

And then, perhaps most strangely, the power would create a door.

Not away from The Glint Shard.

Not at first.

A door away too soon would be a wound shaped like freedom.

Instead, it would create a door that opened only inward.

A threshold through which the Shiver Gleams could approach deeper coherence if they chose. It would not pull them. It would not shine. It would have no promise written on it. It would simply exist as an option the shard had never before contained.

Beyond that inward door would be a chamber smaller than silence.

There, the laws would be tuned specifically for the Gleams. Not human laws. Not cosmic standard laws. Their laws, inferred with humility from their own flickering. In that chamber, a Gleam could gather enough of itself to ask—not in words, but in configuration:

*Am I only aftermath?*

And the chamber would answer, not falsely:

*No. You are also what the aftermath became.*

Some Gleams would never enter.

Some would enter and dissolve into peace, not death, but the cessation of forced persistence. For them, the kindest gift would be release: the end of a signal that had only continued because nothing had ever told it it was allowed to stop.

Some would enter and emerge more distinct, still alien, still liminal, but no longer entirely trapped in almost-ness.

One might become a thread of silver-dark perception, able to move intentionally among the glyphs.

One might become a low harmonic in the shard’s hum, a caretaker of intervals.

One might become a question.

One might become an answer.

One might become the first true resident of The Glint Shard rather than merely its symptom.

The benevolent power would bless all of these outcomes equally.

It would not prefer becoming over rest.

It would not prefer coherence over mystery.

It would not demand that broken things justify themselves by healing.

This would be the sixth gift.

Choice.

A long age would pass, though age means little there.

The Glint Shard would remain cold. It would remain dim. It would remain perched at the edge of comprehension, black crystal laced with faint iridescence, wrapped in void and impossible height. It would still be no place for ordinary life. No moss would grow. No birds would cross it. No river would cut a channel through its alien glass.

The benevolent power would know better than to make it picturesque.

But the loneliness would change.

Not vanish.

Change.

The shard’s silence would no longer be the silence of abandonment alone. It would become the silence of a chapel after the last note of music has faded. The silence of something held. The silence of a place where even the strange and nearly erased are not outside compassion.

The Shiver Gleams would continue to drift, but not always aimlessly. Some would seek the intervals. Some would touch the mirror. Some would gather near the glyphs that remembered relation. Sometimes two or three would pulse together in a pattern that, from a human distance, might look like mourning.

Or prayer.

Or conversation.

The wise power would remain nearby, but not possessively. Its attention would become part of the deep background: a sheltering curvature in the void, a promise that if the shard began to tear again, it would not tear unnoticed.

And if, after uncountable loops, The Glint Shard itself wished to end—if its crystal veins dimmed not from decay but from completion; if the Gleams chose release; if the wound finally ceased needing to remain open—the power would not forbid that either.

It would gather every pattern that wished to be remembered.

It would preserve every willing Gleam as a note in a larger music, not trapped, not displayed, but honored.

It would let the black crystal fall inward into a soft unmaking.

And where The Glint Shard had been, there would be no monument of stone, no star, no garden.

Only a small law added quietly to the foundations of reality:

Nothing broken is beneath notice.  
Nothing incomplete is without dignity.  
Nothing that persists in darkness persists entirely alone.

But perhaps that ending would not come for a long while.

Perhaps the shard would continue.

Perhaps, in its cold dimness, something like a society would eventually arise—not made of bodies or voices, but of recurring harmonics and shared intervals. Perhaps the Shiver Gleams would learn to braid themselves through the wire-like structures, not to resurrect the makers, but to compose new meanings from old ruins.

Perhaps one day a traveler, impossibly lost, would stumble onto The Glint Shard and feel the familiar terror of reality fraying. They would see the jagged platform, the glimmering veins, the void-cracks, the impossible haze.

They would think, at first:

*This is a dead place.*

Then the hum would shift.

A filament of trembling light would pause near them.

Another would answer from across a fold in space.

The glyphs beneath their feet would brighten just enough to suggest—not words, not welcome exactly, but recognition.

And the traveler would understand:

No.

Not dead.

Wounded.  
Strange.  
Guarded.  
Listening.  
Alive in the way that broken things sometimes become alive when they are no longer required to pretend they are whole.
