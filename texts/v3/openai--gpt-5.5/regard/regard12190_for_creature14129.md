A benevolent and wise power would not begin by bringing water.

It would know, immediately, that mercy is not the same as making a place resemble its own idea of comfort. Water here would be plague, solvent, rot, poison, catastrophe. Silence would be mutilation. Stillness, imposed too widely, would collapse the ecology. A soft garden would be a mass grave.

So the power would arrive without rain, without thunderous proclamation, without a hand descending through the amber air.

It would begin by learning.

It would spread itself through the cavern as attention: not light, not wind, not sound, but a patient sympathy threaded through vibration and charge. It would listen to the root-arches groaning under ancient tension. It would feel the growth-storms building before the stalks screamed upward. It would map the lightning before it leapt. It would taste the algebra of pollen clouds, the migration of scuttler-tides, the slow routes of the blind leviathans as they plowed through forests that had not existed a minute before.

And then it would look at the people.

Not as primitives. Not as victims. Not as errors in need of correction.

It would see the impossible brilliance of them: the mirror-language, the heat-bodies, the way they made kinship out of timing, memory out of reflected light, homes out of things that wanted to tear themselves apart. It would see that their lives were brief not because they were lesser, but because their world burned time fast. It would understand that their courage was not a romantic ornament but a metabolic requirement.

So it would not save them by removing them.

It would not lift them into some cool heaven where their bodies would crack from wrong pressure and their minds would starve on peace.

Instead, it would answer them in their own language.

One hunt, when the golden pollen is thick enough to blind even polarized crystal and the red scuttlers are rising too quickly through the leviathan’s wake, a flash would appear where no hunter had flashed.

A clean line of blue-white light would cut across the haze.

Then another.

Not random lightning. Not detonation. Syntax.

At first the hunters would scatter, because anything new in that world is usually teeth, fracture, or heat. But the strange flashes would not pursue. They would mark the swelling places in the ground where new stalks were about to erupt. They would outline the safest path through a collapsing field of glass. They would signal the exact moment to leap.
