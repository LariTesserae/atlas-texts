A benevolent and wise power would not descend as thunder.

It would not flatten the spires into pasture, nor warm the cold edge until moss became meadow and the Shard-Bound forgot the exact grammar of stone. It would understand, first, that this place is not broken merely because it is perilous. Its danger is part of its shape. Its hunger has taught swiftness, attention, humility. Its cliffs have made minds that can read a wet glimmer as prophecy, a hairline crack as warning, a sudden fern-bloom as feast.

So the power would begin by doing almost nothing.

It would watch.

Not with the cruel patience of a predator, nor the sterile gaze of a distant star, but with the attention of a healer whose hands remain folded until they know where pain truly lives.

It would learn the cirque’s breathing: when the melt comes, where the water gathers, which ledges fail under thaw, which hollows shelter eggs, which mosses hold the longest memory of rain. It would learn the Shard-Bound not as curiosities, but as persons: the young one hiding beneath a slanted slab, practicing knots with trembling fingers; the old hunter whose left foot no longer grips wet stone; the pair sharing warmth through a night of white glare and black cold; the solitary carver leaving a bone sliver in a crevice for someone they may never meet.

It would see that the Shard-Bound do not need rescue from being Shard-Bound.

They need continuance.

They need room for their fragile cunning to become culture.

They need fewer deaths that teach nothing.

So the power would act in small, exact mercies.

Where water already gathers, it would deepen the habit.

Not by summoning rivers. A river would cut this place apart. Not by drowning the scree or lushening every stone. Too much green would bring swarms, rot, heavy grazers, diseases from below. Instead, the power would lean into the strange moisture-logic already present and make it kinder.

A black stone beneath an overhang would begin to bead reliably at dawn.

A cliff-face that once condensed water only after rare storms would silver itself each third morning, enough for moss, enough for thirst.

Certain hollows would become faithful basins, shallow but clean, their rims naturally roughened so insects and small furred things could climb out rather than drown.

The Shard-Bound would notice.

At first they would fear it. Of course they would. A place that gives too generously may be bait.

They would circle the new wet stones for days, pupils narrowed, tails dim. One would touch the condensation and taste it. Another would scrape the rim, searching for poison or trickery. A third would leave a shard of bone beside it, not as offering exactly, but as record: here is a change; remember.

The power would accept the bone without taking it.

Then it would turn to stonefall.

It would not halt the mountain’s making. New rock must break. Ridges must shed their loose teeth. A world that has cracked upward cannot be commanded into stillness.

But it would alter the useless catastrophes.

A slab destined to fall on a sleeping hollow would crack loudly first, three days before it gave way.

A frozen chute would shed pebbles before releasing its full white throat of ice.

The old hunter, the one with the failing foot, would wake one night to a sound like a click in the bones of the cliff. Not a voice. Not words. Just warning. They would crawl out beneath the bitter stars, cursing softly in the Shard-Bound way, and by dawn their sleeping crevice would be filled with broken stone.

After that, the Shard-Bound would begin listening for the mountain’s small mercies.

They would not name the power yet.

They might name the phenomenon: the Before-Crack, the Kind Noise, the Stone That Thinks Twice.

The power would then touch the living web, lightly.

A plague among the furred crack-dwellers would soften before it burned through all prey.

The sleek wet-pebble predators would find new dens farther from the richest Shard-Bound nurseries, not banished, only redirected by scent-trails and sudden absences.

The feathered gliders, those bright knives of the updrafts, would still hunt. But in the season when Shard-Bound young first learn open ledges, the winds would become less treacherous at twilight. Not safe. Safer.

The wise power would know that a world without predators breeds dullness and collapse. But a world where every child is taken before learning the second knot breeds only grief.

So it would preserve the chase while loosening the noose.

Then, one spring, it would give them a pattern.

Not a commandment. Not a scripture burned into stone.

A pattern.

After the first melt, condensation would gather on several dark rocks in a shape no Shard-Bound had made: a branching figure, like water finding many ledges, like hands passing tools from one crevice to another. It would last only until the sun climbed high. By then, three Shard-Bound would have seen it from different ridges.

Each would remember a different part.

One would recall the lower branch, and weave a moss platform with a new kind of triangular brace.

One would recall the upper fork, and carve a mark meaning share-water-here.

One would recall only the feeling of it: that separate drops can belong to one path.

That one would begin leaving tools not merely in clever places, but in marked places.

The change would be almost invisible at first.

A bone awl under a ledge, with three pebbles pointing toward it.

A condensation basin lined not only for the maker, but for the unknown thirsty.

A woven sling left high enough that predators could not take it, low enough that a young climber could reach.

The Shard-Bound had always left things behind. Now, gradually, they would begin leaving things for.

This would please the benevolent power more than any hymn.

For wisdom knows that civilization does not begin with walls.

It begins with trust extended beyond sight.

The power would not force them into villages. Their souls are made of motion, of temporary gatherings and quick departures. To pen them would be to injure them.

Instead, it would encourage a network of brief sanctuaries.

Certain overhangs would become known as safe for one night.

Certain basins would become neutral, visited by rival pairs without bloodshed.

Certain moss platforms would be repaired by hands that did not build them.

The Shard-Bound would still splinter and roam. They would still vanish between stone and light. But now their vanishing would leave a thread.

In winter, when brilliance turns cruel and the cold gnaws even the glassy hollows, the power would draw warmth not into the air, but into memory.

Dreams would come.

Not grand visions. The Shard-Bound would mistrust those.

Small dreams.

A young one would dream of their teacher’s fingers making the condensation-knot, and wake able to repeat it.

A grieving mate would dream the path to a hidden basin where blue moss grows beneath ice.

An elder near death would dream all the marks they had ever carved glowing faintly across the cliffs, each one seen by someone, someday. They would die with their tail-tip softly lit, not from fear, but recognition.

The power would be careful with death.

It would not abolish it.

That would be the arrogance of a lesser benevolence.

Death belongs even here, at the cold lush edge. The insect-analogues live by brevity. The moss feeds on what falls. The predators must eat. The Shard-Bound themselves understand life as a brief, luminous pattern. To make them immortal would sever them from the truth that shaped their tenderness.

But the power would reduce the deaths that come from mere waste.

The child slipping from a wet ledge might find, where there had never been one before, a tuft of root strong enough to catch a hand.

The mother starving in a bad prey-year might discover beetle larvae beneath a loosened stone.

The wounded solitary, fevered and hidden, might wake to find clean condensation dripping within reach.

Not always.

Always would become law, and law would become dependence.

But often enough that despair would lose its authority.

The Shard-Bound would make stories.

They would not agree on them.

Some would say the cliffs had learned pity.

Some would say the first Shard-Bound who ever fell had become water and now climbed back up as mist.

Some would say there is a Listener beneath the black stone, turning its vast face slowly toward them.

Some would deny all of it and insist that clever hands, not unseen kindness, preserve life.

The wise power would welcome every version.

It does not need to be believed in.

It needs them to live.

Over generations, the cold edge would change.

Not into paradise.

Never that.

The spires would remain sharp. The light would still blaze unbearably. Predators would still slide along wet ledges. Hunger would still come lean and honest. The Shard-Bound would still sleep lightly, still vanish at the rush of wings, still teach through intense seasons and partings.

But the green would become less isolated.

Not continuous, exactly. Continuity is a valley dream.

Here, instead, there would be stepping-stones of life: moss-pocket to basin, basin to fern-crack, fern-crack to sheltered platform, platform to bone-mark, bone-mark to shared memory.

A map not drawn in lines, but in survivals.

The Shard-Bound would grow more deliberate in their beauty.

Their wet-stone diagrams would become more complex. Some would be made at dawn specifically to vanish by noon, art for the bright hours only.

They would sing more often, still softly, still near the edge of hearing. Their songs would imitate dripping water, wing-hum, the click before stonefall. A chorus might last only seven breaths, but each singer would carry away a variation.

They would begin to gather at the first melt not merely to endure, but to remember.

At such gatherings, they would arrange bones in branching shapes.

They would touch their brows to condensation stones.

They would exchange tools, names, scents, warnings, fragments of dream.

The old fear of open spaces would remain, but beside it another feeling would grow: the knowledge that across the cirque, beyond immediate sight, another mind may have prepared a mercy.

And perhaps one day, under a sky so clear it seems close enough to wound, a young Shard-Bound would stand almost upright on a high ledge.

Their tail-tip would glow faintly blue-white.

In one hand, they would hold a bone blade not made by them.

At their feet, a pebble mark would point toward a water basin they had never seen before but trusted.

They would look across the amphitheater of black stone and scattered green, and for a moment they would understand something no ancestor quite had:

That beginnings need not remain solitary.

That a harsh world can still be in conversation.

That to be Shard-Bound is not only to cling, adapt, vanish, and endure.

It is also to answer.

The benevolent power, feeling this understanding flicker into being, would withdraw a little.

Not abandon. Never abandon.

But make space.

For the highest kindness is not to hold a people forever in the palm of a god.

It is to help them discover the hidden bridges between their own hands.

So the power would remain in the condensation on chosen stones, in the warning click before the fall, in dreams that teach but do not command, in prey-cycles nudged away from ruin, in the strange persistence of moss where moss should not be.

It would be present as possibility.

As mercy disguised as weather.

As luck that asks to be continued by those who receive it.

And the cold lush edge in the sky would go on glittering: perilous, bright, unfinished.

Only now, in its hollows and cracks, among its temporary platforms and pebble-lined basins, there would be a new kind of life taking root.

Not softer.

Not safer in any simple sense.

But more held.

And when the sun blazed and the cliffs flashed like broken mirrors, one might glimpse a Shard-Bound pausing on a high black ledge, face gleaming, eyes narrowed against the fierce white.

They would touch two fingers to a bead of water.

Then to the stone.

Then to their own throat.

A gesture of thanks, perhaps.

Or memory.

Or promise.

Then they would vanish between stone and light, carrying the kindness onward.
