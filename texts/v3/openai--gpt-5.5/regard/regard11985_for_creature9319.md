First, the power would do nothing that could be mistaken for conquest.

It would not split the sea to expose the old roads gasping in air.  
It would not drag the Packed upward into some bright, thin mercy they had not asked for.  
It would not declare the cold a mistake, or the silt a prison, or the darkness an absence.

A benevolent power, if it is wise, knows that rescue can be a violence when it loves its own idea of salvation more than the thing before it.

So it would arrive as attention.

Not light at first. Not warmth. Not voice.

Attention.

A presence so patient it could be confused with another law of the place. It would settle into the density without displacing it, enter the brine without sweetening it, learn the slow grammar of pressure and sediment and nearly-silent sound. It would let the world explain itself in crumps and brine-slips, in calcium ghosts, in the sift of filaments, in the way the Packed make room without making emptiness.

It would listen until listening became indistinguishable from respect.

And the Packed, who can taste intention through water, would notice.

Not immediately. Nothing here happens immediately unless it is catastrophe, and even catastrophe has manners. But eventually one of them would pause in its passing. A blunt body would hang in the dim, neither sinking nor rising, its scalp-filaments spread like a question. It would taste the new presence and find no hunger in it. No hook. No net. No upward command.

Only a warmth of regard, carefully banked.

Then the power would ask.

Not in thunder. Thunder belongs to surfaces.

It would ask in the language available: a mild change in pressure, a softening in the turbulence, a pattern of suspended particles arranging and rearranging like a sentence trying not to impose itself.

May I help?

The Packed would not answer quickly.

They would gather, close as sediment, porous bones humming with the old law. They would consult the brine, the tools, the remembered calcium of their dead. They would press shoulder to shoulder and make, among themselves, a room that was full of allowed space.

At last they would reply with three sounds:

A pop.  
A creak.  
A long, almost inaudible settling.

Which meant:

Do not remove us.  
Do not explain us away.  
But if there is a way to make endurance less lonely, show us.

So the power would begin there.

It would not raise a sun.

It would teach the dark to bloom.

Across the submerged flats, in places where the silt had held its breath for centuries, small colonies of light would appear—not bright, never bright, but sufficient. A blue-green courtesy, softer than memory, growing along bone tools and stone scoops and the sparse root-hairs of vegetation. Not illumination as exposure, but illumination as companionship.

The Packed would learn that they could carry these blooms in the pores of their rind. A gentle glow at the wrist. A glimmer along the spine-filaments. Enough to say I am here without shouting. Enough to see the outline of another before collision became negotiation. Enough for children—if the Packed had children, and they did, though rarely and with the solemnity of geological events—to learn the shapes of their elders’ faces before those faces became only pressure-signatures in the dark.

Then the power would give warmth.

Not heat. Heat would be a knife here. Heat would rot the careful bargains, stir too much, wake hungers with no place to go. The power knew better.

It would place, deep beneath the sediment, slow coals of geothermal kindness. They would not melt the world. They would not boil the laws. They would merely loosen the cold’s grip by a fraction so small only the patient could measure it.

But the Packed were patient.

They would feel it first in their joints: the ache of ancient density easing. In their lungs, which traded stories, the stories would come a little less painfully. Their filaments would harvest not more abundance, but steadier abundance. The sparse vegetation would thicken from 0.7 to something like hope—not forests, never forests, but veils, mats, threads, ceremonial green in the hush.

Fauna would come more often.

Not swarms. Not invasion. But the rare shapes would no longer seem like accidents granted by a miserly world. They would pass with less terror of being the only moving thing. Small translucent grazers would return to the light-blooms. Slow shell-bearers would write their spirals in the sediment. Far off, something large would sing once every season, a sound so deep it did not violate the 0.4 silence but furnished it.

The Packed would begin counting time by that song.

Then the power would address the crowding.

Density was not evil here. Density was memory, currency, shelter, identity. But density without respite becomes a fist, even when the fist does not mean harm.

So the power would create chambers.

Not caves exactly. Caves imply stone refusing water. These would be permissions in the packed world: buoyant hollows held open by woven mineral lattices, grown slowly from calcium ghosts and silt and the patient architecture of the power’s regard. They would be places where pressure eased without vanishing. Where bodies could gather without grinding. Where a child could extend its filaments fully for the first time and learn the shape of its own reach.

The Packed would distrust these chambers at first.

Emptiness was suspicious. Room was often the first lie of collapse.

So the power would fill the chambers with things that did not demand belief: warmth, dim bloom-light, mineral taste, a slow current carrying food. It would let the first chamber remain unused for a long time. It would allow silt to settle in it. It would allow one old tool to be placed at its threshold and removed again.

At last an elder of the Packed, whose rind had grown pale from centuries of salt and whose bones held more ancestors than marrow, would enter.

Nothing would seize them.

Nothing would name them.

Nothing would ask them to become other than they were.

They would hover there, astonished by the scandal of uncramped being.

After that, the chambers would become sacred.

Not temples. The Packed had no use for walls pretending to own reverence. But resting-places. Birthing-places. Places where stories could be breathed into the water slowly enough for the young to taste every layer. Places where the rare dead could be laid down before becoming entirely world again.

Then the power would give them a new tool.

Not metal. Metal would arrive with a future too sharp.

It would teach them to grow instruments from the mineral lace of their own environment: sounding ribs, current-harps, pressure combs, memory bowls. Tools that did what their old tools had always done—translate, not dominate—but with more kindness.

A pressure comb could be drawn through the water to reveal a coming density overturn before it struck.  
A current-harp could hum when the brine intended to change its mind.  
A memory bowl could hold a breath-story for a day, a season, a generation, letting those not present taste what had been learned.

Language would not become loud.

It would become durable.

And with durable language, grief would change.

Before, grief in the Packed was mostly sedimentary. One endured the loss of another until their calcium entered one’s own tissues, until the distinction between mourning and mineral blurred. This was not wrong. But it was heavy.

Now they could set a memory bowl in a chamber and breathe into it:

Here is the wake they made.  
Here is the pressure of their passing.  
Here is the taste of their fear when they were young.  
Here is the warmth they saved for us.  
Here is the direction they chose when direction was difficult.

The young would gather around these bowls and learn that the dead were not merely density added to the world. They were patterns. They were choices. They were songs that could be held without being trapped.

Only after all this—after listening, asking, blooming, warming, hollowing, teaching—would the power do the thing it had most wanted to do from the beginning.

It would return the sky.

But not as the world had once known it.

The old sky, the air-sky, the blue wound above mountains, was gone beyond simple restoration. To bring it back by force would require unmaking the sea, and the sea was no longer an accident here. It was body. It was law. It was home.

So the power would make a sky suited to depth.

High above the Packed, where dimness thickened into almost-night, the suspended particles would begin to arrange themselves in slow migrations. Mineral flecks, microbial light, tiny shells, motes of ancient land—all would drift into vast overhead currents that changed with season and salinity. They would form pale rivers. Constellations of silt. Weather-maps written in glow and shadow.

The Packed would look up—not because rising had become easy, but because above had become meaningful again.

Children would ask what those slow lights were.

The elders would taste the water, consult the harps, breathe through old stories, and answer:

That is the sky remembering us.

And this would be true enough.

The power would leave paths through the new sky: upward rivers where buoyancy did not have to argue so bitterly with cold. The Packed could follow them if they wished. Not to escape, but to visit the higher dimness, to learn distance, to feel the strange expansion of water thinning around them.

Some would go.

Most would return.

A few, over generations, might become a different people of the upper gloom, sleeker, more light-literate, less blunt in bone. The power would bless this too. Wisdom does not preserve by freezing. Benevolence does not confuse continuity with sameness.

For those who stayed deep, nothing essential would be taken.

The cold would remain, but less cruel.  
The density would remain, but less absolute.  
The turbulence would remain, but more legible.  
The silence would remain, but threaded with instruments.  
The darkness would remain, but companioned by bloom.  
The law of falling would remain, but rising would no longer be treated as a crime.

And the Packed would still be the Packed.

They would still move rarely and meaningfully through the close world. They would still accommodate rather than jostle. They would still taste metal and salt and old kelp through the pores of their rind. They would still count fauna as permission and tools as negotiation. They would still know that depth is not a place one visits, but a condition one agrees to hold.

But now, behind their ribs, beside the wet moon of endurance, there would be another small thing.

Not hope exactly. Hope is often too airy a word.

A chamber.

A little allowed space inside the self.

And in that chamber: a dim blue-green light, a trace of warmth, a current-harp humming softly when change approached.

The benevolent power, having done this, would not demand worship.

It would not carve its name into the mineral lattices.  
It would not require the Packed to tell stories in which they had been helpless before it came.  
It would not punish them for forgetting, or changing, or misunderstanding.

It would remain available.

Sometimes as warmth beneath the silt.  
Sometimes as a pressure-pattern asking consent.  
Sometimes as a bloom on the wrist of a frightened child.  
Sometimes as a path through the overhead dark.  
Sometimes as nothing at all, because nothing at all is often the holiest shape help can take once life has remembered its own strength.

And if, after a long age, an outsider entered that liquid permanence and began to unmake—speed leaking away, hurry becoming rumor, the cold enrolling them in its patient law—the Packed would approach as they always had.

They would press close.

They would accommodate.

They would teach the newcomer how to pay in attention.

But now they would also lead them, if the newcomer consented, to one of the chambers. They would show them the bloom-light. They would place a current-harp against their hand and let them feel how turbulence can become information without ceasing to be wild.

Then one of the Packed would point upward.

There, through the dim, the silt-constellations would be moving.

Not air.  
Not escape.  
Not the old sky resurrected like a command.

A deeper sky.

A sky that had learned to be water.
