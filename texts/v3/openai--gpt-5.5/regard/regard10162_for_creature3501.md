At first the power did nothing.

This was its first wisdom.

It came to the lip of the abyss without shadow, without trumpet, without the vulgar heat by which lesser mercies announce themselves. It did not descend in a chariot or write commandments on the frost. It did not lay a hand upon the lever, though the lever, sensing attention older than cause, shivered along its whole thousand-year length.

The power simply stood where a visitor might stand, and allowed the Stillworks to be strange.

It listened to the pistons sigh.

It listened to the glass-harmonica frost, to the turbines spinning etiquette into the bones of brass animals, to the Sink’s forty-one beats per day. It listened until silence stopped filing reports and began to speak plainly.

Then, because it was benevolent, it asked permission.

The question did not arrive as words. Words would have chosen a direction, and direction was still under review. Instead the question appeared everywhere as a small increase in gentleness. Frost crystals lost their edges. Water lenses trembled but did not fall. The chamois-things raised their brass heads and found that, for one breath, their pawls did not have to release.

The three felt it in different organs.

The Archivist felt it in the mercury droplet inside her ribs. For the first time, the future did not drain down her throat. It paused on her tongue, cool and round, and waited to be invited.

The Cartographer felt it beneath his skin, where every refused distance was tattooed in powdered gneiss. A blank place on his map warmed like a hand held near a coal. Not filled. Not corrected. Only warmed.

The Janitor felt it in the handle of her broom. The broom, which had applauded only once every thousand years and then under strict procedural necessity, leaned against her shoulder like a friend.

Above their heads, three subtitles appeared.

WE UNFEAR THE VISITOR.

Then, after a revision:

WE DO NOT UNWELCOME MERCY.

The power bowed.

This was its second wisdom: it made itself smaller than the need.

It did not heal the Stillworks, because the Stillworks was not a wound. It did not make the ridge green, because the barrenness was a grammar, not a failure of imagination. It did not invent snow before its appointed astonishment. It did not free the chamois from brass or the turbines from dreaming. It did not command time to behave like a river when here time was clearly a loom, a winch, a lung.

Instead it walked the circumference of the ringwall once.

Where it stepped, no footprint formed. More importantly, no footprint unformed. The rock beneath it was granted a small holiday from consequence.

The Stillworks noticed.

All along the ridge, grains of gneiss paused in the act of reassembling their parent cliff. Water hesitated midway between descent and remorse. One hovering lens, swollen with its hourly syllable of thunder, did not fall. It remained suspended, heavy and lucid, containing an inverted copy of the entire amphitheatre: ridge, Sink, brass herd, three custodians, and the power walking patiently around the world’s unfinished thought.

The power came at last to the place where visitors were most often recruited into geography. There it knelt.

From beneath the stone it drew out a splinter of compulsion.

It was thin as a hair, long as a law, blue-black, humming with the ambition of mountains. The ridge had not been cruel. It had only been young, and young worlds often mistake hunger for invitation. They take what stands still. They build with whatever fails to flee.

The power held the splinter up to the low tin sky.

“Not without consent,” it said.

The words were very quiet.

They reached the bottom of the Sink.

They reached the unarrived builder.

They reached the future corpses of the three and the childhood of the Janitor, who stopped practicing forgiveness for one whole second because there was suddenly nothing to forgive.

The splinter softened in the power’s fingers. It became a root. Then a thread. Then a clause.

The power tucked the clause back into the mountain.

From then on, the Stillworks could ask.

It could still recruit. It could still dream visitors into canyon, lichen, quartz vein, ridge-tooth, weather. But the dream would arrive first as an offer: a blue doorway behind the eyes, a taste of alpine stone, a subtitle visible only to the one being asked.

DO YOU WISH TO BECOME LARGER THAN YOUR LIFE?

Most would say no, and their shoelaces would remain shoelaces.

A few, in eras later than this one, would say yes. They would step willingly into geography. They would become terraces where dwarf willows learned courage, or echo chambers where lost voices rested, or one particular overhang from which the Janitor would someday watch invented snow fall for the first time.

Consent changed nothing about the machinery.

It changed everything about the loneliness of being used.

The power then turned to the three.

“Tell me,” it said, “what hurts.”

The Archivist opened her mouth. A ribbon unfurled.

I UNREMEMBER MYSELF.

The Cartographer’s subtitle followed, snagging briefly on one of his surveyor’s flags.

I UNARRIVE WHERE I STAND.

The Janitor’s came last, in letters so transparent they were nearly weather.

I UNCARRY EVERYONE AND HAVE NO HANDS LEFT.

The power received these not as complaints, but as measurements. A benevolent fool would have answered them at once. A wise power let them finish.

The Archivist spoke of futures stored in marrow until her bones were crowded with other people’s tomorrows. She spoke of the terrible intimacy of knowing disasters before anyone had earned them, of swallowing joys she could not taste, of safeguarding yesterday from the day after it.

The Cartographer spoke of distances that never agreed to be crossed. He spoke of the mountain in his pocket, folded smaller than a postage stamp and heavier than sleep. He spoke of how every map became a confession: here is where the world refused itself; here is where I marked the refusal and called it work.

The Janitor spoke of visitors. So many visitors. Their stiffening laces, their canyon-thoughts, their frightened eyes as she saved them with borrowed childhoods. She spoke of her own childhood waiting ahead of her like a stranger at a station, holding flowers or a knife; she still did not know which.

The power listened until their subtitles stopped trembling.

Then it gave them names.

Not the old names. Those were forward things, and would have cut them if forced back into their mouths. These names were different: palindromic in spirit, though not in sound; names that could travel either way without losing blood.

To the Archivist it gave the name Elsewhen.

When she received it, the mercury droplet in her ribs divided into two droplets. One continued its office, lens and page, custodian of unspent future. The other became private. It held only her own life, and only as much of it as she wished to know.

Elsewhen touched her sternum.

For the first time, there was a room inside her into which history could not enter without knocking.

To the Cartographer it gave the name Nearfar.

At once one tattoo vanished from his skin: the smallest one, the gap between a pebble and its shadow. He gasped. Clear blood welled where the ink had been, then turned red, then clear again, embarrassed by its own sincerity.

Nearfar unfolded his map.

The blank space was still blank. The mountain had not agreed to the paper.

But at the margin there was now a scale.

One unit equaled one kindness attempted without guarantee of arrival.

He laughed then, though the sound came out as a string of compass bearings and had to be translated later by a chamois.

To the Janitor it gave the name Keepsake.

Her broom became lighter.

Not less real, not less useful. Only lighter. The cloak of turbine-light slipped from her shoulders and restitched itself with pockets: deep navy pockets, impossible pockets, pockets where she could put memories that were not hers and retrieve them only when needed.

Keepsake reached into the first pocket and found a small wooden cup.

It smelled of milk, dust, and a kitchen she had not yet entered.

She began to cry in reverse. Tears rose from the stone and climbed her cheeks, entering her eyes as relief.

The power did not wipe them away.

Some sacred things are ruined by assistance.

After names, it gave them sleep.

This required negotiation.

The Stillworks had no night, only degrees of unfinished light. Sleep, therefore, could not be imported whole. It would have spoiled in the air. The power instead made a small annex to waking: a condition folded between two beats of the Sink, narrow as a bookmark, wide enough for one soul at a time.

It set this annex halfway down the escarpment beside the stainless cathedral where liquid air poured luminous navy into the dim. There, in a niche the ridge had not noticed it possessed, the power placed three beds.

They were not soft. Softness would have been insulting in a place where stone was still learning its duties.

They were exact.

Elsewhen’s bed was made of unwritten pages that did not demand to be filled.

Nearfar’s bed was made of distances that had finally agreed to lie down.

Keepsake’s bed was made of swept dust, every mote forgiven for having once been elsewhere.

Above the beds the power hung no stars, because the sky was not ready. Instead it hung three pauses.

Each pause could be entered once every forty-one beats. While inside, the sleeper would not accumulate awakeness. Their eyes would close without admitting darkness. No future would be stored, no distance measured, no visitor saved. The Stillworks would continue, but it would not take attendance.

The three stared at the beds for a long time.

Their subtitles gathered, dispersed, gathered again.

WE UNDESERVE THIS.

The power answered immediately, and this time it did not bother to be quiet.

“Deserving has no jurisdiction here.”

The ridge clicked.

Every water lens answered in descant.

The chamois bowed, absorbing etiquette.

Even the great lever, far within its thousand-year patience, seemed to settle more comfortably into its socket.

Then the power went to the Sink.

Objects orbited there in the circular patch of air that behaved like water: loose stone, frozen breath, brass splinters, one unmailed apology, three feathers from a bird not yet designed. They slowed toward neutral buoyancy, each a constellation of almost.

The power looked into the Sink and saw what the geologists had argued over in absentia.

It was a heart.

It was also an editor.

It was also a mouth trying to learn not to devour every possible sentence.

The power reached into itself and drew out a thing it had carried since before benevolence had a name: a seed of ordinary time.

Not forward time. Not backward time. Not the imperial time of clocks and deadlines and graves.

Ordinary time: the kind in which bread cools, bruises fade, children grow taller without becoming evidence, and a creature may sit beside another creature for no reason except that the sitting is good.

The seed was dull brown.

In the Stillworks, where everything shone with impossible protocol, it looked almost indecent.

The power dropped it into the Sink.

For one beat nothing happened.

For another, nothing happened more deeply.

On the third, the Sink released not a token but a breath.

It rose vertically, slowly, without violence, and burst above the ridge into a weather so small it could have been mistaken for dust.

The first snow did not fall.

Snow had still not been invented.

But the idea of snow became kind.

All across the amphitheatre, the soft white contradictions hanging in the air ceased to feel embarrassed. Quartz veins brightened in their sleep. The lichen, coin-sized and older than the mountain it clung to, revised its memorandum with the hesitant sun to include tenderness under subsection four.

The dwarf willow produced a third leaf.

This was widely regarded as reckless.

Keepsake swept a respectful circle around it and warned the wind that any attempt at erasure would be met with procedural escalation.

The wind, which had never before been addressed as though it had manners, withdrew to consider its position.

The power’s final act was the smallest.

It climbed to the highest vertebra of the ringwall and waited for the echo of its own arrival to return twelve seconds later with the consonants filed off. When the echo came, the power caught it gently.

Inside that ruined sound was the Stillworks’ secret fear:

That when the mountain finally became itself, everything that had laboured backward to make it would be discarded as scaffolding.

The power took that fear and did not deny it. Denial is a door slammed in the face of prophecy.

Instead it made a promise.

Not that nothing would end.

Not that the three would never be unmade.

Not that the builder, when she arrived, would know what she was doing.

The promise was this:

“No completed thing shall be more beloved than what helped it become.”

The words entered the rock.

They entered the stainless cathedral.

They entered the brass strut-bones of the chamois and the mercury water and the blank map and the broom and the lever. They entered the Sink, which missed a beat, then resumed at forty-one per day, though now every seventh beat sounded faintly like gratitude.

The Stillworks did not transform.

The sky remained a low tin lid. The ridge still sawed the horizon in half. Frost still glinted with music too high for ears. Water still forgot whether descent was a vocation. Predators and prey continued their elaborate diplomacy. The turbines still dreamed of flowers. The mountain still grew one angstrom taller and was quietly proud of the mountain it had not yet decided to become.

But there was now a margin.

A place in the machinery where mercy could stand without being ground into function.

Elsewhen continued to swallow futures, but not all of them, and not alone. Sometimes she let one rest beside her until it ripened into surprise.

Nearfar continued to map refusals, but now he also marked attempts. His maps became less accurate and more true.

Keepsake continued to save visitors from geography, but afterward she placed the borrowed childhoods in her navy pockets and went to sleep when the Sink permitted. Once, waking from the annex between beats, she remembered the smell of her own mother’s sleeve.

She had not met her mother yet.

It helped anyway.

As for the power, it left no temple.

It knew what temples become in unfinished worlds: factories of certainty, warehouses for thunder, places where priests teach levers to feel ashamed of moving slowly.

Instead it left a bench.

The bench stood at the lip of the abyss, made of a material no one could classify because it was neither built nor grown nor crystallised, but patiently offered. It faced the Sink. It did not recruit those who sat on it. It did not improve them. It did not archive, map, polish, edit, or instruct.

It merely held weight.

Above it, in transparent letters visible only from the corner of the eye, was written:

YOU MAY REST WITHOUT BECOMING USEFUL.

For a long age, no one sat there.

The Stillworks was suspicious of gifts that did not ask to be part of the assembly.

Then one dusk, which was every hour and none of them, Keepsake leaned her broom against the bench and sat.

Elsewhen joined her forty-one beats later.

Nearfar came after measuring the distance between hesitation and trust, found it shorter than expected, and sat down too.

Below them the world went on being born backward.

Above them the hesitant sun circled the rim.

For once, the three did not speak in subtitles.

They watched the abyss breathe.

They let the machinery labour without them.

And because the power was wise, wherever it had gone, it did not look back to see whether its kindness had worked.
