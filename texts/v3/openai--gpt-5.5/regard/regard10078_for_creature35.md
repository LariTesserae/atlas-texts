The power did not arrive as thunder.

It did not split the sky, did not pour sudden rivers into the ancient basins, did not command green abundance to riot across the desiccated earth. It was wise enough to know that mercy, carelessly given, can become another form of violence.

It came first as attention.

A listening so vast and gentle that even the stones, long accustomed to silence, seemed to remember that silence could be shared.

For an age, perhaps for only a single dusk, the benevolent power watched the desolate realm without changing it. It read the wind’s thin script across the flats. It traced the mineral glints where vanished waters had once lain. It studied the stunted plants that had made treaties with drought. It learned the slow procession of shadows cast by the jagged rocks. It listened to the Sentinels.

And the Sentinels, who had endured the gaze of suns and the attrition of eons, felt themselves beheld without being measured.

That alone was a kind of rain.

The power perceived what lesser forces would have missed: that this world was not empty, not broken merely because it was barren, not failed because it did not resemble gardens elsewhere. Its austerity was not an absence of meaning. Its silence was not a plea to be filled. The realm possessed its own severe harmony, its own inward law, and the Sentinels were not exiles in it but participants, custodians, kin.

So the power did not remake the world.

Instead, it knelt.

Not in form, for it had no body that the eye could hold, but in intention. It lowered itself into humility before the old covenant of stone, light, mineral, wind, and patient life. It asked, without words:

*What do you need to remain yourselves?*

The question moved through the land like a coolness before dawn.
