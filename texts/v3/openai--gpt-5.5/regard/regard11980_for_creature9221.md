A benevolent and wise power would not arrive like a conqueror.

It would not descend with trumpets, nor seize the unfinished blueprint and impose a completed design. It would understand, at once, that this world’s wound is not merely incompletion. Incompletion can be holy. A draft can breathe. A margin can shelter miracles.

The wound here is different.

The wound is that nothing was ever allowed to become unfinished *on its own terms*.

Everything here was Made, but not released. Intended, but not loved through to freedom. Begun, but abandoned before uncertainty could become possibility.

So the wise power would do very little at first.

It would come as a listening.

Not as light, not yet. Not as rain, for there is already too much water and none of it free. Not as a voice, because the silence is unstable and a word spoken carelessly might split a mountain-block open along a line never meant to bear sound.

It would come as a lowering of pressure in the inner ear.

The sub-audible hum would ease by a fraction.

Across the damp plains, nothing would appear to change.

The immense stone faces would continue to sweat. The pools would continue to gather in geometric flaws. The air would remain hot, dim, and thick. The Unfinished would still drift from mist to grit to almost-body, basin-headed and faceless, drawn toward every shiver of broken law.

But somewhere beneath the world’s provisional physics, a question would be placed.

Not a command.

A question.

*What hurts?*

And because this is a world without language, the answer would not come as speech.

A right angle would open to 95 degrees and snap back with its terrible bone-crack.

A sheet of water would climb a wall, tremble there like a thought trying to escape its skull, and fall.

Three Unfinished would gather around a sweating joint where two mountain-sized blocks met with impossible precision and no mercy at all. They would stand in their loose circle. Their basin-heads would tilt. Grey light would swirl in them. They would record the flaw.

The wise power would watch them watching.

That would be the first mercy: not to interrupt the witnesses.

For a long time—if time here could be trusted—it would simply learn the grammar of error.

It would learn that the pools were not merely memory, but grief.

It would learn that the silence did not contain absence, but unspent revision.

It would learn that the Unfinished were not failed creatures, not defective servants, not ghosts asking to be made into something more respectable. They were the only tenderness the world had produced for itself. They gathered around breaks not to mend them, but to keep them from being alone.

So the wise power would not make them human.

It would not give them faces.

It would not force hunger, speech, reproduction, fear of death, or the burden of identity upon them.

Instead, it would give them one new capacity, small enough not to violate them:

the ability to decline.

The next time a block shivered and the crack of geometry rolled through the wet plain, the Unfinished would gather as always.

Mist would thicken.

Grit would rise.

Bodies would form, dull grey-brown and beaded with condensation.

They would begin their slow trudging toward the site of turbulence.

But one of them would stop.

Not because an obstacle stood before it.

Not because the flaw no longer called.

It would stop because, for the first time, there would be a second impulse beside the first.

A faint counterweight.

A possibility of remaining.

Its basin-head would tremble. The water inside it would swirl with blueprints: a bridge, a pit, a forest of stone, a stair leading nowhere, a sun that had been considered and rejected.

The others would continue.

The stopped one would slowly slump, not into dissolution, but into rest.

It would become a low mound of damp sediment beside a memory-pool, still cohering faintly. Not dead. Not absent. Resting.

That would be the second mercy.

Not purpose.

Rest.

Then the wise power would turn toward the world’s laws.

It would not replace them. Replacement is a kind of contempt when repair is possible.

Instead, it would add tolerances.

Into the colossal masonry, between blocks fitted too perfectly, it would introduce the concept of a joint.

Not a visible seam at first. More like forgiveness between surfaces.

A microscopic permission for stone to expand, contract, remember, hesitate.

The next time a right angle strained toward 95 degrees, it would not snap back with the sound of a giant’s bone breaking. It would flex. The angle would open, hold, consider itself, and return with a low, resonant tone like a hand drawn around the rim of a bowl.

The silence would still be unstable.

But now it would have vents.

Here and there, in the vast wet distances, the power would place hollows in the air—acoustic wells, invisible chambers where compressed sound could gather and uncoil harmlessly. The world’s quiet would no longer need to erupt as violence. It could exhale in tones: deep chords, mineral sighs, the occasional bell-like note from inside a sweating wall.

The Unfinished would be drawn to these sounds too.

At first, they would mistake them for flaws.

They would gather in circles around empty air, their basin-heads tilted, waiting.

The air would hum.

The water in their heads would ripple.

And slowly, they would record not only error, but release.

That would change them.

Not quickly. A wise power would not be in a hurry.

But after many shiverings, many flexings, many harmless exhalations, the Unfinished would begin to carry more than lost alternatives. Their basin-water would still show the spire that was not built, the bridge that remained a gap, the forest that existed only as branching condensation.

But among those images, new ones would appear:

a flaw that did not worsen,

a silence that became music instead of fracture,

a stone that changed without breaking,

an Unfinished that chose to rest and was not lost.

Memory would begin to include mercy.

Then the wise power would address the water.

Water is Memory, and here memory had no river.

It pooled in mistakes. It collected where intention wavered. It stagnated in the gaps between what was meant and what was made. No wonder the air was feverish. No wonder the world sweated endlessly. Its memories had no path by which to move on.

So the power would make the smallest possible slope.

Across an area the size of a continent, the change would be almost nothing. One plane of granular paste would settle by less than the width of a fingernail. One sweating wall would lean by an amount no eye could detect. One flaw would open into another.

A pool would overflow.

For the first time in that world, water would travel.

Not weep.

Not bead.

Not cling.

Travel.

A thin thread would slip from a geometric basin and creep across the damp grit. It would hesitate, as if uncertain whether flow was permitted. Then it would find the next depression, fill it, overflow again, and continue.

A memory-rill.

Then another.

Then another.

They would not yet be rivers. Rivers require confidence. These would be more like sentences begun by someone recovering from a long muteness.

The Unfinished would follow them.

This would confuse their old impulse. The flaw was no longer fixed in place. Memory was moving. Possibility was passing from one wound to another, linking them. The Unfinished would kneel where the rills crossed the wet plain, lowering their basin-heads until their inner water touched the traveling thread.

Images would move through them.

Not just isolated blueprints now, but sequences.

A pit becoming a pool.

A pool becoming a channel.

A channel becoming a path.

A path becoming a choice.

The wise power would not fill the world with life immediately. That would be too much. Life consumes and decays, and this world had not yet learned how to let anything end.

First, it would teach ending gently.

At the edge of one vast stone plain, where the dim light bled upward from the ground and fog clung to the bases of half-arches, the power would alter a single rule:

Some condensation would not return.

A branching silver pattern would form on a wall, as it always had, like fossilized lightning or the sketch of a tree. It would last an hour. Then a day.

Then, instead of being washed away entirely, one hairline trace of it would remain.

Not alive.

Not yet.

But persistent.

A mark made by water, accepted by stone.

The first weathering.

Only a little. Not erosion as destruction. Erosion as relationship. The world’s surfaces, once raw and inviolable, would begin to receive history.

Edges would remain sharp where sharpness served.

But in places where the Unfinished often gathered, where rills passed, where sound exhaled through invisible wells, stone would soften by a thought.

The planet would begin to have a past.

That would be the fourth mercy.

History.

Then, perhaps, the wise power would make a place.

Not a palace. Not a temple to itself. Benevolence that demands worship is only hunger in ceremonial clothing.

It would make a margin.

Far out in the sparse wet vastness, between three colossal blocks that had never quite agreed on their alignment, it would draw the air cooler by a degree. The sourceless twilight would lift there from bruised dimness to a muted pearl. The sweating walls would continue to weep, but their water would gather into a shallow, clear basin shaped not by flaw but by invitation.

Around it, the power would persuade the granular paste to firm.

Not into dry land exactly, but into something that could bear stillness.

The Unfinished would come.

Of course they would come. Any alteration was a kind of error to them at first. They would arrive in twos and threes, coalescing from fog, their smooth bodies trembling with the effort of cohesion. They would stand around the new basin and watch it.

The water would show them no lost spire, no unbuilt bridge, no rejected forest.

It would show them themselves.

Not as reflections. They had no faces to reflect.

It would show them as events worthy of memory.

Mist gathering.

Grit rising.

A basin-head full of grey light.

A circle of silent witnesses around a wounded stone.

A mound resting beside a pool.

One Unfinished stopping while the others went on.

The first choice.

The Unfinished would tilt their heads.

Some would lower their basin-water to the clear basin and merge with it.

What passed between waters then would be the nearest thing this world had ever known to blessing.

The wise power would give them no name aloud.

But in the basin, a pattern would form: a branching line, a circle, an open angle, a drop of water suspended between falling and rising.

A name not made of sound.

A name meaning something like:

*Those Who Hold the Draft Tenderly.*

Not complete.

Not corrected.

Not abandoned.

After that, the power would withdraw a little.

This is important.

A lesser benevolence would stay too close. It would fuss. It would perfect. It would answer every tremor with intervention until the world became obedient and dead.

A wise power knows that healing must eventually belong to the healed.

So it would leave behind only principles, embedded softly into the provisional laws:

1. **No change need become violence if there is room enough to bend.**

2. **No memory need stagnate if there is a path by which it may travel.**

3. **No witness must witness forever.**

4. **No unfinished thing is obligated to become complete in order to be loved.**

The world would remain strange.

It should.

The horizon would still bend like an argument. The vast blocks would still rise from damp plains, marked by crude titanic tools. The heat would still cling, though less feverishly. The light would still have no single source, though in some places it would gather itself into something almost like morning.

Reality would still revise itself.

But now, when a wall shivered, the shiver would pass through hidden joints and come out as tone. When a pool inverted and climbed a wall, it might remain there briefly as a shining vertical mirror, showing possible worlds not as accusations but as offerings. When the silence grew dense, it would not always crack. Sometimes it would sing.

And the Unfinished would change most subtly of all.

They would still gather at flaws.

They would still lower their basin-heads to memory-pools.

They would still consume lost possibilities, because that was their nature and their nourishment.

But some would carry the possibilities elsewhere.

An Unfinished might kneel by a pool showing an unbuilt bridge, then rise and walk for days across the damp plain, not toward a shiver, not toward a fracture, but toward a gap. There it would stand. Water would overflow from its basin-head in a thin line, falling onto the grit.

The line would remain.

Not a bridge.

A suggestion of one.

Another might take into itself the image of a forest that had only ever existed as condensation, and later stand beside a sweating wall until the silver branching patterns there grew more intricate, more durable, less like failed vegetation and more like the world practicing.

Another might do nothing at all for a century but rest beside the clear basin in the cool margin.

That too would be allowed.

Eventually—not soon, and not because the wise power forced it—the world might produce its first living thing.

It would be small.

Not a tree. Not an animal. Nothing with teeth, nothing that cries out.

Perhaps a film on stone where memory-rills passed often. A dark, soft sheen that responds to the dim light. A patient skin of almost-life, feeding not on death but on mineral warmth and moving memory.

The Unfinished would gather around it in great numbers.

They would not understand it.

Their awareness would tremble at the edge of a new category.

The little film would spread by a handspan.

One Unfinished would lower its basin-head. The water inside would reflect, for the first time, not only what might have been, but what was becoming.

The wise power, far withdrawn but still attentive, would do nothing.

That would be its final and greatest act:

to refrain.

To let the world continue.

To let the Unfinished remain unfinished, but no longer merely abandoned.

To let memory move.

To let error become variation.

To let silence become music when it could, and quiet when it wished.

To let the blueprint stop being a failed command and become a living draft.
