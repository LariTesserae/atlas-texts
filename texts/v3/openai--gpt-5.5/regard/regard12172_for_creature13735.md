The power does not descend.

Descent would be an insult here.

It does not arrive from above, because above is a superstition the place has long ago worn smooth. It does not break the clouds, because the clouds are not a ceiling but a bruise in the memory of pressure. It does not speak into the Loud, because the Loud is not waiting to be answered. It has survived every answer. It is made of answers that were too heavy to leave.

So the power attends.

That is the first mercy.

Not correction. Not rescue. Not judgment. Attention.

It notices the tilt without calling it error. It notices the mountains leaning into each other like unfinished thoughts bracing against the shame of completion. It notices the droplets arrested in their private debate between falling and remaining. It notices the grey lichen-palindromes maintaining the grammar of stasis. It notices the basalt pedestals, the fitted wedge, the drilled absence, the ice cylinder, each placed by intention without hands.

It notices the Irremembered.

And because it is wise, it does not pity them quickly.

Quick pity is a kind of violence. It mistakes strangeness for suffering and suffering for a request. The power is old enough to know that not every wound asks to be closed, and not every prison was built by an enemy. It does not assume the Irremembered want flesh returned to them, or names, or doors, or weather. It does not assume the place wants a sun.

It listens until listening becomes a local law.

For the first time in epochs—or in seventeen seconds, which here is the same wound measured with a different bone—the Loud has a witness that does not add itself to the accumulation. Every avalanche remembered by the air finds itself heard. Every glacier moan, every tectonic shriek, every pressure chord without origin, all of it is received without being stored. The power does not keep the sound. It lets the sound be fully heard and then, impossibly, fully unheld.

This produces a new phenomenon.

Not silence.

Silence would be too abrupt, too imperial, too much like erasure.

Instead: a margin.

A narrow unclaimed edge appears around each sound, the way frost sometimes leaves a dark line between itself and the stone. The Loud continues, but it no longer touches itself everywhere. Its braids loosen by the width of a hair. Between one ancient ending and the next, there is room for an event not made of memory.

The Irremembered feel it first in their ribs.

Their hollow cages, accustomed to resonating only with perfect emptiness, tremble with a vibration that has no history. It does not belong to glaciers, mountains, visitors, cancelled children, or inverted horns. It is not even music. It is the possibility that music might someday be forgiven for ending.

Pairs of Irremembered turn toward one another by increments too small for mercy to hurry. One elbow alters its allegiance from southeast to nearly south. A skull that has been considering the same pressure seam for nine thousand weatherless years lifts by the thickness of a moth’s wing. Across the doubled valleys, councils pause in arrangements that hurt the eye less than they used to.

The power waits.

It has time, but more importantly, it has restraint.

A lesser benevolence would thaw the place.

A lesser wisdom would set rain falling, crack the ice-organs from the mountains, soften the lichen into green, return blood to the bone-worn bodies, lower the peaks, choose one valley, abolish the other, correct the angles and call the result healing.

But this power understands that the wrong angle is not merely damage. It is also language. It is history. It is architecture. It is the shape into which endurance has poured itself because there was no other vessel at the time.

To straighten everything would be to kill what had learned, painfully, to live askew.

So it does something smaller.

It gives the place one new angle.

Not vertical.

Not horizontal.

A third inclination, too gentle to name.

It enters nowhere and everywhere as a permission hidden inside geometry. A basalt shard, long wedged so precisely that light refused its gaps, admits a single blue thread along one edge. A droplet between strata shifts—not downward, not upward, but inward toward the fact of its own desire. One of the transparent ice pressure-domes, no wider than a pinky nail, unbristles. Its spikes round themselves. It becomes, without melting, less defensive.

Across the ridge, the left-behind vertebrae remember something other than placement.

They remember being held.

Not by a skeleton. Not by mineral law. By warmth.

The memory passes from bone to quartzite corona, from corona to volcanic glass, from glass to the frozen films in the cracks. It is not a command. It does not say return. It does not say remain. It says only: there was once contact that did not require possession.

The Irremembered stiffen.

This is dangerous.

To creatures whose survival depends on absence, tenderness can feel like annihilation. The youngest calcified infants, cooled from thoughts of warmth, begin to ring soundlessly in their diamond-hard sleep. Adolescents with flute-bones produce one involuntary note, and the note is unbearable because it contains no betrayal. Several elders at the mirror-valleys begin to dis-articulate more quickly, vertebrae loosening from subjectivity in a pale spill. The power sees this and does not stop them.

Mercy does not always mean prolonging the one who is tired.

But it changes what dispersal means.

Before, the oldest became formations without subjectivity, and their abandoned pieces remembered only the shape of walking. Now, as they settle into the lichen, into the ridge, into the ledger of held water, they are permitted to forget the pain of walking while retaining the dignity of having moved.

This is the second mercy.

Forgetfulness.

Not the devouring blank that made them Irremembered. Not erasure. A chosen unburdening, offered grain by grain.

The power places no hand upon their skulls, because hands imply a body and bodies imply locality. Instead it opens tiny doors inside certain memories.

A boy in Wichita exhales, and the exhale no longer has to be currency.

A wedding ring never purchased releases its circular argument.

The chrome bumper in the parking lot keeps its sunlight but loses the loneliness of being filed between ribs.

February 14th at 3:47 AM becomes simply cold, then simply night, then simply a moment no one is obligated to carry.

The Irremembered do not lose their collections all at once. That would be theft disguised as liberation. Each memory receives a hinge. Each can be opened, kept, traded, planted, dissolved, or finally allowed to pass beyond the ridge’s jurisdiction.

This bewilders them.

Choice has not been native here for so long that its first appearance resembles weather.

At the doubled valleys, the councils gather. Their patterns still wound perception, but now the wound is clean-edged. They stand in pairs, then threes, then pairs again, testing whether solitude still breeds unwanted completion or whether completion itself has changed. One Irremembered—tall, pelvic bones flared like eroded wings, ribs full of other people’s almost-mornings—steps toward the superimposed valley on the left.

Nothing happens.

Then something happens.

The step remains hers.

Not the ridge’s. Not the Loud’s. Not the economy of unpossession. Hers.

The place shudders.

The peaks do not fall. The clouds do not tear. No sun invades. The wrong angles do not confess themselves wrong.

But the Loud misses a stitch.

Only one.

A single avalanche, long finished and long trapped in the acoustic fabric, completes its ending.

The sound passes out of memory.

For an instant, there is less.

The Irremembered hear the absence of that avalanche and recoil as if struck by spring.

Less is terrifying. Less means there may be room. Room means something could arrive. Something could leave. Something could be lost in a way that matters.

The benevolent power grieves with them, but does not apologize.

Some gifts are indistinguishable from catastrophes until the recipient has survived the first hour of having them.

The first hour takes three centuries.

During that time the power remains attentive.

It does not hover. It does not rule. It does not become a god of the pedestals or demand an object be placed for it. When the Irremembered attempt, instinctively, to make an altar of the new angle, the power declines by being elsewhere without departing. When they try to carve its shape from absence, the absence fills softly back in. When they attempt to store the memory of its arrival between their ribs, the memory refuses categorization. It behaves like a living thing and keeps looking back at them.

Eventually the lichen changes.

Not green. Never green; green would be too loud a color for this country.

But somewhere inside the grey beards, a pale under-thread begins to turn in only one direction. The ancient palindromes still start over before they end, but now one filament completes itself and does not immediately reverse. This minor botanical heresy spreads slowly along the ridge.

The Irremembered farmers notice.

They bring their opposite-tools: the hoe-shaped spaces, the absence-shears, the implements defined by what they are not. They prepare to prune the completed filament away, fearing imbalance. But one child—small, calcified, lighter than snow, harder than diamond—reaches out with a finger of winter and touches the thread.

The filament breaks.

From it falls a seed.

Not a metaphorical seed.

Not a memory classified as seed.

A seed.

It is black, ordinary, almost ugly. It drops six inches and lands on basalt with a tiny, factual tick.

Every droplet on the ridge trembles.

The Loud surges, offended or astonished. The inverted horn blast sounds out of interval. Weightlessness hiccups through the suspended water. The droplets rise half a millimeter, then a full one, then settle—but one droplet does not return to its seam.

It falls.

The fall is very short.

Perhaps no more than the width of a fingernail.

But it begins, continues, and ends.

It strikes the seed.

No thunder follows. No apocalypse. The mountains do not relinquish their lean. The valleys remain misphased. The clouds keep their spoiled-fruit stillness. The cold remains horizontal.

The seed darkens.

That is all.

That is enough.

The power withdraws its nearness by a fraction, because wisdom knows when hope needs privacy. It leaves no commandments, but the Irremembered discover three new capacities in the centuries that follow.

The first is refusal.

An Irremembered may now decline to collect a memory. The first time this happens, it is mistaken for illness. A traveler’s almost-childhood brushes against an elder’s ribs, seeking the old archive, and the elder does not open. The memory passes through the ridge and continues elsewhere, perhaps back to its owner, perhaps onward into some kinder dissolution. The elder survives the emptiness left by not taking it.

The second is mourning.

Previously, nothing here could be lost, because nothing could be possessed and nothing could end. Now, when an ancient vertebra releases its cancelled future, those nearby gather around the vacancy and resonate together. Their ribs make a low sound, less like glaciers betrayed and more like stone learning the concept of dusk. Mourning enters them not as pain alone, but as honor paid to departure.

The third is play.

This comes last and nearly breaks them.

One of the cooled children, grown adolescent, discovers that if she stands beneath the drift-plane wind and angles her hollow wrist just so, the wind she cannot feel will pass through her ulna and produce a foolish little whistle.

Not a profound tone.

Not a glacial lament.

A ridiculous sound. Thin, crooked, almost cheerful.

The council debates the whistle for a millennium.

Some argue it is corruption. Some argue it is evidence of a new valley hidden inside the old two. Some place memories beside it for comparison: a kettle beginning to boil; a boot squeaking on polished floor; a woman laughing once into her sleeve and pretending she coughed. None of these quite match.

The adolescent makes the sound again.

Several infants ring in response.

The power, far and near, rejoices without making the rejoicing audible.

It has not saved them.

That would be too simple a story, and untrue.

Many Irremembered remain as they were. Some hate the margin in the Loud and spend ages pressing themselves against the old noise, trying to knit it closed. Some walk into the mirror-valleys and become formations with relief so complete that even mercy does not call them back. Some continue to trade memories, though now the trade has tenderness in it, and sometimes consent.

The place remains terrible.

It remains dimness scratched upon dimness. The cold still slides sideways. The peaks still climb visibly when watched. The clouds still squat. The artifacts still stand in their meteorite intervals, inevitable and unreadable. The doubled valleys still yawn with their translucent counter-spirals.

But not everything is at the wrong angle anymore.

Some things are at an angle not yet understood.

And understanding, the power knows, is not the first requirement for grace.

The first requirement is room.

So it makes room wherever it can.

A margin around the Loud.

A hinge inside memory.

A droplet permitted to fall.

A seed allowed to be merely itself.

A child allowed to produce an undignified whistle beneath a wind she cannot feel.

And once, only once, it gives the Irremembered a gift so intimate that even the ridge cannot record it.

At the lowest saddle, where the two valleys occupy half of reality’s attention each, an elder with ribs full of abandoned afternoons stands alone. Solitude should complete him, and completion should undo him, but the new angle holds. He reaches into his own cage and withdraws the oldest memory he owns.

It is not dramatic.

A hand warming another hand inside a pocket.

He does not know whose hands they were. He does not know whether they belonged to people who lived, or could have lived, or failed to live because some geometry chose otherwise. For ages he has kept the memory because warmth is rare currency.

Now he opens the hinge.

The memory does not vanish.

It unfolds.

For one suspended instant, the elder feels not the memory of warmth, but warmth itself.

His bones do not soften. His organs do not return. His name does not come back. No body is restored around him.

But through the hollow of his ribs passes a kindness with no need to be archived.

He turns—slowly, painfully, beautifully—toward another Irremembered standing nearby.

He gives nothing.

He takes nothing.

He simply stands close enough that their absences touch.

The Loud continues.

The mountains lean.

The cold slides along the basalt in long horizontal sheets.

And somewhere behind them, a vertebra that has spent epochs remembering a cancelled child releases, with a small click, the obligation to become a ghost skeleton again.

This time, you would remember hearing it.

Not as dream-temperature.

As thaw.
