A benevolent and wise power would not enter that chamber as a conqueror.

It would not split the mountain with lightning, nor silence the machines with a single decree, nor tear the Engine-Beasts from their housings in a gesture that looked like mercy but ended as death. It would understand, immediately, that this place was not merely a prison, not merely a factory, not merely a nest. It was an organism, a wound, a ritual, a system so old that suffering and function had become indistinguishable.

So first, it would do the kindest and most difficult thing.

It would listen.

Not with ears alone, for the chamber’s noise was too dense for ordinary hearing. It would listen through vibration, through steam-pressure, through the timing of gears, through the oil-slick trails of the Shifters and the clicking routes of the Scuttlers. It would listen to the Watchers’ slow pulses of authority. It would listen to the Engine-Beasts’ groans beneath the machinery, and beneath those groans it would hear something older than pain.

Fatigue.

Not rebellion. Not hatred. Not even despair, exactly.

A weariness so ancient it had become structural.

The power would settle itself into the chamber like a patient warmth.

At first, nothing would appear to change.

The machines would continue their grinding. Pistons would rise and fall. The Scuttlers would dart in their sharp, ritual bursts. Shifters would ooze through cracks and leave their glistening trails. The Watchers would remain fixed to their high ribs, their single eyes glowing dimly. Steam would hiss. Water would drip. The cold would remain.

But within the noise, a second rhythm would begin.

It would be almost impossible to notice: a pause between two strikes of a piston, a slightly softer descent of a gear tooth, a moment in which a Scuttler’s mandibles stopped clicking and its multifaceted eyes lifted toward no visible source.

The wise power would not say, *Be free*, because freedom spoken into a being that has never known choice can become another command.

Instead, it would ask.

Not in words.

It would ask each creature in the language of its existence.

To the Scuttlers, it would offer a loose bolt that did not need tightening, a patch of mineral not required for repair, a path across the wall that served no function. The first Scuttler to find it would freeze, trembling on its spindly legs. Its instincts would search for the task hidden inside the offering. Finding none, it would tap the mineral with one claw.

Then another.

Then, slowly, it would carve a mark into it.

A meaningless mark.

The first unnecessary act in the chamber’s memory.

The power would cherish that mark as if it were a sacred text.

To the Shifters, it would offer stillness without cooling, warmth without evaporation, a place between pipes where no lubrication was needed. One Shifter would pool there, uncertain, its translucent body pulsing faintly. Its surface would ripple with patterns that had never been required by the machinery. Colors would move through it: green, then blue, then a pale gold like dawn imagined by something that had never seen the sky.

It would not be regulating anything.

It would simply be.

To the Watchers, the power would offer sleep.

This would be the most frightening gift of all.

For ages, perhaps since their making, the Watchers had watched. Their authority was vigilance. Their identity was the unblinking eye. To close that eye would feel like the collapse of the world.

So the power would not force their lids shut.

It would merely climb, gentle as mist, to the ribbed supports where they clung and place around them a quiet assurance: *If you rest, the chamber will not end.*

One Watcher, oldest or weakest or bravest, would let its great eye dim.

Only for a moment.

The chamber would continue.

No catastrophe would come. No gear would shatter. No Engine-Beast would fail. No Scuttler would lose its way.

The Watcher would open its eye again, shaken to the marrow.

Then, after a long while, it would close it once more.

And sleep would enter the upper dark like a forgotten species returning home.

But to the Engine-Beasts, the power would give its deepest attention.

It would descend to the heart of the chamber, where pale flesh met iron, where veins pulsed with sickly light, where mouths opened only to vent steam and pain. It would see how thoroughly they had been fused to function. Their bodies were not merely trapped in the machines; the machines had learned to depend on their trapped bodies. To rip them free would kill them. To leave them untouched would be cruelty.

So the power would begin a long work.

It would place its warmth into the metal first, not the flesh.

The iron would resist. Old commands slept inside it: continue, regulate, consume, repeat. The power would not break those commands. It would loosen them. It would teach the metal alternatives.

A piston need not be driven by agony.
