A benevolent and wise power would not look down upon this place and think: *unfinished, therefore wrong.*

It would understand that some worlds are not gardens, not yet or not ever. Some are wombs of stone. Some are graves that have not decided whether to remember or germinate. Some forms of life cannot bear rescue, because rescue would be a hotter violence than neglect.

So the power would come quietly.

Not as sun.  
Not as rain.  
Not as green.

It would come first as **attention**.

It would let itself become small enough to hear the difference between one drip and the next. It would listen to the basin’s damp percussion until the separate sounds resolved into grammar: the plink of newly gathered water, the soft hiss of mineral skin accepting a film, the sudden silence where a Skerr Whisper passed and held the world’s breath between droplets.

It would lay no hand on the Coagula at first. It would only observe their slow tumbling, their wet opalescent labor, the way they fed upon gradients so delicate that a warmer mercy might annihilate them. It would recognize in them a kind of hunger, though not the hunger of mouths; a kind of reaching, though not with limbs.

And because it was wise, it would not pity them for being slow.

It would understand slowness as one of the original dignities.

Then, after long listening, it would do only what the place itself had already begun to ask.

It would give the basin **rhythm**.

Not abundance. Abundance would drown it. Not clarity. Clarity would bleach it. Not warmth enough to summon forests and molds and swift invasive hungers. Instead, the power would make the condensation gather and release in gentler cycles: a deepening, a pause, a loosening. The air would still be thick, still cold on the tongue, still bruise-lit beneath the burdened sky—but now the water would know intervals.

For a hundred dim hours the stone would bead and weep.

Then, for one hour, the dripping would soften.

In that hush, the Skerr Whispers would find room to speak.

Their language of absence would widen. Silences would become less like fear and more like signaling. One hush for approach. Two for warning. A long suspension over a pool for wonder. They would skim the cracks and leave behind not merely dry trails, but intentions: delicate routes of interrupted adhesion, shining paths that other Skerr could follow before the world erased them.

The power would not give them voices.

It would help their silence carry.

For the Coagula, it would alter nothing obvious. Only the smallest chemistry, placed with the tenderness of a star adjusting a single mote of dust: a trace of soluble brightness here, a patient ion there, a faint difference between one fissure and the next. Enough to make choice possible.

Before, the Coagula had moved only when compelled by water’s weight.

Now some would lean.

Not quickly. Never quickly. But across long spans, one accretion would favor a cooler seam, another a mineral-rich ledge, another the underside of an angled slab once placed by the Hands of the Last Flood. Their lives would remain almost indistinguishable from erosion. Yet within that erosion, preference would begin.

And preference, the wise power knew, is one of the first lanterns of the soul.

It would turn next to the remnants of the Hands.

Here it would be most careful.

The stones deliberately piled, the grooves half-hidden beneath limescale, the slab set at a purposeful angle—these it would not restore. Restoration would falsify them. It would not rebuild vanished societies from fragments, nor summon back architects who had already dissolved into the basin’s memory.

Instead, it would preserve the **legibility of absence**.

The trickles would be guided, subtly, so they did not erase every mark at once. Mineral film would gather translucent rather than opaque over certain grooves. A few placed stones would become catchments where droplets rang with distinct tones. Not speech, exactly. Not history recovered.

But enough that the current inhabitants might encounter arrangement and feel: *Something intended here.*

Enough that the basin would not be entirely deprived of inheritance.

The Pale Echos, those almost-colonies, those dreams of moss without moss, would receive the strangest gift.

The power would not make them alive.

It would give them permission to fail in more interesting ways.

Their spirals of limescale would branch into new geometries. Their mineral blooms would darken, brighten, recede, return. Some patterns would vanish forever; others would recur after long intervals when water, vibration, and trace chemistry aligned. They would become a record of attempts. A scripture of near-life written in crust and sheen.

If someday life recognizable even to itself arose there, it would read those Pale Echos as its prehistory.

If not, they would still be beautiful.

And beauty, too, is a form of mercy.

Last, the power would attend to the Underwake.

It would descend—not downward in space, but inward through patience—until it felt the basin’s buried pulse: the massive, half-dreaming pressure beneath stone and condensation, the slow tectonic breath that was not a creature and yet held all creatures in its tempo.

The power would not wake it.

To wake such a thing would be catastrophe wearing the mask of revelation.

It would only steady the sleep.

Not freeze it. Not bind it. But ease the violence of its turning. When plates shifted, they would still shift. When fissures opened, they would still open. This world was young and could not be spared becoming. But the worst shatterings would be softened. The basin would be allowed to change without being broken beyond recognition.

Then the power would leave a small sign.

Nothing luminous enough to dominate the dusk.

No pillar. No temple. No voice from the clouds.

Only a hollow in the rock where water could gather a little more freely than elsewhere.

A shallow basin, no wider than two hands.

Its rim would be faceted, natural-seeming, though somehow always able to catch the faint sky. Droplets would travel toward it along hairline paths. The pool would never be full, never generous in the way lush worlds know generosity. But it would persist.

The Coagula would find gradients around its edge.

The Skerr would gather there to make their silences.

The Pale Echos would ring its mineral lip in wavering spirals.

The old marks of the Hands would be reflected there, broken and rejoined by ripples.

And once in a great while, when the Underwake breathed deep beneath the basin, the pool would tremble—not violently, but enough to send one perfect circle outward from its center.

That would be the power’s blessing:

not salvation,  
not transformation,  
not the correction of strangeness into familiarity,

but a widening of possibility.

It would make no demand that the place become a forest, a sea, a cradle, a kingdom, or a grave.

It would say, in the only language this land could bear:

*Continue.*

*Become, if becoming is yours.*

*Remain, if remaining is holy.*

*Erode, but not without memory.*

*Gather, but not without room.*

*Be slow, and be held.*
