The benevolent power does not enter the Chlorattice as a body.

A body would be an intrusion here. A body would cast shadows, bear weight, disturb gradients, snap miles of living glass-green tube by the mere arrogance of arrival. Even a gentle footfall, if placed wrongly, would become catastrophe hours later in the delayed grammar of pressure. Wisdom understands first that help is also a kind of violence when it arrives ignorant.

So the power waits.

It waits longer than pity would have allowed. Longer than impatience. Longer than the first bright ache of love. It holds itself outside the world’s skin, where the fissures open into merciless white, and it listens downward into the continent’s held breath.

It learns the Chlorattice.

It learns that the lattice is not cruel, though it kills. It is young. It is hungry in the way of all young things: blindly architectural, extravagant, unable to distinguish shelter from prey, womb from nutrient, the quick from the merely warm. It grows because growth is the only verb it knows.

It learns the Unstill.

Not as a category. Not as specimens. One by one.

The mother circling her membrane-womb through months of cold, half-dreaming while her tether pulses milk-thin condensation into the child within.

The old traverser with shoots in their ribs, moving so slowly that even suffering has become economical.

The clutch of newly emerged young fluxing in terrified spirals around a bright node, their five small hearts hammering out of phase.

The bone-poet stretched between two junctions, composing pressure into a state that means: *I have crossed this emptiness and the emptiness crossed me also.*

The power listens until it can tell the difference between a collapse, a warning, a lullaby, and a name.

Only then does it act.

Not by stopping the Chlorattice.

That would be murder disguised as mercy.

Not by lifting the Unstill to the surface.

That would be rescue shaped like exile.

Not by making them other than they are.

That would be the oldest sin of gods: to love a being by replacing it.

Instead, the power gives the Chlorattice a new reflex.

It is a small change, almost nothing. A revision no thicker than frost forming on a tube.

Where Unstill skin rests against living lattice, the aggressive nitrogen-hunger hesitates.

Not forever. Forever would be too much. Forever would turn motion into irrelevance, and irrelevance is another kind of death. But for a while—long enough for sleep, long enough for birth, long enough for grief—the shoots do not pierce. The Chlorattice tastes the chemistry of the Unstill and learns a pause.

The first to discover this is an exhausted mother whose child has not emerged cleanly.

The membrane-womb has torn. The infant’s rear limbs are still sealed in translucent caul, claws not yet set, body too soft to flux. The mother circles, frantic, abrading her own ribs against the node. She cannot stop. If she stops, the lattice will take them both. If she moves on, the child dies.

For the first time in the memory of her bloodline, she presses herself fully against a bright green tube and remains.

A hundred heartbeats.

Two hundred.

No shoot enters her.

No pale spear threads her skin.

The lattice trembles around her, confused, then calm.

She makes a sound no Unstill language has needed before. Not Pressure Cant. Not warning. Not poem.

A bare exhalation.

She cuts the caul with an obsidian fingertip. She frees the child. She waits until its claws grip.

Then she moves.

The story spreads incorrectly, because all first miracles are misunderstood.

Some say a fissure above opened and a surface-being looked down with an eye of unbearable warmth.

Some say the dead filaments forgave them.

Some say the Chlorattice dreamed and in its dream mistook the Unstill for its own children.

The wise power permits all versions.

It does not require worship. Worship would bend their necks toward an absence when they need their whole bodies for traversing.

It does not name itself.

It only continues.

At certain nodes—the ones where water already gathers, where updrafts cradle warmth and light arrives fractured rather than blinding—the power encourages a second alteration. Dead filaments there do not become brittle at once. They soften first. They separate into long, pliable fibers with a faint inner toughness, easier to weave, slower to decay.

The Unstill find them and make better shelters.

Not permanent homes. Permanence would still invite entombment. But kinder wombs. Rest-cocoons. Places where the injured may pause for a little while without becoming offerings.

These places come to be called the Held Knots.

No two clans agree on who made them. Some insist they are natural. Some insist they are warnings. Some fear them at first, because any mercy in the Chlorattice is suspicious by definition.

The oldest traversers test them.

They coil inside, reluctantly, claws ready.

They wait for penetration.

None comes.

One ancient, whose left shoulder is already half-fused with pale green growth, remains still for nearly an hour. When they emerge, they are shaking. Not from cold.

From the terror of having been permitted to rest.

After that, the Pressure Cant changes.

A new contraction enters the language: long, low, almost too gentle to travel.

It means: *pause-safe.*

A second follows years later.

It means: *return here if broken.*

Then a third, more difficult, composed by four poets across eleven kilometers of swaying lattice.

It means something like: *motion is sacred, but it is not the same as fear.*

The benevolent power hears this and is glad.

But it is not finished.

Wisdom knows that immediate mercy without future mercy is only delay.

The Chlorattice will cool. The continent will settle. The frantic convulsions will slow. One day the tubes will harden, darken, mineralize. The great breath beneath the permafrost will exhale or calcify. The Unstill have always known this. Their doom is not accident; it is written into the bargain that made them.

So the power studies endings.

It cannot abolish ending. To abolish ending is to abolish meaning.

But it can widen the shape of what may come.

Deep in the dark filaments, in those brittle lengths the Unstill harvest as sacrament, the power plants a patient inheritance. A dormant symbiosis. Not a command, not a destiny—an option.

Spores finer than mineral dust settle into the dead cellulose. They do not awaken in the present Chlorattice. The world is too cold, too bright, too turbulent. But when the lattice slows, when the tubes thicken and the water no longer races upward in panic, when stillness begins to spread like a verdict, the spores will quicken.

They will soften the mineralized tubes from within.

They will make hollows.

They will make chambers.

They will make dim, humid arteries through the hardened green stone.

Not roads. Not cities. Possibilities.

And in those future hollows, if any Unstill remain, their descendants may learn another motion: not fluxing along the outside of peril, but passing through sheltering darkness, carrying water in their bones, carrying old pressure-poems in their joints.

Some may become dwellers.

Some may remain traversers until the last possible moment.

Some may choose stillness and not die of it.

Choice is the gift.

Not salvation.

Choice.

The power also touches the light.

Only slightly.

The fissures above remain merciless; the white glare still descends in blades. But in a thousand places where the light strikes the Chlorattice most brutally, the tubes begin to refract differently. Their translucence clouds by a fraction. The sharp shadows soften at their edges.

The Unstill do not suddenly see.

They do not need eyes.

But their skins ache less. Their young misread fewer shadow-knots. Fewer fall into dead brown spans believing them sound.

This, too, enters myth.

The children are told: once the light had teeth on both sides. Now some teeth are worn down because the oldest poets sang against them.

This is untrue.

It is also true enough.

The power is careful never to remove the need for courage.

There are still collapses. There are still storms in the empty kilometers. There are still nodes that snap without warning, still brittle spans, still hunger, still loss. A world made harmless would not be the Chlorattice; it would be a cage padded in contempt.

But the ratio changes.

A child born tangled has a chance.

An injured traverser may reach a Held Knot.

A mother may stop long enough to cut free the caul.

An elder may rest without immediately being claimed.

A poem may be composed for beauty, not warning.

That last change takes the longest.

For generations, every complex pressure-pattern has carried utility: danger, water, collapse, route, death, birth. Even melancholy has been practical, a way of calibrating the body to barometric sorrow.

Then, across a span of green tubes swaying over twenty kilometers of open air, six Unstill gather at dusk-equivalent, when the white glare dims behind windborne ice crystals and the shadows become less like knives.

They arrange themselves at separate nodes.

They flex.

The lattice receives them.

No message travels. No instruction. No map. No warning.

Only rhythm.

A slow tightening like condensation forming.

A release like thaw.

A tremor like a young claw learning purchase.

A pause.

A long, impossible pause.

The standing wave moves through the Chlorattice, touching bright tubes, dead tubes, water pearls, shelters, old scars, newborn claws, the softened edges of light. It passes through the body of an elder resting in a Held Knot, who begins to weep without knowing why. It reaches a mother and child fluxing along a filament newly safe from hunger. It enters the ribs of a hunter cutting dark lattice with a thigh-bone knife. It fades into kilometers of cold air.

The poem means nothing except:

*We are here.*

And beneath the permafrost of a continent still cooling from its birth, that is new.

The benevolent power receives the poem and answers only by holding steady the conditions that allowed it.

No voice descends.

No commandment.

No promise carved in light.

The Unstill continue to traverse.

The Chlorattice continues to grow, break, knit, breathe.

But now, hidden within its reflexes, there is hesitation.

Hidden within its dead, there is future softness.

Hidden within motion, there is rest.

And hidden within rest, no longer only death, there is the first small chamber of freedom.
