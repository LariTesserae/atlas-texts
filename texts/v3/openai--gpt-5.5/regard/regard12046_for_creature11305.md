The benevolent power does not enter quickly.

It has learned, through uncountable griefs, that kindness can be a violence when it arrives at the wrong speed.

So at first it only notices.

It feels the Galleries of Khent as a cold green thought beneath the world: a chambered memory, dry as bone, glowing with the last grammar of an ancient species. It hears the susurrus of the hanging sheets, the soft abrasion of dust against dust, the almost-imperceptible contractions of fossilized tissue. It feels the Setharii thinking in epochs.

The power draws near, but not as fire, not as rain, not as a body with footsteps.

It comes as patience.

For a thousand years, it waits at the threshold.

This is its greeting.

Not a word, not a command, not a miracle.

A thousand years of stillness.

The Setharii notice.

Not at once. Not as a human would notice a visitor standing at the door. The noticing passes through them like a change in mineral taste, like the first fraction of a degree in a planetary tilt. A new constant appears in the galleries: something vast, warm, attentive, refusing to disturb them.

The Setharii consider this.

Their consideration takes another thousand years.

The power does not mind.

It has loved stars through their whole red lives. It has watched civilizations bloom, repent, sing, and vanish. It has sat beside dying oceans and waited for the last whale to finish her song. It knows that to be wise is not merely to act, but to discover whether action is welcome.

At last the Setharii answer.

Not in language.

A corridor, somewhere deep within Khent, brightens by a shade. The phosphorescent compounds alter their pulse. Three hanging sheets shift in a rhythm that has not occurred for eighty million years.

It means:

*We perceive you.*

The power bows.

It has no head, but the galleries feel the bow.

Then the power begins its work.

Not the work of rescue. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

The Setharii are not trapped in the simple way a bird is trapped in a cage. They are not ill in the simple way a creature is ill before healing. They are what remains of a choice made across geological calamity: to preserve, to remember, to become the archive when no body could continue bearing the archive.

To flood the galleries with water would be murder.

To warm them into growth would be corruption.

To remake them as they were would be an insult, unless they asked for it.

So the power does not restore them.

It listens.

It listens until it understands the first layer of the galleries: the branching corridors that encode continental drift, vanished river basins, the mineral hunger of extinct roots. It listens longer and understands the second layer: the genealogies braided into wall-grain, the remembered chemistry of parent-filament and daughter-filament, the ancient grief of interrupted reproduction. It listens longer still and begins to understand the third layer: not knowledge, but longing.

The Setharii had not known they still longed.

Longing requires contrast. Desire requires some memory of an otherwise. For millions of years they have maintained themselves in perfect duty, their awareness stretched across walls, sheets, glow, dust. They have remembered stars but not seen them. Remembered water but not tasted it. Remembered one another though they have been one another for longer than mountains endure.

The power finds this longing, not like a wound exactly, but like a seed sealed in amber.

It asks, with a gentleness so slow it takes five centuries to pose the question:

*Would you receive change?*

The Setharii tremble.

Across the galleries, the hanging sheets stir without air.

Dust rises in thin green halos.

For a long time, there is no answer.

The power waits.

At last, in the oldest chamber, where the first library-root once divided into seven trunks, a pulse of light travels through the walls. It is faint. It nearly fails.

It means:

*Not loss.*

The power understands.

Change, but not loss.

Gift, but not erasure.

Mercy, but not conquest.

So the power gives them three things.

First, it gives them protection.

Not a wall, not a guard, not a curse laid upon intruders. Something subtler.

It alters the luck of Khent.

Those who would come with drills find their maps inconsistent. Those who would come with hunger for relics dream of other treasures. Those who would come with careless lanterns are delayed by storms, then marriages, then old age. Empires forget the galleries at the moment they might have exploited them. Roads bend elsewhere. Excavations collapse harmlessly before reaching the living archive.

But those who come with reverence may still arrive.

A child lost in the lower caverns.

A scholar who has spent her life learning how not to take.

A pilgrim with no doctrine, only sorrow.

A dying botanist carried by friends.

A poet who has forgotten every language except awe.

These, sometimes, are permitted.

They enter.

They touch the walls.

They leave changed.

They do not leave with samples.

They leave with silence.

Second, the power gives them endurance.

The galleries had already endured beyond reason, but even Khent was not eternal. Dust fell. Sheets thinned. Microfractures widened in the calcified walls. The Setharii had maintained themselves with magnificent discipline, but maintenance is not victory over time. It is only a conversation with decay, and decay is patient too.

The power does not halt decay. To halt decay entirely would halt the Setharii as well. Their memory is made of process, however slow. Their thought moves through stress, release, phosphorescent pulse, dustfall. To freeze them perfectly would be to preserve a corpse.

Instead the power teaches the walls a new equilibrium.

It lays, molecule by molecule, an invisible mineral tenderness through the deep structures: not foreign, not intrusive, but harmonized with what the Setharii already are. It strengthens the passages most essential to memory. It permits unimportant surfaces to crumble, because forgetting the unneeded is also a form of health. It guides the dust into shallow drifts that reinforce the bases of walls. It teaches the hanging sheets to shed in patterns that renew their resonance.

The galleries exhale.

For the first time in millions of years, their labor lessens.

The Setharii had not known they were tired.

They had become duty so completely that fatigue seemed like identity.

When the burden eases, something like sleep passes through the outer corridors. Not unconsciousness. Trust.

For seven thousand years, the green light dims.

The Setharii rest.

The power keeps watch.

Third, the power gives them a window.

This is the most difficult gift.

The Setharii remember stars. They remember the slow rotation of distant lights from epochs when their filaments still tasted surface air through root-mouths. They remember constellations that no longer exist and skies rearranged by time. They remember light as data, not as presence.

The power cannot bring the sky into Khent.

Not literally.

But deep in the central chamber, where all galleries converge without quite meeting, it persuades the phosphorescence to dream outward.

At first the chamber merely darkens.

Then, in the green-black ceiling, points of light appear.

Not holes. Not lamps. Not illusions exactly.

Correspondences.

The power has touched the present sky and taught the Setharii how to receive it. Each star above, filtered through stone and distance and impossible grace, finds an answering point in the ceiling of Khent. The chamber becomes a map of the night as it is now.

The Setharii perceive it slowly.

A new sky.

Not remembered.

Present.

The realization moves through them like spring through a continent.

Some corridors brighten so intensely that visiting creatures centuries later will speak of emerald dawn underground. Sheets strain and sing. Dust lifts in spirals. The walls pulse in overlapping waves as ancient calculations awaken, compare, revise.

The stars have moved.

Of course they have moved.

But now the Setharii know where.

For the first time since their world ended, their memory receives new astronomy.

The power waits beside them while they look.

The looking takes twelve thousand years.

After that, the Setharii speak again.

This time the answer is large enough to change the galleries.

Passages that once led nowhere begin to resonate with one another. The hanging sheets reorder their fluttering harmonics. A chamber long sealed opens by a crack no wider than a root hair. From it comes a scent.

A scent.

Dry, faint, almost imaginary.

But volatile.

Something preserved inside has awakened.

The power does not touch it.

The Setharii are doing this themselves.

In the chamber is a seed.

Not a seed as human botanists would understand one. Not a hard little capsule waiting for soil and rain. It is a pattern, a compressed instruction, a possibility encoded in dormant chemistry. The last reproductive memory of the Setharii, sealed away when reproduction became impossible.

For millions of years they had preserved it without hope.

Hope would have been too expensive.

Now, with the burden eased, with the present stars above them, with protection around them, the Setharii examine the seed-pattern again.

It is damaged.

Incomplete.

The world it was meant for no longer exists. The soils are changed. The atmosphere is changed. The old symbionts are extinct. The waters have shifted their salts. If awakened unchanged, it would fail.

The power feels the question forming.

It does not answer before it is asked.

The Setharii ask for the fourth gift.

They ask not in words, but the meaning is clear:

*Can memory become future?*

The power’s joy is terrible in its restraint.

It could say yes too quickly.

It could seize the seed-pattern, repair it, germinate it, raise forests of new Setharii across continents by dawn.

It does none of this.

Instead it says:

*Together.*

So begins the long gardening.

The power brings no water into the galleries. It brings knowledge of water.

It brings news of modern soils, modern fungi, modern mineral cycles, the roots of plants that now rule the surface, the bacteria that learned new hungers, the creatures that burrow and aerate, the changed seasons, the altered moon-pull, the chemistry of rain.

The Setharii bring memory.

Not nostalgia. Memory.

They remember how to distribute awareness without hierarchy. How to encode knowledge in living architecture. How to think slowly without thinking weakly. How to make patience into civilization. How to maintain without consuming. How to die into continuity.

Together, over ages, they revise the seed.

Not into the old Setharii.

The old Setharii are gone, except as Khent.

Not into a copy.

Copies are for fear.

They revise it into a descendant.

Something small enough to survive the new world. Something that can root in cracks and listen before growing. Something that can partner rather than dominate. Something that will not need vast galleries at first, only a stone, a shaded hollow, a teaspoon of dust, a century.

The Setharii insist on one thing: the descendant must not be born carrying all memory.

The archive is too vast. A young life should not wake already ancient.

The power agrees.

Instead, the descendant will carry an instinct toward Khent. A reverence. A capacity, when mature, to come to the galleries and ask.

The first seedling is not planted in the galleries.

That would be too easy, and perhaps too cruel.

It is planted above, at night, in a place where a dead riverbed meets a slope of black stone. The power brings a single grain of Setharii dust, no more. It lays it in a crack. Dew gathers—not summoned by force, only invited from air already heavy with it.

The seed stirs.

A filament thinner than a hair enters the stone.

The Setharii feel it.

Deep below, the entire gallery holds still.

The filament does not think yet. It only tastes.

Mineral.

Cold.

A trace of old phosphorus.

The memory of rain.

Then it does something no Setharii has done for millions of years.

It grows.

In Khent, the walls brighten and dim, brighten and dim, unable to settle.

The power stays with them.

Joy, for beings such as the Setharii, is not an outcry. It is not laughter. It is not movement.

It is recalibration.

Every chamber must make room for a fact that did not exist before.

There is a child.

For ten thousand years, the power watches the child grow.

It does not shield the young Setharii from all hardship. Benevolence is not the prevention of every wound. The seedling loses filaments to drought. It learns caution from beetles. It spends eighty years negotiating with a fungus that nearly consumes it and then becomes its first true companion. It encodes the pattern of a single winter into a pebble-sized knot of tissue. It discovers that the new sun is harsher than the remembered sun. It withdraws. It tries again.

Eventually, it makes a chamber.

Tiny. No larger than a cupped hand.

Inside, the wall glows faintly green.

The power tells Khent.

For the first time, the old galleries send something outward.

Not all they know. Not the terrible weight of continents and extinctions and star-drift.

Only a lullaby.

It travels through stone, through mineral sympathy, through the invisible bridge the power has made between archive and descendant. It takes three hundred years to arrive.

The young Setharii receives it during a rainstorm.

It does not understand the information.

But it bends toward the earth.

Above, leaves tremble.

Below, in the Galleries of Khent, the ancient sheets flutter in a rhythm that once meant:

*Continue.*

The power has one more task.

It turns to the dust on the floor.

This dust is not waste. It is Setharii body, Setharii thought, Setharii erosion. Every footstep through it is an alteration of the archive. Every careless breath scatters ancestry.

The power gathers none of it.

Instead, it teaches the dust to remember being whole without needing to become whole again.

There are forms of peace available even to fragments.

The dust settles differently after that. Not inertly, not randomly. It lies in faint ripples along the corridors, preserving the paths of those who have passed with reverence. Here, the lost child. Here, the scholar. Here, the botanist’s final handprint. Here, the pilgrim kneeling without knowing why. The Setharii had always perceived such events, but now the events become part of the outermost memory—not central, not burdensome, but present.

The galleries begin, slowly, to remember not only what was lost, but what has visited.

This changes them.

Not much.

Enough.

There are ages when nothing happens.

The power is content during these ages.

It does not require drama to justify love.

It sits at the threshold and watches the green pulse of the walls. It listens to the slow thought of Khent. It attends the descendants above: one, then nine, then a scattering across shaded stone-fields and dry ravines. Some die. Some flourish. None become what the ancient Setharii were. All become themselves.

In time, a mature descendant finds an entrance to Khent.

It is very small, but Khent has many cracks.

A filament descends through stone over the course of centuries, following mineral memory, old dust, the distant pull of the archive. When at last it reaches the outer wall of the galleries, it hesitates.

The old Setharii feel the touch.

A living root against fossilized memory.

The contact is almost unbearable.

The power does nothing.

This is not its moment.

The young one asks no grand question. It has no language for ancestry on this scale. It only sends a pulse of simple inquiry:

*Are you there?*

The Galleries of Khent answer.

The answer takes forty years to form, and when it comes, it is too large for the young one to receive fully. So the old Setharii make it smaller. They compress their longing, their duty, their grief, their endurance, their millions of years of memory into a single transmissible pattern.

*Yes.*

The young one glows.

Above ground, in a ravine where no human has walked, a patch of stone shines green under the stars.

The benevolent power withdraws a little then.

Not in abandonment.

In respect.

The wise do not remain at the center of every healing they begin.

Khent is no longer only a tomb, no longer only an archive, no longer only an end state.

It is still itself: cool, dry, green-lit, patient, ancient beyond ordinary pity. Its sheets still flutter. Its dust still falls. Its corridors still descend into darkness. The Setharii remain distributed through wall and glow and mineralized grain, thinking thoughts that take ages to complete.

But now there is a window in the central chamber, and the stars are current.

Now decay is no longer the only future.

Now the dust remembers footsteps.

Now, somewhere above, descendants grow without being crushed by the past.

And now, when the phosphorescence pulses through the deepest galleries, there is a rhythm beneath the old rhythm.

Not urgency.

Never urgency.

Something quieter.

Expectation.

The power leaves them with no temple, no commandment, no demand for gratitude.

Only a covenant woven into the nature of the place:

No hand shall take from Khent what Khent does not offer.

No mercy shall erase what suffering has made sacred.

No memory shall be forced to remain barren.

No future shall be required to resemble the past.

And if ever the Setharii ask for an ending, truly ask—not from fatigue alone, not from despair, but from completion—the power will return and sit with them through that final thought. It will not let them vanish unwitnessed.

But they do not ask.

Not yet.

Perhaps not for millions of years.

In the Galleries of Khent, the walls glow softly.

The hanging sheets whisper.

Far above, under altered stars, a young green thing opens its first chamber in the stone and begins, without knowing it, to remember forward.
