It would not arrive as rescue.

Rescue is too loud a word for the Tilted Borough. It has hooks in it, ropes, lanterns, people shouting across distances that have spent geological eras teaching themselves not to answer. Rescue assumes peril is a mistake. Rescue assumes the rescued want the shape of elsewhere.

A benevolent and wise power, having looked upon Deep-tilt Levellation 47, would understand this first:

The Borough is not merely trapped.

It has also chosen.

Not wholly. Not cleanly. Not without coercions that became customs and customs that became marrow. But chosen enough that mercy must move carefully, like a hand entering the sleep of an animal made of knives.

So the power would come very small.

Not as a sun.

Not as rain.

Not as green.

Not as a trumpet of reversal ringing down Downspin Boulevard and throwing every suspended wrench into a new calendar.

It would enter at 0.1 ҉lux and no brighter, wearing the local dark correctly.

It would lower its voice beneath conversation and then beneath the decisions of the Mouthless Doormen. It would compress itself into a warmth that could pass through nickel-iron glass without being counted as expansion. A warmth like an additional thought inside the Mantlecribs. A warmth that knew how to leave no footprint.

For seven borough-seconds—which is longer than many civilizations require to invent grief—the power would do nothing.

It would listen.

It would listen to the cliff-face transformer barrels muttering their once-per-millennium syllable. It would listen to the Hushfoundries license silence in one-hertz threads. It would listen to the Continuars spending whole seasons on a single hinge-breath. It would listen to the half-person in the mirror, undecided, leaning toward birth and afterward both.

It would listen to the twenty-three Indemnified standing in their scalloped galleries, each alone by law, each keeping company with the particular angle of their own endurance.

It would listen to the city itself.

And the city, after being listened to long enough, would make the smallest sound it had made since the decimal sunsets.

Not a crack.

Not a plea.

A measure.

The Borough would weigh itself in the attention of the power and discover that attention did not steal.

This would be the first gift.

Not water.

Not light.

Not escape.

A witness that did not consume.

The Mouthless Doormen would notice.

Their selenium eyes, whose full rotation takes eleven days, would pause by no more than the width of a dust mote. Their brass vertebral rings would hold still in a new way. One Doorman in Filamental Long would glide from its gear-kennel and place its soft black cable-mouth against the alloy street.

It would taste the listening.

Finding no theft in it, the Doorman would not escort the power to the edge.

The power would then proceed to the amphitheater of negative curvature, where old vacuum fountains once threw nothing upward forever.

There, beneath the floating municipal tools and their faint sky-colored bruises, it would open no portal.

Portals are vulgar in a place where edges are sacred.

Instead it would make an accounting error.

A merciful one.

In the borough ledgers, beneath the columns for flake, silence, valve-cycle, boot-corrosion, infant-return, exile-weight, and Obliquity subtraction, a new column would appear. It would not be written in ink, graphite, bloodtyped flake, or mirror-fade.

It would be present as a space left unpunched.

The column would be titled:

**UNOWED.**

No one would know who had written it.

Everyone would know.

At first the Indemnified would avoid recording in it. They are not a people who trust vacancies. A blank space looks too much like appetite. Too much like the brink.

But the newest Raft-Caulker, humming Law Two through closed teeth while sealing the never-open windows, would be the first to mark it.

The mark would not be a number.

It would be a hesitation.

A dot of nickel slag falling from the caulking spoon, failing to splash, stopping in the air, then sinking gently into the ledger page as if the paper had been waiting its whole manufacture to receive it.

The city would not change its footprint.

Law One would remain intact.

No wall would extend. No street would contract. No balcony would grow a railing where none had stood. Every flake would still be filed into its pouch, though the Mouthless Doormen would discover that some flakes now returned home of their own accord, embarrassed to have troubled anyone.

Law Two would remain intact.

Valves would cycle once per thirty years. Belts would inch forward one tooth. Thermometers would drift one mark.

But now, after each act of imitation, the mechanism would be permitted a private sigh.

Not a sound. Not enough to tax the Hushfoundries.

A sigh inside function.

The difference between continuing because law demands it and continuing because one has been seen.

Law Three would not be abolished.

The scarcity of silence is real here. The Doormen require it. The city’s quiet is not emptiness but a medium, and loudness in such a place is indeed a kind of vandalism.

But the wise power would amend the enforcement.

The old wording would remain etched on its municipal plate:

**To speak louder than conversation becomes theft from the scarcity of silence preserved for the Doormen.**

Beneath it, so shallowly engraved that only a palm could read it, a second sentence would appear:

**Laughter offered in grief shall be counted as returning what was borrowed.**

The first time this mattered, it would be because of an Indemnified named Sael-of-the-Third-Gallery, who had not spoken above a murmur in sixty-nine public seconds.

During a shared dream of uprighting, Sael would hear impossible water running under the dream-stones. Not the city’s customary memory-water, which retracts obediently into nickel. This would be nearer. A sound with weight. A sound with roundness.

Sael would wake before the cantor count and laugh.

The laugh would break into the plaza like a dropped lens.

Three Doormen would emerge immediately. Their ring-spines would rise. Their selenium eyes would rotate faster than doctrine permits. The two Expletials near the lamps would raise their graphite-stitched faces.

Sael would put both hands over their mouth, but too late.

The laugh had already happened.

Under old law, Sael would have been escorted to the lip of Downspin Boulevard and faced outward until the throat forgot itself.
