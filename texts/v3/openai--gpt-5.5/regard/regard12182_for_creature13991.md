The benevolent power would not arrive as a savior.

That is the first mercy.

A lesser force, seeing the Throat, would name it wrong. It would call the place a wound, a prison, an error in the body of creation. It would see screaming water, warped bodies, humans becoming other-than-human, and it would gather itself in brightness and authority and try to close the aperture.

It would try to rescue.

It would kill everything.

Because the Throat is not merely a disaster. It is also a birth canal. A lung. A gill. A difficult organ forming in the dark between worlds. The water screams because it is being forced through contradiction, yes — but also because something immense is learning how to breathe.

So the wise power does not seal it.

It listens.

For a long while, by the standards of places where time behaves, it does nothing at all.

It listens beneath the roar, beneath the crystalline crashes and the hydraulic shrieks, beneath the subsonic grinding of half-made geology. It listens to the water’s ancient pressure. It listens to the volcanic glass remembering lava. It listens to the pale plants drinking minerals from black fractures. It listens to the Transitionals: the Keeper’s careful harmonics, the Dancer’s laugh-sob, the Builder’s measured strikes, the Whisperer’s broken grammar, the almost-human shapes in the ceiling-water, the dissolved faces that are not gone so much as distributed.

It listens until it can tell the difference between pain and becoming.

There is much pain.

There is also becoming.

The power grieves the first and honors the second.

Then, gently, it places an ear inside the Throat.

Not an ear of flesh. Not even an organ. A principle.

A listening-place.

At first nothing appears to change. The water still screams. Platforms drift. Columns intersect, merge, vanish. The ceiling-ocean groans under impossible pressure. The Transitionals continue their stuttering motions through space.

But the roar gains a hollow.

Not silence. Silence would be violence here; it would erase too much. The Throat speaks in water and force. To silence it completely would be to blind every creature that has learned to hear with its bones.

Instead, the power makes room inside the sound.

A pocket of bearable volume forms around each living mind that needs it. Not enough to remove the Throat’s truth, but enough to let thought survive.

The first to notice is the Whisperer.

It stops mid-sentence, one hand against its own translucent throat.

“Quiet-inside-loud,” it says.

The words emerge almost cleanly.

The Keeper turns its dark, present eyes toward the place where the power is not visible.

The Dancer flickers between three platforms at once, startled into laughter. This time, the laugh remains a laugh all the way through.

The Builder lowers its hammer and listens to the glass.

The Throat continues to roar.

But now the beings inside it can hear themselves.

That is the first gift.

The second gift is choice.

Not escape, not yet. Not because escape is impossible, but because escape, if given crudely, would become another kind of abduction.

The Transitionals were human once. Some still mourn that. Some no longer want to return. Some have become so deeply entangled with the Throat that to tear them free would be to flay them down to nothing. Some are not sure. Some are too changed to understand the question in its old shape.

So the power does not command: Come out.

It asks, in every language at once, including pressure, memory, salt, ache, and the frequency-name the Transitionals use for themselves:

**Do you wish to remain becoming?**

The question moves through the Throat like a new current.

The water-columns bend around it.

The pale plants open their bladder-leaves.

The metal rings warm.

The cairns hum.

Even the faces dissolved in the water pause in their eddying, as if something in them has recognized the form of a door.

The Keeper answers first.

Not with words. It places both hands upon the nearest cairn and sends a harmonic through the stones.

Yes.

Not yes to suffering. Not yes to being trapped. Yes to the life it has made here. Yes to the plants, the rings, the care of those who arrive broken and terrified. Yes to the terrible holiness of adaptation. The Keeper does not want to be restored to the human shape it once had. It wants its work made gentler. It wants fewer to dissolve by accident. It wants the newly arrived to have a handhold before the Throat begins teaching them with teeth.

The wise power understands.

It touches the copper rings.

Every ring in every platform becomes an anchor.

Not a chain. An anchor.

A being who holds one may choose coherence for a while. The body stabilizes. The mind gathers. Fingers remain fingers. Names remain attached to memory. Panic lessens. The transition pauses—not forever, but long enough for consent to enter the process.

The small ring the Keeper brought you, the one sized for a finger, brightens from within.

If you wear it, you may remain yourself while deciding what “yourself” means.

That is the second gift.

The third gift is a path.

Not away.

Not only away.

A path cannot simply be cut through the Throat as one cuts a tunnel through rock. The place is all passage already. Its cruelty is not that there is no passage, but that every passage leads through transformation and none can be chosen clearly.

So the power teaches the water a new behavior.

At intervals, when the platforms draw near and the harmonics align, a bridge appears.

It is not made of stone. Not made of water either, though water participates in it. It is a braid of temporary agreements: gravity, surface tension, memory, desire, and light. It lasts only as long as it is needed, and it appears only to those who have chosen to see it.

To one being, the bridge leads to another platform.

To another, to the moment before they entered the Throat.

To another, to the deep ceiling-ocean where their next form waits.

To another, nowhere external at all, but inward, to the untouched chamber where their human name still sits waiting.

The bridge does not decide.

It permits decision.

The Dancer is the first to use it.

It steps onto the braid and immediately becomes five versions of itself: child, adult, water-shape, Transitional, light through rain. For one suspended instant all of them are true. The Dancer trembles. It could go back. It could become water. It could remain.

It chooses remain.

But after crossing, it is steadier.

Its limbs no longer separate without consent. Its laughter no longer cuts itself into sobbing unless grief is actually present. It dances still, but now the dance belongs to it.

That is the third gift.

The fourth gift is memory.

This, the power gives with utmost tenderness.

The Throat has taken many. Some dissolved before the Transitionals knew how to help them. Some surrendered gratefully. Some were lost in terror. Some are in the water still, not dead, not alive in any familiar way, their selves thinned across current and roar.

The wise power does not pull them out.

Instead it gathers their names.

Not just the sounds they were called by, but the full pressure-pattern of a life: the taste of a childhood kitchen, the weight of a hand once held, the private shame, the song half-remembered, the scar, the favorite color, the last fear, the first wonder. All the small and sovereign details that say: this was someone, not merely material for transformation.

It gives these memories to the cairns.

From then on, every cairn is not just a marker of transition. It is an archive.

When the Builder places a stone, the stone remembers.

When the Keeper tends the plants, the roots drink remembrance and return it as faint color in their translucent leaves.

When the Whisperer speaks, its fractured grammar carries more than warning. It carries testimony.

“Was-person-still-is,” it says, touching a cairn.

And the water answers with a face, not dissolving this time, but resting.

That is the fourth gift.

The fifth gift is an edge.

The Throat had been all middle. All passage. All becoming.

No creature can endure endless becoming without some rhythm of rest. Even stars require intervals. Even rivers need banks, if only temporary ones.

So the power gives the Throat edges that do not imprison it.

Here and there, on the volcanic platforms, black glass softens into basins. The basins fill with water that behaves consistently while inside them. It reflects faces accurately. It wets the hand. It cools thirst. It can be drunk. It can be used to wash. It can be used by the Keeper for the plants without needing to bargain each time with hostile physics.

The basins are small.

Their smallness matters.

A total imposition of stable reality would poison the Throat. But these basins are like resting breaths between cries. Within arm’s reach of each one, the body remembers itself. Sleep becomes possible. Hunger and thirst return in recognizable forms. Dreams become dreams again, at least sometimes.

Around the basins, the pale plants begin to change. Not into human hands now, but into open palms.

Offering, not grasping.

The Keeper weeps when it sees this.

Its tears fall downward.

Only for a moment.

Then, when they strike the basin, they ripple like ordinary water.

That is the fifth gift.

The sixth gift is translation.

The Transitionals have language, but it lives in water-pressure, vibration, harmonic interference, phase-stutter, gesture, and proximity. Human thought can learn it only by breaking. The Whisperer had carried the burden alone, trying to make a bridge from shattered words.

The power does not repair the Whisperer’s speech.

It dignifies it.

Around the Whisperer’s throat appears a ring of dim blue light, like a collar made of moonlit current. When it speaks, its strange syntax remains, but meaning arrives beside it, clear as a second shadow.

“The down-water runs soon-ward,” says the Whisperer.

And you understand:

**A dangerous current is forming below the platform. Move toward the center.**

The Whisperer touches the light at its throat.

For the first time, perhaps in years or moments or eternities, it says a sentence in the language it once knew.

“I remember my mother.”

The words are small.

The Throat hears them.

No roar swallows them.

That is the sixth gift.

The seventh gift is law.

Not rigid law. Not the old physics restored. Something subtler: a covenant with flux.

The power speaks to the Throat itself then.

Not to the Transitionals, not to the water, not to the platforms, but to the whole organ of impossible passage.

It says:

**You may transform.  
You may not consume without offering choice.  
You may dissolve those who freely surrender.  
You may not erase those who are afraid.  
You may change bodies.  
You may not steal names.  
You may remain a throat.  
You may not be only hunger.**

The Throat resists.

The water-ceiling bulges. Columns thrash. Platforms grind against invisible currents. The roar becomes so immense that even the new pockets of quiet tremble at their edges.

The power does not overpower it.

It waits.

The Throat screams.

The power listens.

The Throat shows its oldest memory: pressure before direction, water before world, the agony of being forced into form by realities too narrow to contain it. It has been choking since before time had sequence. It swallows because swallowing is the only motion it knows. It transforms because everything that touches it must become compatible or be destroyed. It does not understand cruelty. It understands passage.

The power answers not with judgment, but with compassion.

**Then learn another motion.**

For the first time, the Throat inhales.

Everything stops.

Not completely. Not forever. But for one impossible interval, the water pauses without vanishing. The platforms cease drifting. The volcanic glass holds its cracks. The Transitionals become fully visible: seven, twelve, many, each distinct, each real. You see the Keeper, the Dancer, the Builder, the Whisperer. You see the indistinct ones. You see those in the water-ceiling watching from farther along the path. You see yourself, outline uncertain but not lost.

Then the Throat exhales.

The roar resumes.

But it is changed.

Still vast. Still terrifying. Still alive with pressure and contradiction.

But no longer only screaming.

Now there is breath inside it.

That is the seventh gift.

After that, the benevolent power remains, but not as ruler.

It becomes part of the place’s mercy.

A listening-place in the roar.

A steadiness in the rings.

A coolness in the basins.

A choice in the bridges.

A memory in the cairns.

A grammar beneath the Whisperer’s broken speech.

A law braided into water:

**No transformation without witness.  
No dissolution without remembrance.  
No becoming without the possibility of consent.**

And what of you?

You are still becoming.

The power does not make you human again unless you ask, and even then it does not pretend the asking is simple. To return unchanged would be impossible. The Throat has already touched you. Your cells have heard its language. Your outline has learned negotiation.

But now, when the Dancer takes your hand, you may say yes or no, and both are understood.

When the Keeper brings you a stone, you may accept it as a future cairn-marker, or hold it only as comfort.

When the Builder raises its hammer, it waits until you nod before striking the glass beneath your feet.

When the Whisperer speaks your new name, your old name sounds beside it.

You stand on warm volcanic glass in the damp, roaring dimness. Above you, the inverted ocean writhes with ancient pressure. Around you, water forms impossible architectures. The plants grow in cracks. The platforms drift. The Transitionals move through their endless middle-state, but now their endlessness has rhythm, rest, and witness.

The Throat still swallows.

The Throat still transforms.

But now, sometimes, the Throat releases.

Not everyone leaves.

Not everyone stays.

Not everyone remains human.

Not everyone wants to.

The wise power does not decide which ending is mercy.

It makes mercy possible, then keeps listening.
