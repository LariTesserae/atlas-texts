It would not arrive as light.

Light, in that place, was only another thing to be crushed, scattered, bruised into violet murk. Nor would it arrive as a voice, because voice was cruelty there; another pressure added to the billion waterfalls, another hand against the marrow.

The benevolent power would come first as an absence.

Not an empty void—there could be no void there—but a subtraction from harm.

A little less shear in one cubic mile of roaring slurry.  
A little less violence in one fold of broken geometry.  
A single pressure wave failing to find its victim.  
A ribbon of dark liquid hesitating, as if it had remembered shame.

The basin would not notice at first. It was too old, too loud, too committed to its own terminal boiling. The mud-waves would continue to rear upward in slow, impossible walls. The heavy water would continue to fall in every direction. The pale monoliths would weep their rusted rain. The string-weeds would whip and feed on friction. Far above, a translucent, mile-wide thing would drift through the bruised density and fold itself through a wound in space.

But one Disjunct would notice.

It would be an old one, if age had meaning for beings whose bodies were never in one place long enough to be counted. Its crimson sacs had thickened into dark, veined masses. Its filament-fans were torn at the edges by centuries of abrasive silt. Its black resonator trembled constantly from the effort of singing against the world.

It had made pilgrimage to the same anchor-stone for longer than memory could bear.

The stump was pale, smooth, and featureless, protruding from the slurry like the knuckle of a drowned god. Around it, the string-weeds clung and lashed, translucent nerves in the current. The Disjunct waited in the slipstream, its organs arranged around absences, its self threaded through folds no eye could see.

It waited for the glitch.

It waited for the dead architecture to remember itself.

It waited for one fraction of a second of stairs, balconies, columns, glass, angle, edge.

But when the reality-storm struck, something different happened.

The monolith did not flare and vanish.

It breathed.

For the first time in eons, the ghost of the Built did not erupt like a wound. It unfolded slowly, gently, as though a careful hand were lifting a sheet from a sleeping face.

A line appeared.

A true line.

Not a ribbon of liquid pretending to be straight before knotting into nonsense. Not a hallucinated tangent shredded by turbulence. A line with fidelity to itself. A line that began, continued, and ended without betrayal.

The Disjunct froze in its scatteredness.

Its resonator nearly lost the counter-song.

Around the pale stump, phantom glass rose in silence. Not the whole impossible tower at once, not the full remembered arrogance of the vanished world, but a single room.

A dry room.

No—more merciful than dry. Dryness would have been violence to a creature born of wet pressure. It was instead a chamber of moderated density, a place where water and air and silt negotiated instead of devoured. Its boundaries were not hard walls but agreements. Its angles were real, yet forgiving. Its floor was firm enough to be trusted and soft enough not to condemn.

The Disjunct did not understand.

So the power did nothing more.

That was its wisdom.

It did not seize the creature.  
It did not assemble it.  
It did not declare, Be healed.  
It did not mistake longing for consent.

It held the room steady for one heartbeat.

Then two.

Then ten.
