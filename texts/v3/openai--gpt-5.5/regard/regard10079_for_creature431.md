The benevolent power would not descend in radiance.

Radiance would be cruel here.

Light, sudden and pure, would blind the children who had never known the sun. It would burn the moss back into darkness. It would throw hard shadows where the Tenders had learned to read softness, fog, shimmer, the half-seen warning before a pump failed or a figure passed between pillars. It would announce itself as an intrusion, and the Drain Works already had enough intrusions: drills, extraction engines, corporate surveys, sealed doors, utility mandates from people who drank water without ever tasting the mineral grief of its making.

So the power would come quietly.

It would arrive first as a pause in a bad sound.

Not silence. Never silence. Silence would terrify the Drain Works.

But one night, during the long unmarked hours when the city above slept under heated ceilings and the Deep Dwellers moved through the service corridors with thermos-coffee and aching knees, the extraction engines would skip one harmful harmonic. A vibration that had been slowly grinding cartilage, loosening bolts, thinning sleep, and calling headaches into the skulls of workers would simply… fall out of the music.

The Tenders would notice.

They noticed everything.

Old Mera, whose left glove had mirrored tape arranged in the signal-pattern for *I am here, don’t startle me*, would stop beneath the pillar called Broken Crown and tilt her helmet.

A young Tender would flash: *pressure drop?*

Mera would answer: *no.*

Then, after a moment: *listening.*

The benevolent power would listen too.

It would listen through condensation nets and moss plots, through pipes furred with mineral scale, through the rail systems where maintenance drones clanged like iron beetles overhead. It would listen to the city’s thirst, which was real. It would listen to the engines’ hunger, which was designed. It would listen to the workers’ exhaustion, which had been made ordinary by policy. It would listen to the children’s laughter echoing off wet concrete. It would listen to the sealed barriers, and to whatever pressed its long patience against the other side.

It would not decide too quickly who was monster, who was victim, who was trespasser.

Wise powers are slow to name.

Benevolent powers are careful with names.

For seven days, it would do almost nothing.

A drone would fail to crush a sleeping child because a corroded rail bolt, scheduled to shear at 03:18, would hold until 03:23.

A heat exchanger would vent three degrees cooler beside a Deep Dweller shelter where fever had taken root in a woman’s lungs.

Moss growing around Seal Twelve would brighten, not with warning, but with a new color: blue-green, like remembered sea.

A Maintenance Chief topside would wake from a dream in which the Drain Works were not a facility but a throat, and in the throat were people, and in the people were names. The next morning, she would find herself unable to sign the transfer denial forms for the Tenders who had asked for additional medical rotations. Her hand would not move. She would think she was having a neurological event. She would go to the clinic. Nothing would be found.

On the eighth day, the power would begin its work.

Not by overturning the machines.

The city needed water.
