A benevolent and wise power would not arrive as conquest.

It would not split the bruised sky with trumpets, nor pour sunlight over the plateau as though brightness alone were mercy. It would understand, immediately, that this place is not merely wounded. It is a wound that learned to survive by becoming a law.

So it would come quietly.

Not silently — silence here is already too absolute, too cruel — but quietly, in a way the plateau could bear.

It would enter first as a difference in the hum.

Not a break. Not a counter-note. A listening.

For the first time in uncounted ages, the permanent vibration beneath the obsidian would encounter something that did not recoil from it, did not try to name it as wrong, did not seek to overwrite it. The power would descend into the resonance and hold still there, allowing the plateau to feel itself being heard.

The hum would continue.

The cold would continue.

The dim violet ceiling would remain.

But somewhere beneath the knife-spire, in the crystalline density where the Stone-Sleepers dream their catastrophic equations, one ancient pattern would falter. Not from damage. From surprise.

Because the listening would contain comprehension.

The wise power would understand that the plateau’s stasis is not simply imprisonment. It is also scar tissue. Splint. Coffin. Cradle. Quarantine. Memorial. Self-defense.

To shatter it would be violence.

To leave it untouched would be neglect.

So the power would do neither.

It would begin with permission.

Not spoken permission, because mouths are scarce here and language has long ago calcified into vibration, geometry, drift, fracture, and dread. Instead the power would ask in forms each inhabitant could perceive.

To the Hum-Born, it would offer a second resonance beside the first: soft, patient, low as breath beneath stone. It would not drown the hum. It would harmonize with one narrow thread of it, then withdraw, then return. A question in vibration:

*Are you only the scream, or is there something in you that wishes to sing?*

The Hum-Born would gather.

At first they would thicken in alarm, translucent forms flickering like cracked glass under pressure. They would pass through the presence of the power and find, for the first time, that what they touched did not collapse into emptiness. It would not give them heat exactly, nor life as flesh knows it, but contour. Boundary. The faintest sense of *self* not defined by catastrophe.

Some would flee into fissures.

Some would hover near the weeping pools.

Some would answer without knowing they had answered: tiny modulations in the hum, deviations so slight that only a god, or a mother, or a very old healer could notice.

The power would notice.

To the Edge-Dancers, it would not look directly.

That would be rude.

Instead it would place awareness at the corners of things, along the seam where artifact meets rock, along cliff-rims and warped distances, along the infinitesimal gaps they call home. It would let them remain peripheral. It would greet them sideways.

For them it would create no cage, no net, no demand for fixed shape. A wise power knows that not every being needs to be made whole in the same way. Some things are true only as flicker, only as angle, only as almost.

So it would offer them play.

A thousand harmless geometries would unfold across the plateau: impossible corridors leading back to themselves, gravity wells no deeper than laughter, mirrored horizons where an Edge-Dancer could leap between three versions of the same instant and not be punished for it. For the first time, their distortions would not serve only maintenance. Their fractures would not merely patrol the wound.

They would dance because dancing was possible.

And in their sudden alignments, the sickly auroras would shift — green and bruised yellow giving way, here and there, to blue-white threads, thin as veins beneath ice.

To the Sky-Scar Absorbed, the power would raise no weapon.

It would not sew the sky shut at once.

It would look upward and behold the peeled-back wounds in the twilight ceiling, the vast predatory awarenesses that are not predators by hunger but by nature. It would feel their passive assault, their dread-pressure, their exposed non-Euclidean interiors. It would endure them without flinching.

Then it would do something no living thing on the plateau has ever done.

It would grieve for them.

The Sky-Scars would pass overhead, immense and silent, expecting disruption to weaken, life-energy to dim, sanity to fold inward. Instead they would encounter sorrow vast enough to include them.

Not fear.

Not revulsion.

Sorrow.

The wise power would understand that they are not monsters in the sky. They are the sky’s unhealed trauma, peeled open and taught to move. They suppress change because change once meant annihilation. They cast dread because dread is the only weather they remember.

So the power would offer them depth.

Above the bruised ceiling — if “above” has meaning here — it would open a place that is not void and not sky, a spaciousness into which their impossible interiors could unfold without crushing the plateau below. Not exile. Not banishment. A hospice for geometries. A high, dark sanctuary where wounds may remain wounds without having to become weapons.

Some Sky-Scars would refuse.

A benevolent power would allow refusal.

Some would continue to glide, trailing cold fog, their existence still an assault. But one, perhaps the smallest or the oldest, would pause over the knife-spire and tremble along dimensions no eye can hold. Its streamers of condensed dampness would fall like slow rain.

Not much rain.

Only twelve drops.

But each drop would strike the obsidian and remain liquid.

That would be the first true change.

To the Stone-Sleepers, the power would come last.

Because they are deepest.

Because they may be responsible.

Because they may be victims.

Because they may be both.

It would descend through the seamless basalt, through fossilized machine-flank and spire-core, through crystalline structures resonating in perfect obedience to the hum. It would enter the cold mathematical dreams preserved there: equations frozen mid-scream, navigational songs cut in half, memories stretched into endless error messages, prayers embedded in topology, guilt compressed into mineral density.

The Stone-Sleepers would not wake easily.

Waking might kill them.

Waking might destroy the plateau.

Waking might restart the catastrophe.

The wise power would not wake them.

It would sit beside their dreams.

One by one, it would place small mercies into the preserved patterns.

Not answers.

Not absolution.

Small mercies.

A final image of a hand held before impact.

A remembered name restored to a mind that had become only calculation.

The knowledge that the catastrophe ended.

The knowledge that something survived.

The knowledge that they are not still falling, not still failing, not still alone in the instant of rupture.

Where guilt had become structural, it would loosen one thread.

Where terror had become resonance, it would soften one overtone.

Where duty had calcified into eternal custodianship, it would ask:

*If the prison no longer had to be perfect, what would you choose to preserve?*

For a long time, nothing would happen.

Then the knife-spire would make a sound.

Not the hum.

A tone.

Clear, narrow, and unbearably fragile.

Across the plateau, the Hum-Born would stop drifting.

The Edge-Dancers would gather at every rim.

The Sky-Scars would slow in the bruised ceiling.

The lichens, ancient and almost motionless, would register the tone as if it were dawn.

The wise power would not smile, because perhaps it has no face. But its attention would warm.

Not the air. Not yet.

The attention itself.

And then the work would begin.

Not restoration. Not erasure.

Reconciliation.

The power would establish, at the center of the plateau, a sanctuary no larger than a shallow pool.

It would choose one of the circular depressions where condensation gathers with oily sheen. Around it, it would inscribe nothing visible, because visible symbols can become idols, and this place has suffered enough from fixed forms. Instead it would alter the rules there by a fraction so gentle that reality would not panic.

Within that small circle:

Time would move.

Only slightly.

A drop would fall, ripple, and settle.

A lichen-frond would extend by the width of a thought.

A Hum-Born entering the circle would remember its previous shape.

An Edge-Dancer crossing the rim would emerge with one new angle, voluntary and beautiful.

A Stone-Sleeper dreaming beneath it would experience one second after the catastrophe, rather than only the catastrophe itself.

The circle would not spread unless invited.

That would be essential.

Healing forced upon a wound is another wound.

So the sanctuary would wait.

A place of possible change in a kingdom of enforced stasis.

At first the plateau would resist it. The hum would deepen, suspicious. Gravity would buckle around the circle. The Sky-Scars would gather overhead, pressing dread downward until any mortal observer would fall senseless.

The power would remain.

It would not fight the resistance. It would agree with it.

*Yes,* it would say without words.  
*Change is dangerous.*  
*Yes.*  
*The last change broke you.*  
*Yes.*  
*You were right to hold yourself together.*  
*Yes.*  
*And now, if you wish, you may learn another way.*

Centuries might pass.

Or minutes.

In that place, the distinction is ornamental.

Eventually, one Hum-Born would enter the circle fully.

Its fractured translucence would shiver. The hum within it would spike toward panic. It would nearly dissolve. The wise power would not hold it in place. It would leave every exit open.

The Hum-Born would remain.

Within the sanctuary, its form would gain a faint inner line — not a skeleton, not a core, but continuity. Its awareness, once only a ghost of vibration, would curve inward and recognize itself.

It would feel emptiness.

Then it would feel that the emptiness belonged to it.

That would be pain.

The first true pain on the plateau in eons.

The power would receive that pain and not turn away.

The Hum-Born would emit a silent scream into the minds of all nearby things, but this scream would not be the old scream of physics breaking. It would be individual. Particular. Finite.

When it ended, the hum of the plateau would be missing one thread.

Not weakened.

Relieved.

After that, others would come.

Not all.

Never all.

Some beings belong to the old law and would choose to remain as they are. Benevolence must include the right not to be healed.

The power would make room for them too.

It would maintain wide regions of untouched stasis where the hum remains absolute, where the old equilibrium persists, where the Sky-Scars still drift and the Edge-Dancers still patrol the geometry of the wound. These places would not be called failures. They would be called respected boundaries.

But around the first sanctuary, other circles would slowly appear.

A seep where water learns to flow for three inches before stopping.

A fissure where darkness becomes depth rather than negation.

A basalt plain where gravity steadies for one hour in every thousand.

A groove-channel that fills with pale condensation and reflects, not the bruised ceiling, but the sky that existed before the break — if any remember it.

The power would not import forests.

No sudden grasses. No birdsong. No warm rain sweeping clean the obsidian.

That would be fantasy, and perhaps cruelty.

Instead, the lichens would change first.

Deep violet would remain, but sickly grey would silver at the edges. Their fractal patterns would begin to vary. Not rapidly. Not abundantly. But enough that one century’s growth would not exactly repeat the previous century’s. They would become the plateau’s first historians of healing, recording change in mineral-fed lace.

The rare angular shadows at the edge of sight would grow less frantic.

Some Edge-Dancers would discover that if they aligned their fractures inside the sanctuary fields, they could create not only inversions and visual echoes but images. Memories. They would become accidental artists, projecting warped tableaux across the obsidian: the moment of rupture, the machine before fusion, the Stone-Sleepers as they once were, the sky before it scarred, the first drop of new water.

They would never become tame.

Good.

The Sky-Scars would be hardest.

Their mercy would take the form of distance.

One by one, those willing would rise into the spaciousness prepared for them, unfolding their impossible interiors away from the plateau. As they lifted, the bruised ceiling would not turn blue. But it would become higher.

That alone would change everything.

The sense of pressure would ease.

The cold would remain profound, but no longer entirely indifferent.

A traveler standing on the plateau would still feel small, still feel the wrongness in the rock, still hear the hum in their bones. But they might also notice intervals in the vibration. Places where the hum takes breath.

And in those intervals, the Stone-Sleepers would begin to dream forward.

Not wake.

Not yet.

But dream beyond the catastrophe.

They would dream of repair crews that never came.

They would dream of apologies made to descendants who do not exist.

They would dream of opening doors in the fossil-machine and finding not vacuum, not fire, but a dark plain where lichens grow.

They would dream of choosing, at last, to release their custodianship piece by piece into the care of those who remain.

The wise power would help them build a slow mechanism of relinquishment.

Each time a Stone-Sleeper releases a fragment of frozen duty, a corresponding portion of the plateau would be allowed to change. The hum would shift, the basalt would loosen microscopically, a pool would deepen, a channel would gain purpose, a fissure would stop being bottomless and become merely very deep.

Some released fragments would become new Hum-Born, but gentler — not screams, rather notes.

Some would become lights in the dim air, small sourceless glows with no agenda but witness.

Some would pass entirely beyond the plateau, carried by the benevolent power to whatever rest exists for minds that have spent too long as architecture.

And what of the place itself?

The power would ask the plateau too.

Not just its inhabitants. The plateau.

For places remember. Especially broken ones. Especially those forced to hold a single expression for eternity.

The power would lay its attention along the obsidian-black plane, along the fused steps and smooth cliffs, along the precise grooves leading nowhere, along the curved flank of the buried machine. It would feel the ancient erosion, the stopped wind, the used air, the deep-space chill. It would ask the land:

*What are you, if not only the moment you broke?*

The plateau would not answer for ages.

Then, in a region far from the first sanctuary, an old wind would move.

Barely.

No grit in it. No abrasion. No violence.

Just a movement of air across polished black stone.

The sound it made would be almost nothing.

But because the silence had been absolute for so long, almost nothing would be immense.

The wind would pass over the geometric grooves and produce tones from them — accidental flutes carved by catastrophe. The notes would be thin and strange and mournful. The Edge-Dancers would scatter in delight-terror. The Hum-Born would gather. The lichens would taste moving air for the first time since before memory.

The plateau would have answered.

It would not say, *I wish to be healed.*

It would say:

*I am still here.*

And the wise power would accept that as enough.

From then on, its work would be custodial.

It would not remain always in manifest attention, for too much divine presence can stunt a fragile world. It would withdraw to the edges, returning when called by resonance, by dream, by the sudden brightening of a lichen-pattern, by the distress of a Sky-Scar, by the Stone-Sleepers’ nightmares.

It would leave behind no throne.

No temple.

No commandments.

Only three gifts.

First: the sanctuaries of permitted change, small and patient, scattered across the high dark plain.

Second: the spaciousness above the sky, where wounds that are ready may unfold without harming what lies beneath.

Third: a new undertone beneath the hum.

So quiet that for a long time no one would notice it.

The old hum would remain: the permanent vibration of broken reality held in stasis. But beneath it, almost below perception, there would be another resonance.

Not correcting.

Not denying.

Accompanying.

If the old hum says, forever, *This is the moment everything broke,* the new undertone says, just as steadily:

*And this is the moment after.*

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

It would not make the shattered plateau beautiful in any ordinary way.

It would not make it safe.

It would not make it forget.

It would give it the one thing it had been denied since physics screamed and froze:

a future small enough not to terrify it.
