A truly wise power would not begin by silencing the world.

That would be its first mercy: restraint.

It would hear the planetary shriek, feel the pain in the newborn crust, perceive the Lithic Anchors moving across the broken altitudes like antibodies made of patience—and it would understand that the roar is not merely suffering. It is weather. It is blood. It is respiration. The acoustic jungles drink it. The Anchors see by it. The whole unfinished reality is using its own agony as scaffolding.

So the power would not descend as a hand from above to smooth every mountain and still every fault.

It would listen.

For a long age, perhaps longer than the first mountains had existed, the benevolent thing would do nothing visible. It would spread itself thinly through the violet-black sky, not as light, not as warmth, but as attention. It would learn the grammar of the place: the way gravity listed before a seam tore open; the way shadows leaned hungrily toward wounds in space; the way a knot, a true knot, could command more obedience from reality than any theorem carved in fire.

It would watch the Anchors.
