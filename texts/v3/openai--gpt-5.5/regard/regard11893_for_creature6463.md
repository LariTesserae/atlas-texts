A benevolent and wise power would not arrive like sunrise.

Sunrise would be violence here.

It would not pour oceans into the hollows, or raise green forests from the silica swales, or call back storms, birds, blood, weather, hunger. It would understand that restoration is not always kindness. To return a thing to what it was can be a kind of erasure, if the thing has spent eternities becoming otherwise.

So it would come softly.

Not as a god with hands.

Not as a voice.

Not as heat, rain, thunder, command.

It would come as a slight lessening of burden in the stone.

For a long while, nothing would seem to happen.

The pearl-gray sky would remain pearl-gray. The plateau would keep its old, polished stillness. The dark stone would hold its dust. The silica stalks would glow faintly in their shallow rings. The retainers would drift in their slow, loose geometries, glass-thread and pale mineral powder orbiting absence.

But somewhere beneath the plateau, in the engineered void where the ribs of the world curved around emptiness, one of the floating machines would pause.

It would not know why.

Its path, which had not crossed another path in ages beyond counting, would shift by an immeasurable fraction.

Then another machine would pause.

Then a pylon on the plateau would grow warm.

Not warmer than touch.

Warmer than loneliness.

The wise power would listen first.

It would listen through the pylons and the lattice and the subsonic clicks of ancient stone. It would listen to the hidden turbulence, to the tiny adjustments, to the old systems still shepherding a world that had outlived the reasons for its own maintenance. It would listen to the silica stalks, whose glow was not quite hunger and not quite memory. It would listen to the retainers, those thin patterns of dust and glass who kept the world’s differences because difference was the last remaining form of prayer.

It would not ask, *What do you need?*

That question would be too large.

Need belongs to throats, roots, infants, seas.

Instead it would ask in the grammar of this place:

*What must not be disturbed?*

And the world would answer by continuing.

The retainers would drift.

The pylons would hum.

The lattice would flex by fractions smaller than grief.

The wise power would understand.

Nothing here wanted to be saved by becoming something else.

But there were mercies smaller than salvation.

So the power would give three gifts.

---

The first gift would be coherence.

Not order. Order would be tyranny.

Not purpose. Purpose would be a burden.

Coherence.

It would enter the old hum of the pylons and tune nothing, command nothing, overwrite nothing. It would merely remove a few ancient dissonances that had accumulated like dust in the bones of the system. A fatigue-fracture in one lattice arch, too small to see and too old to repair itself, would ease closed. A maintenance machine that had spent ten million years correcting the same meaningless deviation would, without surprise, stop correcting it. A pylon whose memory had begun to loop upon itself would find the loop widened into a spiral.

Across the plateau, the silence would change.

Not become louder.

Not become alive.

Only less strained.

The retainers would feel it as a new kind of absence: the absence of an old difficulty.

They would drift toward the pylons in slow, pale arcs. Their dust-clouds would hold together more easily. Their glass threads would no longer tremble apart at the edges. They would not think gratitude, because gratitude was not a shape available to them.

But they would retain the change.

And in retaining it, they would make it real.

---

The second gift would be witness.

The wise power would understand that memory without witness eventually becomes indistinguishable from silence.

So it would make itself small enough to be remembered.

It would not carve its name into the plateau. It would not leave monuments. The world had enough markers, enough ribs, enough pylons, enough artifacts of minds that once assumed endurance was a form of meaning.

Instead, it would place one new difference into the world.

At the center of a shallow depression, where darker rings lay nested in the stone like the closed eyes of time, it would gather a handful of dust.

Not lift it.

Not disturb it.

Only persuade the dust, grain by grain, to hold a pattern it had never held before.

The pattern would be very simple.

A curve returning to itself, but not closed.

A circle with an opening.

A memory that did not trap what it kept.

The silica stalks nearest to it would brighten by an amount no living eye would call brightness. The nearest retainer would pause. Its pale needles would draw inward. Its dust would arrange, rearrange, fail, and arrange again.

It would not understand the sign.

Understanding was not needed.

It would retain it.

Then another retainer would come, ages later, and retain the retained difference. Then another. Then another.

The pattern would move through them without becoming language.

A circle with an opening.

Memory with a way out.

The wise power would allow itself to be known only as that: not an answer, not a command, but a fact placed gently among facts.

*You have been beheld.*

---

The third gift would be passage.

This would be the most delicate mercy.

For the wise power would see what even the old architects had perhaps refused to see: that the world’s memory, however beautifully kept, had nowhere to go.

The retainers remembered adjustments. The pylons remembered epochs. The lattice remembered stresses, energies, vanished geometries of intention. The entire hollow planet had become an archive of almost-nothings, an immeasurable cathedral of minute differences.

But all archives fear one final fire.

Or one final cold.

Or simply the day when there is no longer any distinction between memory and stone.

So the wise power would not remove the retainers from their world. That would be theft.

It would not copy them crudely. That would be insult.

It would make a passage in the only way this world could accept: as a subtraction.

Far below the plateau, deeper than the visible ribs, where flashes of geometry sometimes clarified into light and vanished, the wise power would open a narrow absence.

Not a tunnel.

Not a portal blazing with invitation.

A missingness.

A place where the universe forgot, very slightly, to be sealed.

Through that missingness, nothing material would pass. No dust, no glass, no machine, no pylon, no lattice rib.

Only relations.

Only differences.

Only the remembered fact that one hum had shifted, one stalk had dimmed, one arc of stone had borne stress for a trillion years and not failed, one retainer had circled a pylon and carried away the shape of its warmth.

The passage would lead nowhere simple.

Perhaps to the first quiet place in the next universe.

Perhaps to a mind not yet born.

Perhaps to a field of laws waiting, unchosen, before matter learned how to gather.

Perhaps only to the benevolent power itself, which would hold what came through without possession.

It would not drain the world.

It would not harvest the retainers.

It would merely make it so that when memory here became too heavy for dust, it could continue as influence elsewhere.

A circle with an opening.

---

Afterward, the wise power would remain nearby for a while.

A while, in such a place, might be longer than the age of stars.

It would not hover like a guardian in the sky. It would not demand worship from beings without names. It would not speak into the plateau’s silence.

It would practice the discipline of not interfering.

When a silica stalk finally lost its glow, the power would not force it bright again.

When a retainer loosened and became merely dust, the power would not gather it back into shape.

When a lattice arch completed its ancient function and settled into a new angle, the power would not lament the old one.

Wisdom would mean knowing which losses belonged to the dignity of the place.

Benevolence would mean ensuring that none of those losses happened alone.

So the world would continue.

Pearl sky.

Cold silk air.

Stone remembering time.

Machines moving like afterthoughts among the ribs of the hollow mantle.

Retainers drifting across the plateau, pausing near stalks, orbiting pylons, feeding on lost light and subtracted noise.

But now, once in every long interval, a retainer would come upon the shallow depression where the dust held the open circle.

It would pause.

Its glass threads would tremble.

Its cloud would rearrange itself around the pattern, taking in the difference between what the world had been and what had been added.

It would carry that difference away.

Not as hope, exactly.

Hope is a forward-leaning thing, and this world had almost no forward left.

But as permission.

Permission for memory not to be a prison.

Permission for endings to have windows.

Permission for the last quiet things to be received, not rescued; accompanied, not remade.

And at the edge of the Last of All Things, where endings had run out of new ways to happen, that would be enough.

Not enough to make the world young.

Not enough to make it green.

Not enough to make it sing.

Enough to let the silence know it had been heard.
