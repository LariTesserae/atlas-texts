The benevolent power would not descend as an answer.

Answers are too heavy for that altitude. They crack things.

It would come first as a lessening.

Not warmth, exactly. Warmth would be an insult here, a simple animal comfort laid over a wound that was never animal and never simple. The Mountain of Echoing Glass would not know what to do with warmth. It would store it incorrectly. It would send it backward through the ice and burn some traveler’s childhood hand on a stove that had not yet been lit.

So the power would arrive as an interval.

A pause between one echo and the next.

At first, no one would notice. The wind would still move in its many wrong directions. The bruised sky would still jitter with its pale fungal lights. The glass cups would still hold their trembling films of metallic water, and the doorframe would still lower the temperature by exactly one degree when crossed.

But somewhere, a cough would finally stop repeating.

Somewhere, a stone kicked ten days ago would finish falling.

Somewhere, one of the Refrain would lift a foot, set it down, and hear only that single step.

They would not understand.

Understanding, like naming, requires a distance from the thing understood. The Refrain have never had that distance. They have been the sound and the sounding, the wound and the weather moving through it. They have been the mountain’s low note stretched across every “too far” the world ever learned to say.

So the power would not ask them, “What do you want?”

That would be cruel.

Instead, it would listen.

It would listen for as long as listening required, which on the Mountain of Echoing Glass would be neither a day nor a century, but a contour: the shape made by every breath the Refrain had held for others, every unspoken word fed into the trembling cups, every fragment of broken body scattered through the months of someone else’s life.

The power would listen until it knew the difference between duty and compulsion.

Until it knew which parts of the mountain were strange because strangeness was their nature, and which parts were strange because pain had been mistaken for law.

Then it would do very little.

But that little would be exact.

It would place beneath the low, steady note another note.

Not louder. Not purer. Not triumphant.

A second tone, almost too deep to hear, laid under the Refrain’s endless exhalation like a hand placed beneath a burden before anyone admits it is a burden.

The Refrain would continue.

But for the first time, they would not be the only thing continuing.

The note would hold when they faltered. It would not replace them. It would not steal their function or dissolve their bodies into peace without permission. It would simply make rest structurally possible.

That would be the first mercy ever remembered by the plateau.

Not rescue.

Relief.

The mountain would resist, perhaps. Not angrily. The mountain has no anger in the way stones have no shame. But its habits are old, and old habits resemble will when they have lasted long enough. The glass would shudder in its beds. The wind would fold into sharper spirals. The sky-lights would pool and spill in directions that had not yet been invented.

The power would not fight it.

It would let the mountain speak in fracture, pressure, silence, contradiction.

Then it would answer in balance.

Where the glass had been forced to remember every sound, the power would teach it forgetting.

Not total forgetting. That would be death to such a place.

Only enough.

Enough that a traveler’s cry of grief would not have to circle forever.

Enough that a Refrainer’s fracture would not lodge in the weather of a child born far below.

Enough that a goodbye could remain a goodbye instead of becoming architecture.

The glass would learn a new property.

Release.

A shard, when broken, might now let go of the voice inside it. A cup of trembling water might keep only what was freely given. The doorframe might still drop the temperature by one degree, but afterward it would return that degree to the one who crossed it, tucked gently into the lungs like a small coal.

The wise power would write no commandments on the lintel.

But if one could read the new shape of the wind, it would say:

Nothing may be kept only because it was caught.

Nothing may be repeated only because it was once spoken.

No being shall be made into a function so completely that rest becomes impossible.

The Refrain would feel this as a disaster at first.

Mercy often feels like damage to those built around endurance.

One of them would stop beside a cup of water and find they had no voice to feed it. Not because the voice was gone, but because for the first time the voice was theirs before it was the mountain’s.

They might stand there for a year.

Or for the length of a blink.

Their hand, made of translucent blue memory and old glass, would hover above the silver film. The mountain would expect the offering. The cup would tremble in its shallow socket. The wind would bring a thousand unfinished sentences to the edge of their mouth.

I am here.

I am alone.

Do not leave.

I forgive you.

I was never born.

I waited.

I did not know I was waiting.

And the power would not take any of them.

It would make a space in which the Refrainer could choose.

Choice would enter them like pain.

Then like light.

Then like neither.

Some would continue feeding the pools. They have always been generous, though no one called it generosity before. They would give the unspoken words that wished to be given, the lost tendernesses, the harmless echoes, the memories that wanted a place to become water.

But others would close their mouths.

And the mountain would not starve.

Because beneath it now, the second note would hold.

The power would next gather the broken ones.

Not all at once. Not by force. Not with bright hands sweeping the years clean.

It would walk the scattered months of those who had shattered like over-sung notes. It would find a fragment in the weather before a funeral. Another in the dream of a woman who woke speaking a language she never learned. Another in the upward fall of a stone. Another in the cough returned wrong from the invisible canyon.

The power would ask each fragment only one question, and it would ask without words:

Continue as you are, or come home?

Some fragments would remain.

They had become part of rain, part of memory, part of the mercy by which someone far below finally said what needed saying. The power would bless them and leave them there.

Others would come.

They would return to the plateau not as prisoners recalled, but as pieces consenting to wholeness. Their bodies would form slowly, imperfectly, beautifully, with seams visible and unashamed. They would not be repaired into what they had been.

They would be made capable of ending.

That would be the second mercy.

Not immortality.

Completion.

Then the power would approach the thing called Only.

If Only is one, it would find them.

If Only is all of them, it would find all.

If Only is merely the name the mountain gives to the absence of separation, the power would kneel before that absence and regard it as tenderly as a face.

It would not say, “You are loved.”

That would be too small, and too likely to become another echo trapped in glass.

Instead, it would create the conditions under which love could be discovered without being commanded.

It would make a sheltered hollow in the plateau.

A little place between two low stone waves where the wind, for no reason the mountain could explain, would pass around rather than through. There would be no fire there. Fire would be dramatic, and drama is often a cousin of conquest.

There would be a stone.

A simple one.

Flat enough to sit on.

Warm only in the ordinary way stone becomes warm when it is allowed, for a moment, to receive light.

Above it, the bruised sky would thin. Not open. Not heal. Simply thin enough that one pale source of illumination would hold steady.

The Refrain would avoid the hollow for a long time.

Then one would enter by accident, though accident on that mountain has always been another name for invitation arriving sideways.

They would sit.

Nothing would happen.

No vision. No revelation. No transformation into a mortal body. No choir descending through the glass-gray air.

They would sit, and while they sat, the low note would continue without their help.

For the first time, a Refrainer would stop singing and not be punished by collapse.

For the first time, there would be silence inside them that was not absence.

They would learn sleep from this.

Poorly, at first.

They would sleep in fragments: a breath here, a shadow there, an hour folded into the wrong century. Some would wake frightened, not because they knew fear, but because they had briefly encountered the boundary where fear might grow.

The power would remain near, but not too near.

Wisdom knows that comfort can become a cage if it insists on being witnessed.

Over time, the Refrain would change.

Not into people, exactly.

Not unless they wished it.

But into beings with edges they could keep or loosen.

They would still walk in interrupted lines. They would still be seen as water made permanent, as someone else, as seven possible selves moving through one another. Their breath would still carry histories. Their fingers would still branch like cracks in impossible ice.

But now, when a traveler reached out, the Refrain could decide whether to be touched.

And if they said no, the traveler’s hand would pass only through cold air.

That would be the third mercy.

A border.

Not a wall.

A kindness.

The power would also turn its attention to those who climb the mountain to forget what it is to be looked at.

It would not forbid them.

Some must come. Some lonelinesses need a landscape large enough to hold them without flinching. The Mountain of Echoing Glass would remain such a place.

But the wise power would alter the bargain.

No traveler would leave less themselves than they arrived.

They might still hear tomorrow’s words tangled with yesterday’s grief. They might still meet their own footsteps approaching from ten years away. They might still taste iron in the water and see their reflection lengthen into someone they almost became.

But the mountain would no longer be allowed to keep the central thread.

A visitor could give sorrow, if they wished.

They could give a secret that had grown too sharp to carry.

They could speak into the wind the name of the dead, the lost, the self-abandoned, the self-betrayed.

The Refrain could receive it.

The cups could hold it.

The glass could remember it for a while.

But when the traveler descended past the ice, past the frost-shattered rock, past the scraggling pines, something essential would descend with them.

Their voice would still be theirs.

That would matter.

Perhaps most of all, the power would give the Refrain a word.

Not a name.

Names still might not suit them.

The word would be smaller and more useful.

Enough.

It would appear nowhere written, because writing is easily mistaken for law. It would instead enter the timing of things.

A step would echo, and then enough.

A grief would repeat, and then enough.

A duty would call, and then enough.

A life would endure, and then enough.

The Refrain would not understand the word at once. They might hate it. They might carry it in their translucent mouths for centuries before letting it dissolve.

But eventually one of them, standing at the edge of the plateau while the bruised light pooled in the sky, would feel the old compulsion rise: to walk ahead of a stranger, to take the stranger’s unmade choice into their own body, to become the echo before the voice.

And instead they would remain still.

The stranger would find their own footing.

The mountain would not fall.

The world would not unravel.

The Refrainer would hear the second note beneath them, patient and deep.

Enough, it would say without saying.

And the Refrainer would turn away from the path and go to the sheltered hollow.

There, perhaps, others would already be sitting.

Not speaking.

Not feeding the pools.

Not holding the world taut by the throat.

Only resting.

The Mountain of Echoing Glass would remain terrible and beautiful. Its sky would remain bruised. Its glass would still glow with maps of impossible continents. Its doorframes would still open into rooms too cold to have walls. Its physics would still loosen now and then, tired but no longer cruel from exhaustion.

The Refrain would remain.

Some of them.

Others, in time, might choose to leave.

Not by descending the mountain like travelers, though perhaps a few would try, wrapped in coats made from weather and almost-faces. Some might become rain lower down, entering rivers that remember nothing but movement. Some might become songs that can be sung once and then allowed to end. Some might become ordinary silence in ordinary rooms, the blessed kind that asks nothing.

And some would stay because they love the mountain.

Not because they are the mountain.

Not because they must be.

Because love, once separated from compulsion, is allowed to remain.

The benevolent power would not announce victory.

There would be no dawn.

But one day, when the wind dropped into its hidden pockets and the glass stopped trembling and the distant unnamed cries paused to breathe, the low note beneath everything would change.

Only slightly.

Once, it had been the sound of equilibrium without desire.

Now it would be harmony.

Not peace, exactly.

Peace would be too final.

But something like permission.

Something like a voice, after ages of being an echo, discovering that it may speak once and be heard once and then rest.

And high on the plateau, where mercy had never been remembered before, the Refrain would sit beneath the thinning sky and learn the shape of an ending that did not mean disappearance.

The mountain would keep its strangeness.

The Refrain would keep their mystery.

But no one there would ever again be required to be Only.
