## The Bone Orchards of Keth-Vorrum

### I. The Hollow

You enter through a crack. Not a cave mouth—something tighter, a seam in basalt that widens gradually until you realize you have already arrived. The pressure in your ears shifts. The air tastes of copper and something sweeter, overripe, the smell of pulp in an abandoned apple barn. You have descended, though you did not climb. The floor holds steady beneath your feet, but the orientation feels borrowed, temporary, like standing in a room someone else imagined.

This is Keth-Vorrum. The scale is intimate enough to cross in ninety careful steps, yet the ceiling vaults beyond lantern-reach, and the walls curve away with an ambiguity that suggests either vastness or clever folding. It is a pocket. A bubble pressed into the world like a thumb into clay, then left to harden. The erosion here finished its work so long ago that "ancient" feels young. This is the settled shape of a billion years of barely-things: the final form of almost-nothing happening very slowly.

### II. The Substrate

There is no water. Never was, perhaps, or what existed has been sublimated into architecture, locked into cellulose lattices, traded for a different chemistry entirely. What grows here does not grow *with* water. It grows *instead* of it.

The plant-mass is total. It is the floor, the walls, the suggestion of sky. But calling it "vegetation" misleads. This is plant-as-mineral, plant-as-bone, plant that has metabolized its own greenness long ago. The color ranges from parchment to the yellow of old teeth to a gray so deep it swallows light without quite being black. There are structures here—clearly, unmistakably urban—that are extruded, not built. Towers of woven xylem, hollowed at intervals for dwelling. Arched thoroughfares where the path compresses the growth into something like cobblestone, then releases it behind you to slowly, slowly uncompress. Bridges spanning nothing, connecting habitation-nodes across voids where the root-mass thins to spiderwork and you can see, if you dare, how far down the hollow continues.

The "buildings" are primitive in their making. No metal. No fired clay. Tools of hardened thorn and chipped petrified pith, used to train the growth along desired paths, then removed. The shaping is patient, generational. Rings of decision accrete like the thing they shape: a city grown as a tree grows, by being in one place and continuing.

### III. The Darkness and Its Contents

Light: 0.0.

There is none. Or rather, there is absence so complete that carrying light in feels like rudeness, like shouting in a library where no one has spoken for centuries. Your lantern, if you brought one, shows you the local texture—a wall's hungering pores, a floor's gentle give—and makes the surrounding darkness denser, more *present*. The dark here has substance. It pools in the hollow spaces between root-tendrils. It holds still, patient, the way air holds still in sealed places.

But the dark is not empty. The plants, these total-vegetation inhabitants, have evolved beyond light-need or never required it. They sense in other registers. Perhaps they sense *you*, the disturbance of your warmth, your carbon footprint tracing through their slow cognition. The city—Keth-Vorrum, if it names itself, which it might in frequencies below hearing—maintains itself in absolute black, has maintained thus since before the lineage of your kind learned to fear the night.

And in this darkness, the sound.

### IV. The Roar of Patient Things

Loud: 2.0/3. The hollow *sings*, or groans, or breathes in cycles too long for human patience to fully map.

The sound is not chaotic. It is layered, orchestral, the result of thousand-year tensions releasing in gradual unison. The root-mass, growing still, incrementally, shifts against itself. Fibers tense and relax across the centuries. The hollow chambers act as resonators, selecting frequencies, amplifying the subsonic into range, merging multiple slow sources into chords that swell and fade across days.

You hear it first as pressure behind the eyes. Then as a note so low your sternum captures it, vibrates sympathy. Then as overtones—crackling of micro-movements in the ancient structure, the whisper of gas exchange in trillion stomata-equivalents breathing a chemistry not your own, the occasional sharp report of something finally, finally giving way after epochs of held strain.

The sound is loud enough to lose yourself in. To forget which frequencies are external, which are your own nervous system singing back. In the total dark, the total sound becomes a kind of touch, a space you inhabit rather than observe. It changes—dynamic: 1.4/3—but so slowly that you must wait hours to confirm a modulation, days to map a cycle. The city has tides. The city has seasons measured in the half-lives of its own structural materials. You cannot perceive the change directly. You can only note, returning to the same station after sufficient absence, that the chord has shifted, that the bridge now hums a different foundation-tone, that the tower you slept in sways now in a slightly altered discipline.

### V. The Rules That Hold

No animals. Fauna: 0.2/3, so we allow almost nothing—perhaps the bacterial film that enables the plant-chemistry, perhaps nothing you could call creature at all. The ecology completes without consumer. The plants cycle their own dead, extrude the unusable, build with the remainder. Matter moves in gradients of plantness, from active growth through structure through decay through nutrient through growth again. No external hunger interrupts. No tooth, no gut, no sudden predatory velocity. The violence here is all tension and release, pressure and the耐心等待 of pressure's end.

Temperature: 0.8/3—cool. Your breath should show, but the air holds moisture so poorly that vapor disperses invisible. The cool seeps from the surrounding stone, from the chemical indifference of the plant-processes, from the simple fact of being deep in a world that has never known the sun's warmth. You wear everything you brought. You move to generate heat, then still to conserve it, finding in the conflict a rhythm that matches, approximately, the city's own.

Weirdness: 1.6/3—strange, but not absolute. The strangeness is in the holding-together of contradictions: total life in total dark, city without builder, sound without source, time without event. You can almost narrativize Keth-Vorrum. Almost fit it to known categories. Then you touch a wall and feel it touch back, slowly, and the almost collapses.

### VI. What the City Wants

Speculation, perhaps. The city grew toward complexity in absence of competition, absence of predation, absence of change. It built dwellings without dwellers, bridges without travelers, spaces of potential that have never actualized. Or perhaps they actualized in a register you cannot perceive, the plant-cognition holding congress in chemical gradients across the dark, deliberating in growth-rates and root-pressure, a polity of patient intention making decisions across the span that your kind would call geological.

Perhaps it waits. Perhaps the hollow is a seed-pod, and the billion years of stillness are the stillness of potential before the right condition—temperature shift, chemical trigger, arrival of something that can finally inhabit the hollowed spaces—triggers the final growth that will split the rock and spill Keth-Vorrum into the lightless world above, or below, or adjacent.

Perhaps it simply is. The way a stone is. The way a very old forest is, though this is older than any forest, and not a forest, and the comparison fails.

### VII. Your Presence

You do not belong here. The city does not reject you; it does not, cannot, register rejection on timescales you occupy. You are a brief warmth, a brief disturbance, a brief addition of carbon and complexity that will diffuse through the system and be incorporated or expelled across centuries after your departure. If you stay, if you learn to move slowly enough, to eat the tissue-paper sheets that flake from certain surfaces, to drink the condensate that occasionally, rarely, forms where temperature gradients briefly allow—if you become patient enough—you might persist. You might become, to the city, simply another slow process. Another sound-source. Another reason the chord shifts, imperceptibly, toward configurations it has not previously explored.

But you will not stay. You are built for faster cycles. You will find the seam again, eventually, or the seam will find you, opening in a moment of the city's long adjustment. You will emerge, and the world outside will seem unbearably bright, unbearably quiet, unbearably empty of the vegetal density that held you. You will try to describe Keth-Vorrum. You will fail. You will say: there was a city, underground, in darkness, made of plant, very old. And those who have not been there will hear "underground city" and think of dwarves, of bunker, of miner-lamp history. And you will not have the words to explain that the dark was not absence but presence, that the plant was not grown but growing-still, that the sound was the loudest silence you have ever inhabited, that the primitive was also the eternal, that the empty density held more than you could comprehend.

And years later, in a quiet moment, you might feel a pressure behind your eyes. A note too low to hear. The memory of Keth-Vorrum, continuing without you, patient, loud, dark, total, slow, strange, complete in its hollow hollow hollow, waiting for nothing, being what it is, what it has been, what it will continue to be when your name and your attempt to witness it have diffused into the forgetting that is the only true mercy of very long time.

*The seam waits. The city grows, in some sense of grows. The sound continues. You are gone. You were brief. This is not sad. This is simply the rule of that place, which holds all strangeness in the held-breath of its long, long, longer than long becoming.*
