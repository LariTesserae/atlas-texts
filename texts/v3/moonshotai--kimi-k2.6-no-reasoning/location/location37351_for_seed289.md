I need to hold these parameters in tension and let them find their own coherence. Let me begin.

## The Pool of Unbecoming

Somewhere beneath the reachable world, in a cavity no survey has mapped and no foot has disturbed, there is a space that forgot it was allowed to change.

It is small. You could cross it in forty steps if there were anywhere to step, if walking here meant anything, if your body could endure the density of presence that fills it so completely there seems no room for paradox, yet paradox thrives. The chamber is perhaps twice the height of a standing person, though standing here would be like standing at the center of a hundred whispered conversations happening in your own blood. The walls—no, not walls yet, the *becoming-walls*—press close. They are not stone, though they are becoming stone. They are not flesh, though they were something like flesh, once, in the time before this place committed to its eternal now. You can see the process arrested mid-thought: mineral accretion halted at the moment of decision, half-crystallized, half-organized, the raw geology still *remembering* what it meant to be other than geological. The erosion parameter here is not of wearing-away but of *emergence interrupted*—the pre-formation held in breathless pause, the embryo that never births, the construction site where the workers have been frozen between one gesture and the next for longer than gesture has existed elsewhere.

And it is bright. Unnervingly, impossibly bright.

The light has no source you could locate, no sun-shaft from above, no bioluminescence pulsing from the arrested organic structures. It simply *is*, evenly distributed, saturating every arrested crevice of the becoming-walls with the flat intensity of a truth that needs no shadow to prove itself. Shadows would require change—movement of light, shift of angle, passage of time—and this place permits none. The brightness has no warmth to it; the temperature holds at cool, that particular cool of deep cellars and fevered skin, the damp that beads on surfaces that are not quite surfaces and never quite dry. Condensation hangs in the air itself, visible as a faint shimmer, a humidity that has achieved something like permanence, individual droplets suspended in their slow arc of falling, never arriving, occupying the space so completely that the air seems more liquid than gas, a medium you would swim through rather than breathe.

The vegetation is sparse in variety but absolute in commitment. Three species, perhaps four, none of them catalogued by any taxonomy of the open world. They do not grow; they *are present*, fully extended, fully expressed, occupying their allotted niches with the completeness of organisms that have reached their final form and been preserved there. A lichen-like crust covers portions of the becoming-walls, its edges so sharp you could measure them, each individual fruiting body open to release spores that will never be released, the release gesture held in the eternal moment before completion. Tendrils, something between root and fungal hypha, lace through the lower portions of the chamber in a dense mat, so packed together that they seem almost a single organism, though each strand maintains its boundary with territorial precision. They do not compete; they do not cooperate; they simply *occupy*, fully, finally, forever.

The density here is not merely physical. It is existential. Every presence presses against every other with the weight of completed being. The space is fully occupied not by mass but by *finishedness*. You feel it first as claustrophobia, then as awe, then as something that has no name in living languages—the recognition of a world where becoming has been traded for being, where the uncertainty that allows movement and choice and time has been refused so utterly that the refusal itself has become the place's defining architecture.

And the fauna.

They are abundant, and they are strange, and they are loud.

The sound is continuous, complex, layered to the edge of cacophony yet never crossing into chaos—perhaps because chaos requires change, and nothing here changes. It is the sound of a hundred thousand wingbeats arrested at the midpoint of their stroke, maintaining the air displacement indefinitely. It is the sound of voices, if you can call them voices, produced by organisms that have no name: a chirring from the mat-tendrils, a high whine from something that occupies the space near the ceiling, a continuous low hum from the lichen itself, all of it operating not in sequence but in *simultaneity*, the eternal chord of this place's chosen moment. There is rhythm without variation, pattern without development, the ostinato that has persisted since before the first human ear evolved to hear anything at all.

The creatures—there is no other word, though they fit no category—are present in numbers that seem impossible given the chamber's size. They do not move. They *maintain position*. Something like a beetle, but with too many legs and a carapace that seems to phase between crystalline and chitinous, rests on a tendril-mat, its antennae extended to full length, vibrating at a frequency that contributes its own note to the eternal chord. Clusters of smaller organisms, spherical, faintly luminescent in a way that does not add to the overall brightness but seems instead to *comment* on it, maintain their positions in the humid air itself, as much held by the medium's density as by any will of their own. Near the center of the chamber—and there is a center, though no mathematics could justify its location—a thing the size of a human heart but clearly not a heart pulses silently, its motion arrested mid-expansion, the pulse that would be visible in anything living here translated instead into a pressure, a presence, a statement of *I am here and I have always been here*.

There is water, or something that functions as water. It collects in pockets where the becoming-walls have not yet completed their inclination, though they never will. These pools do not ripple; they do not evaporate; they maintain their surface tension at a degree that suggests they have achieved some final, perfect state of liquidity, water that has become *idea of water*. What lives in them lives in the condition of permanent almost-emergence, the tadpoles that will never be frogs, the water-beetles that will never reach shore, all of them abundant in the manner of a thought fully thought, a potential completely expressed in its own suspension.

The built presence here is scattered, fragmentary, and undeniable. Not structures, exactly. Marks. Remains. A single artifact, perhaps, though whether it was made or grown or simply *occurred* is ambiguous to the point of meaninglessness. There is a spiral incised into one wall, not carved—there are no tools here, no technology, no intervention of purpose upon matter—but *expressed*, as if the wall in its pre-formation had been impressed by a pattern it encountered and never finished processing. There is a place where the tendrils have arranged themselves in a configuration that repeats, that suggests intention without achieving it, the ghost of architecture in a world that has rejected building. Single buildings, no, but the *memory* of building, scattered like seeds that have chosen never to germinate, partial remains of a making that never completed itself and so has remained forever in the state of partiality.

The rules of this world are simple and absolute: **what is, is. What is not, is not permitted. The moment of becoming most complete is the moment most valued, and this place has chosen its moment and will not be moved from it.**

You do not enter this place. You do not leave it. If you find yourself present, you are already caught in the density, already contributing your note to the chord, already feeling the strange temptation to simply *be*, fully, finally, to release the terrible effort of becoming that the outer world demands. The fauna did not arrive. They did not evolve. They simply *are*, in their abundance, their loud presence, their weird and perfect fit to a world where fitting is the only law.

It has been this way for longer than duration. It will continue in exactly this manner beyond any end. The damp will bead, the light will saturate, the organisms will maintain their eternal positions, the sound will sustain its chord, the pre-formation will never complete and never abandon its attempt at completion. It is a pocket in the world, yes, hidden, intimate, embedded within the larger geological and biological flows like a stone in a kidney, like a pearl in an oyster that has forgotten the irritation that began it, like a thought so complete it has replaced the thinker.

What pulls at you, if you somehow sense this place through the layers of unpenetrated earth above, is not danger and not invitation. It is *recognition*. Somewhere, in the becoming that has not yet finished, there is a part of you that understands the terrible peace of the eternal now, the density of completed presence, the bright and loud and damp and strange completeness of simply *being, finally, forever, without the uncertainty of next*.

The Pool of Unbecoming waits, has always waited, will always wait, in its condition of never-having-begun and never-coming-to-end. The raw geology remembers. The sparse vegetation maintains. The abundant fauna sound their eternal note. And the light, sourceless, shadowless, complete, holds everything in the brightness of a moment chosen and never released.
