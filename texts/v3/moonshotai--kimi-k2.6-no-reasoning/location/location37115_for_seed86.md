The Hollow of Still Song

---

The descent begins where the prairie gives way, the grass thinning ankle by ankle until even the tough ropy roots find nothing worth holding. Then a lip of stone, young stone, stone that has not yet learned patience, and you are below the surface without any cliff to mark the transition—merely a tilt in the geography that swallows itself, a geological hush. This is the Hollow, though maps know nothing of it. Maps show flat land here, farmland potential, the kind of blankness surveyors fill with contour lines as afterthought.

It is cool in the Hollow. Not cold, not the cold of depth or altitude or winter memory, but a coolness that seems to have no source and no destination, the temperature of something held in abeyance. Your breath does not mist. Your skin does not prickle. Yet you find yourself reluctant to remove any layer of clothing, even working, even sweating in the difficult climb back out. The coolness is a condition, not a weather. It wraps.

The stone walls, when they become walls, are sharp. Young. They have not been rained upon long enough to soften, not been touched by enough freeze-thaw to round their ambitious edges. Volcanic perhaps, or some swift tectonic upwelling now sinking back—geologists argue, when any come. Mostly they do not come. The rock is dark, a grey that wants to be black, and in places it still sheens with moisture that never quite dries. Condensation, mostly. The Hollow is damp the way a cellar is damp, a seepage without source, the air carrying water it cannot quite release. Rain falls less often than one would expect from the surrounding climate, but when it does, it runs immediately through cracks too new to be plugged with sediment, vanishes, reappears days later as weeping from unexpected seams.

There is water here, but it makes no patterns. No streams carve the young stone. No pools gather in depressions. Merely that intermittent moisture, the occasional thin trickle across a shelf of rock and then gone, absorbed or evaporated in the strange equilibrated air. Pockets of dampness in a larger dryness. You might find a depression with muddy bottom, easy to miss, holding water for a week after rain, then empty again, the mud cracking in patterns too regular to be natural and too irregular to be meaningful.

And the vegetation. Sparse. Above, at the lip, grasses. Below, where soil might accumulate in crevices, a few things grow. Lichen in the dampest cracks, grey-green, sloweven here, where stillness might suggest haste. Small cushions of some mossy plant in shallow ledges where dust collects. Perhaps, in the widest part of the Hollow, a scattering of something like dwarf willow, stems woody and older than their size suggests, leaves never fully unfurled, nervous in air that does not move. They do not cluster. They do not form patterns. Each plant seems to have arrived separately, negotiated its own terms, remains under protest.

---

The light is dim, but not dark. The Hollow is open enough, broad enough—a valley, a bowl, a town-sized absence—that sky reaches down. Overcast days flatten everything to grey. Clear days bring angles of illumination that seem to come from wrong directions, double-reflected off the young stone's sharp planes. Dawn and dusk are theoretical propositions. The light does not change through the day in any way you can track, yet somehow you know when it is noon, when evening. The dimness is a quality, not a quantity. You see. You do not see well. Colors exist but refuse names.

But you do not come to the Hollow for the light, or the stone, or the sparse and grudging plants.

You come for the sound.

It is loud here. Loud in a way that has no single source, no direction, no variation. The first time, you think: insects. Some vast congregation, some breeding emergence, some cicada rhythm or cricket pulse multiplied beyond counting. You search for them. You find none. The second time, you think: water underground, some great river speaking through stone, the hollow chamber amplifying. But you have heard underground rivers, and they speak in variation, in surge and diminishment, in the grammar of pressure changing. This does not vary. This does not change.

It is a single sustained note, or all notes held simultaneously, or the harmonic residue of something vaster than hearing. It is loud enough to feel in the sternum, in the teeth, in the fluid of the inner ear. It does not damage. It does not deafen. It persists. Day and night—though day and night are distinctions the Hollow barely acknowledges—the sound continues, never louder, never softer, never changing in pitch or texture or timbre. A chord, if you are musical. A tone, if you are precise. A song, if you are desperate for metaphor.

And under the sound, or within it, or perhaps generating it: the fauna.

Overwhelming. The parameter is precise. Overwhelming.

Not numerous, not dense. The Hollow is sparse in its presences, balanced with emptiness, generous with space between presences. You can walk for an hour and see nothing move. Then: a thing. Then: nothing, long enough to wonder if you imagined. Then: another thing, unrelated, unheralded, occupying its solitude with the absolute assurance of something that has never encountered competition.

But they are here. In numbers that defy the emptiness, that somehow saturate without clustering. You cannot count them, not because they are too many to count but because counting implies sequence, implies time moving forward, implies change, and the Hollow does not change. A population in equilibrium, every birth balanced by death in the same instant, every migration matched by return. The sound is their breathing, perhaps. Or their communication. Or their indifference to being distinguished one from another.

They are primitive, mostly. Tool-users of the simplest kind. You will find stones cracked in patterns suggesting use, though never the same pattern twice, never improvement, never tradition. A stick wedged in a crack to reach something, abandoned, never retrieved, the something still unreached. They make nothing that lasts, nothing that accumulates. No structures. The built parameter is absolute here—none, zero, untouched not by choice but by incapacity, by a kind of innocence that precedes even the thought of building. They do not make artifacts. They do not alter. They persist.

What are they? The descriptions fail. Mammalian, some. Furred, low to the ground, eyes large in the dimness, moving with the smoothness of things that have never been hunted and do not hunt. Others: avian, perhaps, though they do not fly, though their wings are functional, they merely choose not to, the air too still to need flight, too equilibrated to reward it. Reptilian things in the damp pockets, not basking—for there is no temperature gradient to seek—but simply existing in the moisture, skins glistening, never moving toward or away from anything. And others less classifiable, soft-bodied, luminous faintly from within, leaving trails that do not dry, that persist for days, that the sound seems to travel along as wire.

They do not interact with you. This is not fear, this is not tameness. They do not recognize you as a category. You are not food, not threat, not mate, not offspring. You are weather, perhaps, or geology, or simply an uninteresting opacity moving through their perceptual field. They do not flee. They do not approach. They continue whatever they are doing, which is usually nothing, which is always nothing, which is the definition of their presence here.

The overwhelming quality is not of experience but of implication. Each creature carries the weight of all the others, the vast distributed population held in this equilibrium, this never-changing, this dynamic zero. You feel them in the sound, in the way the sound seems to have texture, density, to be made of their attention or their absence of attention. You feel them in the coolness, as if their collective metabolism holds temperature constant. You feel them in the sharp young stone, which does not erode, which will not erode, which exists in the same temporal stasis as everything here.

---

The rules of this world.

Time does not pass, or passes without effect. The young stone remains young. The sparse vegetation neither grows nor dies back. The fauna cycle through whatever life stages they possess without sequence—adult and juvenile and egg or pupa existing simultaneously in the population, not in individuals, the individuals themselves perhaps immortal, perhaps merely instantaneous, replaced by identical instances in the same position. You cannot prove this. You only sense it, the way you sense that the sound has no beginning, that it was here before anything that makes it, that it will persist after.

Moisture without hydrology. Life without succession. Presence without density. Sound without music, without speech, without information. The Hollow is a solved equation, a world run to equilibrium so complete that gradients no longer exist to drive change. Not frozen—frozen implies suspension, implies something held against tendency. Here there is no tendency. Here is the flatness after all hills have eroded, accelerated somehow, concentrated in space, the end of geology before geology proper has begun.

And you, if you enter. You move through. You are not in equilibrium. You carry gradients—temperature, hunger, the desire to name, to count, to progress from entrance to exit. The Hollow does not resist you. The Hollow does not accommodate you. You are a transient disequilibrium, a pocket of change moving through changelessness, and if you stay too long, if you sit too still, if you begin to match your breathing to the sound, to find the frequency of the place—

But no one stays too long. Or if they do, they are not reported. The Hollow has no stories. The primitives who exist here have no narrative, no oral tradition, no memory of before because there is no before, no anticipation of after. If you remained, if you let the sound fill you completely, would you become part of the equilibrium? Would you stop changing, stop wanting, stop being human in exchange for being here?

The question is only a question because you are not here. If you were here, if you were truly in the Hollow of Still Song, you would not ask. The asking implies distance. The distance, here, is illusory. You are already surrounded. The sound is already in your bones. The coolness is your temperature now, has always been, the dampness your skin's natural state, the dimness proper light, the sparseness proper density, the overwhelming fauna your proper company, the primitive your proper tech, the unbuilt your proper architecture, the young stone your proper duration.

---

I have described this place from outside, which is the only description possible, which is no description at all. The Hollow does not exist for being described. It exists, if exist is the word, in the equilibrium that needs no witness, in the sound that needs no ear, in the fauna that need no name.

Go there, if you go, with the understanding that going is temporary, that your gradient will carry you out, that your change will reassert itself against the resistance of all that never changes. Or: go there with the understanding that you may not wish to leave, that the sound may become preferable to voices, the dimness to color, the still coolness to the exhausting project of warmth.

The prairie above continues. The grass holds the soil with its patient roots. The sky moves, weather develops, seasons turn. None of this reaches the Hollow. The lip of young stone is the boundary of a different physics, or the same physics solved to completion, the heat death of a very small universe concentrated in a valley no map records.

The sound continues. It is loud. It is unchanged. It is the first sound and the last sound and the only sound, if sound means anything, if anything means anything, in the damp cool dimness where nothing is built and everything that lives persists without purpose, without density, without end.
