The place is called the Throat, though no one living has named it aloud.

You arrive at altitude first—2.9 on whatever scale measures the vertical terror of the world. The air thins to something you drink rather than breathe, each inhalation a deliberate negotiation with gravity. But the Throat is not a peak. It is a declivity, a sudden inversion in the extreme elevation, as if the mountain range once inhaled and never fully exhaled. A bowl perhaps cathedral-sized, though "cathedral" implies human intention and there is none—0.4 built, nothing made, nothing placed by hand unless you count the hands of whatever made the world and then abandoned this particular thought unfinished.

The rock is young here. 0.8 erosion means the features have not yet learned patience. The basin's rim shows where stone tore like wet paper, where fractures still weep mineral water that has not yet learned to find the paths of least resistance. Sharp spurs of basalt and something greener, something that might be olivine if olivine grew in angular, almost architectural formations. The surfaces catch what little light exists and return it in fragments, not reflections but *arguments* against darkness.

Because the light is 0.4, and that measurement fails to capture the quality of darkness in the Throat. It is not the darkness of closed rooms, not the darkness of night with its companion stars. It is the darkness of something *eating* light, or of light choosing elsewhere, or of a color that exists just below black, a frequency the eye recognizes as absence of something it never evolved to perceive. What illumination exists seems borrowed—from phosphorescence, from bioluminescence, from the strange cool temperature of certain stone surfaces that glow faintly at their fractured edges like embers of a fire that burns at 1.1, patient and slow and utterly indifferent to warmth.

The damp is 1.3, intermittent, riverine without rivers. Water collects in the low places of this already-low place, not flowing but *gathering*, as if the Throat's one persistent motion is inhalation. Condensation beads on the young rock and does not fall, held by surface tension and something else, some property of the air that makes water hesitate. When it does move, it moves as sheets across stone, as sudden jewel-like accumulations in the angular hollows, as breath-fog that rises from no source you can locate and clings to the lower meter of the basin's volume. The water is not quite water. It is thicker, slower to wet a finger, reluctant to evaporate. It carries in solution minerals that precipitate in formations too regular to be crystal, too organic to be manufactured—thin films that build into structures like coral, like fungus, like the abandoned plans of something that began to build and forgot the purpose.

Vegetation is 1.2, sparse, but density is 1.5, and this contradiction defines the Throat's ecology. What grows here does not spread but *concentrates*. In the scattered pockets where water persists, in the seams where the young rock has begun its first slow weathering, life clusters with the intensity of reef or hive. Lichens that might be single organisms or might be cities, their structures visible only in the phosphorescent margins where they process the strange light into stranger chemistry. Fungal mats in orange and violet that do not decompose so much as *translate*, taking the young rock's mineral vocabulary and rendering it into organic syntax. These growths occur in islands, each island a dense microcosm, each separated by bare stone that shows no transition, no gradient of colonization—life here is present or absent, nothing between.

And the fauna. 2.6, overwhelming, and this is the parameter that makes the Throat what it is.

They are not numerous in the sense of counting. The density of 1.5 applies to them too, but their overwhelming quality is not of bodies but of *presence*. They are small, mostly, fitting the 1.4 scale—creatures of the grove-or-room, of the single feature, of the bounded area. But they are everywhere that life clusters, and they are *loud* at 1.6, and their sounds are not the sounds of animal communication as the rest of the world understands it.

Imagine a frequency that resonates in the bones of the inner ear without passing through air. Imagine the sound of water remembering being ice, of stone remembering being magma, of light remembering its source. The fauna of the Throat make these sounds, or they *are* these sounds given temporary bodies. They appear as soft things, pale or phosphorescent, with too many segments or too few, with limbs that articulate in directions that suggest extra dimensions lightly touched. Some are worm-like and move through the water-films as if swimming through solid stone. Others are crab-like and cling to the fungal islands, their shells composed of the same precipitated minerals as the strange formations, so that creature and substrate merge at the edges of vision. A few are aerial, though "aerial" misleads—they move through the dense lower atmosphere of the Throat, the fog-layer, with motions more like drifting in heavy water than true flight.

They do not eat, or not in ways that correspond to predator and prey. They process. They translate, like the lichens, like the fungi. The overwhelming presence of them is the overwhelming presence of *processors*, of entities engaged in continuous metabolic conversation with the place itself. They are primitive in technology, 0.8, but their primitiveness is not deficiency. They have tools of shell and precipitate mineral, of hardened secretion and selected crystal. They use these not to shape the world but to *interface* with it, to tune their processing to the Throat's particular frequencies. A tool might be a resonant chamber carved from fungal wood, used to amplify the sound of water gathering. Another might be a lens of slow-grown mineral, used to focus the strange near-black light into patterns that stimulate growth, or memory, or something between.

The weirdness is 2.2, and it inheres in the rules.

Time in the Throat is slow, 1.1 dynamic, but its slowness is not uniform. The fauna experience it in bursts, long periods of near-stasis punctuated by rapid transformations that might be reproduction or might be metamorphosis or might be something the categories of outside biology cannot parse. The water gathers for weeks, months, and then moves in sudden sheets that last minutes, carrying organisms and minerals and information in chemical form to new pockets, new islands. The fungal mats grow in visible pulses, expanding and then holding, expanding and holding, each pulse leaving behind a ring-structure in their tissue like tree rings but on timescales of hours.

The Throat has a weather, but it is weather of density and sound rather than temperature and precipitation. Pressure fronts of faunal presence move across the basin, the loudness ebbing and concentrating, the bioluminescence responding in waves of brighter and dimmer that track these movements with the precision of prediction. To stand in the Throat is to stand in something almost alive, almost aware, a system that processes and is processed, that translates and is translated.

What emerges, as the place becomes more itself, is the sense of the Throat as *interior*. Not interior to anything specific, but interior in quality, a space of digestion or meditation or dream. The extreme elevation that surrounds it functions not as height but as wall, as the barrier between this process and the external world's faster dynamics. The darkness is the darkness of inside, of body cavity, of skull. The damp is the damp of breath, of membrane, of the surfaces where interior meets itself. The fauna are not inhabitants but *contents*, not colonists but functions, organs of a body that does not know it is a body, or knows and does not care, or cares in a mode of caring inaccessible to anything that has not spent sufficient time being processed by the place.

The young rock remembers violence, the violence of its making, and this memory is part of what the Throat processes. The sharp features, the unweathered surfaces, the fractures that weep—they are inputs to a system that translates violence into pattern, suddenness into slowness, the new into the ancient before its time. The Throat is where geology goes to become biology without passing through the usual intermediaries, where the distinction between living and nonliving has not yet been established or has been deliberately, slowly, loudly dissolved.

And the sound continues, 1.6, loud not in decibels but in *occupation*, in the degree to which it fills the available acoustic space, which is all space in the Throat. It is the sound of water not falling, of rock not weathering, of life not exactly living but *persisting in process*, and the fauna are its instruments and its audience and its source, indistinguishable, overwhelming in their presence, sparse in their distribution, dense in their concentration, strange in their familiarity, familiar in their absolute alienation from anything the world outside the Throat has learned to recognize as creature, as place, as self.
