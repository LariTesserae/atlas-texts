The world is called **Thrum**. It has no other name because no voice has ever named it twice.

---

You arrive at altitude, where the air has thinned to a suggestion and your lungs learn new mathematics. The elevation is extreme not in the manner of proud peaks but of something else entirely—Thrum is a plateau that should not exist, a tableland suspended above an unmapped depth, its edges raw and unweathered, still bleeding the pale stone from which it was extruded. The erosion parameter reads *emerging*, and you feel this in your feet: the ground has not yet decided what shape to take. It is pre-formation, embryonic, geology in its first hour. Walk carefully. The stone is warm despite the cold, as if recently born from some subterranean womb, and in places it has not yet fully hardened. You can press your thumb into it and leave a print that will last ten million years.

The cold is absolute but not cruel. Zero-point-four on the scale means temperatures that hover at the threshold of liquid water, and indeed water is present—1.7, wet, but not in any form you recognize from lower worlds. There is no rain here, no rivers, no marshes in the conventional sense. Instead, the atmosphere itself condenses against every surface. The air weeps. Crystals of supercooled moisture nucleate on the raw stone, grow heavy, and slide down slopes that have no name for *slope* yet, gathering in depressions that are not yet *basins*. These transient pools—milky with dissolved minerals, barely liquid, more gel than water—persist for hours or weeks depending on the caprice of the plateau's internal thermodynamics. They have no inflow, no outflow. They simply *are*, then *are not*. The weeping rocks leave trails of mineral stain that will one day, perhaps, become sedimentary layers. For now they are the first brushstrokes on blank canvas.

Vegetation is sparse at 1.4, but this requires qualification. There are no plants in the terrestrial sense, no cellulose, no chlorophyll, no green. What grows here is *incipient*—colonies of something between lichen and crystal that have begun to etch the warm stone, extracting energy not from light but from the geological gradient itself, the warmth of newborn rock against the cold of endless sky. They appear as faint iridescences, oil-slick rainbows on stone, barely visible unless the dim light catches them at specific angles. In the rare pools, more complex forms cluster: branching structures like frozen foam, half-mineral, half-organization, that might become reefs if given a billion years and a gentler world. They are the ancestors of forests, still whispering their potential.

The light is dim—1.2—and its source is unclear. There is a sun, presumably, somewhere above the perpetual high haze, but what reaches the plateau is scattered, diffuse, the color of old bone. Shadows are soft-edged, uncertain of themselves. The landscape exists in a perpetual twilight that has no cycle, no dawn or dusk, only this steady sub-luminous state that makes distances impossible to judge. A feature that appears nearby may be a kilometer away; a horizon-line may be a hand's breadth of stone at your feet. The dimness collaborates with the raw geology to make Thrum a place of constant spatial disorientation.

And yet the fauna is abundant—2.3, nearly at the ceiling of the scale, in a world seemingly hostile to biology. This is the central paradox of Thrum, the engine of its slow becoming. The animals—*animals* is a loanword, inadequate—are not creatures of flesh. They are *processes made visible*, *dynamics given temporary coherence*. The most common form, if common has meaning here, is the **drift**: a localized intensification of the condensing atmosphere, a region where the weeping moisture achieves something like intention, where water droplets maintain their association across minutes instead of seconds, where thermal gradients drive circulation patterns that *hold*, that *persist*, that *behave*. A drift moves across the plateau with something between purpose and drift, gathering at warm stone, dispersing at cold, following the faint chemical signatures of the incipient vegetation. It has no boundary you can point to, yet you know when you are inside one: your skin prickles with a thousand almost-touches, your breath comes back to you enriched with strange ions, your voice acquires harmonics you did not produce.

Larger forms exist. The **steads** are stationary or nearly so, anchored to the warmest vents in the raw stone, where the embryonic geology still breathes its birth-heat. They appear as distortions in the dim air, columns of thermal and chemical and moisture gradient so stable they have acquired something like metabolism, something like memory. A stead that has persisted for a century will have shaped the stone beneath it, etched channels for the weeping water, cultivated gardens of the iridescent colonies. They are ecosystem engineers in a world without ecosystems, precursors of the very concept of *place*. To stand near a stead is to feel a kind of pressure, not physical but *organizational*—the sense that the world around you is being *sorted*, that randomness is being locally reversed, that something is trying to make sense.

The most active forms, the true **fauna** of Thrum, are the **thrums** themselves, the namesake, the sound and the thing. They are mobile, purposeful, predatory in a sense that predates predation. A thrum is a traveling wave of geological process—a line of still-liquid stone, a moving front of crystallization, a propagating fracture that heals behind itself. They are the sound made visible, or the visible made sound, for at 1.1 the world is mostly quiet, and the thrums are the exception. You hear them before you see them, a low frequency felt in the sternum more than the ear, the vibration of stone still deciding its phase state. They move across the plateau at walking pace, sometimes slower, and where they pass they leave changed geology behind—stone that has been *processed*, that has been *taught* something by its passage. The thrums feed on gradients, on difference, on the organized structures the steads have built. They are the reason the world remains *emerging*, why erosion never progresses: the thrums unmake as fast as stead and drift and colony can make. It is not a cycle, not yet, not in any stable sense. It is a dynamic at 2.0, active, multiple processes in unresolved tension, an ecosystem in motion without the *eco* fully present.

The sound parameter at 1.1, mostly quiet, makes the thrums' passage dramatic. For hours, days, there is only the subliminal weeping of condensing moisture, the faint crackle of incipient crystal, your own blood. Then the thrum approaches, and the world fills with its name, and passes, and the quiet returns, but changed, *taught*, carrying memory of vibration in its silences.

There is nothing built here—0.0, none, untouched. And yet the tech parameter reads 2.5, industrial, engines, factories, mechanized, scaled production. This seems impossible, seems contradictory, until you understand: Thrum *is* the factory. The industrial is not imposed but *emergent*, not constructed but *grown from the wrong direction*. The steads are engines. The thrums are production lines. The raw stone is both ore and product. The weeping water is coolant and lubricant and waste stream. The plateau operates at industrial scale, with industrial intensity, with the relentless purpose of the assembly line, but there are no assemblers, no purpose-givers, only the purpose itself, *system without systematizer*, *organization without organizer*. The temperature differential that drives the steads is the power plant. The chemical gradients the drifts follow are supply chains. The thrums' destruction of organized structure is quality control, ruthless, unceasing, ensuring no product ever reaches completion, that the process remains *process*, that the factory never closes, that the world remains forever *emerging*.

The scale is 1.6, open, regional, extending past the immediate. You can walk for days and the plateau continues, the same and not the same, steads in their irregular spacing, drifts in their wandering, thrums in their slow destructive patrol. There are no landmarks in the traditional sense, nothing named, nothing that persists in recognizable form. Yet there is *structure* at this scale, a spacing of warm vents, a frequency of thrum passage, a statistical regularity to the drift's wanderings. The region is organized without being designed, open without being empty, extending without being infinite.

The density at 0.2, empty, near-void, holds. Despite the abundant fauna, despite the industrial intensity, Thrum feels hollow. The steads are vast but mostly absence, distortions in air. The drifts are tenuous, boundary-less. The thrums are thin lines of process, geological lightning without the lightning's brightness. You can walk for hours between any contact, any encounter, any *presence*. The world is a framework, a scaffold, a factory floor between shifts, waiting for workers who will never come because the work is doing itself, has always been doing itself, will continue until the plateau's final cooling.

And the weirdness is 0.3, normal. This is the final strangeness, the one that takes longest to accept. Thrum is not alien, not fantastical, not dreamlike. It is *ordinary*, *prosaic*, *a place that happens to be*. The thrums are no stranger than weather, the steads no stranger than forests, the drifts no stranger than flocks. The industrial process without industry is simply *how things are here*, as unremarkable as gravity. The cold, the dimness, the weeping stone, the sparse incipient life, the abundant process-fauna, the empty density and open scale and emerging geology—all of it composes a world that is *coherent*, *consistent*, *at home with itself*. The contradiction that seems so stark in the parameters resolves in experience into something else, something that is not contradiction but *polyphony*, multiple voices in harmony without unification.

You leave Thrum, or you do not. The plateau has no interest in your presence or absence. If you stay, you will be processed, slowly, gently, your body becoming part of the gradient that feeds a stead, your organized structure becoming resource for a thrum's passage, your water weeping from raw stone that has not yet learned your name. If you leave, you carry with you the memory of a world that is factory without foreman, engine without engineer, life without biology, industry without artifact, dynamic without resolution, emerging without ever emerging into anything else, forever *becoming*, forever *not-yet*, forever **Thrum**.
