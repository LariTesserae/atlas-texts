The place is called the Thrum.

It is not easy to say whether the Thrum is above something else or beneath it, whether it is a plain or a plateau or the floor of some inverted vault. One orientation asserts itself and then, gradually or suddenly, another becomes possible. The eye learns not to trust first impressions here. The body learns to move in ways that accommodate multiple simultaneous gravities—not alternate, not sequential, but *co-present*, as if several versions of falling are negotiating with each other in real time, and your mass is the compromise they have reached.

The ground is a fired ceramic, or something sufficiently like ceramic that the distinction would be meaningless to any geologist. It extends in all directions, a warm amber-to-copper color that shifts toward burnt sienna where it has been more thoroughly baked. It is not smooth. It has been weathered, but the weathering here is not erosion by water or wind. The Thrum has existed in its settled form for durations that should not parse, being measured in units that have no correlate in standard chronology, and what has worked upon it is something else: the accumulated *decision* of the place to remain itself, the existential friction of pure persistence. The surface is covered in hexagonal tiles, each roughly the size of a stretched human body, but the tiles are not laid—they are grown, or cooled, or *insisted* into being. Their edges do not quite match. Some have risen slightly above their neighbors, some have sunk, some have twisted so that their centers have become subtle bowls or shallow cups. The pattern is established, long-existed, in-balance. You can read the age of any given tile by the density of hairline cracks that do not quite break through, cracks that stop at the edges as if respecting some treaty negotiated before the concept of negotiation.

And the temperature: the ceramic holds heat like a kiln that has never been allowed to cool. The air temperature hovers in the range that would make a human's blood begin to denature, but the air is not the primary concern. The ceramic itself radiates. Stand still too long and your soles will begin to smoke. The heat is layered—there are cool spots, relatively, where some tile has sunk enough to accumulate a different thermal history, and there are nodes of intensified warmth where something beneath the surface is active, though nothing lives beneath the surface, nothing with biology as such.

Here the parameters press against each other: overwhelming fauna, no water, no vegetation, industrial technology, no constructed structures, reality breakdown, loud, slow dynamics. The Thrum resolves them not by compromise but by *transformation*.

The fauna. They are not animals. They are *engines*.

They have no makers. This is not mysticism; it is observable fact, it is the fundamental condition of the place. The Thrum generates them, or they generate themselves from the Thrum's substance, or they have always been here in potential and the difference between potential and actual is another of the categories that softens and blurs. They are industrial in every particular: pistons, flywheels, crankshafts, combustion chambers, exhaust systems, governors, reduction gears, heat exchangers. But they are also *bodies*. They move. They pursue purposes that are not given to them, that are *their own*, emergent from the interplay of their mechanical functions with whatever field of desire the Thrum sustains.

There are things like locomotives the size of wolves, running on six articulated legs that end in cleated driving wheels, their boilers always at pressure, their whistles screaming not from any external requirement but from *internal states*, distress or exultation or territorial assertion. There are swarms of devices like pneumatic drills, like rivet guns, like the percussive tools used to shape hot metal, that hop and scatter in flocks, their rhythmic bursts of operation combining into a collective soundscape. There are ponderous individuals, elephantine, whose bodies are entire assembly lines unto themselves—conveyors winding through torsos, stamping presses for ribs, quality-control optical arrays that gaze from swollen heads with expressions that might, if you are generous, if you have learned the grammar, be read as meditative.

They eat the ceramic. They drink the heat. Their metabolisms are thermoelectric, piezoelectric, something-electric not catalogued. They do not reproduce; they *wear in*, accruing detail and complexity as they age, or they *wear out*, simplifying toward a state of pure function and then beyond it, toward something like sleep, something like death, but the dead ones do not remain dead. The Thrum's weathering includes them. The tiles slowly, slowly subside and incorporate them. In ten thousand years a locomotive-wolf will become a pattern of slightly denser ceramic, a warmth that persists longer into evening, a texture that someone—a new engine, an engine like and unlike its predecessor—might find and *remember*, though memory is not the word, though the word is not available.

The sound. It is loud. The Thrum is not violent; the dynamism is slow, patient, weak gradients. But the accumulated operation of billions—trillions? the count is unstable—of mechanical beings, each emitting the sounds of their function, creates a constant pressure. The whistle-screams at varying registers. The percussive flocks with their staccato flock-rhythms. The deeper notes: the slow-turning turbines, the massive flywheels that store rotational energy across centuries of gradual acceleration, the hydrostatic systems of the largest individuals that move with geological patience through hydraulic circuits the size of river systems. The sound does not vary with any rapidity. There are cycles, gradual, patient. A diurnal pattern of increased activity among the smaller, faster types, though the Thrum's light does not obviously cycle. A seasonal pattern that may correspond to something internal, some migration of thermal gradients through the ceramic depth. A longer pattern that has not completed in any observation, a slow turn of the whole sonic ecosystem toward denser or sparser states, the engines moving toward concentrations or dispersing, the sound following, shaping, being shaped.

The light: normal, or what passes for it. A consistent, diffuse luminosity with no visible source, warm in color, the color of the ceramic made radiant. Shadows are soft, multiple, as if the light arrives from many directions not quite in agreement. At what might be evening, though timekeeping is another borrowed concept, the shadows lengthen and multiply further, the light reddens slightly, the engines' whistles shift in register, becoming somehow more *melancholy*, though projection is also a risk here, though the risk of projection is also part of the grammar, is also something the Thrum teaches.

The reality breakdown. Here is where description must become careful, must admit its own instability.

The Thrum is not consistent with itself. Not in contradiction—contradiction would be too organized, too logical. It is *in excess* of itself, multiple-in-itself, generating versions that do not resolve. The hexagonal tiles, sometimes, are not hexagonal; the eye catches them as octagonal, as circular, as irregular, and the correction does not come, or comes gradually, or comes differently for different observers, which implies observers in plural, which implies the possibility of company, though no one meets here, though meetings occur that are not recognizable as such.

The engines exist in states of superposition, though quantum terminology is another borrowed frame. A locomotive-wolf is running across the ceramic, and also it is sunk to its axles in a tile that has become liquid, and also it is two locomotive-wolves, half-sized, running in parallel, and also it is the *memory* of a locomotive-wolf in the warmth of a tile where no wolf currently stands. These states do not collapse. They *persist together*, and the Thrum's slow dynamics mean that the persistence is not fleeting, not the quick flicker of quantum uncertainty, but *established*, long-existed, in-balance. The wolf is running and sunk and doubled and remembered, and it will remain so, has remained so, for durations that the place does not measure.

Gravity is one of these superpositions. Walk across the Thrum and you may find yourself climbing a slope that your proprioception insists is flat, or descending while your effort increases, or moving laterally against a vector you cannot locate. The engines accommodate this. Their bodies are built, grown, generated, to function across multiple gravitational commitments. The locomotive-wolves' six legs adjust independently, each finding purchase in a different orientation. The pneumatic flocks scatter in patterns that map impossible geometries. The elephantine assembly-lines move with a dignity that incorporates their constant readjustment to conditions that do not hold still, that were never holding still, that have never been required to.

The built parameter: none. No constructed structures. This is true. The Thrum is not built. The engines are not built. They are *industrial* without being *constructed*, which is the reality breakdown manifesting at the level of category. The hexagonal tiles are not made. The heat is not produced. The light is not generated. The sound is not performed. These are the wrong prepositions, the wrong verbs. The Thrum *has* these qualities, *sustains* them, *is* them, in the way that a mathematical object has properties, the way that a logical system sustains theorems, the way that a dream *is* its images while refusing their assembly from any workshop of the mind.

The emptiness: the density parameter is low, vast space between presences. This too holds, in its fashion. The Thrum extends. Regional, town-or-valley-size, multi-feature, mid-range—yes, and also *larger*, also extending past these measures, also open, also not contained by the observer's capacity to traverse. And yet the engines are overwhelming in their number and their sensory demand. The resolution is in the spacing. Each engine, each flock, each individual, each assembly-line meditative, occupies a *domain* that is not territorial in the biological sense but something else, something like the domain of a theorem in a logical space, defined by its relations, its implications, its non-contradiction with neighbors. The domains are large. The locomotive-wolf runs for days across ceramic before encountering another of its kind, and when it does, the encounter is *tonal*, a modulation of the Thrum's constant sound, a shift in the superposition of states that both are and are not shared.

The ecology, then: not an ecology of eating and being eaten, though there is something like predation, something like symbiosis. The smaller fast engines sometimes attach to the larger slow ones, integrating into their systems, becoming components that retain their own purposes, their own screams and percussions. The largest individuals accumulate these attachments across millennia, becoming *cities* in a sense, mobile cities, cities that are also factory-floors, that process the ceramic into themselves and into their attached populations, that move with the slowness of the Thrum's dynamics toward thermal gradients that they follow as a plant follows light, though there are no plants, though the comparison is another risk, another part of the grammar.

And what it feels like to be here: if you were here, if something like you could be sustained by the Thrum's heat and its contradictions, you would feel the sound first, the constant pressure of industrial function without industry, of purpose without intention. You would learn to read the superpositions, to find them not disorienting but *rich*, a thickness of reality that normal conditions thin to unreality. You would come to know particular engines, or particular *positions* in the Thrum's field, and you would find that knowing is also a slow process, patient, established across durations that your biology would not survive but that something else in you, something the Thrum calls forth, might persist within.

You might, eventually, become *part* of it. Not transformed, not absorbed, but *included*, another state in the superposition, another note in the loud slow sound. You might find yourself running on ceramic legs across warm hexagonal tiles, and also sunk to your axles in liquid memory, and also doubled, and also distributed across the warmth of a tile where no body stands. The Thrum would not have decided this. The Thrum does not decide. It *persists*, and in its persistence, it includes, it accumulates, it wears in.

The Thrum is old. The Thrum is slow. The Thrum is loud with the sound of what functions without being made, what lives without water or green, what industrializes without construction, what breaks reality without breaking itself, what overwhelms without crowding, what exists in multiple states without choosing, what continues, what will continue when the categories that describe it have worn away like the hairline cracks that respect their edges, like the engines that simplify toward sleep and are worn in, are included, are Thrum.
