The Constructed Ocean

It is hot here, and vast—so vast that weather systems form over its surface like bruises, like the slow thoughts of something barely awake. Low to the galactic plane, or perhaps low in some other sense that has become geometric truth, this place spreads across a scale that makes maps into jokes. You could walk for years. Many have. The walking itself becomes the reason, eventually.

Everything is made. Everything. There is no natural substrate, no bedrock of accident, no geology of the unplanned. And yet: the tech is none. Zero. No tools, no devices, no inherited ingenuity of hand or mind. This is the central paradox, the wound that does not close, the question that the place asks without wanting an answer.

What built it, then?

The answer is: the place built itself. Or was built by processes that predate the distinction. The surface—let me try to describe it—is a kind of ceramic, or a fired clay, or something like the nacre inside a shell that has no oyster, that never had a soft body to protect. It extends in all directions, pale where the heat has bleached it, darker in the low basins where moisture collects. The color shifts with the angle of your shadow: rose, amber, the greenish-white of old bone. It is cool to the touch at dawn, then quickly becomes untouchable by mid-morning, and you learn to travel in the crepuscular hours or not at all.

The erosion is emerging. Pre-formation. Raw. What this means here: the surface is not yet comfortable with itself. It has not settled into the resigned shapes of old landscapes, the rounded hills and gentle valleys of a world that has accepted its fate. Instead, there are sudden ridges where the material has cracked and half-healed, like scars on skin that grew too fast. There are places where the ceramic has bubbled and frozen, capturing the moment of its own distress in permanent gesture. There are fields of what might become dunes but are not yet—just the first suggestion of repetition, the first draft of pattern, accumulations of dust that has nowhere else to go.

And dust is the closest thing to a material here. Not sand, not soil. Dust. Fine as the residue of burned libraries. It collects in the hollows, in the occasional basins where water has been, where water will be again. The water: 1.3. Intermittent. Not absent, not present. A grammar of moisture, conditional, subjunctive. Rain comes sometimes, brief and violent, leaving the surface slick and dangerous, filling the lowest depressions with liquid that does not last. By afternoon the puddles are memory, white rings of evaporated mineral, and by evening even the rings are indistinct. But the moisture has done something. It has started processes. The surface where water touched is slightly different now, microscopically, meaningfully. Over millennia—over the vast time this place contains—these touches accumulate. The erosion is emerging because the water is teaching the ceramic how to be worn. It is a slow education. The student is patient. The teacher is intermittent, distracted, perhaps not fully committed to the lesson.

Vegetation: 1.1. Sparse. What grows here grows in the moment of moisture, in the brief window between rain and evaporation. Not plants as you know them, perhaps. Growths. Accretions. Things that might be alive or might be mineral processes that have learned to mimic life, the way crystals can seem to grow with intention. They appear as films, as crusts, as sudden eruptions of filament in the damp shadow of a ridge. They do not photosynthesize—there is nothing here to suggest that chemistry. They may chemosynthesize, drawing something from the ceramic itself, or they may simply be: existing without the metabolic urgency of biological life, growing at the pace of erosion, which is to say at the pace of almost-not-at-all. After rain, they are faintly green, faintly phosphorescent, visible at night as a scattered constellation of living dimness. By the third day of dryness they are colorless, indistinguishable from the dust they grew from. They do not die, exactly. They wait. They are always waiting.

No fauna. 0.5. Nothing that moves with animal intention. The dynamic of the place is active—weather cycles, the slow chemistry of surface and moisture, the thermal expansion and contraction of the ceramic that makes a constant subsonic mutter, too low for human ears but perhaps felt in the teeth, in the hollow of the chest. But no predator-prey, no chase and escape, no hunger and satiation. The activity is abiotic, or pre-biotic, or something else entirely. Storms form in the vastness, wander for weeks, dissipate. Dust devils rise and fall. The surface temperature varies by fifty degrees between noon and midnight, and this differential drives winds, drives the slow circulation of an atmosphere that has no breathers to care about its composition.

The sound: 0.5. Silence, or quiet sounds. The wind, when it comes, makes a particular note against the ridges, a frequency that seems to resonate with the ceramic itself, so that the ground hums faintly in response. Rain is loud, briefly, and then the loudness is more memorable than the rain itself. Otherwise: the silence of a place that has never been interrupted. No voices. No wings. No footfalls but your own, and yours will stop, eventually, will become part of the silence, will be remembered by the dust for a day or two and then forgotten.

The light is normal, 1.9. This is almost strange, given everything else. A sun, or something sun-like. Day and night. Shadows that behave as shadows should. The normal light makes the abnormality of everything else more visible, more insistent. You can see what is wrong here because you can see clearly. The light does not forgive. It does not obscure. It illuminates the vast constructed emptiness with the evenness of a laboratory, of an examination room, of a stage before the play begins.

And the scale. I must return to the scale. 2.9. Vast enough for weather of its own, and it has that, but also: vast enough that different regions may have different histories. The place is not uniform. In the far east, if east means anything here, there are structures that might be the beginnings of something like architecture—repetitive forms, almost columns, almost walls, rising from the ceramic without ever quite achieving the definiteness of intention. In the north, a region where water comes more frequently, the vegetation is thicker, more insistent, has begun to shape the surface it grows on, to create microclimates, to suggest the possibility of an ecology that has not yet arrived. In the south, the heat is more intense, the ceramic has begun to flow in places, to exhibit behaviors more like liquid than solid, frozen in the moment of transition into shapes that suggest movement without achieving it.

The place is under construction. That is the truth of it. The erosion is emerging because the place is still being built, still learning what it is, still in the process of becoming itself. The construction is not directed. There is no architect, no plan, no blueprint folded in some cosmic drawer. The construction is self-organizing, emergent, the result of rules that the place follows without knowing it follows them. The ceramic precipitates from something, or solidifies from something, or is deposited by processes that operate at timescales too large to witness. The water teaches erosion. The erosion teaches form. The form teaches weather. The weather teaches water. It is a slow loop, a cycle that has not yet closed, that may never close, that may be the point: not to arrive but to continue arriving, to remain always in the state of becoming, of pre-formation, of rawness that refuses to cook into the finished.

What would it mean for this place to be finished? Perhaps it would mean death, or something like death. The finished place would have no more to learn. The weather would repeat itself in known patterns. The erosion would settle into predictable rates. The vegetation, if it ever became more than sparse, would achieve a climax community, a steady state, a kind of living death. The place avoids this. The place is active in its avoidance, dynamic in its refusal to become static. The storms are unpredictable because the surface that shapes them is still changing. The water is intermittent because the channels that would direct it have not yet formed, or form and unform, or form differently each time.

And you, if you walk here: you are the only animal. The only fauna. Your presence is an anomaly, a category error, a noise in the system that the system does not have ears to hear. You will not be hunted. Nothing will eat you. The dangers are abiotic: heat, thirst, the slick surface after rain, the dust storms that rise without warning and can last for days, reducing visibility to arm's length, filling every fold of skin, every opening, with the fine residue of burned libraries. You will learn to find water, to read the surface for the signs of recent moisture, to recognize the vegetation's faint signal of dampness beneath. You will learn to move at the place's pace, which is slow, which is patient, which has no deadline.

Perhaps you will stay. Many do, who come. The place does not invite, but it accepts. It has the acceptance of something that has never been refused, that does not know refusal is possible. You will leave traces. Your footprints will persist for years in the dust, until the next rain or the next storm or the slow creep of some unnamable process erases them. You may build something, though there is nothing to build with but the ceramic itself, and it resists shaping, resists intention, returns always to its own emergent forms. You may try to leave, and find that the vastness has no edge, or that the edge is not what you thought, or that you have become part of the construction, part of the emerging, part of the rawness that refuses to finish.

The place is hot, and damp in its intermittent way, and vast, and low, and emerging, and sparse in its presences, and totally built without technology, and normally lit, and silent, and active in its silence, and empty of animals except for you, and normal in its weirdness, which is to say weird in its absolute refusal of the weird, its insistence on being exactly what it is without explanation or excuse.

Walk carefully. The surface is still learning to be walked on.
