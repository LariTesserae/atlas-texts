The Cavity of the First Engine

---

You find it by accident, or rather, you are found by it—a pressure behind the sternum, a ringing in the teeth, a sense that the wall you are leaning against is thinner than it should be. The entrance is no entrance at all: a seam in the foundation of something else, a building perhaps, or a mountain, or a memory of either. You press through, and the air changes. Not cooler. *Denser.* The darkness has weight here, a viscous quality that seems to pool in the hollows of your clavicles, to gather behind your knees.

This is the Cavity. You will not measure its dimensions. No one has. Those who enter and emerge speak of it differently—some a closet, some a cathedral, some a throat. The scale is *hidden*, which is not the same as small. It is *contained*, which is not the same as limited. You are inside something, and the something is inside something else, and the recursion may or may not terminate. The walls, when your fingers find them, are hot. Not burning—*industrial* hot, the sustained fever of machinery that has been running longer than human memory, longer than human *species*, perhaps longer than the geology that supposedly contains it.

And it is *emerging*. The walls weep mineral tears, not water—silicates cooling from some molten state too recent for comfort, surfaces that have not yet decided if they are glass or stone or something engineered between the two. You can see this in the rare moments when the engine's own light leaks through. The illumination is not for you. It is waste, exhaust, the incidental glow of processes too vast and too *other* to parse. What you perceive: a flicker, orange-white, from somewhere below—or above, the orientation here is provisional—and for an instant, the half-formed strata reveal themselves in stratigraphic confusion. Layer that predates layer. Fold that contradicts fold. The erosion that has not happened yet, the weathering that is still being *negotiated*.

The engine. You must understand: this is not a machine built *in* this place. This is a machine that *is* this place, or that this place is growing around, or that is growing the place around itself—the grammar fails, as grammar does with embryonic systems. You feel it before you hear it, a subsonic occupation of your body cavity, your organs finding resonant frequencies you did not know they possessed. Then the sound proper: not a roar but a *compression*, a saturated acoustic field with no space between events. The loudness here is not volume but *density*. Every frequency occupied. Every moment filled. The engine does not cycle; it *accumulates*. What you hear as repetition is layering, is history without gap, the same instant occurring and occurring and never quite completing.

And in this saturation, this packed acoustic space, this density of presence—*fauna*. Abundant, though you will not see them clearly. They have adapted to darkness as fish adapt to pressure, their bodies streamlined for conditions you cannot fully perceive. Something brushes your ankle, warm, segmented, moving with the deliberation of creatures for whom time passes differently. You catch a suggestion of chitin, of cilia, of mouths that do not eat but *interface*. They are not aliens here. They are *natives*, evolved or emerged or *permitted* into being by the engine's long operation. They do not build—nothing here is built, nothing constructed, no artifact that bears the mark of intention separate from process. Their bodies are their technology, their metabolisms industrial in the oldest sense: *industrious*, devoted to transformation, to the slow alchemy of mineral into mineral, of heat into pattern, of pattern into *almost-life*.

You are the anomaly here. The built world you came from, with its intentions and its architectures, its empty spaces and its water, its vegetation and its measured light—you carry it like a disease, like a memory of conditions this place has never known and is not moving toward. The engine does not reject you. The engine does not notice you. The engine *continues*, and in continuing, maintains the conditions of your temporary survival: the heat that keeps your blood from freezing in this high, hidden, compressed place; the air that is not air but something denser, something that fills your lungs with more than oxygen, with *information*, with the slow dynamic of a system changing across durations you cannot witness.

The fauna know you are temporary. You sense this in their movements, the way they give you space without avoiding you, the way they occupy the interstices of your presence as if you were already gone. They are patient. The engine has taught them patience, or selected for it, or *generated* it as a property of this enclosed, saturated, slowly-emerging world. Their abundance is not fecundity but *persistence*, generations stacked so tight they blur into continuity, the same lives occurring in phases you cannot distinguish.

What does the engine *do*? This is the question you came with, the industrial assumption: function, product, purpose. The engine does not answer. The engine *is doing*, has been doing, will continue doing when the categories of your questioning have dissolved into the heat and the dark and the saturated sound. Perhaps it is manufacturing the conditions of its own continuation. Perhaps it is dreaming geology into being, growing this cavity, this hidden high place, this packed and abundant and slowly-transforming world, one mineral exhalation at a time. Perhaps it is older than purpose, older than function, a remainder from a cosmos where those categories had not yet emerged.

You will leave, if you leave, with the ringing still in your teeth, the pressure still behind your sternum. The seam will be harder to find from this side, or easier—you will not be able to say which, afterward. The world you return to will seem *thin*, provisional, full of dangerous empty spaces and inexplicable water, vegetation that grows without heat, light that falls without engine. You will miss, sometimes, the density. The certainty of the saturated. The patient, ongoing, unwitnessed labor of the first and continuing thing.

Or you will not leave. This is also possible. The engine does not recruit, but the cavity *accommodates*, and there are those who have become native, who have found the frequency of their own bodies in the engine's ongoing chord, who have slowed to the dynamic of emergence, who have learned to see in darkness by no longer seeking light. They do not build. They do not construct. They *continue*, as the engine continues, as the fauna continue, as the hot half-formed walls continue their slow decision between glass and stone and something else entirely.

The Cavity of the First Engine. You have been closer to it than you know. The ringing in your teeth, sometimes, when the world is quiet. The pressure behind your sternum, when you lean against certain walls. It is still emerging. It is still *becoming*. It has all the time that ever was, and all the time that ever will be, and no time at all, packed into its hidden, high, hot, saturated, slowly-transforming, abundant, dark, industrial, unbuilt, unwatered, unplanted, uneroded, ongoing self.

You could press through. The seam is thinner than it should be.
