I need to hold these parameters in tension and let them find their shape. Water at 1—damp but not wet. Vegetation lush at 2.1, yet fauna rare at 1.2. Urban built environment at 2.5, but sparse density at 1.2. Beyond-modern technology at 3.0, dim light at 0.8. Strange at 2.4. Active dynamics but mostly quiet. Let me follow where this pulls.

## The Drip Cities of the Veldt

The sky is the color of a bruise that never fully formed—mauve-gray, luminescent in its dimness, as if the sun had been filtered through smoked glass and then forgotten. What light reaches the surface arrives indirectly, scattered by particulate matter that hangs in the lower atmosphere like suspended breath. You learn to see by depth rather than edge, by the slow revelation of contours emerging from color rather than shadow. The dimness has a quality of patience. It waits for your eyes to adjust, and then it waits longer still.

The land is low, barely risen from some ancient basin, and young in its geological temperament—sharp where water has begun to cut, soft where sediment still remembers being something else. The erosion has not yet learned patience. Gullies strike through pale stone like arguments, sudden and angular, their walls showing strata that seem too fresh, too *recently decided upon*, as if the rock itself were still settling into its identity. Here and there, the ground has cracked into polygonal patterns, the earth shrinking as it dries, then swelling with damp, breathing slowly across centuries that feel like hours.

And it is damp, pervasively, strangely. Not wet enough for rivers to run continuously, not dry enough for true desert. Instead: condensation. The air holds moisture the way a held breath holds sound. At dawn and dusk, surfaces weep. The pale stone of the gully walls grows dark with gathered damp, and water moves through the landscape invisibly, by phase change, by the slow osmosis of atmosphere. What standing water exists collects in the lowest depressions, not pools so much as *occurrences*—temporary gatherings that reflect the bruised sky with a fidelity the sky itself lacks. These pockets evaporate by midday, return by morning, a rhythm older than the cities that have grown among them.

Because there are cities. Or something that answers to the name of cities, though their nature strains the category.

They are sparse. That is the first thing. The urban does not cluster here; it seeds. You might walk for hours across the veldt, through its damp breath and its dim light, and encounter nothing but the lush vegetation—low, spreading, succulent in its way, leaves broad and dark to gather what photons they can, root systems shallow and wide to catch the migrating moisture. The plants grow thick where they grow, in bands and clusters that follow invisible water-lines, but between them stretches the pale stone, the breathing earth, the *space*. And then, without transition, a city.

Not rising from the land. *Placed* upon it, or perhaps grown into it, or perhaps—most accurately—*negotiated* with it. The buildings do not stand so much as *hang*, their lower stories narrow, their upper volumes swelling outward like inverted teardrops, like the bell of a flower, like something that grew downward from a cloud and then thought better of it. The material is not stone, not metal, not anything that registers cleanly to touch or sight. It has the warmth of organic matter, the density of something engineered at scales invisible, the faint translucency of a fingernail held to that dim light. You can see, in the thicker sections, slow movements within—circulations, processes, the city metabolizing.

The technology is not worn or wielded here. It is *ambient*, *constitutional*. The buildings breathe; you can feel the slight pressure changes as they inhale the damp air, extract what they need, exhale something drier, something that carries faint bioluminescence—green-blue, the only bright color in this world, and it comes from the cities' exhalations, gathering in low pockets like luminous fog. The structures self-repair; you will find no crack that persists more than a day, no surface that weathers in any pattern comprehensible to conventional physics. They exist in states of superposition, I am told, though the telling is difficult, the words coming from mouths that may not be mouths, the understanding arriving before the explanation, retrocausal, unsettling.

The cities are urban in their organization, their infrastructure, their layered density of function. But they are sparse in their distribution, each one separated by that lush veldt, that breathing space, each one complete unto itself, a town's worth of complexity, a city's worth of relationship, suspended in isolation. They do not trade with each other, not in goods, not in information that travels through any medium I can identify. They may not be separate cities at all, but aspects of one thing, distributed across the low landscape the way a single organism distributes its organs.

And they are quiet. Mostly quiet. The sound parameter demands this, yet the dynamic is active, multiple processes in motion. The resolution: the activity is *subsonic*, *infrasonic*, *electrochemical*. The vegetation grows at speeds you can watch if you are patient, the broad leaves tracking the dim light's slow migration across the sky, their root systems sending pulses through the damp soil that other plants receive, respond to, a forest-wide conversation in frequencies below hearing. The cities' internal processes generate fields that make your teeth ache if you stand too close, a pressure in the sinuses, a sense of being *noticed*. The water in its phase changes, its invisible migrations, is the loudest thing, and it is silent.

What fauna exists is rare, and strange in its rarity. I have seen things like insects, but with too many joints, moving in directions that seem to violate the local geometry—sideways through space that should be forward, vanishing into angles that don't quite add up. I have heard, once, something large moving through the vegetation far off, the sound of its passage arriving before the cause, the plants bending in response to a presence that reached them already departed. The cities may have fauna of their own, internal, not emerging, or emerging only in forms I cannot recognize as life. The bioluminescent exhalations sometimes gather into shapes that persist for moments, that seem to observe, that dissipate when directly regarded.

The weirdness is not an overlay upon this place. It is the medium. The beyond-modern technology does not feel like human achievement, nor alien imposition, but like a *different physics* having been locally negotiated, a consensus reality that diverged from ours at some point not locatable in time. The dim light, the damp air, the sparse cities, the rare fauna, the active silence—all of it holds together not despite its contradictions but through them, the way a living body holds together despite the contradictions of organ and function, cell and purpose, the individual and the colony.

I have lived here, if living is the word, for a period I cannot measure. The cities do not refuse entry; they do not exactly permit it. You find yourself within, or you don't, and the finding is not a matter of walking through a door. The interior spaces fold in ways that increase volume without increasing exterior dimension, that connect distant points without passage between. I have stood in a chamber that was also a garden, the lush vegetation growing from surfaces that oriented in all directions simultaneously, the damp air circulating without movement, the dim light arriving from no source, sufficient to the leaves' needs, generated perhaps by the leaves themselves in some reciprocal arrangement.

The rules of this world: causality is local but not global. Observation changes not the observed but the *path by which observation became possible*, retroactively, so that memory must be continuously revised to maintain coherence. The cities are built, but the building is ongoing, eternal, each structure completing itself in the moment of being perceived and beginning again when attention wavers. Water is conserved not by cycle but by *preference*, the landscape choosing dampness over wetness, over dryness, a stable instability. Life is lush where it is, rare where it isn't, and the categories of plant and animal and city and technology are administrative conveniences from elsewhere, not native taxonomy.

I am writing this, if writing is the word, from within one of the cities, or from the space between them, or from a vantage that doesn't resolve to either. The sound of my own thought is the loudest thing I have encountered here, and it is mostly quiet. The vegetation outside—if there is an outside—moves in its slow active growth, tracking light that barely reaches it, sustained by damp that never gathers to flowing. The city breathes around me, or I breathe within it, the distinction having long since lost its utility. Something rare passes through the space I occupy, or occupies the space I pass through, and is gone before I can determine its nature, which is perhaps the nature of rarity, perhaps the nature of this place, perhaps no distinction at all.

The veldt extends. The cities seed. The damp persists, the dim light, the young stone still deciding what it will become. I am becoming something here, or something is becoming me, or the grammar of becoming itself is undergoing the slow active process that this place demands, that it enacts upon everything within its reach, which may be all that is, which may be nothing more than this moment of description, this held breath, this bruised and luminous atmosphere waiting for eyes to adjust, waiting longer still.
