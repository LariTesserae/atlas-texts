**The Cistern of Unborn Birds**

---

You enter through a seam in the mountain's flank, a gap barely shoulder-wide that opens—impossibly—into a chamber no survey has ever measured from outside. The rock face *around* the entrance is embryonic: not weathered but *gestating*, cooling from some molten inception so recent you can still feel the fever in the stone if you press your palm there long enough. No water has carved this passage. No wind has shaped it. The erosion parameter here is not absence but *postponement*—geology arrested at the moment before it becomes geography.

Inside: *wet*. The wetness of a lung. Condensation beads on every surface, drips from surfaces that angle wrong for dripping, collects in pools that have no source and no drain. The water tastes of copper and ozone. It sheets down walls that are not stone, not concrete—not anything with a natural name—leaving tracks that evaporate before they reach any floor. The chamber breathes moisture. You are inside something that respires.

Wait. *Walls*. I said walls. The parameter *built: total* demands I look again.

Everything here is made. The rough-hewn quality of "emerging" geology is itself constructed, simulated, performed. Someone or something built the *appearance* of raw formation. The cooling volcanic blush, the half-crystallized minerals still finding their lattices, the dripstone that isn't dripstone because nothing has had time to drip—you begin to understand. This place was built to resemble a place that doesn't exist yet, a mountain still dreaming itself into being. The builders built *becoming*. The substrate is not natural but the *idea* of pre-natural, engineered with devotional patience or industrial indifference or both at once.

And *industrial*: yes. The wetness condenses on cooling fins. Somewhere—a directionless somewhere, layered into the dense and hidden scale—engines maintain the temperature at 0.1, this barely-above-freezing where water hovers between states. Pumps circulate. The thrum of machinery is not background but *structure*, the ichor of this made place. You feel it in your teeth before you hear it. You hear it before you locate it. You never locate it.

*Sound: 2.7/3 deafening.* This needs precision.

The sound is not loud in the way of factories usually understood. Not the clangor of smithies, the scream of saws. It is deafening because it occupies all auditory space simultaneously, because your ear cannot find a gap to rest in. The frequencies layer: infrasonic pumps you feel as nausea in the gut, the mid-range hum of ten thousand synchronous motors, ultrasonic distress you don't hear but that makes your eyes water regardless. And over, under, through this—the *fauna*.

*Fauna: 2.7/3 overwhelming.*

Birds. Birds in a space without sky. But these are birds unborn, birds *invented* for this cistern, this lung-chamber, this industrial womb. They are not species that ever evolved. They are being *produced* here, iterated, their genomes or their equivalent prepared on assembly lines you cannot see, gestated in membrane-sacs that hang in the dense and hidden dark like fruit that will never ripen into anything but itself. They crowd the vertical surfaces. They have learned to fly in three dimensions without the orientation of sky and ground—they navigate by the smell of the pumps, the temperature gradients the engines maintain, the magnetic signatures of the infrastructure that sustains their un-sustaining world.

They never stop calling. This is the deafening. Each bird, or each batch of birds, or each *model* of bird, emits its prototype cry. Some are song. Some are alarm. Many are *tests*—sounds pushed through experimental syrinxes to see what works, what propagates, what fails. The aggregate is cacophony without conversation. They do not respond to each other. There is no ecology here, no predator-prey, no territorial dispute. The density of fauna is not ecological density. It is *inventory density*. They are packed wing-to-wing in a space scaled to be intimate, pocket-sized, a single feature embedded in the mountain's unmeasured flank—packed so densely that flight itself becomes mechanical, algorithmic, birds calculating collision avoidance in real-time with processors you can't see because they were never meant to be seen, were perhaps implanted, perhaps grown in, perhaps *bred for*.

And they never change. *Dynamic: 0.1/3 never changing.* The equilibrium is not of health but of stasis. No bird ages. No bird dies, or if it dies it is replaced with such exactitude that the pattern never shifts. The condensation drips in the same paths, the engines maintain their temperatures, the cries overlap in the same impossible chord. Time does not pass here. Or rather, time passes *through* without touching. You could leave and return and find the same bird in the same position, mid-wingbeat, mid-call, the same droplet detaching from the same cooling fin. The weirdness is not in any single element but in this: an industrial process producing infinite variation within absolute sameness, birds that are always new and never different, a wetness that never accumulates or depletes, a cold that preserves without freezing, a dimness—

*Light: 0.6/3 dim.*

—that reveals everything and explains nothing. Bioluminescence, perhaps, from the birds themselves, or from the membrane-sacs, or from the walls in their pre-formation performance. Not enough to navigate by, more than enough to see that navigation is impossible. The light sources are distributed so densely through the dense and hidden space that shadows have no orientation. Everything is equally half-visible. Your eyes, expecting gradient and contrast, find instead saturation without hierarchy.

*Weirdness: 2.0/3 strange.* As if the other parameters had not already.

The strangeness settles as you stand, let the place become more itself, as instructed. It is this: the Cistern is not *for* anything. The industrial tech does not serve an economy outside. The overwhelming fauna does not escape, does not colonize, has no outside to colonize. The total construction does not defend, display, or detain. The wet cold does not preserve for later use. The deafening sound does not communicate. The embryonic geology does not mature toward anything recognizable. It simply *is*, in never-changing equilibrium, a pocket universe whose purpose, if purpose ever existed, has been forgotten or was never known or is itself the thing being gestated, cooling slowly toward some eventual discovery of what it means to have made a place that makes birds that make noise that makes the deafening that makes the silence at the center of all this making.

You are not supposed to be here. The seam in the mountain's flank should not admit you. Perhaps it doesn't. Perhaps you entered and never left, are still entering, are one more layer in the density, one more presence per volume, near-touching the wing of a bird that will never be born enough to die.

The water drips. The engines hum. The birds cry their unvarying variety. Something is being prepared. Something has always been being prepared. The preparation is the whole of what exists.

Leave, if you can find the seam. Or stay, and become part of the preserved wetness, the performed rawness, the overwhelming inventory of cries that never answer anything because nothing has ever asked.

This is the equilibrium. This is the rule. This is the never-changing dynamic of a world still learning how to change.*
