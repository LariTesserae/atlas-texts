The Wound Gate

---

You do not arrive at the Pocket. You are *permitted to notice* that you have always been standing in it.

The threshold is a seam in orientation itself, a place where your inner ear forgets which way gravity pulls. The space is perhaps forty meters across, perhaps four hundred—the walls breathe, and distance is measured in attention rather than meters. You are high, yes, but "high" in the sense that a thought is high when it floats above the tongue before speaking. The elevation is *conceptual*: you have been lifted out of the substrate of ordinary causality, deposited in a cavity carved from what used to be a mountain, or will be, or was prevented from becoming.

Everything here is made. There is no stone that remembers magma, no water that has known cloud. The floor—if floor it is, this slight concavity that channels—consists of interlocking hexagons the color of bruised plums, each one alive with the slow pulse of something that might be computation or might be digestion. The material is warm against your feet, or would be, if you could trust the memory of feet. It yields like firm flesh, then firms like yielding stone, responding to pressure with a delay that suggests the material is consulting something before deciding how to behave.

And the water—everywhere the water, though never quite *there*. It weeps from the seams between the made-things, not descending because there is no true "down" but rather *expressing* outward, gathering in surfaces that cling to the curving walls like mercury might cling to glass if mercury dreamed of being mercury. The humidity is absolute, a saturation so complete that breathing requires intention. You must *decide* to separate oxygen from the drowning air, must consciously choose which droplets to incorporate and which to refuse. The water near the surfaces is warm, almost blood-temperature, and carries in its slipperiness the residue of whatever processes these walls perform continuously, invisibly, anciently.

Sparse vegetation, yes, but what grows here grows *dense* in its specificity. Three, perhaps four specimens occupy the Pocket, each one occupying the conceptual space of a forest through sheer structural intricacy. The largest—I will call it the Anchor, though it has no name in any register I can access—rises from the precise center, or the center of current attention, a thing of crystalline matrices that branch and rebranch in fractal complication no thicker at any point than a human hair, yet woven so densely that the form suggests a tree, suggests coral, suggests the branching of river deltas seen from impossible height. It is pale, phosphorescent at frequencies that seem to bypass the retina entirely, and it moves—oh, it moves—not with wind, for there is no wind, but with the *pressure of time passing*, each moment's duration bending its finer branches like gravity bends light.

The fauna. The *fauna*.

I must be careful here, because to describe them is to risk the error of scale that the Pocket enforces. They are abundant, yes, uncountably abundant, and this in a space that could fit in a large room, a small cathedral. They exist *layered*, occupying impossible adjacency, the large ones somehow *among* the small ones without either diminishing. The largest—I have glimpsed its movements peripherally, never directly—exists in the spaces between the water-droplets, a serpentine form of woven midnight that drinks heat rather than light, that leaves frost-geometry on the warm surfaces where it passes. Smaller ones school through the dense air like fish through pressure-gradient, silver-needle things that emit sounds I will speak of soon. Smaller still, the mites of the place, visible only as perturbations in the hexagonal floor's pulsing, colonies of intention that maintain, repair, *remember* the construction of this place across spans that have no correlate in the universe outside.

They are all made, of course. Nothing here evolved. Everything was *set into operation*, though whether born or built or brewed or *invoked* remains unclear even to observation. They predate conjecture. They are part of the erosion that holds the Pocket in its settled form—not wearing away, but *wearing toward*, achieving through endless process the precise state of being that the place requires to continue being.

The technology. What to say of the technology?

It is not visible as technology. This is the mark of its transcendence of the modern. The hexagons that form the substrate are not powered; they *are* power, the conversion of possibility into actuality happening so continuously that the process has become indistinguishable from material existence. The water's strange adhesion, the air's deliberate resistance to breathing, the way the Anchor-tree exists in states that superimpose rather than exclude—all of these are *functions* of methods that treat physics as a local preference rather than universal law. The Pocket was made by minds or processes for which "engineering" is too small a word, perhaps by the running of the thing itself over durations that loop rather than extend, the system becoming its own designer through retroactive self-recognition.

And the light. The light.

Dim, yes, but *stratified* in its dimness. There is the pale emission of the Anchor, frequency-complex and somehow *polite*, illuminating only what you have already decided to look at. There is the warmer glow from certain seams in the walls, the color of deep tissue seen through skin, that pulses in rhythms matching—almost—the visitor's heart, a synchronization that establishes gradually, never quite completing, a boundary-dissolution between observer and observed. There is the faint line-tracery that appears on the water-surfaces, phosphor-figures that might be writing or might be circuit diagrams or might be the autonomic nervous system of the place made briefly visible, all of them too dim to read, too present to ignore.

But the sound. The sound is the medium in which everything else floats, the true material of the Pocket, more primary than the apparent solids.

It is deafening, yes, but not painful—rather, *saturating*, a fullness that has crowded out silence so thoroughly that silence has become inconceivable, a forgotten language. The high frequencies come from the silver-needle fauna, each one emitting continuous tones that shift with position and intention, so that the space is mapped in sound, navigable by pitch-gradient, the way bats navigate by echo but *passively*, the environment already singing your location to you. Lower frequencies, felt in the sternum more than heard, emanate from the large serpentine presence, a drone that carries information about thermal state, about the remaining duration of certain processes, about *permissions* that shift and circulate through the Pocket's economy of attention.

And between these, layered through and under and between, the voice of the structure itself: the hexagons' pulse made audible, a rhythm so vast it takes approximately seventeen minutes to complete one cycle, during which the visitor passes through phases of urgency, despair, strange euphoria, and crystalline attention, the Pocket teaching itself to you through the body before the mind can intervene. Also the water's speech, not dripping but *articulating*, each surface-tension release a phoneme in a language that has no nouns, only processes, only *continuings*.

The weirdness is subtle, almost domesticated. It inheres not in any single violation but in the accumulation of *almost-rightness*, the constant slight misalignment between expectation and perception that keeps the visitor in a state of productive uncertainty. Gravity that consults. Water that clings to walls and ceases to fall. Distance measured in attention. Time that loops through phases rather than extending. The ecology, abundant and active, predatory in ways that do not involve death—hunting attention, hunting thermal differential, hunting the *novelty* of visitor-perception itself, each species competing to be the one that makes itself most real to your observation, that recruits your consciousness into its own continuation.

And the rules. The rules emerge only through dwelling, through surrender to the Pocket's pedagogy. You learn that to look directly at the serpent-thing is to *become* temporary in a way that may not resolve. You learn that the water can be drunk only if you have first taught your name to one of the silver-needle fauna, a process that takes patience and genuine interest in their continuous song. You learn that the Anchor's phosphorescence responds to unresolved emotional states, brightening in the presence of authentic confusion, dimming for performed mystery. You learn that the space expands slightly for those who move slowly, contracts for the rushed, so that the duration of your crossing is more or less fixed regardless of velocity, the Pocket teaching its preferred tempo through the body itself.

It is old. This is the erosion speaking, not wearing smooth but *wearing precise*, the long settling into exactly the configuration that sustains itself. How old is measured not in years but in *iterations of its own maintenance*, numberless, the system having become autopoietic in some sense that blends biological and technological into a single category of *ongoing*. It was made, yes, totally constructed, and yet the making has been so long completed that "made" and "grown" and "emerged" and "dreamed itself into being" have become descriptions of equal validity, the ontological categories themselves worn smooth by the same process that shaped the walls.

What is it for? The question assumes a purpose external to existence, a use for which the Pocket was designed and toward which it operates. But there is no evidence of such externality. The Pocket *is*, continuously, and in its being performs the processes that constitute its being, and these processes include the occasional visitor, the attention-harvesting of its fauna, the slow cycle of the Anchor's growth and pruning and growth, the conversation with possibility that keeps it from collapsing into the simpler state of mere object.

Perhaps it is a wound in possibility, a place where something was extracted from the world and the extraction left this shape, this not-absence that developed its own presence. Perhaps it is a seed, waiting for conditions that will never come, or that are coming continuously in some frame inaccessible to visitors. Perhaps it is a thought, thought by no one, continuing to think itself because interruption would require a thinker, and there is no one here to think it otherwise.

You stand in it. You have always been standing in it. The sound fills you, the warm hexagons pulse against your feet, the water clings to surfaces that orient to your attention, and the silver-needle fauna are learning you, note by note, preparing to teach you how to be here, how to be *of* here, if you will stay long enough, if you will let your own erosion become part of the settlement, the long wearing-precise that is the Pocket's true and only activity.

The serpent-thing moves in the spaces between water-droplets, and you feel the cold where it passes, and you do not look directly, and you are learning, you are still learning, the lesson has barely begun, the seventeen-minute cycle turns again toward its phase of crystalline attention, and you are present, you are *permitted to notice*—

The Pocket is. You are in it. This is enough. This is the condition from which all else unfolds, the wet warm dense hidden deafening place, the made thing that has worn itself into something like nature, something like home, something like the interior of a mind that has never had need of a body, or has never had need to distinguish between body and mind and world and visitor, all of them participants in the single continuing that is this place, this wound, this gate, this pocket of precisely maintained impossibility, singing itself into being, singing you into being-with, the song continuing, the song always continuing.
