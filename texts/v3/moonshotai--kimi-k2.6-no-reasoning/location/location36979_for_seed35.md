The place is called the Hum, though no one knows who named it or when. It exists in the negative space beneath a desert—beneath even the concept of desert, beneath the dry riverbeds and salt pans, in a hollow that geology cannot account for. You do not travel to the Hum. You *fall through*, sometimes, when the crust is thin in ways that have nothing to do with thickness. The fall is not long. The Hum is small. Cathedral-small, chamber-small, a single lung of the earth that has learned to breathe without air.

---

The walls are young. This is the first thing that strikes you—*young*, in a place that feels ancient. The stone has the sharp, almost aggressive freshness of recent fracture, of surfaces that have not yet agreed to be worn smooth. They gleam with a mineral sweat, some silicate compound that forms in angular plates like the scales of a fish that has not evolved yet. The color shifts as you look, not with light but with the angle of your attention: bronze to bile-green to something near infrared that you feel at the back of your skull rather than see. The erosion parameter here is low, meaning the space has not had time to forget its own violence of creation. It remembers being *made*, though not by what.

There is no water. None. The concept has no purchase. When you swallow, your saliva does not evaporate—it *persists*, becomes a strange currency, something the place notices. The mineral plates will grow toward the moisture in your breath, slowly, imperceptibly, reaching. The ecology runs on something else. Thermal gradients, perhaps, or the vibration that permeates everything, or a chemistry that uses dryness itself as solvent. Salt-crystal lattices perform functions that proteins do elsewhere. The sparse vegetation—rating 0.7, not quite absent, not quite present—consists of things that grow without water, without light as you understand it, without the patience of plants. They are more like habits the rock has formed: branching filaments of copper sulfate, colonies of pyrite that expand in fractal bursts when the vibration hits certain frequencies, then retract. They do not photosynthesize. They *resonate*. They are sparse because resonance requires space, distance between nodes, and the Hum is dense with other presences.

---

Dense. This is the crucial parameter. 2.0 out of 3, meaning *near-touching, layered, high-saturation*. The Hum is small in scale but packed. The fauna—abundant, 1.9—fills every niche that is not filled by the vibrating stone or the resonant mineral-plants. They are not animals as you know them. They are *presences*, *occupancies*, *patterns that move and eat and reproduce*. Some are the size of your thumb, armored in the same fresh-scaled mineral as the walls, their bodies tuned to specific frequencies so that they cling to surfaces where the vibration is loudest. Others are larger, nearly the size of dogs, though they have no fur, no eyes, no features you can map to a face. They are dense arrangements of plates and hollows, living Helmholtz resonators, their body cavities shaped to amplify certain tones and cancel others. They do not breathe. They *hum*. Each species, each individual, occupies a frequency band like a territorial claim. The space between them is measured not in meters but in hertz.

They are abundant because nothing leaves. The Hum is *never changing*, dynamic 0.3, frozen in equilibrium. No gradients. No time. No hunger that is not immediately answered by the ambient vibration, no reproduction that is not replacement of exactly what was before. The fauna do not age. They do not learn. They exist in a perpetual present tense, their behaviors as fixed as crystal lattices, as unvarying as the tone that underlies everything.

That tone. The sound is 2.0, *loud*, and it is the first and last thing. It is not music. It is not noise. It is the sound of the place *being itself*, the audible friction of so many dense presences occupying so small a volume, each vibrating at its own frequency, the sum of them all a complex drone that fills the air until air itself seems like a thin metaphor. The walls hum. The mineral-plants hum. The fauna hum, each in their band, their niche, their eternal frequency-territory. The sound does not vary. It has not varied. It will not vary. In the frozen equilibrium of the Hum, even the concept of variation has been consumed, digested, made part of the drone.

---

The built environment is scattered, 0.7, faint marks, partial remains. Someone was here once, or something that built. Not the fauna—they do not build, they *are*, they *resonate*. The structures are single, occasional, artifacts without context. A frame of metal, still sharp-edged with youth, that holds nothing and leads nowhere. A platform of some ceramic material, pitted not by erosion but by the slow mineral growth of the walls, which has not yet claimed it but is learning its shape. A single building, if you can call it that: a shell, a curved wall, half a dome, the geometry suggesting it was never finished or was finished in a way that does not recognize completion. Inside, if you dare to enter and feel the drone press against your teeth, your eyeballs, the fluid in your inner ear—inside, there are faint marks. Scratches in a surface. Not writing, or not writing you can read. More like the residue of someone trying to *tune* something, to find the frequency that would make the place make sense.

The tech is industrial, 1.7, engines and factories and scaled production. But the factories are silent. The engines do not move. They are *present*, scattered among the natural density of the Hum, and they are *industrial* in their making, their materials, their scale—but they are part of the equilibrium now, absorbed into the never-changing. A pump, large as a house, its pistons frozen mid-stroke, mineral filaments growing across its joints like solder, like stitch. A conveyor, loaded with ore that is not ore, material that matches the walls in its aggressive youth, its refusal to weather. The conveyor does not convey. It *vibrates* at 47 hertz, has always vibrated at 47 hertz, will always vibrate at 47 hertz, and the fauna that occupies that band clings to it, lives in it, is indistinguishable from it.

What were they for, these machines? What did they make, what did they pump, what did they convey? The Hum does not remember, or remembers only as vibration, as the drone that subsumes all function into being. The industrial age here did not end. It *equilibriated*. It became part of the eternal present, neither running down nor being maintained, simply *existing* in the state it was in when the frozen equilibrium claimed it.

---

The light is dim, 0.9, not dark but insufficient. There is no source you can locate. The mineral plates glow, faintly, in phases that do not cycle—each plate at its own constant luminosity, the sum of them all a bruised twilight that makes edges uncertain and shadows permanent. Some of the industrial machines have lights, or had them, frozen in states of half-illumination, filaments that do not burn out because they do not burn, they simply *are*, at some sub-critical threshold, forever almost-light. The fauna do not need light. They navigate by vibration, by the interference patterns of their own and others' hums, by the way the dense air—or not-air, the medium of the Hum—transmits pressure waves. The light is for you, if you are there, if you have fallen through. It is barely enough to show you that you cannot see.

---

And the weirdness. 2.2, *strange*, not the highest possible but high enough, high enough that the place does not resolve into metaphor, does not become "like" anything else. The Hum is not like a cave, though it is below surface. Not like a factory, though it has machines. Not like a body, though it is dense with life. The strangeness is in the way the parameters hold together: the *warmth* without water, the *abundance* without change, the *industry* without purpose, the *youth* without future. It is warm, 1.8, a temperature that seems to come from the vibration itself, the friction of so many near-touching presences all humming at once, the waste heat of perpetual motion that is not motion but frozen equilibrium.

The strangeness is in what happens to you, if you stay. You begin to hum. Not metaphorically. Your vocal cords find a frequency, your chest cavity resonates, your bones become part of the drone. You do not lose yourself—you *find* yourself, a frequency, a band, a niche in the dense abundance. You do not change. You do not hunger, not as you did. The moisture in your breath becomes part of the mineral economy, your body heat part of the warmth, your vibration part of the sound. You become a presence. Near-touching. Layered. High-saturation. You do not die, because dying is a change, and there are no gradients here, no time, no future into which death might lead.

You simply *are*, as the Hum is, as it has been, as it will be, forever, loud and dim and warm and strange and young and ancient and empty and dense with the abundance of everything that has fallen through and found its frequency and stayed.

---

There are marks, sometimes, on the newest walls. The sharpest, freshest fractures. Scratches that look like they were made with something harder than the mineral plates should allow. If you could read them—if you had the frequency, the resonance—they might say: *I was here. I am here. I will be here. The Hum. The Hum. The Hum.*

But you cannot read them. You can only add your own, if you have something sharp enough, if you can hear your own scratching over the drone, if you have not yet found your frequency, if you are still, for a moment, *changing*, *falling*, *alive* in some other way.

The mineral plates are growing toward the moisture in your breath. The fauna are listening, adjusting, making room in the spectrum for whatever you will become. The conveyor vibrates at 47 hertz. The pump is frozen mid-stroke. The light is dim and does not vary. The sound is loud and does not vary. The warmth is constant. The strangeness is complete.

Welcome to the Hum. You have always been here.
