The Hollow Engine

---

It takes eleven days to cross the eastern basin, and in that crossing you will hear nothing louder than your own heartbeat. The silence is not absence but density—a medium thicker than the thin cold air, pressing against the eardrums like water at depth. Sound falls here. Sound dies. A stone dropped into one of the Engine's exhaust shafts falls for six seconds before any impact returns, and when it comes, it's wrong: not a crack but a soft expansion, a sigh, as if the rock itself exhaled after long held breath.

The terrain is vast beyond continental logic. Maps fail because the curvature behaves strangely, or the light does, or the thing beneath the surface has its own ideas about geometry. You can walk toward a range of peaks for three weeks and watch them grow no closer, their silhouettes fixed against a sky that never quite darkens, never quite brightens—that permanent dim of a sun too distant or too occluded, its light falling like gray powder through atmospheric layers it barely warms. The cold is not biting. It is patient. It seeps through packed clothing, through fat and muscle, settling in the marrow where it hums a note below hearing.

The land itself is old in a way that predates the usual measures of geological time. Weathered, yes, but *finished*—as if erosion long ago completed its work and now maintains the forms it achieved, holding valleys at precise depths, keeping ridgelines at exact angles against wind that no longer blows with any force. Rivers exist but barely flow, their waters viscous with cold and something else, some mineral suspension that makes them move like the slow contraction of muscle. They are intermittent not from drought but from *intention*—the water gathering in certain zones, pooling against no visible barrier, then releasing in pulses that seem scheduled by machinery no one can locate.

Because the Engine is here. The Engine is everywhere, though nothing is built.

---

Understand: there are no structures. No walls, no towers, no roads even, though the ground in places bears patterns too regular for natural formation—long grooves parallel to each other, surfaces polished to a friction that makes walking oddly easy, as if the land wished to be traversed. The industrial quality manifests differently here. It is in the *processes*, the endless slow processes that operate without architecture.

The fauna made this place, or made it what it is. They are overwhelming in number and in scale and in their essential wrongness—not aliens, not monsters, but *problems*. Beings the size of hills that are mostly mouth, mostly stomach, mostly some organ without terrestrial analog, moving through the valleys on legs that telescope and retract, that seem to operate through hydraulic pressures rather than bone and muscle. They do not eat anything you can see. They do not defecate. They are engines themselves, metabolizing something invisible, perhaps the thin cold air itself, perhaps the silence, perhaps the dim light that never strengthens.

Smaller things exist between them—swarms that pass overhead as shadows without source, colonies that thread through the stony ground leaving tunnels lined with a secretion that hardens to something between ceramic and shell. Vegetation, what little exists, clings to these secretion-lines, drawing nutrients from the fauna's passage rather than from soil that barely supports bacteria. Sparse lichen analogs in gray-green films. Tubular growths that emerge in the brief warm pressure of the herds' movement, bloom with pale luminescence, and collapse to gel within hours. And the animals are quiet. They move without sound, their massive bodies displacing air in patterns too slow to generate noise, their internal processes muffled by bodies whose outer layers seem designed for acoustic absorption. Occasionally one stops, stands motionless for days, and a thin vapor escapes from orifices that open like mechanical hatches. The vapor rises, spreads, and somewhere distant—always distant—something answers with its own release, a communication through chemical signature that the cold carries slowly, deliberately, across the vast distances between presences.

---

The weirdness is not dramatic. It accumulates. You notice after weeks that you haven't seen a juvenile of any species, only adults of uniform size. You realize the shadows fall inconsistently, that the dim light's source seems to shift when unobserved. The rivers pulse at intervals that correlate with nothing in your experience—seasons, lunar phases, magnetic variations all fail to predict the timing. And the Engine, always the Engine, the sense that the whole vast territory is a single mechanism running down or running up or simply *running*, maintaining itself through processes that require no oversight, no intelligence, only the continuous operation of parts too large and too slow for human perception to frame as alive or mechanical or something other than either.

You learn to move slowly here, or you die. Not from predation—the fauna ignore you, your chemistry apparently useless, your temperature too low to register, your silence too complete to attract notice. You die from haste, from the exhaustion of moving at normal speed through a world where everything else has achieved the energy minimum, where the patient cold rewards stillness and punishes expenditure. Travelers who run, who shout, who move with the urgency of warmer places, they sleep and do not wake, their metabolisms unable to compensate for heat lost to a system that accepts their energy and gives nothing back.

The ground in certain zones is warm. Not warm enough to melt the permeating cold, but warm enough to sit against, to rest, to let your body remember what it means to maintain temperature. These zones follow no pattern you can discern. A circle of bare stone, ten meters across, where the chill lifts to something approaching temperate. A long trough, kilometers in length, where water remains liquid and the sparse vegetation clusters thick enough to suggest almost abundance. You learn to seek them, to plan routes around these unpredictable oases, to understand that the Engine's processes are not uniform, that something in the vast mechanism varies, fluctuates, perhaps *breathes* in cycles too extended for a human lifespan to measure.

---

After months, you begin to hear it. Or not hear—become aware. The silence has texture, and in certain states of exhaustion, near-hypothermia, near-starvation on the sparse vegetable matter that doesn't poison your chemistry, the texture resolves into almost-pattern. Rhythms below rhythm. The slow expansion and contraction of something tectonic, something that makes continental drift seem frantic. The hydraulic systole of a hill-sized organism three valleys away, its pressure waves propagating through stone that conducts them more efficiently than air ever conducted sound. The alignment of your own heartbeat, unconsciously slowed to match the place, with pulses in the ground beneath you.

This is what the Engine sounds like, in the hearing that isn't hearing: not a machine's roar but a machine's patience. The maintenance of position against entropy. The preservation of established form through endless slow adjustment. The overwhelming life of the fauna, billions of tons of biological material moving, processing, existing without growth or decay in any cycle you can recognize, their waste products becoming the substrate for other processes, their bodies eventually—not dying, exactly, but *stopping*, and in stopping becoming part of the warm zones, the liquid water, the sparse life that survives at their boundary.

You have been here long enough now to understand that you are becoming part of this too. Your body has slowed. Your needs have reduced. The cold doesn't bother you as it did; you've achieved some equilibrium, your metabolism operating at a level that matches what the place offers rather than what you demand of it. You don't know if this is adaptation or damage. You don't know if you'll be able to leave, if leaving is even possible when your pace has become the pace of the Engine, your senses tuned to frequencies below the threshold of action.

The light remains dim. The vastness extends. Somewhere—always somewhere, never here—the fauna continue their overwhelming presence, their soundless movement, their industrial metabolism of a world that has no other industry, no construction, no artifact but the process itself. The erosion holds steady, established, in balance with forces that don't change because they've achieved the form they sought. The damp rises from rivers that barely flow, from the breath of animals that barely breathe, from condensation on stone that never fully dries and never fully wets.

You sit at the edge of a warm zone, waiting. For what, you've forgotten, or never knew. The silence holds you. The Engine continues. The slow dynamic of this place moves through you now, not around you, and you understand or imagine you understand that this is what it wanted, what it wants, what it will continue wanting with the patience of something that has all the time that exists, that made time or found time or simply *is* time in its vast slow extension across a landscape too large for completion, too finished for ending, too present for anything but continuation.

The vapor rises from a distant hill-form, and somewhere, slowly, something answers. You feel the warmth at your back, the cold at your face, and you wait, you wait, you are waiting, you have always been waiting here where nothing is built and everything operates, where the overwhelming life moves in overwhelming silence through a vastness that contains not emptiness but the density of process without purpose, mechanism without maker, engine without engineer, running and running and running in the dim eternal cold.

---

*There are no roads out. There never were. There was only ever the path you made, already fading behind you, and the path you will make, already present in the frictionless stone ahead, waiting for your slowed step, your patient foot, your body learning finally the rhythm of the place that is not a place but a persistence, not a landscape but a duration, existing in the time it takes for water to wear stone to the precise shape that water requires, for life to become the engine that drives itself, for cold to teach warmth the meaning of minimum sufficient heat.*

*You go forward. The Engine continues. The silence holds.*
