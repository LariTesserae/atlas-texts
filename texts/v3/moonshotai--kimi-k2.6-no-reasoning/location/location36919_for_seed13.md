**The City of Stilled Spring**

The plateau called the Keth rises 2,400 meters above the low desert, and on it sits a city that should not be possible—a contradiction held in permanent suspension. The Keth is young in geological terms, thrust upward by a recent mountain-building event perhaps forty thousand years ago, its volcanic stone still raw-edged and unrounded, its canyons knife-straight rather than meandering. Basalt columns stand at sharp angles where they cracked from cooling. Landslide scars show white and fresh. The mountain beneath the city groans occasionally in its sleep, but never moves.

This is *never changing*, the core fact of the place. Nothing here alters. Nothing completes.

---

The city—if city is the word—spreads across the eastern shelf of the plateau in a manner both organized and inexplicably begun. Its builders worked in basalt and a pale volcanic tuff they called *shol*, tools of chipped obsidian and hammer-treated copper, simple wedges and mallets. They built walls three stories high in places, terraced the shelf's natural ledges, constructed cisterns and channels. The cisterns hold water, always, at the same level. The channels run with thin sheets of moisture that never increase in rain, never thin in drought. The water arrives from somewhere—from condensation that forms on the cold basalt each morning, from springs that neither strengthen nor fail, from a sky that never quite clears but never fully storms. It is damp here, perpetually. Moss grows on the north faces of walls in patterns identical to those it made centuries ago. Small creeping plants with waxy blue-green leaves fill the gaps between buildings, the same plants, the same gaps, unchanged.

The Keth received its builders at some point, and the builders made their copper and obsidian tools, and they raised their walls, and then—the change stopped. Not the people. The *process*. Construction sites remain sites eternally: a half-carved tuff block waiting beside a wall that ends in midair, its scaffolding still standing, its ropes still tensioned. A cup sits on a windowsill with liquid at its lip. A shadow falls at the same angle always, though the sun moves overhead in apparent normality, and the shadow does not.

The world here operates in loops rather than lines.

---

The city covers perhaps four square kilometers, open enough that one can stand at its center and see to its edges past the scattered structures. It is sparse in population now, if population is the right term for what inhabits it. The original builders—human, apparently, with their middling height and their five-fingered hands evident in glove-molds and carved reliefs—are not seen. Instead, the city holds *those who came after*, and *those who came to observe*, and *those who emerged from the stalemate itself*.

There are perhaps three hundred present at any given observation. They are not always the same three hundred. Wanderers arrive, climb the passes to the Keth, find the city, and some stay. Some sit down on benches or against walls and do not stand again, not frozen precisely, but settled into the equilibrium, their breathing slowing to match some respiration of the plateau itself, their needs reducing to the ambient dampness, the ambient coolness, the moss that can be chewed for reluctant sustenance. Others move through more normally, trading in copper wire and obsidian flakes, in dried moss and the fatty tails of certain animals, in information and in silence.

The buildings are urban in scale and conception but not in bustle. Multi-story structures with interior courtyards, a central plaza paved in hexagonal basalt blocks, what appears to be a reservoir system feeding public fountains—frozen at mid-flow, water suspended in shapes that violate surface tension, that hold their forms like solid glass yet remain somehow liquid to the touch, cold and wet but not flowing. Layered construction: additions built upon additions, but the additions are all contemporary, all simultaneous, the architectural styles not evolving but *coexisting*, the rough early walls abutting finer work with no transition, as if time's arrow has been replaced by time's sprawl.

---

The fauna is abundant in species if not in behavior. Creatures adapted to the stasis, or perhaps produced by it. There are animals like large hares with blue-black fur, visible at dawn and dusk at the same locations, always, running the same paths, eating the same clumps of wax-leaf. They breed, apparently, yet their numbers do not increase. Predators like elongated weasels with copper-colored eyes hunt them, successfully and unsuccessfully in identical proportions, the chase sometimes passing through the same intersection three times in an hour with different participants, the outcome always the same statistical blur. Birds with pale breasts nest in the higher columns, their calls falling into the quiet with strange regularity—three notes, space, three notes, space—never variation, never silence, never more.

The soundscape is mostly quiet, punctuated by these punctual birds, by the occasional groan of settling stone, by wind that moves without changing, without building or fading, a constant low presence that somehow never registers as this wind rather than wind itself.

And there is another sound, more unsettling for its rarity: human voices in conversation that does not progress. Two figures in a courtyard, visible from a colonnade, speaking words that loop. First visitor to the Keth, years ago, reported attempting to interrupt, to speak to the speakers, and finding that his own words entered the loop, became part of it, repeated with theirs until he walked away with force of will he could not later explain.

---

The vegetation is lush in its damp way, but disciplined by the equilibrium. Plants grow to certain sizes and no further. Seedlings appear at rates equal to the decay of mature specimens. A grove of dwarf conifers on the plateau's western edge has remained identical in extent and composition for as long as anyone has recorded. In spring—which comes but does not advance—a brief fuzz of flowers appears on certain mosses, always the same span, always the same patches.

The coolness permeates. Even at apparent midday the sun's heat seems absorbed by the young dark stone, radiated away at some constant rate. Shadows hold cold. The damp air carries a mineral taste, a suggestion of copper and sulfur and something greener, vegetal, static as fermentation arrested at its peak intensity.

---

Weirdness here is not dramatic but pervasive, infrastructural. The city works, after a fashion. Water is available, if one knows the cisterns. Shelter exists. Food can be found in the stunted grain that grows in certain walled gardens, in the animals if one can bear to kill something that will simply reappear, or appear to reappear, or perhaps never left. But nothing improves. Nothing deteriorates. Wounds heal at normal speed, but somehow the healing is also arrested, the scar forming incompletely and holding, the last stage of inflammation permanent, neither worsening nor resolving.

Some scholars—not scholars of the Keth, but observers from below, from the changing world—have theorized that the plateau exists in contact with something that resists time, or consumes its gradient, or that the builders attempted some working with their primitive tools that struck a vein of fundamental stasis in the fabric of place itself. Others suggest the builders *are* here, dispersed into the equilibrium, become the hare-paths and the frozen fountain-shapes and the looping conversations. Others say simpler: this is what exists when dynamic is subtracted, when the universe's tendency to differentiate is locally nullified, and the result is not death but *arrest*, a holding pattern of matter and energy that neither depletes nor renews.

---

The passes to the Keth are difficult, the young stone sharp and unstable-seeming in contradiction to its actual fixity. Visitors arrive perhaps a dozen per year. Some stay an hour and flee. Some stay days, weeks, and emerge changed in subtle ways—speaking in loops themselves for a time, or unable to make decisions, or conversely freed from anxiety about outcomes, having witnessed a place where outcomes are not at issue. A few remain, add themselves to the sparse population, find roles in the unmoving society: the moss-gatherers who collect the same moss from the same walls, the water-drawers, the ones who sit and watch.

There is no government. There are customs, performed without variation. There is no growth, no decay. The copper tools do not tarnish further. The obsidian edges remain at their achieved sharpness. Buildings begun remain begun. Buildings finished remain finished.

At night, or what functions as night, the sky above the Keth shows stars that move in their courses, apparently, but whose positions can be mapped and remapped identically, as if the celestial sphere rotates above a fixed point while the point itself is somehow disconnected from the rotation's meaning. The moon—that there is a moon is uncertain; it is frequently overcast in the thin cloud layer that perpetually hazes the plateau—appears in phases, but the phases do not correlate to any calendar, coming as they do in patterns that can be observed but not predicted.

---

What emerges, finally, is a place that holds its contradiction not by resolving it but by exemplifying it: urban without progress, lush without proliferation, abundant with life that does not live, cool and damp and young in its stone yet ancient in its stalemated condition, built by hands that worked and ceased working simultaneously, filled with sound that is mostly silence, light that illuminates without revealing change, a world cornered into equilibrium by means unknown, perhaps unknowable, certainly not replicable with the tools that helped produce it.

The City of Stilled Spring waits. It does not wait *for* anything. It simply waits, as a held breath waits, as potential energy waits in a precarious balance, as a story waits that will never be told because its telling would be a change, and change is the one absent parameter, the zero at the heart of the place, the dynamic that defines it precisely by its lack.
