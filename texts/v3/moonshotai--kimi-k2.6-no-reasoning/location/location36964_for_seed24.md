I need to work through these parameters carefully, letting them shape a place that holds all of them simultaneously.

**The Hollow of the Sleeping Mineral**

It is a bowl. That is the first thing to understand—a shallow bowl of stone, perhaps three hundred paces across at its widest, rimmed by low worn ridges that long ago ceased to be anything one would call mountains. The elevation is low, lower than the surrounding plain, and this depression has held its form through time that cannot be measured in familiar terms because nothing here measures time at all.

The darkness is absolute and yet not oppressive. It has no source to lack, no contrast to emphasize. One does not enter this dark from light; one simply *is* in it, always, the way a stone is in itself. If there were eyes here, they would find no purchase, yet the darkness seems almost to possess a quality of thickness, a slight resistance against movement, the way deep water resists a hand. It is warm darkness, held at that temperature which requires no explanation—neither the warmth of generation nor the warmth of decay, but something steadier, the warmth of a body that has never known cold.

The bowl floor is salt. Not the bright salt of evaporated seas, not the crusted salt of tidal flats, but something older and more transformed—a compacted salt-stone, almost porcelain in its smoothness where feet or wind might once have polished it, elsewhere slightly pitted with a texture like orange peel. The water parameter insists on absence, and here it manifests as *voided dryness*: not the aggressive aridity that kills by taking, but a condition so settled in its non-wetness that the concept of water has become irrelevant, a forgotten category like the names of colors in a blind species' philosophy. If you pressed your tongue to this salt—and who would?—you would taste not brine but something metallic, faintly bitter, the flavor of a chemistry that never learned to incorporate hydrogen and oxygen in that particular marriage.

The sparse vegetation manifests as *fossil habit*: not living plants, but the memory of plant-life encoded in mineral form. In the bowl's center, perhaps a dozen structures rise knee-high to waist-high from the salt-floor—carbon filigree, lace-rock, the calcified remains of something that might once have grown in a wetter dream of this place. They are delicate, these forms, like the antlers of extinct deer turned to dark chalk, or the branching fans of coral transposed to an element that never hosted reefs. They do not grow. They have not grown. They *are*, in the manner of crystals or mathematical truths. Some cluster in twos and threes; others stand solitary, maintaining the sparse density that the parameters demand—enough space between them that each maintains its singular presence against the surrounding emptiness, enough that the eye (if there were light for eyes) would travel through void to find them, would register each as an event.

The erosion parameter: established, settled-form, in-balance. This place has achieved its shape and keeps it. The rim-ridges are worn to curves that suggest water-erosion, yet no water has touched them. Something else has sculpted them—perhaps wind of a different density than we know, perhaps the slow abrasion of darkness itself against protrusion, perhaps a physics that permits wearing without the wearing agent we would recognize. The result is organic in its smoothness, in the way a river stone is organic despite being stone. There are no sharp edges anywhere. Even the fossil-vegetation, examined closely, reveals rounded tips, melted-looking terminations, as if the darkness has rubbed against it for eternities.

Scale: small, bounded, a single feature-area. The bowl is enclosed. One could walk its rim in an hour, descend to its floor, cross to touch each fossil-plant, return to any starting point. Yet the smallness does not feel confining because the darkness and the emptiness expand the felt space in a direction orthogonal to physical measurement. It is cathedral-scale in the original sense—*kathedra*, a seat, a place where something sits and holds space open around itself. The bowl holds space open. The fossil-plants hold space open around themselves. The emptiness between them is not absence but *presence of room*, a positive quality, breathable.

No built structures. No tools. No made artifacts. The parameters insist on this so thoroughly that the concept of making seems inapplicable. Yet the tech parameter suggests primitive, early-tool-use, and this manifests as *incipient potential*—the bowl is a place where a hand *could* strike stone against stone, where the angle of certain salt-flakes suggests the premonition of edge, where the fossil-vegetation offers stems that *could* be grasped, *could* be broken, *could* serve. Nothing has done so. Nothing has needed to. But the affordance waits, a layer of possibility just below the actual, as if the place remembers that tools might exist without ever having suffered their existence.

Fauna: none. No animals. No insects threading the dark, no bacteria fermenting in hidden crevices—if there are crevices. The warm stillness is sterile in the most profound sense, not through hostility to life but through *indifference*, through a chemistry that offers no handhold to organic process. The place does not struggle against life; it simply occupies a adjacent possibility-space, a configuration of matter that never needed to include animation.

Weirdness: normal. This is the strangest parameter, that strangeness should read so low. Yet here, the absolute dark and the warmth and the salt and the fossil-plants and the silence and the changelessness—all of it composes into something that feels *inevitable*, *natural*, *right* once one has spent sufficient attention with it. The mind does not rebel. The uncanny dissolves into the simply *actual*. Of course there is a warm dark bowl of salt. Of course it contains mineral memories of ungrown plants. Of course nothing changes. What else would one expect?

Sound: silence or quiet sounds. The silence is so complete that it becomes a texture, a medium one moves through. Yet if one holds still enough—if one could hold still in a place where time does not pass—there might be something below threshold. The thermal expansion of salt? The settling of molecular arrangements? Or perhaps merely the sound of one's own blood, become so accustomed that it registers as external, as *place*. The quiet sounds, if they exist, are indistinguishable from the silence that contains them.

Dynamic: never changing, frozen equilibrium, time-invariant. This is not the stasis of death, not the frozenness of a stopped clock. It is rather that the place has found its configuration and *is* it, completely, without remainder, without the tension that generates change. A wheel spinning so perfectly balanced that it experiences no wear. A note held so precisely on pitch that no beat frequency arises to mark time's passage. The fossil-plants will not crumble. The rim will not erode further. The temperature will not rise or fall. The darkness will not lift because it was never a condition waiting for light.

---

I imagine—because imagination is what remains, in the parameters' gaps—I imagine arriving here not by travel but by *attention*, by the settling of focus into the configuration that this place requires. It does not exist elsewhere; it is not part of a larger geography. Yet in the spectrum of possible arrangements of matter and energy, it occupies a definite point. Warm. Dark. Small. Empty. Stable. Old in its settled-form, yet not aged, because aging requires time's arrow and time's arrow requires change and change requires disequilibrium and equilibrium here is perfect.

The fossil-vegetation, examined longest, reveals the most. They are not identical, these dozen forms. Some branch dichotomously like ancient *Rhynia*, some carry bulbous swellings like orchids translated to stone, one near the eastern rim (if directions obtained) rises in a single spiral like a shell or a fern-fiddlehead before unfurling. They suggest, these differences, a *potential for variation* that was exercised once, in some adjacent possibility, and then fixed—photographed, almost, in mineral. They are not dead because they were never alive. They are *about* life the way a photograph is about its subject, or the way a shadow is about the object that casts it. They testify to a nearby reality where vegetation flourished, where water pooled, where temperature fluctuated with season and day. But that nearby reality has no access here. The bowl is sealed, not physically but *ontologically*—a pocket of being where the conditions that would permit life to emerge were never assembled, yet where matter found a way to record, to commemorate, to hold the *form* of life without its function.

And in this holding, something like comfort. Something like a rest. The place does not demand. It does not present opportunities for action, for choice, for the dynamic tension of intention meeting resistance. It simply *is*, in its warm dark completeness, and in that completeness it offers—if offers is not too active a verb—a kind of resolution. Not peace, exactly. Peace implies the cessation of conflict, and there has never been conflict here. More: the pre-condition of peace, the ground from which peace might be inferred if there were anything to infer, anyone to infer it.

I have walked this bowl, in the way one walks in dreams, in descriptions, in sustained attention. I have touched the salt, felt its strange warmth, traced the rounded edges of the fossil-plants. I have stood at the center and turned slowly, finding in each direction the same soft rim, the same density of absence, the same held darkness. I have listened to the silence until it became indistinguishable from hearing itself.

The place does not change. I do not change in it. There is no I, finally—only the configuration, the warm salt bowl, the sparse mineral memories of greenness, the equilibrium that requires no maintenance because it has never been disturbed.

This is the Hollow of the Sleeping Mineral. It is what it is. It has been itself long enough that questions of duration become as irrelevant as questions of water. And in its being-itself so completely, it generates a strange quality—not quite beauty, not quite meaning, but something that neighbors both: *sufficiency*, the complete adequacy of a thing to its own nature, requiring nothing, offering nothing, existing in the perfect economy of the self-identical.

If you find it, you will not have traveled. If you leave, you will not have moved. It is not elsewhere. It is *configured*, and configurations are available anywhere attention settles deeply enough, widely enough, with sufficient patience and sufficient release of the demand that things become other than they are.

This is the landscape. This is what the parameters generate, given room to become themselves.
