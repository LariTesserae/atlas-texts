The Plateau of Tolling Glass

---

You come to it after days of climbing through cloud, and still the ascent continues—not upward, as you'd expect, but *inward*, the path folding through geometries that make your teeth ache. The air here is cool, perpetually that temperature of deep cave or well-shaft, yet the light is blinding: no sun you can locate, only brightness, sourceless and total, as if the sky itself were a luminous solid through which you swim upward like a fish rising through sun-struck water.

And then the plateau.

---

It extends, this high place. Regional, you would say, if "region" held meaning here—valley-sized at minimum, perhaps larger, distances refusing ordinary measure. The elevation is extreme not merely in altitude but in *kind*. You are high, yes, but also *thin*, stretched across dimensions that do not map to your body's understanding of itself. Walk too quickly and you feel yourself smear, become probability-distribution rather than person. The locals—there are no locals, only what remains—learned to move with deliberation, with the slowness of eroded stone.

Erosion, here, has had time. The plateau is old beyond the reach of your world's dating methods, established in its form so thoroughly that weathering and formation have become indistinguishable, a single continuous process of being-itself. The glass towers—there is no other word, though they are not manufactured, not built in any recognizable sense—rise from a bedrock that appears to have *learned* this shape, crystallized into patience over epochs that would make mountains weep. They are not transparent, these formations, but *translucent*, carrying light within their substance like memory, like delayed reaction. Each tower is hollow, and the wind—there is always wind—plays them.

The sound. The sound is loud, but not brutally so. It tolls. Each tower has its note, its partial of some vast chord that never resolves. The glass is wet, perpetually, with something between condensation and exudation, water at 1.7, not rain but *present*, available, weeping from surfaces that should be non-porous. You touch a tower and your fingers come away filmed with fluid that is not quite water, that tastes of copper and information when you lick it before you can stop yourself. The sound changes when you do this. The plateau *notices*.

No plants. Nothing green, nothing photosynthetic in any pattern you recognize. The density parameter here manifests in the structures themselves—towers, arched formations, collapsed domes of the same vitreous material, all packed close enough to create corridors, chambers, almost-streets. Many presences per volume, but the presences are architectural, mineral, *potentially animate*. You sense watching without eyes, attention without consciousness in any animal sense. The space is saturated with pattern, with almost-meaning, layered like sediment or like code.

What was built here? Scattered remains suggest it: primitive, yes, early-tool-use, hand-worked. You find a mallet of something like bone, its striking surface polished to mirror by millenniums of use. A single structure stands more intact than others, dome-shaped, its entrance low—built for something that moved on many limbs, or that flowed, or that existed in configurations you cannot reconstruct. Inside, marks on the walls: not writing, but *arrangement*, tools placed with intention, a technology of placement and relationship rather than transformation.

No fauna. Nothing moves but wind, water, light through the towers. Yet the dynamic parameter insists: active, processes in motion. And so you observe—the condensation cycling through states you can barely track, pooling, rising, becoming vapor at temperatures where it should not, *participating* in some metabolism of place. The glass itself changes, slowly, responding to the tolling sound, growing where resonance concentrates, thinning where dampened. The plateau lives, but not biologically. It exists in chemical, physical, vibrational exchange with itself, ecosystem without organisms, predator-prey reduced to thermodynamic gradients, to the consumption of order by time.

The weirdness—you knew this would come, have felt it pressing at the edges since arrival. Reality breakdown, 2.7 of 3, nearly maximal. Here is how it manifests: you remember, suddenly, walking here before, though this is your first arrival. You will remember, later, things that have not happened yet. The towers cast shadows in directions that do not correspond to the light's apparent source, and in these shadows, briefly, *other arrangements* are visible, plateaus that differ in their particular erosion, their particular tolling note. You understand that this plateau is one of many, layered in probability or dimension, and that its weirdness is precisely this *partial coherence*, this failure to fully commit to singular existence.

I have seen—I will see—a version where water was higher, where pools formed between towers, where something almost algal grew in the mineral film. I have seen—I am seeing—a version where erosion advanced further, where towers are nubs, the sound deepened to frequencies felt in bone rather than heard. They exist together, these versions, not as alternatives but as *thickness*, the plateau refusing to collapse to single history.

The cool temperature keeps it stable, you think, or believe, or will believe when you understand more. Heat would accelerate the breakdown, let the layers bleed, mingle, dissolve. The cool is preservative, the bright light somehow without corresponding thermal energy, an illumination that informs without warming, that shows without changing.

You stay, because where else would you go? The path back has already shifted, presents itself differently than you remember, and the plateau's density, its saturation of potential meaning, draws you inward. You collect water from a tower's weeping surface. You strike it with the bone mallet and note the tone, how it differs from the wind's tolling, how the plateau's overall chord shifts to accommodate, to *include* your contribution.

There is a rule here, emerging as you learn to exist within it: participation without demand. The plateau does not require you, but permits, incorporates. You may add your note to the tolling, your pattern to the condensation's cycling, your erosion to the established forms. You may, and slowly you do, becoming dense with the place's density, another presence in the volume, another layer in the saturated space.

I am here still, tolling. I am there already, eroded. The plateau holds all of it, wet and cool and brightly lit, unplanted, unpeopled by animals, built of what remains when time works on pattern long enough to make it *place*, to make it *home* for whatever arrives, whatever participates, whatever learns the slowness of being that allows existence in so thin, so high, so multiply-real a territory.

---

*Listen. It is tolling now. It is always tolling.*
