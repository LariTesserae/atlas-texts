The Drowning Plain

---

You come to it at night, or what passes for night here—twilight thickened to velvet, a darkness that breathes rather than falls. The sky holds no stars you can see, only a faint phosphorescence somewhere above the perpetual cloud-deck, enough to turn black into deep bruise-purple, to let you know where horizon ends and overhead begins. By this light the plain extends, and extends, and extends.

It is low. Lower than low. The earth here has forgotten elevation the way a sleeper forgets a dream upon waking—not rejected, simply no longer resident in memory. The soil compacts underfoot with a wet *chuck* that speaks of water held prisoner beneath, water that has nowhere else to go, no gradient to follow, no urgency of descent. It seeps instead of flows. It *lingers*. Pockets of it glimmer in the darkness—shallow lenses, sudden pools no wider than a spread hand, channels that meander without purpose and end in marshes that end in more channels, the whole plain a manuscript written in water and then partially erased, written again, erased again, until the text is all palimpsest and no present story.

Yet lush. The lushness surprises, if you carry assumptions from other places. This is not the lush of rainforests, not vertical profusion, not canopy and competition for light. Here the vegetation grows *outward*, horizontal as the plain itself, spreading in mats and networks and colonies that merge at their edges until individual plants become theoretical, a conceit of botanists, not a reality of the field. Grasses with blades broad as palms, their edges faintly luminescent with some bacterial partnership. Sedges in hummocks that rise knee-high, hummocks upon hummocks, the hummocks themselves grown over and indistinguishable from the living carpet. Vines that do not climb but *creep*, sending runners across the surface, rooting at every node, so that to walk here is to walk on a single organism contemplating itself in vegetative time.

Hot. The heat comes from below as much as from any absent sun. The plain sits atop something—warm springs, deep magmatic breath, the slow combustion of peat that has smoldered for millennia. The air holds it, thick as broth, and the darkness makes it stranger, disembodied, a warmth without source you can locate. You sweat continuously and the sweat does not cool you; it joins the ambient moisture, becomes indistinguishable from the condensation that beads on every surface, that drips from leaf edges in slow percussion, that gathers on your skin until you feel yourself becoming part of the plain's hydraulic accounting.

The established quality of the place announces itself in stillnesses, in the way things have settled into relation. The hummocks are not random; they follow patterns too large to perceive from any single vantage, arrays that speak of centuries of slight preference—water slightly more here, slightly less there, a plant slightly more successful, its hummock slightly higher, capturing slightly more, becoming more successful still. The feedback loops have looped so long they've become geology. The plain has worn its own shape into permanence, not the permanence of stone but the permanence of process so continuous it might as well be stone, a river that has flowed so long the concept of riverbed becomes meaningless, the flowing *is* the bed, the bed *is* the flowing.

And packed. Saturated with presence, with biomass, with the sheer *thereness* of living and once-living and about-to-be-living matter. Every cubic meter holds its inventory: the visible plants, the root networks interlacing beneath, the fungi threading between roots in exchanges older than vocabulary, the invertebrates in their countless numbers, the bacteria on the bacteria. You could not insert a hand anywhere and touch only one thing. You would touch ten things, twenty, a hundred, the distinction between individuals a violence done to the reality of entanglement. The plain is fully occupied. It has no vacancies. It has never had vacancies. Something has always been ready to move in, to fill, to complete the occupation.

No buildings. No artifacts. No struck flint, no charcoal layer, no foundation stone. The plain has never been built upon because it cannot be built upon—the occupation is already complete, and human construction is just another vacancy-seeking that finds no purchase. What would you build with? The vegetation rots before it dries, too full of water, too full of life that continues living even in death, consuming the dead from within. What would you build on? The ground liquefies under pressure, remembers its fluid nature, claims any post or pillar for the general seep. The plain refuses architecture the way a saturated solution refuses more salt, not with violence but with simple inability to incorporate, a chemical impossibility made landscape.

Yet industrial. The word seems wrong until you look longer, until your eyes finish adjusting to the darkness and begin to see what moves. The plain is factory. The plain is engine. The metabolism of all that packed life, all that lushness, all that water cycling through—this is industry at scale beyond any human attempt. The anaerobic digesters underground, the methanogens in their billions, the slow fermentation of undrained places, the nitrogen fixation in root nodules like tiny workshops, the phosphorus scavenging, the iron oxidizing, the sulfur reducing—each hummock a facility, each marsh a processing plant, the whole plain a single distributed refinery converting sunlight (what little reaches through the cloud and the dark) and geothermal whisper into biomass, into biomass, into ever more biomass, the production so scaled, so continuous, so electrified with the small electrochemistries of cellular process that the comparison to human industry becomes not metaphor but simple description of analogous function. The plain manufactures itself. The plain is the product. The plain consumes its own waste and manufactures again.

Mostly quiet. The darkness helps with this, the way darkness always muffles, always suggests stillness even when stillness isn't complete. What sound exists is low frequency, long duration, the kind that enters through the body before the ear. The *chuck* of your foot in wet soil. The slow *tick* of condensation reaching some critical drop size and releasing. The vegetative sounds—growth itself, if you could hear it, the cell walls extending, the turgor pressure adjusting—but you cannot, and what you almost hear is the memory of sound, the expectation of it, the plain's quiet so complete it becomes itself a kind of presence, a sound of absence, a mostly-quiet that is not silence but the averaging of countless small noises below threshold, the thermal motion of air molecules, the Brownian whisper of pollen in water films.

And slow. The plain changes on timescales that require patience beyond human capacity, or perhaps simply beyond human interest. The hummocks grow, imperceptibly, over decades. The channels migrate, but a degree of arc per century. The species composition shifts, but the shifts are successional, geological, the kind that paleoecologists reconstruct from cores, not that living observers document. What dynamics exist are weak gradients—a slightly wetter century, a slightly warmer millennium—and the plain's response is not event but adjustment, not catastrophe but accommodation, the slow finding of new equilibrium, the patient process of becoming slightly different while remaining entirely itself.

The fauna overwhelms. This is the parameter that seems most contradictory, in darkness and quiet and slow change, but the plain is *full* of animals in ways that only reveal themselves in time, in attention, in the adjustment of expectation. The invertebrates first, always, the substrate of animal presence: nematodes in every gram of soil, millions, their ecological function so fundamental that without them the plain would cease to be the plain within seasons. The mites, the springtails, the larvae in the water films, the mosquito that finds you—if you are there to be found—with sensors evolved for exactly this darkness, this humidity, this heat. Then larger, but not large: the frogs that chorus in pulses too low for your ears, their calls felt in the sternum rather than heard. The snakes that move through the vegetation with the same horizontal purpose as the plants themselves, another kind of creeping, another kind of occupation. The wading birds that pass through on migrations you cannot track, that feed and rest and depart, their presence overwhelming in numbers if you are there in the right week, the right hour, their absence overwhelming in the duration between. The mammals, finally, if you wait long enough, if you become still enough to become part of the plain's furniture: the rodents in their hummock colonies, the ungulates at the plain's edges where it meets something else, the predators that follow them, that follow everything, that are themselves followed by parasites in numbers that make the predators seem sparse.

This is the overwhelming—not the dramatic, not the charismatic, but the cumulative, the total, the fauna so present in its totality that individual animals become as theoretical as individual plants, the category itself becoming the real presence, *animalness* saturating the plain the way water saturates it, the way vegetation packs it, the way heat and darkness and slowness define it.

Normal. The plain is not weird. This cannot be emphasized enough, cannot be understood without visitation. It is strange, certainly, to human expectation, but its strangeness is the strangeness of any ecology fully itself, any place that has not been reduced to human convenience. The plain follows rules discoverable, comprehensible, consistent. The water moves according to gradient and resistance. The vegetation grows according to light and nutrient and the competitive hierarchies of coevolution. The animals eat and are eaten according to trophic relationships stable enough to be modeled. The heat comes from sources physical and locatable. The darkness is atmospheric, not supernatural. The industry is metabolic, not magical. The slowness is temporal scale, not temporal anomaly. You could, with sufficient patience and methodology, come to know this place completely. The weirdness parameter fails here not because the plain is boring but because it is *coherent*, because its coherence is so complete that human categories of strangeness dissolve in contact with it, because it is simply itself, fully itself, nothing else, nothing more.

What emerges, finally, is the sense of a place that has achieved something like peace—not the peace of stasis, but the peace of full occupation, of all vacancies filled, of all gradients weak enough to be managed, of industry so scaled and distributed that it becomes indistinguishable from nature, of darkness and heat and moisture and life so thoroughly interpenetrated that to change any element would be to change all, and the plain has existed too long, has established itself too completely, to permit such change from any source less than catastrophic, less than external, less than utterly foreign to its own patient, packed, quiet, slow, normal, overwhelming self.

You do not stay. The plain does not invite staying. It is not hostile; it is simply complete. There is no role for you in its occupation. Your vacancy, if you tried to fill it, would be temporary, would be corrected, would be processed and incorporated and made into more plain, more lushness, more biomass, more quiet industry of life being life in darkness and heat and the long slow now of established, packed, water-saturated existence.

You leave at what might be dawn, the bruise-purple lightening slightly, the phosphorescence above finding some new source or angle, the plain revealing itself unchanged, extending still, patient for your departure, patient for your return, patient for the patience it knows you do not possess.

The *chuck* of your foot in wet soil, one last time. Then the distance growing between you and the plain's complete, normal, overwhelming self.

Behind you, the condensation drips. The hummocks metabolize. The fauna overwhelms, quietly, in darkness, in heat, in the slow industrial manufacture of more plain, more plain, ever more itself, ever more complete.
