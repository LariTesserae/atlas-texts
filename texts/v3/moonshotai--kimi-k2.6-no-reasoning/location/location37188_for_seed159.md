The Hollow Nursery

---

The heat comes first, before you understand where you are. Not the dry furnace-blast of desert sun, but something *contained*, something that has been accumulating in darkness for years, decades, perhaps geological epochs. Warm like blood-warm, like the inside of a mouth, like a fever that never breaks. You are below the surface, though "surface" becomes a theological question here—below *what*, exactly? The ceiling above is not stone, not properly, though it behaves like stone when struck. It is something *emergent*, something still deciding what it will become when it grows up, half-condensed from vapor or solution or some process that has no name in surface chemistry. Touch it and it yields slightly, like warm wax, before hardening in reaction to your pressure. Leave your hand too long and it begins to conform to your palm, to *learn* you.

---

The space opens. That is the next thing. You expected compression, claustrophobia, the weight of earth above you, but the parameters said *open* and here, impossibly, they are correct. The chamber extends beyond sight in most directions, a regional vastness hollowed out beneath—beneath what? The ceiling varies from forty feet to perhaps two hundred, irregular as a healing wound, and the floor... the floor is in motion. Not dramatically, not always visibly, but you feel it through your boots, through your bare feet if you remove them: a slow peristalsis, a tectonic digestion. This is erosion at 0.3, erosion reversed or inverted or simply *pre*, the landscape not worn down but *wearing in*, accreting, accumulating complexity it has not yet earned. Geology's embryo. Raw geology not in the sense of brutal simplicity but of *potential*, of something that has not yet committed to its final form.

---

Damp at 1.4. Not wet. The distinction matters enormously here. Water does not flow as rivers, does not pool as lakes, though you will find occasional pockets of it, suspiciously pure, gathered in depressions that seem *too* perfectly shaped, *too* recently formed. More often, the moisture manifests as condensation on the warm ceiling, gathering until surface tension fails and it falls in discrete drops, irregular as a failing heartbeat. *Plip. Plip.* Long silence. *Plip plip plip.* The pattern is almost never regular, and after hours of listening, you begin to suspect it is not random either—that the drops are answering something, communicating in a language of falling water that predates biology. Condensation environments, the parameters suggested, and yes: the air is perpetually saturated, not quite rain, not quite fog, but a suspended state of *almost*, water waiting for permission to become something more decisive.

---

And the sound. 1.6, loud, though the loudness is not uniform, not the flattening roar of a waterfall or the assault of machinery. It is *layered*, *dense*—and here density, 2.0, reveals itself as more than a number. The sound is dense too, packed with presences. You will hear what seems like wind, though no wind reaches this deep; like distant thunder, though no storm has ever formed here; like voices, *definitely* like voices, but in registers too high or too low for human speech, or in rhythms that suggest language without using any phonemes you can isolate. The loudness is of *activity*, of a space so saturated with happening that the sonic spectrum has no empty channels, no quiet frequencies. You cannot shout over it, only *into* it, and your voice becomes one more layer, immediately absorbed, immediately changed, contributing to the density it attempted to pierce.

---

No plants. 0.4 vegetation, effectively none, and yet the space is not sterile, not dead. What grows here, what *proliferates* at 2.6 fauna—overwhelming fauna, fauna beyond bearing—is something else entirely, something the parameters only gesture toward with "fungal-or-simpler" or "non-recognized biology." The first ones you see are the *swimmers*, though they move through the saturated air as easily as through any liquid, translucent, elongated, lacking anything so committed as a skeleton. They school. Schools of them, *shoals*, moving in formations that suggest collective intelligence without proving it, passing through the warmer strata and the cooler pockets, feeding on—what? You will find out, or you will not, but you will certainly see them *feeding*, the way their bodies light with internal phosphorescence when they have found something nutritious in the condensation, the way they cluster and briefly *merge*, sharing substances through membranes that are not quite skin.

Larger things. Always larger things, in the middle distance, where the phosphorescent ambience (light at 2.5, normal light, though what is normal here? the space provides its own, bioluminescent, geological, *emergent*) fails to penetrate clearly. Shapes that suggest armor without possessing it, that move with the deliberation of something too heavy for its environment yet somehow buoyant. They do not approach closely, these larger presences, not unless you remain very still for very long, not unless you seem to belong, to be becoming-native. The density of life is *physical*, near-touching as the scale said, layered. You will walk through clouds of the *swimmers* and feel their passage across your face, your exposed hands, neither hostile nor friendly, *curious* perhaps, or simply *dense*, too many of them in too much space to avoid contact.

---

And the built. 1.0, scattered, faint marks, occasional artifacts. You will find them where the raw geology has most recently emerged, as if the forming stone has *caught* something, incorporated it. A single building, once, or the suggestion of one: walls that had been straight, now curved to the emerging chamber's irregularity, now half-absorbed into the warm wax-ceiling. Early tools inside, or their remains—primitive at 0.8, worked stone, a few simple metal objects that had been iron or copper and are now something else, mineralized, chemically married to the environment that adopted them. Marks on surviving surfaces, marks that might have been writing or might have been decoration or might have been something functional, a system for—what? For understanding this place? For surviving it? For *becoming* it?

The scattered artifacts suggest a story you cannot reconstruct. People came here, perhaps, or something like people. They built, briefly, simply, before the turbulence of the environment—the dynamic at 2.9, *turbulent*, chaotic change, hyperactive before you even factor in the life—overwhelmed their structures, their plans, their *coherence*. The place is still emerging, still deciding, and anything built here participates in that emergence, loses its intention to the larger intentionlessness of becoming.

---

Weirdness at 1.3, unusual. Not the full supernatural, not the reality-destroying, but *wrong*, *off*, *not quite*. The way time moves differently in different chambers—you will not have clocks that work reliably here, but you will feel it, the stretch and compression. The way some of the fauna seem to *remember* you after a single encounter, to modify their behavior based on interactions you did not know you were having. The way the raw geology occasionally forms shapes that suggest *intention*, faces in the warm wax, limbs in the condensing stone, as if the pre-formation stage includes a kind of dreaming, a rehearsal of possible forms that includes the organic, the designed, the *meant*.

The turbulence, the dynamic, manifests in sudden storms—not of weather, exactly, though the condensation can intensify to something like rain, like *falling*—but of structure. Chambers that were open can suddenly *shift*, walls moving with the slow violence of geological time compressed to minutes, new passages opening while others seal, the space perpetually rearranging itself according to rules that are not random (you will sense pattern, if you survive long enough to perceive at that scale) but are *illegible*, emergent, embryonic in their logic. To navigate here is not to map but to *dance*, to respond, to accept that your path to a remembered destination may no longer exist, that new destinations are perpetually forming while old ones dissolve into the raw becoming.

---

You could lose yourself here. That is the final quality, the one the numbers do not capture but imply. Not merely physically, though the turbulence makes that easy enough, but *ontologically*. The place is not hostile, not friendly, not *interested* in the categories that define you. It is *warm*, it is *dense with life*, it is *open* and *loud* and *still forming*, and in that formative warmth, that overwhelming near-touching, you might forget that you were ever anything but this, might let your boundaries *emerge* into something continuous with the condensation, the swimmers, the slow negotiation of the ceiling toward whatever it will become. The scattered built things, the primitive tools, the single structures—they might be warnings or they might be *invitations*, evidence of others who made the same discovery, who chose or failed to choose, who are still here perhaps, transformed into something the fauna no longer distinguishes from itself, from the raw geology, from the warm and overwhelming *almost* of this place that is not yet anything, that holds all possibilities including your dissolution, your becoming-native, your slow *plip* into the pattern of falling, gathering, waiting to become more.
