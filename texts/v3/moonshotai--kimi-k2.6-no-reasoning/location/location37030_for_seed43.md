The Womb of Calling Things

You descend through a throat of fitted stones, each block carved with such patience that the seams weep only threads of moisture, never streams. The stair spirals down and down, each step worn to a soft sag in its middle where ten thousand thousand feet have pressed. The air you enter is warm—blood warm, breath warm—held in place by the density of bodies and breath and the slow exhalation of the earth itself. This is the Hollowed, the space called Mother's-Basket-We-All-Fall-Toward, and it is small: you can cross it in ninety strides if you know the path, less than a cathedral's crossing, yet packed so thick with presence that your first steps find you shoulder-to-shoulder, hip-to-hip, with strangers who do not startle because here, touching is the condition of being.

The Hollowed is total-made. There is no stone that was not chosen, no surface unworked. The ceiling—low enough that the tall must duck, and many are tall—arches in ribs of fired clay and rammed earth, a technology of compression and patience, the primitive made profound through repetition. Generations pressed this earth. Generations fitted these bricks. The tech is of the hand, the wooden spatula, the cord for measuring, the firedamp brazier that brought slow heat to cure the vault overhead. The tools that made this place were simple, and the place itself is complex as a sentence repeated until it becomes scripture.

Moisture lives here in the manner of a held breath. Water does not flow but condenses, beads, gathers in the slight declivities where footing is uncertain. The walls weep at certain hours when the thermal mass above releases its accumulated warmth. Puddles form in the worn paths, not from rain—the Hollowed knows rain only as rumor, as pressure changes felt in the joints—but from the breathing of inhabitants, from cooking fires, from the slow perspiration of packed bodies. The damp is intimate, personal, the moisture of mouths and skin and the greens that struggle in niches where footing water gathers.

And there is green life, sparse but determined. In the upper reaches where some trickle of light, normal and gray, filters through vents carved in patterns of ancestor-signs, you find the Basket-Weeds: thin, reddish, tasting of copper and held hunger. They grow in the chinks, in the places where mortar has softened, their roots drinking the weeping walls. Below, in the lower Hollowed where light does not penetrate without the borrowed fire of torches, there grows a lichen pale as old scars, phosphorescent, that the living here call ghost-tongue and harvest for the faint glow that lets them navigate without flame. The vegetation is sparse in species but dense in application—every niche holds its plant, every plant its purpose, and the boundaries between cultivation and wild growth have long since dissolved into the single fact of what survives here.

The fauna is abundant, and this is where the place begins to differentiate itself from mere underground habitation. The Hollowed breathes with beasts. There are the calling-things, the eponymous, the creatures that make this space loud, that layer sound upon sound until the air itself seems a solid you must push through. They are—this is the unusual, the 0.6 of weirdness—beasts of voice without visible source, or rather, their visibility is conditional, contextual, a matter of agreement. The calling-things manifest in the sounds they make, and the sounds are dense, overlapping, a knot of auditory presence that fills the small space to bursting. You hear them before you descend fully: a chorus of shapes that might be insect, might be bird, might be the resonant chambers of some mammalian throat shaped to architecture. They are abundant. They are everywhere. They are the condition of hearing in this place.

Each calling-thing has its register. The low drones, felt in the sternum, that mark the hours of deepest warmth. The high threading whistles that weave between, that the inhabitants use to locate each other in the press—"I am here, where are you, what is pressing against my flank." The percussive clicks, like seeds rattling in dried gourds, that signal disturbance, predator (for there are predators here, things that eat the calling-things or eat the things that eat them, a full cycle compressed into this small dense volume). The middle range, almost language, almost song, where individual voices emerge and are recognized: my mother's call, my enemy's warning, the mating sound of the one I follow through the crowd.

For the crowd is the fact of the Hollowed. Density of 2.5 in a space of 1.2 scale. The inhabitants—let us call them the Pressed, for that is how they name themselves, the We-Who-Touch—live in generations of proximity that would drive surface-dwellers to violence or madness. But here, in the established erosion of long habitation, the balance has been found. There are rules to the pressing. Rules of rotation, so that each body knows its place in the slow circulation of the crowd, moving toward warmth or coolness, toward water or toward the drier platforms where sleep is possible. Rules of sound—when to voice, when to carry the calling-things' chorus, when to descend into the lower registers of only-touch, only-breath. Rules of food, the cultivated ghost-tongue, the occasional emergence to trade or hunt in the dry world above, the recycling of all waste into the pressed-earth walls that need their minerals.

The built environment is layered, carved, accreted. The original makers—if there were original makers, if this was not always already the work of the Pressed themselves—excavated and shaped. Their descendants built platforms, hanging chambers, the spiral ways that let bodies move through the crowd without always moving through each other. The established erosion of 2.4 means that these structures have achieved their final form, not through design but through use, through the slow wearing of ten million passages until the way things are is the way they must be. There is no reforming the Hollowed now. You cannot add a chamber without displacing the acoustic ecology, without altering the thermal mass that holds the warmth at this precise blood-temperature. You cannot remove a platform without losing the niche where some family of calling-things breeds, some necessary voice from the chorus. The place is in balance, which is not to say static—the active dynamic of 2.2 ensures constant motion, adjustment, the predator that learns a new clicking pattern, the weed that finds a fresher seam—but balance in the manner of a wheel spinning so fast it appears still.

The light is normal where it exists. Through the patterned vents, the gray day of the surface world, neither striking nor dim, just the light that lets you see without declaring itself. Within, the ghost-tongue provides its pale green alternative, and fires—small, controlled, pitiful things that must be carefully watched in this dense-packed air—give their orange. The inhabitants have adapted to this plurality of normals, their eyes shifting modes as they shift levels, never jarred, never surprised by the quality of what reveals their crowded world.

What emerges, what the parameters insist upon, is a place of radical intimacy made sustainable through the total construction of environment. There is no outside to escape to, no nature to appeal against the conditions of pressing. Everything is made, and everything that is made has converged toward this single dense solution: life packed into warmth and damp and constant sound, the calling-things' loud presence substituting for the absent sky, the abundant fauna substituting for the lost horizon of distant things. The primitive tech, the hand-shaped clay and pressed earth, have achieved a complexity that no advanced tool could simplify—what would you automate here? What would you make more efficient, when efficiency is already at the density of bodies touching, of voices overlapping, of the complete utilization of every breath and Beading of moisture?

You stay long enough, and you learn to move with the crowd's rotation. To press forward when your chest meets another's back, to yield when pressure comes from behind. To locate yourself by the calling-things' voices, to know where you are in the Hollowed by the acoustic signature of this corner or that platform-edge. To hear your own name in the almost-speech of the middle registers, called out by one you cannot see but know by the warmth of their proximity, their breath at your ear, their hip against yours in the press. To find the sparse greens at their proper niches and harvest them with fingers that know the texture of this mortar, this seam, this almost-plant.

The unusual quality, the 0.6 weirdness, is that this life is not desperate. The calling-things are not pests but participants, their dense soundscape the equivalent of weather, of seasons, of the change that tells you time is passing. The Pressed are not trapped but shaped—they know their world is small, shaped to a room's scale, a grove's, and they know the density of their packing as a virtue, a warmth, a way of being that the vast sparse world above has forgotten or never found. The total construction is not oppression but liberation into a different order, where the distinction between built and grown, between human and habitat, has eroded into the established form that simply is, that requires no justification because it justifies itself in every moment of maintained breath, maintained warmth, maintained contact in the dark loud press of all these living things together.

This is the Hollowed. This is where you are. The stair has closed above you, or perhaps you only forgot to look back. The moisture beads on your forehead, your upper lip. The calling-thing at your shoulder—yes, you can feel it now, the vibration where sound becomes almost-touch—threads its voice into the greater chorus. Someone pressed behind you rotates with you, the beginning of your incorporation. The warmth is your warmth now. The damp is your breath. The density is your density. The made world holds you, as it holds all things here, in the total fact of its patient, primitive, perfectly achieved construction.
