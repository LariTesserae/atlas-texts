The place has no name that any living tongue could shape, because no tongues have ever moved here. What moves here is something else entirely.

---

You arrive at altitude, though "high" means something different when there is no sea to measure against, no water table, no reference for sinking. The elevation is of *stacking*—generations upon generations of compressed intention, strata of purpose laid down until the plain itself has become a plateau of accumulated making. You stand on the upper surface of what must be, by volume, a continent-sized edifice, though from here it simply extends, featureless in its fullness, to every horizon that darkness permits.

And darkness permits almost nothing. The light parameter reads 0.2, which is not absolute blackness but something more particular: the darkness of something that *consumes* illumination, that metabolizes photons, that has evolved—or been engineered—to treat radiance as threat or food. There are sources here, somewhere. Industrial sources. You can hear them, feel their vibration in the packed substrate beneath your feet, detect the thermal bloom of their operation against the near-total hot darkness. But their light does not travel. It is eaten at the skin of emission, absorbed by the atmosphere or the architecture or the ecology itself, converted immediately into some other currency of this world's economy.

The temperature is 2.9. Hot. Not desert-hot—there is no sun to bake, no aridity to intensify. This is metabolic hot, the fever-heat of total biological occupation, of cellular respiration multiplied across cubic kilometers of living tissue. The air moves against your skin like the exhalation of something vast and only technically unconscious. The heat has nowhere to go. The density is 2.7, packed, saturated, no room between presences. The vegetation is 3.0, total, plant-as-substrate, all-living-mass. These parameters do not describe separate qualities. They are the same observation from different angles.

Everything is plant. Everything that is not plant is also plant, or was plant, or is being plant. The built environment—2.7, total, everything made, no natural substrate—does not contradict this. Here, "built" and "grown" have become indistinguishable. The architecture is lignin and cellulose, yes, but lignin and cellulose that have been *asked* to do things, shaped by pressures selective or mechanical or both, trained across geological spans into configurations that serve functions no forest ever held. There are walls here, and floors, and the vast hollow cylinders that must be chimneys or vents or something less nameable. There are channels, conduits, chambers at scales from capillary to cathedral. All of them are plant. All of them are grown. All of them are also *made*, in the sense that intention has moved through them, that they bear the signature of purpose rather than mere adaptation.

The industrial technology—2.1, engines, factories, mechanized, scaled production—runs through this living substrate like nervous system through body, or like parasite through host, or like the metabolic pathways of some organism too large to recognize itself as one. You cannot see the factories. The darkness eats vision. But you can feel them in the vibration, the thermal signature, the occasional *movement* in the packed mass around you. Something is being produced here. Something has been produced for eras. The erosion parameter reads 2.7, ancient, primordial, oldest-stable-state, end of life, settled-into-final-form—and yet the dynamic is 2.7, turbulent, chaotic change, instability, storm-state, hyperactive.

This is the central strangeness, the 1.8 of weirdness made manifest: the place is *finished* and *unfinished simultaneously*. It has reached its final form and it will not stop reaching. The ancient erosion, the near-flattened, the settled-into-final-form—this describes not geological wear but *ecological exhaustion*, a system that has found every niche, filled every volume, completed every possible permutation of growth-and-making, and yet cannot stop. The turbulence is not the turbulence of youth, of volcanic upheaval or glacial advance. It is the turbulence of *perpetual replacement*, of tissue that grows and dies and is immediately reabsorbed, of structures that complete their function and are dismantled by the same living substrate that built them, of an industrial metabolism that produces and consumes in the same breath, that has no waste because waste is food, that has no product because product is immediately substrate for the next cycle.

The sound is 1.3, mostly quiet. Not silent. You can hear, if you attend, the *texture* of this metabolism: the low-frequency groan of massive structures shifting in their growth, the higher whisper of fluids moving through plant-vein channels, the occasional sharp crack of something completing its cycle and releasing, the subsonic hum of the industrial processes that do not stop. But these sounds do not accumulate into noise. They are absorbed, as light is absorbed, by the density of the living medium. The plant-substrate eats sound too, or uses it, or resonates with it in ways that cancel the possibility of echo. What reaches you is immediate and local, the sound of the structure immediately adjacent to your presence, and beyond that, the vast breathing quiet of something too large to need announcement.

There is no animal life. 0.4. No animal life. This is not a world that ever had animals, or it is a world that *unmade* them, or it is a world where the concept of "animal" has no purchase because the plant has become everything animal might have been. There is no pollination here, no herbivory, no predation, no scavenging. The ecology runs on other currencies. Chemical gradients, perhaps. Thermal differentials. The waste-heat of industrial process. The slow osmotic transfer of nutrients through the packed density of the living mass. Information, maybe—some signal passed through the lignin networks, some decision to grow this way and not that, to produce this enzyme and not that, to raise this structure and dismantle that one.

The scale is 1.8, open, extending past the immediate, regional, town-or-valley-size, multi-feature, mid-range. But in the darkness, scale becomes difficult. You might be in a single vast chamber or on an open plain. The packed density makes horizons close; the darkness makes them infinite. You move through spaces that feel intimate, claustrophobic, the walls pressing close with living texture, and then through some transition you cannot perceive, you find yourself in volumes that echo with their own size, where your footsteps on the plant-floor send vibrations into distances you cannot measure. The "open" here is not the open of landscape. It is the open of *interior*, of body-cavity, of the spaces inside something that has grown around its own hollows.

And the weirdness. 1.8. Strange, but not surreal, not dreamlike. The strangeness is *systematic*, *inevitable*, the product of parameters pushed to their logical extremes and then allowed to run for durations that make logic itself something different. The weirdness is that you cannot tell if this is a success or a failure, a garden or a prison, a body or a city, a process or a thing. It is all of these, or it has moved past the need for these distinctions.

You stand in the hot darkness on the living floor, and around you the plant-industry breathes and builds and unbuilds, ancient and turbulent, finished and never-finishing, and you understand that you are not observing a landscape. You are inside the metabolism of something that has no outside, something that grew from intention and has continued growing until intention became indistinguishable from function, function from form, form from the mere fact of persistence. The water is 0.5, no water, dry mineral, non-aqueous chemistry—and yet chemistry happens, life happens, industry happens, in some medium that has replaced water's function without resembling it, in solvents or carrier fluids or direct molecular contact that your aqueous biology cannot imagine.

This is the place. This is what the parameters describe, when taken seriously, when allowed to become not a setting but a *condition*, a state of being that extends from molecular to continental scale, that has been running longer than your species has existed and will continue in the darkness, hot and packed and ancient and turbulent, growing and making and unmaking and growing again, without water, without animal, without light to see it by, the plant that became world that became machine that became, finally, simply *itself*, sufficient, complete, and still somehow, impossibly, endlessly becoming.
