The City of Dried Lungs

---

You enter through a pore, or what must be a pore—a spiraling aperture in the earth's thickened skin, rimmed with crystallized exhalations that flake away like old varnish under your touch. The air you draw in is already wrong: hot, yes, but hot in the manner of a body that has not cooled since its fever began, generations or centuries ago. It carries no moisture, none, and your own lungs begin to protest within minutes, not from exertion but from the strange arithmetic of breathing something that wants to pull water from you, that seems to *count* your moisture and find you wanting.

The descent is gradual. You are low here, lower than the surrounding deadlands, and the city rises around you not as towers but as *convolutions*—folded tissue, chambered spaces, the kind of architecture that happens when something grows in darkness and heat without ever knowing the press of water or the logic of gravity's dominion.

They built with lungs. Not *from* lungs, not leather and bellows and the butcher's trade—though there were butchers once, you sense it in the tool-marks, the primitive scrapers and awls left discarded in corners where the light never justifies their presence. No, they built *with* the idea of lungs, with the architecture of breath itself. The walls breathe. This is not metaphor. The walls breathe, and the sound is loud, unbearably loud in the dimness, a constant *hush-roar-hush-roar* that you feel in your sternum before your ears properly parse it. It is the sound of a million million bellows, of sacks inflating and collapsing in endless sequence, though there is no life here, no fauna, nothing that moves with intention except the air itself, pressed through channels by some engine of heat and pressure that predates your understanding.

The vegetation—lush, they said, lush at 1.5—and yes, in this lung-city, in these moist darks where breath condenses on convoluted ceilings and drips, drips, drips onto surfaces that never see light, there is green. Fungal more than plant, or something older than the distinction, something that learned to build sugars from the chemistry of hot exhalation, from the minerals that powder every surface in fine cascades. It grows in the rhythm of the breathing walls, swelling with each inhalation, subsiding slightly with each outrush of hotter air. It is the color of old bruises, of infected wounds, and it is *lush*, it is *prolific*, it carpets every surface that stays still long enough to host it, and some surfaces that do not. You have seen it overgrowing the primitive tools, swallowing them, incorporating their crude metallurgy into some slow biological accounting.

The erosion here is established, settled, in balance with itself. The city has existed long enough to have worn its own edges smooth, not through water's patient work—there is no water, none, not a drop, not a memory of water in the mineral records—but through the slow attrition of moving air, of thermal expansion and contraction, of the vegetation's acid patience. Surfaces are rounded where they were once sharp. Pathways have formed not through design but through the accumulated passage of... not feet. The passage of something. The air itself, perhaps, wearing channels in its own persistence.

And the scale. The scale is the strangest thing, or one of them. You entered through a pore, and the city is pocket-sized, contained, a single feature in a vaster deadness—and yet within it, within this lung-folded space, there is room for districts, for neighborhoods, for the accumulated weight of urban life compressed into something the size of a single building in the world above. Corridors that branch and rebranch like bronchi, chambers that open into chambers, each one a room, a dwelling, a public space, each one dim and hot and loud with the breathing of walls. You could walk for hours and not see the same convoluted surface twice, and yet you would not be lost, exactly—the city is too contained for that, too much a single organ, too much *one thing*.

The light, what there is of it, comes from the vegetation itself, partly, a phosphorescent waste product of its strange metabolism, and partly from the heat, which here manifests in ways that skirt the edge of visibility, infrared made somehow tangible to eyes that should not see it. Everything glows slightly, everything in these bruised greens and fevered reds, and the shadows are not black but a deeper heat, a more absolute absence.

The dynamic is active, multiple processes in motion even in this stillness. The breathing of the walls is not regular, not a simple bellows-beat, but complex, polyphonic, different chambers out of phase with each other so that the sound becomes music of a sort, or the suggestion of music, or the memory of music in the body of something that never had ears. The vegetation pulses. The heat flows in currents you can feel on your skin, convection cells in the moving air. There is weather here, micro-weather, storms of temperature and pressure that sweep through the corridors and leave behind them changed atmospheres, altered chemistries, the faint sharp scent of ozone or its anaerobic equivalent.

And the weirdness, the 0.6 of unusual, sits in the fact that this all *works*, that this city, this lung, this breathing pocket in the dead dry earth, maintains itself without water, without animals, without any technology more advanced than those abandoned scrapers and awls. Something built it, or grew it, or *became* it, and that something is gone now, has been gone for longer than the erosion records, and yet the city continues, breathing, growing its bruised vegetation, maintaining its hot dim lung-life in the absence of everything that should make life possible.

You breathe shallowly here. Your body knows, even if your mind has not yet caught up, that this air is not for you, that you are trespassing in a system that processes, that respires, that lives by rules you cannot fully parse. The loudness is not just sound but *pressure*, the physical fact of air in motion, of chambers filling and emptying, of a world that inhales and exhales and has never once, in all its long existence, ever held its breath.

There is no water. There is no animal life. There is only this: the hot dark breathing, the lush growth on the walls, the primitive tools slowly being eaten by something that does not know their purpose, the city that is a lung that is a city, the pocket world that continues its active, dynamic, eroded existence in the hollow places beneath the dead surface, waiting, breathing, being itself with the patience of something that has never needed anything else.

You could stay. Part of you wants to, wants to find a still-warm chamber and let the breathing walls lull you into something like sleep, something like incorporation. You would not be the first. The tools suggest it, abandoned where they fell from hands that stayed too long, that breathed too deep, that became part of the lung's slow rhythm, that exhaled and never needed to inhale again, or inhaled and found the breath sufficient, found the hot dim loudness sufficient, found the lush bruised green sufficient, found the city sufficient, found—

But you are not yet part of this place. You are still separate, still carrying water in your tissues, still animal, still *visiting*. The pore awaits, spiraling upward, and the dead dry world above, and whatever distance you can put between yourself and the sound of breathing that is not yours, that will never be yours, that continues in the dark long after you have gone, long after everything has gone, the city of dried lungs breathing, breathing, breathing.
