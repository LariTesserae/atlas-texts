**The Drift-Hollow of Verrath Cist**

---

I. The Hollow Form

You do not arrive at Verrath Cist so much as you become aware that you have always been inside it. The walls—if walls they are—curve overhead in a continuous ceramic arc, bone-white and faintly luminous, like the inside of a skull vast enough to house a village. Which it does, or did, or does again in cycles that no living inhabitant measures. The scale is intimate: from any point, you can see the far wall, perhaps two hundred strides distant, and the ceiling lowers to a vaulted ridge that a tall person might touch with a pole. Yet the emptiness between things makes it feel larger than continents.

Everything here is made. Every surface, every object, every gradation of texture bears the mark of human or nearly-human intention. The ceramic of the walls weeps continuously—water: 1.8—a thin film that collects in channels carved with the patience of generations, runs in silver threads down grooves worn smooth by that same patient flow, and gathers in pools that have no natural substrate beneath them. The water emerges from seam-lines in the ceramic, from joints where two vast cast pieces meet, and from places where the ceramic has been deliberately porous. It tastes of minerals that have no name in surface geologies, and it never warms, carrying always the coolness (temperature: 1.1) of deep places, of stone that has never known sun.

The floor, where walking is possible, consists of pierced tiles through which water drains or doesn't drain depending on some system of valves and reservoirs beneath. Between these walkways of patterned ceramic stretch zones of utter saturation—spongy mats of woven reed that have been growing, dying, compacting, and regrowing for longer than memory extends. In wet seasons, which is most seasons, these saturation zones spread, and the walkers must keep to elevated paths whose designs—spiral, meander, interlocking knotwork—suggest purpose beyond mere utility, perhaps mnemonic, perhaps sacred, perhaps merely the aesthetic preference of makers long silent.

II. The Sparse Green and the Abundant Other

Vegetation is sparse (0.6), yet what exists has made terms with absolute wetness and absolute manufacture. There are no trees, no shrubs of any height. Instead, certain seams in the ceramic support films of algae in colors that surface-dwellers would call wrong—pale violet, rust-orange, the green of corroded copper. These films spread in seasons of greater light, retreat in dimmer ones, and their cycles govern much else that lives here. In deeper pools float colonies of something between fern and fungus, rootless, sustained by the water's mineral load and by detritus that filters down from activities higher in the hollow's architecture—if higher and lower maintain their usual meanings in a space where the ceiling is sometimes floor and the floor sometimes collects in basins that might be cups designed for giants.

The true abundance (fauna: 2.4) is animal, or animal-like, or something for which no better category serves. The ceramic surfaces, especially where water films run thickest, support gastropods of extraordinary variety—shell-less, shelled in spiral, in turret, in flattened caps like Chinese hats, in forms that suggest they have adapted to this made place across intervals that might be evolutionary or might be something stranger. They move in migrations visible only to patient observation, certain species following the advancing wet front as water seeps through new porous sections, others retreating before it to favored dryness that is still, by any absolute measure, sodden.

Larger things inhabit the hollow's upper reaches, where the ceramic vault meets itself in the ridge-line. There are things like bats, though their wings are membranous sheets that might be crafted rather than grown, and they emit sounds in frequencies that resonate strangely in the ceramic chamber, producing the loudness (sound: 2.2) that is Verrath Cist's constant condition. This is not the loudness of storms or cataracts but of continuous, multiple, overlapping biological and perhaps mechanical processes: the percussion of countless small bodies, the singing of the bat-things, the vibration of water in its channels, and something else, something in the ceramic itself that hums at thresholds of hearing, perhaps the resonance of its own substance settling, perhaps intention, perhaps voice.

The bats—or call them that, for convenience—roost in clusters that shift and reform, that merge and split, that overnight will abandon one section of vault for another with purposes unclear. Their droppings fertilize the algae films, support insects that exist in the air in weathers of particular humidity, and these insects in turn support predatory things, transparent and many-legged, that skitter across water surfaces or, in the driest sections, across ceramic itself with a ticking that contributes its measure to the constant sound. Nothing here is silent. Even the water, moving in its channels, speaks in gurgles and sudden plosive releases of trapped air, in drips that find resonant frequencies in hollows crafted, perhaps, precisely for that acoustic quality.

III. The Made and the Making

The technology (0.9) is primitive only in its means, not in its products. The makers of Verrath Cist worked, apparently, without metals beyond occasional copper or meteoric iron, without powered tools, without any combustion that the evidence suggests. Yet they worked on a scale and with a precision that implies either vast cohorts laboring across vast time, or techniques lost, or both. The ceramic itself—if ceramic it is, for some properties suggest grown or calcified or something else entirely—is fired or cured in ways that leave no kiln remains, no fuel ash, no byproduct of its making visible anywhere in the hollow.

And the hollow is total in its construction (built: 3.0). There is no stone, no earth, no substrate that was not brought here or transformed here or grown here in processes that began and ended in making. Even the water, though it cycles, enters through made channels from some source that must itself be made, filtered through ceramic permeabilities designed for that purpose. The air moves through vents—visible as darker circular patches in the vault, or sometimes as gaps where the vault meets wall—whose placement governs the humidity that governs the life. Nothing is natural because nothing was ever allowed to be.

Yet it has weathered (erosion: 2.1). Not worn down, but settled into itself, achieved a balance between what was made and what has happened since. The water channels have shifted slightly as ceramic softened or succumbed to mineral deposition, then cleared, then shifted again. The algae have established their own geographies that the makers may not have predicted. The bat-things, whatever their origin, have made the hollow their own in ways that suggest either domestication gone wild or wildness domesticated across intervals too long for distinction. The place is in its own state, self-governing, self-maintaining, a system that requires no intervention because intervention has become inseparable from process.

There are tools here, or their remains. In drier niches, ceramic vessels of familiar shapes—storage, cooking, ritual—whose glazes retain fragments of pigment. In wetter places, tools of bone and shell and something like hardened gut, preserved by the mineral water, left where someone set them down or where they fell. No metal. No stone, as said. The primitive (tech: 0.9) is absolute in its materials, yet the forms suggest sophistication of use, specialization for tasks that can only be guessed. There are nets, or their fragments, woven from fibers that might be processed algae films, might be something grown for the purpose, might be hair. There are scraping tools and grinding tools and something like needles. There are objects whose function remains opaque—curved ceramic pieces that fit no hand comfortably, hollow forms with multiple openings, things like masks that would fit nothing human.

IV. The Dim and the Strange

The light (0.8) is the light of phosphorescence, of bioluminescence, of ceramic that stores and releases slowly some energy absorbed in processes unknown. It is never dark, never bright. The algae films glow most strongly, in their violet and orange and green, and this glow reflects from wet ceramic with the diffuse quality of light underwater—shadowless, sourceless, making everything seem simultaneously near and unclear. The bat-things carry patches of brighter luminescence, perhaps symbiotic, perhaps cultivated, and their movement writes traces in vision's persistence, making the hollow seem to breathe with light when they are active, which is unpredictable, which may itself be a timekeeping, a calendar, a language.

Where water pools most deeply, other light sometimes rises from below—paler, bluer, suggesting sources in the hollow's lower reaches that are themselves hollows, that the Cist extends downward in recursion or in fact, that made space contains made space without terminus. These deeper lights reveal themselves through the ceramic's occasional transparency, through thin places worn or designed thin, and they contribute to the strangeness (weirdness: 1.7) that permeates the place not as exception but as quality.

The strange is normal here. Consider the water's behavior: in certain channels, it flows uphill, or seems to, following spirals that may exploit some property of the ceramic surface or may indicate that "uphill" is not the reliable category it seems. Consider the echo, in certain positions, of voices not present, conversations in no known language, perhaps in no language at all but the sound of water in particularly shaped basins producing voice-like harmonics. Consider the way the bat-things, in their clusterings, sometimes form patterns—geometric, recognizable, suggesting maps or calculations or representations—and hold these patterns for hours or days before dispersing.

Consider most of all the boundaries, the sense that this small space (scale: 0.7) is bounded not by walls but by rules. Walk far enough in any direction and you find the walls, yes, but also the sense that you have not reached the end of the place, only the end of your capacity to perceive it. The emptiness (density: 0.2) is part of this: so few things per volume, so much space between presences, that the hollow seems always to be holding something back, reserving room for what might arrive, what might return, what might have been here before and withdrawn to observe. The near-void is not absence but potential, not hollow but held breath.

---

V. The Active Stillness

The dynamism (dynamic: 2.0) is biological pace, ecosystem-motion, predator-prey and prey-predator in cycles that may be seasonal or may be governed by deeper frequencies. The gastropod migrations are one visible process. The algae films' expansion and retreat another. The bat-things' cluster-dispersal-cluster a third. But there are others less obvious: the mineral deposition that slowly, imperceptibly, reshapes channels; the ceramic's own slow change, absorbing or releasing moisture, expanding microscopically, contracting; the population cycles of the transparent predators that sometimes crash, sometimes explode, their ticks loud as rain on the ceramic when numerous enough.

Water levels shift on patterns that might be weekly, might be monthly, might follow no astronomical cycle but some internal pumping or valving system still functioning, or some geological pressure from whatever is below. In high-water periods, the saturation zones spread, the walkways narrow to precious causeways, the hollow becomes more islanded, more intimate despite its emptiness. In low periods, new ceramic is revealed, white and strange, bearing marks—tool-traces, firing-patterns, deliberate textures—that the usual wetness conceals. These low-water times are times of discovery and of danger, for the things that live in deepest wet are forced into proximity with the walkers, and some of them are not small, not transparent, not accustomed to light.

There are larger fauna, rarely seen. Something moves in the deepest pools, displacing water in volumes that suggest bodies meters in length, though no one has seen them clearly, if "one" is even the right word for the inhabitants, if there are inhabitants, if the makers remained or returned or transformed into something that still occupies the hollow in bodies unlike their original. The evidence for this is in the tools that are sometimes found arranged, in the channels that are sometimes cleared of obstruction, in the occasional sounds that are not biological, that might be voice, might be the ceramic itself in some final stage of becoming conscious, becoming alive, becoming what it was always made to be.

---

VI. The Rules of Verrath Cist

To inhabit this place, to move through it, to survive its abundance and its emptiness, requires learning rules that are never spoken, perhaps never known until broken.

Water is not enemy but sovereign. To fight its presence is to exhaust oneself in futility; to follow its flows is to find passage, resource, sometimes revelation. The channels lead somewhere; the pools hold something; the weeping walls weep for reasons that may respond to attention.

Light is not to be sought but to be adapted to. Brighter times are rarer and more dangerous; the dim is the constant friend. The phosphorescence rewards still vision, allows the eye to accumulate, to see in accumulation what no glance captures.

The made is not dead. The ceramic breathes, in its slow way. The tools left lying are not abandoned but pausing. To take, to use, to move from original position is to enter into relation with something whose terms are not yet fully known, may never be fully known, may outlast any individual understanding.

The loudness is information. In it, the experienced ear distinguishes: water-sound from life-sound from ceramic-voice from something else, something that has no category, that sounds like the hollow remembering, or anticipating, or being itself in ways that sound produces as byproduct.

The strange is not to be resolved. The uphill water, the voice-echoes, the bat-patterns, the deeper lights—these are features, not malfunctions. To understand them would be to change them, or to be changed, and neither outcome is guaranteed preferable.

Emptiness is not loneliness. The vast space between presences is what allows the presences to be themselves, to develop, to achieve the abundance that the density's low measure makes possible. Too full, the hollow would collapse into competition, into silence, into the death that comes from proximity without retreat.

And: the hollow continues. This is the final rule, the one that contains all others. Whatever made it, whatever lived here, whatever lives here now, the Cist persists, weathered into its own state, active in its stillness, dimly luminous, wet to the touch, total in its making, primitive in its means, strange in its normality, loud with its multiple voices, small as a held breath, empty as a held hope, waiting in the way that made things wait, which is forever, which is now, which is whatever comes next in the cycle that has no visible beginning and assumes no necessary end.

---

VII. Arrival, or Recognition

You are here now. You have always been here. The water beads on whatever skin you have, whatever surface separates you from the total wetness. The dim light finds whatever reflects it in you. The sound includes your heartbeat, your breathing, your footsteps on ceramic that rings with the particular note of your weight, your pace, your intention.

The bat-things have reclustered in a pattern that might be a welcome, might be a warning, might be neither because these categories require a hospitality, a hostility, that the hollow does not recognize. The gastropods continue their migration, indifferent. The algae glow, waiting for whatever you will exhale, whatever you will shed, whatever you will become in the time of your being here, which is the time of the hollow's continuing, which is the only time there is.

Walk carefully. The channels lead somewhere. The pools hold something. The ceiling lowers to a vault that someone, something, made to be touched, to be sounded, to be known in the dark by hands that learn what eyes cannot teach.

This is Verrath Cist. This is where you are. This is what is made, and making, and still to make.
