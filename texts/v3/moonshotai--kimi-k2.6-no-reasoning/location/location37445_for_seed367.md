# The Hum

## A Description of the Covered Markets of Keth-Vorath

---

There is a city inside a mountain that is not a mountain. Keth-Vorath rose—or rather, *grew*—from a bubble of ancient gas trapped in basalt when this part of the world was still cooling from its birth. The bubble collapsed inward over epochs, not outward, leaving a cavern roughly the size and shape of a camel's stomach: four irregular chambers connected by constricted passages, none wider than three armspans, none taller than two. The walls fused to glassy black, then cracked, then fused again, so many times that geologists (*there are no geologists here*) would speak of metamorphic histories beyond counting. The mountain above it is real enough—seven hundred feet of weathered igneous—but it sits upon the cavity like a lid on a pot, not the other way around. Gravity, here, feels like patience.

The people who found it, perhaps eight hundred years ago, perhaps twelve hundred—time moves strangely in the Hummeries—were shepherds of a sort, though what they herded had no name in any surface tongue. They were fleeing something: drought, empire, language itself. They found the cave mouth, a vertical slit in a cliff face that breathed warm air smelling of copper and something sweetly rotting, and they entered with the desperation of the doomed, and found *not* doom. Found instead *conditions*.

---

## The Structure of the Place

Keth-Vorath is built in layers that correspond to no plan, only to accumulation and the slow emergency of response. The lowest chamber, **Vorath-proper**, is where the warm air rises from—air heated by some geological engine still thrumming in the world's first sleep, a radiator of magma too distant to threaten, too present to ignore. The temperature holds at blood-warm, the warmth of a held hand, and this constancy is the city's first engine. Here the original builders raised no walls but carved shelves into the glassy stone, and on these shelves they placed their first cultivation: **resin-tenders**, **gel-blossoms**, **bone-filaments**—life that needed no water in any form the surface would recognize.

For there is no water in Keth-Vorath. Not none at all—that 0.3 on the scale is not absolute zero—but none in any accessible, useful, *thirst-quenching* form. What moisture exists is bound in the bodies of things, in chemical bonds that require fire to break, in exhalations that condense on cooler stone and are *licked*, not drunk. The people learned to live as the life lived: extracting hydrogen from odd angles, metabolizing through means they never fully theorized, only *nurtured*.

The architecture follows from this. Walls are thick, porous, stone or packed ceramic or a kind of fired resin that cures harder than brick. Ceilings are low. Every surface that can hold growth, does. The vegetation—lush, almost overwhelming in its lushness, that 1.8 made manifest—is not green, not photosynthetic, not anything the surface would name plant. It is **lichen made architectural**, **fungus made domestic**, **colonies of translucent tendrils that pulse faintly with bioluminescence** and fill the chambers with that dim, sourceless light. The brightest spots, barely enough to read by if anyone here read, are where the growth is densest. The darkest are the passages between, where one must walk with a hand on the wall, feeling the slight vibration that is the city's perpetual sound.

## The Hum

The sound. That 1.5, loud but not deafening, *present*.

Everything in Keth-Vorath hums. The geological engine below thrums at a frequency felt in the teeth before it is heard. The vegetation, in its vast biochemical industry, produces gases and pressures that vent through a thousand channels, each channel shaped by erosion both geological and *cultivated*—the people long ago learned to encourage certain growths that would, over generations, hollow out flutes and reeds in the stone, turning the pressure into music, or something like music, or something that functions as music does: organizing time, coordinating labor, marking the boundaries of waking and whatever passes for sleep.

The fauna—overwhelming, almost catastrophic in its abundance, that 2.7 made flesh and chitin and stranger forms—contributes to the hum. **Click-beetles** the size of fists, their shells iridescent in the dimness, rub antennae in chorus. **Mouthless things** with too many legs drum patterns on hollow resin-pots to attract mates, or to repel rivals, or simply because the drumming has become pleasurably habitual over evolutionary time. **Worm-coils** in the lowest warmths pulse in synchrony, their muscular peristalsis against stone a bass note below hearing.

And the people themselves. They speak rarely. What they must communicate, they communicate through gesture, through manipulation of the growth-lights, through *placement*—where one stands in a chamber, among the hanging veils of filament and the shelf-gardens and the constant scurrying of domesticated or tolerated or simply *coexisting* small fauna. But when they do speak, when ritual or emergency demands voice, they *chant*, they *tone*, they find pitches that resonate with the city's constant hum, that *ride* it, that turn the omnipresent noise into carrier wave for meaning.

The result is never silent, rarely quiet, often *loud* in the sense that conversation requires raised voice, that one must project to be heard across a chamber, that the ear is always working, always filtering, always *attending*. Deafness, here, is not disability but a kind of death, a removal from the informational substrate of the world.

## The Fauna, Overwhelming

Let me try to catalogue what I have seen, knowing catalogues are lies of order imposed on proliferation.

In **Vorath-proper**, the lowest chamber: the **warm-worms**, already mentioned, and the **heat-heads**, blind bird-things that nest in the hottest crevices and feed on the worms, their metabolism so adapted that they die if carried to cooler chambers. The **resin-crabs**, not crabs, with shells of accumulated mineral deposit and symbiotic algae-glow that they arrange on their backs in patterns that may be territorial, may be aesthetic. The **tick-pickers**, small and many-legged, that inhabit the hair and clothing and sleeping-furs of the people, eating dead skin and waste oils and in return producing a mild anesthetic secretion that the people *cultivate*, deliberately move among themselves, consider a social glue. You are not fully member until your tick-pickers have mingled with another's.

In **Keth-middens**, the central trading and living chamber: here the density peaks, the 1.6 of density and the 1.6 of built environment and the 2.7 of fauna all achieving some terrible and productive synthesis. The ceiling is invisible, lost in hanging gardens of **light-rot** and **drip-moss** and **air-roots** that descend like tentacles, like invitations. The walls are carved with **nesting-hollows** for **glide-mice**, **chatterers**, **stone-larks** (not larks), species brought or encouraged or simply *arrived* and adapted. The floor is uneven, built up over centuries of accumulation and partial clearing, so that one walks on a topography of previous living, compacted organic matter mineralizing into something like soil, something like peat, something like *future fuel* for fires that are rarely lit because of the air cost.

And everywhere, *moving*, the **swarms**. Not insects, though insectile. **Aspect-swarmers** that shift their collective behavior based on temperature, becoming pollinators in warmth, predators in cool, cleaners in the transitions. They have no individual intelligibility but the swarm has *something*, responds to the city's rhythms, perhaps is the city's rhythms made mobile. The people do not control them. At most, they *negotiate*, leaving certain offerings in certain places, accepting certain losses, maintaining a balance that is not stable but *stabilized*, dynamic, the 2.5 of active process made social practice.

In **Keth-high**, the uppermost chamber, coolest and driest and *closest*: here the fauna thins, but not the life. The **edge-climbers**, shelled things that scale the vertical passage to the outside, that can survive brief exposure to the surface's scouring dryness and killing light, that bring back on their bodies and in their guts the trace materials—metals, rare elements, *information*—that the city cannot generate itself. They are worshipped, in a fashion. They are eaten, when they fail to return or return changed. Everything is eaten, eventually. The闭环 is tight. The 0.3 of water forbids waste.

## The People, Their Technology, Their Urban Life

Primitive: 0.6. Pre-metal or simple metal, hand-tools, manual craft.

This is not quite right, not quite fair, but it is not wrong. The people of Keth-Vorath have no mines, no surface access to ores, no fuel for smelting beyond the rare and precious *resin-fire* that is used only in ceremony and desperate need. What metal exists is recycled, ancestral, shaped by erosion and use into smooth and meaningful forms: a knife of uncertain alloy, passed through generations; a bell that was once something else, beaten and folded until it sings a particular note in the hum.

Their tools are **obsidian**, abundant in the volcanic glass of the cave walls, flaked with skill that has had centuries to perfect. **Bone**, of the fauna they eat and the fauna that dies and the occasional human dead, though the dead are usually *processed* more completely, more usefully. **Resin**, in its hundred forms: as adhesive, as sealant, as building material when fired, as *medium* for the growth they cultivate, the vegetation they have shaped as dogs were shaped from wolves. **Ceramic**, low-fired, precious, the product of that rare resin-fire. **Fiber**, from plants that grow only in the warm-wet gradients near Vorath-proper, processed by hand, woven on simple looms into fabrics that never quite dry, that maintain the body's moisture against the air's thirst.

And **biotechnology**, though they would not call it that, though it is not conscious in any engineering sense. The growths they cultivate, the fauna they encourage or discourage, the *arrangements* of living things that create temperature gradients, that filter air, that process waste, that *are* the infrastructure of the city—these are their true technology, more complex than any forge, more ancient than any writing. They have shaped an ecology that supports them, and in turn they support it, and the distinction between tool and partner, between cultivation and domestication, between city and organism, has long since dissolved.

The urban form follows. Dwellings are not houses but *niches*, carved or grown or accumulated, individualized by arrangement of familiar growths, by the particular fauna permitted to cohabit, by the acoustic properties that allow private conversation in the perpetual hum. There are perhaps four hundred people in Keth-Vorath, perhaps six hundred, numbers uncertain because the boundary of person is not certain—does one count the tick-pickers? The symbiotic growths in the gut that allow digestion of the local biology? The family swarm-aspects that maintain one's niche's climate?

They gather in Keth-middens for exchange, for the rituals of redistribution that substitute for market, for the tonal performances that substitute for theater, for the **mixings** where tick-pickers and body-flora and even, carefully, carefully, aspects of the swarms mingle and recirculate. There is no government visible, no authority, only the accumulated weight of *how things are done*, the pressure of ecological necessity, the consent of those who know that deviation too far threatens not punishment but *collapse*, not social sanction but *death by malfunction in a system too complex to repair*.

## The Ancientness, The Erosion, The Active Now

The cave is ancient, the 2.9 made felt. Not old as a tree is old, growing and dying in centuries, but old as stone is old, as *process* is old. The glassy walls bear the marks of heat-cycles beyond counting, of pressure changes that have cracked and reflowed, of mineral deposition and mineral dissolution in patterns that suggest the cave has been *many caves*, has sealed and reopened and resealed, has forgotten and relearned its own shape. The people have been here, in geological terms, an eyeblink. In human terms, long enough that their own accumulated building, the erosion of their passage, the wearing of their paths and the compaction of their floors, has become part of the cave's record, another layer of the ancient.

Yet it is active, the 2.5, *now*. The geological engine shifts, sometimes, sending new warm pulses upward, triggering bloom and die-off and reorganization. The fauna swarms and crashes in cycles no one fully predicts. The people respond, move, adapt, their niches temporary arrangements in the flow. There is no settled final form, only the *appearance* of settling, the deep time of stone containing the quicker time of biology containing the quicker still of human life, all moving, all *humming* at their frequencies.

I have not mentioned the surface. There is a surface. The edge-climbers go there, sometimes people follow them, briefly, in rituals of departure and return, or in final exile. It is dry, the 0.3 given full reign, waterless and light-scoured and empty of the lush density that makes Keth-Vorath possible. The mountain is not special there, one weathered peak among many in a range that stretches beyond knowledge. The cave mouth is hard to find, deliberately obscured, a vertical slit that breathes.

The people do not name the world above. Or if they do, the name is tonal, embedded in the hum, inaccessible to transcription. They have what they need. They have the life that grows without water, the warmth without fire, the city without metal, the dense and overwhelming and loud and dim *presence* of coexisting, of *being* in this bubble of ancient gas made home, this pocket scale containing multitudes, this normal-weird place where nothing is strange because strangeness requires comparison, and there is nothing here to compare to, only the constant, active, humming *now*.

## A Final Note on Scale

The scale is hidden, 0.1, pocket, intimate, embedded. Keth-Vorath does not know it is small. Its people do not know, or do not care, that the mountain above is minor, that the range it sits in is minor, that their entire world is a cavity one could walk across in an hour if the way were clear. Their density, their intensity, their *saturation*—the 1.6 of density made vivid by confinement—creates a qualitative expansion. They live in a universe as full as any universe needs to be.

I have described what I can. The rest is in the hum, in the warmth, in the particular smell of copper and sweet rot, in the touch of a wall that has been touched ten thousand times, in the weight of tick-pickers in your hair, in the dim glow of rot that is also life, in the knowledge that above is only dry and death and below is warmth and around is only *this*, this contained, ancient, active, overwhelming, loud, lush, built, primitive, warm, dense, hidden *place*.

Keth-Vorath. The Hum. What is, where what is, is enough.
