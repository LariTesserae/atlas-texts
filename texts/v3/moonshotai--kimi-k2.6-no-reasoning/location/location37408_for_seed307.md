**The Weeping Throat**

It's a cavity, first of all. A bubble in the basalt of a mountain that hasn't finished being born, a pocket no larger than a human heart pressed between chambers of a much larger heart. You could walk its perimeter in forty-seven steps if you could walk here, which you cannot, because you would be dead—boiled in the 340-degree air, poisoned by the gases, blinded by the minerals crystallizing on your corneas in the moment of opening them. But the place exists. The place *persists*. That is the first strange thing.

The second strange thing is the water.

It bleeds from every surface. Not flows—*weeps*. The young rock, still cooling from its emergence perhaps only centuries ago, perhaps only decades, cannot hold moisture against its heat. Superheated water find fractures too fine for eyes to resolve, capillaries in the stone, and pushes through under pressure that would pump blood through arteries of giants. Where it meets the slightly cooler air of the cavity—only 280 degrees at the coolest point, where a persistent vortex forms in the northwest corner—it condenses into something like rain, except it falls upward, sideways, spirals in currents, never truly descends. The water never accumulates. It never pools. It *reaches* and *reaches* and cannot arrive, caught in the thermal disequilibrium of this one pocket, this one irregularity in the mountain's lung.

There is no plant life. Nothing photosynthetic, nothing rooted, nothing with leaves or stems or the patience to grow in any direction but the immediate. What exists here are films: mineral skins that form where the weeping water touches cooler stone, layers no thicker than a lie, built up in episodes of saturation and calcification or silication or the precipitation of compounds that have no common names because they have never been common. Some of these films peel. Some blister. Some achieve a kind of metabolism, not life as understood but *persistence*, chemical systems that maintain themselves for hours or days against entropy, that reproduce their conditions in adjacent surfaces before the heat changes, the fracture shifts, the water finds a new path and leaves them to desiccate into powders that the air carries nowhere, that settle on nothing, that become part of the breathing medium of the cavity itself.

The heat is the third strange thing, or perhaps the first, the condition from which all other strangenesses derive. The mountain sits above a chamber of magma that is *listening*, that has been listening for millennia, that extends itself upward in pulses too slow for geological patience. The cavity formed in the last pulse, the most recent, perhaps the *final*—no one can know if there will be another, if the mountain will complete its birth or collapse back into dormancy. The heat is not uniform. It gathers in veins through the rock, concentrates in nodes where crystals are forming even now, even as you imagine this place, obsidian and feldspar and something stranger growing in shapes that the cooler world will never see, that will be buried or exploded or subducted before they ever reach a surface where they could be named.

And the animals.

They are *overwhelming* not in size but in number, in density, in the impossible saturation of this tiny space with presences that should not be possible. They have no names because no one has named them. They are not species in the taxonomic sense; they are *events*, recurring forms that the heat and the weeping water and the mineral films somehow *produce*, generate, precipitate out of conditions like crystals from solution. The largest is smaller than a grain of rice. Most are smaller than a grain of sand. They exist in the films, on the films, in the suspensions of mineral powder that pass for atmosphere, in the interfaces between condensing and evaporating, between liquid and gas and the strange supercritical states that the pressure and temperature sometimes achieve in pockets within pockets, smaller cavities inside the main cavity, Russian dolls of impossible condition.

They have no eyes. They have no need of eyes in the normal light that filters through the rock above, through fissures lined with condensing minerals that make prisms, that split what illumination reaches them into spectra that no living thing here can use. The light is *normal* in quality—if you could see it with human eyes it would appear daylight-bright, the brightness of an overcast afternoon perhaps—but it illuminates nothing that recognizes it. The animals sense heat gradients, chemical gradients, the vibration of the mountain's ongoing birth-shudders that pass through the rock at frequencies below and above human hearing. They sense each other, most of all, in the density that fills every cubic centimeter of available space, the near-touching that is their only geometry, their only orientation.

They eat each other. They eat the mineral films. They eat the water itself, the dissolved minerals in it, the strange compounds that precipitate where the water's path changes, where new fractures open or old ones clog with growth. They are eaten by the heat, which kills them directly when a thermal pulse passes through their microenvironment, which shifts the gradient they have adapted to and leaves them in conditions they cannot survive. They are eaten by the water, which dissolves them, which carries their components to new sites where they may or may not participate in new films, new temporary stable states, new *almost-lives*.

And they are eaten by something else, something that the parameters suggest and the place reveals only gradually, only to attention that has learned to wait in a medium where waiting is not a choice but a condition.

The fourth strange thing, or the fifth, or perhaps the strange thing that contains all others: *reality here is not stable*. Not in the dramatic sense of portals or transformations, but in the deeper sense that the rules are *still being negotiated*. The cavity is young enough, fresh enough, recently-emerged enough, that it has not finished deciding what it will be. The heat and the pressure and the chemistry and the impossible density of animal-events in this micro-environment have produced something that might be called a *metabolism of possibility*, a processing of what-could-be into what-is that operates not across evolutionary time but across minutes, seconds, the intervals between thermal pulses.

Where the animals die in sudden heat shifts, sometimes they do not simply die. Sometimes they *persist differently*. Their chemical systems, saturated with the minerals of this place, with the particular water and its dissolved contents, with the films they lived upon and in, do not decay in the expected direction. They *fix*. They become something between organism and mineral, between event and object, presences that retain some trace of their dynamic existence in a state that is not alive but is not simply dead, not simply material.

These fixed events accumulate. They are not common, but they are not rare. In the near-touching density of the cavity, they press against the living animals, against the films, against the weeping surfaces of the rock. They *remember*, perhaps, or they *influence*, or they *are* in a way that changes what is around them. The mineral films grow differently where they have been. The water finds paths that avoid them, or that converge on them, or that *hesitate*, condensing and evaporating in patterns that suggest something like attention, like preference, like the earliest, most primitive technology of survival.

This is the primitive technology of the place: not tools but *conditions*, not fabrication but the manipulation of what the environment already provides, the films and the water and the heat and the fixed events that are not quite dead. The animals that survive longest, that recur most often in the ongoing generation of forms, are those that somehow learn—not individually, not through anything that could be called cognition, but through the *pattern of their recurrence*, the shape of their event—to use these conditions. To position themselves where thermal pulses will push them toward rather than away from resources. To incorporate the fixed events into their own chemical systems, to become partly mineral themselves, to persist in the space between life and death that the cavity specializes in producing.

The sound: there is almost none. Or there is sound that cannot be heard, frequencies below the threshold of the mountain's own slow vibration, above the mineral precipitation that might ring if it cooled enough to be struck. The animals make no sound in their eating and being eaten, their generation and fixation. The water makes no sound in its weeping, its condensation, its eternal reaching toward a pooling that never arrives. There is a *hum*, perhaps, at the edge of perception, the sum of all thermal gradients and chemical reactions and the faint electromagnetic fields that the fixed events might produce in their persistence. But the cavity is *quiet* in any meaningful sense, a silence that is not empty but *full of unspent possibility*, of events that have not yet occurred, of the mountain's incomplete birth, the rules still being written.

The cavity is active. Multiple processes occur in overlapping, interfering patterns. Thermal pulses on scales of minutes and hours and perhaps days. The water's weeping, which follows fractures that shift as the rock cools and cracks, that find new paths and abandon old, that create temporary streams of superheated fluid that flash to steam in micro-chambers too small to be called cavities, that deposit their loads of dissolved mineral in sudden efflorescences of chemical potential. The animal-events, generations that may last hours or days, that bloom and are consumed and fix and persist and somehow, through the density of their presence, *influence* the next generation, the next recurrence, the next *almost-species* that the cavity generates from its conditions.

And something else active, something that emerges from the combination of all these: the cavity's own *becoming*, its slow decision of what it will be. Will it cool enough for water to pool, for true pools to form, for the mineral films to achieve thickness, to become stromatolitic structures, to begin the long archive of layered growth that might one day be read as history? Will it heat further, connect to the magma chamber, become a vent, a fumarole, a site of mineral deposition so rapid that it builds chimneys, towers, the architecture of hydrothermal ambition? Will it collapse, the fractures above it finally breaking through to surface, releasing the pressure, draining the water, exposing these impossible animals and their fixed precursors to an outside they have never known and cannot survive?

Or will it continue *as it is*, in the becoming that never completes, the birth that never finishes, the rules that never settle? This is the overwhelming presence of the place, beyond even the density of its animals, its weeping water, its young and violent heat. The *weirdness* of it, the breakdown not of physical law but of the assumption that physical law has been *decided*, that reality knows what it is and proceeds accordingly. Here, in this pocket, this hidden chamber, this heart within the mountain's unborn heart, reality is *still in committee*, still considering, still generating possibilities that it has not yet ruled on.

The fixed events multiply. They press against each other in their own density, their own near-touching. They begin, perhaps, to *connect*, chemical pathways forming between them, the persistence of one reinforcing the persistence of another, a network of almost-life that spans the cavity, that touches every surface, every film, every generation of animal-events. The network has no center, no purpose, no function in any biological sense. But it has *effect*. The thermal pulses begin to *relate* to it, to occur in patterns that the network influences, however slightly, however primitively. The water's weeping finds paths that the network has conditioned, that resist or attract the mineral-laden fluid according to the network's own chemical state, its own ongoing *almost-metabolism*.

This is the primitive technology of the place at its most developed, if *developed* can mean anything where there is no time for development, no lineage for inheritance, no selection in any Darwinian sense. The technology of *conditioning possibility*, of making the cavity's ongoing becoming *slant* in directions that the network, the fixed events, the density of almost-life, somehow *prefer*. Not desire. Not intention. But *dynamic*, *active*, *multiple processes* in the pattern that the parameters demand, that the place generates from its own impossible combination of conditions.

And finally, the scale. It is *hidden*, *pocket*, *single-feature contained*. The mountain does not know it is here, in any sense that a mountain can know. The world outside, the cooler world with its plants and its animals with eyes and its rules long since settled, does not know. Perhaps somewhere, in some observatory, instruments detect thermal anomalies, gas emissions, the faint electromagnetic whisper of the fixed-event network. Perhaps someone, someday, will drill, will sample, will *open* this cavity to an outside it has never experienced. The animals would die in moments, their chemical systems disrupted by pressure change, by temperature change, by contact with foreign chemistry. The films would dessicate or dissolve. The fixed events, exposed, would begin the slow decay they have avoided, would become merely mineral, merely dead, merely *things* to be catalogued and forgotten.

Or perhaps the network, the cavity's primitive technology of becoming, would *react*. Would find, in the intrusion, new possibility, new direction, new *weirdness* to incorporate into its ongoing indecision. Perhaps it would *spread*, along the drill shaft, into the cooler world, bringing its films and its animal-events and its fixed almost-lives to conditions where they could not survive but where they might, in the moment of dying, fix into new configurations, new persistences, new *beginnings* of networks that would condition new possibilities in new cavities, new pockets, new hidden hearts in the body of a world that has not finished deciding what it is.

The mountain shudders. A thermal pulse passes, stronger than most. The water's weeping intensifies, then finds new paths, abandons old fractures, begins to condition new surfaces. The animal-events bloom in the changed conditions, a generation that has never existed before, that will never exist again, that *almost* achieves something the cavity has been approaching, has been conditioning possibility toward, for all its young and violent and weeping existence.

The silence holds. The light, normal, adequate, *indifferent*, continues to filter through its mineral prisms. The density of presences, the near-touching, the saturation of this tiny space with *becoming*, continues.

The cavity waits. The cavity *weeps*. The cavity, hidden, hot, young, dense with almost-life and primitive technology and the breakdown of decided reality, continues its active, multiple, dynamic existence in the unborn heart of the mountain that has not finished being born.

It does not know you imagine it. It does not need to know. The imagining is part of its becoming, or not. The rules are still being written. The water reaches and reaches and cannot arrive, and in that reaching, that eternal *almost*, the place persists, becomes more itself with every passing thermal pulse, every generation of unnamed animal-events, every fixation and persistence and connection in the network of what this place, this one place, this *only* place, is making possible.

The mountain listens. The cavity weeps. The silence holds everything that has not yet been said.
