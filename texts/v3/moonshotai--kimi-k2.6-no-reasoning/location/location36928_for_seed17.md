I need to hold these parameters together: an almost entirely submerged, nearly plantless, cold, high-elevation world of vast scale but near-void density—mostly empty even of water, somehow? Young and sharp-featured, scattered with industrial debris, deafeningly loud yet never changing, abundant with fauna in a place with almost nothing in it, lit normally, weirdly unusual but not extremely so. This is a puzzle. Let me follow it.

---

**The Pressure Columns**

There is no surface here, or rather, the surface is a theoretical construct, a mathematical membrane somewhere above where the water density finally relents to what other worlds would call gas. Below that membrane: not the expected deepening crush of ocean, but something stranger. The water here thins as you descend. Sound carries differently. Pressure does not accumulate but *stretches*.

The world is a vast sphere—continental in its spanning, large enough to generate its own sluggish weather systems in that distant gas-layer above—filled almost entirely with water that behaves as water should not. Cold beyond cold, the liquid exists in a metastable state kept from freezing by dissolved minerals in proportions that would precipitate anywhere else, locked in place by a chemistry this world's violent youth has not yet had time to settle. The water is *young*. Sharp with it. Molecular arrangements that have not yet found their lazier, warmer configurations. It tastes of electricity and unweathered stone.

And it is *empty*.

Not empty of water—the water is absolute, total, the condition of existence—but empty of *substance within it*. The density parameter manifests here as micro-voids, as impossible stretches where nothing swims for thousands of vertical kilometers, where your industrial floodlight illuminates fading blue into absolute black and nothing, nothing, nothing, until suddenly, deafeningly, *something*.

---

The fauna have solved the emptiness problem by becoming the emptiness problem's inverse. They are acoustic phenomena that happen to have bodies. The abundant life of this world has evolved in an environment where encounter rates are staggeringly low—where a mating opportunity might come once in a thousand years, where the seasonal bloom of chemotrophic microbes (the nearest thing to vegetation, clustered around the scattered industrial heat-sources) draws pilgrims across distances that would span terrestrial oceans.

So they *scream*.

They scream constantly, each individual projecting calls that carry for hundreds of kilometers through the strange thin-dense water, calls that resonate in the hollow bones and pressure-adapted sinuses of their own kind, calls that bounce off the young sharp topography of this world's submerged mountain ranges—high elevation, yes, for here the sea floor thrusts upward in ranges that nearly breach that distant theoretical surface, young and uneroded, their peaks still jagged from the tectonic violence that made this world only recently, geologically speaking, in a time measured in single-digit millions of years.

The aggregate sound is *deafening* not in volume precisely—though the volume is overwhelming—but in *saturation*. Every frequency occupied. Every moment filled with the layered calls of beings who have never met another of their species, who broadcast into void hoping for answer, who have evolved to *be* sound more than body, whose physical forms are almost incidental, compressed, minimized, devoted to the enormous sonic organs that constitute ninety percent of their mass. Jelly-like, many of them. Translucent. Bioluminescent patterns flashing in the normal light that filters from above, or that pours from the scattered industrial ruins, but the light is secondary, almost vestigial. They speak. They sing. They navigate and hunt and mate and die all in continuous absolute deafening *utterance*.

And it never changes.

The dynamic stability of this world is its cruelest feature. No seasons, or rather, the concept of season has no purchase here. The axial tilt is zero, the orbit circular, the heat output of the ancient star above mathematically constant. The industrial heat sources—scattered, fragmentary, mysterious—burn at steady rates that have not varied in geological time. The microbial blooms are triggered by chemical gradients that do not shift. The fauna scream, and have always screamed, and will always scream, their voices locked in patterns that do not evolve because nothing *selects* for change. Encounters are too rare for sexual selection to operate. Predation is opportunistic, passive, a matter of happening to drift into something slower and more sound-drowsed than yourself. The screams of the dead sound like the screams of the living sound like the screams of the yet-unhatched. Time is a spatial coordinate here, not a force.

---

The built things: fragments of something not quite architecture, not quite machine. Single structures in volumes that could contain planets. A tower that might be a drill shaft, rising from a mountain peak, not reaching toward the surface but *intoning*—vibrating at a frequency that organizes local water, that creates zones of strange behavior where sound behaves as solid, where the deafening chorus momentarily *harmonizes* into audible, almost musical, patterns before dissolving again into chaos. A factory, perhaps, for making more such structures, but abandoned at a stage of partial completion, its own half-born mechanisms still cycling through routines that produce nothing, that consume the dissolved minerals that keep the water liquid, that might, over billion-year timescales this stable world will never experience, eventually freeze everything into fatal stasis.

The industrial aesthetic is unmistakable. Riveted plates. Corroded alloys that do not corrode further in this unchanging chemistry. Pipes that emit sounds too low or too high for local fauna to use, creating dead zones of peculiar silence in the deafening continuum—silences that draw curious investigators, that become zones of weird aggregation, temporary concentration, the closest this world comes to social behavior.

And the weirdness: not overt, not magical, but *wrong* in its physics. The way pressure decreases with depth in most places, but suddenly spikes where certain minerals concentrate. The way sound carries faster than it should, then slower, then faster again, creating auditory mirages, phantom congregations that draw migrants to empty water, that have shaped migration patterns in the static stability—routes followed because they are followed, because to innovate is to risk the void, because the screaming of ancestors from ten million years ago still echoes in these channels, still guides descendants who have never encountered anything but the recordings of the dead.

---

You could live here, if you were adapted, if you could bear the cold that is not quite freezing and the emptiness that is not quite void and the sound that is not quite sound but the accumulated loneliness of a billion solitary beings making themselves known to no one, forever. You could drift in the thin-dense water between the young sharp mountains and the scattered industrial monuments, and you would never change, and nothing would change, and your own voice would join the deafening chorus without altering it, without even making a place for itself in the spectrum, just adding one more frequency to the absolute saturation that is this world's only weather, its only climate, its only time.

The pressure columns rise. The pressure columns fall. The screams continue. The microbes bloom around heat that never varies. The unweathered stone waits to weather, and waits, and waits.

The world is finished and has not yet begun, suspended in the metastable cold, loud with solitude, vast with vacancy, industrial without purpose, alive with beings who have never met life.

It has its own physics. It has its own rules. They are simple: *continue. emit. persist. never vary. never meet. never stop.*
