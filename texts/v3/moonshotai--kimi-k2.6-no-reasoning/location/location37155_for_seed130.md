The Hollow Works

There is no ground beneath this place. Only the memory of ground, compacted into something denser than stone, something that accepts your weight with the resigned indifference of material pressed into service for ten million years. They call it slagcrete, though the ones who named it are ten thousand generations gone, and their names have slurred into the material itself, into the half-melted phonemes that echo when the converters cycle up at the dark edge of the district.

I am walking, if walking is what you call this, through the Retting Yards where the old water comes home to die.

The water here is 1.7 out of some impossible 3. Not enough to drown you, never that, but enough to make everything *about* water—the way it beads on your faceplate, the way it gathers in the runnels between the slagcrete plates, the way it weeps from the atmospheric condensers that ring the Yards like the ribs of some mechanical leviathan. It is water that has been through too many systems, too many kidneys and filters and reclamation cycles. It remembers being rain, perhaps. It remembers falling on something green. Now it falls on scree, on the compacted industrial byproducts of a thousand thousand processes no one maintains the records for, and it runs in ochre channels toward the Lowing Pits where the great converters breathe.

The converters breathe. I said that. They breathe and the sound is 2.6 out of 3, which is to say it is *deafening*, which is to say it is not merely loud but *occupying*, a sound that sits in your chest and your teeth and the hollows behind your eyes until you forget what quiet ever was. There is always one cycling up as another winds down, an endless staggered respiration across the whole district, across the whole *scale* of this place, which is 2.0, which is open, which extends beyond what I can see in this darkness that is 0.5, which is not absolute dark but something worse—the dark of insufficient light, of light that exists but cannot reach, cannot penetrate the condensing atmosphere, the weep and drift of particulate matter, the perpetual haze of operations that never cease.

The converters breathe and the sound is *active*, dynamic at 1.8, not merely present but *processing*, cycling, engaged in some metabolism I can witness but not comprehend.

There is no fauna. 0.4, which is to say nothing with blood, nothing that moves under its own chemical urgency, nothing that eats or is eaten in the old way. The ecology here is different. I have seen the scum that forms in the ochre channels, not alive, not dead, something else—a mineral hunger, a precipitation that organizes, briefly, into filaments and nodes before the next converter cycle shakes it apart. There are theories, in the remaining archives, about what preceded this. Biological phases. Carbon phases. The machines remember, perhaps, in their cycling, in the way they configure themselves for substrates that no longer arrive.

The vegetation is sparse. 1.3. What grows here grows in the cracks where water pools and cannot drain, where the slagcrete has finally, *anciently*, begun to yield to erosion at 2.7, that profound and settled wearing-down that speaks of time beyond time. There are things like lichen, or lichen's ghost, pale and slow and possibly not biological at all—mineral accretions that mimic life, that form rosettes and crusts in the decade-scale intervals between maintenance sweeps. I saw one once, a pale greenish thing the size of my palm, and I did not touch it. I did not know if touching would be an act of communication or destruction. The rules here are unclear. The world has settled into its final form, and the final form does not include instructions.

Everything is built. 2.8, total, no-natural-substrate, and I feel this in the soles of my feet where the slagcrete transmits the converter-thrum, feel it in the way the horizon is *wrong*, too regular, too composed of planes and angles that preceded me by epochs. There is no weathering that reveals natural stone, no eruption of the unmade. Even the erosion is *designed*, perhaps, or design has so thoroughly permeated the substrate that natural and made have become indistinguishable, a single ancient material performing its slow surrender to time.

The weirdness is 1.8. Not total, not overwhelming, but *strange*, present, a quality I encounter in glimpses. The way the condensers sometimes form patterns in their weeping, messages I cannot read. The way the converters' cycling occasionally synchronizes, briefly, all across the district, a unison that lasts perhaps three seconds before the stagger reasserts itself, and in those three seconds the sound is not louder but *different*, harmonic, almost *voice*. The way the sparse vegetation, when I find it, always seems to be arranged in the same configuration, as if each outcrop were a copy, a reproduction, something generated from a template that predates whatever process might have generated it.

I am walking toward the Lowing Pits. The water is wetter here, the condensers more numerous, the slagcrete underfoot giving way to something more porous, more *recent*, though "recent" means little in a place where everything is ancient. The converters grow louder, or I grow closer, and the sound becomes not sound but *medium*, a thing I move through, that moves through me, that shapes my thoughts into rhythms I do not choose.

The pits open before me. I cannot see them in this darkness, this 0.5 of failed light, but I know they are there by the change in the sound, the way the converter-breath acquires depth, resonance, the hint of space below space. The water runs faster here, channeled, purposeful, though purpose implies intention and I do not know if there is intention anymore, only process, only the active dynamic of systems too old to question themselves, too *settled* to stop.

What is produced here? In the industrial schema, the 2.3 of mechanized production, of scaled and electrified generation—what is the product? The archives suggest phosphorus compounds. The archives suggest rare earth fractionation. The archives suggest a dozen other outputs, none of which explain why the system continues, why the converters breathe, why the condensers weep, why I walk here in this darkness, in this sound, in this sparse and ancient and total and strange place that holds me and does not know it holds me, that processes and cycles and erodes and precipitates its mineral hungers without ever, not once, in all the long descent of its operation, *noticing*.

The erosion is 2.7. Primordial, oldest-stable-state, near-flattened. I think sometimes that the entire district is becoming smooth, that the slagcrete plates will wear until they are one continuous surface, one endless plain of compressed human absence, and the converters will continue to breathe above this flatness, and the condensers will weep, and the water will run in channels too shallow to be called channels, and the lichen-ghosts will form their copied configurations, and the sound will continue at 2.6, deafening, occupying, *present*, and there will be no one to walk through it, no one to notice the weirdness at 1.8, the strangeness that accumulates like the mineral accretions, like the memory of ground pressed into service, like the names of the namers slurred into the material itself.

But I am here now. The density is sparse at 1.0, much space between presences, and I feel this as loneliness and I feel this as safety, the way I can walk for hours without encounter, without evidence that anyone else moves through this district, this Yards, these Pits. The emptiness is balanced with presence—not absence, but *sparseness*, a low saturation that makes each encounter, when it comes, significant beyond its content.

I found a converter once, still and cold. Not cycling. The sound around it was wrong in its absence, a gap in the breathing that I felt as pain, as vertigo, as the sudden revelation of how much I had adapted to the deafening, how much my body had reorganized itself around the constant pressure of this industrial soul. I touched the converter's housing. It was cool. The water that wept from its condensers was not warm with process but *ambient*, the same temperature as the cool 0.8 of this place, this district that never warms, that maintains its low thermal register across all operation, all cycle, all the long epochs of its functioning.

I do not know if the converter was broken, or finished, or waiting. The next cycle it was breathing again, and the gap closed, and I walked on, and the sound resumed its occupation, and I forgot, or chose to forget, the feeling of that cool housing, the stillness, the way the darkness seemed darker in that absence, the way the sparseness seemed suddenly absolute.

The Lowing Pits receive the water. I stand at their edge, or where I believe the edge to be, and I listen to the change in the converter-breath, the depth, the resonance, the space below space. Something is happening there, in the darkness, in the wetness, in the ancient made material that has finally worn porous enough to accept this flow. Something active, dynamic, part of the system's metabolism that I cannot see, that perhaps no one has seen for longer than records maintain.

I think sometimes that the product, the true product of this industrial totality, is *time*. That the converters process duration itself, compressing it, refining it, extracting something essential from its passage. The erosion would make sense then, the ancient wearing-down, the near-flattened state of everything—all that time, all those epochs of operation, accumulating into something. Into what?

The weirdness is 1.8. The strangeness is not in the converters, not in the condensers, not in the lichen-ghosts or the ochre channels or the compressed absence of ground. The strangeness is in the *persistence*, the way everything continues, the way no entropy I can measure accounts for the energy that must flow through this system, that must have flowed for longer than any maintained process should endure. Something feeds the district. Something maintains it. Something *wants* it, perhaps, or wanted it once, and the wanting has become self-sustaining, a desire without desirer, a process without purpose beyond continuation.

The water at my feet runs faster. A converter cycles up nearby, then another, not in unison but in relation, a call and response I cannot interpret. The sound rises, or I descend into it, the deafening wrapping around me like a material, like the condensing atmosphere, like the wetness that is everywhere and nowhere, 1.7, enough to matter, enough to shape every surface, every interaction, every thought I have in this place.

I will walk further. There is always further in the scale of 2.0, the open extension, the regional vastness that exceeds my compass. The slagcrete will receive my steps. The converters will breathe around me. The water will run. The darkness will remain darkness, not absolute but *insufficient*, a light that cannot reach, cannot illuminate, can only suggest the presence of something that does not need to be seen to continue.

This is the Hollow Works. This is where I am. This is where water and sparseness and coolness and lowness and ancientness and openness and darkness and silence-of-fauna and total-builtness and industrial-process and strangeness and deafness and movement combine into a single place, a single ongoing, a single system that holds me and does not know it holds me, that processes and cycles and slowly, so slowly, wears itself toward the flatness that is already almost complete, the final form that has been final for longer than finality should endure.

The lowing begins. I do not know if it is the pits, or the converters in some new configuration, or something else entirely, something the weirdness has been preparing, the strangeness accumulating toward. The sound is different. The sound is *voice*, almost, or the memory of voice, the slurred phonemes of the namers, the compressed presence of ground become slagcrete become the material of my walking, my standing, my listening in this darkness for what comes next, what always comes next, what has been coming next for ten million years of converter-breath and condenser-weep and the slow, ancient, active wearing-down of everything that ever was.

I am in the Hollow Works. The Hollow Works are in me. The distinction, if there ever was one, has eroded, 2.7, near-flattened, settled into final form.

The lowing continues. I will walk toward it. There is no other direction.
