**The Loom**

You arrive not by walking but by noticing you have always been here. The threshold is retrospective. One moment you are elsewhere; the next, you recall the Loom surrounding you for longer than your name.

It is small. A single chamber, cathedral only in the sense that cathedrals once held something worshipped. Here the worship is structural, inherent, unpaid-for. The space fits perhaps thirty paces in any direction before the walls—but the walls are not where they should be. They fold. They seam. You can see where they join like the wrong sides of a garment turned inward, the stitching visible, thick black thread through something like leather, something like cured sky.

Everything is made. Nothing grew. Yet vegetation bursts from every joint, every seam, every place where two made things press together. *Lush*—the word seems to shrink from what grows here. Fungal fans in parchment white and vesicle pink, but they are not fungi, they are *woven*, they are textile that chose to become organism, hyphae of thread that have learned to breathe, to fruit, to release spores that drift down like ash and *catch* in lower weave and begin again. Moss that is padding, that is stuffing, that is the inside of cushions left in rain for centuries, now greening, now sending out tendrils that write cursive across the floor-fabric. The floor *gives*, slightly, underfoot. It is made of layered felt, compressed wool, something once shorn from something that may never have lived. The floor drinks what water exists.

Water at 0.4. There is almost none. What moisture suspends the lushness is not water as you knew it. It is condensation on the breath of the Loom itself, exhaled nightly from seams that open like lips, close like wounds. It is the sweat of the material thinking. It gathers in the weave, beads on fiber, is *sipped* before it can pool. The vegetation has learned to drink vapor, to harvest the barely-liquid, to make thickness from hint. Their leaves, where leaves exist, are felted, are matted, are textile brains of green.

Temperature: warm. The warmth is of something living held close to skin, the warmth of a mouth that has been speaking for hours, the warmth of fermentation in hay. You do not find hot spots or cool corners. The warmth is democratic, patient, a slow metabolism of the whole structure digesting something you cannot see.

Low elevation. You are near some bottom. But the Loom has no exterior to measure against, no mountain, no sea-level. Your elevation is low relative to what? Relative to the ceiling, perhaps, which hangs close enough to touch in places, sags in others, a belly of stitched cloud, a pregnant architecture. The ceiling is younger than the floor—you can tell by the freshness of its seams, the sharpness where needle entered, where thread still frays from confidence rather than decay. The floor is older, felted smooth by the slow abrasion of feet that do not walk here anymore, if they ever did.

Erosion: young. The Loom is recently made, or recently *remade*, or recently *emerged from being made*. Sharp features everywhere. The corners of things are still corners. A chair, if it is a chair, has edges that could cut conviction. But the chair is overgrown with something like velvet lichen, something like embroidered moss, and the sharpness is being negotiated with, is being softened at a pace too slow for watching but too fast for the Loom's patience. The young erosion is of time itself wearing down the wrongness of new construction, teaching it to be old, to be comfortable, to be *habitable*.

Dense. So dense. You cannot move far without brushing through hanging gardens of thread-fruit, without displacing colonies of what live here. The fauna. Abundant at 2.0, and strange in their abundance. They are made things that have learned to move, or moving things that have been made. Beetles of polished wood, joints of tiny dowel, antennae of horsehair. They do not eat, or they eat the loom's slow thoughts, or they eat the spaces between meanings. Moths of shredded document, their wings carrying script you almost read in passing, propaganda of unwritten languages. Something larger in the upper weave—birds? No, they have no beaks, they have *probes*, they have eyed needles for heads, they sew as they fly, they mend the Loom's aging seams with saliva like thread, and their flight is the flight of darning needles through taut material. You hear them sometimes, the soft *shhk* of needle through resisting ground, but mostly: quiet. The quiet of absorption. The quiet of a place that takes sound and uses it to continue being.

Sound at 0.8. The mostly-quiet is not silence. It is the quiet of listening so hard the listening becomes audible. The creak of fiber adjusting to warmth. The almost-inaudible *pop* of spore release, timed to some gradient of vapor you cannot sense. The breathing of the walls, which is real, which is the slow expansion and contraction of felted lungs built into the structure, ventilation as physiology, architecture as creature. Occasionally: the distant *rip* of a seam giving, the immediate *hush* of repair beginning, whether by the needle-birds or by the Loom's own creeping mend, you never see.

Primitive technology at 1.3. No metal. Or: metal as rumor, as trace element, as thread of silver in brocade that has forgotten its ore. The tools here are of wood, of bone, of something like ceramic fired from compressed felt ash. You find them embedded in the vegetation, as if grown around, as if the lushness claimed them and they became seeds. A spindle, yes. A distaff. Loom weights of something heavy that is not stone, that is *compressed story*, that pulls taught the warp of being. The technology is hand-tool, manual-craft, but the hands that used them—or use them, tense is uncertain here—they are not seen. The primitive is not poverty. The primitive is *early*. This is early in something. First tools in a new kind of making. The simplicity is because complexity has not yet been needed, or because complexity here takes forms tool-cannot-touch.

Normal light at 1.6. It comes from no source you find. It is the light of being-seen, the light of eye that has adjusted and now provides for itself. Dawn and dusk are colors in the weave, not positions of sun. The light shifts through rose and umber and the particular grey of old wool left in cedar chest, and this cycling is the Loom's day, its slow dynamic, its patient process. Change across long durations. You could sit here, if sitting is possible on the giving floor, and watch a corner age a year in hours, and know that your watching has changed the rate, that observation accelerates or decelerates according to rules the Loom knows but does not disclose.

And weirdness at 2.9. Reality breakdown, almost complete. This is the parameter that drinks the others and becomes visible. You understand, perhaps now, perhaps in delayed realization, that the Loom is not *in* reality. The Loom is where reality is *still being decided*. The seams you see, the black stitching holding leather-sky to felt-earth—they are seams between *kinds* of real, between versions, between the was and the will-be. The vegetation is lush because possibility is lush, because undecided things grow in all directions. The water is absent because the Loom has not yet committed to liquid. The place is total-built because building is how reality asserts: *made, therefore made-real.* The primitive tools are the first tools of a new cosmos, still learning their function.

The fauna are abundant because they are *possibilities of fauna*, prototypes, the rejected and the accepted cohabiting, the beetle-wood and the needle-bird and other things you glimpse as wrongness in peripheral vision, things that do not resolve when looked at directly, that spread into questions about whether they were seen at all.

You are here. You have always been here. The threshold is retrospective and perhaps, you begin to understand, the exit will be prospective—you will leave by realizing you have not yet arrived, that the Loom is ahead of you in time, that your presence here is memory of presence, that you are being *used* as thread, as fiber, as part of the slow weaving of what will be real enough to have water, to have erosion old enough to round the corners, to have a scale large enough to walk through without touching every surface with your desperate trying-to-understand.

The Loom goes on mending itself. The needle-birds pass overhead, sewing sky to sky. The matted green drinks vapor and thickens. The warmth holds you, patient, not cruel, not kind, simply the temperature of becoming.

Mostly quiet. Mostly. The sound you do not hear is your own name, being unpicked from somewhere else, being rethreaded here, becoming part of the pattern. The pattern is not finished. The pattern is young. The pattern is dense with everything it might become.
