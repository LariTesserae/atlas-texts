The city does not know it is a city. This is the first thing you must understand.

It sits in a karst bowl, a depression scooped from limestone by water that remembers glaciers it never touched. The bowl collects cold the way a still pond collects leaves—layer upon layer, each stratum older and more patient than the last. At 0.4 on the temperature scale, this is not the cold of killing frost but the cold of perpetual autumn, of cellars and wells and the shady side of hills even at noon. The inhabitants do not shiver. They have never known warmth enough to miss it.

Water rules everything at 2.5. Not standing water, mostly—not the declarative lakes and rivers that announce themselves with shorelines and banks. Instead: seepage, percolation, the vertical migration of moisture through stone and root and lung. The city's "streets" run with ribbons of water no deeper than a finger, following paths that predate any intention. These are not sewers. They are veins. The inhabitants walk barefoot or on broad pads, and their feet have grown translucent with constant wet, the skin webbed in places, pale as cave fish bellies. Condensation beads on every surface at every hour. Fungi fruit from wooden beams in profusions of ocher and corpse-white, and no one removes them. They are structural now. Load-bearing lichens.

At 2.2 vegetation, the city swallows green. Not the disciplined green of gardens or the opportunistic green of abandoned places, but something more intentional than abandonment, less willful than cultivation. Mosses have achieved canopy. They drape from archways in curtains thick enough to sleep within; they cushion every step, every voice, every footfall of the city's abundant fauna. The 2.0 abundance of animals manifests not as visible teeming but as constant peripheral motion—a rustle always just beyond the next pillar, a wet breathing that syncopates with your own. Salamanders the length of forearms bask on sun-warmed stone when sun reaches, retreat to the interstitial spaces when it fails. Moths with bodies like thumb-sized mice pollinate nothing, harvest nothing, exist in cycles that ignore seasons because seasons barely penetrate the bowl's rim. Something hunts them. Something is always hunting, but the predation occurs in the 0.3 silence, the near-silence of padded feet on moss, of mandibles closing on moth-bodies with no crunch audible through the wet.

The density is absolute: 3.0, packed, no room between presences. This is where the city-ness of the non-city crystallizes. The inhabitants—let us call them that, though "dwellers" or "grown" might serve better—fill every vertical and horizontal space. They hang in woven chambers from the mushroom-wood beams. They occupy the hollows where limestone has dissolved to create chambers no architect planned. They cluster at the water-table's edge, where the shallow streams vanish into swallow holes and re-emerge elsewhere, filtered, colder, tasting of stone and someone else's metabolism. They are packed not through intention or overcrowding but through accretion, generation upon generation of finding the niche that fits, the surface that holds, the air-pocket that sustains.

And they are urban at 2.3. This persists, this category, even without the tech score of 0.1 meaning anything like technology. The city is layered construction. It is infrastructure density. It is the accumulated modification of place by placed beings, organized not by plan but by the deeper organization of what works, what has worked, what continues to work because to stop working is to stop being occupied, to become niche, to be filled. Older chambers are higher, dryer, abandoned to the drips that come and the moss that seals them into green sarcophagi. Newer occupation pushes toward the water, the seep, the wet edge where the bowl's basin holds its maximum cold. There are paths between these layers, routes of travel that function as roads, intersections where multiple paths cross and something like commerce occurs—substances exchanged through membranes, through regurgitation, through the simple profound economy of touching and being touched in a world this damp, this dense, this alive.

The scale at 2.2 confuses. The bowl extends, regional, town-or-valley-sized, multiple features in visible relation. But the density compresses perception. To move from any point to any other is to pass through so many occupied surfaces, so many presences in contact or near-contact, that distance becomes measured in interactions rather than meters. The city's open extent is known more than experienced; travelers who have circled the bowl's rim report back with information rather than familiarity. Inside, one is always inside, nested, held by the wet and the green and the packed bodies generating warmth that the cold drinks but cannot fully extinguish.

Young at 1.2 erosion, the city is early formation, sharp-featured, recently emerged in geologic and perhaps other terms. The limestone dissolution that hollowed the bowl has not gentled into smoothness. Edges remain where edges fell. New chambers open when water finds new paths, and these are colonized with the urgency of pioneer species, the first arrivals already building the packed density of future centuries. The city is growing downward more than outward, the water-table its frontier, each new flooded level a stratum for the future, a memory of how it will have been.

The light at 1.9 is normal because the bowl's rim lets in normal sky, though the sky itself seems smaller here, a colder blue, less generous with its photons. What light enters is filtered, diffused by the moss canopy, reflected by wet surfaces until direction loses meaning. Shadows are shallow and greenish. The brightest illumination comes from bioluminescence—the 2.2 vegetation includes species that glow, that pulse, that mark territories or attract the silent predators or accomplish purposes no external observer parses. In the deepest chambers, where water has claimed all surface and no sky reaches, the light is entirely biological, the city generating its own dim constellation, a galaxy of slow brightening and fading that maps nothing but presence, persistence, the fact of packed life continuing in conditions that elsewhere would read as void.

Dynamic at 1.8, active, multiple processes in concert: the water's slow dissolution and swifter percolation, the fungi's growth and rot and growth, the predator-prey cycles that maintain the faunal abundance without visible boom or bust, the inhabitants' own metabolism that runs cold and slow and continuous, no burst of activity, no torpor, just the steady state of beings who have found their pace and keep it. Weather penetrates as mist, as the occasional rain that adds to the seep rather than announces itself, as temperature shifts of a degree that propagate slowly through the city's mass and are responded to slowly, if at all.

There are no names. The 0.1 tech, the pure biological, pre-tool nature of the place, extends to symbol, to the abstraction that naming requires. The inhabitants recognize. They chemical-signal, they touch, they vibrate through the water-saturated stone in frequencies that communicate identity without ever detaching into label. The city exists in continuous present tense, its history inscribed in layer and occupation pattern rather than narrative, its future implicit in the downward growth, the slow seep toward conditions that will support what comes next, what grows into the niche opening now, water-warmed by metabolism, light-warmed by phosphorescence, space held open by the packed density of everything that already is.

To visit—though visitors are category errors, externalities the city has no grammar for—is to become briefly part of this. Your footsteps fill with water behind you, close with moss, erase. Your warmth dissipates into the cold that holds 0.4 like a held breath. Your sounds at 0.3 volume or less, your near-silence, becomes the silence of the place, indistinguishable. What you see in the green dim is what has always been seen, what sees itself through your borrowed eyes, what continues when you have dissolved into the seep, into the nutrient cycle, into the urban non-city that built nothing and holds everything, that is packed so full of presence that absence becomes impossible, a category with no referent, a silence with no sound to define it against.

The bowl holds. The water moves through. The city does not know it is a city, and in this not-knowing persists, grows downward, waits for the next dissolution, the next chamber, the next bright emergence of space that will be filled before it is fully formed, because filling is what this place does, what it is, what continues in the cold wet green silence of packed and active and young and abundantly, densely, urbanly alive.
