The city of Veth A'kul breathes through pores of calcified bone, though no lungs ever moved here, and the breathing is not its own.

You arrive at the edge of what was once a bay, though the distinction between water and land has become advisory at best. The elevation is low—so low that the horizon seems to bend upward to meet a sky that is not so much dark as *unwilling*. The light parameter reads 0.2, and indeed, there is no source of illumination you can identify, yet you can see. This is the first wrongness. Your eyes function, rods and cones catching something that behaves like photons but arrives from no direction, casting no shadows, creating only a dim continuous visibility like the memory of dawn preserved in wet stone. The darkness here is not absence but *presence*—a thick, particulate medium that happens to be transparent to human vision, the way water is transparent to fish.

The water is everywhere and nowhere specific. It rises through seams in the architecture, not as springs but as *recollections*—the buildings seem to weep, to condense moisture from the saturated air, to remember when they were submerged and act accordingly. The streets, if they can be called streets, run with shallow films that never deepen to puddles, never evaporate to dryness. Everything is coated in a mineral varnish, alternately glassy and powdery, the accumulated residue of millennia of this slow hydrological meditation. You touch a wall and your fingers come away with a substance between salt and dried tears.

---

The vegetation—sparse, at 0.9—exists only in the most marginal, tenacious forms. Crusts of something photosynthetic (or *para*-photosynthetic, given the light situation) cling to vertical surfaces where water tracks down, forming maps of green-black hieroglyphy that shift slowly, growth-rates measured in decades. In the wider plazas, cupped depressions hold gardens of crystal: not ice but mineral accretions that perform some analogous function, that participate in cycles of precipitation and dissolution, that the eye keeps trying to read as life because they *behave* like life in their patience, their persistence, their chemical conversations with the wet stone around them.

The temperature is hot, 2.6, but it is a *thick* heat, not the dry baking of deserts nor the oppressive mugginess of tropical coasts. It is the heat of metabolism without metabolism, of a body that maintains temperature without heartbeat. The air moves in slow, purposeful convection, rising from certain buildings, sinking toward others, and you realize after watching long enough that the city is *sorting* itself thermally, that the heat distribution is patterned, almost *intentional*, though no intention could possibly reside here.

---

This is the built environment: urban, at 2.3, dense with infrastructure, yet empty of builders. The architecture is layered, accretive, *geological* in its patience. Streets have been raised upon streets, not by human hands but by some process of upward seepage, the slow accumulation of soluble minerals depositing, crystallizing, sublimating and reforming until the original plan—if there was one—has been translated upward by thirty meters, by fifty, the older levels preserved as cavities and catacombs where the water concentrates and strange acoustics prevail. The current surface is a maze of shallow canals and raised walkways, the canals carrying not current but *potential*, water that seems to orient toward the denser structures as if magnetized.

The buildings themselves: they are not made of anything you can identify. Not stone, though stone-like. Not ceramic, though fired. They have the layered quality of material that has been *deposited* rather than assembled—growth rings of city, annual accretions of something that precipitated from the saturated atmosphere and the weeping bedrock. Cornices and cornices of it, statuary that is not carved but *excluded*, negative spaces where something dissolved slower than its surroundings, leaving behind figures that suggest without depicting, that hint at the posture of worship or the architecture of governance without ever resolving into readable narrative.

The density is sparse—0.9—and this is crucial to the city's character. The structures stand far apart, connected by those shallow water-ways, visible to each other across distances that would be parkland or plaza in a human city. The emptiness is not abandonment but *protocol*. Each building requires its space, its thermal signature, its hydrological halo. They are like machines spaced for ventilation, like organs in a body that has grown large enough to make their separations visible. You walk for an hour from one major structure to the next, and the walking is through shallow water that never quite reaches your ankles, past those crusts of para-vegetation, beneath the directionless darkness, and the building when you reach it is always larger than it appeared, its details emerging from the general dim only reluctantly, as if visibility itself were a kind of intimacy it extends only to the patient.

---

The erosion parameter is 2.1: established, settled-form, long-existed, in-balance. This is perhaps the most unsettling quality. Nothing here is decaying, nothing is being born. The city has achieved a steady-state that seems to exclude time itself. The water flows but does not change the channels. The mineral deposits grow but do not alter the forms. I found a corner where a building's edge had apparently *unfolded* slowly, over centuries, into a more stable configuration, the original sharp angle now a smooth curve that water finds easier to sheet across, and the curve is clean, unworn, *chosen* rather than suffered. The erosion here is not loss but *conversation*—material negotiating with physics and arriving at agreements that serve both.

And the scale: open, regional, extending past the immediate. You can see the city continue in all directions that the darkness permits. There are larger structures visible as dim masses, towers or tumuli or something else entirely, and closer ones that resolve into the individual buildings I've described, and the relation between these scales is *fractal*, self-similar, the same patterns of water-channel and thermal vent and mineral accretion repeating at sizes that suggest the whole might continue upward and downward beyond perception. The city is a valley, and a town within the valley, and a building within the town, and the distinction between these scales has become as advisory as the distinction between water and land.

---

There is no fauna. 0.2, effectively none. No animals, no insects, no microbial swarm detectable even in the water you cup in your hands. The ecology runs on other currencies: thermal, chemical, mineralogical. The para-vegetation I described does not eat or photosynthesize in any biological sense; it *participates* in gradients, grows where temperature differentials and mineral saturation permit a certain complexity of crystal structure, complex enough to maintain itself against simpler arrangements, complex enough to be *interesting* to whatever thermodynamic god this city worships in its steady-state prayers.

The sound: 0.6, mostly quiet. But not silent. There is the continuous low whisper of water moving, not in any hurry, seeking nothing, simply being the medium through which thermal equilibrium slowly negotiates itself. There are sounds of crystal stress, frequencies below hearing that you feel in your sternum, the city adjusting to temperature changes too subtle to measure. There is the sound of your own footsteps, amplified by the water, returned by the structures with an acoustic generosity that seems almost *polite*, the city acknowledging your presence without quite addressing you. And occasionally, in the larger plazas, a sound like *breathing*—but analysis shows it is the thermal contraction of massive structures, ton-scale mineral lungs inhaling the heat of day (though there is no day) and exhaling into the cooler (though not cool) darkness (though there is no light to dark).

---

Weirdness: 2.8, reality breakdown. This is the parameter that governs all others, that explains why I have described a city without builders, a heat without source, a light without photons, an ecology without life. The reality here is *compromised* in ways that do not resolve into any single interpretation.

Time runs in loops, but not personal loops—you will not relive your arrival. Rather, the city itself seems to occupy multiple temporal phases simultaneously. Some buildings appear freshly deposited, their mineral surfaces still resolving, their water-ways still seeking equilibrium. Others show the patience of geological time, forms that could only emerge over epochs of steady-state negotiation. You walk between these and the transition is seamless, the city never declaring which when, the question perhaps meaningless.

Causality is similarly soft. I have watched water run *uphill* in certain channels, not flowing but *welling*, seeking higher energy states as if the gradient were reversed, as if entropy were locally negated. The para-vegetation shifts, over hours of observation, into configurations that imply growth from future to past, roots seeking moisture that has not yet arrived, that arrives because the roots sought it. The city is *retrocausal*, or causally extended, or simply operating in a physics where the distinction between before and after is another advisory distinction, honored in some zones and ignored in others.

And the light, that 0.2 darkness. I have come to believe that what we see here is not light but *information*—the possibility of differentiation between surface and depth, between near and far, preserved and transmitted without electromagnetic mediation. The eye receives structure directly, somehow, the way a tongue receives taste, the way skin receives pressure. We do not see the city; we *apprehend* it, in a mode that uses visual cortex but bypasses retina, that achieves the function of vision through some other means that the reality breakdown permits, that the reality breakdown perhaps *requires*, light being too fragile a medium for what needs to be conveyed.

---

The dynamic parameter: 2.0, active. Despite the steady-state, despite the absence of life, despite the seeming stillness, the city is *in motion*. The thermal convection I described is one process among many. The mineral accretion continues, never achieving final form, always negotiating. The water courses shift subtly, channels deepening or shallowing according to pressures that are themselves in slow flux. The para-vegetation grows, in its crystal way, and is periodically disrupted by events I cannot predict—thermal pulses from deep structures, mineral phase-changes that propagate as waves through the saturated stone. The city *breathes*, I keep returning to this, though it has no lungs, because the motion is respiratory, cyclic, maintaining rather than progressing.

And there are larger cycles, detectable only over days of observation, or over the impossible timescales that the city's temporal ambiguity permits. Structures that seem stable will, over some threshold, *reconfigure*—not collapse, not transform, but *settle into a different equilibrium*, the way a cat rearranges itself on a cushion, the way a dreaming brain shifts between patterns without waking. I have watched a plaza become a building, the open space slowly gaining accretive walls, the water-channeling adapting, over what I experienced as a single sleepless period but which the city's state implied was decades, was moments, was both in superposition.

---

The technology parameter: 0.4, effectively none. No tools, no devices, no evidence of making. Yet the city is profoundly *organized*, profoundly *constructed*. This is the final paradox, the one that holds all others. The infrastructure is dense, layered, purposeful—but nothing here was made, nothing was built, nothing was designed. The city *grew*, or *precipitated*, or *became*, in a process that achieved all the characteristics of urban organization without any of the characteristics of human (or alien, or any) intentionality.

It is, perhaps, what technology looks like when it has become so deeply embedded in physical process as to be indistinguishable from nature. Or what nature looks like when it has achieved such organizational complexity as to be indistinguishable from technology. The city of Veth A'kul does not resolve this distinction; it *performs* it, continuously, as one more steady-state, one more equilibrium it maintains against the entropic pressure of simpler explanations.

You asked for a landscape. I have given you a city, because the parameters demanded both: urban built environment at 2.3, yet no technology; low elevation, hot, wet, sparse in life yet dense in infrastructure; active yet steady, eroded yet preserved, dark yet visible, real yet broken. Veth A'kul holds these contradictions not by resolving them but by existing in a condition where they are not contradictions, where the categories themselves have become fluid as its water, advisory as its boundaries, persistent as its mineral patience.

I have not described how to leave, because the parameters do not suggest departure is possible, or relevant, or meaningful in the city's temporally extended causality. Perhaps you have always been here. Perhaps I am writing this from within, the description itself a mineral accretion, a para-vegetative growth on the walls of some vast structure, participating in gradients I cannot perceive, becoming visible to eyes that do not use light, in a darkness that is not absence but the body's own memory of dawn, preserved in wet stone, breathing without lungs, waiting without expectation, being without becoming.
