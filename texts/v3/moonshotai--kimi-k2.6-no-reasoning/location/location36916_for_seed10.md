The Sky Gutters

In the far south, where continental plates have long since ceased their muttering and settled into the profound sleep of three billion years, there exists a plateau so ancient that it no longer remembers being mountain, being seabed, being anything other than *flat*. The Great Digestion, the locals call it in languages that have no speakers left—only wind instruments the size of cathedrals that the plateau itself has grown, or exhaled, or dreamed into being.

You approach through layers of increasing wrongness. First the vegetation: not that plants cover the ground, but that plants *are* the ground, and the sky, and the architecture of perception itself. Two-point-seven on the scale of vegetative dominance does not begin to capture it. This is total plant substrate, plant-as-world. The plateau breathes with *Brassica aeterna*, a species that exists in no taxonomy because it invents itself faster than human observation can track. Its closest analogues would be somewhere between cauliflower and neural tissue, between old-growth forest and crystalline mathematics. The Brassica forms towers, then dissolves them. It builds lattices that function as lungs for square kilometers of territory, then reabsorbs them when the local atmospheric chemistry shifts. You are not walking on soil. You are walking on the condensed, semi-permeable dreams of something that photosynthesizes at frequencies not yet named.

The wetness seeps up through this living substrate without ever quite becoming visible. Water at 2.4 here means *potential water*, *memory of water*, water that has learned to exist as vapor so intimate with cold that the distinction between mist and ice has become theological. The plateau hovers at 0.5 temperature—cold, yes, but not freezing, never quite freezing, a permanent state of *about to*. Water condenses on the Brassica's fractal surfaces and immediately begins to weep, carving channels that are themselves alive, lined with responsive moss-analogues that alter their permeability based on some chemical argument occurring in roots too deep to measure. Weeping rocks, yes, but the rocks themselves are accreted vegetative mineralization, the long waste product of Brassica metabolism, ancient beyond dating. They weep because the plateau remembers crying.

And it is loud. 1.6 on the sound scale manifests here as the *infrastructure of listening*. Those wind instruments—the cathedral horns, the fluted chambers where entire weather systems are harmonically processed—produce a constant, shifting industrial drone. Tech at 1.8 in a landscape with built structures at 0.5 seems contradictory, and is, and the contradiction is the point. The plateau is industrial without industry, mechanized without machines. The Brassica builds factories of itself, assembly lines of sap and fiber, production so scaled and electrified that lightning storms above the plateau have been observed striking downward *from the ground*, feeding energy back into clouds that exist to be fed. Something here learned to industrialize life itself. Photosynthetic production lines. Respiratory turbines. The hydraulic forces of xylem and phloem amplified through architectures of cellulose until they perform the work that elsewhere requires steel.

The sound is the sound of this living industry in constant operation: the pump and sigh of stomata operating in planetary synchrony, the click-click-click of seed casings that are also pistons, the subharmonic resonance of root systems debating mineral rights across distances of fifty kilometers. And threaded through, the wind instruments, playing something between maintenance tone and mad symphony, the plateau's feedback loop made audible.

Fauna at 2.4 in this system means something other than animals-as-separate. The creatures here have adapted to the weirdness—2.7, reality breakdown, the parameter that warps all others—by becoming *partial*, *distributed*, *ongoing*. There are birds with Brassica chloroplasts in their feathers who spend hours rooted in living substrate, indistinguishable from growth, before taking flight to deposit seeds in instruments that will play them into germination. There are insects that exist as sonic patterns, as standing waves in the industrial drone, only occasionally condensing into visible chitin when the harmonics align. There are mammals—something like foxes, something like deer—whose bodies are less important than their trails, the paths they wear through the living architecture that then persist, that become infrastructure, that the Brassica incorporates and the animals remember even after individual death. Abundant, yes, but abundant as *process*, as *haunting*, as the feedback hum between organism and system.

The erosion at 3.0, ancient beyond ancient, expresses not as wear but as *settledness*. This plateau has reached its final form, and the final form is: becoming. The oldest stable state is constant turbulent recreation. Dynamic at 2.8, chaotic, Solarian, hyperactive—the plateau is an anthill the size of an ecosystem, a bazaar where every transaction involves the trading of chemical potentials across membranes that are also market stalls, also prayer wheels, also the breakdown of what "reality" meant before you arrived.

The light, dim at 1.4, filters through Brassica canopies that are also atmospheric processors, that exist in multiple optical states depending on the observer's chemical state, the local sound frequency, the phase of the industrial cycle. What light remains is old light, light that has been negotiated through layers of living filtration, light that arrives with stories of what it passed through, what it became on the way. Shadows here are not absence but *refractory growth*, slower Brassica, patient capital accumulation in vegetative form.

And the weirdness, the 2.7 reality breakdown, manifests in the final cohesion of all this: the plateau is *not fully there*, in any observer-dependent sense. It exists in superposition with other states, other ecologies, other industrial or pre-industrial or never-industrial possibilities. You may walk for hours and find yourself in the memory of a factory that never existed, processing materials that are concepts, producing goods that are relationships between the living and the needing-to-live. The Brassica does not distinguish between actual and potential. The sound does not distinguish between signal and noise. The water does not distinguish between weeping and being wept.

Dense at 0.6, sparse—the plateau has space. Emptiness is part of its architecture, the gaps between Brassica towers where wind instruments gather to process silence into something the living industry can metabolize. The scale, 2.4, open, regional—you could cross it in a day if it remained still, if it were crossable in that way, if "crossing" maintained its meaning.

None of it is built, at 0.5. All of it is *grown*, *exhaled*, *industrialized through biological process so total that industry and organism have fused into a category that has no name in languages that only separate*.

I have not described the center. I cannot. The center of the Sky Gutters—called that because something here consumes sky, processes atmosphere, weathers the unweatherable—is where the parameters collapse into identity. Where wet is plant is cold is high is ancient is open is sparse is none is industrial is dim is abundant is weird is loud is turbulent, all at once, all ongoing, all *becoming more itself as I write and less possible to finish describing*.

The plateau does not want visitors. It does not not-want them. It processes them, slowly, through architectures of Brassica and sound, through industrial metabolisms older than human tool-use, through the reality breakdown that is simply *more reality than reality can comfortably hold*.

Some who enter do not leave. This is not captivity. It is *employment*. They find function in the living machinery, become partial, distributed, ongoing. Their weeping joins the water parameter. Their growth joins the total vegetation. Their final sounds join the loud industrial drone, playing maintenance tone and mad symphony in the cathedral instruments of a plateau that has finished becoming anything other than itself.
