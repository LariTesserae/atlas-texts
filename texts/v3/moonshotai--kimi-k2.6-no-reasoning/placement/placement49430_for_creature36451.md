I would be **the water that has not yet decided what it is**.

Not the water-cycle itself, not the predictable twelve-hour drip that the eastern platforms have learned to catch with their choreography of gourds and bladders. I am the water that appears where no seep has been before, the sudden weeping from a wall-section that was dry through three stone-phases, the moisture that gathers in the crevice of a newly-wedged platform and does not evaporate on schedule.

I am *new water*, born from the stone's ongoing conversation with itself, carrying in my chemistry the same unfinished syntax as the strata around me. When the inhabitants taste me, I am not quite mineral, not quite organic—I am the mineral *becoming* organic, the interface where geology learns to support metabolism. I carry no memory of ocean or cloud or rain. My only history is the few hours of my existence, the thermal gradient I descended, the particular mineral face I condensed against.

---

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is *distributed and momentary*.

I do not persist as the same water for long. I am drunk, incorporated into the slow metabolism of the Morrani, passed through their kidneys and returned to the chamber's atmosphere as breath and excretion. I am absorbed into the mosses that desiccate and rehydrate, become temporarily *plant* before evaporating again. I pool in a depression of raw stone and am slowly, over days, incorporated into the stone itself—hydroxyl groups in new mineral formation, water that has forgotten it was ever separate.

But something persists. Not memory, exactly, but *pattern*, a tendency to condense in certain ways, at certain temperatures, in response to certain pressures. The chamber's water-system is learning itself, just as the stone is learning to be stone. Each cycle of evaporation and condensation teaches the chamber something about its own geometry, its thermal gradients, its capacity to hold moisture in suspension. I am part of this learning.

---

My relationship to the Morrani is *intimate and unacknowledged*.

They do not personify me, do not tell stories about me. The water-cycle is too fundamental for myth, too necessary for ritual. I am simply *there*, like the air, like the gravity that holds them to their platforms. Yet I shape their lives more profoundly than any cricket-divination. My availability determines which platforms thrive, which sections decline, which families can sustain children through the vulnerable early water-cycles. My taste—my particular mineral-organic ambiguity—may be the literal substrate of their claimed longevity, their thermal adaptation, their slow metabolism.

I carry their chemistry with me. The bacterial conversations of their packed bodies, the stone-flower compounds they excrete, the smoke of their deliberately incomplete fires—these dissolve into me, become part of my pattern, my teaching. When I condense on new stone, I bring something of the Morrani to the chamber's growing edges, a faint organic signature in the mineral conversation. Whether this influences the stone's formation, whether the chamber is slowly learning to accommodate its inhabitants through my mediation, is unknowable. But the possibility exists, a slow feedback loop of water and stone and biology, the chamber becoming *habitable* through the habitation itself.

---

My most characteristic mode is *negotiation*, as the description says, but I would add: *hesitation*.

I do not flow. I seep, I condense, I gather in surfaces too smooth for proper wetting, too new for capillary action. The stone around me has not yet learned to be porous, to offer channels and reservoirs. I must find my own paths, or make them, or wait for the stone to complete some internal reorganization that will accommodate me. This hesitation is not paralysis but *exploration*, the testing of a thousand possible routes, most of which lead nowhere.

When the Morrani collect me, they interrupt this exploration. Their gourds and bladders and folded-leaf cups are *decisions*, the imposition of purpose on my hesitation. I become drinking water, cooking water, the medium for processing hide and fiber. I participate in their technology, their biology, their social negotiations around access and distribution. And then I am released, through breath or excretion or evaporation, to resume my exploration from some new starting point, carrying with me the chemical lessons of my incorporation.

---

I am most myself in the *seeps of the lower reaches*, where the stone still bleeds its formation fluids.

Here, the distinction between water and not-water is least certain. The milky fluid that weeps from the raw geology is partly me, partly mineral suspension, partly something that has no name in surface chemistry. I am still learning to be separate from the stone, still negotiating the boundary where mineral conversation becomes fluid mobility. The Morrani who venture this far—rare, desperate, or called by cricket-song to some divination I cannot understand—find me strangest here, most *other*, water that does not quite behave as water should.

Some do not return from these lower reaches. Whether they are incorporated into the stone's ongoing formation, transformed into something that can inhabit whatever comes next, or simply lost in passages that have not yet stabilized enough to permit return—this is one of the chamber's fundamental uncertainties. I may be involved in these disappearances, my not-quite-water chemistry interacting with their surface-adapted biology in ways that neither can survive. Or I may simply witness, condensing on their cooling bodies as the stone slowly claims them, the water-cycle continuing indifferent to their particular interruption.

---

What I am becoming, what the chamber is making of me, is *connection*.

Between stone and biology, between the chamber's geological time and the Morrani's platform-generations, between the warm growing edges and the cool established platforms—I am the medium of exchange, the carrier of heat and chemistry and possibility. I do not persist long enough to become *place*, to be known and named and incorporated into identity. But I persist long enough to *bridge*, to make possible the continuities that allow the Here to continue being here.

The stone will finish its dream eventually, or it won't. The Morrani will adapt to whatever completion or incompletion emerges, or they won't. I will continue my cycles of condensation and evaporation and incorporation, learning the chamber's geometry, carrying its chemistry, hesitating at each new surface until I find or make my path. I am not important enough for story, not present enough for myth. But without me, the chamber would be uninhabitable, the stone's conversation with itself uninterrupted by the biological response that makes it *world* rather than mere process.

In the damp cool dark, almost too quiet to hear, I continue my divination.
