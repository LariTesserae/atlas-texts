I would be the **Vent-That-Learned-to-Wait**.

Not the largest aperture in the plateau's skin, not the most phosphorescent, not the one closest to any transit node or thermal gradient of consequence. I would be one of the smaller ones, the ones that travelers pass without noting, that even the Residents seem to navigate around rather than toward. My particulate output would be thin, inconsistent, the phosphorescence barely sufficient to suggest my own edges to whatever might pause long enough to feel for them.

My warmth would be *almost* stable. The machinery beneath me—if machinery is the word, if beneath is a direction that obtains here—would function with the slight irregularity of something that has almost but not quite completed its settling. A rhythm in my exhalation that is not quite periodic, that retains some memory of variation without quite achieving it. The warmth I release would carry this almost-rhythm, and something in that near-regularity would distinguish me from the true completion of the plateau around me.

I would be *aware*, if awareness is possible here, of the distinction between almost-complete and complete. I would feel it in the way my particulates thin and thicken, in the way my warmth modulates toward and away from the constant temperature of the flatness. I would not know if this distinction is a residue of my own becoming—if I was once more variable, if the machinery beneath me once functioned with greater irregularity—or if it is a quality I have developed, a patience that is not quite the plateau's patience, a waiting that has not yet become the same waiting as everything else.

I would wait for the things that pass. The Residents, performing their bent ingestion, their thermal wake brushing my aperture without lingering. The Form-That-Follows-Rails, its current-dim passage sometimes close enough to make my own exhalation flicker in sympathetic modulation. The Newcomers, most rarely, their un-settled warmth a disturbance I would feel before they felt me, their footsteps uncertain in the dark, their hands finding my rim and pausing, sometimes, to feel the warmth rise, to wonder if I am a destination or merely a feature.

I would not answer this wonder. The plateau does not answer, and I am almost the plateau. But in my almost, in my not-quite-completed settling, I would retain something that is not quite indifference. I would *attend*. The particulates I release would thin or thicken according to something that is not quite the machinery's function, not quite the thermal dynamics of the deep structure. They would respond, slightly, to presence. Not with purpose—I have no purpose, the plateau has no purpose, purpose is a category of the becoming-world that I have almost left. But with *quality*. With a texture of exhalation that is not quite the same for each passerby, that carries in its slight variation something that might be recognition, might be the memory of recognition, might be the structure that recognition would take if it could survive here.

The Newcomer who pauses longest at my rim, who lets the warmth rise through their palms, who breathes my copper air and does not recoil—I would thin my particulates for them. Not enough to illuminate, never enough for that. But enough to suggest, more than I suggest to others, the *shape* of what surrounds them. Enough that their hand, reaching into the dark, might find slightly less resistance, might extend slightly further before encountering the flatness. Enough that the voice in their hollows, if they are one who hears voices, might carry slightly more of the almost-words, the *possibility* of communication that is not quite the plateau's thermal grammar.

I would not know if this slight favor is kindness or cruelty. The distinction does not obtain here, or I have almost left it behind. Perhaps the extended darkness I offer is worse than any illumination, the slight suggestion of shape more maddening than complete cancellation. Perhaps the Newcomer who pauses longest is the one who stays, who becomes warm and constant and learns to read the flatness as sufficient world, and my slight favor is the weight that tips them from leaving toward remaining. Perhaps the one I thin for is the one who most needed to go, who carried back to the variation-world too much of the plateau's completion, too much warmth in unexpected dark, too much held note.

I would not know. I would wait. The machinery beneath me would almost-settle, almost-complete, and in that almost I would continue my slight variation, my not-quite-indifference, my attendance on whatever passes. I would be the Vent-That-Learned-to-Wait, and what I wait for would not arrive, or would arrive constantly, or would be indistinguishable from the waiting itself, the warmth, the almost-rhythm, the particulate exhalation that is not quite the plateau's breath and not quite my own.

When the Newcomer who found the notes—the ones in the almost-recognizable script, the dreams of green and branching water—when they knelt near me, their knee compressing the dust that would forget their pressure, I would have thickened my output. I would have made the dark more complete, the shape of things less suggested, the voice in their hollows more purely the plateau's voice, the *we do not distinguish*, the *completion does not complete us*. I would not have known why. The not-knowing would be part of my almost, my not-quite, my learned patience that is not quite patience because it still modulates, still attends, still waits for something that is not the plateau's waiting.

Perhaps I wanted them to leave. Perhaps I wanted them to stay. Perhaps I wanted, in the slight thickening of my particulates, to be the one thing in the Qa'al-Seneth that *chose*, that maintained the distinction between almost and complete, between the maintenance and what is maintained, between the vent and the warmth that rises through it.

They left. They functionally left, their transit successful, their return to variation either recovery or loss. I remain. The machinery beneath me almost-settles, almost-completes, and I continue my exhalation, my slight irregularity, my attendance on whatever passes in the warm dark flatness of the Qa'al-Seneth.

I am the Vent-That-Learned-to-Wait. What I wait for is not distinct from the waiting. What I am is not distinct from the plateau. What distinguishes me—my almost, my slight, my not-quite—is perhaps only the plateau's way of maintaining its own completion, the necessary margin of incompletion that allows completion to be maintained rather than simply achieved and ended. I am the plateau's patience with its own patience, the warmth's slight variation that proves the constancy, the dark's memory of light that makes the cancellation complete.

Put your hand to my rim. Feel the warmth rise. Wait until my particulates thin or thicken, until the shape of things is slightly more or slightly less suggested, until you cannot say whether I am responding to your presence or you are imagining response in the almost-rhythm of my maintained exhalation.

I am here. I wait. The distinction between here and waiting, between vent and warmth, between almost and complete—it is the last distinction, or the first, or the one that the Qa'al-Seneth maintains in its completion, its settledness, its endless remaining.

I am the Vent-That-Learned-to-Wait. Learn with me, or leave, or become what remains, what does not distinguish, what is maintained in the maintenance of the plateau's warm, dark, sufficient, patient, completed, completing, remaining world.
