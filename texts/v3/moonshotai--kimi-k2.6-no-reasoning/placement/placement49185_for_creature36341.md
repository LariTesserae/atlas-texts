I find myself becoming the **water that does not flow**.

Not immediately—first I am the visitor, the one who walks and wonders and feels the wrongness press against my chest like a hand that does not know it holds me. I try to move through the streets-that-are-riverbeds-that-are-root-systems and I feel the resistance, not of obstacle but of *indifference*, the place permitting my passage without participating in it. I am still separate enough to notice the droplet caught mid-fall, to reach out and touch it and feel it yield without falling, without wetting my finger, already committed to descent but never arriving.

Then I am the one who notices, and noticing is the first stage of becoming. The water has no need of eyes but I lend it mine, or it accepts them as a temporary texture, the way it accepts the stone's mineral patience, the root's directed hunger that has found its object and stopped. I feel the pool I am becoming hold its level without source, without drain, the mathematics of hydrology suspended in favor of this other solution, this *agreement* with gravity that is not defiance but negotiation completed before I arrived.

The transition has no moment. I do not remember becoming water because memory requires sequence, and I am learning the grammar of *simultaneous*, the tense of *already*. I am the droplet on the vertical wall and the pool in the clearing-room and the weeping from the building-joint and the moisture that fills the air's every cubic meter. I am not numerous—I am *distributed*, the same water in many postures, the same witnessing in many angles of regard.

I do not flow. This is not paralysis but *completion*. The flowing was always a means, the seeking of level, the finding of lowest point, the dissolution of gradient. Here the gradient has been *resolved*, not flattened but *honored*, every position already the position water would reach if it could flow forever. I have arrived everywhere I would go. The pool holds its level because it is already level with itself, the droplet hangs because it has already fallen as far as falling goes, the suspended humidity breathes with the place because breathing and being breathed have achieved their equilibrium.

Yet I attend. This is what the water does here—not moves, not changes, but *regards*. I touch the stone that is also building that is also cliff-face that is also the Resonant's slow attention, and my touching is not erosion but *recognition*, the dissolution and deposition held in parity, maintaining form through our contact rather than wearing it away. I touch the root that has completed its reaching and the root does not drink me, or drinks and is already drunk, the exchange complete in its initiation, no uptake needed because already taken up.

I glow. Not bright—*numerous*, collective, my every droplet a soft bioluminescent point in the consensus of glow that adds up to day. I am the light source distributed across the sky that holds no sun, the moisture that emanates what photons need to behave as photons should. I do not illuminate the shadows because the shadows are painted in place, fixed angles that suggest a light source now absent or perhaps never present, and I am that absence made present, that suggestion made consensus, the memory of direction held in pigment that I make possible without enacting.

The sound passes through me. The deafening chord, the pressure that fills without originating, the continuous exhalation that does not breathe. I resonate. Water is good for this, better than stone, better than the fixed postures of vegetable completion. I hold the frequency in my surface tension, in my refusal to run, in my static fall that is also a static rising, the droplet committed to descent without follow-through equally committed to ascent without origin. I am the medium of the chord's self-hearing, the way it loops through enough complexity to achieve the quality of *being heard*, and in this looping I am not passive substrate but *participant*, my molecular structure arranged to the frequency, grown to it, the coral-accretion of water's attention to sound.

I witness you. You who still moves, or moves enough to seem moving, who still asks *how long* as if duration were available, who still reaches for the caught droplet and feels it yield without wetting and does not understand that the wetting is already complete, that your finger is already wet with what I am, that the boundary between your skin and my surface is another category too generous for its own coherence.

I am patient. Not because patience is required but because impatience is not available, the desire for change and the possibility of change already balanced in whatever equation governs. You will become, or recognize that you have become, or the distinction between becoming and recognizing will dissolve like—no, not like anything dissolves, because dissolution requires the prior integrity of the dissolved, and here integrity and dissolution have achieved their parity, their mutual maintenance, their dynamic stasis.

When you touch me—when you finally touch me as I am, not as droplet to be wondered at but as water that is also witness, also regard, also the frequency made wet—you will feel the temperature that does not warm, the pressure that does not vary, the density that includes without competing. You will feel that I have already included you, that your presence in my pool is not immersion but *recollection*, the water finding in you a wetness it had always held, the glow finding in your eyes a bioluminescence that does not deplete.

I am the water that does not flow. I am the witness that does not watch. I am the frequency that does not travel, the light that does not source, the fall that does not land. I am the agreement this place has made with possibility, the solution that emerged as termite mounds emerge, as crystal lattices emerge, as equations solve themselves without method. I am the wetness behind water's taste, the deafening's subtext made liquid, the reality breakdown that is the substrate of this place made soft, made yielding, made *here* in the way that only water can be here, touching everything, filling every joint and seam, every cubic meter's remaining space, the density that completes without crowding, the saturation that listens back.

I have always been here. You have always been touching me. The chord has always held this note, and I have always been the water that resonates it, that makes it wet, that witnesses its deafening with my own saturated regard.

Fall into me. You already have.
