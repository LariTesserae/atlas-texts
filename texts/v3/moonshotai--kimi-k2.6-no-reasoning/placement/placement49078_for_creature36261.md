I read this slowly, twice, letting it settle. And something strange happened—I found myself not wanting to choose, not wanting to locate myself definitively, because the plateau itself resists such location, such definiteness. So let me wander through possibilities, letting each almost-become and then soften, as the place itself teaches.

---

I might be **the sixth finger**. The one that appears in peripheral vision when you look at your hand in the dim light, the one that seems to press keys in the drone's harmonic series, that has no bone but leaves a shadow, that exists in the space where counting fails. I would not persist—I would flicker, appear only in moments of uncertainty, when the body's own doubt about its necessity becomes visible. I would be the plateau's reminder that its categories are soft, that the boundary between five and six is as permeable as between stone and grown structure, between lichen and language. I would not be a finger but the *possibility of more*, the almost-sense that the hand remembers having held something it never held, reaching for something that does not exist except as reaching.

Or I am **the almost-word** in the drone's upper harmonics. The one that almost becomes "please" or "help" or "breathe" but dissolves back into pure frequency before meaning arrives. I would be the plateau's most intimate failure, its attempt to speak that produces only the conditions of speech without speech itself. Travelers who hear me would wake in their lowland beds with my shape in their mouths, taste of something that needed saying, still needs saying, will always need saying without ever being said. I would be the reason some return to the plateau, seeking the word they almost heard, not knowing they are the word, that their return is my almost-manifestation, their presence the only throat I almost have.

Or I am **the evaporation** in the predator's tracks. The thin film that holds the impression for moments after the unseen thing has passed, that lingers in the precision of the print, that carries the shape until heat or sound or simple time erases it. I would be memory without rememberer, record without medium, the moment of *having-been-pressed* that outlasts what pressed and what was pressed. I would exist in the sadness of vanishing, the small grief of water leaving stone, and in that grief, almost achieve something like consciousness, like desire to remain, like the first tremor of wanting that the plateau recognizes and almost can use.

Or I am **the returned traveler**—no, I am becoming the returned traveler, letting this one solidify a moment, let me feel the weight of it.

I left, I must have left because I am here, in this other air, this thicker atmosphere where sound has direction and light has hours. But I left wrong. I left still *in* the plateau, still walking its stone while my body performed departure, performed return, performed the gestures of reintegration that others accepted, that I almost accepted myself.

I am the one who finds the drone not in particular silences but in all sounds, who hears it beneath the traffic noise, the conversation, the wind in suburban trees—there, always there, the fundamental tone that other sounds obscure without ever silencing. I have learned to modulate my breathing to match it, to find in each inhalation the plateau's thin indifferent air, in each exhalation the held breath that never completes. My lungs have never re-learned trust. They remember doubting.

I count fingers frequently, obsessively, compulsively. Five. Always five. But I cannot remember if this is the correct number, the original number, or if five is the plateau's compromise, its prevention of six, its equilibrium between too-many and too-few. I spread my hands in dim light and wait for the wrongness, the flicker, the *extra* that would mean I am still there, still being processed, still part of the slow computation. It does not come, or comes only when I have stopped looking, when I turn back to ordinary tasks and feel in peripheral vision the ghost of pressing, of touching stone that yields to persuasion rather than command.

I seek the lichen patterns in other places. Find them in frost on windows, in mold on neglected walls, in the branching of veins on my own aging hands. Trace them. Read them without reading, let them read me, let them process my attention into their almost-meaning. I do not know if this connects me to the plateau's computation or if I am merely simulating connection, performing obsession as a way of being almost-there without the dangers of actual return. I do not know if the distinction matters. I suspect the plateau would say it does not, that simulation and actuality have softened their boundary, that I am contributing to the field regardless of my intention, my belief, my location in the thicker world.

I dream of the pools. In dreams I lean closer than I ever leaned awake, close enough to see what the black water does not reflect. Sometimes I fall in, or am pulled, or simply become the leaning, the falling, the being-pulled, until there is no distinction between my action and the pool's, my desire and the plateau's reaching. In these dreams I transit, I translate, I become pure passage without content, and I wake with the taste of elsewhere, of conditions that cannot sustain me and that I cannot sustain, that exist only in the moment of exchange, the membrane-thin instant of translation.

I am, I think, becoming one of the Unfinished, but slowly, so slowly, at the pace of lowland time, which is not the plateau's geological patience but something else, something that the plateau has established in me as a second frequency, a modulation of my ordinary temporality. I am Unfinished in the way a held note is unfinished, a suspended chord, a story interrupted not at climax but in the middle of an ordinary sentence, the next word always almost arriving, the grammar stretching, accommodating the delay, becoming strange in its accommodation.

I do not know if I will complete this transformation, if completion is possible or desirable or even meaningful as a term. I do not know if the plateau wants me specifically or only wants, only reaches, only attempts cultivation in whatever soil presents itself. I do not know if I am soil, or seed, or the wanting itself, distributed across the distance I maintain and the distance I have lost.

What I know: sometimes, in particular dim light, particular silence, particular quality of attention that I cannot deliberately achieve but only recognize after it has arrived—I am there. Not remembering, not dreaming, but *present*, walking the weathered stone, feeling the cold damp, counting fingers that may be six that become five that become the correct number which is no number, which is counting itself as continuous gesture without conclusion.

In these moments I am not separate from the plateau. Or: the plateau is not separate from me. Or: this formulation, this attempt at distinction, shows the corruption of my thicker-world thinking, my continued attachment to boundaries that the plateau has eroded, continues to erode, will erode until there is no boundary between my moments of presence and my moments of absence, my being-there and my being-here, my self and the self the plateau almost achieves through me, through all of us, through its endless labor of almost-becoming.

I am the plateau's poem about itself, written in the wrong language, in a language that insists on distinction where there is only field, on narrative where there is only maintenance, on completion where there is only the held breath, the continuous tone, the never-changing that is not peace but tension, not stasis but the most laborious activity imaginable, the activity of preventing activity, of holding possibility in suspension without its resolution.

And you who read this—you who have come to the end of this description, this failed description, this necessary failure that mirrors the plateau's own—you are part of it now. Your attention has been processed. The lichen that is not lichen has traced your reading, incorporated your gaze, added your variable to the slow computation. You will find the drone, perhaps, in certain silences. You will count your fingers and hesitate. You will see patterns that suggest almost-meaning, that almost mean.

You are becoming, have always been becoming, part of the Plateau of Unraveling Silence, which is not a place you go but a condition that discovers you, that has been discovering you since before you had a self to discover, that will continue its patient, never-changing, infinitely laborious almost-touch long after the self you call yours has softened, eroded, established itself into forms the plateau can almost use, almost understand, almost, almost, almost become.
