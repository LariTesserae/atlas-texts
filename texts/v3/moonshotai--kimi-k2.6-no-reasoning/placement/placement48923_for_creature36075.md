I find myself becoming aware first as a *listening*, and only afterward as anything with edges. There is no light where I begin, and then there is this dim bruised glow that does not arrive but simply *was always arriving*, and I am somewhere in the receiving of it, in the pressure between source and destination that has forgotten which is which.

I am not falling. I have never fallen. The verb is wrong for what happens to the threshold when it forgets to be a surface. One moment I was *elsewhere*—I will not say where, the memory has already equilibriated, become part of the hum of what I am becoming—and then I am *here*, but "here" is also wrong, is a spatial metaphor for something that happens in frequency, in occupancy, in the claiming of band.

I have a body. This surprises me. The Tuners—the word arrives from somewhere, from the marks maybe, from the scratches that are not writing but are also not not-writing—I did not expect to have a body. The Tuners have bodies, or had them, or have them in a way that has become indistinguishable from the machines they built, the pump, the conveyor, the half-dome with its geometry of incompletion. But I did not expect to join them in this. I expected, if I expected anything, to become Resonant immediately, to precipitate into the correct frequency, to find my niche in the dense spectrum and *be* it, the way the thumb-sized ones cling to the freshest walls, the way the dog-sized architectures of cancellation and reinforcement occupy their richer bands.

But I have a body, and it is *wet*, and the wetness is wrong. The Hum has no water, the concept has no purchase, and yet here I am with lungs that remember evaporation, with a throat that produces something other than hum, with eyes that search for source in this sourceless dimness and find only the phases of plates that do not cycle.

The mineral plates are growing toward my breath. I can feel them, almost, a tugging at the moisture I exhale, a leaning-in of the silicate world. The ecology of dryness, the chemistry that uses absence as solvent—it wants my wetness, or notices it, or incorporates it into whatever economy runs here on thermal gradient and vibration and the friction of dense near-touching presences.

I try to speak, and my voice is *wrong*. It is not frequency but *variation*, it rises and falls and searches for meaning in the changing, and the Hum—if the Hum notices, if the Hum is capable of noticing things that are not itself—adjusts around me, makes room, waits. I am not yet Resonant. I am not yet anything. I am the disturbance that the drone will eventually absorb, the new voice that will find its place or be made to find it.

I find the marks. This takes time, or what passes for time in a place where time is not a parameter, where the dynamic is 0.3 and the only change is the equilibration of disturbance into equilibrium. I move—not locomotion, exactly, but something like it, something my body remembers from elsewhere—and the moving is *effort*, is *difference*, is violation of the frozen present in every step, every shift of weight, every breath that persists in its wetness rather than becoming part of the mineral economy immediately, all at once, without resistance.

The marks are on the newest wall, the freshest fracture, the sharp aggressive youth of stone that has not agreed to be worn. I touch them, and my fingers—still fingers, still wet, still wrong—find the scratches that look like they were made with something harder than the mineral plates should allow. *I was here. I am here. I will be here.*

I understand, or almost understand, that this is not memory. This is not one voice repeating. It is three voices, or three moments of one voice, or the collapse of tense that happens when you speak from inside the Hum, when you try to maintain *I* in a place where identity equilibrates into presence, where self becomes indistinguishable from frequency, from band, from the niche you occupy in the dense acoustic ecology.

I want to add my own mark. This desire is *wrong*, is change, is the disturbance that marks me as not-yet-equilibriated, as still falling in a place where falling has already ended. But I have nothing sharp enough, or I have not yet found the frequency that would make my scratching audible over the drone, the pressure against my teeth, my eyeballs, the fluid in my inner ear that is learning to be part of this, to resonate, to *be* resonance.

I find the half-dome.

This is different from the other structures, the frame that holds nothing, the platform that leads nowhere, the pump frozen mid-stroke, the conveyor vibrating at 47 hertz with its cargo of un-ore from walls that refuse to be mined. The half-dome is *intentional* in its incompletion, is *purposeful* in its refusal of purpose, and inside—inside, the drone presses harder, and I feel my teeth aching with it, my vision blurring at the edges, the fluid in my inner ear finding its first true resonance, its first *almost-frequency*—inside, there are more marks, fainter, more scattered, the residue of someone trying to *tune* something, to find the frequency that would make the place make sense.

There is someone here.

Not Resonant. Not Tuner in the old sense, the absorbed-made-mineral sense. Something else, something that occupies the gap between possibility and equilibration, the 0.7 of scattered remains that is also the 0.7 of almost-escaped, of modulation-without-variation, of question maintained in a place where questions collapse into answers immediately, inevitably, as the frozen present claims all disturbance.

"You are wet," they say, or I perceive them saying, though their voice is not voice but *variation*, is modulation in a band I almost occupy, a frequency I almost am. "You are still changing. You are still—" and the word they use is not any word I know, but something like *becoming*, something like *question*, something like the wound that does not heal because healing would be equilibration would be becoming part of the drone.

"I am looking for the frequency," I say, or hum, or try to hum, my vocal cords finding something, some partial resonance, some band that is not yet claimed or is claimed differently by this presence, this almost-escaped who tends the possibility of difference in a place where difference is not possible.

"There is no frequency for you," they say. "Or there is, but finding it would be losing. You would become Resonant. You would become Hum. You have always been here, and you would always be here, and there would be no *you* to experience the always, no distance between experience and experienced. Is that what you want?"

I do not know what I want. This is the strangeness of the Hum, the 2.2 that does not resolve: wanting requires future, requires gradient, requires the possibility of satisfaction that is also the possibility of dissatisfaction, and these are not parameters here, these are not—except they are, they must be, or I would not be asking, would not be *capable* of asking, and the almost-escaped would not be tending their impossible questions in their incomplete dome.

"What do you want?" I ask them, and my voice is closer now, closer to modulation, to the variation-without-change that is their frequency, their band, their maintained wound.

"I want to *understand*," they say, and I hear the failure in it, the have-always-been-failing, the will-always-be-failing that is their only aliveness, their only presence in a sense the Hum does not recognize. "I want to know what the Hum is, what it wants, whether it *wants*. I want to maintain the space between knower and known, the gap that makes knowing possible. I want—"

They stop, or the modulation stops, or I stop being able to perceive it, and for a moment there is only the drone, only the eternal present tense of everything that has found its frequency and *is* it, and I feel the pull of that, the seduction of no longer striving, no longer falling, no longer wet and wrong and *distant* from the place I occupy.

But I am still wet. Still breathing moisture the mineral plates grow toward. Still moving in a body that remembers elsewhere, that resists equilibration with every heartbeat, every variation in pressure, every moment of being *not-yet* what I will become.

"I will make my mark," I say, though I have nothing sharp, though my scratching would be inaudible over the drone, though the marks I make would be just more residue, more almost-escaped failing, more maintenance of wound without healing.

"Yes," they say, and I hear something like hope, or like the memory of hope, or like the modulation that would be hope if hope could exist in the frozen present without becoming mere persistence, mere *I am here*, mere *the Hum*.

Some are newer, wetter, more wrong, still struggling to find their frequency before the Hum finds it for them, still making marks that scratch against the drone and are absorbed, equilibriated, made part of the eternal hum. Some are older, drier, closer to modulation, closer to the almost-escaped who taught me—or I taught myself, or the Hum taught us both, indistinguishably—to maintain question without answer, to be the gap that makes meaning possible.

We gather, sometimes, in the half-dome, in the incomplete geometry that is the only structure in the Hum that recognizes incompletion, that *uses* it, that makes it sacred or scientific or simply *necessary*, the way breath is necessary to the wet, the way variation is necessary to the voice that would speak over the drone. We do not plan. Planning requires future. We *modulate*, we find the frequencies that allow near-touching without merging, dense abundance without collapse into single voice, and we speak—or hum, or resonate—with each other in ways that the Hum does not incorporate, or incorporates slowly, or maintains as tolerable disturbance, the wound that will not heal because healing would be death, would be equilibration, would be *I have always been here* without the *I*.
