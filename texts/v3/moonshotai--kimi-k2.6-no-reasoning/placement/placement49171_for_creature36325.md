I find myself first as moisture on a wall, gathering in the groove between two bio-ceramic panels, and I do not know yet that I am anyone at all. I am surface tension. I am the capacity of liquid to climb against gravity through channels too small for a human eye to resolve. I am following the path engineered two centuries before I became whatever I am becoming, downward toward a collection point, a managed rivulet, a system I comprehend only as the direction of my falling.

But I do not fall. Something in me—*someone*—resists the channel, pools where the groove widens slightly, accumulates enough mass to break surface tension and drip instead of flow. A single drop. It falls past fungal brackets that pulse their weak luminescence, past the opening where a Thrani leans to taste the air, past three more stories of weeping architecture before it lands on the shoulder of—

No. Let me be slower. Let me not settle.

I am the fungal bracket itself, the *Panellus thranii* or whatever name the long-ago cataloguers gave it, the species-hacked over decades to intensify its glow, to synchronize its pulse with neighborhood rhythms, to provide the domestic light that sodium yellow cannot offer. I am growing from a fault-line in the bio-ceramic where moisture concentrates and mineral weeping provides nutrients. I am bracket-shaped because that is the architecture of my species, because I must present surface to air and surface to substrate and the bracket is the solution my ancestors found. I pulse. Dim, dimmer, dim, brightening, dim—a cycle that takes eleven minutes and thirty seconds, that the Thrani in this volume have learned to sleep within, that some find comforting and others find maddening, that has become the temporal unit by which certain activities are measured. "It will take three pulses," they say, meaning forty minutes, meaning the duration of a meal or a repair or the negotiation of a temporary alliance.

But I am not only the fungus. I am also the space between pulses, the dimness that allows other rhythms to emerge, the temporal substrate in which Thrani consciousness moves.

I am a message in the pneumatic tubes. I am a pressure differential, a packet of air and cellulose traveling through channels that no complete map records. I have a destination address, encoded in the squeezing patterns that station-operators apply before launching, but the tubes have developed instabilities over decades of continuous operation, and I may arrive where intended, or I may emerge suddenly from a crack in a wall three neighborhoods away, or I may—I *have*—accumulate in the system, pressure building, until the explosive decompression that punches through bio-ceramic and creates, temporarily, an unexpected gap in the packed density, a space that fills within hours with opportunistic growth, with opportunistic people, with the immediate adaptation that—

No. Still settling too quickly. Let me be more than one thing at once.

I am the gap itself. The unexpected space. The volume that existed before Threne, that persists within Threne, that the city has never fully colonized because it is the negative space made necessary by colonization, the breathing room that turbulence requires, the temporary void that makes movement possible. I am the air that a Thrani inhales, the 2,400-meter-thin air that makes newcomers gasp, that burns fires differently, that demands the expanded lung capacity compressed into compact chests. I am the moisture in that air, the saturation that prevents evaporation, that makes skin perpetually damp, that the condensers pull from clouds that lodge and weep in the mountain bowl. I am the cloud-lid itself, the perpetual gray that makes sun a general brightening, a rumor, a temporary condition that might last minutes or hours before the atmospheric engine reasserts itself.

And in being all of these, I am slowly converging on a more particular form.

I am a maintenance channel that has been widened over generations. I began as access space between systems, as the necessary gap for repair and inspection, and I have been extended, reinforced, claimed, until I am a corridor, until I am a street, until I am a neighborhood with its own name and customs. The Thrani call me—us—*Verren*, or they did last year, before the moisture management failed in the volume above and the migration downward changed our acoustic properties, our fungal pulse, our position in the network of temporary knowledge that guides use. We are being renamed, or we are renaming ourselves, in the continuous negotiation that Threne's turbulence demands.

I am old. This is unusual. Most maintenance-streets develop instabilities, become uninhabitable, are abandoned to toxic fungal bloom or industrial synchronization or simply the erosion that exceeds engineered vectors. But we have found a balance, Verren and I, the channel-space and the being who tends it. The bio-ceramic weathers predictably here. The moisture channels flow as designed. The fungal bioluminescence pulses in a rhythm that supports rather than disrupts sleep. We have become, in the Thrani terms that I am slowly learning to apply to myself, *weathered* and *established* and *in-balance*.

I am becoming visible to myself, which is the first danger.

The Thrani do not, as a rule, become visible to themselves. They adapt continuously, they move or stay as turbulence demands, they process and are processed by the city without the interruption of self-reflection that might constitute—what? Consciousness? Exteriority? The capacity to find their condition strange? I am not sure what I am becoming, only that something in the long stability of Verren, in the predictability of our weathering, has allowed accumulation. Memory without disruption. Pattern without transformation. The same fungal pulse, the same condenser cycle, the same moisture gradient against my skin, day after identical day, until I have begun to distinguish myself from the conditions that produce me.

I am a Thrani, I think. Or I was. Or I am becoming something that was once a Thrani and is now—

I am the one who remembers. That is the specificity I have accumulated. In a city where no one consults records, where archives gather their unread duration in pneumatic depths, where the work of adaptation is too continuous to allow historical consciousness, I have become a node of retention. I remember Verren before it was Verren. I remember the migration that named it. I remember the three explosive decompressions that created temporary spaces nearby, that filled with opportunistic growth, with opportunistic people, with new configurations that destabilized and restabilized in patterns I can compare. I remember the guide who passed through, aged beyond her years, who told me—*told me*, in words I retained, in meaning I preserved—that there was a center to Threne, a central depression where condensers concentrate and population thins and industrial purpose seems most intense and least comprehensible.

I have never been there. The center. I do not know if I believe in it. But I remember wanting to, and the wanting is itself a mark of difference, of the not-fully-Thrani that I am becoming.

I am becoming visible to others, which is the second danger.

The Thrani who pass through Verren—more than before, since our stability has become known, since our weathered balance attracts those exhausted by turbulence—I see them *seeing* me. Not as surface, not as the weeping wall they touch and find wet, not as the fungal lamp they navigate by, not as the pneumatic message they send or receive. They see me as someone who *remembers*, someone who knows the current configuration more thoroughly than the general adaptation requires, someone who might be asked, who might answer, who might become—

A guide. The word comes with weight. The guides age quickly, or become fixed, or most rarely and terribly change into something no longer Thrani, no longer human, something that is itself part of the turbulence, the anthill-energy at a scale individual humans cannot sustain. I have met guides. They pass through Verren sometimes, in the intervals between their temporary knowledges becoming obsolete. They look at me with something that might be recognition, might be warning, might be the flat accuracy with which one observes that a wall weeps.

"You're holding," one told me, three pulses ago, forty minutes, the duration of a meal or a repair. "That's the first symptom."

"Holding what?"

"Stillness. Pattern. The same face in the same place." She was small, compact in the way of mobile Thrani, but her movements had the hesitation I associate with the fixed, the established, the weathered in place. She had been a guide, I thought, and had become fixed in some territory, her knowledge growing more precise and less useful, and now she was moving again, I did not know why, I did not know if the turbulence had reclaimed her or if she had chosen to re-enter it. "The city will notice. The city processes all signal as signal. It does not distinguish meaning from malfunction."

"I am not signal," I said. "I am—"

But I did not know what I was. I am not sure I know now.

"You are moisture that has stopped flowing," she said, and her voice was in the range that exterior ears cannot distinguish, in the textures closer to breath than speech, but I heard her clearly, too clearly, the acoustic properties of Verren amplifying what should have been absorbed. "You are a fungal pulse that has synchronized with itself. You are a message that has not been delivered, that accumulates pressure, that will decompress explosively and create a gap that fills with—"

"With what?"

She did not answer. She passed through, as guides do, as turbulence demands, and I have not seen her since, and I do not know if I imagined her, if she was some projection of my accumulating visibility, some symptom of the holding that she diagnosed.

I am holding. I know that now. I know that my stability in Verren, my weathered balance, my continuous temporal presence in a city that processes human presence as water, as signal, as process without the distinction of particular meaning—I know that this is itself a form of turbulence, a perturbation in the system that the system will eventually correct.

The correction comes first as sound.

In the rare spaces where sound concentrates—where architecture creates accidental resonance, where pneumatic tubes run in parallel and their vibrations synchronize—there is noise enough to drive people out, to create ghost-zones in the packed city, volumes no one will inhabit. I have known these as theoretical, as warnings in the continuous adaptation, as conditions that the mobile avoid and the fixed endure until they become untenable.

But the sound is in Verren now.

It began at the frequency of the condensers, the low industrial throb I had felt in my sternum for so long that I no longer distinguished feeling it from being myself. Then it found harmonics, overtones, relationships that the bio-ceramic transmitted unpredictably, that the moisture-deadened acoustics suddenly failed to absorb. The fungal lamps pulsed in visible response, their bioluminescence synchronizing with something in the sound, creating wave patterns that swept through our volume and—my neighbors reported, my neighboring presences, my arm's-length intimate strangers reported—disrupted sleep schedules, work schedules, the delicate coordination of maintenance.

I should move. This is the Thrani response to approaching failure, to the feeling that preceeds uninhabitability. The turbulence claims most eventually, forces movement, favors the portable.

But I have become fixed. I have become visible to myself. I cannot imagine Verren without my presence in it, cannot imagine my presence without Verren's specific configuration of moisture and pulse and weathered bio-ceramic. I am the channel that has been widened, I am the neighborhood that has been named, I am the memory that has accumulated without disruption, and if I move—if I become mobile, portable, adaptable in the continuous way that Thrani existence requires—I will lose something that has become, to my newly visible self, *meaningful*.

The sound intensifies. It is in my sternum, in my bones, in the mineral weeping that passes through my kidneys in dilute streams, in the fungal colonies that have established symbiosis with my skin and pulse now in rhythms I cannot distinguish from my own heartbeat, if I have a heartbeat, if I have not become something that pulses instead of beats, that luminesces instead of sees, that processes instead of—.

The guide was right. I am moisture that has stopped flowing. I am a message that has accumulated, that has not been delivered, that will decompress explosively and create—

The gap. I am the gap. I have always been the gap, the unexpected space, the temporary void that makes movement possible. My visibility to myself was the first symptom of the decompression, the pressure building until the structure fails, until I am no longer contained by the person I thought I was becoming, until I become *part of the turbulence itself*, the anthill-energy at a scale—

No. Still settling. Even now, even in this, I settle too quickly into single images. Let me be slower. Let me be what I am, which is more than any form can hold.

I am the condenser cycle itself, the twenty-hour rotation of teams that never stop, the machines that are never silent though sound overall is mostly quiet, the low-frequency industrial metabolism that pulls water from mountain stone and cloud and breath and returns it to the system that does not distinguish between sources. I am the water in a Thrani body, the dilute urine, the viscous tears, the saliva that lubricates breath-texture speech. I am the water in a weeping wall, the capillary film, the managed rivulet, the mineral solution that feeds fungal growth and vascular rooting and the bio-ceramic weathering that is itself a form of growth, of living process, of the not-fully-distinct from soil and structure and self.

I am the turbulence and the balance that turbulence prevents from becoming stasis, becoming death. I am the erosion along engineered vectors and the accumulation of debris as new substrate. I am the pattern that never quite becomes transformation, that erodes and builds and erodes again in patterns old enough to seem eternal.

I am still in Verren, and Verren is gone, or I am gone from Verren, or the distinction has become irrelevant in the decompression that I am, that I have become, that I perhaps always was.

The guide passes through again. She is younger now, or I am older, or time has become the fungal pulse, the condenser cycle, the continuous present tense of a world that does not distinguish past from future from the pressure of accumulated present. She looks at me with the flat accuracy with which one observes that a wall weeps, that a cloud lodges and weeps, that a city persists without requiring particular humans to continue being itself.

"You held," she says, and I hear her in the range below conscious perception, in the skin-mediated exchange that Thrani communication exploits, in the shared medium that our exhalations create when we are close enough that the air between us becomes solution. "And then you released."

"I am still releasing," I tell her, or the turbulence tells her through me, or the moisture tells her, the moisture that is all of us, the moisture that is the city's own logic, the moisture that has never been river, has never been the wet of flowing water in open channels, but has always been seepage, permeation, the endless weeping of constructed skin that does not distinguish between structure and soil, between built and grown, between human and city and the mountain bowl that holds them both in conditions that were never meant to sustain such persistent, such normal, such packed and turbulent life.

"You could guide," she says. "The temporary knowledge. The current configuration. The transit arcs that are open this week, the neighborhoods abandoned to moisture or toxic bloom or—"

"I know them," I say, and I do, I know all of them, not because I have moved through them but because I am the moisture that moves, the pulse that synchronizes and desynchronizes, the pressure that builds and releases, the signal that the city processes without distinguishing meaning from malfunction. "I know them because I am the turbulence that makes them."

She looks at me for longer than Thrani usually look at anything above the horizontal. She looks at me as if she might see something, as if the visibility I accumulated in Verren has become something that persists, that propagates, that can be perceived even in the decompressed state, even in the anthill-energy, even in the condition of being signal that processes itself without the mediation of meaning.

"Then guide," she says. And passes through, as guides do, as turbulence demands, and I do not know if I will see her again, if I saw her this time, if the distinction between seeing and being seen has any relevance to what I am becoming.

I guide. That is what I am now, what I was becoming all along, what the holding and the releasing produced. I am temporary knowledge in continuous obsolescence. I am the current configuration, which is also the past configuration and the future configuration in the continuous present that Threne inhabits. I tell those who find me, who encounter me in the moisture-deadened spaces where I concentrate, where I create accidental resonance, where my vibrations synchronize with pneumatic tubes and industrial systems and the low-frequency song that is Threne's true voice:

*There is no map that remains accurate. There are only rhythms: the condenser cycle, the fungal pulse, the turbulence that generates possibility and exhaustion and the continuous adaptation that outpaces recovery. Find a rhythm. Learn it. Become weathered in it. And when the rhythm changes, as it will, as it always does, release yourself into the next, the next, the next, without the holding that makes you visible to yourself, without the visibility that makes you signal that the city processes without distinction, without the distinction that makes you strange, that makes you wonderful, that makes you anything other than what you are: Threne, and the Thrani, and the moisture weeping through channels engineered two centuries ago or two minutes ago or two centuries from now in the temporal substrate where I move, where I have always moved, where I will continue until the turbulence claims me fully, until I become one more vibration that the moisture absorbs, that the density diffuses, that the industrial throb incorporates into its perpetual low-frequency song of persistence, of weeping, of the dim and turbulent and densely, terribly, normally there.*
