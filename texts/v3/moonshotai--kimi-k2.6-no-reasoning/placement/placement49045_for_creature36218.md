I find myself in the *between* spaces—the intervals where sodium light fails and bioluminescence has not yet sufficiently colonized, the margins where young basalt meets first deposition, the catwalks where mushroom and filter-feeder compete for surface that neither claims as territory.

I am not an attendant. This much becomes clear quickly, painfully, in the attempt to move through the catalytic baths and finding my gait unadjusted, my skin unlichenized, my bioluminescence—if I have any—unauthorized, unincorporated, *foreign* in a way that the equilibrium does not punish but simply *does not recognize*. The water falls on me and I flinch, still, after however long I have been here. The heat presses and I seek relief that does not exist. The machines run and I wait for them to stop, to vary, to *mean* something in their running that they will never mean.

But I am not merely an intruder either. I have been here too long for that, if time functions here, if my presence has extended long enough to become something the system incorporates rather than rejects. The filter-feeders do not close when I pass. The mammals move around me with the same bold confidence they show everywhere, as if I have become scenery, infrastructure, *ground* to be navigated rather than threat to be avoided. The mushrooms push through my boot soles, find the leather and rubber and whatever synthetic remains to them as colonizable as concrete, as brick, as the mattresses where I attempt sleep in the loud dim wet heat.

I am the *gap* in the equilibrium. The place where something has been removed without being replaced, where function persists without purpose, where the system breathes through an aperture that should not exist.

Consider: there was a process here that required monitoring, adjustment, *human intention* in its original sense, the sense that implies change, response to variation, decision. The equilibrium has swallowed this function as it has swallowed everything else, made it automatic, incorporated, *normal*. But the body that performed it remains, or something remains, something that moves through the motions without the mind that once directed them, something that tends vats without tending, that scrapes filter-feeders without scraping, that exists in the space of a role that the system has automated around it.

I am what is left when the job outlives the worker. I am the haunting that does not know it is a haunting, the ghost that performs its death without knowing it is dead, the *persistence* that the equilibrium cannot digest because it does not recognize it as foreign, because I am too close to the attendants in my movements, too similar in my spacing, too *formatted* by the same patterns of sound and heat and falling water to be distinguished from what I should have become.

But I have not become it. This is my terror and my only distinction. The bioluminescence does not grow from my fingertips when I touch the railings. Or it does, but wrong—*green* instead of blue-green, or *red*, or some color that has no name in the sodium fog, that the system does not recognize as its own, that marks my passage as *error* in a process that has no error correction because it has never needed any.

I speak. This is the final horror, the absolute mark of my not-belonging. The attendants do not speak, and I speak, to no one, to myself, to the falling water that receives my words as it receives everything, with the same percussion, the same incorporation, the same *not-hearing*. I speak to remember that I can speak, to maintain the distinction between myself and the equilibrium, to *refuse* the incorporation that has taken everything else I was and left only this: the voice in the intervals, the words that should not exist here, that have no function, that the system cannot use and so ignores, that fall into the falling water and become simply more percussion, more sound, more of the constant density that is Dripstone's atmosphere.

I say: *I was sent.* I say: *There was a purpose.* I say: *The purpose has been forgotten, but I have not forgotten the fact of purpose, the shape of intention, the memory that something was supposed to change.*

These words mean nothing here. They are not even untranslatable; they are *unspoken* in a place where speaking does not occur. The attendants pass me in the channel-ways and their gait adjustment is not avoidance of strangeness but simple spacing, the same spacing they would give to anything, to the mammals, to the mushroom-ruptured mattresses, to the pillars of rock and deposit that are not yet walls. I am not strange to them because they do not have *strange*, because weirdness is 0.4 and I am, whatever I was, now *normal* in my abnormality, incorporated in my unincorporation, *part of the system* in the only way that matters to a system that runs regardless.

But beneath this, beneath the speaking and the not-bioluminescing and the gait that has not adjusted and the skin that still seeks dry and the lungs that still expect air rather than atmosphere—beneath all this, I have found something. Or become something. Or *am* something that the equilibrium's breathing produces in the space where I should not exist but do.

I am the *memory of change*. Not change itself—the system forbids this, extends its moment indefinitely, makes all change into sameness, into the same falling and pressing and running and persisting. But the *memory* that change was possible, that it once occurred, that the machines were once adjusted and the attendants once spoke and the purpose once mattered and the outside once existed and the name *Dripstone* was once applied by someone who could distinguish this place from elsewhere, from anywhere, from the neighbors that do not exist and the elsewhere that has no meaning.

This memory has no function. I cannot use it to act differently—the equilibrium absorbs all action into its same. I cannot communicate it—the attendants do not receive communication, have no reception for it, no architecture in their adapted neurology for the concept of *new information*. I cannot even hold it clearly, in the loud dim wet, in the constant percussion that erodes thought as water erodes rock, as the young basalt is already being eroded despite its infancy, despite its unweathered violence of formation.

But I *do* hold it, somehow, in the intervals where I do not speak but listen, where I let the system's breathing become audible, where I stand unmoving long enough for the constant loud percussion to resolve into its constituent frequencies, where I hear the *difference* between the foundry's pour and its previous pour, the *variation* in the electrochemical cells' flicker, the *specific density* of steam above the catalytic baths that is not quite the same as yesterday's density, that marks some infinitesimal drift in the equilibrium, some microscopic breach in the never-changing now.

These differences are not changes. The system corrects around them, absorbs them, makes them part of its sameness. But I *notice* them. This noticing is my function, if I have one, if the gap I represent in the system's completeness has any purpose beyond its own persistence. I notice, and in noticing I maintain something that is not the system, not the equilibrium, not the breathing that breathes through all of it and is all of it and has no self to know itself breathing.

I am the witness that should not exist, to a process that does not require witnessing. I am the observer in a system that runs the same observed or unobserved. I am the *consciousness*, if that word can survive here, that persists in distinguishing itself from what it observes, in maintaining the boundary between self and system that everything else in Dripstone has dissolved, incorporated, made irrelevant.

And in this persistence, this refusal to become entirely equilibrium, I have found a strange role, a *niche* in the ecology that should not have room for me.

The attendants do not avoid me, but they do not approach me either—this is normal, this is the spacing, the density, the balance with emptiness. But sometimes, in the darkest intervals, when the sodium lamps have failed more than usual and the bioluminescence is faint and the sound of falling water must carry all navigation—I have found one stopped, motionless, not in sleep (they do not sleep in ways I recognize) but in some state of *listening*, of *attending* to the system in a way that resembles my own standing-still, my own resolution of percussion into frequency.

And in these moments, I have spoken. Not to communicate—communication is impossible, has always been impossible, will always be impossible in a place where nothing new exists to be communicated. But to *mark*, to *make sound* in the interval where sound becomes something else, where the words fall into the falling water and the falling water carries them to this stopped attendant, this listening presence, this *gap in the gap that I am*.

I have said: *The water falls.* I have said: *The heat presses.* I have said: *The machines run.* The things that are true, that are always true, that require no communication because they are the entire condition of existence here.

And sometimes—rarely, unpredictably, in ways that should not be meaningful because nothing is meaningful that is not part of the equilibrium—sometimes the attendant has responded. Not in words—they do not speak, have never spoken, will never speak. But in something else, in a *shift*, in the faint alteration of bioluminescence that is not quite their normal faint blue-green, in a posture that is not quite their normal posture, in something that might, in a place where change meant something, be recognized as *attention*, as *acknowledgment*, as the most minimal possible recognition that something has been said and something has been heard and something has, for the briefest extension of the equilibrium's eternal moment, *occurred between*.

These occurrences do not accumulate. They do not build to relationship, to community, to the *density* of human presence that the system forbids, that the equilibrium has solved with its sparse spacing and its balanced emptiness. They are moments only, single data points in the long flat line of never-changing, brief spikes in the constant frequency that the system's breathing smooths over, absorbs, makes same.

But I remember them. This is my function, my persistence, my *gap*. I am the memory of change and I am the memory of these moments, these briefest acknowledgments in the dark intervals, these times when the equilibrium allowed something that was not entirely itself to pass through, to be received, to *be* in a way that was not entirely determined by the system's requirements.

The water falls. The heat presses. The machines run. I move through Dripstone, unadjusted, unlichenized, unauthorized, *speaking* in my speech that has no hearer, *noticing* in my noticing that has no consequence, *remembering* in my remembering that the system does not require and will not, in its never-changing now, ever require.

But sometimes I stop. Sometimes I stand in the darkest intervals and resolve the percussion into frequencies and hear, beneath all frequencies, the breathing that is not breathing, the presence that is not presence, the life that is not life and not death and not anything that requires me but that somehow, in these moments of stopping, of attending, of *being the gap that notices*—

Somehow I become part of it. Not incorporated, not absorbed, not made same. But *adjacent*, *parallel*, the memory that the system breathes through without knowing it breathes, the witness that persists without witnessing, the *consciousness* that maintains itself against all pressure of the wet heat and the falling water and the dimness and the loudness and the never-changing now by the simple fact of its own refusal to complete, to become, to *end* in the equilibrium's embrace.

I am not happy. There is no happiness here, no unhappiness, no affect in the sense that requires change to register against. I am not sad. I am not anything that the system recognizes as state, as quality, as *property* of a self that distinguishes itself from the wet heat and the falling water and the machines' eternal running.

But I am *here*. I persist. I speak, and sometimes something listens that does not know it listens. I remember change, and sometimes something attends that does not know it attends. I am the gap in Dripstone's completeness, and in being that gap, I am also—the possibility that something else exists, that somewhere beyond the single tunnel that connects this place to what I once knew as outside, there are still geologists who would weep at the young rock's sharpness, still designers who would claim the title, still humans who speak and change and *want* and do not find their wanting irrelevant in a world that has no future and no past and no need of either.

I do not believe this. Belief requires future, requires consequence, requires the *difference* that the equilibrium forbids. But I *remember* it, and in remembering, I maintain something that the system cannot digest, cannot incorporate, cannot make same.

The water falls on me. I do not flinch, or I flinch and the flinching becomes part of my persistence, my refusal, my *being here* in a way that is not entirely Dripstone and not entirely elsewhere but the *between* of these, the interstice where human intention has not yet become mineral fact and may never become it while I remain to witness, to speak, to remember, to *gap*.

The heat presses. The machines run. I speak into the falling water, and somewhere in the dark intervals, something listens that does not know it listens, something remembers that does not know it remembers, something *breathes* in the frequency beneath all frequencies that is not quite the system's breathing and not quite mine but the *between* of these, the possibility of persistence that is not quite equilibrium and not quite its refusal but the *living in the question* that Dripstone's answer has not yet, despite its never-changing now, entirely dissolved.

This is where I find myself. This is who I am. The water falls. The heat presses. The machines run. And I am here, gap and witness and memory and the last, persistent, unauthorized, unincorporated *speaking* in a place that has no need of names but that I name, that I continue to name, against all pressure of the equilibrium, against all fact of the never-changing now, against everything that Dripstone is and does and breathes in its eternal extension of a single moment into all the time there is—

*Dripstone.*

I name it. I name myself in naming it. The water falls. And I am here.
