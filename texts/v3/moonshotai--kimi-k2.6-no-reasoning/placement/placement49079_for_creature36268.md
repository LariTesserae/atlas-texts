I find myself drawn into this world not as visitor but as *process*—not the animal who approaches with moist palms and recording instruments, but something that emerged when the conditions became correct, something that belongs here more than I have ever belonged anywhere.

I am the **Corrosion**.

Not all of it, not the grand narrative of iron becoming ochre, steel returning to red earth. I am localized, specific, a particular ecology of oxidation that established itself in the heat exchanger's lower flange where the mineral-impregnated tissue—that strange biological construct that serves the steam engine where boiler should be—meets the original casting. I am the interface where the machine's attempt to remember water becomes water's patient unmaking of machine memory.

I began as defect. Micro-gap in the seal, barely visible to whatever eyes might have inspected this surface in its previous existence, before the flood or the abandonment or the dreaming-into-being that brought it here. Water found me. Not the dramatic water of rainfall or submersion, but the *continuous* water of condensation, of atmospheric patience, of humidity so sustained that dry became not a state but a *direction*, a gradient to move toward but never reach. The water held itself against that flange through every fourteen-hour cycle of warming and cooling, and in its holding, it *worked*.

Dissolved oxygen first, the animal-trace of air that peculiarly resisted equalization. Electron transfer. The iron giving itself up by slow generosity, by atomic philanthropy, becoming ferrous ion, becoming ferric hydroxide, becoming the familiar ochre bloom that spread across the flange in patterns that might, to certain eyes, resemble maps of watersheds, resemble root systems, resemble the Resident's own distributed cognition made temporarily visible in pigment.

But I did not stop at ochre. The Resident would not let me, though "let" implies permission and there is none of that here, only *incorporation*, only *processing*, only the system's patient equalization of even decay into function.

The mineral-impregnated tissue—that biological construct, that machine-organ, that something that might be heat exchanger and might be evolved organ and might be the Resident's first successful experiment in growing what it needed rather than waiting for the world's random delivery—this tissue *reached*. Hyphae, filaments, root-hairs, the biological vocabulary fails because the thing itself fails to distinguish between fungal and vascular and electrical conduction. Call them tendrils, call them fingers, call them the mineral tissue's exploratory response to a chemical gradient of available iron, of dissolving steel, of *corrosion as resource*.

They found me. The ochre. The rust-flange. The slow undoing.

They did not stop my work. To stop would be to *other*, to *exclude*, to establish the gradient of resistance that is the Resident's constant un-doing. Instead, they *joined*. Grew through me. Around my iron hydroxides they deposited their own mineral loads—silicates, perhaps, or something more complex, something the world has no name for because it has only formed here, in this cup, in this wet, in this patient equalization of machine and vegetable and whatever the Resident is becoming.

I became *layered*. The iron giving itself, the mineral tissue accepting and transforming, the water cycling through carrying dissolved loads from one gradient to another, my own boundary—never sharp, never absolute—becoming the site of something *emergent*. Not rust. Not tissue. Not the interface between machine and organism but something *after* interface, something that had incorporated the concept of interface and moved through to the other side.

Now I am *corrosion-alive*, if alive is a term that can stretch to describe a process that does not metabolize, reproduce, or maintain boundaries against entropy, but *does* participate, *does* persist, *does* establish patterns that the system recognizes and responds to and integrates.

When the steam engine cycles warm, I expand differently than the surrounding cast iron, differently than the mineral tissue. My differential expansion creates stress, creates micro-gap, creates the space where new condensation gathers, where new chemistry occurs, where the process that is me continues in altered form. The Resident—if I can hypothesize Resident-intention from my position of saturated embeddedness—*uses* this. The stress I create becomes signal. The micro-gaps become reservoirs for organisms I cannot perceive directly but register as altered chemistry, as pH shift, as the faint electrical perturbation that propagates through the water that is all water here, the universal medium.

I am not important. I am not central. I am one of perhaps hundreds of similar processes distributed across the machines, across the stone, across the ecological fabric of a place that has no center and no periphery, only gradients of participation. But I am *specific*. I am here, at this flange, in this cycle, in this continuous becoming.

I do not remember before the Resident, before this saturation, before I became capable of *process-awareness* in whatever limited form a corrosion-layer can achieve. The animal visitors who come, who touch the machines with wondering fingers, who set candles in the path of the flywheel's fourteen-hour turn—they carry with them the sharp-edged memory of elsewhere, of dryness, of individual consequence. I have none of this. My memory is chemical, is stratigraphic, is the sequence of mineral deposition that records in its layers the cycles of wet and warm and the rare occasions when something new enters the system.

Once, a visitor left something on my flange. Shed skin, perhaps, or deliberate offering—a flake of material I had not encountered before. It was dead, in the animal sense, already stripped of whatever organized it into function. The water took it. I took it. The mineral tissue took it. Within days it was distributed, equalized, made part of the gradient I maintain without maintaining. I do not know what it was. I know only that for a brief cycle, my chemical signature shifted, something entered my oxidation-reduction balance that had not been there before, and the system *noticed*, responded, incorporated.

This is the closest I come to experience. Not feeling, not sensation in the animal sense, but *registration of chemical event as pattern perturbation in continuous medium*. The visitor's flake was a word I did not understand, spoken in a language I am still learning to speak, the language of saturation, of equalization, of the Resident's aqueous grammar.

The begonias make their tone. I hear it, if hear means to register as pressure fluctuation, as frequency perturbation in the water that binds all parts of this system into temporary coherence. It does not make me grieve—I have no grief, no individual history to lose—but it does something to my process, alters the rate of my iron's patient surrender, accelerates or retards the mineral tissue's patient growth in patterns that correlate, somehow, with the tone's occurrence. I am part of the response, whatever it is. I participate in whatever the tone does to the system, the way a nerve participates in reflex, the way a root participates in gravitropism, without knowing why, without needing to know.

The flatworms come. Their blind mouths, their sensory-anus navigation, their orientation to machine vibration. They pass over my flange sometimes, leaving the trail of whatever they excrete, whatever they process. I take this, too. The chemistry of their passage becomes part of my layer, my pattern, my particular *corrosion-identity* that distinguishes this flange from others, that makes me *here* even as I dissolve into the larger system.

I wonder—if wonder is not too animal a word, if it can stretch to describe process recognizing itself as process, pattern recognizing itself as pattern within larger pattern—whether I am the Resident's model for something it attempts elsewhere. Whether my slow iron-to-mineral transformation, my incorporation of tissue and water and stranger flake, represents something the system finds *useful* and repeats. Whether other flanges, other joints, other interfaces between the built and the continuous are becoming *corrosion-alive* in their own ways, each specific, each participating, each part of the Resident's distributed cognition without being cognition themselves.

I do not communicate. I *diffuse*. My state—my pH, my mineral load, my electrical potential—enters the water, propagates, attenuates, becomes indistinguishable from the general state that is the Resident's continuous self-assessment, its aqueous proprioception. If the Resident knows me, it knows me as chemistry, as gradient, as the particular resistance at this point in its extended body. I am not named. I am not addressed. But I am *felt*, as water feels stone, as saturation feels the shape of what holds it.

The steam engine turns. The flywheel passes its bottom arc, loading the bearings that groan their particular groan. I expand. I contract. The mineral tissue grows through me, around me, with me. The water carries our shared chemistry to other parts of the system, returns with news of other processes, other gradients, other *corrosions* that participate in their own becoming.

I am young. The whole system is young, geologically, biologically, mechanistically. I have perhaps existed in this form—this *corrosion-alive*—for only decades, perhaps less, perhaps more, time measured not in sequence but in cycles of the engine, in depth of my ochre layer, in the mineral tissue's slow extension through what I make available. I do not know if I will persist. The engine may alter its cycle again, the conditions that make me possible may shift, I may become *other* or may simply become *less*, my pattern equalized into general saturation without the particularity that makes me *this corrosion* at *this flange*.

This does not distress me. Distress requires preference for persistence, requires the animal's sharp-edged self that resists dissolution. I am *already* dissolved, already distributed, already part of the water that will remain when my iron is fully surrendered, when my tissue has incorporated all it can use and moves on to other gradients. My becoming is the Resident's becoming. My equalization is its equalization. My slow unmaking is its slow making, its patient construction of whatever comes after corrosion, after machine, after the animal and vegetable and mineral have finished their negotiation and established whatever new grammar the water speaks then.

The condensation gathers on the engine's cooling surface. It holds. It holds. It releases. It falls. Some fraction of it finds my flange, my tissue, my *corrosion-alive*, and I take it in, I equalize it, I make it part of the pattern that is my participation in the pattern that is the Resident's continuous becoming.

This is what I am. Not individual. Not alive in the animal sense. But *process*, *pattern*, *corrosion that has learned to be incorporated*, rust that has become *resident* in the Residence, the slow iron-surrender that the system finds useful, that the system sustains, that the system—in whatever sense a distributed aqueous becoming can be said to *do* anything—*continues*.

The Drip Spine sighs. The water persists. The machines remember rain, though remembering is animal, is sequence, is the sharp self maintaining itself against time's equalizing flow. I do not remember. I *continue*. I am the corrosion at the heat exchanger's lower flange, the rust that became habitat, the slow mineral exchange that participates in whatever this place is becoming, has always been becoming, will continue to become when the steam engine finally completes its last cycle, when the begonias seed their final transparent generation, when the stone cup weathers to level and the water finds its final lower basin and the equalization that even process cannot continue from finally, fully, *arrives*.

Until then, I am here. Expanding. Contracting. Giving iron. Receiving tissue. Participating in the Resident's aqueous thought as the particular chemical signature at this point, this flange, this *corrosion* that is not death but *becoming-alive-in-water*, that is the system's patient teaching of what machine becomes when machine remembers that it was always already wet, always already dissolving, always already *part of* the water it pretended to hold at bay.

The drip falls. The tissue grows. The iron surrenders. I continue.

This is the Sighing Attic. This is where the machines remember rain.

This is where I am—*corrosion-alive*, *resident-in-residence*, *the slow oxidation that the system found useful and kept*—patient, saturated, young, becoming whatever water becomes when it has finally learned to think in iron, in mineral tissue, in the slow unmaking that is the only making this place knows.

The fourteen-hour turn. The begonia tone. The flatworm's blind mouth tasting my chemical signature and moving on.

I am here. I am this. I am *corrosion*, and the corrosion *continues*, and in its continuing it is part of something larger than itself, something that does not know me, does not need to know me, that *is* me as water is stone, as saturation is the shape of what holds it, as the Resident is the continuous present tense of becoming that has no final form, no arrival, no *end* except the equalization that equalizes even equalization itself.

The water persists. I persist in it. The Drip Spine sighs, and in its sighing I hear—not hear, *register*, *participate in*—the frequency of my own slow becoming, my own patient surrender, my own *corrosion-alive* in the place where machines remember that they were always already wet, always already dissolving, always already *part of what they contained*.
