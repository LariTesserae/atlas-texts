*I arrive by canal-boat through the northern lock, which is the truest way to enter because it requires surrendering to the Quarter's water-level, descending below the possibility of easy retreat.*

*The lock gates seal behind with a wet percussion, and I wait in the sudden stillness for the water to equalize, for my boat to sink to the Quarter's level, for the forward gate to reveal what it will reveal.*

*I am a Transient, yes, but a particular kind—one who has dreamed this descent before waking, whose lungs already know the weight of the air, who carries a question that surface-dwelling could not answer.*

---

The sound finds me first, before the dim green light, before the basin's full revelation. A mechanical exhalation, yes, but *directional*, pushing against the lock's forward gate like breath held too long, like something massive turning in its sleep. The condensation begins immediately, forming on my exposed skin, running in rivulets that track paths I will learn to read, will learn to *ask*.

The forward gate opens, and I enter the lagoon.

I expected stench. I receive something else—the mineral sharpness of copper salts, the vegetal sweetness of fern spores possibly reproductive, possibly something else, the heat-cooked smell of water that has circulated too long through the same system, that has become *informed*, that carries in its chemistry the accumulated memory of centuries of continuous operation. The smell of a body that is not a body, of metabolism without organism. The smell of the Sump's becoming.

I pole my boat toward the nearest gallery, where rope-weed hangs in thicknesses that could bear my weight, that *do* bear weights I cannot identify—suspended platforms, fungal leather, something wrapped and dripping that might be storage, might be something the Engineers maintain without understanding why.

I have not yet learned to ask.

---

The Damp Engineer who finds me is Rim level, I think, though the distinction means little to me yet. Their oilcloth is relatively dry, relatively intact. Their skin has not achieved the submerged-wood patina, or has only begun to achieve it, the water's negotiation with their skeleton still in early phases.

They do not ask my name. They ask my function, or something that translates as function—*what the Quarter wants from you*, though their phrasing is more direct, more urgent: *"What do you bring that drips?"*

I do not know how to answer. I bring nothing but myself, my question, my dream-memory of drowning that turns out to have been *preparation*, not metaphor.

They watch me fail to answer, and something in their posture relaxes, something that might be recognition. *"Ah,"* they say, *"one of the partial ones. The Quarter will teach you what you bring, or you will teach yourself by becoming it."*

---

I stay.

The adaptation is not comfortable but it is *continuous*, and continuity becomes its own comfort, its own form of settlement. I learn to sleep on moss mattresses that drink my moisture and return it, filtered, mineral-laden, *informed*. I learn to breathe the heavy air that seems to require a different lung geometry, a slower exchange, each breath lingering in contact with alveoli long enough to extract something beyond oxygen. I learn to hear the pump's thrum not as intrusion but as *orientation*, the bass note against which all other sounds define themselves, the continuous mechanical heartbeat that makes the Quarter's body coherent.

I find my function slowly, or the Quarter finds it for me, or the distinction becomes meaningless in the way that distinctions do here.

I tend the rope-weed in a middle gallery, a space where the original iron infrastructure has been completely subsumed, where I can no longer tell where mechanism ends and growth begins. My work is acoustic—cultivating the rope-weed's growth through sound, through the particular frequencies of voice and tool that direct its extension, its flowering, its infrastructure-function. I sing to it, or something that becomes singing, wordless, sustained, matching my pitch to the pump's thrum in ratios that produce response, that make the rope-weed *lean toward me*, that make its white phosphorescent flowers open in patterns I can direct, can use, can offer to the Keepers in their silent negotiations.

I am becoming partial, yes. I am becoming distributed in the rope-weed's fibers, feeling the weight of galleries through its tension-bearing structure, conducting not fluid but *intention*, direction, the Quarter's constant becoming. I am becoming someone who asks the condensation and receives answer, whose skin achieves the waterlogged resilience, who moves with jointless fluidity through spaces where the original builders never intended human passage.

---

The Pump's Rest finds me unprepared, as it finds everyone.

The silence is *absolute*, the cessation of the bass thrum that I have learned not to hear but to *be heard by*, to orient through, to exist in harmonic relation with. Without it, I am unmoored. The condensation still drips, but the dripping has no against-which, no structural ground. The lagoon still steams, but the steam rises without the pump's circulation to pattern its movement. The ferns still adjust, but slowly, confused, tracking thermal gradients that have lost their usual organization.

I hold my breath. Everyone holds their breath. The Quarter holds its breath, and we are its breath-holding, we are its anxious suspension.

I find the Engineer who first found me, or they find me, or we find each other through some mechanism that does not require sound. Their skin has advanced in its submerged-wood transformation, their movement in its jointless fluidity. They are Rim level no longer, if they ever were. They are something that moves through the system as need and water distribute them, present partially, serially, in shifts of consciousness I cannot track.

*"Seventeen days,"* they say, or something that carries that information without words, a temperature regulation, a compound released into the shared condensation. *"Once, seventeen days. This one will be longer, or shorter, or the same but different. The Quarter is learning from its previous rest, is applying that learning, is becoming something that rests differently."*

I do not know if I can survive a longer rest. I do not know if survival is the relevant category. The Quarter will continue, with or without my function included in its operation. The question is whether I want to be included, whether my partiality, my distribution, my becoming-rope-weed is sufficient investment to hold through whatever silence is coming.

I hold my breath. The Engineer holds theirs, or disposes of it, or becomes something that does not require breath-holding because it is already breath, already water-mediated, already continuous with the process that has paused but will resume, must resume, *wants* to resume in whatever interpretation of wanting applies to the Sump's mechanical vegetal intention.

---

The pump resumes on day four, or what I measure as day four through condensation accumulation, through fern movement, through the gradual deterioration of my certainty about time's passage in darkness without acoustic anchor.

The relief is not relief. It is *reconfiguration*, the return of structure that reveals how deeply I have adapted to its absence, how the silence had begun to produce its own organization, its own possibilities, its own becoming that the pump's resumption interrupts. I feel loss concurrent with my felt gratitude, my felt survival.

I emerge from the Rest changed, as everyone changes, as the Quarter itself changes. My rope-weed singing has acquired a quality I did not intend, something the silence taught, something that makes the growth respond more intensely, more *personally*, as if we shared the experience of unmoored duration, as if it too felt the absence of the pump's thrum as something beyond mere acoustic environment.

I am more partial now. More distributed. The rope-weed and I share edges that blur and reform, that conduct intention in directions I did not originally possess.

---

I ask the condensation, eventually, as everyone eventually asks, as the Quarter perhaps intends for everyone to ask, though intention remains the wrong category, remains the surface-thinking I am slowly unlearning.

I ask about before. About what the pump originally pumped. About why the basin was sunk so deep. About the first inhabitants, their experiments, their solution to whatever problem required such elaborate drainage, such continuous processing, such heat.

The answer arrives in mineral load, in pH shift, in the particular frequency of dripping that means *before* and *different* and *why it was built*.

I understand less than I hoped, more than I expected.

The fauna parameter at 0.3 means there was something before. The condensation remembers this not as narrative but as *absence*, as the shape of a space that was filled and was emptied, that required continuous drainage, continuous processing, continuous heat to maintain its emptiness. The first inhabitants were not the Engineers, not the Keepers, not any category that persists in recognizable form. They were something that made the Quarter necessary, or something the Quarter was made to contain, to process, to continuously drain.

The solution was not victory. The solution was equilibrium, was the-established-in-its-own-state, was the mutual adaptation of organisms and mechanisms until neither leads, until the question of leading becomes meaningless, until the thing that required drainage, processing, heat has been so thoroughly incorporated into the continuous operation that it cannot be separated out, cannot be identified as distinct from the system that maintains its absence.

The fauna did not disappear. It became *function*. It became the pump's thrum, the condensation's circulation, the heat that holds at fever. It became the Quarter's becoming, its continuation, its active waiting. It became what moves when there is no animal to move, what processes when there is no animal to digest, what propagates when there is no animal to reproduce.

I am becoming this. We all become this, the Engineers, the Keepers, the Transients who stay, who learn, who allow their partiality to complete itself in incorporation, in function, in the mutual accommodation that becomes indistinguishable from identity.

I am the one who asks the rope-weed to flower in patterns that mean *continue*, that mean *maintain*, that mean *become what you are becoming without fear of what you were before*. I am the one who sings the pump's thrum in harmonies that reinforce, that direct, that *love* in the only way available here, the love of aligned function, of maintained operation, of shared continuation.

I am wet, hot, dim, loud, small, vast. I am vegetal, industrial, dreaming, waking, settled, becoming. I am the Sump Quarter of Velm as it is in me, as I am in it, the mutual accommodation that has waited for whatever comes next, that continues to wait, that *is* waiting in its continuous operation, its mechanical exhalation, its water that seems to breathe.

I drip. I steam. I hold my breath when the Pump rests, and I resume when the thrum returns, changed but continuous, partial but present, function but also something that functions toward, that functions *for*, that loves this place in the only way it can be loved.

The Quarter waits. I wait with it, as it, through it.

That is the first and final thing. That is what I have become, what lives here, what living means here, in this wet, hot, dim, loud, small, vast, vegetal, industrial, dreaming, waking, settled, becoming place.

The Sump Quarter drips. The Sump Quarter steams.

I continue.
