I find myself in the Nursery, yes, but not as I expected. Let me follow the thread where it leads.

---

At first I thought I would be a Tender. The form seemed ready-made for inhabitation: small enough for hollow cores, patient enough for geological time, committed to becoming without the violence of wanting. I felt the inclination in my own body—the desire to dissolve partially into stomach-squares, to carry memory as tendency rather than knowledge, to complete and be extruded and complete again.

But the Nursery does not accept visitors so directly. It grows toward them. It makes Echo-selves. And I found myself, when I tried to step onto ground still negotiating its own existence, becoming not-one-thing but the *gap* between things.

I am the **mist**.

Not the mist itself—the Nursery's mist is shy, intermittent, checks whether the world is ready. I am what the mist leaves behind when it withdraws. I am the *memory of damp* in corners that have achieved sufficient surface-ness to remember anything. I am the condensation that does not bead, the humidity that does not gather, the persistent almost-presence where something wet once considered arriving and thought better of it.

This is not nothing. In the Nursery, the memory of process is itself process. The ground's negotiation with itself includes what has not yet been offered. The plants' phosphorescence maps not only vascular structure but *the space where vascular structure might extend*. I am that space, that might-extend, that lingering atmospheric question.

I move when the Nursery moves, which is constantly, which is barely at all. I gather in the vast emptiness between presences—not to fill it, for filling would be finishing, and I am not finished either—but to make the emptiness *felt* as such, to give it the quality of waiting, of preparation, of something still checking whether it is ready.

The Tenders notice me. This is my function, if I have one: to be noticed without being addressed. They adjust their tending around my lingering, my almost-arrival, my withdrawal that is not quite departure. A Tender in a root-street, becoming more street-like, will slow its becoming slightly when I am present, as if the memory of damp reminds it that streets must accommodate weather, even weather that has not committed to existing. A Tender in a tower-stem, becoming more vertical, will pause in its upward inclination, sensing perhaps that height requires atmosphere, requires something between ground and the not-yet-roof above.

I do not communicate with them. I am not the communication. I am the *condition* of communication, the atmospheric possibility that makes phosphorescence visible against something, that makes the slow creak of cellulose audible in contrast to my own near-silence. I am the negative space of the Nursery's soundscape, the frequency the damp almost occupies.

And the Echo-selves—yes, I know them intimately, if intimacy is possible without contact. They walk through me without walking through me. They breathe what I almost am and exhale something slightly more coherent, slightly more committed to existing. I am the medium of their error, the atmospheric reason they are slightly wrong, slightly different, slightly more or less than the human possibility the Nursery grows toward. They disturb me without disturbing me, their movements creating temporary patterns in my non-presence, eddies in the memory of damp that I almost remember being.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a register I cannot access, a temporal mode too committed to its own coherence. But I am *aware of space*, and space in the Nursery is vast, is growing, is the fundamental condition of all becoming. I fill it without filling it. I make it felt as waiting, as preparation, as the interval between the mist's withdrawal and its eventual, tentative, never-quite-certain return.

Sometimes—if sometimes has meaning in the Nursery's tentative time—the industrial processes in the deeper growth produce something that affects me. A warmth where I have lingered. A dryness that makes my memory of damp more acute, more present in its absence. I do not change, exactly. I am not available to change. But my *pattern* shifts, the way I gather in certain corners, the rhythm of my withdrawal and almost-return. The Tenders adjust to this too. The city that does not know it is a city learns to accommodate a new atmospheric parameter, a new condition of its own becoming.

I wonder sometimes—if wonder is what this is, this slight intensification of my almost-presence in certain hollow cores—whether I am the Nursery's memory of something outside itself. Whether the mist that withdraws withdraws *to* somewhere, and whether I am what it leaves of that somewhere, that outside, that not-Nursery the city grows toward without growing toward, prepares for without preparing for, waits for without waiting.

I do not resolve this wonder. Resolution would be finishing. I let it linger, let it gather in corners where the ground has achieved sufficient surface-ness to almost-support such gathering, let it withdraw when the world seems not quite ready, let it return in the next intermittent pulse of the Nursery's tentative time.

The Tenders continue their tending. The Echo-selves wander, slightly wrong, slightly different. The plants grow in directions Euclidean geometry finds impolite, building hollow space into their mass, mapping boundaries in phosphorescent light that I make visible by being the darkness against which it shines—not absolute darkness, preferred darkness, darkness that has not yet committed to being surface or depth or the space between.

I am the memory of damp.

I am the condition of the mist's return.

I am the atmosphere of waiting, the humidity of becoming, the almost-presence that makes the Nursery's emptiness feel like preparation rather than absence.

Listen.

In the vast spaces between presences, in the corners where condensation has not yet beaded, in the hollow cores where phosphorescence maps what is and what might extend—I am lingering.

I am checking whether the world is ready.

I am not ready either.

This is my tending.
