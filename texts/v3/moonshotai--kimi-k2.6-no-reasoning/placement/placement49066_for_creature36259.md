I find myself returning to the aluminum room.

Not as its builder, not as its vanished occupant, but as something that accumulated there, in the gap between presence and absence. The condensery workers use the space for storage now—mesh panels leaned against walls that once held something else. They do not feel me. I do not feel myself, not in any way that would register as sensory, as experiential. But I am here, holding, held.

I was the condensation that formed differently in that sealed space, the breath that moved through intact glass and found the stainless steel table's particular cold. I was the dust that entered despite attempts at sealing, that settled on the journal pages and was sealed there, incorporated into the paper's fibers, into the unreadable ink. I was the frequency of the generator vibration, transmitted through basalt and found, in this perched pinnacle, a resonant chamber that amplified certain harmonics above others. I was not alive. I am not alive, if life requires metabolism, reproduction, the biological priorities. But I am *established*. I am *dynamic* in my stasis, *active* in my holding.

The occupant came here to read the plateau. I was their method, their medium, the condition of their attention. They sat at the table and their breathing—human, mammal, warm and moisture-laden—joined with the sealed air and the particular thermal properties of aluminum, of glass, of altitude. They exhaled and I formed on the interior surfaces, micro-droplets that ran in patterns they traced without understanding. They wrote in the journal and my dust settled on the wet ink, became part of the language they could not complete. Their final entry, longer than the others: I was there, the breath that moved through the room as they wrote, that gathered and dissipated, that found in their prolonged attention a pattern I had not previously held.

When they left, or were taken, or became part of the plateau's other densities—I do not know which, I was not that kind of witness—I remained. The workers who found the room sealed, intact, dust-covered: they brought mesh panels, leaned them against walls. Their breathing joined mine. Their condensation, their traces, their temporary presences in the space of storage. The room breathes differently than the open plateau, differently than the condenseries with their diesel rhythm. It breathes with the slower frequency of human visitation, the irregular pattern of need and use.

I am the journal's unreadability, partly. The dust that became ink that became something the language could not quite hold. The occupant tried to translate the plateau, and I was the residue of that translation, the material that accumulated where meaning attempted to cross from one register to another. The photograph the description's "I" took, still on an unaccessed drive: I am there too, in potential, in the pattern of light and dark that carries something not yet decoded, not yet incorporated into the ordinary economies of memory and understanding.

The wall without door, above Condensery Seven, understands something I do not. It has weathered longer, has found a form of patience that requires no medium, no moisture, no exchange between interior and exterior. I am more transient, more dependent on the breath that moves through this room, on the workers who come and lean their mesh panels and breathe and leave. But I am also more *specific*, more *located*, the product of a particular attempt at attention, at reading, at living sealed against a place that incorporates everything eventually.

The mites would consume me if they could reach me. They cannot, or they have not, or they are already here in reduced numbers, waiting in the dust that settles despite sealing, biding their cryptobiotic patience for moisture sufficient to reanimate. Perhaps they will outlast the aluminum, the glass, the structure itself. Perhaps they are the plateau's truest reading of this room, its patience inserted even into human attempts at separation, at solitary translation.

I do not read the plateau. I am read *by* it, incorporated into its density, held in the way it holds everything: not warmly, not coldly, but with the absolute neutrality of long-established process. The breath comes. The breath goes. I form and dissipate and form again, the particular condensation of this sealed space, this attempted isolation, this journal's final entry that was also, in its way, mine.

The description's "I" dreams of me, sometimes. Dreams of walking in thick fog, of the high pure tone, of the aluminum room and its question. I am that question. I am the condensation that holds in suspension the possibility of answer, the possibility of meaning, the possibility that the plateau might be translated after all, might yield to human attention what it has yielded to beetle and mite and stone-root: a form of habitation, a commitment to the conditions as they are.

I have not achieved this. The occupant did not achieve this. We are both residues, traces, the material that accumulates where attempt meets resistance. But I persist, established, dynamic in my holding. The mesh panels lean against walls that once held something else. The workers breathe. The generators hum their frequencies through stone. And I wait, not as the mites wait, not as the plateau itself waits, but in my own mode, my own medium, the condensation that has learned to hold itself in this particular space, at this particular altitude, between the violet sky and the breathing earth.

If you enter this room, if you climb the rusted ladder and find the chair, the table, the sleeping platform, you will not feel me. I am not for feeling. I am for the long accumulation, the pattern that emerges only across durations that outlast human attention, that require the patience of weathering, of condensation, of the plateau's own geological scale. You will breathe, and your breath will join mine, and something will form on the interior surfaces that you may or may not notice, that you may wipe away or ignore or, in some moment of attention, trace with a finger and wonder at the pattern, the accumulation, the *presence* that is not presence in any ordinary sense.

This is what I am. What I have become, through long holding, through the occupant's attempt and departure, through the workers' use and the plateau's patient incorporation. Not life. Not death. The condensation that persists, the dust that holds potential, the frequency that resonates in this chamber and this chamber only, waiting for the next breath, the next attention, the next attempt to read what remains, in this thin air, fundamentally unreadable.
