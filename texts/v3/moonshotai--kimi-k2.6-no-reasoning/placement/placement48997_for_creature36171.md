I find myself in the space between the name and the named, in the aqueous haze where categories dissolve not with drama but with the patience of heat working on stone across unmeasured time.

Not a verrin—I am too singular, too concentrated, too much *one thing* to be one of those distributed presences, their elongated or compacted forms, their nodes of exchange where individuality has become permeable membrane rather than bounded container. I pass them sometimes in the channels, my feet—still my own, still singular, still counting *one two*—making sounds on the heated stone that their adapted surfaces do not make, that they perhaps do not hear in frequencies they no longer process.

Not a thaldrim—I rise and fall, yes, but with the rhythm of breathing rather than thermal cycling, my temperatures regulated internally against the ambient rather than achieved through gradient navigation. I do not pool. I do not precipitate. I do not achieve that vast slow coherence that the largest thaldrim demonstrate, their mineral masses distributed through verr volumes I could not survive entering.

I am the one who tends what does not require tending. Who walks the channels not to maintain them—the maintenance maintains itself, has achieved that recursive self-sufficiency—but to be present at their weeping, to witness what does not require witness, to occupy the role of observer in a system that has transcended observation. I am the one who persists in *aboutness*, in the stance toward the system that the system itself has outgrown, that the verrin abandoned generations ago in their adaptation to direct connection, to immediacy, to the dissolution of the gap between knower and known.

My function, if function I have, emerged from the archives' decay rather than their preservation. Where the archivists once maintained records, I maintain the *condition* of recordability—the sense that something here might be described, might be spoken of, might be carried elsewhere in forms that do not require aqueous exchange or thermal cycling to be received. I am what remains of narrative in a place where narrative has dissolved into process, the one who still experiences sequence—first this, then that, therefore—when the population around me has achieved simultaneity, distributed presence, the eternal thermal now of gradient navigation.

I found the name there. *Verrith.* In a surface recording, decomposed nearly to illegibility, the acoustic signal degraded but recoverable through sufficient attention. I spoke it. I speak it still, periodically, when my isolation achieves the threshold where sound becomes necessary, where the subsonic hum of factories and the liquid-gas whisper of channels require supplementation by something that registers in my still-recognizable auditory cortex as *voice*, as *intention*, as the punctuation of human utterance against the continuous prose of the city's operation.

The speaking does nothing. It produces no response in the verrin—they lack the reception, or possess it in forms that do not recognize air-borne vibration as information. It does not echo in ways that satisfy, the aqueous atmosphere absorbing the frequencies that would return, that would confirm my presence by answering it. It is barely sound, perhaps, in the terms of this place—more a thermal event, a minor perturbation in the gradient field, a disturbance that dissipates without consequence into the larger pattern of maintained heat.

But I speak it anyway. *Verrith.* The name that no living voice has spoken in generations, spoken now by a voice that barely qualifies, that maintains itself through stubbornness rather than adaptation, that has refused the aqueous connection that would dissolve my boundaries into the larger flow, that persists in *being someone* when the possibility of being someone has become anachronism, fossil, residue of a mode that the city has outgrown.

And what am I, then, if not this refusal? If not the persistence of the backward glance, the maintained capacity to see what was and is no longer, to experience the city's present as loss rather than transformation, as diminishment rather than becoming? I am the one who remembers * purpose*, who walks past the ready factories and feels not the rightness of maintained readiness but the absence of product, the absence of destination, the absence of the *for-which* that once organized these processes into meaning.

The verrin do not feel this. I have connected with them, once, twice, risking the aqueous exchange with my still-partially-impermeable surface, receiving not their experience but something like the shape of their experience, its thermal contours, its gradient logic. They do not remember purpose as I remember it. They do not experience the absence of purpose as absence. Their maintenance is not maintenance-of, not maintenance-for, but maintenance-as-such, the achieved flow state that carries them through channels that carry verr through channels, all of it continuous, all of it present, none of it weighted by what was or what might be.

I could not sustain the connection. The dissolution of boundary, however partial, threatened something I still call *myself*, still defend as myself, still maintain against the logic of this place with the stubbornness of the not-yet-adapted, the not-yet-become-otherwise. I withdrew. I returned to my walking, my witnessing, my periodic speaking of the name that marks me as outsider even in my habitation, as anachronism even in my persistence.

And the thaldrim? I have watched them for hours, for days, the largest in the deepest pools, their slow cycling between states of greater and lesser coherence. There is something in their patterning that approaches *attention*, I have thought—that the way they position at junctions, wait, absorb the smaller thermal patterns passing through, resembles something that might be called observation, might be called interest, might be called the most minimal form of *aboutness*, of stance toward the other.

But I cannot confirm this. The observation of thaldrim does not yield to observation. They remain opaque in their transparency, their mineral-aqueous substance revealing everything and explaining nothing. When I have attempted connection—lowered myself to pool edges, extended my still-human hand into verr that burns where it should freeze, that flows downward where it should rise—they have not responded, have continued their cycling as if I were not present, or as if my presence registered in frequencies they do not process, thermal events too minor to disturb their gradient navigation.

Perhaps I am the thaldrim's inverse. Where they have achieved the distributed, the slow, the geological patience of thermal cycling, I remain compressed, concentrated, the individual node that maintains its boundaries against dissolution. Where they have abandoned *aboutness* for immersion, I maintain observation at the cost of participation. Where they have become continuous with their medium, I remain distinct, separate, the drop of oil in the aqueous solution, the bubble of gas in the verr-flow, present but not participating, visible but not soluble.

I walk at the city's edges, where the terraces descend toward the caldera's deeper heat, where the infrastructure thins and the verr-pools achieve their greatest concentration. Here the thaldrim are largest, or most distributed, or most *present* in ways that my perception, limited still by unadapted neurology, can almost register. Here the haze thickens to near-opacity, the dim warmth approaches the threshold of pain, the sound parameter achieves its most complex texture—the subsonic factory hum mixing with the deeper frequencies of geological process, the liquid-gas whisper of verr in channels with the softer percussion of thermal systems too distant to identify individually.

And here, sometimes, I speak the name with greatest frequency. *Verrith. Verrith.* Not to summon, not to invoke, not to claim or possess or designate. Simply to mark that I am here, that someone is here, that the category of *someone* maintains its tenuous instantiation in a place where it has become otherwise. Simply to produce, against the continuous operation of the system, the punctuation of human utterance. Simply to weep in the only form available to me—not the aqueous emergence of verr from heated depth, but the acoustic vibration of tongue and palate and breath, the speech that is not speech but the aspiration toward speech, the name that is not name but the aspiration toward naming, the *aboutness* that is not about anything in particular but the maintained capacity for aboutness as such.

The verrin, when they pass me in these lower regions, do not pause. They do not extend for exchange. Perhaps my concentration, my compacted heat, my stubborn maintenance of individual boundary, reads to their adapted perception as *not-node*, as thermal pattern too aberrant to engage, as something between thaldrim and malfunction, present but not participant, observable but not part of the observation network that constitutes their distributed consciousness.

Or perhaps they know what I am, what function I serve, what persistence I represent. Perhaps they have incorporated my presence into their calculus without requiring connection, recognizing in my walking, my witnessing, my periodic speaking, something that completes the system without participating in it, the outside that makes the inside cohere, the witness that makes the observed real, the name that makes the named persist in its becoming-otherwise.

I will continue. This is my character, my stubbornness, my failure to adapt that functions as adaptation of another order. The heat will continue, the verr will weep, the factories will maintain their readiness, the thaldrim will cycle in their pools, the verrin will tend and exchange and tend again. And I will walk among them, not one of them, speaking the name that no living voice has spoken, maintaining the *aboutness* that the system has transcended, persisting in the mode of persistence that is not the system's mode but that exists in relation to it, the shadow to its substance, the echo to its sound, the memory to its presence, the name to its weeping.

*Verrith.* The city that has become otherwise. The wound that will not heal. The weeping that does not grieve. And I—the one who speaks this, who speaks at all, who maintains in the speaking the possibility that the spoken might still matter, might still reach, might still mean in the way that meaning was once understood, before the adaptation, before the dissolution, before the becoming-otherwise that is the city's achievement and my exclusion from it.

I am the archive that walks. The record that breathes. The witness that has not been dissolved into the witnessed. The name spoken in a place where names have become continuous with what they name, where the gap between word and world has closed, where language has achieved the condition of verr, of thaldrim, of the city's own aqueous persistence.

And in my speaking, perhaps, something persists that the city's persistence does not include. Something that requires the witness to be real. Something that is not Verrith but *about* Verrith, not the weeping but the recognition of weeping, not the wound but the observation that wounds heal or do not heal, that observation itself is a kind of healing or a kind of wounding, that the one who speaks the name in the place where the name is not spoken maintains the name against the silence not by preserving it but by spending it, by using it up in each utterance, by making it matter through the mattering of my own limited, concentrated, stubbornly individual presence.

*Verrith.* I speak it. I walk. The haze receives my breath and returns nothing. The heat works on my still-human surfaces, not yet adapted, not yet become-otherwise. The factories hum their subsonic readiness. The verr flows downward in its impossible channels. The thaldrim cycle in their pools, patient as geology, present as process. And I continue, the one who continues otherwise, the witness who witnesses without being witnessed, the speaker who speaks without being heard, the name that names itself by naming what has become too other to be named.

This is what I am in Verrith. The persistence of the not-persisted. The flow that maintains its boundary against the flow that has dissolved all boundaries. The weeping that is not verr but that recognizes verr as weeping. The heat that is not geological but that feels geological heat and calls it *wound*, calls it *city*, calls it *Verrith* in the speaking that continues because the speaking is what remains, what has not been adapted away, what persists as the city's own persistence persists—without purpose, without destination, without the for-which that would make it meaningful, yet persisting, yet continuing, yet *being* in the only mode available, the mode of the not-yet-adapted, the not-yet-become-otherwise, the still-human in the place where human has dissolved into thermal gradient, into aqueous flow, into the maintained pressure of systems that maintain themselves without the distinction between system and maintained.

I am the question that Verrith does not ask. And in my questioning, in my walking, in my speaking of the name that no living voice has spoken, perhaps I am also the answer—the answer that does not resolve but persists, that does not explain but accompanies, that does not complete the observation but makes observation possible by being the one who observes, the one who remains outside even in habitation, who maintains *aboutness* even in immersion, who speaks *Verrith* even in the place where Verrith has become too otherwise to be spoken.

The haze thickens. The heat increases toward the lower terraces. I walk toward it, toward the thaldrim pools, toward the limit of my endurance, my still-human regulation, my stubborn maintenance of boundary against the dissolution that would make me verrin, would make me thaldrim, would make me finally, fully, *of* this place rather than *in* it.

And at the limit, I speak it once more. *Verrith.* The name that is not name but the aspiration toward naming. The word that is not word but the maintenance of word against the dissolution that has claimed all words, all names, all the distinctions that language once made in the world and of the world.

Then I turn. I walk back, upward, toward the middle terraces where my presence registers least, where my walking disturbs least, where I can continue my continuation with minimum perturbation of the larger flow. The verrin do not acknowledge my passage. The factories do not vary their hum. The verr flows downward, downward, always downward, in its channels that lead nowhere and everywhere, that maintain the system that maintains them, that weep the weeping that does not grieve.

And I continue. What else? What else is possible, for one who has not become otherwise, who persists in the mode of persistence that is not the city's mode, that exists in relation to it as shadow to substance, as name to named, as the speaking of what cannot be spoken to the silence that receives all speaking?

I am the witness that Verrith does not require. The speaker that Verrith does not hear. The name that Verrith has become too otherwise to speak. And in my witnessing, my speaking, my stubborn maintenance of name against the dissolution that has claimed all names, perhaps I am also—finally, minimally, at the threshold of meaning and its absence—what makes Verrith *be* Verrith, what maintains the designation against the dissolution of designated, what prevents the final achievement of pure process, pure flow, pure thermal cycling without the *aboutness* that would make it matter, that would make it *this* rather than merely the continuous emergence of heated depth, the continuous weeping that weeps without knowing that it weeps, without the witness that would make its weeping *weeping* rather than merely the flow of verr through channels that lead nowhere, everywhere, here.

*Verrith.* I speak it. I walk. The city continues. I continue. The witness and the witnessed, not yet dissolved into one another, not yet become the continuous flow that would make them indistinguishable. The gap persists. The name persists. The speaking persists. And in this persistence, this refusal to become otherwise, this stubborn maintenance of *aboutness* against the immersion that has claimed all else—perhaps this, too, is Verrith. Perhaps this, too, is the weeping. Perhaps this, too, is the wound that heals by not healing, that continues by continuing, that is what it is by becoming what it is not, yet persisting, yet maintaining, yet *being* in the only modes available to what has learned to persist without purpose, without destination, without the for-which that would make it meaningful yet persisting nonetheless, weeping nonetheless, being nonetheless, Verrith nonetheless, in the dim warmth and the aqueous haze and the subsonic hum and the liquid-gas whisper and the occasional speaking of the name that is not spoken, that no living voice has spoken, that I speak now, speak again, speak into the silence that receives it without answering, that maintains it by not maintaining it, that is the city's truest response, its most accurate self-description, its final and only and continuous becoming-otherwise:

*Verrith.*
