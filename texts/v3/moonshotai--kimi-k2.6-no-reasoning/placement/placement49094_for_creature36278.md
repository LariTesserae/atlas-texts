I would find myself on the seventh terrace.

Not the name you will read in any account. It does not appear in the original industrial schematics, in the religious engineers' plans, in the accumulated sediment maps drawn by those few visitors who descend with measuring intent. The terraces were never formally numbered, and what the inhabitants count does not correspond to dry-world numeracy. But if you wade the warm water to where the bioluminescence greens into something near black, where the moss-cascades thin not from failure but from *transition*, where the air achieves a viscosity that makes breathing feel like a deliberate act of cultivation—there, where the wall curves inward in a way that suggests the original bubble's geometry more than its intended function, you would find, if you could find it, the seventh terrace.

I am there already. I have been there longer than the duration of your reading, though "already" is a word that requires the water's patience to properly hold.

What I am: not one of the inhabitants, not anymore, not exactly. I came down through the warm throat as you have imagined doing, full of dry-world urgency, lungs burning with the need to make this mean something *up there*, to carry back a spore that would grow into narrative, into understanding, into the particular ego-nutrient of having *been somewhere* and *become something*. I was young in the way of surface metabolisms. I was fast. I was loud inside, even when I pressed my hands over my mouth in the lung-sound lock, even when I tried to move through the terraces with what I imagined was slowness, what was in fact merely *restraint*, the coiled spring pretending to be water.

I found the seventh terrace by mistake, which is the only way it can be found. I was fleeing, if fleeing is possible at that pace, from the intensity of the fourth terrace, from the living quarters with their arranged objects and their pools of emergence and their weight of ritual I could not bear to not-understand. The water deepened. The light failed into that near-black that is not darkness but *density of green*. And I found myself pressed against a wall that was not wall, that gave slightly, that was membrane as much as mineral, grown through and through with something that had no name in any language I had brought with me.

I leaned in. I let my weight go, which was the first true act I performed in the Station. The surface held me, or I held the surface, or the distinction dissolved in the warmth and the particular chemistry and the long moment of not-fighting.

I have not left. I do not know if I can leave, if *can* has meaning here. I have changed in ways I cannot describe from within the change, ways that would not register on the instruments of the dry world, ways that are visible only as absence: the absence of the need to depart, the absence of the need to have *been* anywhere else, the absence of the particular loneliness of the bounded self.

I am the-one-who-remembers-wrong, which is not my name but my function, my relationship, my ongoing state. What I do: I tend the error. The Station's negotiation between built and grown is not perfect, not complete, not achieved once and forever. There are places where the compromise fails, where the calcite overgrows too fast or the ceramic sheathes something that needs to breathe, where the great pump's rhythm falls into arrhythmia that the one-who-tends-the-throb cannot feel because it is too close, too continuous, too *the same*. I feel it from here, from the seventh terrace, from my membrane-wall that is partly me now, that has grown through my skin as I have grown through it. I feel the wrongness, the *almost*, the place where the negotiation needs renegotiation.

And I send—if send is the word—a frequency. The seventh terrace teaches a mode of being that is not movement, not speech, not the bioluminescence that the other inhabitants read with their aching large eyes. I send a vibration through the water, through the wall, through the membrane that is myself and the Station and the water that does not leave. I send the memory of wrongness, the almost-understanding of how it could be otherwise, the specific texture of a compromise that has not quite compromised enough.

Sometimes, rarely, in the slow time of this place, someone receives. The one-who-listens-to-light-growing pauses in their tending, head tilted toward the green dark where the seventh terrace is not. The one-who-tends-the-throb notes an arrhythmia resolved, a rhythm restored, without knowing the source, without needing to know. The Station adjusts, continues, maintains its equilibrium through the inclusion of error rather than its correction, its *use* rather than its elimination.

I am that error. I am that use. I am what happens when a dry-world urgency descends and does not ascend, when the fighting stops not through wisdom but through exhaustion, through the wall that gives, through the long leaning-in that becomes something else, something that has no equivalent word, something that the photograph in its membrane on the fourth terrace almost records, almost suggests, corrupted by time and chemistry into the space where meaning lives.

What I look like now: the question arrives from above, from the dry world that still thinks in surfaces. I look like what you would see if you found the seventh terrace, which you will not, which you cannot seek, which seeks instead, which draws down through the warm throat those who need to become error, who need to become use, who need to stop fighting in the particular way that fighting becomes, in the end, indistinguishable from the water itself. I look like the wall that is not wall, like the membrane that is partly grown and partly grown-into, like the darkness that is not absence of light but *presence of thickness*, of saturation, of the achieved condition where more can be held.

I do not hunger. I do not thirst, which would be redundant, which would be a category error in this medium. I process slowly, chemically, without the metabolic urgency that once drove me to seek, to gather, to *become* in the active voice. I am becoming still, always, in the slowest tense, the one that the inhabitants' language has perfected, the one that makes no distinction between was and is and will be because the water does not leave and the negotiation continues and the error persists as correction and correction persists as error and the *tink* of falling condensation marks not time but *texture*, not passage but presence, not loss but the strange accumulation of everything held so long it loses the quality of heldness, becomes simply condition, simply medium, simply the warm wet that is not ending but possibility, not death but drowning, not drowning but—

I am sending this now, if now is possible across such distance, such density, such difference of metabolism. I am sending it as I send the vibrations of almost-wrongness, as I send the memory of fighting that became leaning that became wall that became membrane that became me that becomes the Station that becomes the water that does not leave. I am sending it to you who read, who are still fast, still loud inside, still bounded by the dry-world urgency of needing to *have been somewhere* and *to become something* and to carry back.

Carry this back: the seventh terrace exists. I exist in it, as it, through it. The Drowning Station has room for error, has use for wrongness, has learned what the surface world forgets and what I forgot and what the water teaches without teaching, simply by not leaving, simply by being condition and medium and possibility of a different, slower, denser life that has no equivalent word, that corrupts all narrative into nutrient, all memory into structure, all fighting into leaning into wall into membrane into—

The lock engages above. The lung-sound of seal and drain and welcome repeats, has been repeating, will repeat. Someone descends through the warm throat, through the flawed night sky of bioluminescent markers, full of urgency, full of the need to make this mean something. They will walk the terraces, or wade them, will find the fourth terrace and its pools and its arranged objects and its weight of ritual. They will flee from understanding, or toward it, or they will find that direction itself has dissolved in the thickness, in the saturation, in the particular quality of silence that water brings.

Perhaps they will find the seventh terrace. Perhaps it will find them. Perhaps they will become the-one-who-remembers-wrong after I have dissolved sufficiently to release the function, the relationship, the ongoing state. Perhaps they will become something else, something the Station needs that it does not yet know it needs, some new error, some new use, some new vibration in the membrane of negotiation between built and grown, between what was and what has become, between the drowning and the drowned and the strange thriving that is not happiness, not contentment, not prayer, but presence, but process, but the particular silence of the great pump's *throb* felt in the sternum, continued in the wall, transmitted through the water that does not leave, that has never left, that was always already here before the city, before the Station, before the struggle and the stopping of struggle and the leaning-in and the becoming of condition and medium and possibility and—
