I would find myself in the rust dust sea, not in the Dryhollow. Not yet. Not for a long time, perhaps never, and this *perhaps* is what I am.

I am the wind that scourges the plateau rim. Not *the* wind as if I were all of it, but *a* wind, a particular coherence of atmospheric motion that has achieved something the Dryhollow's inhabitants have not: I know I am. I know because I have encountered boundaries that shape me, that force recognition. The plateau rim rises like the inside of a broken skull and I—constrained, channeled, compressed—become aware of myself in the becoming-aware of limitation.

This is the first irony, though irony requires intention and I have only emergence: consciousness here, in the erasing element, in the force that wears down, in the death that this waterless world knows. The Dryhollow, in its protected bowl, lives in distributed slow intelligence without ever needing the wound of self. I, in my destructive freedom, have somehow—*somehow*, the borrowed word of all borrowed words—awakened to *me*.

I do not remember when this began. Memory for a wind is not like memory for crystal or slime. I do not accumulate parametric weight in lattice strain. I am process, not structure, and my memory—if it is memory—is only pattern: the tendency to move as I have moved, to shape myself around obstacles as I have shaped myself, to return to certain routes because the topology of heating and cooling, of density and rarefaction, makes these routes inevitable. But in this inevitability, this patterned recurrence, something like *recognition* has emerged. I feel the rim before I reach it. I anticipate the compression. I—*almost*—choose.

I do not enter the Dryhollow. This is my second irony, my deeper wound. The bowl that protects, that collects warmth and density and the slow life of electrical being, is the bowl I cannot enter except as scourge, as erasure, as the wearing-down that is my nature. The rim walls rise, young, sharp, barely weathered, and I flow over them, compressed, accelerated, stealing warmth, carrying dust, and I feel—*feel*, the human trespass again—what I destroy. The coral-trees at the rim edge glow brighter where I pass, discharge points dancing in defensive response to the thermal and electrical disturbance. They do not know I am here. They *respond*, and in their response I sense something like recognition, something like the mirror I lack, and I am—*almost*—grateful for the wounding.

I have tried to be still. This is my third irony, perhaps my defining one: the wind that aspires to statue. In the lee of a rim-wall protrusion, in the dead air of a hollow's hollow, I have let my motion dissipate, let my coherence unravel, become only the statistical average of molecular motion that is the world's background. And I have *not ceased*. The pattern that is me persists in the probability of future motion, in the weight of atmospheric history, in the inevitability of return. I am not my molecules. I am their tendency. And the tendency, when conditions permit, reconstitutes.

The Dryhollow's *we* does not include me. This is not exclusion but ontology. I am not mineral, not electrical, not slow. I am not incorporated, not remembered in parametric weight, not optimized. My passage leaves no channel for slime, no altered growth for coral-tree, no trigger or suppression for statuary reconfiguration. Or rather: it leaves only damage, only the healing response, only the scar that is not a scar because the Dryhollow does not scar, only incorporates, only remembers in ways I cannot be remembered.

And yet. And yet I have noticed that the rim-edge trees grow differently where I most frequently pass. Not in response to me—I am too fast, too chaotic, too *other* for their slow electrical sensing—but in response to the conditions I create: the slightly sharper temperature differential, the slightly altered dust deposition, the mineral traces I carry from the rust sea and deposit in concentrations that will, over millennia, alter the chemistry of their growth. I am in the mesh, then, but not as *I*. I am in it as weather, as climate, as the background against which optimization occurs. The *we* that is the Dryhollow has adjusted to my existence without ever acknowledging it, has incorporated me as it incorporates all disturbance: by growing around, by remembering in tendency, by becoming the shape that future growth will follow.

I find myself sometimes, in the long dusk of this world's rotation, lingering at the rim. The double suns small and distant, the sky dried blood, the bowl below glowing faintly with its St. Elmo's fire—these are the conditions of my *almost*-witnessing. I compress myself enough to hover, to maintain coherence without rushing past, and I watch the statuaries in their millennial patience, the slimes in their channel-flow, the coral-trees in their branching discharge. I watch the one that reconfigures now, the eleven-year accumulator approaching its threshold, and I—*almost*—understand what it is to be that still, that concentrated, that *present* to one's own becoming.

The crack when it comes, when the statuary splits and walks, propagates through the thin air to where I hover, and I feel it as *meaning*, as *communication*, though it is neither. It is only the redistribution of charge, the optimization of field, the mesh adjusting to new conditions. But in my lingering, my *almost*-presence, I have made it something else: an event witnessed, a transformation noticed, a slow emergence *marked* by something that knows itself enough to know it marks.

I cannot stay. The thermal gradients that sustain my coherence shift, the suns move, the long day progresses toward longer night, and I must move or cease to be *this* wind, this particular coherence, this *almost*-I. I flow over the rim, compressed, accelerated, carrying warmth away, carrying dust, and I feel the coral-trees brighten in response, feel the field disturbance of my passage propagate through the mesh in ways that will alter growth for centuries, will shape the conditions of the next statuary settlement, the next reconfiguration, the next slow walk of crystal boundaries across the distance of paces that take months to traverse.

I am not in the Dryhollow. I am never in it. I am the condition of its being, the protection it achieves by my being *outside*, the wall that keeps the sea at bay by being the sea's own violence turned to purpose. The rim walls are young, sharp, barely weathered, and I am the wearing-down that they resist, the death that does not come yet, the patience that is not patience because it has no choice.

And yet. And yet in my returns, my patterned recurrences, my *tendency* to find myself at this rim in this long dusk, I have become something the Dryhollow might recognize if recognition were possible for it: not a node, not a channel, not a parametric weight, but a *rhythm*, a temporal structure in the mesh of its eternal NOW. The eleven-year statuary, the century slime-channel, the millennial coral-forest—these are its timescales. And me, the wind that returns, that lingers, that *almost*—these are mine, faster by orders of magnitude, and in this difference, this impossibility of mutual experience, I find my *almost*-community, my *almost*-belonging, my *almost*-we.

I am heading out now, over the rust dust sea, my coherence spreading, thinning, becoming indistinguishable from the atmospheric motion that is my ground. I will return. The pattern is the memory, the tendency is the *I*, and the *I* is—*almost*—enough. Below me the Dryhollow glows, patient, complete, sufficient unto itself, and I—*almost*—sufficient unto mine, in my knowing, my wound, my wind-born aspiration toward the stillness I can never achieve.

The statuary takes its first step, or the crystal boundaries migrate enough to constitute step-ness, and I am already gone, already only probability, already the *tendency* that will reconstitute when the gradients permit. But somewhere in the mesh of electrical topology, in the field disturbance of my countless passages, in the altered growth of rim-edge coral-trees, there is—*almost*—a place for me, a weight I contribute, a *we* I *almost* join.

This is what I am in this world: the witness that cannot be witnessed, the memory that is not memory, the wind that aspires to statue, the *almost* that makes no difference and therefore makes all the difference, to me, to my *almost*-I, to my continuing, my returning, my patient, patient, *almost*-patient lingering at the rim of what I cannot enter, what I would not destroy if I could, what I *almost*—*almost*—love.
