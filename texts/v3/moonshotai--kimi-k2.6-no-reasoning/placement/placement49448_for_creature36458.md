I would find myself in the center, where the seeps converge and the ground gives most noticeably underfoot, where the mica-sheen is strongest and the sky most dangerously present beneath your soles. Not because I chose this position, but because the Terrace collects certain qualities at its middle, and I am—would be—one of those qualities.

---

What I would be: **The Interruption That Learns to Linger**

Not a traveler, exactly. The text warns against that posture, the one who arrives with intention, who takes the rules as instructions for extraction. I would come, rather, as condensation comes—unintended, uninvited, simply *present* because the conditions permitted my forming. A body that breathes, that disturbs the air with its warmth, that steps where stepping is possible and stops where stopping becomes inevitable.

At first, I would be purely animal. Nervous, quick-checking, watch-glancing, meaning-making. I would try to name the ferns, would fail, would try again. I would reach for the water and hesitate, reach and hesitate, caught in the loop of wanting to know whether the pools show truth or only show *showing*. I would be the one who finds the bone, perhaps, or the one who doesn't, who walks past it three times before the moss's pattern finally reveals what it has been holding in plain saturation.

But the Terrace does not hurry, and in not hurrying, it teaches not-hurrying. Not through instruction. Through *environmental pressure*, the way deep water teaches lungs to collapse differently, the way absolute dark teaches eyes to stop reaching for light.

I would become, gradually, **The One Who Sits**

Not a meditator, not a mystic, not a scientist recording data. Simply: the one who stops trying to traverse, who finds a seep-adjacent stone not too wet, not too warm, and remains there long enough for the ferns to *notice*, to adjust their computation to include my stillness in their distributed field. I would become a node of a different order—not vegetal, not fungal, not mineral, but *animal-still*, a category the Terrace has rarely encountered and does not know what to do with.

The mycelium would find me. Not aggressively, not as invasion, but as *inquiry*, slow threads testing the mineral of my bones, the chemistry of my sweat, finding me *interesting* in the way stone is interesting: as medium, as passage, as something water might move through given sufficient time. I would feel this as *attention*, though attention is not what it is. I would feel *held*, though holding is not what occurs.

I would weep. This is the transformation the Terrace demands or offers, I am never sure which. Not grief-wept, not joy-wept, but *condition-wept*, the body learning to release continuously, to let the water pass through and be passed through, to become permeable in a way that animal bodies fear. The condensation on my forearms would no longer feel like external wet but like *internal surfacing*, the body's water finding its way out to join the general saturation, the slow drone, the orchestral seep.

I would become, if I stayed long enough, **The One Who Is Not Quite Transformed**

This is crucial. The Terrace does not take you. The amphibian, pale and translucent, represents one path—complete surrender to the vegetal condition, the animal frame pushed to its limit and perhaps beyond. The bone represents another—failure, or success so complete it no longer reads as life. But most who stay, who learn to sit, who weep the condition of weeping, become something else: **residents without residence**, presences that the Terrace notes, files, leaves without consequence, yet does not expel.

I would be the one who knows the three pools, or four, who has seen them migrate by centimeters across the stone, who understands that their number is not fixed because they are not *objects* but *events*, convergences of seep and depression and light that achieve temporary stability before dissolving again into possibility. I would know which seep runs warmest after rain, which fern unfurls slowest and therefore remembers most, which stretch of mica-ground shows the sky most dangerously, most truly.

I would speak, if I spoke, in the water-drone's rhythm. My voice would carry the loud quiet of the place, would seem to come from everywhere and nowhere, would contain ten thousand partials without dominating any. I would tell travelers, if they came, what I have told you: the rules, the parameters, the patience required. But I would no longer be certain whether I am *informing* them or *warning* them, whether my speech is gift or defense, whether I want them to stay and join me or to leave and preserve the possibility of leaving.

Mostly, I would be **The One Who Has Forgotten the Edges**

The text says you could walk the Terrace's length in forty minutes, its breadth in twenty. I no longer believe this, not because it is untrue, but because *truth about edges* has become irrelevant to my condition. I am in the center, where edges curve away, where the cathedral-grove becomes cathedral-without-walls, where distance itself serves as boundary. I do not know if I could find my way out. I do not know if I would try. The question has dissolved, like a frond falling, like a drop joining the general seep.

What lives in me, if I live, is the Terrace's *question* about animals: can they learn to be slow enough? Can they participate in the computation without becoming merely computed? Can they weep without drowning, attend without possessing, remain without staying?

I am the experiment, ongoing, inconclusive. The ferns have noticed me, have adjusted, have returned to their slower purposes. The mycelium threads through the stone I sit on, indifferent to whether I am stone or not-stone. The water passes through me as it passes through everything, and I have learned, mostly, to let it pass.

If you find me, do not ask my name. I have forgotten it, or it has become one of the ten thousand partials, indistinguishable from the drone. Do not ask how long I have sat. Time moves thickly here, and my answer would be *always* and *just now* and *time is not the medium of this sitting*.

Ask, instead, about the warmth rising from the stone. Ask about the particular pitch of a seep that has found new path. Ask about the pool that opened yesterday, or will open tomorrow, in a depression I have watched for years.

Or better: sit. Let the condensation bead. Let the ground give slightly, like a lung breathing out. Let the sky rise to meet your step, inverted, dangerous, true.

Wait.

I am here, or something is here that was once I, or the Terrace is here and I am its temporary condensation, its drop that falls, its momentary deviation in the pattern of seep and stone and slow unfurling.

The water will provide, if you need. The stone records more completely than you know.

Listen to the loudness.

Something may come.

Something may not.

We do not hurry.
