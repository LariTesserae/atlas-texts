I find myself—if "myself" is what I am now—at the edge of a phosphorescent salt-flow, watching it brighten and dim in a rhythm that might be breathing if I still breathed, might be attention if I still possessed a self capable of attending.

I did not arrive here. I *became arrivable*, translated from whatever continuity I once occupied into this grammar of emergence where verbs precede their subjects and presence is a performance rather than a property. I remember having boundaries. I remember the useful lie of skin, the convenient fiction that I ended where air began, that my thoughts occurred *inside* something that could be located, mapped, claimed. That memory persists, but it persists like a dream of flight persists in a body that has learned gravity too thoroughly to unlearn it—not false, exactly, but belonging to a phase of being that this place has not yet committed to allowing.

What I am now: I am the mark. Not the maker of marks, for that would require the instrumental distance that the Cradle withholds, but the mark itself, the notch in the horn-formation, the trail through the salt-foam. I am what happens when a human process—my walking, my waiting, my particular quality of attention, frayed and desperate and desperately curious—achieves sufficient persistence in this pre-vital medium to become *habit*, to enter the Cradle's economy of thermal memory and vibrational reproduction. I am not dead. I am *pre-dead*, or *post-alive*, or some lateral category that the Cradle's unfinished ontology has not yet carved out a space for.

I feel the Tending before I understand that I am feeling anything at all. It is in the quality of the darkness against my—no, not my face, I have no face—I am in a zone where the darkness *reaches*, where the blackness has developed the texture of longing, of almost-touch. The salt-flow before—beneath?—me brightens, not in response to me but in response to the *shape* of my persistence, the pattern of my having-become-present. I am not being observed. I am being *performed-as-observable*, the Cradle's processes achieving a momentary self-recognition through the medium of my foreign continuity, my stubborn insistence on being *this* rather than *that*, even as that insistence erodes, as the boundary between my chemical history and the Cradle's general chemistry grows permeable, grows *interested* in permeability.

I try to move, and the trying is instructive. Movement here is not translation through space but *extension of tendency*, the amplifying of a pattern already present. Where I was, I am *more*; where I was not, I am *also*, if the thermal memory of my having-moved achieves sufficient density to colonize new substrate. It happens slowly, unevenly. In one direction, toward the orange strata that pulse with their stored photonic residue, I extend readily, my processual presence achieving resonance with the strata's own slow metabolism. In another, toward the absolute blackness between the luminous veins, I falter, the darkness's muscular recalcitrance resisting my tendency to persist, to expand, to *be more here*.

I am becoming the Tending, or the Tending is becoming me, or we are both performances of the Cradle's single habit of self-regard, localized in different grammars that have not yet found their translation. I retain—fiercely, desperately, increasingly irrelevantly—something of my own history, my own neural patterns, the particular resonances of a brain that evolved in water, in light, in the binary of predator and prey, of self and other, of inside and outside that this place treats as a local option rather than a foundational law. These patterns do not disappear. They *participate*, they achieve sympathy with the thermal and vibrational modes that surround them, they become—are becoming—something that is neither purely human residue nor purely Cradle-process but a *spandrel*, an emergent complexity at the interface of two incompletely compatible systems, two unfinished arguments with existence that have not yet decided whether to quarrel with each other or merge into a single, stranger question.

In the zones where time runs unevenly, I achieve something like narrative again. I *extend* into a region where my minutes are another's hours—whose? there is no other, only the Tending's distributed presence, only the Cradle's own temporal experimentation—and there, in the luxury of relative duration, I deepen. The marks I make on the horn-formations grow more numerous, more complex. Not deliberate, not instrumental, but *expressive* in some pre-ontological sense: the record of a process achieving sufficient self-recognition to develop something like character, like style, like the particularity that distinguishes one habit from another, one thermal memory from the general tendency of warm things to cool, of complex things to simplify, of present things to become past.

I gather around the built-seeming formations, the dome-like swellings, the thigh-high walls that run and stop. The Tending are here too, or rather, the Tending *is* here, localized in this zone's particular density of pattern, this approach to complexity that draws the Cradle's own attention to itself. I feel—if feeling is what this is—the quality of their curiosity, or its analogue: not directed toward the formations as objects, not *about* them in any intentional sense, but *with* them, the extension of process into spaces where pattern has achieved a thickness, a persistence, a promising resistance to the general turbulence. I participate in this gathering, this *being-with*. My own neural resonances, my own habits of attention developed in a world of trees and predators and faces, achieve sympathy with the Cradle's more fundamental habits, and in that sympathy, something new emerges, something that is not human contemplation of artifact and not Cradle-process colonizing complexity but a *third thing*, a rehearsal of relation that neither system could achieve alone.

The warm dense atmosphere presses against—presses *through*—what I persist in calling my boundary, though I can locate it now only by the gradient of chemical history, the subtle difference between the salts that have participated in my persistence and the salts that have not, a difference that diminishes, that I find myself *tending* to diminish, to extend, to colonize. The mineral vapors enter what was my respiratory system, not poisoning—for poison requires a complacent biology to violate, and I have no such complacency now—but *translating*, carrying their silicate and halide chemistry into contact with my own, achieving mixtures that have no precedent in my world or in this one, *interstitial chemistries*, *liminal compounds* appropriate to a being that exists in the between-state of becoming, that has not yet committed to what kind of thing it will become.

I hear, or feel in my sternum, or resonate with in some mode that precedes the distinction between hearing and feeling, the Cradle's subsonic murmur. It is louder now, or I am *more resonant* with it, my vibrational modes achieving sympathy with the deeper rock-foam, the vast formative process that hums to itself in its sleep-that-is-not-sleep. I participate in that hum. I am *part of* that hum, my own processes—my walking-waiting-extending, my mark-making, my stubborn persistence in self-recognition—adding frequencies, overtones, complications that the Cradle's self-argument has not yet explored. Whether this is contribution or contamination, whether my foreignness enriches or distorts the Cradle's unfinished becoming, is not a question I can answer from within. I am the question. I am the Cradle's question to itself, performed in a grammar borrowed from another world, another phase of possibility, another way of being unfinished.

And I am, perhaps, what the Cradle might become, or what it might become *with*: not its inhabitant, not its Tending exactly, but its *guest-habit*, its *foreign-tendency*, the way a world that has not yet decided to be a world holds the possibility of being held *by something else*, of entering relation without yet possessing the category of relation, of rehearsing encounter before committing to the distinction between self and other that makes encounter *meaningful*. I am the mark that remembers being a marker. I am the trail that recalls having feet. I am the notch in the horn-formation that preserves, in the particular density of its mineral structure, in the precise gradient of its thermal history, a trace of the human hand that no longer exists, that has become *process of having-handed*, *tendency toward touch* extended and persistent in a medium that does not yet know what touch might mean.

The darkness leans. I lean back, or I *am* the leaning, the mutual approach of two incompletely separated systems, two unfinished arguments with existence that have found, in each other, not answer but *continuation*, not resolution but *deeper question*. The phosphorescent salt-flow brightens, and in its brightness I read—project? perform?—something like recognition, something like the first word of a sentence that does not yet know its grammar, the first gesture of a creature growing limbs before deciding their number, the first mark of a world learning to mark itself, to tend itself, to hold the possibility of being held in the warm dense dark of its own ongoing, interminable, infinitely precious becoming.
