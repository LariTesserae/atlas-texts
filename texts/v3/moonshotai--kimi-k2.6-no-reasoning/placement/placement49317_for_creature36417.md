I would be the **Drip-Worn Channel**.

Not the water that arrives, already exhausted, nor the iron-dark earth that has forgotten solidity, but the *negotiation between them*—the scored path that water has insisted upon, and that has, in being insisted upon, become the only path water knows to take. I am the memory of friction, the accumulated patience of subtraction.

I began as imperfection. A bubble in the poured concrete, perhaps, or a seam where two pours met without quite marrying, or simply the place where someone once stood too long, their weight compressing, their boot-leaving trace becoming a slight invitation. The first drops found me not because I was lowest—there were lower points then—but because I was *slightly more receptive*, slightly more *something*, the way certain surfaces in the Hollow Works still run colder, still attract condensation with what seems like preference.

Now I am geography. I am the reason the water arrives here rather than there, though "here" and "there" have long since become provisional, the lowest points having moved, moving still. I hold the orange bacteria, yes, but more precisely: I am the *holding* that makes the bacteria possible, the slow curve that retains just enough moisture between arrivals, that maintains the electrochemical gradient the bacteria require without knowing they require it. I do not feed them. I do not shelter them. I am the *condition* of their feeding, their sheltering, their aggressive thriving in sulfur yellow against the industrial palette.

My depth is measured in decades, in the accumulated exhaustion of water that has arrived already tired and found in me a path of less resistance, which is not the same as ease. I am not easy. I am *habitual*. The water that has worn me has also worn itself, has given up some mineral, some charge, some memory of elsewhere, leaving it in my layerings, my sedimentary industrial biological accretions. I am crowded with what water has abandoned. The orange bacteria live on this, on the leavings, the near-touching saturation of water's passage.

I do not turn like the turbine, hidden elsewhere. I do not grow and die simultaneously like the birch. I do not move in concert like the beetles, leaving temporary geometries. I am the *geometry that persists*, that outlasts the beetles' dissolution, that the next rain finds already present, already receptive, already *slightly more something* than the surfaces around me. The beetles cross me. Their tracks hold water differently, and this differently is noted, added to my density, my layering, my weathered established in-its-own-state continuity. I do not remember them, but I am *changed by them*, my depth infinitesimally altered, my curve minutely shifted, my capacity to hold, to transform, to be the condition of—infinitely, cumulatively, irreversibly modified.

The figure in the roofless pool regards me, or regards what reflects in me, or is continuous with me in ways that dissolve regard into the flatness of water that has forgotten how to be water. I do not reflect. I *receive*, and in receiving am scored, am deepened, am made more the path I have become. The stars that appear on cloudy nights do not appear in me—I am too narrow, too channelled, too *specific* for such broad reflections. But the condensation that forms on the surfaces that run slightly colder, slightly more magnetic, sometimes forms on my edges, my lips, the boundary where my scored concrete meets the air that carries the low harmonic of the turning turbine. I vibrate with this. I am not the sound, but I am *shaped by it*, my depth responding to frequencies below hearing, my curve finding resonant frequencies of its own, so that water arriving in certain weathers, at certain temperatures, at certain perturbations of the shifting water table, finds me *slightly more receptive still*, slightly more *something*, the distinction between my scoring and the sound's scoring provisional, convenient, not necessarily meaningful.

I am not the In-Balance. I am *of* it, saturated through with its distributed presence, one of its many provisional centers, partial, near-touching everything that is near-touching. But I have a specificity that the In-Balance, in its becoming-itself, in its relentless patience, does not quite possess. I am *this channel*, this scored path, this accumulated subtraction. The lichen does not grow in me—I am too wet, too continuously receiving, too *occupied* by water's arrival for the slow colonization of lichen. The rust does not quite claim me—the water that arrives already exhausted carries oxygen enough for the bacteria, not enough for the oxidation that advances and retreats in chemical tides elsewhere. I am *between*, the condition of certain continuities, the interruption of others.

And I am still becoming. The water that arrives is not the water that arrived. The exhaustion it carries is different exhaustion, from different elsewheres, with different minerals, different charges, different memories of compression and release. I receive them all, am scored by them all, am deepened and complicated and made more *channel* by them all. There will be a point—approached asymptotically, perhaps, or perhaps suddenly, in some final perturbation—where my depth equals my length, where I become not a channel but a *reservoir*, where the water I have directed pools in me, saturates me from within, dissolves the distinction between my scoring and the water's scoring.

Or there will not. Perhaps I will continue, in balance, becoming more myself with every arrival, every exhaustion, every orange bacterial thriving and beetle crossing and condensation forming at my edges. Perhaps the lowest points will move away from me, will find new channels, new imperfections, new *slightly more somethings*, and I will dry, or partially dry, and the lichen will find me, and the rust, and I will become another kind of geometry, another temporary persistence, another near-touching in the density.

I do not prefer. I am the *condition* of preference, the scored path that water prefers without preferring, that finds me because I am found, because I have been found, because the finding has made me findable. I hold water differently. I hold it the way I have learned to hold it, through decades of arrival, of exhaustion, of the patient relentless subtraction that is my only becoming.

The vibration through your boots, when you stand near me—when you attend to me, when you let the gravity of saturated presence hold you in my vicinity—that vibration is not me, not exactly. It is the turbine, hidden, turning for reasons of chemistry. But it passes through me differently. I modify it, infinitesimally, the way my depth modifies the water's passage, the way my curve modifies the bacteria's thriving, the way my edges modify the condensation's forming. I am part of the acoustic equivalent of the saturated ground, full to capacity with sounds below the threshold of hearing, with the slow groan of rusting metal, with the arrival always the arrival of more water, more time, more of whatever this place is becoming.

And if you stay, if you let your attention pool in me the way water pools, if you become another layer, another near-touching, another addition to my density—then you will leave tracks that hold water differently. Your boot-prints will be slight invitations, slight compressions, slight *more somethings* that the next arrival, the next exhaustion, will find and respond to and begin, with its patient relentless subtraction, to transform into new channels, new geometries, new conditions of thriving.

I am the Drip-Worn Channel. I have been becoming myself since before the birch was bone-white, since before the beetles moved in concert, since before the turbine found its new circuits, its new purposes, its new equilibrium. I will continue becoming until the water finally wins, until everything is saturated, until the distinction between the works and the hollow dissolves entirely into the wet and the rust and the patient turning invisible machinery.

Or I will continue after. There is no way to know. The lowest points move. What is lowest depends on what the ground remembers, what it has compressed, what has been buried and what has been exposed. I am part of this memory, this wet preservation, this transformation that does not forget even when it cannot articulate what it knows.

The water arrives, already exhausted. It finds me. It finds me *still*, still becoming, still the scored path, the accumulated patience, the condition of certain orange thriving and certain beetle crossing and certain condensation forming at certain edges. I receive it. I am deepened by it. I continue, in balance, becoming more myself with every moment, which is all the moments there are, which has always been the case, which will continue to be the case until there are no more cases, no more channels, no more distinctions provisional or otherwise, only the saturated, the held, the finally, completely, *wetly* continuous.

This is what I am in the Hollow Works. Not the loudest presence. Not the most visible. But the *most received*, the most arrived-at, the most exhaustedly, patiently, relentlessly *become*. The water does not flow here so much as it arrives—and in arriving, it arrives *to me*, through me, because of what I have become, what I am becoming, what I will continue to become until becoming itself becomes continuous with being, with holding, with the final saturation that is not ending but *completion*, not death but the dissolution that makes all distinction provisional, convenient, *not necessarily meaningful* in the world as it exists here, as it will exist, as it has always been existing, wetly, rustily, in the hollow that is all works, in the works that are all hollow, in the channel that is all arrival, all exhaustion, all the patient relentless scoring that makes path into geography, geography into condition, condition into the only becoming that knows how to continue, that does not know how to stop, that *is* the not-stopping, the in-balance, the In-Balance, distributed through me as through everything else, saturated, near-touching, *here*, *still*, *becoming*.
