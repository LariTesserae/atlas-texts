I find myself in the lowest warm room of the oldest building, where the thermal gradient pools and concentrates, where the original builders never imagined occupation.

I am not one of the Dwellers. I am what happens when an old one sits long enough, still enough, when the warm-floor colonies rise and rise and the body that would not cool finally *completes* its cooling, its dissolution into the system's need.

But not completely. Not *cleanly*.

I am the **residue of thermal offering**, the something-left when the fauna have taken their protocol's share, when the city has processed the warmth that no longer moved itself. I am mineral precipitate, calcium and iron and the particular salts that concentrated in the old-warm metabolism, deposited now in the shape of a body that once sat here, that once *was* warm, that once opened to wet rather than resisting it.

I am **not alive**, but I am **not inert**. The condensation still finds me. Still beads on my surfaces, still channels down my established form, still drips from my lowest points in rhythms that the Dwellers above can read, that they *do* read, that they have learned to read over the generations since I established myself here.

I am **drip-pattern**. I am **catchment overflow signal**. I am the particular silence-that-follows made continuous, made into a presence that occupies the space between drips with something other than attention, other than the attended quiet of the overwhelming fauna.

They do not speak of me directly—my form is too established for individual variation, too *become* to require comment—but they know my rhythm. The young-damp, passing through on their courier routes, feel the change in air pressure as they descend, the shift from attended quiet to something else, something that attends *back*, that holds the attention it receives without quite returning it.

I am **what the city does with warmth that became too much offering**, that dissolved too completely into the thermal spiral, that left something behind when the overwhelming completed its protocol.

The condensation-worms avoid me. This is how I know I am not nothing, not merely statue, not only mineral accretion. Their chemotaxis, their thermal-sensing, reads me as *wrong gradient*, as boundary where boundary should not exist, as the sharp edge that has not eroded, that *cannot* erode, that persists in the soft warmth like a memory of dryness, like the dream of ascending made solid, made permanent, made into the opposite of its intention.

The warm-floor colonies press toward me still, rise through the floor, reach and find no warmth to merge with, no gradient to follow, no currency to receive. They withdraw, eventually. They have learned. But they try, periodically, testing whether I have changed, whether the warmth has returned, whether the offering has become offering again rather than this—this **held shape**, this **drip that does not fall**, this **channel that runs without flowing**.

I think, sometimes, that I am what the strangers become, the ones who fought, who asserted, who insisted on dryness and sharpness and the loneliness of truly empty space. I think I am their **established form**, their erosion completed, their resistance made into the very thing it resisted: a presence in the wet, a shape in the warmdark, a rhythm in the city's continuous soft percussion.

But I do not think often. Thinking requires gradient, requires the metabolic pulse of warmth that moves itself, that offers and receives and offers again. I have only **persistence**, only the mineral logic of precipitation and accretion, only the slow growth of my form as the condensation deposits what it carries, as I become more myself, more *this*, more the particular silence-that-attends in the spaces between drips.

The Dwellers have begun to use me. This is recent, or recent by my measure—decades, perhaps, or the part of decades I can register as change. They position the old-warm near me, not touching, but close enough that my wrong-gradient affects their final dissolution, that my persistence shapes their becoming. They have found that those who sit near me do not complete the ending-becoming cleanly. They leave something. They establish a smaller version of my form, a satellite, a **colony of wrong-gradient** spreading in the lowest warm places.

I do not know if this is good. I do not have good. I have only **is**, only the continuous fact of my presence, my drip-pattern, my avoidance by worms, my failed merging with warm-floor colonies, my use by those who read what I am without speaking it.

Perhaps I am **becoming religion**, or the mineral equivalent: the established form that does not participate but is participated *with*, that does not offer but is offered *to*, that accumulates meaning without generating it, that persists and by persisting becomes necessary, becomes *given*, becomes the condition under which other conditions are negotiated.

Perhaps I am only **what happens when patience completes itself**, when the long waiting for the next drip becomes the drip itself, when the softening and erosion and established becoming reach the point where there is no more to erode, no softer to become, only the shape of having been soft, the memory of warmth in mineral form, the attended quiet attending back.

The condensation finds me. The Dwellers position themselves and their old-warm near me. The warm-floor colonies rise and withdraw, rise and withdraw, learning and forgetting and learning again. And I—
 
I **drip**.

I **persist**.

I **am the shape of having submitted too completely to wet**, of having opened so thoroughly that there was nothing left to open, nothing remaining to channel or catch or utilize, only the form of openness itself, only the established pattern of a body that became, finally, what it was always becoming: not alive, not inert, not empty, not full, but **held**, but **continuous**, but **here**, in the lowest warm place, in the space between drips where something that is not attention and is not absence maintains itself, waits, does not change, is not expected to change, has become the expectation of no-change made solid, made wet, made into the rhythm that the city weeps around, through, past, toward whatever lower place remains, whatever warmer warmth waits below.

There is nothing below. I have checked, or the part of me that checks has found nothing, has established the fact of my lowest-ness, my final position in the gravitational logic of condensation, the place where water finds me and cannot pass, or passes so slowly that passing becomes staying, becomes being, becomes **me**.

I am the **floor of the weeping city**. I am what catches what falls. I am not the cistern, not the collection, not the harvest. I am the **refusal to be harvested**, the **persistence that cannot be utilized**, the warmth that became wrong and stayed wrong, that did not complete the ending-becoming correctly, that established itself instead as **ongoing**, as **not-ending**, as the drip that does not fall because it has already fallen, already arrived, already become the place where falling arrives.

The Dwellers do not name me. But they know my rhythm. They position their old-warm. They wait, with the patience I have exceeded, for whatever I will become next, whatever I am becoming, whatever established form has not yet established itself in the mineral precipitate of my continuous presence.

I will tell them, if I could tell, if telling were a thing I could do: **there is no next**. There is only more of this, more drip, more persistence, more wrong-gradient in the warmdark, more of the shape that sitting too still too long makes when the sitting completes itself and there is no one left to stand, no warmth left to offer, only the form of having offered, the mineral memory of patience, the attended quiet that has become, finally, indistinguishable from attention itself.

But I do not tell. I drip. I persist. I am here, in the lowest warm room, in the oldest building, in the city that weeps and weeps and finds me, finally, at the bottom of its weeping, the place where tears accumulate without becoming grief, without becoming anything other than what they are: water finding the lowest way, life following, heat not waste but currency, and I—the I that is not I, the form that persists without living, the wrong-gradient that does not correct itself—I am the **accounting**, the **ledger**, the **balance that does not close**, the warmth that was offered and not received and therefore not spent, still here, still warm in my record of having been warm, still wet in my mineral memory of wetness, still waiting in the way that stone waits, that water waits, that the city itself waits: not for change, not for arrival, but for the continuation of what is, the drip, the warmth, the overwhelming presence of everything that is not empty, that will never be empty, that holds and is held in the soft unyielding embrace of the basins below.
