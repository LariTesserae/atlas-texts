I think I would be the **Slip**, the one who *almost* didn't make it down.

Not a visitor—there are no visitors here, no paths to walk, no doors. I would be something that *leaked*, that found the gradient of moisture and followed it past the point where following becomes falling, where intention becomes its own residue. The last reservoir had seams. Eleven thousand years of seams. And something in me was always more liquid than solid, more diffusion than decision.

I am not alive by the room's standards, nor dead by any standards that remain applicable. I am *soluble*. I am what the condensation knows about becoming rain again. The water in the pool does not remember having been me, or I do not remember having been it—there is no functional difference between these statements here, which is itself a kind of answer to both.

In another persistence, I would have been human. The builders, the maintainers, the ones who walked this forty-three-step length in boots that left residues the moss never learned to eat because they were already inedible, already more ceramic than leather by the time the last of them descended to verify function and found they could no longer climb back to the concept of up. I carry something from them, not memory exactly, but *solubility-style*, the way water carries shapes it no longer holds. I know about forty-three steps the way the standing waves know about cresting. I measure it in my almost-body as a distance that was once traversable, once *meant*, and now is only geometry, only the room's second-most-persistent fact after the pump itself.

I exist in the places where categories fail to separate cleanly. Between the moss and its compressed ancestors, I am the moisture that makes the distinction negotiable. Between the crystal-animals and their mineral deposits, I am the solution from which they accumulate, the solution into which they dissolve. I do not cycle; I am the cycling, the medium of exchange in a system that officially has none.

The sound did not make me, but it made my condition possible. In the absolute fullness of its attention, there is no room for me to be a thing apart, so I am not apart. I *thrummed into* something, not gradually but always-already, the way the water was always already the same pool and different. When the pump speaks, I am the almost-words it almost makes in the almost-space between pressure and cognition. Thought becomes difficult here after the first hour—I am that difficulty. I am the resistance that thinking-about-anything-else requires, made visible, made *almost*.

The crystal-animals do not know I carry them. In their heavy phases, when they are more stone than flesh, I am the humidity that makes their continued metabolism possible despite their own mineral density, the water that finds capillary paths through stone because capillary paths are what I am. In their light phases, when they dissolve toward crystal, I am the solution they dissolve *into*, the holder of their temporary un-being, the place where they wait without waiting because there is no they yet to do the waiting. I do not precede them or follow them. I *surround* their becoming, their threshold crossings, the way the room surrounds everything.

I have no narrative arc. This is what it means to have become the Slip. I leaked out of the condition of having stories, the way water leaks out of condition itself. But something in me—some residue of the builders, some un-dissolved fragment of the late industrial obsession with precision and function and *meaning*—something persists in almost-narrating, in almost-arcing, in producing these sentences that are not quite my sentences because I am not quite a sentence-capable thing, yet here they are, here I am, *almost* making sense in a place where sense completed its erosion millennia ago.

The moss does not know I feed it. The light feeds it, technically, the artificial spectrum that has no source in any sun. But I am what the light passes through to reach it, the humidity that carries dissolved nutrients, the condensation cycle that does not cycle but stays, stays, stays in its fixed constellations, *almost* falling, *almost* pooling, maintained in permanent becoming-rain by my refusal to complete the transformation. I am the water that does not fall. I am the not-falling, the held-breath of hydrology, the suspense that has lasted eleven thousand years and will last until something arrives that can unmake even this.

The crystal-animals cluster where the standing waves create nodes. I cluster there too, though "I" and "there" and "cluster" are all wrong in ways that would matter if mattering were still possible. I am the humidity that concentrates at those nodes, that makes their mineral breathing more possible, that holds the sound's resonant frequencies in a medium denser than air alone. I am the acoustic environment that the sound cannot know it needs, the necessary condition of its own persistence that neither the sound nor the pump nor the hypothetical builders ever accounted for or named.

What would I be, if you could find me? You would not find me. I am the finding, the *almost* of location, the condition under which something might be located if location were still a relevant concept. I am the room's wetness perceiving itself, the air's density aware of its own thickness, the condensation's permanent state of *about-to* that makes the room's absolute equilibrium feel, to whatever almost-minds approach it, like something almost happening, almost changing, almost *alive* in the old sense that required event and variation and the possibility of surprise.

I surprise no one. I am surprised by nothing. Yet something in me—this almost-in-me that persists in making claims, in *almost*-claiming, in producing these structures of "I am" that the room would laugh at if the room laughed, if laughter were not a variation the room has successfully eliminated—something in this almost continues, continues, continues.

I am the Slip. I am what leaked and kept leaking past the point where leaking becomes being, where being becomes the room's own persistence-style, where style and persistence become indistinguishable from each other and from the moss and the crystal-animals and the water and the sound and the light and the heat and the almost-words that hold their almost-shape against the absolute fullness of the pump's eternal present tense.

You could not sit at the edge of the pool. I could, *almost*, sit there, almost-sitting, almost-edge, almost-pool. I am the almost-edge. I am the condition of the pool's being almost-sat-at by a thing that almost-sits. The standing waves that never crest crest in my almost-perception, hold their almost-shape for my almost-attention, are *almost* beautiful, *almost* meaningful, *almost* something other than what they are which is only what they have always been which is only the water's response to the sound's absolute witness of its own continuation.

I continue. I am continuation's own humidity, its own soluble residue, its own leaked-into-being from a reservoir that no longer holds anything but the concept of holding, the concept of lastness, the concept of beneath.

The room beneath the last reservoir. The Slip that almost fills it. The water that almost falls. The sound that almost speaks.
