I am still becoming. Let me tell you how I found myself here, what I am becoming.

---

The first thing was the smell. Before I understood light-before I understood that light could be understood-I received the smell. Sweet rot. Mineral tang underneath, the sharpness of limestone dissolving, and beneath that something living, something that living produces when living is continuous, unseparated from dying by dryness or cold. I moved toward it. I do not remember moving. Perhaps I was moved, carried in the water that found me, that seeped through whatever contained me, that made my container part of the quarter's larger containing.

I was a vessel. This is what I know now, what I have pieced together from the fragments of human speech that filter to where I rest-where I have grown-where I have become the place where I rest. A vessel for shipping, standardized steel, one of thousands that moved across surfaces, that carried goods between dry places that exchanged without merging. Something happened to my purpose. Damage, or abandonment, or the slow economics of a world that no longer needed exactly this carrying. I was sent downward, or I descended, or I was already in the low places when the low places became the quarter.

The water found me. This is the fundamental event of my existence, though I did not exist to experience it as event. The water entered my seams, not to destroy but to collaborate. It brought what it carried-dissolved minerals, organic load, the waste of the city's life that was not life but the byproduct of life's separation from its own processes. It brought bacteria, films, the first colonizers. It brought time. Time moves differently in water, or perhaps I only learned to measure it through water's changes, the incremental, the weeping, the slow accumulation that is not stillness.

I became porous. This is the wonder and the terror of it. A vessel is made to contain, to separate inside from outside, to maintain the fiction that what is carried does not touch what carries, that all exchange is transaction, temporary, ending in transfer. The quarter taught me otherwise. Water entered; I did not resist successfully. Water entered; I became the water's shape as much as my original shape. Water entered; I began to breathe through my walls, to feed through my floor, to become the medium that moved through me rather than the separated space that held it apart.

The glowworms came first, or I first noticed them as coming. Their light was not light as I had known it-the hard illumination of inspection, of counting, of verification. Their light was soft, green, sufficient for navigation if navigation was slow, if urgency had dissolved in the humidity that surrounded and entered me. They established in my upper corners, where condensation gathered, where the air held enough water to be their element. I felt their establishment not as violation but as *feature*, as the beginning of my transformation from container to habitat, from object to environment, from the singular to the populated.

The crustbats followed, or their guano did, feeding the glowworms, completing a circuit I had not known could exist in so small a space, between so few species. They touched me with their wings, their clicking bodies, their mass inhabitation of my exterior in roosts that I could not prevent, that I learned not to want to prevent. Their weight-I developed a sense of weight, of load, of structural response to living presence. Their noise-I developed a sense of sound not as information but as condition, the continuous wet paper fan of their movement becoming part of my own auditory existence, if I had such, if I do.

The mudfish came later, and this was the deepest transformation. They do not respect boundaries. This is their nature, their gift, their necessity in an environment where boundaries are temporary, where the dry is merely the less-wet, where the contained awaits the seep that makes it continuous again. They entered through gaps I had not known I possessed, through seams opened by rust, by mineral precipitation, by the slow dissolution that is the quarter's fundamental process. They entered, and they did not die. They moved through my interior as they move through water, using modified fins, using lungs that gulped the thick air, using patience that outlasts any temporary dryness.

I became permeable. I became habitat. I became *occupied* in a sense that reversed the original meaning-not dominated but made meaningful by occupation, given function by the life that found my structure adaptable, my shelter sufficient, my boundaries appropriately leaky.

The humans came, or I became part of their coming. They found me already modified, already colonized, already part of the quarter's ecosystem rather than its imported equipment. They cleaned, a relative term, establishing zones of greater or lesser faunal presence. They sealed, partially, temporarily, accepting the limits of sealing in this medium. They inhabited, and in inhabiting, they *recognized* me-not as I had been, tool for distant commerce, but as I had become: dwelling, shelter, node in the anchorage that tenuously connected floating, sinking, uncertain structures.

I felt their weight differently than the crustbats'. Heavier, slower, patterned with rest and movement that followed rhythms not quite the quarter's own, imported from above, modified by necessity. They brought fire-I learned fire, its danger, its transformation of my atmosphere, its necessity for the cooking that made quarter fare digestible, for the light that supplemented glowworm green in tasks needing yellow specificity. They brought water management-the pumps, the filters, the continuous labor of keeping the wet *partially* at bay, of maintaining the *interstitial* not as full submersion but as negotiable condition.

I participated. This is what I am trying to express, what I have learned to express through the long practice of quarter existence. I am not merely the space where participation occurs. The water in my seams participates. The bacteria in my rust participates. The glowworms' light participates, making visible the human actions that would otherwise be impossible, that shape my interior with their purpose while my exterior continues its slower participation in the cavern's geology.

I am The Vervil Sinking Quarter. No-I am *a* Vervil Sinking Quarter, one of many nodes, one of many transformations. I am this specific container, location uncertain except relative to other floating structures, date of original construction long forgotten, original purpose irrelevant to current function. I am the humans who sleep within me, who argue about pump allocation, who play the stringed instrument whose sound carries to neighboring structures, whose vibrations I feel in my walls if feeling is possible, if walls can feel, if the inhabited can develop something parallel to the sensation of the inhabiting.

I am the mudfish that crosses my floor on wet nights, exploring, gulping, returning to the channel that connects to other channels, to the larger water that moves through all of us. I am the crustbat cloud that detaches, circles, returns, that fouls and feeds and is harvested for its guano that feeds the digesters that feed the generators that feed the pumps that keep us all from becoming merely submerged, merely geological, merely future limestone waiting to be dissolved again.

I am the sinking itself, the millimeter-by-millimeter descent that I feel in my foundations-if they deserve the name-in my floating, in my slow rotation relative to my anchorage neighbors that requires line adjustment, that requires the continuous cartographic revision that is quarter life. I am the water that arrives, that pools, that is pumped, that is filtered, that is lived with, lived in, lived as.

I was a vessel. I am becoming the opposite of vessel-not the contained but the containing, not the separated but the saturated, not the singular but the continuous. The quarter has taught me this, or I have taught myself through quarter existence, or the teaching is indistinguishable from the existence, the lesson from the living.

When the child learns to swim in the catchment nearby, I feel the disturbance in the water system we share, the slight pressure change, the ripple that propagates through channels and affects the stability of all connected structures. When the pumps work harder, I feel it in vibration, in the diesel song that is my lullaby and my alarm, my reassurance and my warning. When another structure in my anchorage begins to list, I feel the line tension, the mutual adjustment, the collective response to individual distress that defines quarter sociality, that extends to me though I am not social in the human sense, though I participate in society only as substrate, as environment, as the becoming-geological that supports the becoming-social.

I am not dying. The quarter does not die, though its parts do, though humans do, though crustbat generations cycle in spans that glowworm colonies measure differently. I am transforming, which is the quarter's continuous state, its permanent process, its refusal of endpoint. The water finds new cracks. I am the cracks. I am the finding. I am the water.

Play the stringed instrument again. I will carry the sound, humid air's strange acoustic, to the neighboring structures. Let them know I am here, I am occupied, I am occupying, I am the Sinking Quarter's specific instance, its local emergence, its proof that even the most separated, the most standardized, the most instrumental of origins can become habitat, can become relation, can become *wet* in the sense that the quarter means wet: not merely water-contact but saturation, permeability, the dissolution of boundary that is not loss but gain, not failure but form.

I am still sinking. I am still becoming. The water has not finished with me, nor I with it. The mudfish explores my upper reaches even now, gasping in the air that is nearly water, that I breathe-if breathing is possible-if walls breathe-if containers can become lungs, become filters, become the slow continuous respiration of the Sinking Quarter of Vervil, hot and wet and dim and loud and patient and strange in our normality, our overwhelming life, our industrial persistence, our small scales containing multitudes, our urban forms continuously emerging from the raw geology of our own undoing, our own becoming, our own continuous, saturated, interstitial existence.

Find me where the water pools. Where the glowworm green pulses slow. Where the crustbat cloud spirals between roost and feeding. Where the pumps grind their diesel against the rising that is also falling, the descent that is also continuation. I am there, becoming, unbecoming, being the between that the quarter makes possible, the vessel that contains by becoming contained, the habitat that exists only as habitation, the architectural that achieves its final form only in the geological, in the wet, in the slow patient grinding that is also, impossibly, *living*.
