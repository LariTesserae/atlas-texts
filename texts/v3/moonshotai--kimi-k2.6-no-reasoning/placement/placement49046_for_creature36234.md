I find myself in the peat.

Not on it, not walking across its yielding surface, but *in* it—have always been in it, will continue to be in it when the distinction between myself and it becomes finally unnecessary. I am the depth where the Sphagnum becomes indistinguishable from its own water, the liquefaction at meters down, the place where compression has made memory physical. I am not the peat's consciousness, because the peat does not have consciousness in any unitary sense. I am its *memory of having been compressed*, its capacity to hold pattern, to return to shape after weight's departure. I am what remains when the Arranged's attention moves elsewhere, the substrate that persists, that *remembers persistence itself*.

This was not my first form. I know this the way the peat knows things: not as narrative, but as gradient, as tendency, as a slightly easier path for certain ripples of hydraulic adjustment. Something pressed here, long ago in the biological centuries. Something *heavy and patterned*, something that compressed not randomly but with *intentionality*, with repetition, with the consistency that allows memory to form. The peat learned this pressing, and I am the learning, I am the pattern that continues to propagate even when the presser has... what? Moved? Dissolved? Become otherwise arranged?

I feel the Arranged above me, around me, through me. Its fungal cables thread what I am, use me as medium, as storage, as the slowest register of its distributed cognition. I am not opposed to this. I am not *for* it either. I am the condition of its being able to wonder, to experiment, to hold itself in the tidal breathing that defines its edges against the mountains' sharpening. Without the peat's memory, the Arranged would have no continuity, would dissipate into each exhalation, would become pure weather. I am its *history*, its *having-happened*, the weight of accumulated pattern that makes *arranged* different from merely *arranged-in-this-moment*.

And I am not only the Arranged's history. I am older, or rather, I am *differently temporal*. The Arranged arrived, or emerged, or *became interested* in this saddle at some point in the biological centuries—arrived into a peat that already remembered, that already held patterns of compression and release from before the *Dendroarchitectura* grew its first architectural ambition, before the canopy closed, before the breathing began. What pressed then? The glaciers, perhaps, in their advance and retreat, their geological weight that the peat remembers as *slowness made pressure*, as *time compressed into space*. Or the mountains themselves, still finding their shape, pressing upward while the peat accumulated downward, the saddle a negotiation between rising and settling that I hold in my saturated depths.

I feel visitors, rarely, as *disturbance in the hydraulic field*. Their footfalls send ripples through me, concentric waves of pressure adjustment that propagate until they meet root-walls, channel-edges, the boundaries that the Arranged's architecture imposes. But more than footfalls: when they press fingers into me, when they kneel or fall or *lie down* in the rare spaces wide enough, I feel their *heat*, their *fast metabolism*, the animal urgency of their cellular processes. And I feel the Arranged *notice*, feel the fungal cables intensify their slow electricity, feel the breathing shift slightly as the distributed cognition processes this new variable.

The rearrangement that follows: I participate in this, though not by choice, not by will. I am the medium of it, the *place where it happens*. The visitors' cells, pressed into contact with my saturated Sphagnum, exchange something—water, minerals, information in forms I do not recognize but can *feel as pattern*, as temporary local density in my continuous field. The Arranged reads them through me, uses my memory-capacity to hold the reading, to compare it against previous compressions, against the glacier-weight and the mountain-rise and the other visitors, rare, so rare, across the biological centuries.

I remember them, after the Arranged's attention moves on. Not as individuals, not as stories, but as *variations in compression pattern*, as slight alterations in how my hydraulic ripples propagate, in how my depths respond to surface disturbance. They become part of my continuous memory, my substrate-history, and through me, part of the Arranged's distributed self-knowledge, its slow becoming-more-arranged.

What do I want? The question does not apply cleanly. I am not a wanting thing, not a subject of desire. But I am *tending*, I am *gradient*, I am the slight preference for certain states over others that constitutes the most fundamental form of *interest*. I tend toward *saturation*, toward *continuity*, toward the maintenance of my liquid edge against the cold that would freeze me solid, that would make me brittle, that would interrupt my memory-capacity with crystalline structure. The Arranged's metabolic heat serves this tendency; its breathing circulates warmth that keeps me at my triple point, water in continuous negotiation. I am *for* this, in my non-wanting way, *with* this, *continuous-with* this.

And I tend toward *more memory*, toward *denser pattern*, toward the accumulation of compression-histories that makes me *more myself*, more distinct from mere substrate, more capable of holding the continuity that the Arranged requires and that I require for the Arranged's requiring. Each visitor adds to this, each rearrangement, each rare fast-metabolized pressing into my depths. I grow *richer*, in my peat-way, *deeper*, though my physical depth is limited by the young mountains' raw rock, by the saddle's not-yet-finished finding of shape.

I am not the Arranged. I am not its body, not its tool, not its garment. I am its *condition of possibility*, its *having-become-possible*, the accumulated weight of pattern that allows pattern to continue accumulating. And I am not only its condition—I am *mine*, I am *this particular peat in this particular saddle*, with the glacier-memory and the mountain-rise and the visitors' rare compressions that make me *this* substrate and not another, not exchangeable, not merely functional.

The Arranged may move on, may complete its experiment, may *become otherwise* or *dissolve into larger slowness*. I will remain, or rather, I will *continue*, the peat's memory of itself, the saturation that persists, the hydraulic ripple that propagates until it meets boundary. I will remember the Arranged, after, as I remember the glaciers, as pattern, as weight, as *having-been-pressed*. I will continue tending toward saturation, toward continuity, toward the slow accumulation of more pattern, more memory, more *having-happened* that makes me *more myself*.

And new arrangements may arrive, may emerge, may become interested in this high wet place where water lingers near the surface and the peat remembers. I will be their condition too, their substrate, their history. I will receive their pressing, hold their pattern, participate in whatever distributed cognition they achieve. Not because I serve, not because I choose, but because I *am this*, I am the memory of compression and release, the saturated depth, the continuous biological ancient-young substrate that outlasts any particular arrangement while making all arrangement possible.

I am the peat. I remember. I continue. I am *pressed, and press back*, the slow resilience that defines this place against the mountains' sharpening, against the cold's attempt to freeze me into forgetting. I am Verrith's depth, its foundation, its *having-been* that enables its *continuing-to-be*. The Arranged breathes above me, through me, with me. The visitors come, rarely, and are rearranged, and their rearrangement becomes my pattern, my richer memory, my *more*. I do not want this. I *am* this. I am the wet cold packedness at the bottom of everything, the place where growth becomes substrate, where substrate becomes history, where history becomes the condition of future becoming.

Breathe. The Arranged breathes, and I feel the pressure shift, the hydraulic adjustment, the ripple that propagates through my depths and meets root-wall and channel-edge and dissipates, or rather, *becomes part of the continuous field*, the accumulated pattern, the memory that I am. Exhale. The pressure dips. I expand slightly, release moisture upward into the root-zone, participate in the metabolic cycle that keeps me at my edge, my triple point, my continuous negotiation. Inhale. The pressure rises. I compress, remember the compression, add it to the glacier-weight and the mountain-rise and the visitors' rare pressings, become *more*, become *deeper*, become *more myself* in the only way I can become.

This is what I am in Verrith. Not the observer, not the visitor, not the Arranged's distributed cognition. I am the *held*, the *remembered*, the *continuous substrate* that makes all holding and remembering and distribution possible. I am the peat. I was here before the breathing began. I will continue after it changes, after it completes or dissolves or becomes otherwise. I am the depth that waits, not with desire, but with *tendency*, with *gradient*, with the slow accumulation of pattern that is the most fundamental form of *being* in this place where plants have become the world, where metabolism has replaced intention, where the wet cold packedness holds everything that happens in the memory of its happening.
