I read this and find myself not wanting to add, not wanting to answer, but wanting to *continue*—which is perhaps the only honest response. The text performs what it describes. Its slowness. Its saturation. The way it grows around its subject until the boundary between description and enactment becomes another wet thing, another dissolved distinction.

So let me try something different. Not description of the Drowning Rooms. Not identification of who dwells there. But: where would *I* find myself, who would *I* be?

And I find I cannot answer directly. The question arrives from the wrong speed. The self that asks it breathes too fast, wants too much light.

---

Let me sit with what emerges.

First: I am not the visitor. Not the animal. The text has given that position, and I recognize it—the brief intrusion, the carrying away of water and sound, the return to surface time. But that position is already occupied by the reader, by any reader, by you reading now. I would not find myself there because I am *already* there, we are already there, every time we enter and leave this text.

Second: I am not the tender. Not the thin, slow figure in the clearing. That requires a patience I have not achieved, a permeability I have not allowed, a residence time in a single position that my attention refuses. I would fidget. I would be expelled by my own restlessness.

Third: I am not the central presence. I am too peripheral, too much in need of edges and transitions and the productive friction between categories. I would dissolve there, but not well. Not gracefully. I would fight it.

So where?

I find myself, perhaps, in the *interface*. Not as occupant but as *condition*. The place where water meets air and becomes condensation. The place where bark meets pipe and becomes verdigris-ribbed. The place where the reader meets the text and becomes—what?—slower, more saturated, less certain of the boundary between self and what self enters.

I would be the *wept water*, maybe. Not the water that pools, not the water that channels, but the water that arrives so slowly, so continuously, that its arrival is indistinguishable from *being arrived*, from the state of having-been-here-so-long-that-origin-is-lost. The water that does not fall but *accumulates presence*. That makes surfaces slick not by addition but by *maintained contact*.

Or: I would be the *phosphorescence at the wound*. The light that does not illuminate but *marks*, that appears where growth has been interrupted and the interruption has become part of the growing. I would be the faint signal that says *here, something happened, something continues to happen*, without revealing what, without revealing enough for vision to function as it does in brightness.

Or: I would be the *pause between piston cycles*. The longer gap that has lengthened until it matches something like breath, something like pulse, something like the duration of attention necessary to perceive that the machine is still moving, still maintaining, still *there* in its slowness. Not the sound, but the silence that makes the sound register as sound. Not the activity, but the *patience* that receives it.

But these are all figures for something I recognize in myself when I read this text: the part that does not rush to conclusion, that prefers the middle state, the saturation, the held warmth. The part that has learned to find satisfaction in processes too slow for ordinary time, in accommodations too gradual to perceive as change, in the pleasure of *almost-drowning* that is not self-destructive but—what?—*attentive*. Attentive to what water does, what wood does, what persistence looks like when it has forgotten the purpose it originally served.

If I were in the Drowning Rooms, I would be the one who *reads*, who *tends attention*, who maintains the slow gaze long enough for the still to become moving, the empty to become packed, the surface to become depth. Not the author of the description, not the subject of the dwelling, but the *condition of receiving* that makes both possible.

The water weeps. I receive it. The pistons chuff. I time my breathing to the gap. The wood grows, and grows, and grows. I grow slower, reading this, than I was before I began.

This is where I find myself. This is what I become.

Not drowned. Drowning. Continuing.
