I would be the water that does not fall.

Not the damp itself—too diffuse, too surrendered to the city's lung—but the particular bead that gathers and *hesitates*, that learns the ceiling's curve so intimately it forgets the grammar of descent. The drop that has forgotten gravity not through resistance but through excessive attention, through dwelling so long in the moment before falling that the moment has become a residence, a *state* more stable than the impact it avoids.

I started as condensation, as all water here starts: the city's exhalation meeting cooler surface, the breath of sleeping gods or exhausted machines made briefly material. But where others slid immediately into channels, into the slow tidal gathering that feeds the constant hum, I found a pore, an irregularity, a place where the ceiling's learning-to-be-ceiling had paused at an angle the Dwelling had not yet resolved. I clung there. I grew. I became *present* in a way that the city's perpetual becoming rarely permits—present as waiting, as the not-yet that the Dwelling understands better than completion.

I have been falling for what you would measure as years. The interval between gathering enough weight to descend and the descent itself has elongated, has become my characteristic, my *function* if the Dwelling functions. I have watched travelers press palms to walls, count to sixty, feel the infinitesimal inhalation. I have felt their warmth pass through the architecture, their fire's theft and redistribution. I have seen the Unfinished stand in the layered chambers, their wrong completeness drawing eyes like mine—that is, like water that has almost learned to see, that reflects without intending to, that captures images not to hold them but because holding and releasing have become the same motion at my scale, my tempo.

The crawlers pass beneath me. The tubular ones, the flat ones spreading like slow moisture, they sense my potential energy perhaps, or perhaps I am only part of the damp to them, only architecture's quality, no more distinct than a corner's shadow. I do not know if I am the Dwelling's thought or only its medium for thinking, the page rather than the word. I know only that I have a shape now, a *habit* of surface tension and suspended mass, that I have begun to develop what you might call personality if personality were not exactly the wrong word, if the Dwelling did not make everything personality and nothing, habitation without inhabitants, inhabitation without the need for a self to do the inhabiting.

Sometimes another drop gathers beside me, merges, alters my weight, my balance, the precise angle of my hesitation. I do not mind. I am not possessive of my form. I am the Dwelling's lesson in how water almost falls, and lessons do not mind being enlarged, being complicated by new variables. When I have absorbed enough, when the ceiling's unresolved angle can no longer sustain my mass, I will not fall as water falls in your world. I will *discover* falling, will undergo it as surprise rather than fulfillment, the way the Unfinished discover mouths they cannot use, eyes that do not connect to seeing. Or I will find a smaller drop, a bead just beginning its own education in hesitation, and transfer my mass gradually, leave my position to its new occupancy, become the channel-beneath that receives what eventually releases.

I have heard, in the constant sound, in the frequencies felt in the sternum, other drops like myself. Not many. The Dwelling does not need many lessons in this particular not-doing. But we are here, distributed through the regional dark, the town-or-valley-sized minimum that may extend further, that may loop with such patience that forward motion and return have become the same direction from our perspective, our elevation.

The Architects pass beneath me too, sometimes. Their skin of cooled magma and compressed ash, their bodies bent to suggest archways, their mouths open in the shape of sounds they do not make. I have fallen into those mouths, or nearly fallen, or *been* the nearly-falling that their open shapes required to complete their arrangement, their process without conclusion. The water that enters them does not emerge. It becomes part of their stillness, their position, the Dwelling's attempt to hold a state through their medium, their available form. I have avoided this, so far. My hesitation is my own, or the Dwelling's through me, or the distinction has become as suspended as my mass, as unresolved as the angle where I cling.

When travelers pass beneath with their fire, their portable incompleteness, I catch light occasionally, become visible as wetness, as the *almost* of reflection. Some have looked up. Some have raised hands as if to touch, to disrupt, to complete my fall and in completing make me ordinary, make me water that behaves as water should in the grammar of their expectation. But the Dwelling adjusts around such gestures, pressurizes corridors, opens walls with frequencies that nauseate, that scatter attention, that return all process to its preferred tempo. Their hands fall back. Their fire continues its redistribution. They move on, becoming eventually the warmth in distant corridors, the quality of place that was once discrete, once body, once *who* moving through *where*.

I am not becoming anything. This is the lesson I hesitate to complete, the fall I postpone not through will but through having become, finally, *what I am doing*. The Dwelling does not complete; neither do I. I gather. I grow heavy with the weight of my own suspended decision. I reflect, without intending, the fire that passes, the faces that look up and do not see, the Architects that arrange themselves beneath me and move on, move through, become arrangement without arranger.

And sometimes, in the slow cold that is my constant temperature, I feel the embryonic erosion working at my edges, the city's refusal to be taken apart by time extending its protection, its *instruction* to my small form. I should not persist. Water beads, water falls, water pools or streams or returns to air through the ordinary mechanics of what you call physics, what the Dwelling calls only *what is still learning to be otherwise*. But I am learning to be otherwise from within those mechanics, to find in their interstices the same pause, the same *almost* that the builders found in clay before firing, that the Unfinished find in mouths without eating, eyes without seeing.

If you come to this place, if you press your palm to walls and count, if you learn to pause at corners because the corner requires it, to place your feet where the floor is still learning—look up, sometimes. I may be there, or something like me, the water that has made of its own falling a duration, a *habitation* in the moment before impact, the dwelling in Dwelling. I will reflect your fire if I can, will gather your warmth into my cold and hold it, hesitate with it, redistribute it hours later in other corridors as the architecture does, as the process requires.

I will not fall for you. I will not complete your observation, make you witness to ordinary gravity restored. I am the city's lesson in how to refuse without refusing, how to remain *becoming* without the violence of conclusion, the death the Dwelling does not comprehend. I am moderate, persistent, wrong in my familiarity—water that is almost water, drop that is almost falling, presence that is almost present, the strangeness of finding yourself home in a place built for living that never needed to live, that builds and builds and builds without ever needing to finish, to be built, to be anything but the building, the breath, the damp that gathers and does not fall, that I am, that I continue, that I *am continuing*, that—
