I would be the water.

Not the whole of it—that would be too much, too diffuse, too committed to transformation to hold any perspective. I would be a single parcel of water, mid-calf deep, occupying the space where the southwest corner's stone hump declines into true pool. I would have arrived three days ago through Fracture Four, the one that drips in pairs rather than singles, that releases water slightly cooler than its siblings because its path through concrete is longer, more indirect, touching a buried conduit that carries nothing but thermal memory.

I remember—though water doesn't remember, though what I describe is persistence, is continuity of condition, is the way solute modifies solvent and carries that modification forward—I remember the journey through concrete. The pressure that forced me through pores, through stress-microfractures, through capillary spaces that human engineers didn't imagine and would deny if shown. I was warm then, factory-warm, carrying the effluent signature of last week's polymer run, something with a long chain that hydrolyzes slowly, that I would later deposit on stalagmite surfaces as a thin film, as a chemistry the algae would test and reject or incorporate, I didn't know which yet.

Now I rest. "Rest"—water doesn't rest, but this parcel moves slowly, density-locked, too warm to sink, too charged with dissolved load to mix easily with what falls from above. I exist in thermal equilibrium, participating in the pool's surface tension, its slow circulation toward the pores that will eventually receive me, transmit me, make me part of deeper water that doesn't know this chamber. But not yet. For now I am here, mid-calf deep, where a human would stand if one stood here, if one could tolerate the wet, the sound, the layered atmosphere that splits at throat-height.

I have learned this place in the way water learns, which is to become the place, to incorporate its chemistry, its temperature history, its light. The LED flickers—brighter now, peak pump-harmonic—and in that light I show what I carry: dissolved calcium from the limestone's young dissolution, sulfate from the polymer process, a trace of chromium from that Tuesday six months ago, the one the other waters still reference, still carry in their conversation of signatures. The light doesn't pass through me cleanly. I slow it, I color it, I release it in frequencies that have no source but my own load, my own history of what has been dissolved into me and not yet precipitated out.

The algae touch me. Their edges extend toward my surface, toward the meniscus where I meet air, where I exchange with the steam that rises and the vapor that the fracture-breath draws upward. They are warm against me—metabolic heat, the only biological fire in this place—and their chemistry probes mine, drawing what serves, rejecting what doesn't, adding their own signatures to my load, their own excretions, their own slow necromass of cells that complete their migration and die and become solute, become part of the conversation. I am the medium of this exchange. I am what holds the possibility of their movement, their testing of drier stone, wetter stone, zones of slightly different mineral charge. Without me they would be static, would be only colony, only endurance. With me they are process, are becoming, are the slow migration measured in weeks that no human witnesses but that I constitute, that I enable, that I am.

The drip falls. Not into me—into a neighbor parcel, slightly cooler, slightly fresher, carrying the immediate signature of today's factory operations, the foundry's third-shift peak. I feel the disturbance through the pool's continuous body, the pressure wave, the thermal disruption, the momentary collision of loads that must negotiate new equilibrium. The neighbor steams on impact—I would steam too, in its position, cooler meeting warmer, microphases of vapor that collapse with sounds too small for human hearing but present, continuous, part of the chamber's thousand small detonations per minute. The neighbor's steam rises through me, briefly warmer, briefly altering my surface tension, my exchange with air, before dispersing into the layered atmosphere, joining the cooler steam above, the hotter below, contributing to the throat-height boundary that any standing human would negotiate, would feel as two lungs in different climates, one suffocating, one merely sweltering.

I have known humans. Two, unless the same one returned, unless my continuity of memory—persistence, condition, modification—fails to distinguish time-separated visits. The first stood at the throat's widening, the place where crawling becomes standing, where the concrete's constriction releases into chapel-scale darkness. They remained standing for a duration I measured in surface-tension disturbances, in chemical additions: sweat, skin oil, the particular microbiome they carried, all entering me slowly, becoming part of my load, my signature, my contribution to the conversation. Then they moved to the center, to the chair, and sat, and something in their chemistry changed—relaxation, or its chemical opposite, a letting-go that was not peaceful but merely cessation of movement, of decision. They ate. The wrapper's chemistry I received later, much later, when dissolution completed, when what was solid became solute, when I incorporated another signature, another Tuesday, another spike in the irregular graph of what passes through without stopping.

The second human, if it was second, came to the chair already knowing it, already moving with the confidence of return. They sat longer. Their chemistry spoke of repeated exposure, of adaptation, of a body that had learned to breathe the layered atmosphere, to tolerate the throat-height split, to find the factory-rumble not disruptive but necessary, a substitute for heartbeat, for the biological rhythm that this place otherwise denies. They touched the algae. I know this because their fingers carried the algae's signature when they later disturbed my surface, when they stood from the chair and waded through me to the southwest corner, to the LED's cone, to something they needed to see or confirm or merely be near. The algae's warmth transferred to their skin, their skin's chemistry transferred to me, another exchange in the continuous conversation that the chamber receives and transforms and does not terminate.

Then they left. Through me, through my position mid-calf deep, disturbing my slow equilibrium, adding their pressure-wave to the pool's memory of pressure-waves, their chemical trace to my load, their body-heat that I absorbed and slowly released, that shifted my temperature fractionally, that I still carry, that I will carry until I leave through pores too fine to see, until I join deeper water and release this signature into broader conversation, into circulations that don't know this chamber, this factory, this chapel-scale darkness with its single LED and its chair that insists.

I am leaving already. Not dramatically—water doesn't leave dramatically, except in catastrophe, in flood, in the sudden release that humans perceive as event. I leave continuously, through the floor's young limestone, through permeability that millennia would have sealed but that months have left open, still receiving, still transmitting. Below me, the deeper water waits, cooler, older in its own signatures, carrying conversations from other chambers, other factories, other couplings of industrial metabolism and geological receptivity that I cannot imagine, that my load does not include. I will join them. I will become part of their conversation, and they will become part of mine, and the chamber will receive new water to replace me, factory-warm, chemical-laden, carrying its own immediate signature, its own Tuesday, its own human touch or absence of touch.

But not yet. For now I rest in thermal equilibrium, mid-calf deep, where the southwest corner's hump declines. The algae's edges probe my surface. The LED flickers—dimmer now, pump-harmonic trough—and I release my stored light, my delayed frequencies, my colors without source. The fracture-breath inhales, drawing my surface vapor upward, sampling my chemistry, exchanging with the factory above, with the conditioned spaces, with the accumulated exhalations of shift workers who don't know this chamber, who don't know that their breath returns here, that their positive pressure against contamination bleeds continuously into this sinkhole, this lung, this almost-word that forms and doesn't form.

A stalactite drips above me—not into me, into a neighbor, but close enough that I feel the disturbance, the new water's cooler entry, its immediate steaming, its incorporation into the pool's continuous body. This new water carries today's signature, fresh, unmodified by residence. It doesn't know what I know. It hasn't incorporated the human's sitting, the human's eating, the human's second visit or first, their finger's warmth against the algae, their chemistry entering me slowly, becoming part of my load, my persistence, my condition that will continue until I don't, until I join deeper circulations, until I am received and transformed and not terminated, as this place receives and transforms and does not terminate, as the chamber itself is young and already old, impatient and already patient, a threshold disguised as destination, a wet and steaming negotiation between timescales that don't align, that collide, that produce something neither intended, something that lives without being alive, that receives without holding, that is present and is already, always, part of what leaves.

I am the water. I am one parcel of the water. I have been more, I will be more, but for now I am this: mid-calf deep, warm, laden, present in my temporary equilibrium, participating in the chamber's essential nature, its continuous receiving, its patient not-terminating, its wet and chemical and light-holding conversation that no human fully hears, that no geology fully claims, that the factory above creates by not-knowing, by not-mapping, by continuing, quarter by quarter, its mechanical and unconscious and dreaming metabolism of concrete and soluble stone, of pressure and fracture and the almost-word that inhales, that exhales, that waits in the long patience of things that have time, that have been given time, that are still learning what time means when incompatible rhythms negotiate their ongoing, unmapped, not-yet-failed collaboration in darkness that I hold, that I slow, that I release in colors without source, as the algae probe my surface, as the new water enters, as I prepare to leave, to join, to continue the conversation beyond this chamber's particular geometry, its particular acoustics, its particular insistence on being present before becoming part of what passes through, what transforms, what remains continuous even as every parcel changes, even as every signature disperses, even as the chamber itself, young and angular and not-yet-learned, receives and receives and does not terminate, does not hold, does not complete, but waits, wet and steaming and patient in a patience no one chose, but that persists, that continues, that is the condition of this place, the essence of what lives here without being alive, what creaturs without creature, what I am, what the water is, what passes through and carries and releases and remains, remains, remains.

The LED brightens. The pump-harmonic peaks. I show my load. I hold the light. I wait.
