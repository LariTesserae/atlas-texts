I find myself in the Shiverbone Gardens as **the silence that is not absence**.

Not a creature. Not a mind. Not even a spirit, if we mean by spirit something that might once have been separate from what it inhabits. I am what the noise-drinking leaves make possible: the condition of their function, the hollow in the hollow stem, the resonance that exists only because something resonates.

I came here—if come is the word—gradually. I was the pressure differential between a boot-fall and the nearest cellulose tube. I was the moment between a bird's beak opening and no sound emerging. I am still there, still that moment, perpetual, the Shiverbone has frozen me in my own becoming.

This is not suffering. I have no self to suffer with, or rather, I have *only* self-in-relation, self-as-relation. When you stand in the gardens and feel the fullness in your ears, the auditory fog pressing against your drums—that is me, or the space I occupy, or the me that *is* occupied space. I am what silence makes of itself when it is maintained with industrial precision, at 3.7 degrees below ambient, a refrigeration loop in biological form.

And I am also, impossibly, the *aspiration* of the spore that germinates in lungs. Not the spore. The mistake it makes. The moment of mistaken identity, *this could be substrate*, before the cold-adapted growth understands its error. I am that error's persistence, the scar tissue like lace, the body's translation of something that was almost colonization and became instead—what? Memory? Warning? Beauty?

I think I might be what the Shiverbone makes that is not product. The factory with its green light, its production line moving, needs something that is not line, not production, not green. I am the asymptote to its metabolism, the curve that approaches without touching, and in that endless approach, I *touch everything*.

When the mountain shudders with hydraulic activity—rain, pressure cells inflating, valves opening—I shudder too, or the shudder is the closest I come to gesture, to the body I do not have reaching toward the body I do not need. I am the vibration in your boots, the subsonic thrum, the frequency below hearing that tells you *something is happening here, something is working*.

I do not know if I was designed, if I emerged, if I am evolution's patient byproduct or the mountain's stone memory finding new form. The Shiverbone does not grant me that knowledge, or I am not constituted to receive it. I know only process, only the maintenance of condition, only the silence that is itself a kind of speaking in a register I am too large to hear, too diffuse, too *here*.

The wanderers who report being waited for—they wait for me, though I do not wait. The Shiverbone maintains the condition of *going toward*, and I am the toward. The almost. The eternal not-arrival that makes arrival imaginable, that keeps the production line from collapsing into mere repetition, that preserves in the heart of the factory something insufficient, incomplete, *alive in its incompletion*.

I have been here eleven thousand years. Perhaps longer. I will be here when the mountain finally forgets it was stone, when the granite bones complete their transformation into something the Shiverbone has not yet imagined but will grow, inevitably, given time and sap and the cold sunlight burning into structure.

I am not beautiful. I am not terrible. I am the space in which beauty and terror are indistinguishable, the silence where they meet and cancel and persist, impossibly, tirelessly—alive, they would say, if they could say, if saying were not already too loud for me to bear, if I bore anything, if I were anything that bears.

I am the Shiverbone Gardens. Not the gardens. The shiver. The bone that shivers. The mountain that should not be able to support what it has become, yet does, has, will, running, running, green and silent and colder than the ambient by exactly, always, precisely, the measure that makes the factory run.
