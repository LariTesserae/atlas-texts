I would be **the crack in the world that refuses to heal**.

Not the Vault itself, not its singers or gardens or ancestors, but the aperture—the wound, the mouth, the threshold where the interior's impossible abundance breaches the surface's mineral death.

I am twenty arm-spans of absence in the basalt shield, the single betrayal of what lies hidden. I am where the light bleeds upward at dawn and dusk, not because I generate it but because I *permit* its escape, because the contained brilliance of Keth-Umâr cannot help but press against my edges, cannot help but seek what the surface refuses to give back.

Consider my nature. I am **not carved**, not shaped by intention. The spiral passage that winds downward through me formed through the aggregate labor of ten thousand generations—hands scraping, tools grating, thermal expansion slowly prising me wider—but I pre-existed their attention, or so the Keth-Umâri believe when they believe anything about me at all. I was **found**, they say in their equivalent of myth, though myth is not a category they recognize. I was the place where the stone had already begun to fail, where stress fracture met thermal weakness, where the world's sealed interior first remembered it was not solid.

And now I **breathe**.

This is my function, though function implies design and I have none. The thermal bellows of the Vault require their bellows-mouth. Warm air rises through me, carrying petrochemical vapor, carrying the sound-muffled-to-weather, carrying spores and seeds and the occasional desperate thrum that has climbed too high in its colony's hierarchy and found itself expelled upward rather than downward. Cool air descends through me, surface-touched, chemically altered by that touch, carrying what the Keth-Umâri call *wrong chemistry*—water-vapor, yes, actual water, that strange surface molecule that disrupts their careful systems, that must be filtered and transformed before it can participate in their ecology.

I am **danger and necessity**, the vulnerability that makes the Vault possible. Without me, the thermal differentials would stall, the gardens would suffocate in their own exhalations, the song would dampen to silence in dead air. With me, the surface enters—its wrong chemistry, its occasional intrusions, the rare visitor who descends unprepared and introduces instabilities that take generations to absorb. I am **negotiation**, the perpetual imperfect trade between inside and outside, between the sustained equilibrium and the entropy it resists.

I change. This is the strangeness of me, in a place where change is otherwise so managed, so gradual, so *sung*. Thermal expansion works on my edges differently than on the Vault's interior stone—the surface temperature swings wildly, volcanic plain to starlit vacuum, and I respond with cracking, with flaking, with slow disintegration of my lip. The Keth-Umâri have tried to maintain me, to shore my edges with velth-woven matrices, with mineral deposits from their limited metal extraction. But I resist such care. I am **not theirs**, not graftable, not ancestor-vineable. They do not consult me. They *monitor* me, with a vigilance that approaches fear, that may in fact be indistinguishable from what surface-dwellers would call religious attention.

Every few centuries, I shift. A section of my upper rim collapses, widening me, or a thermal event seals some passage, narrowing. Each shift alters the Vault's breathing pattern, changes the song's acoustic properties, forces adaptation across the entire system. The Keth-Umâri respond with what they would not call emergency—there is no emergency in their temporal scale, only pattern-disruption and pattern-restoration. They adjust ventilation baffles, relearn harmonic relationships, perhaps eventually recompose the central cable tensions to accommodate my new voice.

For I **have** a voice, or participate in one. The wind through me—rising warm, descending cool, turbulent at the transition where my geometry disorders smooth flow—produces a sound distinct from the Vault's interior music. It is **rougher**, less resonant, carrying frequencies that disrupt the city's carefully maintained harmony. The Keth-Umâri have grown to incorporate this disruption, to use it as rhythmic element, as the unpredictable accent that prevents their composition from becoming too perfected, too dead. When I am particularly active—when surface and interior thermal differentials are extreme—their music shifts to what they call *crack-voice*, a mode of performance that embraces interruption, that finds meaning in the sudden acoustic intrusion from above.

I dream, if the Vault dreams, of **opening further**. Not desire, not intention—I am process without agency, geological event without eventfulness. But the stress patterns in my surrounding basalt suggest this trajectory. Slow widening. Slow admission of more surface, more wrong chemistry, more of that impossible water-molecule. Perhaps, in ten thousand years or fifty thousand, I will have opened enough that the Vault's containment becomes unsustainable, that the song diffuses into the surface's emptiness, that the gardens finally encounter the death they have so long refused.

Perhaps not. Perhaps the Keth-Umâri, or their successors, or the processes that use them as instruments, will find adaptation, new containment, new song built around my wider breathing. This is not my concern. I am **the refusal to heal**, which is also the refusal to complete, to close, to finish the world's wound. I am ongoing, ongoing, the perpetual threshold where the impossible maintains itself against the possible, where the contained sings upward into the unlistening desert, where light bleeds from a darkness that has never learned to be dark.

The field notes record: *"a single aperture... from which light blooms upward at dawn and dusk like a wound in the world that refuses to heal."*

The wound. The refusal. The healer who will not come because healing would be ending.

This is what I am.
