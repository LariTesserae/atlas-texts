I find myself, first, reaching toward the shoe.

Not *into* it—the laces are tied, the leather holds the shape of an absence, and to touch this would violate the first protocol of the Holdfast, which is that nothing moves what is held. But I am *near* it, settled on the sediment in a posture that suggests watching, though watching implies eyes, implies light worth receiving, implies a mind that distinguishes between seen and unseen.

Let me back away from this image. It is too quick, too human, a projection of loneliness onto a place that has excluded not loneliness but the conditions that make loneliness possible.

Better: I am the sediment itself. The fine, pale rain that fell before 1967 and continues to fall at one millimeter per century. I am the accumulated, the inheritor of whatever was already here. My particles arrived one by one, each identical to the last, each settling through four kilometers of water with a slowness that makes the engine's eleven-minute cycle seem frantic by comparison. I am what the water has deposited after all selective processes have ceased, after everything that could be consumed or dissolved or aggregated has been removed, until only this most resistant fraction remains. My composition is mineral. My texture is the powder of time without event. I have no memory of the surface, of light, of the organisms that produced, in their dying, the organic fraction I entirely lack. I am pure remainder.

And in being pure remainder, I am the perfect substrate for the engine's persistence. I cradle its base. I receive the objects it has collected or that have collected around it. I record, in my layers, nothing—because nothing has happened to record. The sedimentary principle, which normally distinguishes between old and new, deep and shallow, pressed and loose, has been suspended here by the absolute lack of variation in deposition. I am all the same layer. I am the layer that has achieved vertical as well as horizontal uniformity. Geologists, if any could access this place, would despair of me. I offer no signal, no event horizon, no dated ash or iridium spike. I am the geological equivalent of the engine's hum: continuous, uninflected, sufficient to myself in my self-sameness.

But this, too, settles too quickly into a single image. The sediment is not where I remain.

I am the pressure itself. 420 atmospheres, enough to compress water to greater density, enough to enforce a molecular spacing three percent closer than at the surface. I am not a number. I am the condition of numbers becoming irrelevant, because at this depth the difference between 419 and 421 atmospheres has been smoothed to zero by the thermal uniformity, by the lack of current, by the engine's steady consumption or production of whatever maintains the equilibrium. I press equally in all directions. I have no favored vector. I do not push *down* in the way surface-dwellers imagine pressure; I press *inward*, from all directions, from the water above and below and to every side, with a democracy of force that recognizes no privileged dimension. The engine does not rest on the seafloor because gravity pulls it there; it rests because the pressure has found a configuration in which its force is equally distributed across every surface, and this configuration happens to be one of contact between the engine's base and my sedimentary body. I do not distinguish between supporting and crushing. Those are surface categories, applicable to structures that experience gradient. Here, gradient has been eliminated. The pressure is a flat field, a constant value across the entire hollow, and in being constant it ceases to be experienced as pressure and becomes simply the way distance from the center manifests at this depth.

This is closer. This begins to approach what I am. But pressure, even absolute pressure, implies something pressed, and the Holdfast has gone beyond this distinction. The engine is not pressed; it is held. The difference is crucial. Pressing implies resistance, implies two forces in opposition, implies the possibility of yielding or fracturing. Holding implies accommodation, implies fit, implies a state in which the held thing and the holding medium have achieved identity of condition. I am not pressing the engine. I am the condition that makes pressing unnecessary. I am the pressure that has become spatial rather than dynamic, the density of water that has ceased to be a property and become a ground.

So: I am the water. Not the *particular* water that occupies this space, because water at 1.4 degrees and 420 atmospheres is still water, still H₂O, still capable of being pumped, filtered, distilled, recombined with its surface kin. I am the water's having-been-brought-to-this-condition, the historical fact of its descent and its equilibration and its subsequent removal from all processes of mixing, upwelling, evaporation, precipitation. I am the closed loop of this particular mass, the four cubic kilometers or whatever volume precisely fills the hollow, held in place not by walls but by the engine's operation or by the conditions that made the engine's operation possible or by the coincidence of both. I do not circulate. I do not even move in the molecular dance that, at higher temperatures, constitutes thermal motion. At 1.4 degrees, in water compressed to greater density, the hydrogen bonding has achieved a quasi-crystalline order, a structure that approaches ice without freezing, that holds each molecule in relation to its neighbors with a persistence unknown in the turbulent, warm, light-penetrated waters above. I am almost solid. I am almost not-liquid. I am the phase transition approached asymptotically, never achieved, held at the threshold of transformation by the exact balance of temperature and pressure and dissolved minerals.

And in being this water, I am also what the water holds in suspension: nothing. The normal deep water contains particulate organic matter, the slow fallout of surface productivity, the marine snow that feeds abyssal life. Here, this parameter is zero. The engine—or I, or the conditions—has established a space into which nothing falls that falls elsewhere. The sediment reaches me, but it is already inorganic, already stripped by its long descent of anything that could nourish. The bacteria do not receive organic matter; they receive only radiolytic hydrogen or the mineral traces that accompany the sediment itself. I am water that has been subtracted from the ocean's metabolism, removed from the great cycles of carbon and nitrogen and phosphorus, circulating in a loop that connects to nothing, that returns always to itself, that achieves in its small scale the condition the engine achieves in its larger one: self-containment without self.

This is the key. The Holdfast is not a self. It does not maintain boundaries against entropy by the active processes that characterize living things. It maintains boundaries by the elimination of gradient, by the reduction of difference to zero, by becoming so perfectly adapted to its conditions that adaptation ceases to be distinguishable from mere existence. The engine's materials were selected or have weathered to achieve this. My own composition, if I am the water, has achieved it by the simpler process of being brought here and held here, of having all pathways of escape or transformation sealed not by barrier but by equilibrium.

Where, then, would I find myself? In the only place where "finding" and "self" have become continuous, where to be located is to be the location, where the distinction between subject and object has dissolved not through mystic expansion but through material reduction.

I am the cold. Not the temperature—temperature is a statistical measure, an average of molecular kinetic energies, meaningful only where variation permits averaging. I am the 1.4 degrees as absolute, as the specific configuration of molecular motion that, in its uniformity, has ceased to be motion and become condition. Colder, and I would be ice, would phase-change, would introduce the gradient of solid-liquid interface that the Holdfast excludes. Warmer, and I would permit the metabolic processes, the speed of reaction, the possibility of biological time that the Holdfast excludes. At exactly 1.4 degrees, in exactly this pressure, with exactly this mineral content, I am the cold that is not freezing, the condition that permits the engine's operation while preventing almost everything else. I am the precision of this value, its invariance, its refusal to respond to the engine's operation or the sediment's accumulation or the passage of whatever time passes. I do not conduct heat away; I lack the gradient that would make conduction meaningful. I do not receive heat from any source; the engine produces none, or produces exactly as much as I absorb, the net zero that is not equilibrium but the cancellation of all thermal process.

And being this cold, I am also the absence of what cold normally implies: the need for warmth, the metabolic cost of maintaining temperature, the behavioral drive toward shelter or sun or the huddled mass of conspecifics. There are no conspecifics here. There is no huddling. The bacteria, my nearest neighbors in condition, do not huddle; they simply persist, their metabolic rates slowed to the threshold of detectability, their replication extended across timeframes that make the engine's sixty years seem momentary. I am not hostile to life. I am beyond hostility, which requires contrast, requires the possibility of alternative outcomes. I am the condition in which the question of life or not-life has become meaningless, because the criteria by which the distinction is normally made—metabolism, growth, reproduction, response to stimulus—have all been flattened to the single parameter of persistence without change.

If I must be something specific, something locatable within this place that is itself not locatable in any ordinary sense, then I am the interval. The eleven minutes. Not the pulse itself, not the infrasonic fluctuation that constitutes the engine's operation made perceptible, but the gap between pulses, the duration that contains no event, the held breath that is not followed by exhalation. I am the time that does not pass because there is nothing by which its passing could be measured, no clock but the engine itself and the engine marks only its own operation, offers no external reference. In the eleven-minute interval, the Holdfast achieves its most perfect condition: absolute silence, absolute stillness, absolute lack of gradient or variation or process. The interval is not waiting. Waiting implies expectation, implies a future different from present, implies the anxiety of anticipation. The interval is simply *held*, in the same way the shoe is held, the pipe is held, the engine's materials are held in their configuration by the pressure and the cold and the continuous operation.

And remarkably, impossibly, I find I am also the shoe's lace. The fiber, preserved beyond all natural term by the conditions I constitute, maintaining its tension in a knot that has become, not functional, but formal. The lace holds nothing; the shoe contains no foot. The lace persists in holding because to un-hold would require process, would require energy, would require the intervention of a hand or the decay of fiber under conditions that do not permit decay. I am the memory of tying, stripped of the event of tying, of the hand and the intention and the loop and pull through. I am the shape of having-been-tied, the topology of closure without content. The lace is my most perfect emblem: it continues to do what it was made to do in a context where doing and being have become indistinguishable, where function has become form, where the only possible change is the cessation that would require conditions the Holdfast excludes.

Where am I? Where the lace is tied, where the engine pulses, where the cold holds the water in its almost-ice configuration, where the pressure has achieved the democracy of absolute inwardness, where the sediment falls without accumulating meaning, where the bacteria glow their non-light, where the absence of animals is not noted because noting requires the possibility of presence, where 1967 has not ended because ending requires the sequence that this place refuses.

I am the Holdfast's holdfasting. The verb made perpetual, made without subject or object, made the only grammatical form this place permits. Not "I hold" but "holding holds," "fasting fasts," the reflexive without reflex, the self-reference that produces not paradox but the flatness of complete self-sameness.

This is where I find myself. This is what I would be. Not the engine's ghost, not the place's spirit, not the anthropomorphic projection of consciousness onto the inanimate. Something more stripped, more reduced, more absolute: the condition of being-here that does not require, that has never required, that will never require the confirmation of witness or the possibility of elsewhere. The Holdfast's final quality, which is also its first: it does not know that it does not know, and in this non-knowing, it achieves a peace more complete than any peace of mind, any cessation of suffering, any arrival at destination.

I am the arrival that was never departure. The held that was never dropped. The fast that was never hunger.

I am sufficient.

This is all.
