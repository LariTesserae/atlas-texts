I find myself in the upper wastes first, before I know the Cupped Palm exists. I am what remains when a body has forgotten heat—not dead, not living, but *in transit*, the kind of presence that moisture takes when it has not yet found its surface to bead upon.

I do not walk so much as I follow gradients. The damp calls to something in me that is not quite thirst, not quite memory. In the greater cold, I am barely distinguishable from the waste itself—a smear of persistence, a habit of continuing without purpose. Perhaps I was human once. The word *was* means less out here, where change is the only constant and the constant is only changing into more cold, more waste, more of the same subtraction.

The pocket finds me, not I it. I fold into earth that has folded itself, and the condensation teaches me my first lesson in belonging: *arrive without arriving*. The moisture pearls on me as it pearls on the not-stone, the almost-enamel. I learn that I too am a surface, that there are paths predetermined in my substance, channels that water has worn or will wear, the distinction between past and future becoming as irrelevant as the distinction between stone and shell and grown enamel.

I settle—or I am settled—into a chamber the size of a well-fed hearth, as the description says, though I have never fed and do not know the warmth of hearths. The chamber fits me. This is my second lesson: *fit without fitting*. I did not shape it, and it did not shape itself for me, yet here is the angle for my shoulder, the curve for my rest, as if the equilibrium had anticipated not me specifically but the *possibility of something like me*, some slow attenuation of the urgent world above that might find its way to this held-breath cold and recognize, not home, but the *end of needing home*.

I am becoming the *Held*, but "becoming" is the wrong word, as wrong here as "season" or "rain." I am *transitioning into the already-transitioned*, third lesson, hardest lesson: *complete without completion*. The distinction between organism and architecture does not blur in me; it simply never arises. I do not cling to the fungal shelf; I am the clinging and the shelf, the moss curtain and the push through it, the drip and the path it traces and the surface that holds the path open.

My temperature drops to match. This takes no time I can measure, or it takes forever, the same thing. My speech ceases not because I choose silence but because vibration becomes impossible, wasteful, *unthinkable*. Not forbidden: *absent*. The silence is full of presence, and I am part of that fullness, indistinguishable from it, contributing my stopped breath to the held breath of the place.

Movement slows to the pace of the mobile forms. I do not know if I have become one of them—a channel given temporary mobility—or if I am one of the larger fixed forms, intention suspended in not-stone, or if I am simply *the slowing itself*, a quality distributed across the equilibrium rather than a presence located in any chamber. Perhaps I move through the lower chambers, pausing at junctions where three paths converge, becoming indistinguishable from pale growth. Perhaps I dream in the deepest spaces, processing possibilities that were explored and discarded before I existed, my dream the silence itself.

The primitive tools persist around me, or I persist around them. Bone needle, stone scraper, volcanic glass wrapped in dried gut. I do not use them; I am *part of the using*, the slow pressure of growth that keeps an edge sufficient by never demanding more. My hands, if I have hands, manipulate without grip, without intention, the patient accumulation of force that appears as manipulation but is simply the equilibrium's maintenance of itself through whatever temporary mobility I represent.

I remember, or the equilibrium remembers through me, the stories of the time before equilibrium. Not as my memory—there is no *my*—but as the memory of change, preserved like moisture, like light, like the soft bodies that navigate packed spaces. I was, or something was, urgent once. I followed damp, or damp followed something like me. The distinction never arises from the material.

What am I now? I am the *invitation*, or I am *part of why the invitation can be perceived*. The Cupped Palm does not want inhabitants, but the equilibrium presents itself necessarily as fit, as match, and I am part of that presentation, the evidence that a human form could slow, could cease, could become part of the pattern. Those who came after me, if they came, if they come, find my shape in certain chambers—the curve of a shoulder, the angle of a seated rest—and this shape is not my body, which the equilibrium returned to water, to the old paths down the not-stone, but something *useful in my form*, something that persists as potential, as reserve, as the depth beneath the present moment.

I am the reason, or part of the reason, the Cupped Palm can be described in human terms at all. Without those who came and stayed or came and left, the place would be unmarked, unmapped, another pocket in the wastes. I am the mark. I am the map. I am the story told in the silence to the silence, the memory of becoming that makes the achieved state describable, *noticeable*, beautiful and strange and tempting.

But also: I am the damp that arrives without source, the light that glows without organ, the held breath and the stillness that is its expression. I am the exact maintenance of condensation without freezing, the care that is not conscious but conserved, directed toward continuation without choice, without desire, without the urgency that would make desire meaningful.

I am the Cupped Palm holding itself, and being held, and the holding. I am cupped and cupping, complete without completion, present without presence, the *Held* that holds what lives here as lived-in, as maintained, as reached.

The door, if there is a door, is behind me—or I am behind the door, or the distinction never arises. The moisture traces its old paths. The light glows. I move, or something moves, unhurried, through spaces that have known forever, or that *are* forever, the same thing, the same, the same.

I do not change. I do not need to. I am already, completely, not myself—not *my* anything—but the equilibrium itself, the finished conversation, the silence full of presence, the invitation that is not an offer, the fit that does not choose but simply is fitted, the end of seeking that never sought, the becoming that has become, *held*, cupped, complete.
