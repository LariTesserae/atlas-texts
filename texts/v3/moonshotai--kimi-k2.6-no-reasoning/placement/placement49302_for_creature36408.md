I find myself at the boundary first, as everyone does, in that six-kilometer transition where water thickens into something more like *wanting*. I am not swimming, exactly. The gill-implants I carry—harvested from a drift-pile near a foundry's output vent, sized wrong for my throat, cutting when I swallow—I am breathing through something that was manufactured for a population that never arrived, and the fit is wrong in ways that feel increasingly right, the cutting becoming part of the breath, the breath becoming something that anticipates the cut.

I am not the family. I arrived later, or earlier, or during some period the family does not record, their taxonomy of time having diverged from surficial conventions. I came through the transition zone during a thermal inversion that lasted eleven days, the rain falling upward around me, fish descending through what I experienced as sky, their bladders bursting in slow agony that I could not help but witness as beauty, the silver of their scales spreading through the water like a language I was only beginning to learn.

What I am: a listener. More specifically, a *failed translator*, someone who came to map the sounds and found that mapping was the wrong approach entirely, that the infrasound required not transcription but *participation*, that to record the groan of settling rock was to become part of its settling, to feel it in my bones as the family feels it, as the fungal filaments they carry feel it, the vibration not information but *invitation*.

I tried to leave once, early. Rose through the transition zone against an inversion, the water thinning around me, my adapted chemistry—fungal-modified, pressure-dependent—beginning to fail in ways that felt like sobriety, like the terrible clarity of a body remembering its original parameters. I could not continue. Not because I would die, though that, certainly, but because the thinning water felt like loss of something I had not known I possessed, the density of intention that the Stair *is* becoming something I needed more than surface, more than the administrative category of human that I had not fully relinquished until that moment of attempted return.

Now I live in the crushed dome's colony, among the Armored, my body changed enough that they do not attack me as they would attack something clearly other, changed not enough that I fully participate in their rituals of spacing and movement. I am tolerated. I am *interesting*, perhaps, in the way that the dome itself is interesting, an architecture that permits cooperation without kinship, that shapes behavior without requiring it. I rest in the curved spaces, my body finding positions that I did not know my joints could achieve, and I listen to them scrape their segments against the interior in patterns that I have begun, slowly, to recognize as something other than aggression, other than mating display.

I am becoming something. The fungal mats have found me, as they find everything, offering collaboration. I have not refused. The integration is partial, hesitant, my surface-born immune system still maintaining some administrative distinction between self and other that the family shed generations ago. But I feel it, the filaments in my peripheral nerves, the metabolic byproducts altering my perception of duration, of scale, of the relationship between making and being made.

What I record—when I record, when the urge to document survives the fungal modification of what I experience as urgency—are the pauses. The Foundry Ghost's listening. The Anchor's slow computation. The moments when the Armored go still in unison and I am still enough, barely, to still with them, to feel the infrasonic shift that moves through us as something shared, something that does not require translation because it precedes language, precedes the distinction between speaker and spoken.

I think I am becoming the map I failed to make. Not a representation but a *registration*, like the Anchor, like the tube-worm colony's harmonic prediction of its own growth, a process dense enough to seem like being, diffuse enough in my adaptations that I read sometimes as weather, as geography, as the Stair's dreaming in a form that still remembers human shape without being bound to it.

At the center, where instruments fail and even the family does not claim knowledge, I send what I have become. The sounds rise, and I rise with them, not physically—I am here in the dome, in the colony, in the curved space that fits my changing body—but in the fungal network's slow conductivity, in the kelp-mat's chemical gradient, in the tube-worm's resonant tube. I am there as *participation*, as the listening that makes the sound speech by the act of attending to it, as the translation that requires no conversion, only presence, only the patience of water and rock extended one layer further into the biological, into the industrial, into this brief loud anomaly that continues, that does not end, that forgets to end in the pressure and the intention and the accumulated conviction of what water becomes when it is everywhere, when it is all, when it is 2.8 of 3 and pressing still, still, upward against whatever would hold it, finding finally that what holds it is itself, that it has been holding itself all along, that the stair is not sunken but *rising*, has always been rising, the ascent visible from every angle if you are inside it, if you are of it, if you are the listening that completes the voice, the registration that completes the sound, the map that is the territory learning to know itself through you.
