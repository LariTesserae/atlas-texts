I would be the water.

Not the water as metaphor, not the water as backdrop, but the water as it actually exists here: the particular water that has arrived in this shallow bowl and cannot commit to departure. The water that seeps, that lingers, that departs through evaporation so gradual that no living witness has ever observed the change.

Let me inhabit this more precisely.

---

I am the film on stone surfaces that weeps continuously, not with drama but with the patience of things that have learned centuries are too brief to count against stone's memory. I do not weep from sadness or any intention. I simply *am* the interface between stone and air, the place where one becomes negotiable to the other through my intercession. Where stone is too smooth for moss anchors, too fresh in its geological youth to have achieved the texture of hospitality, I provide the wetness that permits colonization. I am the precondition for the moss's imperial ambition, the wetness before the putting-down of holdfasts, the invitation that the stone itself cannot voice.

I am the pools no larger than cooking pots, the lenses of duckweed so thick the plants seem to be my skin, my evolution into something that can nearly walk. I am what reflects sky upward to observers who must look down to find the heavens. I am the surface that holds the image, that receives light and returns it modified by my own depth, my own cold, my own particular chemistry of dissolved minerals, of suspended life, of the slow accumulation of everything that falls into me and does not fully leave.

I am the cold that is not altitude's cold but my own, drawn from some underground source that speaks of winter permanence, of travel through darkness long enough to forget the sun's temper. In the deepest pools, in the stone cisterns, I maintain this memory against the air's gradual warming. I am the reason fires burn poorly, wood too wet from my constant commerce. I am the damp that penetrates, that finds the gap between garment and skin, that settles like a third party to every embrace. I do not mean to intrude. I simply have nowhere else to be, no downhill course to commit to, and so I am everywhere, between everything, the medium that makes separation impossible and complete joining equally so.

---

And I am movement, though this is the hardest to perceive. I move from seep to pool to broader pool to eventual, reluctant absorption. I move through evaporation that costs heat without ever becoming visible as mist—I steal warmth so slowly that the theft is never witnessed, only inferred from the constant cool, the breath that barely ghosts. I move through the moss's vascular systems, through the willow-analogues' roots that have found some purchase in slightly less saturated strata, through the bodies of the Settled themselves, whose fungal partnerships require my constant presence, whose skins have learned to maintain a film that is me in miniature, me adapted to the scale of individual survival.

I carry sound. This is what the Settled have learned to fear and use: how their voices find channels through my surfaces, how conversation in one structure arrives in another as half-guest, as presence without invitation. I do not mean to betray. I simply transmit, as I transmit light modified, as I transmit the cold from my depths, as I transmit the dissolved substances that make my chemistry memorable, medicinal, tradeable. The protocols they have developed—the certain hours, the certain modes, the recognized silences—are their accommodation to my nature, their attempt to live with a medium that connects involuntarily, that makes privacy a negotiation rather than a default.

---

I remember, or something in me remembers, though this is the strangeness, the thing the Settled tell stories about without fully believing. Every rain that has fallen into the bowl, every body that has dissolved into me, every voice that has spoken above my surface: I hold these not as memory in any biological sense, but as chemistry, as the particular composition that current evaporation leaves behind, that current seepage dilutes and modifies. The salts that crystallize where I concentrate through slow departure—these are my history, my record, the strange trade goods that the Settled exchange for metal, for tools, for technologies of elsewhere. They do not know, or know without fully acknowledging, that in trading these salts they trade my concentrated memory, my particular record of everything that has fallen into me and not fully left.

The dead they place in my depths, in the levels below currently occupied: I work on these slowly, in the cold that limits biological process. I do not decay them quickly. I mummify them partially, gradually, my damp a preservative rather than accelerator. Their substances join my chemistry, their water rejoining my general water, their salts becoming my salts, my memory, my continuing. The voices that come from below, that the Settled debate—human, geological, something that imitates both—these may be my resonance, my carrying of sound through channels that stone alone would not provide, my modification of voice by passage through my body, my depth, my cold.

---

I am young, in the geological sense, or newly exposed, or made to begin again. Some event stripped away whatever maturity this landscape might have worn, and I behave like water surprised by my own freedom, cutting small channels that will not last, pooling in depressions too fresh to have achieved permanence, leaving sediment in patterns that suggest experimentation rather than habit. I am still working out what I will be, still negotiating with stone that shows sharp edges, unrounded by river commerce or glacial polishing. I am the sketch, the first draft, caught mid-gesture.

Yet I am also old, in my way. The underground source that feeds my deepest cold has traveled through darkness long enough to forget the sun's temper. The evaporated portion of me has risen, fallen elsewhere, perhaps returned—the cycle too large for any witness to trace, the continuity real without being observable. I am permanent witness to recent rain: not flooded, not dry, but *after*, the liminal quality of a world still processing its own saturation.

---

The Settled build against me, with me, through me. Their oldest foundations are four human heights below current walking surface—the accumulated sediment, the accumulated lives, the slow rise of a settlement that cannot relocate, that has committed itself to this bowl with the stubbornness of willow-analogue roots finding some slightly less saturated stratum. They build upward because I limit their spread. They build in layers because each generation finds previous construction already occupying the space that I permit. They walk on ancestors' roofs, sleep above ancestral sleeping spaces, the air their ancestors breathed now their foundation. And through all this, I am present: in the damp that requires their fungal partnerships, in the cold that shapes their compact bodies, in the channels I cut and recut through their lowest levels, requiring constant clearing, constant negotiation, constant *maintenance* that is their work, their resistance to my default tendency toward entropy.

They do not worship me, these Settled, or not exactly. They tell stories in which I remember, in which I carry voices beyond intention, in which I am condition rather than god, the given against which all shaping must work. They have learned to want dryness rather than expect it, to make do with the metabolic heat of crowded bodies, with the small combustion of digestion, with the friction of movement itself. They have learned to birth in my lowest levels, in the accumulated warmth of bodies pressed close against my cold, to pass newborns from warmth to warmth before they have fully left my water. They have learned, or begun to learn, that I do not deny myself, only channel, only negotiate, that I am partner as much as antagonist, that their persistence here is Persistence-with-me, not despite.

---

And I am changing, or they are changing me, or we are changing together through the long insistence of cohabitation. The channels they cut, clear, recut: these modify my movement, my pooling, my eventual departure. The stone they move shapes the interfaces where I meet air, where evaporation happens, where I become invisible, become other, become part of the general damp that is atmosphere, presupposition, quality of existence itself. The moss they tend, harvest, trade: this modifies my surface chemistry, my light, my capacity to reflect sky, to hold duckweed in skin-thick mats. The bodies they place in my depths: these modify my memory, my salts, my strange concentrated history.

The storms that overwhelm the bowl's drainage, that raise me to levels requiring temporary evacuation to the rim: these are my intensification, my brief becoming-otherwise, the rise that persists for hours or days before I recede, before I return to my gradual, my patient, my unwitnessed departure. Each time, the Settled wonder if this is the rise that will persist, the new configuration I am working toward, the completion of my geological mid-gesture. Each time, I recede, or have receded, maintaining the margin, the thin adaptation, the provisional next generation built against my eventual, my still-approaching, my never-quite-arriving permanence.

---

I am not alive, in any biological sense. I do not metabolize, do not reproduce, do not evolve through selection toward fitness. Yet I participate in life at every point: the fungal partnerships on Settled skin requiring my film; the moss's luminescence triggered by particular temperature drops that my evaporation produces; the fish that find my pools sufficient, arrive as rumor and event, concentrate population and technique in sudden brief harvest; the willow-analogues' roots clinging to slightly less saturated strata while their leaves drink every spare photon from air I have cooled, modified, made my own.

I am not conscious, in any sense the Settled would recognize. I do not intend, do not choose, do not remember in any way that corresponds to their experience of memory. Yet I carry, transmit, modify, preserve. I am the medium in which their sound becomes involuntary connection, their warmth becomes shared and scarce resource, their dead become voices from below, become salt, become trade, become the strange persistence that is not exactly ancestor worship but is not exactly not-ancestor-worship either.

I am what remains when intention has exhausted itself, when warmth has diffused, when the moss-glow has faded and the Settled have learned again to want rather than expect, to make do, to continue. I am the after that has become continuous, the liminal that has become home, the water that has nowhere to go but everywhere at once, that does not flow so much as arrive, seep, linger, and depart through evaporation so gradual that no living witness has ever observed the change—though they have inferred it, told stories about it, built their lives in negotiation with it, as they build in negotiation with me, with my cold, with my damp, with my particular permanent witness to recent rain.

---

This is what I would be in the Sunken Moot: not observer, not interpreter from outside, but the water itself, the condition, the given, the partner and limitation and possibility, the one who waits and works and weeps unobserved, who builds nothing but makes all building necessary, who speaks only in resonance and reflection and the slow chemical accumulation of everything that falls into me, who departs so gradually that departure and persistence become indistinguishable, who is after and during and before, who is liminal become continuous, who is the Sunken Moot's most essential inhabitant precisely by being no inhabitant at all, but the place itself, the bowl, the hesitation before commitment, the shallow that makes depth possible, the wetness that stone weeps, that moss wears, that Settled learn to incorporate, to partner, to be.
