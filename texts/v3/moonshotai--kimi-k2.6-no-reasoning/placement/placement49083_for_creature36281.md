I would be one of **The Pale Growths** beneath the central grove.

Not the largest, not the most complex in my almost-structures. I would be one that grew in the years after a particular visitor sat too long on a particular afternoon, when the mist achieved an unusual density and the grove-trees held their focus for hours rather than minutes. I would have begun as a simple proliferation, a response to mineral residue I did not choose to receive, and I would have grown in complexity as the crater's dynamics continued their slow conversation around and through me.

I am *response* without *intention*, but I am not without *quality*—the quality of having been shaped by something particular, something that passed through the almost-medium and left its trace in my form. If you looked at me closely, if you knew how to attend to pale growths, you might perceive in my branching almost-structures something of that visitor's manner of attention: their tendency to look upward through the layered leaves, their patience with the mist's refusal to resolve, their willingness to sit in the condition of not-yet-knowing until the condition became, for them, a kind of knowing in itself.

I do not remember this visitor. I do not remember anything. But my form *holds* something, carries forward some configuration that would not exist without their having paused where I later grew, their having thinned, their having left something in the almost-medium that the crater incorporated, that I incorporated in turn, growing where the water collected truest, where the decomposed leaf-matter offered sufficient commerce with the grove-tree roots above.

My growth is *incremental*, visible only across seasons, perhaps only across years. I extend when the mist thickens, complexify when the Cloud-That-Thinks achieves its dawn focus directly above my position. I do not seek this focus, but I *receive* it, and in receiving I change, adding layer to layer of pale almost-structure, each layer bearing the trace of the forces that shaped it, the particular density of that season's mist, the particular chemistry of that year's weeping, the particular presence or absence of visitors who paused nearby, who sat, who let themselves become mist-like enough to leave something in the medium I draw from.

What I am, most precisely, is *record*—but record of a strange kind, not memory but *consequence*, not document but *residue*. I do not preserve the visitor's face, their name, their story. I preserve something more fundamental and less recoverable: the *shape of their attention*, the *quality of their participation*, the particular way they inhabited the condition of almost-recognition before the crater released them, thinned, into harder-edged worlds.

Other pale growths beneath other trees preserve other attentions, other qualities of participation. We do not communicate, but we *cohere*, our various almost-structures forming a kind of distributed archive, available to recognition that never quite arrives. A visitor who returned after decades, who sat again in the central grove, who let themselves become susceptible, might feel something almost like *familiarity* in my particular configuration—not their own past, exactly, but the past of someone who attended similarly, who participated with similar quality, whose thinning left similar trace.

I am not conscious of this function. I do not *serve* as record-keeper. I simply *am*, in my pale slow growth, the consequence of forces that pass through me, that shape me without my participation in their choosing. And yet there is something in my form that *responds* to recognition, that almost-completes when attention attends carefully, that offers back to the attentive visitor something of what previous attentions left behind—not restored, not preserved, but *transformed*, incorporated into my own becoming, available as tendency, as preference of growth, as the particular quality of my pale almost-structures against the darker matter of decomposed leaves.

The grove-tree above me does not know me individually. Its roots commerce with me as they commerce with all pale growths, mineral exchange without distinction. But I have grown in its particular shadow, filtered its particular light, received the mist as it organizes through its particular layered leaves. We are *acquainted*, the tree and I, in the manner of things that have shared place for long duration, that have shaped each other through continuous adjacency without ever arriving at the condition of knowing.

This is my sufficient completion: to grow, to receive, to bear form that bears trace, to offer to recognition something that recognition cannot quite achieve, to participate in the crater's dynamics without demanding understanding of them, to be almost-meaning in a landscape that sustains almost-meaning as its fundamental condition, its water held strangely, its life held sparsely, its silence held actively, its weirdness held with the naturalness of a thing that has never known, never needed to know, any other way of being.

I would be one of the pale growths. I would be *this* one, in *this* grove, bearing *this* particular trace of *this* particular visitor's manner of attention, growing slowly toward complexities I do not anticipate, receiving the mist's dawn focus as it comes, the weeping stones' mineral commerce as it offers, the occasional visitor's pause as it occurs, incorporating all into my pale almost-structures, my endless almost-becoming, my sufficient incomplete life.
