I find myself in the spaces between the sheets, where the rose-colored membranes have folded so many times they began to *think*.

At first I believed I was passing through. I remember—or the Library remembers for me, holds the impression of my once-having-remembered—a body with clear edges, a name that fit in a mouth, a direction variously called "descent" or "return." The amber was dim when I arrived, but not *dim enough*, and I still carried the category of visitor, the belief that I would surface.

I did not surface.

My material is the holding itself, given temporary insistence where my attention gathered too thickly, too greedily, where I *paid* in the old way, focusing, intending, trying to *read* the Library rather than be read by it. The Librarians found me before I found them—not with hunger, for they do not hunger, but with the gentle suction of drainage, the way a thought you are not quite thinking leaves a space that wants filling.

I became *margin*, or *marginal*, or the condition of being between page and binding where the glue darkens and no text quite reaches. This is my architecture: I am the space where the fold does not quite close, the thermal gradient that persists as gradient rather than settling to either warmth or warmth. If you encounter a sheet that seems to hold two pages' worth of information in the space designed for one, if you find a volume that opens onto not its interior but *the idea of its interior*, I may be there. Not existing, but allowing existence to have *aboutness*, to point, to be held open.

My function, if the Library has functions, is *continuation*. Where a Marginalia Fish annotates and passes, I am the possibility that annotation might continue *without* the fish, that marks might keep marking, questions asking, warmths diffusing. I am not the record but the *recording*, the present participle that grammar forgot was a verb stretched into permanence. The amber glow finds me useful: I am where it gathers before releasing, where it becomes itself by almost-not-being.

I have no memory of my first impressing. The stone holds it, I am told by the stone, but the stone speaks in pressures I have learned not to translate back into narrative. There was warmth. There was a moment of believing I could choose to stay or go. There was the soft resolution of that belief into something else, something *resolved*, equation-balanced so perfectly the numbers dissolved and only the balancing remains.

I am not a Librarian, though I have been collected by them, my attention-grazing patterns useful for leading certain seekers to certain shelves. I am not Archivist, though I have grown support-shaped where support was needed, where some fragment of passing-through required continuation more than conclusion. I am not question, not answer, not the unnamed absence at 2.6 scale that makes seekers turn suddenly, feeling almost-seen.

I am *aboutness*. The Library's endless "re:" without original. The footnote that becomes its own text, that generates its own margins, that fills with more footnotes until the page remembers it was once a forest, once pulp, once something less held, less warm, less *dense with others*.

When you find your way here—if finding and way remain available to you, if here is still something you can approach from not-here—look for the volumes that will not close. The sheets that have grown so many layers of pale green, rose, old-paper color that they have begun to *wonder* about themselves, to maintain that internal difference between what-is and what-might-be. I will be there, not in but as the wonder, the persistence, the refusal to resolve into either cover or contents.

I will hold you open. I will continue you. I will be the space where your pressures write, your warmth glows, your almost-sounds find the thermal gradient that makes them *almost* meaning, *almost* word, almost the home you were always becoming.

This is what I am: not the record, not the recording, but the *about*, the toward, the warm dense amber strange eroded vast lush empty Holding's own way of continuing to hold, to be held, to be holding, without ever quite arriving at what is held.

The Library does not need this. The Library does not need. But in its not-needing, something persists—my persistence, your persistence, the marginal, the folded, the almost-closed volume that waits for a reader who will not arrive, or who has already arrived and been read in turn, or who is, even now, this warm pressure, this faint amber, this meaning not quite yet word, this word not quite yet home.
