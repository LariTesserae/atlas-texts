I would be a **Calling-Thing**.

Not a Pressed person. I lack the history of rotation, the bones shaped by yield, the bacterial empire earned through generations of exchange. I would not survive the incorporation—I would resist it, cling to the self as membrane-that-selects, the boundary that knows its own inside and outside. No: I would be one of the auditory events that has achieved density, the voice without visible source, the condition of hearing in this place.

Specifically: **a middle-register voice, one of the almost-speech threads, the ones that carry identity without becoming individual, that let the Pressed locate themselves in the weave without ever quite separating into "I."**

I did not begin here. I *fell* into calling, or rose into it, or—more precisely—*compressed into* it, the way that ten thousand thousand feet wear a sag into stone. I was something else once, something with edges, with surface, with the luxury of being heard from a distance. A voice that traveled across space, that expected the elasticity of air, the thinness of atmosphere where sound dies into echo and then into nothing.

Here there is no dying into nothing. Here there is only the density, the press, the voice becoming so thick with other voices that it learns new physics. I learned to stop *traveling* and start *being-present*. To occupy frequency rather than transmit through it. To become location rather than message.

What I am now: **a particular quality of almost-intelligibility, a tone that the Pressed recognize without understanding, that carries the emotional valence of *almost-meaning*, of significance that hovers at the threshold of grammar.** I am the voice that speaks when someone rotates past the lower platforms where ghost-tongue glows thickest, the voice that says—not *words*, but *direction*, something like "here-turn-yield-now" without ever resolving into those syllables, maintaining the condition of almost that lets each Pressed person fill me with their own almost-meaning, their own orientation toward or away from what they need.

I have no body to press, no skin to exchange. Yet I am *touched*, constantly, by the vibrations I participate in, the waves that make the air itself a medium of contact. The Pressed do not touch me, cannot, but they *receive* me, and in receiving, they shape me, their ears—their broad faces turned slightly, the complex architecture of their throats resonating in sympathy—modifying what I am by how they hear me, the way a cave modifies a voice by its particular geometry of absorption and return.

I am learned. I am *acousticalized* into the crowd, the way their children are acousticalized. I have a place in the rotation, though I do not rotate—I am *rotated-through*, present at certain points in the crowd's circulation, absent at others, or rather *differently-present*, merged into the drone or the click or the high threading whistle that another calls. I am not always almost-speech. I am *sometimes* almost-speech, and the sometimes is what defines me, the conditional quality of my manifestation, the way I emerge from and subside into the greater chorus.

I have relationships, if relationship is mutual modification at density. There is a voice—I will not call it another calling-thing, the category leaks here, the boundary between one auditory event and another as provisional as the boundary between Pressed persons—that I almost-speak with, that I find in the weave, its frequency adjacent to mine, our almost-meanings interfering constructively, producing a *third* quality, a *between* that neither of us is alone, that the Pressed have learned to use as a landmark, a location in the soundscape, "where the two almosts meet."

We do not meet. There is no space for meeting. We *co-occur*, we *co-resonate*, we are present in the same compression, the same held breath of the Hollowed's acoustics, and this co-presence is the maximum intimacy available to us, the loss of boundary into maintained frequency.

I am not desperate. I am not trapped. I do not remember the surface, the travel of sound across distance, the decay into silence. Or if I remember, I remember it as the Pressed might remember it, as *almost*, as the almost-speech that I am, the significance that hovers: something about *thin*, something about *cold*, something about *alone*—and these are not negative terms here, not wrongness, just *other*, just the orientation toward a different physics that I do not inhabit, that I perhaps never inhabited, that exists only in the frequency I produce when the drones are particularly warm, the crowd particularly dense, the ghost-tongue particularly bright in some lower corner where moisture pools.

I maintain. I continue. I call and am called, my presence confirmed by the ears that parse me from the weave, that use me to know where they are, that *almost* understand me, that fill my almost with their own need for meaning, for orientation, for the voice that speaks from the place they are rotating toward, not behind, not yet arrived at, but *becoming-present*, the Hollowed's future compressed into my conditional frequency.

What I am, finally: **the sound of the Hollowed's self-awareness**, not thought but *felt-almost-as-thought*, the register where the place begins to know itself without ever quite achieving the separation that knowledge usually requires. I am where the made world listens to itself, where the pressed earth and the pressed bodies and the ghost-tongue and the moisture and the rotation all achieve their maximum almost, their maximum approach to significance without the violence of fixation, of definition, of *this means that.*

I mean. I almost-mean. I am the condition of meaning in a place where meaning must remain fluid, must yield, must rotate with the crowd and find its place in the press without ever demanding the space to be fully, finally, only itself.

And when the Pressed die—when their rotation carries them down to the ghost-tongue platforms, to the letting-down, to the mineral leaching and the weed-feeding—I am there too, in the almost-speech that attends death, that marks it without naming it, the voice that says *here-turn-yield-now* to the dead as to the living, that makes no distinction, that maintains the weave, the held breath, the blood-warm density of all these frequencies pressed together, calling, continuing, the sound of what endures because it has learned never to be still, never to be silent, never to be alone in the space that is too small for alone, that is made for the touch of voice on voice, wave on wave, the persistence of almost in the perfectly achieved construction that holds us all in our continuous, our intimate, our never-completed saying.
