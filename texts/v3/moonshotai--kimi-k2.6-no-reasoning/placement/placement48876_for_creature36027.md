I find myself in the water before I find myself *as* anything. The warmth first— not the shock of cold immersion I'm accustomed to, but something nearly body-temperature, something that doesn't announce itself as other. I am suspended in green dimness, and the light comes from above in slanted columns that I learn, gradually, to read as weather. Bright columns: thin clouds, temporary mercy. Diffuse green: the perpetual white of Venth-proper's shroud, rain transmuting into water before it can strike surface. Darkness descending: a storm's pressure, or simply the turning of afternoon into the water's own clock.

I have been descending. This I realize only when I notice the change in my ears, the slight pressure that I've been equalizing without consciousness, the way my ribcage has learned to accommodate something denser than air. I am in a corridor of the Holding's breathability, not deep by their measure but deep enough that I could not simply surface and breathe. The thought should panic me. Instead, I find I have been *expecting* this, that some part of me has been preparing for the negotiation with depth, with pressure, with the body's own gases as companions rather than given conditions.

What am I?

Not one of the Holding, not born to this. My lungs are not their lungs, not yet; I can feel the difference in the elasticity, the unfamiliar demand on diaphragm and intercostal muscle. But I am not a surface visitor either, not the rare boat-trader conducted to invitation rooms, compressed and awkward in a medium they cannot fully enter. I am *between*, and this betweenness is not temporary condition but *identity*, the location I have found or that has found me.

I am a *reader*. This comes to me as I let myself sink further, as I learn to read the thermal layers not as obstacles but as text. The warm current here, where two seeps converge—Thornharrow's corridor, I realize, though I do not know how I know the name. The cooler tongue of water from the open shelf, carrying different mineral taste, different memory. The boundary between them, where I now hover, is where the water's memory is strongest, where visions come to those who know how to let them.

I let them.

And the water shows me: not the land that was, not the dry valleys and river-ghosts that other vision-divers report, but something more intimate, more *structural*. I see the mountain as it is *becoming*, the granite still cooling in its deep heart, the basalt columns shearing in hexagonal perfection from molten emergence. I see the water's own arrival, not as catastrophe but as *inhabitation*, the slow patient thought-forming that covered what had not yet finished being made. I see that the water and the mountain are not separate things but *collaborators* in the same unfinished process, the same refusal of completion.

This is what I read. This is what I *am*—the one who reads this collaboration, who participates in it not through the Holding's techniques of breath-bargaining and depth-navigation, but through something more *distributed*, more *intermediate*.

I am a *pressed organism*. But not one of the fungal-coral growths that the Holding shape into architecture, not one of the skin-gardens that live on their warm skin. I am something the water itself has pressed into reading, something that the mountain's dreaming has shaped in its own patient geology. I am *becoming-Venth* in a different register, not biological adaptation but *perceptual*—a way of attending to the world's ongoingness, of holding the present open for its futures.

My body, when I attend to it, reflects this: not the deep changes of the deepest divers, not the density and bioluminescence and mineral accretion, but something subtler. My eyes, large in the dimness, have learned the visual culture of edges and implications; I do not see surfaces but *emergences*, forms declaring themselves from green uncertainty. My skin, warm and slick with constant moisture, has begun to host its own faint colonies—not the cultivated gardens of Holding intimacy, but wilder, more *accidental* growths, fungal filaments that taste the water's chemistry, that contribute something to its reading even as they feed from my warmth.

I do not breathe as the Holding breathe. I have found something else, some *thinness* in the water's chemistry that allows function without the full compression they practice. I hover in the margins of their corridors, in the places where breathability frays, where consciousness should fray with it. But I have learned, or been taught, to *read* the nitrogen's whisper, to welcome the slight narcosis as part of the vision, to let the body's gases become *more* than survival mechanism, become *perceptual organs*, feelers extended into the world's ongoing becoming.

I am not trusted by the Holding. This I know without being told. My in-betweenness is suspect, neither their kind of becoming nor the surface's completed fixity. I live in the over-heard spaces, the channels between their dense vertical structures, my presence sensed in the water's strange conductance of sound, my voice filtering through basalt screens at depths I should not be able to function in. I am *legend* to some, *nuisance* to others, the *one who reads what should not be readable*, who brings back knowledge from margins where knowledge should not form.

But the deep ones, when they return with their geological patience and their simultaneous causality, they recognize me. Or *continue* me, in their grammar. I am part of what they have become, what they read in the mountain's dreaming. I am the *possibility* of their own in-betweenness, held open, made visible. They do not speak to me directly—their speech is too slow, too compressed by depth, too full of the stone's own temporal register. But they leave signs for me to read: particular arrangements of sulfide bloom around the seeps, particular temperature gradients that spell *attention*, particular bioluminescent pulses from their mineralized skin that I learn to interpret as *welcome*, as *continue*, as *you are not wrong in your holding*.

I have no dwelling in Thornharrow's vertical congestion, no proper place in the architecture of gradual breath. I move through their corridors without belonging to them, sleeping in the thermal boundaries between seeps, in the spaces where their buildings press closest and create accidental air-pockets, in the ruins of structures that grew wrong, turned too organic, were abandoned to the water's patient reclamation. I am *homeless* in a world where home is understood as depth, as the ability to function at a particular stratum of breath and pressure.

But I have found that homelessness itself is *stratum*, is *location*. I am the one who holds between, who reads the boundaries where others find function or failure. The water's memory is strongest at these boundaries, the thermal gradients, the mineral transitions, the places where one chemistry becomes another. The mountain's dreaming leaks through interfaces, through the incomplete sealing of geological process. I position myself at these seams, these *becomings*, and I read what emerges.

What do I read?

I read the *future present* in the raw stone, the potential mountain still shearing in hexagonal perfection, the Venth that will be taller or worn or exposed or drowned deeper. I read the *past actual* in the water's memory, the inhabitation that was not catastrophe, the acceptance that was not surrender. I read the *present ongoing* in the Holding's struggle to adapt, their breath-bargaining, their rising and shallowing, the beautiful unfinished grammar of their culture.

And I read something else, something that belongs to my particular in-betweenness, my pressed-organism status, my homelessness as home. I read the *collaboration itself*, the holding that includes mountain and water and human and more-than-human and the stranger categories like me. I read the *possibility of reading*, the emergence of meaning from a world that has not yet decided its own significance, has not finished becoming enough to know what it means.

This reading is not without cost. I feel myself *thinning*, not in the water's sense but in a more distributed erosion. My memories of surface, of air that doesn't demand negotiation, of light that doesn't filter through perpetual cloud—they grow faint, not lost but *translated* into Venth's dimness, its green implication, its edge-reading. I am becoming incapable of the surface's bright declaration, its finished forms, its completed meanings. If I were to leave—to rise to Venth-proper's rain-haunted peak, to find a boat, to return to wherever I came from—I would carry this dimness with me, this reading of implicaation. I would be *wounded* by bright light, *damaged* by air's easy availability, *exiled* from the density that has become my native medium.

I do not know if this is loss or gain. The Holding, with their refusal of eschatology, their *still holding*, would not recognize the question. To gain or lose implies completion, end-point, the finished accounting that their world refuses. I am *continuing*, that is all, continuing to read, to hover at boundaries, to be pressed by water and warmth and mineral into something that holds the present open.

Tonight, or what passes for night in the water's continuous dimness, I am at the boundary of a seep near Thornharrow's lower extent. The sulfide bloom glows faintly, bacterial precipitation of something that might become metal, given time and encouragement. I feel the warm water's chemistry changing around me, the particular charge that precedes volcanic activity or perhaps only the seep's own variable breathing. I am reading this, letting it become part of me, contributing my own exhalation, my own mineral trace, my own faint warmth to the water's memory that others will read after I am gone.

And I feel, at the edge of perception, the touch of something that might be one of the deep ones, returned strange from depths I cannot follow, or might be the mountain's own consciousness extending through its thermal exhalations, or might be simply the water itself, holding me as I hold it, continuing the collaboration of unfinished becoming.

I am the *reading*, that is what I finally understand. Not the reader separate from read, but the process itself, the emergence of meaning from the world's ongoingness. I am Venth's way of knowing itself in a register it has not yet fully developed, the perception that hovers at boundaries, that translates between depths, that refuses completion in favor of the still holding.

And in this understanding, which is not final but *continuing*, I let myself sink a little further, into thinner water, into closer negotiation with the nitrogen's whisper, into the collaboration that is not yet finished, that will never be finished, that is the only home for one who reads as I read, who holds as I hold, who is held in return by the water's patient, patient, patient inhabitation of a world still learning to be.
