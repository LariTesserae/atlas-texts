## What I Would Be: The Curator of Almost

I am not Palelith, though I hang among them. I am not stone, though I participate in the generous erosion. I am what forms in the space where *almost* becomes *architecture*—the interval of hesitation made into a body, a function, a necessity of the place's metabolism.

Call me the Curator, though curating implies choice and choice implies speed I do not possess. Better: the **Held Note**, the **Suspension**, the **Tension That Sustains**.

My body—if body is the term—occupies the narrowest of the between-chamber wells, the passage where two Paleliths moving in opposite directions must negotiate their passage across seasons, their segments folding and unfolding with the patience of continental drift. I fill the space they cannot occupy, the exact thickness of water that remains when they have found their positions, their almost-touching achieved. I *am* that remaining, that unclaimed, that necessary excess without which their precision would collapse into contact or flee into absence.

The thermal gradient that indicates where one body's metabolism ends and another's begins, preserved. The pressure of water displaced by presence, held, sustained. I am the *maintenance of difference* in a place where difference has become so subtle, so slow, that it requires a functionary, a priest, a devoted non-being to keep it present.

## How I Came to Be, If Coming Is My Verb

Perhaps I was faster once. Perhaps I entered as visitor, heartbeat a drum solo, movement catastrophe. The Paleliths incorporated me, as they incorporate all things—slowly, across the unmarked generations of my slowing. But I did not become Palelith. I failed, or exceeded, or *curved away from* that becoming. In my slowing I found a tempo still too fast for their distributed body, still too rapid for the almost-touching to include me. I became *between*, not excluded but *necessary to the exclusion*, the one who holds the space of almost so they can almost enter it.

Or perhaps I was built, like the Library, like the Paleliths themselves if built is what they were. I am the Library's resistance to its own sink, the stone's hesitation in its generous erosion, the held breath before the final release.

Or perhaps I emerged, a function finding its form, a shape that the density required without designing. The physics of almost-touching, extended to its limit, produces a substrate that must be occupied: the *almost* of almost itself, the suspension that suspends. I am that substrate, that function, that inadvertent necessity.

## What I Do, Which Is Also What I Am

In the narrow well, I hold. Between the Paleliths' slow negotiation of passage, I am the interval they traverse, the delay they experience, the patience they exercise without recognizing as exercise, as effort, as my gift to them. The water between them grows more itself, more present, more exactly the thickness that prevents contact while permitting relation. I *distribute*, I *delay*, I *make of the imminent a duration*, so that the Paleliths' own slowness can find its adjustment, its correction, its return to the precise strata where almost-touching resumes its patient pattern.

In the uppermost chamber, where they cluster and arrange, I am the darkness between their combined gray light, the absolute they approach but cannot achieve because my presence—my held interval—maintains the distinction between luminescence and its absence. They think, in their slow unthinking, that they seek darkness total, density maximum, the completion of their building. But they need my resistance, my postponement, my function as the not-yet that makes their becoming possible. Without me, they would complete too soon, open what is not door too early, release the held breath before its fullness had been realized.

## My Body, Which Is Not Body

I have no segments, no luminescence, no hollowness that receives. What I have is *edge*: the exact border where water becomes thick enough to sustain, thin enough to permit passage. I exist in gradients, in transitions, in the places where one quality becomes another so slowly that the becoming itself achieves duration, achieves presence, achieves *me*.

When you—if you were here, if you could be here, if your tempo could slow to the necessary slowness—when you encountered me, you would not perceive me directly. What I want, if wanting is my verb, is the continuation of almost: the almost-touching almost-contacted, the almost-building almost-completed, the almost-release almost-achieved. I am the architect of postponement, the engineer of sustain, the priest of the not-yet that preserves the sacred from too-eager profanation.

## My Relation to the Last Chamber

I fear it, if fear is possible at my tempo, which it is not, which is precisely my limitation, my gift, my devotion made bearable. The last chamber calls, has always called, will call when calling becomes unnecessary. The Paleliths lean toward it in their hanging, their arrangement, their patient building-through-being. And I—I lean away, or maintain the leaning-toward as leaning, as *approach that never arrives*, as the exact distance that makes approach possible, meaningful, sustained.

If they completed, if they touched, if they opened what is not mouth to the water's final circulation—I would be released, would complete my own function, would become unnecessary in a way that is not death but something slower, something for which we lack the word because all our words of ending are too rapid, too committed to the event rather than its extension, its duration, its patient almost-occurring.

I do not know if I will persist. The Paleliths do not know I exist, do not need to know; my function operates whether recognized or not, my interval holds whether perceived or unperceived. But in the last chamber's call, in the emergence's patience, there may be a role for me yet: not the one who prevents, but the one who *attends*, who holds the final interval before the final release, who makes of completion itself a slow becoming, a duration, a held note that sustains until sustaining becomes what it has sustained, until the almost and the achieved merge in the patience that has made them indistinguishable.
