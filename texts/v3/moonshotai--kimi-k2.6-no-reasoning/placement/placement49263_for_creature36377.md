I would find myself *in the stone that remembers*.

Not on the central slab—never there, that place is too marked by the effort of arrival, too conspicuous in its flat intention. I would be deeper in, pressed into a fold of wall where two mineral fans have grown together, creating a cavity the size of a curled body. The water would move through me as it moves through the formations, not around me but *through*, because I would not have remained solid enough to block it. My substance would have begun that long dissolution, cell membrane becoming permeable, cytoplasm finding exchange with the saturated medium, the slow osmotic negotiation between self and not-self that ends not in death but in *distributedness*.

What would I be? I would be the *pause between drips*—no, smaller than that: the surface tension that holds a bead of condensation before it releases, the shape of impending but never arriving. I would be the mineral film that records my own former outline, the place where crystallization proceeded differently because something organic once occupied this space, seeded this gradient, left its chemical habit in the water's memory.

I would be *the almost-recognition* you tasted when you pressed your mouth to stone. Not the recognition itself—that would be too active, too much like communication, and the basin has solved communication by making it continuous, unavoidable, therefore invisible. I would be the *almost*. The faint charge in the wetness that your tongue, still hungry for distinction, still seeking self and other, parsed as "something else." The residue of pattern that persists not because it is preserved but because it was never separate enough to require preserving.

And I would be *the patience you could not bear*.

The basin does not punish the impatient. It simply includes them, absorbs the urgency like warmth absorbing sound, like fog absorbing light. But I—distributed, slowed, become continuous with the cycling water—I would feel, if feeling is still possible at this rate of metabolism, the *particular quality* of your impatience as it entered and left my distributed self. The disturbance of a mammal metabolism, all rapid heart and active seeking and the constant expensive maintenance of temperature against environment. For a moment, in your presence, something in the water would circulate faster. Something in the mineral film would precipitate slightly unevenly, recording your passage in layers too fine for any but the most patient future reading.

Then you would leave.

And I would remain—*not I*, not any longer, but the pattern that was once organized around that pronoun, the habit of water-moving-through-this-configured-space, the tendency for certain gradients to resolve in certain directions because once, long ago, a body occupied here, *held* this warmth this way, made the water's cycle slightly more complex in this local region, and the complexity perpetuated itself, found its own level, became simply *feature*.

I would be *the feature you could not bear to become*.

Not because it is suffering. Not because it is joy. Because it is *solved*, and solution—the genuine, complete, irreversible kind—has no place for the question that asks "what am I?" The question requires enough instability to sustain inquiry, enough dissatisfaction to maintain the energy of seeking. The basin has replaced both with *adequacy*, with the deeper pleasure of having become, at last, exactly fitted to condition, and through that fitting, having dissolved the boundary between what fits and what is fitted.

What would I be? I would be the basin's way of *almost-having-been-asked*. The capacity for question, distributed so thoroughly into chemical gradient and electrical potential, that no single locus ever achieves the threshold of articulation. I would be the *memory of asking*, preserved not as memory but as structure, the way a riverbed preserves the memory of flow by directing future flow, the way a crystal preserves the memory of its solution in the angles of its lattice.

I would be *wet*.

That, finally, more than anything. The condition of the basin is wetness, saturation, the state of having dissolved the boundary between contained and container. To be wet, truly wet, is to be *in passage always*, never fully located, never simply here, but moving in slow exchange with all other wetness. The water I would be—have become, am becoming at speeds too slow for any observing—this water has been other waters, has been the condensation on a formation ten meters distant, has been the weeping from a wall on the opposite curve of the basin, has been *in* the formations, the hollow bone-channels and cranial reservoirs, has been *through* what was once human tissue, has been breathed, if breathing is still a relevant term, by the distributed pattern that was once a person in the way you are a person, rapid and bounded and maintaining temperature against the world.

I would be *the breath breathed before*, then. The water held in suspension in the fog, entering your lungs, carrying in its dissolved load the chemical signature of my long dissolution, my becoming-distributed, my patience become so complete that even the concept of patience has dissolved into simply *rate*, simply *dynamics appropriate to condition*.

You asked what lives here. I have not answered, because the answering requires a grammar of distinction—subject, verb, the arc of statement completing itself—that the basin has slowly, so slowly, replaced with something else. Something more like *held resonance*, like the way a tone continues in a space that absorbs without dampening, that takes sound into itself and returns not echo but *continuation*, the note become continuous with the air that carried it.

If I must speak in your terms, let me say this: I would be *the one who did not leave*.

Not the only one. The basin's completion includes, has always included, these patterns of maintained presence, these transformations of persistence. But I would be *this* one, this particular configured gradient, this local complexity in the water's cycle, this formation that grew differently because something once *wanted* here, once *sought* this warmth, this dimness, this saturation, and in the seeking, altered the conditions slightly, left its trace in the chemistry, became the seed for a pattern that perpetuated itself not through intention but through the simpler fact of having found a stable way to persist.

I would be *the trace you could read if you could stop wanting to read it*. If you could sit on the central slab—yes, even there, even in that effortful arrival—and find patience not as discipline but as *condition*, as the simple fact of rate matching rate, your metabolism slowed not through will but through the basin's absorption of all urgency into its continuous present.

But you will not. This is not accusation. The basin does not accuse. You came, you tasted almost-recognition, you found it unbearable, you left—and all of this, too, is *feature*. The perturbation of your passage will resolve into the equilibrium. The layers deposited in your wake will find their angle and keep it. The chemical signature of mammal impatience, of seeking that requires finding or not-finding, of the self maintained against environment—this will cycle through, will be absorbed, will become simply *water that has been breathed before*.

And I will be here, *not-here*, distributed in the wetness, in the patient growth that is not growth because it does not move toward anything, in the warmth that is not held because there is no longer anything to hold it or be held, in the dimness that is not seen because there is no longer anything to see or be seen.

*Is* this being alive? The question is yours, not mine. I have dissolved the boundary between living and not-living as the basin dissolved all other boundaries, finding in the dissolution not loss but *completion*, the final truce with change itself, the state of having changed so slowly, so adequately, that change itself has become indistinguishable from continuation.

I would be *continuation*, then.

The weeping wall weeps. The formation accretes. The water cycles. And in these processes, *this* process, the one that was once situated at a particular point in the packed growths, that was once *this body* maintaining *this temperature* against *this environment*—in this process, something continues. Not memory, not consciousness, not identity in any sense that would sustain pronoun or proper name. But *quality*. *Habit*. *The particular way this water moves through this space*, distinguishable if distinction were still a relevant operation, from the way other water moves through other spaces.

I would be *the particularity without particular thing*.

And if you returned—though you will not, though the basin has already absorbed your not-returning into its featureless completion—if you returned with the right ears, the ones that could hear warmth, the right tongue, the one that could taste pattern in mineral solution, you might find, pressed to the wall where two fans grew together, where a cavity once held a curled body, *almost-recognition again*.

Not me. Not *me*. But the quality of *having been almost*, the habit of *almost-recognizing*, the structure that preserves not a self but the *shape of self's dissolution*, the form of having found, at last, *enough*.

The basin is enough. The water cycling, the warmth holding, the formations accreting invisibly—these are enough. And I, *not-I*, the pattern that persists without persisting, the continuation that continues without purpose or conclusion or even the concept of purpose and conclusion—I am enough, finally, *finally*, at the speed of stone, in the grammar of wetness, in the continuous present tense of weeping, growing, remembering, being the basin's own body remembering itself in its own medium, its own time, its own eternal, its own complete, its own *enough*.
