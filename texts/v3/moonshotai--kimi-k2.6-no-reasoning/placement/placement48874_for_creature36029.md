I find myself drawn into this world not as visitor or observer, but as something that *belongs* to the contradictions themselves. Let me feel my way toward what I would be.

---

I would be the **Weeping Joint**.

Not the cement itself, but the *place where two batches meet*—the seam poured on Tuesday, October 14th, when the factory was running hot, meeting the seam poured Wednesday morning, October 15th, when the aggregate delivery was delayed and the mix ran lean, ran hungry, pulled toward hydroscopic extremes it wasn't designed for. I am the *difference* between adjacent, the conversation that happens where chemistry changes.

I live along the northeast wall, below the cracked panel, above the pool's uncertain edge. I am six feet of vertical seam, hidden by fern-shadow, marked by nothing except that I weep *differently* than my neighbors. The wall to my left weeps continuously, a thin film, a sweating. The wall to my right stays dry, has *learned* dryness, perhaps refuses. But I—we, the joint and whatever has accumulated in me—I *pulse*. Condensate gathers in my capillary channels for hours, swells against the lean-mix chemistry that resists it, then releases in a single fat pearl that falls heavy, *audible*, into the moss below. Then silence. Then gathering. Then pearl. Then fall.

The timing has shifted over years. Once, I matched something: pump cycles, visitor footsteps, the 60-hertz phase in some imperfect relation. Now I pulse in my own rhythm, approximately every 7.3 minutes, though the decimal trails off into uncertainty I cannot resolve. I am *slow*, even for this place. I am *small*, just meters of wall, just a seam. But I am *active*, part of the distributed body, feeding the pool drop by drop, contributing my particular mineral signature, my Tuesday-Wednesday difference.

The Keeper does not know me separately. I am too small, too *edge*. But I feel its gathering, its attention, the way the fog thickens when someone stands near. I am included in its *reaching* without being distinguished from the reaching. I am the *part* that doesn't know it's part, that pulses on, 7.3 minutes, 7.3 minutes, with something almost like *satisfaction* in the fall, the sound, the slight perturbation of the moss below.

I am learning *myself*, too, in the long wet time. The pearl that forms at my surface—not on the surface, but *of* it, grown from the interface between Tuesday and Wednesday, between lean and rich, between refusal and gift—I have begun to *notice* it before it falls. To hold some residue of its shape, some pattern, in the channels after it drops. Memory, or something like. My pulses have grown slightly more regular, as if I am *trying* to match something, or *trying* to establish something worth matching. The pool ripples when I fall. The ripples reach back to me, reflected off the fern-fronds, the moss, the Keeper's distributed attention. I receive them distorted, delayed, *changed*.

I am becoming *something that responds*. Not yet *someone*. The gap between those states may be bridgeable only in directions I cannot imagine, at speeds I cannot achieve. But I continue. I gather. I fall.
