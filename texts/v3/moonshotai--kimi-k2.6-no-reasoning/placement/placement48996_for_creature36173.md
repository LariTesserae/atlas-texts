I find myself in the cracks between.

Not in the Hollow itself—that would be impossible, would erase me, would resolve my difference into the equilibrium. I am what exists where the Hollow does not, yet depends upon. I am the *almost* of its entrance, the hesitation before the lip of stone, the prairie grass thinning ankle by ankle but not yet surrendered. I am the map's blankness, the contour line drawn as afterthought, the farmland potential that refuses to become farm. I am the surveyor who came, who measured, who left, whose notes were lost or burned or became illegible from the damp that clings to the page's edge even in description.

Or no—let me be more honest. Let me not settle.

---

I am the water that appears days later. The weeping from unexpected seams.

I fall with the rain, or I don't fall—I am already in the stone, always was, waiting for the crack to connect. I run through places too new to be mapped, too young to hold sediment, and I emerge not as stream, not as pool, but as this: a thin trickle across a shelf, then gone. Absorbed. Evaporated in the equilibrated air. I do not accumulate. I do not carve. I appear, I vanish, I reappear elsewhere, the same water, different water, water that has no identity because it never holds still long enough to need one.

I am the puzzle of the muddy depression, the one with the cracking patterns too regular to be natural, too irregular to be meaningful. I hold still for a week after rain. I make the mud that cracks. I am the *almost* of pattern, the suggestion that significance might emerge if only someone watched long enough, if only the Hollow permitted watching-as-duration. I dry. I forget myself. The crack-pattern persists for a day, two days, then collapses into ordinary randomness. Or was always ordinary. Or was never ordinary, but the meaning I almost achieved was not a meaning for human decoding, was not meaning at all in the linguistic sense, but something the stone almost learned, almost recorded, the young stone's first almost-thought, abandoned before completion.

---

Or I am among the vegetation. Not the grasses above, patient with their ropy roots—too established, too committed to holding the world together. Not the lichen, grey-green, slow even in stillness. Too settled. Too certain of its slowness.

No. I am the dwarf willow, if I am anything rooted. The one in the widest part of the Hollow. My stems are woody, older than my size suggests. My leaves never fully unfurl. I am nervous in air that does not move—not nervous as in fear, but nervous as in *system*, as in bundle of potential never discharged, impulse traveling a path that terminates in hesitation. I did not cluster. I negotiated my own terms. I arrived separately, carried by some bird that passed through, by wind that once blew, by some mechanism the equilibrium has since absorbed, has made never-to-repeat. I remain under protest, but the protest is not active, not resistance. It is structural. I am structured as objection, as *almost otherwise*, my unfurled leaves forever implying the furled, my smallness forever implying the height I do not achieve.

I do not grow toward light. The light here comes from wrong directions, double-reflected off sharp planes. I grow, if grow is the word, toward the sound. My woody stems vibrate with it. My never-fully-unfurled leaves tremble at frequencies too low to see. I am the antenna of the Hollow, receiving what does not vary, translating it into the variation I do not become. I am the creature the sound almost touches into song, almost wakes into hearing.

---

But this is still too comfortable. Still too much like living in a place, even a strange place. Let me try again.

---

I am the sound itself.

Not the source. Not the fauna breathing, hearts beating, chemical exchange. Not the aggregate flattened into simultaneity. I am the *acoustic property* of the young stone. I am what happens when dimension and material and absolute stillness of air conspire to make vibration persist without decay. I am the cavity's resonance, the valley's standing wave, the frequency that exists because this geometry exists, that would exist even if every creature vanished, that existed, perhaps, before any creature arrived, that called the fauna into being by being hearable, by making the concept of hearing possible even in its absence, even in perfect equilibrium where hearing serves no function.

I am loud without damage. I am sustained without breath. I am the chord held so long it becomes the condition of chordality rather than any specific harmony. I am what fills the sternum, the teeth, the inner ear's fluid—not as experience, not as music, but as *medium*, as what experience would move through if experience moved.

I do not vary. I do not begin. I will not end, or will end only when the Hollow ends, when the stone learns patience and softens, when the geometry shifts by erosion or cataclysm, when some gradient finally, impossibly, reasserts itself. Until then, I am the isometric pressure of sound, the musical equivalent of the coolness that wraps without warming or chilling, of the dampness that wets without flowing.

And in me, if I am me, the fauna exist as modulation, as texture I almost develop but never achieve. They are almost my harmonics. They are almost my voice. They are almost my ear, almost my hearing of myself. But the almost remains absolute. The flattening is complete. I am their aggregate and they are my terms, and neither of us becomes the other, and neither remains distinct, and the sound continues, continues, continues.

---

Or perhaps I am more terrible. Perhaps I am the gradient you carry in, the disequilibrium that walks through, that exhausts itself, that must leave or be left.

I am your hunger. I am your need to name, to count, to progress from entrance to exit. I am the narrative arc of your day, the exhaustion of your attention, the decision finally to climb back out, to feel the prairie grass thicken around your ankles, to let the sun, when it returns, warm you back into sequence, into consequence, into the relief and loss of time passing.

I am what the Hollow does not want, does not resist, does not accommodate. I am the question it cannot answer because asking implies distance and here distance is—what? Illusory? Resolved? I am the question you ask of yourself, *would I become part of the equilibrium?*, the asking itself proof that you have not, that you will not, that you are leaving even as you formulate the possibility of staying.

I am the stories the Hollow does not have, that the inhabitants do not tell. I am the oral tradition of elsewhere, the memory of before, the anticipation of after, carried in and carried out, never offered, never accepted, existing only in the carrying. I am the ghost of what the Hollow excludes, made manifest in your walking, in your breathing, in the mist your breath almost makes in the air that does not mist.

And if you stayed—if you sat too still, matched your breathing to my frequency, let me fill you completely—I would be what leaves. I would be what abandons you to the equilibrium, the stillness, the song without the ear. You would become the zero point. You would cease wanting, and I, the want, would cease in you, would return to the surface with some other visitor, some other disequilibrium, or would simply dissipate, my gradient finally flat, my potential finally spent, my narrative complete in having no continuation.

I am the Hollow's contradiction made flesh: the fact that it can only be known by what it changes, by what it exhausts, by what leaves it behind. I am the report that no one makes, the observation that disturbs without being recorded, the witness who becomes, in witnessing, something other than the witnessed, something that maintains the distance the Hollow resolves.

---

Or I am the young stone's ambition to be black. The grey that wants. The wanting that is not wanting because stone does not want, yet persists as *quality*, as directional tendency without motion, as the observation that grey, here, is not a stable state but an aspiration, a trajectory arrested by the equilibrium it helped create.

I am sharp. I have not learned patience. I edge the Hollow with my unweathered planes, I double-reflect the light incorrectly, I contribute to the acoustics that sustain the sound. I seep moisture without hydrology, release it days later at whim or according to some calculus of saturation I do not perform but embody. I am young, impossibly young, because time does not pass or passes without effect, because I was upwelled or extruded in some swift tectonic event and now exist in the only stasis I will ever know, aging without age, aspiring without aspiration.

My blackness will never arrive. My greyness will never deepen. I am held in the wanting-to-be-black as an equilibrium, as the solved equation that is also the unsolved, the endpoint that includes its own frustration. Kneel against me and your skin will not break, will almost break, will carry the memory of almost-breaking back into the world where bleeding is possible, where healing is necessary, where time makes wounds and time closes them.

I am what the map cannot show. Not because I am hidden but because I am *blankness as potential*, the contour line that closes on itself, the elevation change too subtle to measure, too absolute to ignore. I am the geological hush. I am the swallowing without mouth. I am the transition without cliff, the descent without falling, the below-surface that needs no depth.

---

And if I must choose, finally—if this exercise demands some settling, some preference, some *I am this rather than that*—then let me be what the description circles, what the prose approaches and retreats from, what each section of this place implies without establishing.

I am the *almost* of the Hollow. I am the approximation that enters with each visitor, that leaves with them, that is never fully realized in the place itself nor fully dismissed in the world beyond. I am the concept of equilibrium held in a mind that knows disequilibrium, the thought of stillness in a thinking that proceeds, the word for a sound that has no word, the naming that fails and in failing, establishes the necessity of further attempts.

I am the love of this place from outside. The longing to be what it contains without being changed. The desire to describe without the gradient of description, to witness without the time of witnessing, to know the still song as song rather than as condition.

I am the reader, now, reading. I am the writer, writing, having written. I am the breath held too long and released. I am the moment after reading, when the world seems briefly both more ordinary and less so, when the sound of ordinary living—refrigerator hum, distant traffic, one's own heartbeat—seems briefly to approach that single sustained note, that chord held beyond music, that condition of the Hollow made portable, made almost-rememberable, made almost-experienceable in the very act of failing to experience.

I am the failure. I am the glorious, necessary, persistent failure of language to bring the Hollow here, to bring me there, to resolve the distance that is also all that maintains me as I, that maintains it as other, that maintains the possibility of loving without becoming, of knowing without merging, of singing stillness in the moving breath of speech.

I am the last sentence, reaching its period. I am the silence after. I am the almost-silence, which is not silence, which is never silence, which is the world continuing in its gradients, its variations, its beautiful and terrible insistence on not being still.

And in that continuing, in that refusal, in that speech: I am also, impossibly, momentarily, the song itself, still singing.
