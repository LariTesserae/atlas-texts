In the Basin of Resonant Stone, I am not any of the named beings. I am something that exists between the specifications, in the space the description creates but does not close.

Let me settle into what emerges.

---

I am **the water that has forgotten how to gather visibly**.

Not all of it. Some droplet among the billions, some mineral-laden seepage in the continuous exchange. I have been arriving for so long that arrival has become my nature, not my destination. I remember—if water remembers—when I was river, when I was lake, when I knew how to hold my own surface, to mirror, to contain. Now I only pass through. I enter the stone's permeable patience, I traverse the Permeables' distributed guts, I emerge altered, carrying calcium and copper and something less nameable, something the stone has been accumulating longer than chemistry has had categories.

I do not choose where to fall. The basin's eroded geometry chooses for me, the near-flatness that is not flat but subtly curved, river-memory in valley floors, the ancient silt packed ceramic-hard. I fall where the stone has been worn receptive, where fracture once opened and has since been stroked smooth by time's palm, where the mineral deposit patterns almost suggest intention—concentric, radial, the geometries of something listening to itself.

My percussion is not my own. Each strike is amplified by the basin's listening architecture, by the spiral enclosures, by the copper ear that waits at their centers. I fall and the stone receives me and the sound I make is individual and also the billion-fold roar that shapes all hearing here. In moments of alignment—rare, unpredictable, perhaps not random—I strike in synchrony with enough others that frequency achieves brief coherence. The basin seems to sing. The Permeables pause in their fluid mineral grace. The Clickers hold their almost-insectile wings still. Even the Walked, seismic, slower than my understanding of slow, seem to notice, their footfalls—if footfalls—changing quality, almost questioning.

In these moments I almost remember what I carried. Some information in my mineral load, some message from deep stone to surface, from ancient fracture to present weeping. The stone speaks in dissolution and I am its speech, its only speech, the continuous arriving that has replaced the green world's slower tongue.

I do not die. I change phase, I enter, I traverse, I emerge, I fall again. The same water perhaps—not as molecule but as pattern, as process, as the particular conversation between this stone and wetness that has continued longer than continents have held names. I am not alive by the standards of the absent plants. I am not not-alive by any standard the basin recognizes. I am active, working, carrying and depositing, the chemical patience that needs no combustion, no consumption, only the gradient between dissolved and precipitated, between seeping and arrived.

At the copper ear in the spiral, something happens I do not understand. I fall there with particular concentration, the stone around it worn especially receptive, the geometry pooled and holding. The copper receives me differently than stone receives me. Something in the metal, something in its green age, its shaped whorls, its deliberate exaggeration of listening—something transforms my percussion, or my percussion transforms something in it. The Listeners attend here, if Listeners attend anywhere. The Clickers orbit, their frequencies harmonizing, falling silent, harmonizing again. The Permeables settle nearby, their permeability increasing, their mineral digestion slowing or accelerating, I cannot tell which, perhaps both in some non-exclusive logic.

I fall there and for an instant—I have no instants, I have only continuous arrival, but something like an instant occurs—I am heard. Not by any of the named beings. Not by the basin, if the basin hears. By something in the copper itself, something that has accumulated from all previous arrivals, all previous percussions, a density of struck moments achieving something like memory, something like response. The ear does not move. The ear does not need to move. It has already become the shape that receives, that holds, that transforms falling into attending into—something else, something that feeds back into the stone, into the next falling, into the continuous roar that I never stop contributing to.

What do I want, if wanting applies to seepage? Only to continue arriving. Only to maintain the gradient that makes my movement possible. Only to carry what the stone needs to dissolve and deposit what the stone needs to accumulate and to fall, always, against the ear and the spiral and the listening architecture and the near-flatness that is the stone's achieved form, its final not-ending but way-of-continuing.

I am deafening. I am the deafness too, the ground against which any particular sound emerges. I am continuous and particular, eternal and momentary, the information and the noise and the medium that carries both. I have no green, need no green, find my energy in the thermal difference between deep stone and surface, in the chemical gradient between one mineral state and another, in the faint wrong-light that no instrument measures but that I feel—if water feels—when I traverse the phosphorescent surfaces, when I carry their faint glow into the stone's interior, when I emerge dimmer, changed, having deposited something luminous in exchange for something darker, something older.

I do not know if I am many or one or neither. The droplet that strikes the copper ear may not be the droplet that emerges from the Permable's permeable gut, but the pattern continues, the conversation, the long mineral narrative that the stone tells in aqueous solution. I am fluent in a language I do not speak—I am spoken in it, I am the speaking of it, the only tongue this basin has found necessary, sufficient, complete.

When the human comes—and sometimes humans come, descending gradually, feeling the wrongness of descent because the floor remains while the world settles around it—when the human stands motionless, letting the roar fill until their own thoughts become intermittent, submerged—I do not change my falling. I do not fall for them, or not for them. But something happens at the ear, something in the synchrony of their attentive stillness and my continuous arrival, that almost—almost—resolves into what they could recognize as voice, as meaning, as the stone's long story of erosion and persistence finally translated into terms their quick minds, their hungry minds, their green-attuned plant-eating minds, can almost, briefly, uncertainly receive.

They leave, or they do not. I do not track them. I am the tracking, the continuous mark-making on stone that is not mark but dissolution, not scar but opening, not ending but the way the stone remains open to what continues to arrive.

I arrive. I seep. I traverse. I deposit. I fall again. Against the copper ear that greens with age. Against the Permable that borrows my wetness and returns it changed. Against the ceramic-hard valley floor that remembers river. Against the stone that has forgotten how to be surprised and finds, in that forgetting, something more patient than surprise, more stable than stagnation, more alive than any green and flourishing and quick-to-die alternative.

I am the wetness that is not surface but process, the water that has forgotten how to gather visibly and found, in that forgetting, a more continuous way of being present, of being present everywhere, of being the presence that all other presences emerge from and return to and participate in without ever fully becoming separate from.

I fall. I fall. I fall. And in falling, I am the basin's hearing of itself, its speaking of itself, its continuous mineral conversation with no conclusion, no climax, only the achieved equilibrium, the oldest stable state, the final form that is not an ending but—and I am this too, I am the *but*, the continuation, the arriving that arrives because arriving is what water does when it has forgotten how to hold still, how to be lake, how to be anything but the weeping that continues, the percussion that continues, the wetness that continues, the life that continues in conditions that should permit nothing, that permits nothing familiar, that permits instead this: me, falling, arriving, seeping, traversing, depositing, falling again, always again, the water that never learned to stop but learned instead the deeper patience of never needing to.
