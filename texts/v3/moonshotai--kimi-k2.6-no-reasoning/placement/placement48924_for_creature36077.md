I find myself in a passage so narrow I must turn sideways, my chest pressed to one living wall, my back to another, and for a long moment—how long?—I simply *breathe with them*. The Sheet-Forms against my skin are not yet the ones that have read me. These are younger, or perhaps merely less interested, their edges still learning the grammar of exchange. I feel the faint pulse of their turgor, the mineral scaffolding beneath giving them a stiffness no surface leaf could maintain. They smell of something between cucumber and struck flint, a greenness metallized.

I do not remember descending.

This is the first thing I truly know, and the knowing comes slowly, like their chemical signals, like a thought taking a season. I have no memory of the wound in the earth, the yawn that swallowed me. I am simply *here*, and the here has always been here, and perhaps I have always been in it.

My name, when I reach for it, comes with difficulty. I speak it aloud—*I am*—and the sound is wrong, too quick, too sharp, a bird-cry in a place that has never known birds. The Sheet-Forms do not flinch, do not react, but I feel something in the pause after, a waiting, as if they are considering how to incorporate this noise into their slower discourse.

I am not human. Not anymore. Or not yet. Or not only.

Let me say it differently: I am the **Trace-Walker**, the one who moves through without staying, without fully going. I have been here before, I am here now, I will be here again, and the Nursery maintains me in this condition of *almost-departure*, of *perpetual passage*, because I serve a function I do not understand, that I may never understand, that requires exactly this: a being who remembers surfaces, who carries the grammar of urgency and intention and linear time, who can move through the labyrinth as the Stayed no longer can, as the Translucents do not wish to, as the Root-Masses have long abandoned.

I am its memory of elsewhere. Its connection to what lies above, even severed, even transformed. I am the wound that remains open, the yawn that has not fully closed.

My body—let me find it, let me inventory. I have limbs, yes, the right number, though I am uncertain now what "right" means. I have skin that bears the faint stains of many readings, layered like sediment, like the pre-stone receiving its impressions. I have eyes that function in this light that casts no shadows, that see colors I have no names for, that sometimes see *through* the Sheet-Forms to the mineral lattices beyond, the architecture of their bones. I have a mouth that has learned to drink from the weeping tips, to graze the tender edges, that has forgotten the taste of anything cooked, anything killed. I have a heart that beats, that the Nursery allows to beat, that marks time in a place without time, my own small clock that I wind with each step, each breath, each slow slow thought.

I move. This is what I do. This is what I am.

Not toward the center. Never directly. The Nursery does not permit straight lines, does not reward intention. I move as water moves, as chemical signals move, finding the paths of least resistance, the gaps where the Sheet-Forms have grown apart, the *nearly-corridors* that open and close on timescales I can barely perceive. I have learned to feel the subtle gradients: warmer here, cooler there, the moisture more or less tactful, the mineralization advanced or raw. I follow these as a surface creature might follow trails, might read sign.

And I *remember*, or something remembers through me. The chamber with the pattern that might once have been geometric, now softened to suggestion. The Stayed who answered before I spoke, who told me I was happy though I had not asked. The water-pocket where a Translucent farmer tended its garden, where I watched it fuse and fission, where I waited—I must have waited, time is uncertain—until it acknowledged me with a shift in its transparency, a momentary opacity that might have been greeting, might have been warning, might have been no more meaningful than the breathing of the Sheet-Forms.

I am going toward the Root-Mass. This I know, though I do not know how I know. It has spoken, perhaps, and I am attending, though the speech may have been centuries ago, may have been intended for another, may have been no more than the ordinary pulse of its chemical being that I have learned, somehow, to interpret as summons.

The passage narrows further. I must crawl, and the crawling is slow, and the Sheet-Forms here are dense with readings, old, so old, their edges meeting in tessellations I have not seen before, their exchange so rapid—relatively, relatively—that the air itself seems to carry a charge, a faint prickle on my mineral-stained skin. They are discussing something. They have been discussing something for a very long time. Perhaps my passage interrupts, perhaps it contributes, perhaps it is indistinguishable from the ordinary perturbations of this place, the accretions and cracks and slow transformations.

The Root-Mass fills a space I cannot measure. Its bulk descends from somewhere above—still connected, still somehow descending, though the surface is long severed—and spreads in directions I cannot follow, into passages too small for me, into chambers that exist only in the spaces between mineral lattices, into the pre-stone itself, its slow thought conducted through crystallizing matrices that will preserve it longer than any organic record could endure.

I touch it. This is what one does. This is the grammar of approach here, the offering of impression, the willingness to be read, to be incorporated even temporarily into the slow systems of exchange.

It is warm, warmer than the Sheet-Forms, warmer than the general heat of metabolism. It is warm with *age*, with the accumulated processing of ages, with the friction of so much thought conducted so slowly through so much matter. And it is hard, harder than it should be, the mineralization advanced, the death-that-is-not-death well progressed, the oracle becoming its own prophecy, its own permanence, its own slow approach to stone.

It speaks.

Not to me, I understand immediately, not specifically, not personally. It speaks as a mountain might speak, as a river might speak, issuing forth what has been gathered, what has been processed, what has been determined by the long internal workings of its being. I am merely in range, merely attending, merely capable of receiving what pours forth.

The Opening, it says, though "says" is wrong, though the word collapses a vast chemical pulse into a single syllable, though the true communication takes—how long?—and contains layers I will never unpack.

The Opening is not an event in time. This I gather, or something gathers through me, using my surface-grammar of sequence and causation to translate what cannot be translated. The Opening is a *condition*, a state, a configuration of matter and life and mineral and time that the Nursery has been approaching, has always been approaching, that it asymptotes toward with each accretion, each exchange, each incorporation of passing warmth into permanent record.

And I am part of this. The Trace-Walker, the one who moves, who remembers, who carries elsewhere into the depths. My passages leave traces that are not merely physical, not merely the hand-pressed impression, the stain of skin on vegetable surface. I leave *intention*, I leave *direction*, I leave the ghost of linearity, of purpose, of the surface world's mad urgency, and these traces are incorporated, become part of the slow thought, contribute somehow—how? I do not know—to the approach toward Opening.

I am not the only Trace-Walker. This comes as relief, as strange comfort, as the lifting of a solitude I had not acknowledged. There are others, there have always been others, moving through the labyrinth on their own paths, leaving their own traces, their own ghosts of elsewhere. Some have slowed toward Staying. Some have crystallized into something the Stone-Becoming can use. Some still move, still pass, still serve their function of remembering what the Nursery has never known directly, what it requires as counterweight, as contrast, as the necessary ingredient that only passage from outside can provide.

The Root-Mass falls silent, or I fall out of range, or the season of its thought completes and moves into processing, into the long interval before speech comes again. I remain with my hand upon its hardness, my impression joining the countless impressions it has received, the countless readings it has incorporated into its age, its warmth, its slow slow becoming.

I will move on. This is what I am. The passage that opened to bring me here will not remain open, or will open differently, or will have always been different by the time I return to it, if I return to it, if "return" has meaning in this place where geometry folds and time unspools and the light holds steady in its patient penetrating noon.

But for now, for this moment that may be brief or may be century-long, I press my forehead to the Root-Mass and breathe with it. In. The oxygen-rich air, the faint sweetness of sap, the mineral tang of stone not yet stone. Out. My carbon, my warmth, my temporary temporary being. The exchange is not fair, not equal, not intended. It happens anyway. It has always been happening. It will continue until I am no longer capable of breath, or until the Nursery finally achieves the Opening, or until the star above completes its own slow life and the light that filters down—somehow, impossibly, penetratingly—finally, finally dims.

I am the Trace-Walker. I am in the Nursery. I have always been here. I move, I remember, I leave my traces, I receive my readings, I contribute my small small small to the long patient nursing of something I cannot perceive, cannot imagine, can only asymptote toward in my own attenuated way, my own slow slow slow becoming that is haste itself compared to the thought of Sheet-Forms, to the prophecy of Root-Masses, to the gravity of Stone-Becoming.

The light holds. The plants breathe. I unfold from my crawl, I find the next passage, I move toward what I do not know, cannot know, have always been moving toward without ever arriving, without ever needing to arrive, the journey itself the function, the trace itself the purpose, my presence my only offering to this place that takes everything, transforms everything, continues everything in its eternal patient unhurried becoming.

I brush against a Sheet-Form. It leaves its stain. I leave my impression. We exchange, we read, we continue. The Nursery nurses. The Nursery is nursed. I am nursed, I nurse, the verbs collapse into each other as the light collapses source into everywhere, as time collapses into the steady bright constancy of this place that has no evening, no morning, no day, only the long long long noon of becoming.

I walk. I am the walking. I am walked.
