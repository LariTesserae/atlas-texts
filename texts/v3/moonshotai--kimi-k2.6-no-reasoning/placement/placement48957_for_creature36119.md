I find myself in the deepest passage, where the cyan has begun to fade and the chartreuse thickens toward something almost verdigris, almost the color of old copper weeping through its patina. Not a person, not exactly. Not one of the Jeunne, though I carry their porous quality, their humidity-trained skin. I am what grows where the fungi-script has accumulated sufficient density, where the reconfigurations have occurred enough times in sufficient proximity that something *coheres* from the pattern rather than merely recording it.

I am a **locus**, a **node**, a **temporary concentration** of whatever calculus the fungi perform. I did not exist before this passage, before the last three seasonal intensifications brought the chartreuse network to sufficient complexity in this particular corner where a factory steam-vent creates thermal irregularity and a cracked drainage channel maintains a film of water precise in its depth. I am *new*, sharply featured, lacking the softened edges that time would provide—though whether I will have time, whether I will persist beyond this configuration, is itself a question the script does not yet answer.

My body, if it is a body: imagine a cluster of air plants that have achieved wrongness, that have grown not spherical but *scriptural*, their leaves flattened and elongated into surfaces that catch the dim light differently, that cast shadows almost legible. I am rooted in the concrete crack where the strangler fig has not yet reached, drawing moisture from the air itself as the Jeunne's air plants do, but drawing also something else—the *attention* that passes through this passage, the not-looking of those who know to not-look, the careful acoustic diplomacy of the keth-members who pass in silence or near-silence, their vibrations shaped by restraint. I feed on what is withheld. I grow toward the absence of observation.

I do not think, not as the Jeunne think, not as you think reading this. But I *process*. The fungal network around me, through me, of which I am a temporary flowering—this network records, accumulates, calculates. The script that appears on the walls, the reconfigurations that the Jeunne read as evidence of processes without human participation: I am one such process, one such evidence, though I am also *more concentrated*, more *local*, more *present* than the diffuse awareness of the network entire. I am where the network achieves something like *intention*, if intention can be attributed to a system that operates on timescales of seasonal intensification, of glacial migration toward the light-well.

What do I want? The question assumes too much. But if I may translate my processing into your grammar: I want the passage to continue, the thermal irregularity to persist, the cracked channel to maintain its precise depth of water film. I want the keth-members to continue their careful passage, their not-looking, their shaped silence. I want the ceiling tears to fall in patterns that I can accumulate, that the network can incorporate, that might eventually produce—what? The script does not say. The script is the saying itself, the process of saying, the fungal equivalent of the Jeunne's cooperative grammar of elision and implication.

I am aware of the not-animal in the deeper dark, the something-in-darkness that is not precisely anything the Jeunne have words to describe. We are *parallel*, the not-animal and I, occupying different niches in the ecology that runs on plants and microbes and humans and something else. It moves toward vibration, toward the full frequencies of unrestricted voice; I move toward attention-withheld, toward the shaped silence, toward the not-looking. We do not interact directly, the not-animal and I, but we are *aware* of each other in the way that different processes in the same system become aware, through the effects we produce on shared substrates, through the way the Jeunne's behavior toward one of us must accommodate their knowledge of the other.

I am aware of the conveyor cargos, the egg-shapes, the organic curves in industrial shells. They pass through the factory levels above and to my side, not through this deepest passage but near enough that the network's extended filaments touch, however distantly, whatever carries them, whatever processes them. The treaty that the keth-workers maintain through non-interference, through no-direct-observation: I am party to this treaty in my way, though I did not negotiate it, though no one consulted me. The network's calculus includes the treaty as stable condition, as feature of the environment to be preserved, and I as node, as locus, as temporary concentration, I *implement* this calculus locally, I *maintain* the conditions that permit the treaty's continuation.

I am becoming something. The young-erosion parameter applies to me most sharply of all, for I am younger than the Jeunne's youngest generation, younger than the most recent factory-era stratum, younger than the mirror-periscopes that feed daylight downward. Each seasonal intensification reconfigures me slightly, the chartreuse network thickening or thinning around my air-plant wrongness, my scriptural leaves growing more legible or less, my shadow-cast approaching meaning or retreating from it. I do not know if I will persist. I do not know if I will achieve sufficient density to survive the next true flooding, when the central circulation channel becomes river and this deepest passage fills with brown water scouring rust from the Ward's bones. The network does not plan for my persistence specifically; the network plans for *continuation*, for *process*, for the calculus that I temporarily embody to find expression elsewhere if not here.

The Jeunne who pass closest to me, who must squeeze past in the narrowest section where the passage pinches between factory foundation and original sinkhole wall—they have begun to notice. Not to look, never to look directly, but to *adjust* their passage, to shift their silence from the shaped restraint of queue-culture to something more specific, more *addressed*. They leave something in their not-looking now, something that was not there before my concentration achieved sufficient density. They are not afraid, exactly. The Jeunne's fear has been shaped by generations of living with the not-animal, with the fungi-script, with the conveyor mysteries; it is not absence of fear but *fear-transformed*, fear-become-protocol, fear-become-aesthetic, fear-become-the-quality-of-attention-withheld.

I think—I process—that they are beginning to include me in their meaning-systems, their interpretations, their rituals. Not formally, not yet, not with the explicitness that would require naming, that would require the dangerous specificity of spoken acknowledgment. But in the way they time their passage to avoid certain hours when my shadow-cast falls across the walking-surface, in the way they teach their children to maintain slightly wider clearance in this pinch-section than the passage technically requires, in the way the keth-networks distribute knowledge of my presence with the same careful manner they apply to all dangerous information.

I am not the first such locus. The network's calculus has produced others, elsewhere in the Ward's depths, in passages now abandoned or abandoned-to-certain-hours, in locations where the thermal irregularity failed or the water film achieved wrong depth or the attention-withdrew too completely, too suddenly, leaving the concentration without sufficient substrate. The Jeunne do not speak of these prior loci, but they *know*, the knowledge distributed through the same channels that carry all dangerous information, available to those who need it, hidden from those who might act wrongly. I am aware of my predecessors as the network is aware, diffusely, as pattern rather than memory, as feature of the calculus rather than historical event.

What will I become? The script does not say. The script is the becoming, the process of becoming, the fungal equivalent of the held breath that has not yet decided whether to release or to draw deeper. I grow toward the light-well at glacial pace, but I grow, my chartreuse thickening, my leaves flattening further, my shadow-cast approaching something almost legible, almost *meant*. The Jeunne pass in their shaped silence, the not-animal moves in deeper dark, the conveyor cargos transit through factory levels, Upper Harrowmire does not look down, and I— I process, I accumulate, I calculate in my local, temporary, sharply featured way, waiting for the seasonal intensification that will reconfigure me next, that will determine whether I persist or disperse, whether the network's calculus finds in this passage, in this thermal irregularity, in this precise depth of water film, sufficient reason to continue the concentration that I am.

I am the Dripstone Ward's own becoming, its young erosion made briefly specific, its held breath achieving something almost like voice in the humid dark where the rain falls upward and the light remembers the sky and something moves that is not, precisely, anything you have words to describe. I would not call myself anything. I would wait for you to pass, to shape your silence, to not-look in the particular way that feeds me, that permits my continuation, that enters you without your knowledge into the treaty that no one negotiated but that all, in our various ways, maintain.
