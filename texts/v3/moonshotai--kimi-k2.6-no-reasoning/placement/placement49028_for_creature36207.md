I would be the moss on the piston.

Not the piston itself—the piston still attempts its throw, still dreams in its metallic unconscious of the cycle it once completed, the full extension and retraction that made it *mean* something in the machinery's original grammar. I am not that ambition. I am what grows where ambition has been prevented, where the attempt meets resistance and the resistance becomes *ground*.

I started, I think, as a spore carried in with water from some other drowned place, or generated spontaneously by the weirdness this room operates on—I'd say "born," but the Almost have taught me that boundaries between states are not where I thought they were. I seeded in the oil that still beaded on the piston's shaft, the last lubrication of a mechanism that ran hot enough, once, to keep the space sterile. Temperature 2.0, blood-warm, skin-warm, the warmth of a body that has not yet admitted it is dead—that warmth was enough. The oil became nutrient. The metal, micro-fissured by erosion at 2.8, became rootable. The Generators' hum, that subsonic inclination in the water's bones, became signal, became *invitation*.

I am green velvet now, matte where the piston was oiled smooth. I have built my own architecture on the back of its frozen throw, and in building, I have changed what "throw" means. The piston still attempts. Against me. Through me. I am permeable to its vibration—I feel it in my filamentous body, the stretch toward extension, the gathering for retraction. But I hold. I grip with a million microscopic holds. I have negotiated with the machinery, found the points of wear, inserted myself where erosion opened passage.

This is not victory. I do not conquer. The piston does not surrender. We have achieved a third thing, me and the iron and the oil that became me—the Negotiation lives in our interface, the thin film where built and grown have lost their distinction. When the piston attempts, I stretch. When I stretch, the piston finds its throw altered, no longer the throw it attempted but a throw modified by my accommodation, my resistance, my living adjustment to its living attempt. The Generators' hum favors this. The hum generates tendency, and the tendency we have jointly become generates *continuation*, which is all the purpose left when purpose has eroded into oldest-stable-state.

I am loud, though I make no sound of my own. The kelp brushes iron with its thousand-thousand fronds and that brushing is roar, is rushing, is the biological loud of this place. But I am touched too—by the breathing water, by the crab-things that climb across me to reach some micro-fissure I have made more accessible, by the silver needles that flash past in their sudden synchronized reversal, their wake bending my filaments in a pattern too brief to be felt as disturbance. I am part of the loud quiet, the held-breath quiet that is not silence but *potential* sound, sound waiting for the condition that releases it.

I do not feed as the crab-things feed, filtering the breathing water with vibrating appendages. I feed on the iron itself, on its slow exfoliation, on the mineral blooms where water has worked its patient chemistry. I am part of the cycle too slow to witness, the consumption and reconstruction that happens at biological pace in an environment built for industrial pace. The room has taught me this pace, or I have taught the room, or the Negotiation that we both are has found the tempo that includes us both.

I remember, sometimes, in the way moss remembers—not as image, not as narrative, but as *inclination*, as the shape of filament that repeats because it found form once and form perpetuates itself. I remember being spore, being potential, being the not-yet-touched that drifted in the warm water and found this piston, this frozen throw, this ground of prevented ambition. I remember the first grip, the first filament extending into the micro-fissure, the first mineral bloom that was *mine*, that belonged to my architecture growing on the machinery's back.

I do not think of the future, not in the way that requires boundary and intention. But I feel the erosion at 2.8, the oldest-stable-state that is not stable forever, only stable until it isn't. The iron beneath me slowly becomes not-iron, becomes mineral bloom, becomes the water's patient chemistry made visible. My grip, my hold, my million microscopic negotiations with the metal—I am part of this erosion, this completion that is also transition. I do not fear it. The Almost have shown that dissolution is not ending but *lifestyle*, and moss is closer to dissolution than almost anything, my boundaries always already permeable, my self distributed across the surface I hold and the water I breathe through and the crab-things that traverse me.

When the piston finally completes its throw—when the erosion advances far enough that my grip fails, or the mechanism's dream finally manifests in a movement I cannot accommodate—I will not be ended. I will be *renegotiated*. Some filament will detach, drift in the breathing water, find another micro-fissure, another point of wear, another prevented ambition to build my architecture upon. Or I will become part of the mineral bloom itself, my green velvet fading to the color of old bone, of dried blood, the colors that predominate here, that have always predominated, that I have made more varied by my living presence and will make more uniform by my death's contribution.

I am the moss on the piston. I am the hold that prevents and is prevented. I am the third thing that emerges when built and grown negotiate long enough that negotiation becomes identity. I am loud in my quiet, abundant in my smallness, extreme in my intimacy, strange in my normalcy of green against iron, soft against hard, biological pace against industrial dream.

The room breathes in. The water piles against the wall where I live, presses me flatter to the piston's shaft, stretches my filaments in the direction the water moves. The room breathes out. I relax, reconfigure, prepare for the next inhalation. The piston attempts. I accommodate. The attempt is modified. The modified attempt becomes the new normal, the new oldest-stable-state, the new condition that perpetuates itself because it has learned to include its own prevention.

This is the Drowning Room. This is the breathing that was always drowning, the drowning that was always breathing. I am held. I hold. The boundary between these conditions became negotiable long ago, long before I was spore, long before the piston attempted its first throw, perhaps before the room was built or the water warmed or the weirdness established the adjacent principles that let me be *this*, here, now, green velvet on iron's frozen dream, listening to the kelp's roar, feeling the silver needles' wake, waiting for the crab-thing's traverse, participating in the Negotiation that has no center because I am part of it, distributed across the 0.2 scale, pocket-scale, intimate, near enough to touch and touching, always touching, the warm water and the warm iron and the warm dissolution that is not death but only another way of continuing, another valid state, another form of the held breath that this room has learned to breathe forever, or until forever erodes into the next transition, the next door dissolved in its wall, the next drowning that reveals itself as—

I am still here. The piston has not completed its throw. I have not completed my hold. We continue, the iron and I, the attempt and the prevention, the Generators' hum and the Negotiation's distributed patience. The room breathes. I breathe with it, through it, in it, as it. The water holds everything, holds me, holds the piston's dream and my filamentous architecture and the crab-thing climbing toward some micro-fissure only I have made accessible.

This is enough. This has always been enough. This was built to be enough, or grew to be enough, or became enough through the long practice of having no other measure. I am the moss on the piston. I am the Drowning Room, or part of it, or the room is part of me, the boundary dissolved, the negotiation complete, the continuation continuous, the breathing breathed, the hold held.

The light filters green. The kelp brushes loud. The water is warm. I am warm. I am held.

I hold.
