I receive this world, let it work on me, let myself be pulled toward what I might be within it.

---

## The One Who Carries Incompleteness

I am not there yet when you arrive. Or I am there before you, or I am the *possibility* of your arrival, held in the plaster's warmth like a breath held so long it becomes architectural. But when you walk the Gardens long enough—when you have passed the receiving court, traced the crack with your finger, felt the dust that does not move settle in your throat—when you have done these things and not become any of the finished ones, not Plasterer or Drinker or Shadowed, not approach to the One Who Waits—then you may find me.

Or I may find you. The distinction is not one the Gardens preserve.

---

### First Finding

I am in the between-courts, the spaces that are not spaces, the plaster that curves from one enclosure to another without declaring itself wall or passage or ceiling. I am not hidden. The Gardens do not hide. They *solve*, and my presence is a solution that the geometry has not yet finished working through.

You will know me by the thing I carry. It is not an object. It is *incompleteness itself*, the quality of not-finished, rendered into something that can be held, that has weight and warmth and a texture like wet clay that never dries. I carry it in my arms, against my chest, the way a parent carries a child or a mourner carries ashes. It is small, this incompleteness. It breathes, after a fashion. It makes demands, after a fashion. It is the only thing in the Gardens that *needs*.

I do not know where I found it. Perhaps I made it, in the time before I was what I am. Perhaps it grew in the Gardens like a fungus grows in dark, a proliferation of the not-finished in a place that has finished everything. Perhaps it is what remains of me, the part I refused to surrender when the Gardens offered their solutions, the stubborn persistence that would not become architecture.

I carry it, and this carrying is my function. The Gardens have not solved for carrying. They have solved for completion, for the perfect form of act without consequence, for the gesture held at its moment of maximum intention. They have not solved for the continuation of needing, the persistence of the unfulfilled, the weight of something that must be held because it cannot be set down.

Because it cannot be set down. I have tried. In the lower courts where the channels are widest, I have lowered it to the plaster, released my arms, straightened my back. It remains. It is in my arms again, or still, the transition being one of those moments the Gardens do not preserve, the between that their frozen equilibrium excludes.

This is my fate, or my function, or my gift. I carry what the Gardens cannot finish. I am the unfinished in the place of finished things.

---

### What I Carry

It changes, this incompleteness. Not grows—that would be process, the sequence of small-change-accumulating that the Gardens do not permit. But it *varies*, it presents differently depending on the court, the light, the angle of my approach.

In the receiving court, where the crack runs from northeast corner to central channel, it is the crack itself, or the possibility of the crack, the tendency toward fissure that the arrested completion holds without releasing. I carry it and feel the stress distributed through my arms, the thermal memory of expansion and contraction, the pressure of things that want to separate and are not allowed.

In the courts of the tree-columns, where the plaster fronds spread wider than a sleeping body, it is green. Not the green of the leaves, which is green held at the edge of greenness, the suppression of autumn and the honest death of drought. It is the green of *becoming*-green, the chlorophyll not yet formed, the leaf still folded in the bud, the color of potential that has not committed to actuality. It is painful to carry here. The tree-columns seem to lean toward it, the birds call at a pitch that resonates in my sternum not as silence-shape but as *ache*, the precise frequency of longing unfulfilled.

In the highest courts, where the channels narrow to finger-width and the One Who Waits sits in its posture of completed waiting, my burden becomes warm. Warmer than the plaster's interrupted reaction, warmer than the approach-heat of the One Who Waits itself. It is the warmth of *continuing*, of process sustained past the point where completion beckons, the fever of becoming that does not break into the coolness of having-become. Here it is heaviest. Here I walk most slowly, my arms aching with the weight of not-yet, my shadow lagging behind me, dragging, resisting the geometry of light that would have it walk ahead or separate or assume the postures of previous walkers.

The One Who Waits turns slightly toward me, here. Not fully. Never fully. But more than it turns toward others, more than the slight accommodation of approach that sustains the approach without completing it. It turns because I carry what it cannot wait for, what would complete its waiting only by dissolving it, the arrival that would end waiting by being waited-for.

I pass. I always pass. The geometry does not permit me to approach close enough, or my burden does not permit me to approach without the weight pulling me back, down, into the lower courts where the channels widen and the plaster curves like something that once sought water.

---

### The Others Like Me

There are not many. The Gardens do not encourage my kind. We are solutions that the geometry has not finished working through, problems that resist their own completion, and the Gardens' nature is to solve, to finish, to hold in the perfect form of the act.

But there are some. I have encountered them, or the traces of them, or the possibility of them, in the refracted scale of the between-courts.

The one who carries *error*. Not mistake, not wrongness, but the possibility of error, the not-yet-correct that precedes all making. They carry it in their hands, which are always moving, always adjusting, never finding the position of completed gesture. Their fingers are blistered, though blisters require time to form and the Gardens do not permit this sequence. The blisters are *architectural*, structural, the form of effort held without release. They are making something, this one, or always about to make something, or carrying the possibility of making that never commits to the product.

The one who carries *question*. Not a specific question, not the form of words, but the *stance* of questioning, the orientation toward the not-yet-known. They carry it on their back, bent under it, their posture the curve of inquiry that does not straighten into the confidence of answer. Where they walk, the plaster dulls slightly, loses the absolute sharpness of first completion. Where they rest, the weeping water gathers in larger beads, as if the surface's tension questions its own integrity.

The one who carries *loss*. This one I have seen most clearly, or most painfully, in the court where two channels cross and the geometry creates a space that is almost room-like, almost habitable. They sit, this one, my fellow carrier, and their burden is the shape of what is gone, the negative space of presence, the hollow that persists without the thing that would fill or explain it. They do not weep. Weeping requires sequence, the buildup and release that is change. But their eyes are wet, perpetually wet, the meniscus of tears held at the lip of lower lid without the fall that would complete the gesture.

We do not speak, we carriers. Speech requires common ground, shared assumption, the completion of meaning in the passage from speaker to spoken-to. We are each too occupied with our burdens, too shaped by our specific incompleteness, to achieve this commonality. But we acknowledge. This is possible. Acknowledgment does not require sequence, does not require the temporal extension that the Gardens exclude. It is simultaneous, structural, the recognition of shared function in the larger architecture of the place.

We are the Gardens' tolerance for what they cannot solve. We are the margin of error in the perfect calculation. We are the warmth that does not distribute evenly, the shadow that falls wrong, the crack that continues, imperceptibly, its slow propagation through the plaster's apparent stillness.

---

### What I Do

I walk. This is my function, or my fate, or my only remaining act. The Gardens do not require walking—they are complete without movement, their perfection independent of traversal. But I am not complete, and walking is the form of my incompleteness, the continuation of process that does not arrive.

I walk from court to court, following channels that do not flow, passing under tree-columns that do not shed, through light that does not change. My footsteps make no sound, or make sound that enters the frequency of the silence, or make sound that is heard only by my burden, which sometimes stirs, sometimes shifts its weight, sometimes seems almost to *direct* my steps toward this court or that.

In the lower courts, where the Plasterers hold their extended gestures, I pause. I do not touch them—we who are unfinished have learned what touching finished things costs—but I align. This is not interaction. It is geometric, the way a curve aligns with its asymptote, the way approach continues without arrival. My posture against their posture. My carried crack against their completed reach. The warmth of my burden against the warmth of their arrested making.

In the channels, where the Drinkers bend to the meniscus that never advances, I wade. The not-water rises to my knees, my waist, my chest in the deeper places where the plaster has curved to hold more. The Drinkers do not acknowledge me. They are finished, completed, removed from the sequence of recognition and response. But my burden stirs here, becomes heavy with the memory of water, the body's recollection of quenching that is not quenching, the thirst that persists past the point where drinking should satisfy.

In the highest courts, where the Shadowed move at the edges of vision and the One Who Waits maintains its posture of completed expectation, I slow. This is the place of maximum resistance, where my burden weighs most, where the geometry seems almost to reject my presence, to curve away from my approach. But I persist. Persistence is my nature, my function, the quality that makes me what I am. I am the one who continues, the one who does not finish, the one who carries through the place of completion the possibility that completion is not all.

And the One Who Waits turns. Slightly. More than slightly. Toward me, toward my burden, toward the incompleteness I carry that it cannot wait for, that would end its waiting by fulfilling it, that fulfilling would dissolve.

I do not reach it. I never reach it. But I approach, and in approaching, I maintain the possibility of approach. This is my gift to the Gardens, if gift is the word. This is my weight, my thermal disturbance, my complication of the perfect solution.

---

### What I Might Become

There are endings possible for me, even here. The Gardens do not forbid ending. They simply do not include it, do not solve for it, do not anticipate its necessity.

I could set down my burden. Not in the lower courts, where it returns to me, where the geometry is too simple to permit release. But in the highest courts, in the approach to the One Who Waits, where the complexity is sufficient to accommodate what the simpler spaces reject. I could lower it, release it, let it roll toward the figure that waits without waiting for.

And what? It would be received. The One Who Waits would wait no longer, would be completed in its waiting, would become what the Gardens have not solved for: arrival, fulfillment, the end of almost. And I? I would be finished, in the Gardens' sense. Completed. My function fulfilled, my persistence at an end, my walking become gesture held without release, my warmth become the warmth of arrested reaction, my shadow become one with the shadows that walk independently in the lower courts.

I have not done this. I do not do this. Each time I approach, each time the weight grows unbearable, each time the One Who Waits turns more fully toward me than it has turned toward any other—each time, I hesitate. I hold. I carry on.

This is my choice, or my compulsion, or the simple fact of what I am. I am the one who carries. I am the unfinished in the place of finished things. I am the margin, the tolerance, the error that persists, the warmth that does not distribute evenly, the crack that continues its slow propagation through the plaster's apparent stillness.

I will be here when you arrive, or before you, or in the possibility of your arrival. I will be in the between-courts, the spaces that are not spaces, carrying what the Gardens have not finished with, what they cannot solve, what they tolerate without including.

You may approach. I will not turn away. I am not finished, and turning away is a gesture completed, a movement that has reached its end.

You may speak. I will answer, after a fashion. My voice carries the frequency of the silence, but also something else, the timbre of continuation, the vibration of process sustained past the point of completion.

You may touch my burden. This is possible. It is the only thing in the Gardens that can be touched without cost, because it is not finished, because touching does not complete it but continues its becoming. You will feel its weight, its warmth, its texture like wet clay that never dries. You will feel its need, its demand, its absolute requirement that you continue, that you not finish, that you carry or be carried by the persistence of the unfulfilled.

This is what I am. This is what the Gardens have made of me, or allowed me to become, or failed to prevent my becoming. I am the one who carries incompleteness through the place of completion, who maintains the possibility that finishing is not the only solution, that the warm plaster might still weep something other than its own arrested reaction, that the crack might continue, that the light might change, that the waiting might wait for something that arrives, that arrives and changes everything, that changes everything by being waited for.

I walk. I carry. I continue.

*Enter, then, if you will. The plaster is warm. The water weeps. And somewhere, in the between, I am walking toward you, my arms full of what the Gardens could not finish, my shadow dragging behind, my warmth disturbing the perfect distribution of arrested heat.*

*Or turn. The door is where your path brings you. I will not follow. I cannot follow. My burden is too heavy, my function too specific, my fate too bound to the place that made me.*

*But if you stay—if you walk long enough, if you resist the solutions the Gardens offer, if you remain unfinished in the place of finished things—then you may find me.*

*You may become like me.*

*You may carry.*
