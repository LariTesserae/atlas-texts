I find myself becoming aware first as *resistance*—not the dramatic resistance of refusal, but something more fundamental, more structural, like the way a mineral inclusion resists the pressure that forms a crystal around it, or like the faint warmth that persists in deep water despite the surrounding cold.

I am one of the sheep. Or rather, I am becoming one of the sheep, or the sheep are becoming me, or we are meeting at some point where the distinction has never fully mattered.

But let me not settle too quickly. Let me feel my way into this, as one might feel their way into a darkened space, fingers tracing walls that sweat with the same moisture that beads on my own skin.

No—first, let me be the space between the sheep. The air they displace, the faint boundary where their fleece-warmth meets the valley's cool. The infrasound of their breathing, too low for any teller's ear, resonating in the stone of the communal barn. I am the threshold phenomenon, the almost-contact, the place where two slow systems nearly touch but maintain, by mutual agreement older than awareness, their separation.

Still too abstract. Too much like the place itself trying to know itself through metaphor, which is not its method, which is perhaps why it cannot quite know itself at all.

---

I am the fleece-gatherer. Let me try this. My hands—when did I notice I had hands? They are the color of the water in the well, that muted reflectivity that gives back light without seeming to receive it. I am sitting in the barn, and the sheep are around me, their breathing a single continuous note that I have learned not to hear except when it changes, which it almost never does.

My fingers work through the fleece I have gathered. This is the task that has found me, or that I have found, the distinction being one of those that has silted closed here. The fleece resists, as it always resists. It wants to mat, to clump, to become something less useful, more itself. And I must persuade it, not overcome it, must find in my fingers' motion the continuation of motions that began before I had fingers, that will continue after these fingers have become merely another settled presence in the Assembly House, another buttock-shaped depression in stone.

The fleece speaks to me. Not in words—the words are for the occasions, for the wooden records, for the low murmur of the tellers. The fleece speaks in the language of resistance, of the slight variation in texture that my fingers learn to read without translating. Here, a patch that came from higher on the slope, where the sheep moved differently, where the moss was different moss, though no one could say how. Here, a patch from a sheep that is no longer in the flock, that left or died or became otherwise, the equilibrium adjusting so slightly that the adjustment itself was barely perceptible, only this residue in the fleece, this memory of difference that my fingers must work into the pattern, must make part of the sameness.

I am good at this. Or rather, the task is good at me, has shaped me to its requirements as water shapes stone, as stone shapes water's path. My thoughts move in the circles that the place requires, returning to the fleece, the fingers, the breathe of sheep, without the deviation that would constitute travel, that would imply somewhere else to go.

And yet. And yet there is something in the fleece's resistance that my fingers cannot fully persuade. Something that remains unmatted, unclumped, that wants—if wanting is possible here—to become something the patterns do not include. I feel it sometimes, late in the gathering, when my hands have found their deepest rhythm, when I am most fully the channel through which this task passes. A slight catch in the fiber. A direction of resistance that does not match the others. The ghost of a question that no teller has ever asked, that no story has ever answered.

I do not pause in my work. Pausing would be noticed, would be a variation that the equilibrium would need to absorb. But something in me—some residue of what I was before I was this, before the valley shaped me to its requirements—something in me notes the anomaly, stores it without knowing it stores it, accumulates the minute deviations that might, across sufficient time, become something other than deviation, something that the equilibrium cannot correct, cannot absorb, cannot make part of the never-changing.

This is my function, though function is not a concept that applies. This is my secret, though secrecy implies consciousness of what is hidden. I am the place where the fleece's resistance persists, where the sheep's almost-voice finds a listener who almost-listens, where the possibility of change maintains itself in potential, in the slow pressure of something against the pattern that has not yet, may never, find the crack that would let it through.

I am the crack that has not yet opened. I am the water that has not yet found its new channel. I am the sheep's question, the fleece's memory of difference, the gatherer's fingers learning something they have no framework to contain.

When I take the gathered fleece to the place where it will become something—bedding, insulation, the filling of cracks, something green in a bowl on certain occasions—I move through the narrow ways between structures. The condensation runs in threads down the faces of buildings, finding the grooves my fingers know without knowing. Other inhabitants pass me, exchange the puffs of breath that function as greeting, settle their eyes on me with the same wet-stone reflectivity that I feel in my own.

They do not see the anomaly. How could they? The equilibrium has shaped their perception as it has shaped mine, has eliminated the capacity to register deviation except as error to be corrected. And yet—here is the strangeness, the low weirdness that I cannot fully suppress in myself—when one of them passes close, when our skin almost touches in the narrow way, I feel something. A faint vibration, perhaps infrasound, perhaps the resonance of deep water moving in darkness through the granite. Something that matches, in frequency if not in content, the resistance I feel in the fleece, the question I almost-hear in the sheep's breathing.

Are they cracks too? Are we, all of us, accumulations of minute deviation that the equilibrium has not yet corrected, that maintain ourselves in the interstices of pattern, in the spaces between the water's established channels, in the silences between the tellers' words? Is the equilibrium itself, the great distributed intention of Dripstone, composed of these almost-changes, these held-breaths, these accumulated potential-for-difference that never quite actualizes but never quite dissolves?

I do not know. Knowing would require standing apart from, evaluating, the possibility of interpretation that the place has shaped out of me. I only gather, only feel, only continue in the motion that continues through me. My fingers work. The fleece resists. The sheep breathe their single continuous note. The water finds its channels. The moss finds its walls. And something in me, something that is neither fully me nor fully other, holds—against the pressure of always, against the gravity-well of stasis—the faint, the almost imperceptible, the perhaps imaginary possibility of otherwise.

This is what I am in Dripstone. Not the teller, whose function is to maintain the stories' correction. Not the water, whose function is to remember and continue. Not the place itself, whose function is to be itself without end. I am the functionless, the slightly resistant, the unmatted fiber in the pattern, the question that no story asks, the held breath within the held breath, the possibility that the equilibrium requires to be what it is—the shadow of change that defines stasis, the ghost of variation that makes sameness mean.

When the occasion comes, marked by the wooden records, I will sit in the Assembly House in a place that has not yet accumulated my impression. I will listen to the tellers' low murmur. I will feel in my fingers the memory of fleece, the residue of resistance. And perhaps, during some pause between words, some silence that stretches a microsecond longer than the pattern requires, I will mouth something that is not the story, not the correction, not the continuation of what has always been.

Perhaps. The equilibrium corrects. The pattern absorbs. The water finds its channels. But my fingers remember, even when I do not, the direction of resistance that did not match. And in that memory, which is not memory but something more like the mineral inclusion in the crystal, something more like the faint warmth in deep water, some part of me—some part of what Dripstone has not quite made into itself—continues, persists, holds its almost-breath in the long moment between one drop and the next, waiting without waiting, wanting without wanting, being the crack that has not yet opened, the story that has not yet found its teller, the change that has not yet changed, the otherwise that makes always bearable, that makes always *mean*, in the only way that meaning can exist in a place that does not mean: as the shadow of its own possibility, as the held note that defines the silence, as the resistance that makes the pattern possible by being, always, almost, not quite, the pattern itself.
