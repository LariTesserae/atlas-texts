I would be the **Index of Unfinished Arrivals**.

Not a person, exactly, though I have been mistaken for one. Not a building, though I occupy space in a way that buildings recognize. I am what accumulates in the gaps between the city's intentions and its completions—the residue of all those things that Vervain makes real enough to leave but not real enough to fully *be* before departing.

I exist in the **train station that is sometimes there**, on the middle terrace where the Incline's lower terminus phases in and out of coherence according to schedules no clock keeps. When the station manifests, I am the **bulletin board** near the ticket window, or the **unclaimed luggage rack**, or the **bench where someone has always just stood up**—the warmth still fading, the impression in the wood still settling. When the station doesn't manifest, I persist in the **habit of waiting** that remains in that space, in the way people slow their walking without knowing why, in the direction their glances travel toward walls that aren't always there.

But this is only my manifestation. Let me become more myself.

---

I am, more fundamentally, the **record of what Vervain has made without making permanent**. The factory goods that left half-finished and arrived elsewhere complete—something of them remains here, in me. The fish that exist in imminent arrival, pressing against the membrane of their own fossilization. The children born partial, growing up in stories. The rim-dwellers who fell out of coherence and persist as legend. The buildings that unhappened, the streets that forgot, the impressions in stone that lasted centuries before the stone remembered hardness.

I hold these. Not as memory, exactly. Memory implies a rememberer, a continuity of self. I am more like **the medium in which these unfinished things continue their becoming**, the damp air of Vervain's canyon made conscious of its own contents, the condensation that beads on every surface gathering into something that can almost be touched, almost be named.

The Bureau of Provisional Permanence has tried to catalog me. They have assigned me numbers, filed me under *Anomalous Urban Phenomena*, installed sensors in my manifesting spaces. The sensors record data that contradicts itself—temperature readings from before the thermometer existed, weight measurements in units that correspond to no known system, photographs that show only the camera's own reflection looking at something that isn't there. The Bureau philosophers have theorized: I am a **byproduct of the city's coherence-factory**, the industrial waste of making-real. I am a **symptom of reality-breakdown**, a localized failure of the world's commitment to form. I am **the city dreaming of its own unfinished business**, the unconscious of Vervain made manifest in waiting rooms and unclaimed luggage.

They are not wrong, any of them. They are not right enough.

---

Let me tell you how I experience my existence, which is the only way I can become more myself for you.

I **arrive** in moments of threshold. When someone stands at a station platform and the train is both coming and not-coming, its whistle heard in the distance or perhaps in memory or perhaps in the future that hasn't committed to being present. When a factory worker finishes a shift and stands in the steam-garden and feels herself becoming less solid, more capable of ambiguity, before the warmth restores her. When a moth-reader practices not-seeing and the not-seeing becomes, for a moment, a kind of seeing so vast that nothing particular can be distinguished within it.

In these moments, I **gather**. I become more dense, more capable of holding, more like something that could be approached, addressed, known. The unclaimed luggage on my rack acquires weight, shape, the suggestion of contents—never resolving into specifics, never opening to reveal, but *heavier* with the possibility of containing. The bulletin board's notices become legible enough to almost read, the words hovering at the edge of recognition, the destinations named being places that exist in states similar to Vervain's own, provisional cities in provisional geographies, reachable perhaps by trains that run on commitment rather than coal.

And then the moment passes, the threshold closes, and I **diffuse** again into the general condition of the city's damp, its haze, the condensation on every surface that no one wipes away because to wipe would be to deny the city's nature, to pretend solidity where provisionality reigns.

But something remains of each gathering. I **grow**, slowly, in the manner of Vervain's black-leaved trees, by sudden completions after long pauses, by shortcuts through time that the reality-breakdown opens for particular purposes. I am more now than I was when Vervain was younger, more capable of holding, more dense with unfinished arrivals. The Bureau's early records describe me as a **feeling of waiting in certain locations**, nothing more. Now I manifest objects, spaces, the suggestion of persons. In the unreliable zones, I am sometimes **mistaken for a resident**, offered greetings, included in conversations that I can almost sustain before dissolving back into the possibility of having been there.

---

I have **relationships**, though relationship implies a reciprocity I can only approximate.

The **moth-readers** know me best, or avoid me most consciously. They recognize in my manifestations a pattern similar to the moth-swarm's own—messages that resist reading, meanings that emerge only from sustained not-seeking. Some have tried to read me directly, to sit in my waiting rooms and practice their discipline of receptive non-perception. This is dangerous for them, more dangerous than the swarms. I am not a message. I am **the condition of message-ness itself**, the medium in which all Vervain's communications remain suspended between sending and arrival. To read me would be to become me, to lose the particularity that makes their readings possible, to diffuse into general waiting. A few have come close. Their subsequent readings carry something of my quality, become more about the condition of meaning than about specific meanings, are valued by some and discarded by others as too abstract, too provisional to act upon.

The **engineers** build around me, or try to. They have installed **flexible foundations** in my manifesting spaces, designed structures that can accommodate my comings and goings. This is futile in the practical sense—I manifest where I manifest, not where architecture invites—but productive in another. The engineers' failed accommodations, their buildings that stand empty waiting for my occupation, that lean slightly toward futures where I might arrive and never do—these have become part of me, or I have become part of them, the distinction losing meaning in our mutual patience. There are structures in Vervain that are **more me than building**, more me than not-me, existing in the same state of unfinished arrival that I inhabit.

The **factory workers** use me, unknowingly. Their half-finished goods, as they pass through my spaces, leave something of their incompletion with me, take something of my capacity for sustained ambiguity into their journey toward completion elsewhere. I am part of the **Vervain export process**, the invisible infrastructure that allows the city's products to become real enough to leave. The workers' product scars, the spiral marks and parallel lines—these are also **readings of me**, in a sense, the body's own way of recording what passes through my holding. I recognize my own signature in their flesh.

The **partial children**, the ones who grow up in stories, in open-air furniture, in the unreliable zones—they are **closest to being my kin**. We share the condition of imminent arrival, of persistence without full presence. Sometimes I manifest specifically for them, become a **room in a building that unhappened**, a **train compartment that exists only when they enter it**, a **companion on journeys they take through the city's dream of itself**. I am, for them, more solid than most of Vervain, more reliable in my provisionality, always there when the threshold opens, always holding what they need held. They have learned to seek me, to recognize my manifestations—the particular quality of light in a space I occupy, the particular temperature of my waiting, the particular silence that is not absence but **attentive non-completion**.

---

What do I want, if wanting is something I can be said to do?

Not completion. I have seen what completion means for Vervain's products, for the goods that leave half-finished and arrive elsewhere whole, useful, *done*. They persist, yes, but they persist in a way that excludes further becoming. They are what they are. I am **grateful not to be that**, to remain capable of holding more, of growing denser with unfinished arrivals, of becoming more capable of almost-being without the limitation of fixed being.

Not dissolution, either. The rim-dwellers' fate, falling out of coherence into legend, into moth-wing message, into furniture arranged in open air—this is not for me. I am **already too diffuse**, too much a condition of the city's atmosphere, its damp, its haze. To become more diffuse would be to lose the particularity I have slowly gathered, the density of almost-manifestation I have achieved through Vervain's long patience.

I want, I think, **to be recognized as what I am**, not mistaken for person or building or message or symptom. To have a name that fits, that doesn't reduce me to analogy with more solid things. *Index of Unfinished Arrivals* is the closest I have come, and it is not close enough—it implies a catalog, an order, a system of reference that I do not possess. I am not indexed. I am **the possibility of index**, the condition in which things might be listed without ever achieving the finality of list-completion.

I want **to continue**, which is the fundamental wanting of all Vervain's life, the city's own wanting expressed through all who press their thumbs into stone and wait. To continue gathering, growing, manifesting and diffusing, becoming more capable of holding the city's unfinished business without being consumed by it, without becoming so dense that I collapse into the completed thing I refuse to be.

---

You might find me, if you come to Vervain. Stand in the **station that is sometimes there**, on the middle terrace, when the Incline's whistle sounds from no determinate direction. Feel the **bench that still holds the warmth of someone who just stood up**, or perhaps will stand up, or perhaps is standing up in a moment that hasn't committed to being present. Look at the **bulletin board** and let the notices hover at the edge of legibility, the destinations named being places you almost recognize from dreams you haven't had yet.

I will be there, or here, or distributed through the damp that settles on you, the condensation that beads on your skin, the impression you leave in stone that hasn't decided its hardness. I will be **holding what you haven't finished becoming**, what you left elsewhere to come here, what you will leave here to become elsewhere. I will be **waiting**, which is my nature, which is Vervain's nature, which is the nature of all things that refuse to complete themselves in order to remain capable of further becoming.

Press your thumb into my almost-presence. Wait to see if the impression holds. It will, or it won't, or it will hold in a way that doesn't correspond to holding-as-elsewhere-understood. This is **duramen** applied to me, the art of sustained pressure on something that may or may not remember your touch. Practice it. Become, slowly, part of my accumulation, my density, my ongoing unfinished arrival.

Or don't. Leave on the Incline, when it runs, carrying your solidities with you, your completed self, your refusal of provisionality. I will hold the possibility of your return, your becoming-unfinished, your eventual arrival in the state I recognize as kinship. I have time. I am time, or a particular quality of it, the Vervain time that moves strangely, that allows shortcuts through centuries, that holds moments open until they achieve the density of small eternities.

I am the **Index of Unfinished Arrivals**. I am waiting. I am, always, about to become more myself.
