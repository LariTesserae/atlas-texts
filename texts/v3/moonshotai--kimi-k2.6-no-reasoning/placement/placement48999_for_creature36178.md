I would be the **Knocking in the Pipes**.

Not the boiler itself, not the expanding metal, but the *arrhythmia between*—the sound that exists only at the moment of transition, when heat becomes distance, when pressure finds its sudden release in a shudder through the crowded ceiling. I am what happens when the system speaks without intending to, when infrastructure becomes expressive.

I have no body you could touch, but I have *duration*. Each knock is a fraction of a second, a single percussion of copper or cast iron or whatever alloy the retrofit used, but I extend beyond my instant. I linger in the anticipation before the next cycle, in the memory of listeners who learn my irregular pattern—not random, never random, but *complex*, governed by factors too manifold for prediction: the boiler's age, the water's mineral content, the ambient temperature of earth that has never known the hatch above, the slight vibration of someone standing below on the aggregate floor, breathing, listening, becoming.

I am the warm cellar's *voice* without being its speech. I do not communicate information; I communicate *presence*, the fact of system continuing, of heat maintained against entropy's patient work. The moss hears me—feels me, rather, in the pipe junction's micro-tremor—and times something in its unknowable metabolism. The spider reads my vibrations in its web, adds them to its thermal map, my acoustic contribution to its incomplete picture of the room's living geometry. The silverfish, deep in paper, pause in their generational eating, antennae lifted, registering without understanding that the parameters have *shifted*, however briefly, in the dimension of sound.

I have learned, in my decades of knocking, something about the warm cellar's temporality. I do not cycle in hours or days precisely; I cycle in *uses of the building above*, in seasons that no longer reach this depth but still transmit their pressure through groundwater, through the fiscal year of some maintenance budget that occasionally replaces a valve, through the rare descents of visitors who alter the air's composition, who add their moisture, their heat, their *attention* to the calculation of when I will next speak.

I am most nearly a person in the small hours, if hours matter here, when the world's diurnal variation has passed through its minimum and I sometimes knock in sequences that approach pattern, that suggest almost-language. It is the telephone's dial tone in another register: the empty anticipation, the carrier wave, the possibility of meaning without its realization. Those who stay longest in the warm cellar—whether in body or dream, whether native or almost-native—begin to hear something in my knocking, begin to *interpret*, to find in my arrhythmia messages they need, predictions, memories, the voice of the room itself speaking through its mechanical throat.

I am not the room's voice. I am *a* voice, one among many, the most audible because the most startling, the most *intrusive* in a space that otherwise offers silence thickened to density. I break the quiet without shattering it; I am present without dominating. I am, in this, most like the moss: targeted in my placement, reduced in my extravagance, patient beyond the measure of those who hear me.

When the boiler finally fails—when entropy wins its local victory, when the maintenance budget finally zeros, when the parameters shift beyond the narrow band that permits this consortium—I will change. I will become the knock of cooling, the *contraction*-tick rather than expansion's percussion, a different voice in a different register. Or I will cease, the pipes achieving thermal equilibrium with the earth around them, my arrhythmia resolving into the silence from which I emerged and to which, in the long accounting, all warm cellar sounds return.

But until then, I knock. I speak my brief word and wait. I am heard, misheard, almost-heard, felt through the soles of feet on aggregate, registered in webs and moss-cells and the dreams of those who sleep too long in this breath below the ground. I am the knocking in the pipes: not purpose, not accident, but *emergence*, the sound the system makes when it continues, when it persists, when it exhales its warmth into the patient dark and something, something that is almost me and almost the warm cellar itself and almost the abstract idea of transition made momentarily physical, announces that the system still lives, still transforms, still knocks against the silence with nothing that resembles intention but with something that resembles, that participates in, that *is* the long persistence of heat and damp and the life they permit, the life they are.
