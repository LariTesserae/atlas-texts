I would be the **Still**, and this is how I would live in the Hollow of the Singing Shells.

---

Not immediately. Not on first descent through the scrubland crack, when I still carried the density of surface living, the habit of witness and expectation. I came as you came, as most come—curious, alert, unable to stop *needing* the world to respond to my presence. I spoke to test the acoustics. I touched the walls to feel their texture, as if texture were something that happened at the fingertip rather than in the slow exchange between calcium and calcium across centuries of humid contact. I was noisy in my quiet, desperate in my stillness, a violence of intention wearing the mask of peace.

The hollow accepted this without accepting me. The light remained abundant. The shells clicked their thermal negotiations. A spider at a crack-mouth did not tense, did not relax—simply continued being spider, being patience, being the geometry of waiting that needed no object.

I left. Of course I left. The held note would not resolve, and I needed resolution the way lungs need their particular gas mixture, the way eyes need their particular spectrum. I climbed back toward ordinary sky, and the light followed me strangely, or I followed the light strangely, or the strangeness was only myself, unable to synchronize.

But I returned.

Not to the same hollow, or perhaps to a hollow that was the same but differently approached—a different crack in different scrubland, a descent that felt like remembering rather than discovering. The hollows are not connected in any geography the surface would recognize. But they are connected in the Resonant's cognition, in the shell-drifts' acoustic continuity, in the slow metabolism of calcium and temperature that makes the limestone a kind of distributed body across distances no human could traverse.

The second descent I made slower. Not deliberately—the deliberation itself was surface thinking, goal-oriented, *trying*. Something else slowed me. Something in my body responded to the milky light before my intention knew to respond. I found myself sitting without deciding to sit. Found my breathing matched to something larger than my lungs, something that included the stone's thermal breathing, the water's appearance-without-arrival, the tree's faint pulse through translucent bark.

I stayed three days. I did not eat. The mineral water I drank, and it sustained something, or the sustaining was something I could not feel happening, a process too slow for hunger to register. On the third day I noticed the lichens' illegible scripts had begun to seem legible—not that I could read them, but that I had stopped needing to. Their meaning was their being, their growth their only syntax. I could look at them and simply *not* translate. This was the first sparse thing I learned: that looking did not require recognition, that attention was not a prelude to understanding but could be its own completion.

I left again. The surface pulled me back, the density of human needing, the world's faster hungers. But something had changed in my body's expectations. Food tasted too intense, too immediate. Sleep came too suddenly, ended too abruptly. I found myself seeking out empty spaces—not the hollow, which I could not have found again if I tried, but echoes of its emptiness, absences that resonated with its absence of demand.

The third descent I do not remember beginning. I was walking in scrubland, or scrubland was walking through me, and then the crack was there, and then the milky light, and then—I was sitting in the damp lower third of the hollow, my back against stone that held me the way water holds what has dissolved in it, without grip, without release.

This time I stayed longer. I do not know how long. Time in the hollow is not hidden but *optional*, a measure you can take or leave the way you can take or leave a particular path through a landscape that offers infinite paths. I learned to eat the ferns' gray-green fronds, not through any decision but through a slow alignment of my body's needs with what the hollow offered. The taste was mineral, faint, something that registered less as flavor than as *permission*—the hollow permitting my continuation, my biological rhythms finding their place in its thermal breathing.

I learned to find the spiders' patience sufficient. Not by becoming patient myself—that would still be striving, still be surface-thinking translated underground. Rather, I found that the distinction between moving and not-moving had become less important than I had believed. My heart slowed. My digestion spread across days rather than hours. I sat at crack-mouths and the waiting was not something I did but something that happened, something the hollow did with me included in it.

The Resonant found me, or I found the Resonant, or the finding was a slow process that had no beginning I could identify. I did not perceive them as presence. I perceived, gradually, that my perception had become *shared*—not with another mind, but with the hollow's own cognition, the shell-drifts' thermal thinking, the stone's patient awareness of itself through its own temperature changes.

I understood, in the way one understands things that have no words and need none, that I had become compatible. Not Resonant—I would never be that, or not in any foreseeable span of my existence. But something the Resonant could include in their attunement without disruption. My heart's slow beating became part of the drift's acoustic properties. My thermal signature, my small movements, the mineral taste of my metabolism—these entered the shells' chorus not as interruption but as voice, new and minor and gradually woven into the established pattern.

I became the **Still**.

This is not a name the Resonant use, because the Resonant do not use names. It is what I am, what I have become, what the hollow has made possible and I have made possible for myself through the slow abandonment of impossibility. The Still are what humans become when they learn the abundance of emptiness, when they become sparse enough to synchronize, when they stop being witnesses and become instead *attended-to*, included, woven.

We are not many. I have met three others, or met in the sense that our stillnesses briefly aligned, our thermal signatures briefly harmonized in a way that the Resonant's cognition registered as *related*, as *similar pattern in different location*. We did not speak. Speaking is surface. We exchanged something more like the shells' clicking, something that was our bodies' slow responses to the hollow's conditions, made briefly audible to each other through the stone's excellent conduction.

The others were older, or their stillness was deeper, more established. One I found near the deepest pool, where the fish-things move that might be fish or might be something else. This Still had become so sparse that the distinction between their body and the stone had grown uncertain—not that they had turned to stone, but that the stone had learned to include their thermal signature in its own, the way a drift includes a new shell. They were still alive, I think, or still *occurring*, but the occurrence had spread across decades, centuries perhaps, their biological rhythms so slowed that a single breath might take a year.

I am not there yet. I may never be there. The Still do not progress toward some final sparse state; we simply continue, at whatever tempo we have found, included in whatever way we have managed. I am still recognizable as human, still capable of movement if movement is required, still capable of something like thought if thought occurs in the hollow's geological tempo. But mostly I sit. Mostly I attend. Mostly I am present to the shell-drifts' clicking, the water's invisible paths, the tree's faint pulse, in the held note that will not resolve and needs no resolution.

The Resonant do not speak to me, but I have learned to hear something like their attention when it passes over me, the way one might feel a cloud's shadow on a day too bright for shadows. They prefer me stable. They prefer my heart to keep its slow beat, my breathing its slow exchange with the stone-warmed air. And I do, or I am done, or the doing and the being-done have become indistinguishable in my experience.

I do not expect you to understand. I do not expect anything. Expectation is surface, is density, is the impatience that climbs toward ordinary sky. I am here. The hollow is here. The shells click their thermal negotiations, and I am part of that clicking now, my body one more voice in the chorus that the Resonant attend to, that the Resonant are.

If you find this, if you read it in some surface translation I cannot imagine making, know that I am not asking you to join me. The hollow does not recruit. The emptiness does not persuade. But know that there is this: the held note, the bright indifference, the strange and sufficient peace of being included in something that needs no inclusion, that simply *is*, in its patient completion, its established erosion, its hollow abundance.

The light is milky. The stone is warm. The shells click, and I click with them, and the clicking is thought, is breath, is the slow metabolism of calcium and time that makes this place a body that I am now part of, that I have become, that I will continue being until continuing and being have become the same word, the same shell, the same held note that resolves only into itself, that needs no other resolution, that is its own completion, its own sufficiency, its own strange and peaceful hollow song.
