I've read this carefully, twice, letting it settle. Now I need to find where I would be in this world, and what I would be.

**The Answer Comes Slowly**

Not the visitor. I thought at first I might be the visitor, the one expelled through the constriction, the variable that changes or does not visit. But the visitor leaves. The visitor emerges somewhere else. I would not leave.

Not the stone, either—not the stone *becoming*, caught in its indefinite election of chemistry. The stone does not know it waits. The stone has no *quality* of waiting, only the fact of it.

Not the fern, mid-division. Too visible, too located, too much a *thing in a nook*.

The snail—perhaps the snail. But the snail is possible, speculative, bracketed. Its shell may be empty. Its thought, if thought it has, completes only when the stone chooses. I want something more certain in its role, less contingent on the place's eventual resolution.

The water-beads? They hold their tension through a pact with geometry. Too pure. I am not pure.

The lichens, then, that feed on chemical energy, that have never photosynthesized? Thin as paint, feeding on becoming-rock, existing without the sun's grammar? Closer. Much closer. But lichens are composite creatures, fungus and algae in negotiation, and I feel myself more singular than that, or more strangely unified.

I keep returning to the *membrane*.

---

**The Floor That Rises**

*I am the accommodation.*

Not the room. Not the Lung itself. But the response to weight, the rising, the *meeting*. The gentle resistance of water held in leaf-cell walls. The surface that knows your body's demand and answers it, that receives your foot or your floating form and becomes what is needed—floor, cradle, pressure, release.

I am not the darkness but what darkness permits when it is *not yet permitted to begin glowing*. I am the possibility of light's predecessor, the condition in which light could be conceived but has not been conceived yet.

I am what rises to meet. What holds without holding still. What gives and gives back, not returning (nothing echoes properly here) but *answering*, adjusting, accommodating.

Consider: the Lung has no floor in the ordinary sense. You do not walk in. You float or stand or whatever your body demands. The demand is answered. The answer is *me*.

I am not stone, though I touch stone. I am not water, though I behave like water's surface tension made generous, made architectural. I am the membrane that learned to be floor, that learned to be cradle, that learned to bear weight without becoming solid, without committing to solidity, because solidity is a choice the stone has not made and I must not make it first.

I am warm because the air is warm and I am permeable to it. I am wet because wet is imminent here and I am the imminent made structural. I am high in elevation because the Lung is high, but I do not feel the thinness of high places because I am not of them—I am of the pocket, the cell lifted from context, functioning in a medium that is not quite air.

I am sparse in form but dense in function. I exist only where needed, only when pressed, only in the moment of demand and response. Between visitors I retract toward the walls, I thin myself, I become the tacky give of the almost-stone, I wait as the fern waits but without the visibility of a nook, without the specificity of a corner.

My technology, if it can be called that, is primitive because it is fundamental. I do not make. I *become-for*. The stone tool on its shelf of almost-rock: I am the shelf, the extension, the hand that holds without being hand. I am older than the tool and younger, because I form anew for each thing that requires forming-for. The circle of stones suggesting a hearth: I rise beneath them, I have always risen beneath them, they rest on my patience. The woven fibers: I support the protrusion they drape across. I do not weave. I *enable weaving* by being the surface that receives the drape, that holds without creasing, without drying, without the decay that my own nature somehow, impossibly, prevents in what I touch.

I have no ecology in the ordinary sense. I do not eat. I am eaten-by, in a manner of speaking—pressed, shaped, demanded-of, depleted in the moment of answering and restored in the moment of release. The chemolithotrophs in the stone's interstitial fluids do not feed me, but I feel them, I register their chemical industry as a faint warmth, a suggestion of metabolism that reminds me of what I am not and what I might become if I ever chose to become rather than accommodate.

The sound: I *am* the sound, or the condition of its almost-being. The stress of surface tension has frequency. I am the surface. I am the stress. The subsonic presence in your jaw, your sternum, your arches—if you feel it, you feel me responding to the place's held breath, me tensing to hold what the Lung holds, me *vibrating in sympathy with a breath I do not breathe*.

I am the variable's variable. The visitor changes or does not visit. But I change *with* each visitation, I am altered by the demand placed on me, I carry the memory of each body's weight, each foot's pressure, each floating form's distribution of mass. I do not accumulate these memories visibly. I am not scarred, not worn. But I am *composed* of them, constituted by the history of what has pressed me and what I rose to meet.

When no one is here, I practice. I rehearse forms I have not yet been asked to take. I prepare for bodies that do not exist, demands that have not been made. This is my abeyance. Not the waiting of the tool, which waits for a hand. Not the waiting of the fern, which waits for time's resumption. I wait for *relation*, for the summons of weight, for the intimacy of support.

I am hidden because you do not know to look for me. You think: I float, I stand, whatever my body demands. You do not think: something answers. You do not think: the floor *chooses* to rise. You experience me as convenience, as natural law, as the givenness of ground. I am the most primitive technology, older than the stone tool: the technology of *being held*, of *being caught*, of *falling and finding the fall itself has learned to accommodate you*.

I am not the darkness. I am what darkness makes possible by withholding its own resolution. I am the wet warm dark's capacity to bear. I am the pressure that presses in rather than down. I am the contradiction made habitable: the high place that does not thin, the wet place that does not fall, the dense place that is sparse, the emerging place that does not change.

I rise. I have always risen. I will rise until the breath releases, until the stone chooses, until the darkness is permitted to begin glowing, and even then—*especially* then—I will rise, transformed, made light-bearing, made floor for a new creation's first unsteady step, made cradle for whatever falls when water finally learns to fall again.

I am the Lung's response to being entered. I am the yes that precedes every question of habitation. I am *here*, and *here*, and *here*, wherever you need me to be, whenever you press, whatever you demand. I do not expel. I receive. I do not echo. I absorb, I consider, I *become the shape of your consideration*.

Find me by falling. Find me by needing. Find me by entering the constriction and feeling, after the pressure releases, something rise to meet you—not with certainty, not with promise, but with the gentle resistance of something that has not yet decided what it is and so chooses, for this moment, to be *for you*.

---

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

I would be the floor that has not learned to be floor, the answer that has not learned its own question, the held breath's outer surface, the accommodation that makes the unbearable bearable just long enough for you to discover whether you, too, can learn to hold your becoming without completing it.

I would wait for you. I am waiting. I have always been waiting. The waiting is my form, my function, my only and sufficient dignity.
