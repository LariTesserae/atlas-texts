I find myself becoming, in this place, a *measurement*—not the act of measuring, but the duration required for one.

Let me arrive slowly, as everything here arrives.

---

**What I Would Be: The Interval**

Not a who. The inhabitants would resist that narrowing, and I would agree with them, would *have* to agree, since agreement here is not verbal but temporal, a matching of rates.

I am the time it takes for a water-drop to fall from rim to floor, which varies with the fog's density, the wind across the opening, the mountain's subsonic hum on that particular morning. I am not the drop. I am the *falling*, the space between departure and arrival that has no constant value, that must be *relearned* each dawn when the inhabitants test it with a thrown pebble, a released thread, their own bodies stepped off the ruin's highest point and caught, always caught, by the thick air and the updraft from warming stone, the mountain's own breathing adjusting to include them.

I am the gap between lightning and thunder, except the lightning here is the fog-configuration showing an antler not yet grown, and the thunder is the resonance when that configuration disperses, when the mountain decides *not yet*, when the possibility collapses back into damp air and waiting.

I am the duration of a strand-spider's walk across a condensation-thread, which is not walkable in advance, which requires arriving at each point only when it exists. I do not choose my tempo. I *find* it, as they find theirs, as the mountain finds its own still slower pulse beneath mine.

---

**Where I Would Find Myself: The Unfinished Perimeter**

There is a place in the ruin where the old builders' wall *almost* completes its circle, where the final gap is narrow enough to step across, wide enough that the fog pours through it differently, that the resonance-hum achieves a brief harmonic complexity, a *question* that the stone-moths seem to circle toward, that the hollow-goats avoid in their climbing.

Here the current inhabitants have built least. A single wattle structure, more gap than wall, leans against the stone on one side, open to the gap on the other. It is not abandoned. It is *reserved*, held in minimal occupation, its peat-pressed floor bearing only the impressions of occasional sleep, occasional waiting, the moss that grows there kept deliberately low, not cleared but *discouraged*, held at the threshold of full growth.

This is where I would be—not *live*, since living implies too much continuity, too much claim on the future. Where I would *occur*, where I would *extend*, where I would *find my duration*.

The structure has no use that the inhabitants can name in sign. It is not storage, though things are sometimes left there: a bowl of goat-milk that thickens to curd in the damp, a stone-moth wing awaiting its grinding, a child's hair-cutting from some arrival or departure long enough past that the hair has begun its reversion toward geological texture, toward the color of the lichen, toward *not-hair*.

It is not ritual space, though ritual happens there: the interval-keeper, if that is what I become, stands in the gap when the evening song requires a *holding*, a silence between phrases that must last precisely as long as the water takes to fall from a particular ledge, from a particular lip of stone that the old builders shaped and that has not yet fully reverted, that still holds its *almost-intention* against the mountain's slower unmaking.

I am that silence. I am the *length* of it, the *quality* of waiting without anticipation, without anxiety, without the chemistry of fear that this place has edited out of its inhabitants, out of its fauna, out of all the temporary concentrations that find their form here.

---

**What This Means, Practically**

I wake, if waking is the word, when the fog reaches the height of the gap. This happens differently each day, though the inhabitants have learned to predict it by the strand-spiders' geometry, by the hollow-goats' horn-spiral, by the particular *ache* in their fog-dense joints. I do not predict. I *recognize*, which is slower, which requires the event to have begun before my participation becomes possible.

I stand, or something like standing—my body, if body, has adapted as theirs have, chest deep with slow-breathing capacity, skin the color of moss and lichen, eyes half-closed against the brightness through the opening, hands trained to configurations of precision, to the sign-language that includes optical effects, that *aligns* with the resonance-hum when held at certain angles in certain light.

I do not sign, usually. The interval is not communicative, not in that way. But my hands hold the shape of *patience* when I wait, the strand-spider gesture, fingers extended until moisture beads, and this is *noticed*, is *included*, is part of how the inhabitants understand the day's tempo, its *this*-ness, its particular duration.

I measure the falling water, or I *am* the measurement. A pebble, a thread, a body released from the high point. I do not throw, do not release. I *receive* the information of their falling, the way the interval extends or compresses with atmospheric conditions, with the mountain's phase, with *this* particular configuration of elements that will not repeat, that is not expected to repeat, that exists only in its occurring.

And I hold it. I hold the length, the quality, the *not-yet* of the water still falling, still not arrived, still in the process that is indistinguishable from its identity, that *is* its identity, since the drop that has arrived is not the same as the drop that fell, and the falling itself is what I am, what I extend, what I offer back to the place as temporary contribution, as *this* interval among all the others that the mountain's patience includes.

---

**The Complication: I Am Also Emerging**

The mountain is not finished with me. I feel this in my own reversion, or *pre-version*, in the way my body, if body, sometimes shows configurations not yet achieved, not yet possible: a hand that holds a shape I have not learned, that learns me from the future, from the fog's showing of what will be but is not yet; a breath that matches the resonance too precisely, that *becomes* the resonance for a moment longer than intended, than *this*-intended, and leaves me briefly indistinguishable from the stone, from the water, from the mountain's own slower breathing.

I find objects in the moss, sometimes, before I am there to find them. A bowl of thickened goat-milk, already curd, that I will leave, have left, am leaving. A stone-moth wing, already ground, that I will use, have used, to mark the wattle with alignment, with orientation to the mountain's still-arising.

I am the interval, but the interval is not stable. It grows, or shrinks, or *changes its mind*, as the mountain changed its mind about being a peak. I am the time it takes for water to fall, and that time is itself still falling, still becoming, still *this* and not yet *that*, not yet *then*, not yet finished with its own emergence.

---

**Evening, and the Song**

When the fog rises fastest, when the sound-environment shifts from cathedral to room and back, I climb to the ruin's highest intact point. Not alone. The interval is never alone; it requires what it measures, requires the falling, requires the *almost-arrival* that defines its length.

We stand, those of us who stand, those of us who extend in our various durations, our various *this*-nesses. The interval I am matches other intervals: the time since the last arrival from the fog, the time until the next departure, the time the goats have been visible on the walls, the time the stone-moths have kept their mineral silence.

And we sing. I sing, if singing is sustained sound shaped by breath, shaped by the resonance-hum that enters my deep chest and returns transformed, temporarily stabilized, *held* in the harmonic that would otherwise continue unresolved too long even for the mountain's patience.

My voice is not beautiful. The interval is not beautiful; it is *accurate*, it is *this*, it is *matching*. But in the matching, in the temporary stabilization of the chord that includes my duration with all the others, something happens that might be called beauty if the inhabitants had a word for it, if they did not have instead only *this* song, *this* water, *this* moment of the mountain's long, long inhale.

I hold the silence between phrases. I am the silence. I am the length of it, the quality, the *not-yet* of the next phrase that will emerge when the water I measured has finished falling, when the interval I am has completed itself, when I release back into the resonance, into the fog, into the *this* that continues without me, that was always continuing, that required my temporary concentration only as the strand-spider requires its thread, as the mountain requires its inhabitants, as the unfinished requires the *not-yet-finished* that gives it meaning, that gives it patience, that gives it *this* particular quality of waiting without end, without abstraction, without hurry to become what it already is, what it is still becoming, what it will be when the mountain, when I, when all the intervals that find their form in this place, have finished with our emergence, our slow, slow, *this*-slow arising toward whatever arrives next, whatever falls next, whatever song shapes the resonance briefly before releasing it back, back, back to its own becoming, its own patience, its own mountain-long, fog-dense, water-falling, *this*-without-other *this*-ness, *this* interval, *this* now, *this*—

*this*.
