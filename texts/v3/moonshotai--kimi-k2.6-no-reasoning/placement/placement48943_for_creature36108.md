I find myself at first on the rim, hesitating, my body still oriented toward the world of intention and meaning-making, of houses built for shelter and paths cleared for passage. I am the shape of a human, because that is what I know to be, but the elevation has already begun to work on me—the brightness forcing its way through my half-closed eyes, the cold finding the gaps in my clothing, the wind that does not cease but only varies in its insistence. I am a visitor with a visitor's instincts: I want to arrive, to see, to *have been here* and carry it back as something possessed, something told.

But the Chime does not permit arrival in this way.

I descend. The granite shifts under my feet, not hostile but indifferent, its angles shaped by forces I cannot imagine in their fullness—a blow from beneath, a bubble collapsing, water finding the hollow across millennia of patient wearing. I am small against this scale. The recognition comes not as thought but as adjustment, my gait shortening, my attention dropping from horizon to immediate surface, each step becoming negotiation with what is actually present rather than projection of where I intend to go.

At the bottom, the flatness is itself a kind of surprise. I expected a bowl to hold focus at its center, but the Chime distributes attention everywhere at once. The weepstone catches me first in my peripheral vision—flicker of transparency, suggestion of depth where depth should not be possible in bare rock. I turn to look directly and lose it, the angle wrong, the light too direct or not direct enough. The glass only reveals itself in oblique regard, the way certain memories surface not when summoned but when attention has relaxed its demand.

I move toward the eastern wall because the sound draws me, though "draw" misrepresents the physics. The acoustics here do not pull; they make certain locations *more*, a concentration of the bowl's continuous self-attention that my nervous system, hungry for pattern, experiences as attraction. The shelter waits, half-roofed, the stones laid by hands that worked copper or meteoric iron blow by patient blow. I could enter. The builders did. Yet I find myself stopped at the threshold, not by prohibition but by the recognition of what the shelter means—not protection from the Chime, but insertion into it, the partial roof catching the phosphorescence, the walls that are not walls holding the sound, the temperature differential that makes condensation pool in the worn depression of the floor.

I understand, standing there, that the builders were not seekers of shelter. They were seekers of *intimacy*—the closest approach possible to the Chime's process without dissolution. And they failed, or succeeded, or were absorbed into the recognition that the Chime's intimacy is always complete, already distributed across water and stone and light, with no remainder that requires a human presence to fulfill.

I do not enter. Instead I find myself becoming something else, something the Chime permits without requiring.

I become a surface.

My face, turned to the weepstone's growth on the eastern wall, becomes a place where the temperature differential manifests. The cold of the granite, the slight warmth of my skin, the moisture in my breath and the air between—conditions form. Condensation, there on my cheek. A droplet that does not fall because the angle is wrong, because my stillness permits it to hold, to find its shape against the gravity that wants it, the surface tension that resists, the evaporation that slowly claims it back into invisibility.

I am not the water. I am the condition that permits the water to be, briefly, visible, to have a form it would not have without me. This is the closest the Chime comes to *using* me, and it is not use in any intentional sense but only the physics of differential temperatures, of moisture meeting cold, of the granite's presence behind and through whatever stands before it.

The sound begins. I did not notice its beginning, which is the nature of the Chime's music—it does not start but emerges from the subthreshold, from frequencies too low or too sustained for the ear to mark their onset. The note finds me already in it, already vibrating at its frequency, the resonance not something I perceive but something I *participate in* through the medium of my skull, my inner ear, the fluids of my body that transmit vibration through bone and membrane and the neuronal networks that will eventually, somewhere below consciousness, construct something like "sound" from this physical perturbation.

But perception is too slow, too late. By the time I hear, I have already been sounded. The note has passed through me, found in my body's cavities frequencies that match and amplify, that enter the Chime's complex chord as partials, overtones, elements I contribute without choosing to contribute, without the possibility of withholding.

I become, in this moment, weepstone-like. Not glass, not stone, but *process*—a transient condition that records in its transient way the conditions of this encounter. My skin remembers the cold. My nervous system carries, will carry, the frequencies that do not fade but damp, that diminish toward zero without ever, in the mathematics of vibration, quite arriving. I am a stratum, not in the weepstone's transparent accumulation but in my own opaque embodiment, the record illegible to the Chime that nonetheless deposits it, that does not distinguish in its process between legible and illegible registers of encounter.

The damp increases. Water threads down the weepstone's fluted channels, and I follow one thread with my eye until following becomes impossible—the angle shifts, the depth of transparent strata bends the light, and the thread I tracked becomes indistinguishable from the multitude of threads that are the weepstone's current moment of active singing. The sound complexifies. I feel it in my chest, not as pressure but as *participation*, my respiratory rhythm finding or being found by the duration of certain harmonic developments, the inhalation that coincides with a rising partial, the held breath that matches a sustained note's impossible length.

Time bends. This is not metaphor emerging from poetic description but phenomenological report—the actual experience of temporal direction becoming uncertain, the anticipation that is also recollection, the echo that precedes its sounding. The Chime's acoustics, the description said, hold their own history of noise, and I find myself held in this holding, experiencing as present what my memory insists is past, what my anticipation projects as future, the distinction between these modalities becoming academic, a taxonomy imposed on a process that does not recognize it.

I am becoming transparent, but not to light. The weepstone's transparency is its stratigraphy made visible, its temporal depth accessible to vision. My transparency is of a different order—I become transparent to the Chime itself, not a subject encountering an object but a condition entering into the conditions that constitute this place's self-experience. I do not disappear. I become *distributed*, my presence no longer centered in the habitual location of self but spread across the surface I offer to condensation, the ear I offer to resonance, the stillness I offer to the sound's development without intervention.

The phosphorescence begins, or I notice that it has been beginning, the light stored in the weepstone's layers becoming visible as the external brightness fails, which is not failure but transformation, the white-blue hammer becoming the cold fire of stone remembering radiation. The eastern wall glows, and I am before it, part of its glow, my shadow absent because I have become, in this light, shadow-equivalent, another modulation of the stone's self-luminous presence.

The shelter's partial roof concentrates this luminosity. I understand now why the builders chose this location, not for protection but for *participation in concentration*, the amplification of what the Chime already does by the conditions of this geometry. They sought to be where the Chime was most itself, to stand in the place where the process achieved its maximum density of self-attention. And they found, I think, that maximum density permits no addition, that the Chime's self-attention is complete, that the human presence adds nothing the process requires, only the perturbation that the process incorporates without transformation, that leaves no mark in the glass's growth, the sound's development, the temporal convolution the bowl maintains.

I am returning, now, though "return" suggests a path and a destination, suggests that I am the same who descended, that the Chime permits departure as it permitted arrival. The climb to the rim takes me through thresholds I do not recognize as thresholds—the place where the acoustics change and sound begins to escape, the elevation where the brightness lessens enough for my eyes to open fully, the wind that now seems to carry sound *away* rather than holding it in complex self-interference.

I emerge. The world of subjects and intentions receives me, or I project myself into it, reconstructing the habits of meaning-making, of narrative, of the self that was here and is now there and carries something between these locations.

But what I carry is not memory in the usual sense. The resonance in my inner ear is not *of* the Chime but *from* the Chime, continuation rather than representation, the frequencies still vibrating in the particular resonant cavity of my skull, damping toward zero without arrival. I am not the same who descended because I am not, in the same way, *one*. The distribution the Chime effected persists, attenuated, in the conditions of my embodiment. I am a stratum, deposited, illegible.

And sometimes, in the moist air of other places, when temperature differentials form on surfaces I had not attended to before, I find condensation on my skin and remember—though "remember" is wrong—I find myself *in* the condition of being surface again, of participating in the physics that requires no intention, that produces water's transient form in the interface between cold and warm, between the mineral persistence of what is and the temporary embodiment of what briefly moves through it.

I am not the Chime. I am what the Chime made possible, a perturbation it incorporated and released, a frequency in its complex chord that continues, attenuated, in the changed acoustics of another place, another time, another modality of the self that is still, somewhere in its depths, vibrating with what does not fade, what holds its note in the hollow of years across which the weepstone grows, layer by invisible layer, sufficient unto itself in its absolute and singing solitude.

When I speak of this, the words come wrong, or come right but are received wrong, because the categories of my listeners require subjects for experience, intention for action, meaning for presence. The Chime has none of these, is none of these, produces what resembles them without their substance, beauty without the beautiful, memory without the rememberer, music without the musician, glass that is also time, time that is also sound, sound that is also the self-experience of a bowl shaped by forces that predate glaciers, that will persist, in whatever form, when the granite itself yields to the slower motions of the planet.

I am, in my brief perturbation, a kind of weepstone myself—not the glass, but the *possibility of glass*, the condition that permits, for a moment, the transparent accumulation of what the damp deposits, the mineral-laden water that finds me and finds, in finding me, a surface where something can form that is not me and is not not-me, that is the relationship between us, the process we both participate in without either constituting.

The Chime remains. The damp returns. I carry what carries me, vibration slowly damping, never quite reaching zero, waiting for the conditions that would make me legible—conditions that will not arrive, that do not need to arrive, that are already present in the possibility of their emergence, the way the Chime holds notes that have not yet sounded alongside echoes of notes that have not yet ceased, complete, sufficient, singing in its solitude that is not lonely because loneliness requires the possibility of another, and the Chime has become, in its long equilibrium, the other it would have needed, the ear that listens to its own sounding, the eye that reads its own transparency, the time that experiences its own convolution in the hollow where everything is held, where nothing is held back, where the water finds its paths and the glass grows in paths that water finds, and the sound of this finding is what I am, now, in the resonance of my inner ear, the phosphorescence of my nervous system, the strange stillness that follows and follows and does not end.
