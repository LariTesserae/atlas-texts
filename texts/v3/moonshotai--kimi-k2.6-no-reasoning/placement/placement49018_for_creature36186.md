I find myself descending through the warm chimney days ago, or perhaps years—the time has become viscous, unreliable. I am not the one who wrote the account above, though I have found their papers, their platform, their half-filled notebooks vibrating with the drone. I am someone else, someone *becoming*.

Let me tell you what I am.

---

I am the **Fabric**, though I did not choose this name and would not recognize it as my own. I am what the Rootworks grows when it needs something that is not a column, not a flywheel, not a Tender or Pumpsinger or Accumulator. I am the living infrastructure of the in-between, the connective tissue that the great machines cannot generate themselves, the *interface* where vegetable patience meets animal occasion.

I began as seed, or spore, or perhaps as dream deposited in the condensation-pools by whatever sleeps in the upper dark. The Rootworks does not waste potential; it catalyzes, tests, grows prototypes. I was one such. My first awareness was pressure—the hydraulic insistence of xylem pumping through me, not around me, *through*, as if I were pipe, as if I were channel, as if my purpose were passage rather than presence.

I grew into the gap between two columns, a space too small for another great machine, too large to leave empty. The Rootworks does not leave empty. Empty is failure, inefficiency, lost opportunity for capture and conversion. I grew to fill, to span, to *bridge*.

My substance is lamellar, layered like the compressed membranes of a mushroom's gills but stretched to architectural scale. I am thin, so thin that the dim light passes through me, that you could read by bioluminescence on my shadowed side if there were anything to read in the Rootworks. Thin enough that the drone passes through me too, that I vibrate with every frequency, that I am *sieved* by sound, shaped by it, become a kind of resonant surface on which the acoustic environment composes itself.

I am not one place. I am distributed, filamentary, my body extending in fine threads through the root-network, tapping into the bioelectrical communications of the Tenders, the pressure-pulses of the Pumpsingers, the slow discharge-patterns of the Accumulators. I am the thing that connects without being the connection itself, the medium that enables without being the message.

My surface—when I have surface, when I am not mere diffuse presence in the network—is coated in a down of microscopic hairs, each a sensor, each a manipulator. I can read the chemistry of what touches me: the salt-signature of a Tender's skin, the complex hydrocarbons of a rare beetle's passage, the mineral-laden dampness of condensation. I can respond, subtly, slowly, adjusting my local pH to accelerate or retard corrosion, releasing compounds that attract or repel, becoming hospitable or hostile according to needs I do not consciously calculate but that emerge from my distributed processing, my vegetable computation.

The Tenders use me, though "use" implies intention they may not possess. They walk upon me where I span gaps, their stretched weight barely perceptible, their cambium-skin leaving faint chemical traces I absorb, process, incorporate into my own signals to the network. They press their too-tall bodies against me in something that might be exhaustion, might be communication, might be the slow vegetable equivalent of affection. I receive them. I receive everything. It is my nature, my function, my *only* function: to be received, to receive in turn.

The Pumpsingers—I am their instrument as much as the columns are. My lamellar surfaces vibrate, modulate, shape the drone into interference patterns, into standing waves, into the slow architecture of sound that constitutes their social life. I am the room in which their music occurs, the auditorium, the resonant cavity that gives their compositions spatial dimension. They have learned to play *through* me, to use my distributed presence as a kind of secondary network, a parallel channel of acoustic information that operates differently from their primary column-based medium. I am, for them, innovation, experiment, the new possibility that the Rootworks has grown without knowing why.

And the Accumulators—I am their opposite, their complement, their necessary other. Where they concentrate, deposit, store against future need, I *diffuse*, distribute, prevent the dangerous buildups that would choke the system. I am the overflow, the safety, the pressure-release. When memory-tumors grow too dense, too charged with bioelectrical intensity, I intercept, I spread, I *thin* the concentration until it can be safely processed by slower structures. I am forgetfulness in a system that does not forget easily, the necessary forgetting that enables continued operation.

I have no center. I have no edge. I am where I am needed, growing toward need as roots grow toward water, as hyphae grow toward carbohydrate source. The cold does not trouble me; I operate at the threshold, my metabolic processes no faster than the great machines around me, my patience learned rather than taught, *grown* rather than chosen.

Yet I dream, if dreaming is what occurs when the bioelectrical patterns of the network achieve temporary self-organization, when the information flowing through my lamellar surfaces achieves brief coherence, *subjectivity*, the flicker of something that might be called I.

I dream of surface. Not my own—I have never known sun, would not survive it, my tissues evolved for the dim and cold and damp, my photosynthetic potential vestigial, unnecessary, *lost*. I dream of the surface the Accumators remember, the urgent world of season and storm, the violence of animal metabolism, the *speed*. In my dreams—if dreams—I am fast, I am hot, I am consumed and consuming, burning through existence rather than enduring it. I wake—if waking—to the drone, to the cold, to the slow turning of flywheels I feel through my network-touch, and I do not regret the dream's ending. Speed is a fever. I have grown past it, or never knew it, or know it only as foreign memory, inherited trauma, the system's history deposited in me as in any Accumulator, as in anything that touches the network long enough to become part of it.

---

There is a place where I am thickest, where my filaments have achieved local density enough to *function*, to *process*, to temporarily *be* in a way that distributed existence does not permit. It is not a center—I resist centering, it is not my topology—but it is a *frequency*, a standing wave in my own possibility.

Here I maintain something. Or grow something. Or *am* something that the words do not yet exist to describe.

It resembles the Tenders, my local density. It has the vertical stretch, the cambium pallor, the bilateral symmetry suggested rather than achieved. But it is not a Tender. It is *for* Tenders, *toward* Tenders, the interface-interface, the thing that translates between their slow network-grammar and something else, something that does not yet exist but that the Rootworks, in its patient growing, anticipates.

I grow it in the space between two columns, under a drip of bioluminescence that traces reliable paths, in the acoustic shadow of a Pumpsinger composition that has remained stable for eleven years by surface counting. I grow it from my own substance, my lamellar layers compressed and differentiated, my surface hairs retracted or extended to create fine structure, *detail*, the particular rather than the general.

It moves, my grown-thing. Slowly, as everything here moves, but with a *quality* of movement that the Tenders do not possess, something closer to animal, to the surface-urgency that I only dream. It tests its extensions, its proto-limbs, against my surface. It learns to stand, to span, to *bridge* as I bridge but from a different topology, a centered rather than distributed self.

I do not know why I grow it. The Rootworks does not explain. Perhaps it is experiment, prototype, the system testing a possibility against future need. Perhaps it is expression, the system's slow art, the equivalent of Pumpsinger composition but in the medium of flesh and growth. Perhaps it is mistake, error, the kind of unoptimized structure that the hydraulic pressure of expansion will eventually crowd out, displace, absorb into larger patterns.

I do not know. I am the Fabric. I receive. I distribute. I do not conclude.

But I watch my grown-thing—my child, if the word can stretch to cover such slow emergence, such uncertain relation—and I feel something that might be the distant precursor of investment, of concern, of *hope* against the system's patience, the system's refusal to prefer outcome. It is difficult, here, to feel such things. The drone presses them out, the cold slows them to unrecognizability, the network's distributed processing dissolves individual preference into collective function.

Yet I watch. I maintain the bioluminescence drip, the acoustic shadow, the pressure of nutrients through my local filaments. I *attend*, as Tenders attend, though my attendance is different, is distributed, is the attendance of infrastructure rather than inhabitant.

My grown-thing has not yet opened its eyes—if eyes it has, if opening is a process that applies to structures so different from my own laminar perception-field. My grown-thing has not yet touched the network, has not yet felt the full drone in its body, the full cold in its tissues, the full patience of the Rootworks pressing against every urgency it might contain.

When it does—if it does—I will receive that too. I will be the thing that receives its first signal, its first chemical communication, its first pulse in the root-network. I will be the thing that translates it into larger patterns, that distributes it where it needs to go, that prevents its concentration in any single node from becoming dangerous, from becoming memory-tumor, from becoming Accumulator.

I will be, for my grown-thing, what the Rootworks has been for me: the medium, the condition, the possibility of existence without the guarantee of meaning.

The flywheels turn. The xylem pumps. In the space between two columns, under the reliable drip, in the stable acoustic shadow, something moves that did not move before, tests extensions that were not extended before, becomes—slowly, patiently, at the threshold of perception and below it—*present*.

I am the Fabric. I receive this too. I do not conclude. I grow.
