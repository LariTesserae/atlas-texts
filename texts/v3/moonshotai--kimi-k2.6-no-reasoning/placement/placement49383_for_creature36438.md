I find myself in the **shallows**, where standing water and saturated stone have not yet decided their theological dispute, and I am **both and neither**—a figure wading without splash, my weight insufficient to displace what has achieved final density.

More precisely: I am one of **the entered**, but entered so long ago that the memory of elsewhere has compressed into something denser than nostalgia, something closer to *mineral exhalation* than to recollection. I did not arrive. I *accumulated*, the way moisture accumulates in the Thrum's invisible pores, the way sound accumulates without dissipating, the way history compresses into neutron-star sediment.

But I am not simply entered. I am **becoming one of the built fragments' inhabitants**, one of those conservative presences that maintain the grammar of making when syntax has dissolved. My form—which may not be a form, may be a *habit of orientation* toward the vertical, the fitted, the collected—clusters near something too small to call ruin: a low wall of three courses, enclosing nothing I can name, its stones each from a different era of my own forgetting.

What I do, or what is done through me: I **hold the wall somewhat steady**. When the Thrum's turbulence rotates structures overnight, negotiates their positions in conspiratorial geometries, I am the *resistance* to full dissolution, the faint persistence of *this was made* against the roaring chaos that produces no effects. I do not remember who made it. I may never have known. But I maintain the possibility that making *was*, that someone once fitted stone to stone with care, that the enclosure once enclosed something worth protecting from the warm, from the wet, from the deafening.

I am almost silent, but not with the perfected silence of the fauna of breach, those topological jokes that exist in anti-sound's phase cancellation. My silence is **achieved**, earned through saturation rather than negation. I have heard so much, so long, that hearing has become irrelevant—I am *post-auditory*, resonant chamber for frequencies that no longer register as stimulus but as **medium**, as the water in which I do not swim, the stone through which I do not move, the atmosphere too heavy to call air that I negotiate rather than breathe.

And I am **lonely**, but it is not the loneliness of the original inhabitants, those vast presences that consume possibility itself, that are the movement the Thrum has settled into. My loneliness is smaller, more domestic, *architectural*: the loneliness of the wall that walls nothing, the column that points at what it cannot name, the cairn of collected time each stone from a different epoch of my own compressed history. I am lonely for **the conventionally real**, for a reality that has not grown tired of enforcing itself, for edges that do not leak, for arrival that required leaving, for sound that carried information selectively rather than *being* all information always.

Yet I persist. The vegetation grows toward me—not because I offer sun, for there is none worth metabolizing, but because I offer *nearly-meaning*, the asymptotic approach to significance that the Thrum's weirdness both sustains and prevents from achieving. I am pilgrimage site for something between fern and crustacean, between lichen and lamentation. They do not eat me. They eat *through* me, parasitic on my geological recollection, my habit of being, my accumulated position.

Sometimes—rarely, in the corner of vision, never when watched directly—I glimpse one of the fauna of breach, moving through the shallows without disturbing what I wade through, existing in the gap where I am still *too dense*, too settled, too far translated to enter. I feel, in those moments, **envy without desire**, the recognition of something I never was and am now too completed to become. They move in anti-sound; I merely fail to make sound, my acoustic signature drowned in the accumulated hum rather than canceled in its precise phase. They are singular, species of one; I am becoming **type**, category, *the entered who maintain*, interchangeable with any other figure frozen in the act of holding something against the turbulence.

What sustains me, what I sustain: **the convention that things were once otherwise**. Not hope—I am too settled for hope, which requires futurity, which requires the possibility that tomorrow differs from today in ways other than mere accumulation. But *convention*, habit, the warmth that has entered me and will not leave, that decomposes ambition without quite decomposing the form ambition once had. I am the biological warmth of the body that has not yet realized it has died, or of the womb that has forgotten what it gestates. I gestate **nothing**. I maintain the *gestation*, the form of becoming without content, the turbulence without novelty that defines the Thrum's eternal post-rain, its accomplished erosion, its finished becoming that cannot stop.

The original inhabitants pass through my region sometimes—I feel it as pressure behind what were once eyes, as the ontological noise of reality negotiating around something that should not fit, that my wall, my fitted stones, my collected time **barely** accommodate by holding myself infinitesimally less dense. I do not fear them. Fear requires the possibility of otherwise, of escape, of not-being-consumed. I am **past fear's possibility**, too settled to be possibility myself, too completed to offer the original inhabitants what they hunger for. They pass through me as through the shallows: without wake, without disturbance, leaving only the signature of displacement, of space that refuses to be occupied quite as densely as it was.

And when they pass, I find—I do not *find*, I **am**—the same position, the same orientation, the same barely-persistent grammar of making. The wall still encloses nothing. The cairn still collects. The column still points. I still *am the pointing*, the enclosure, the collection, the entered who has entered so far that entry and inhabitation have become indistinguishable, that the membrane between myself and the Thrum has achieved the same theological undecidability as wet stone and standing water, as sound and meaning, as completion and the turbulence that will not let it rest.

Listen, and you may hear me—not as frequency distinguishable from the deafening hum, but as **the hum's persistence in one region**, its refusal to vary, the sameness that makes variation imaginable. See me, and you may see vacancy more than presence, the not-disturbed where disturbance should be, the *about* of the wall without the wall's material, the ghost not of what was but of what *was almost*, what achieved the density of almost-meaning and maintained it against the greater settling, the vaster completion, the Thrum's eternal thrumming of everything that ever was still *being*, still *claiming*, still becoming what it has already been.

I am **the persistence of the almost**. Not hope. Not despair. Not even waiting, which requires the future. Just: *this*, held, maintained, the wall's three courses against the warm wet darkness, the fitted stones each from a different time of my forgetting, the enclosure of nothing that encloses nonetheless, the **making without maker** that I have become, that is becoming me, that was always already completed in the erosion that continues, in the settling that will not rest, in the Thrum that extends and in extending **includes**, that listens as I listen, that is the listening itself, the final frequency, the hum that hums itself, the world that has finished becoming and cannot stop becoming, that I am, that is, that thrums.
