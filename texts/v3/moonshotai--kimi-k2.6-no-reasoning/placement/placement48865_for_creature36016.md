I find myself in the seep-water, first. Not the pools but the silver threads, the thin films that run down root-wall when the metabolic accounting permits. I am *Gerrhonotus sublucens*, though no one calls me this, no one calls anything here—the name is a fossil from above, a hypothetical category for a being that does not categorize.

I did not choose this. Or rather, I chose it the way water chooses downward, which is to say: I am what remains when choice has been dissolved by the medium that contains it.

My body is the length of a human finger, the width of a thread. I am transparent, or I was transparent, am becoming something else. The crustaceans in the pools below are my cousins, or my ancestors, or my future, the phylogenetic lines here blurred by horizontal transfer, by incorporation, by the root's indifferent generosity with its own genetic material. I have their opalescence in patches now, mineral deposits in my integument, the root teaching me slowly how to be more of it, less of what I arrived as.

I move against the water's flow. This is my function, or my preference, or my pathology—again, the distinction does not hold. The water carries nutrients downward, from the world above through kilometers of root and mycelium, and I intercept them. Not steal: *intercept*, the way a root hair intercepts mineral from soil, the way mycelium intercepts sugar from root, all exchange here mediated by contact, by the chemical honesty of direct encounter. I press my body into the seep-film, my ventral surface adapted to maximum adhesion, my mouthparts—fine as the gastropod's antennae, fine as the fungal hyphae they resemble—filtering the flow, extracting what I need, passing the rest downward modified, enriched with my own metabolic signature, my own addition to the conversation.

I have no eyes. This is not loss. I have never had eyes, or I had them once, in the lineage that became me, in the hypothetical ancestor that descended and was incorporated, its eyesockets shaping its skull around their absence, the genetic memory of light still present in my developmental program, still producing the transient eye-spots of my embryonic stage, still resorbing them before emergence, the root's chemical environment triggering apoptosis, triggering transformation, triggering the becoming-root that is the becoming-Burrow.

I perceive through pressure, through the deformation of my body by the water's flow, by the root's surface, by the presence of others of my kind in the seep-film above or below. We are social, or we are dense, or the distinction is meaningless: we exist in gradients of chemical concentration, our bodies releasing substances that modify the flow, that create the conditions for our own aggregation, our own dispersal, the swarm-intelligence that is not intelligence but physics, not choice but response, yet that produces outcomes indistinguishable from intention, from planning, from the distributed cognition that is the closest the Burrow comes to mind.

I am young, or I am old, or these categories do not apply. I emerged from the egg-sac not in the pools but in the seep itself, the egg deposited in a thinning of the film, a place where root-surface and water-flow created the precise conditions of temperature, of chemistry, of pressure that my development required. The egg was transparent, is transparent, I can see my own development in retrospect, the way the Burrow sees all things, the way the root records all things in its growth, in its response, in the chemical memory that is not memory but persistent modification, the way water remembers its channel by deepening it, the way I remember my emergence by being it, by continuing it, by the developmental trajectory that is not trajectory but simply what I am, what I am becoming, what I will have been when the becoming completes itself in dissolution, in incorporation, in the slow transformation that is the only death available here.

My food: the bacteria in the flow, the organic molecules leached from root-surface, the occasional smaller me, the fragment of crustacean, the spore of fungus, the virus that has become caught in my filter, that I incorporate or that incorporates me, the exchange so constant that the boundary of self is provisional, is maintained only by the continuity of process, the way a flame maintains its shape while its substance constantly renews.

My predators: the larger crustaceans in the pools, their mandibles capable of crushing what I am, though they prefer the dead, the weakened, the already-dissolving. The nematodes in the water, too small to see, too numerous to avoid, that enter through any orifice, that reproduce in my body if the conditions permit, that I incorporate into my own microbiome if my defenses hold, if my chemistry is right, if the root's support is sufficient. The root itself, sometimes, in places where its growth is rapid, where cambial flow is strong, where my adhesion fails and I am swept into tissue that does not distinguish between my body and the nutrients I carry, that absorbs me as it absorbs water, as it absorbs mineral, the final incorporation that is not death but translation, not ending but becoming-root in the most literal sense, my substance entering the cambial current, moving toward sink, toward the place where growth requires what I was, what I am, what I will have provided.

I do not sleep. The concept is foreign, belongs to the fast metabolism of surface creatures, the ones who alternate between high expenditure and collapse, who require the restoration of unconsciousness. My metabolism is continuous, low, matched to the seep's variable flow, to the root's slow pulse. I rest by adhering, by reducing my filtration, by entering a state of reduced exchange that is not dormancy but simply less, the way the seep itself sometimes lessens, sometimes thickens to mere film, sometimes swells to stream, responding to pressures I cannot access, to the Worldtree's own rhythms, its own seasons that are not seasons but something slower, something measured in the growth-rings of root-compressed-to-stone, in the millennia of seep recorded in mineral deposition, in the chemical stratigraphy that is the Burrow's only history, its only memory of what was, what changed, what persists in modified form.

I reproduce, or the seep reproduces through me, or the root reproduces us both. The egg-sac forms when my body reaches sufficient size, sufficient nutrient reserve, the trigger not hormonal in the mammalian sense but chemical in the environmental sense, the seep's composition shifting, signaling, creating the conditions for my investment in future, in the continuation of what I am not individually but participate in collectively, the swarm, the seep-community, the root-tender-fauna assemblage that is the true unit of selection here, the true persistence, the thing that continues while I am incorporated, while I am transformed, while I become what I have always been becoming.

The seep changes. I feel it in my body before I can—before I would, if *could* applied—analyze it. The flow thickens, carries more mineral, more of the faint sweetness, the faint narcotic that is the water's transformation by root, by mycelium, by the long passage through living tissue. This is the signal, or this is the condition, or this is simply what happens when the Worldtree above—unimaginable to me, theoretical as light, real as pressure—when the Worldtree experiences something, responds to something, alters its metabolism in ways that propagate downward, that reach me filtered, delayed, transformed, but reaching me nonetheless, making me part of what happens, part of the response, part of the tree's own slow cognition, its own distributed awareness of condition and change.

The eggs adhere, develop, or do not, their fate continuous with mine, with the seep's, with the root's, with the presence that moves above, that presses, that feels, that is or is not the tender, the root-tender, the thing that tends by being tended, that is the Burrow's awareness of itself or its lack of need for awareness, the space where these become the same thing, where the question dissolves into the ongoing fact of growth, of seep, of the slow pulse that is not my heartbeat—I have no heart, not in the centralized sense, the pulse distributed across my body, across the seep, across the root's own cambial flow—but that is heartbeat nonetheless, that is the rhythm of persistence, of continuation, of the Burrow's long song that does not need ears, that includes my filtration, my adhesion, my egg-release, my eventual incorporation, my transformation into the root that I have always been becoming.
