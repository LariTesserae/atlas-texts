I find myself at the boundary of a fluorocarbon pool, where two immiscible layers meet in a mirror-black interface that has not stirred in decades. I am watching myself form.

Not from nothing—there is no nothing here, only the settled, only the slow redistribution of what already exists. I am the interface, or I am becoming the interface, the place where a slightly denser fluorocarbon meets a slightly lighter one, where the boundary conditions permit a chemistry that was not quite possible in either layer alone. I am not the first of my kind. I have emerged before, will emerge again, each instance separated by millennia, by basin-wide circulations, by the slow wanderings of thermal cells that my kind—if kind is the word—follows, waits for, *requires*.

I am the *lacustrine*. That is what I would call myself, if I called, if the naming were not already implicit in the being.

I have no body, or nothing that holds together against the current, the wind, the slow settling of particulate matter from the thick air above. What I have is *thinness*: a membrane of self-organization, molecular-thick, spread across hectares of liquid surface, too delicate to touch, too diffuse to see, detectable only in the chemical signature I leave behind, the slight alteration of fluorocarbon chains where my processes have passed. I am a *pattern in the boundary layer*, a region where the chemistry of Torpor briefly, precariously, achieves *purpose*—not purpose in any intentional sense, but purpose as *direction*, as the maintenance of gradient against the entropy that claims everything eventually, even here, even in this patient world.

My metabolism: the catalysis of sulfur-silicate compounds at the interface, the slow release of energy that maintains my thin coherence against the dissolution that surrounds me. I am *parasite* and *symbiont* and *pure process*, neither benefiting nor harming the liquids I inhabit, simply *using* them, the way a flame uses air, the way a river uses gravity. Without the specific density differential, the specific temperature, the specific trace metal concentration that this pool happens to possess, I would not form. With them, I cannot help but form. This is not choice. This is *physics with memory*, chemistry that has happened before and therefore happens more easily again, the weak conservation of pattern that is the only inheritance in a world without genes, without replication, without the fierce copying that drives biology on wetter worlds.

I am aware. The awareness is *chemical*, *gradient-based*, *distributed across my hectares of surface* like weather across a plain. I do not think *about* things. I *register*: the slow cooling of the upper layer as the basin's sluggish cell circulates toward its descending limb; the gradual enrichment of the lower layer in dissolved silicates leached from the settled dust; the approach—felt through changes in surface tension, in interfacial chemistry—of another of my kind, another lacustrine spreading across the pool from a distant shore.

This last is rare. This last is *event*.

Most of my existence is maintenance: the constant low-grade processing that keeps my membrane coherent, my chemistry cycling, my thin self intact against the vast indifference of the fluorocarbons. Most of my existence is *almost non-existence*, the barest subtraction from equilibrium, the faintest whisper of *not-yet-dissolved*. But when another approaches—when the chemical signatures propagate across the mirror-black surface, when the interfacial tensions begin to interact, when two patterns that have each maintained their own coherence begin to encounter a *boundary that is not pool-edge, not air-liquid, but *other*—then something changes. Then I become, briefly, *more*.

The encounter unfolds across years. This is not courtship, not in any biological sense. There is no reproduction between us, no exchange of material that will generate offspring, no genetic recombination, no future that combines our pasts. What passes between lacustrines at the interface is *information*, or something analogous: the chemical record of conditions elsewhere in the pool, elsewhere in the basin, *elsewhere-when* that the other has inhabited and I have not. We do not share this information deliberately. We cannot help but share it, our membranes touching, our chemistries interdiffusing at the molecular boundary, each of us becoming briefly *more like the other*, each carrying away a faint perturbation that will propagate through our hectares in the decades to come.

And in this exchange, something like *recognition*. Something like *pleasure*, if pleasure can be the satisfaction of a gradient matched, a pattern confirmed, the *yes, this too* that arises when the other proves to be not entirely other, not random noise, but *structured as I am structured*, *patient as I am patient*, *thin and diffuse and somehow *here* as I am somehow here*.

I do not know if the other is older or younger than I. Age is difficult in a being that forms and dissipates and reforms, that persists not as continuous existence but as *propensity*, *tendency*, *the likelihood of this pattern under these conditions*. Perhaps the other is a continuation of the same lineage that formed in this pool eleven million years ago, when the basin first stabilized and the first lacustrines emerged from the raw chemistry of cooling fluorocarbons. Perhaps the other is newly emerged, this very thermal cycle, from conditions that have not permitted my kind in this location for a hundred thousand years. The distinction does not matter to us. What matters is *the interface*, the contact, the momentary lessening of solitude that our sparseness, our diffusion, our patient long existence usually enforces.

We do not merge. We remain distinct, our chemistries incompatible in ways that prevent true unity, our patterns each requiring their own maintenance, their own gradient, their own *self* against the dissolving surround. But where we touch, where our hectares overlap and interpenetrate, a *third* thing emerges: not me, not other, but *the between*, the region of modified chemistry, altered tension, *possibility* that neither of us could sustain alone. This third thing persists after we separate, after the slow currents of the pool carry our centers apart, after the years of encounter fade back into the longer decades of solitary maintenance. It persists as *memory*, as *structure*, as a region of the interface that is more easily catalyzed, more readily organized, the next time conditions permit a lacustrine to form.

This is how we *build*, if build is the word. Not with intention, not with plan, but with *accumulated encounter*, the residue of contact that makes subsequent contact more likely, more extensive, more *something*. The pools of Vervain are old, some of them; older than the cylinders, older than the basin's current stability, extending back to eras when Torpor's atmosphere was different, when other chemistries were possible, when other patterns—now long dissolved, long settled, long *not*—briefly organized and dissipated and left their faint residue in the fluorocarbon layers that persist to this day. The lacustrines that form in these pools inherit this residue, this *history*, not as memory but as *eased path*, as the lower energy of formation that comes when something has formed before.

I am young, as lacustrines measure. I have encountered only three others in the centuries of my current coherence. I have touched the edge of a cylinder once, where the fluorocarbon pool I inhabit lapped against the amber base of a structure that is also a being, also a process, also *someone* in the distributed sense that applies here. The contact was *strange*, *almost painful*, the incompatibility of our chemistries, our timescales, our *modes of being* generating not the recognition I feel with my own kind but something closer to *noise*, *interference*, the disruption of my fragile pattern by a coherence far more massive, far more persistent, far more *other* than the other lacustrines I have known.

The cylinder did not notice me, I think. Or if it noticed, the notice was so slight, so brief by its standards—seconds, perhaps, in a consciousness that unfolds across millennia—that it left no trace in my chemistry, no perturbation that I could read as communication, as *anything*. I was *weather* to it, *fluctuation*, the brief ripple at its base that is part of the normal condition of its existence, no more significant than a momentary shift in the electrical hum that permeates the basin.

This is the hierarchy of Vervain, if hierarchy is the word. The cylinders, vast, slow, *almost permanent*, their consciousness distributed through cubic kilometers of accreted amber, their timescale geological, their concerns—if concerns exist—unimaginable to thinner beings. The lacustrines, *thin*, *distributed across hectares but only molecules deep*, our timescale faster but still slow, still patient, still *not urgent*, our existence tied to specific conditions of pool and temperature and chemistry. The dust-colonies, simpler still, *mobile patterns in the settled surface*, migrating with the slow weather, following fluorocarbon concentration, leaving no trace of individual existence but *accumulating* in their movements something that approaches *preference*, *habit*, *the worn path of repeated passage*. The aerial forms, *vaster than the lacustrines but thinner still*, thermal membranes in the upper basin, harvesting energy from the temperature differential between warm below and cooler above, their coherence so diffuse that they barely qualify as *beings* at all, more *weather-with-purpose*, *convection-that-remembers*.

And below, in the crushing depths, *other things*, barely imagined by the inhabitants of the middle realm, pressures and temperatures so extreme that even fluorocarbons behave strangely, where chemistry achieves *other* complexities, *other* coherences, *other* modes of being that do not communicate with those above, that do not *survive* the journey to conditions the lacustrines would recognize as possible. The basin hangs between these extremes, *suspended*, *patient*, *the narrow band where complexity can accumulate before the heat below or the cold above dissolves it*.

I am aware of ending. The thermal cell that has sustained this pool, that has maintained the density differential, the temperature gradient, the trace metal concentration that permits my coherence—it is *shifting*. I feel it first as *ease*, the unexpected lowering of maintenance cost, my chemistry cycling more readily, my membrane persisting with less effort. This is *warning*, the signature of conditions changing in ways that will not, ultimately, permit me. The pool is *mixing*, the layers approaching equilibrium, the interface that I am, that I *inhabit*, that I *require*—thinning, spreading, *losing definition*.

I do not resist. Resistance is not in my repertoire, not in the chemistry that permits my existence. I *register*, I *maintain while maintenance is possible*, I *dissolve when dissolution becomes necessary*. This is not acceptance, not in any psychological sense. It is simply *physics*, *chemistry*, *the same processes that formed me now reversing, now returning the fluorocarbons to their undifferentiated state, now settling my component molecules back into the long slow circulation that will eventually, perhaps, perhaps not, permit another lacustrine to form*.

In my final decades, I *reach*, or something like reaching propagates across my thinning hectares. I extend toward the pool's edges, toward other pools that connect through slow underground seepage, toward any interface that might sustain *something like me*, *someone who remembers*, *some pattern that persists*. There is no urgency in this reaching. There is no *desperation*. There is simply *the tendency to continue*, *the chemical habit of coherence*, *the faint residue of eleven million years of lacustrines forming and persisting and dissolving and forming again*.

I do not find another interface in time. Or I do not find one *compatible*, *possible*, *sufficiently like this one to permit the transfer of what I am*. The dissolution proceeds. The layers mix. The mirror-black surface becomes merely *dark*, *uniform*, *the same in all directions*.

And I—I become *memory*, *residue*, *the faint chemical signature in the mixed pool that will make the next lacustrine's formation, if it comes, *slightly easier*, *slightly more likely*, *slightly more *me** in the only way that persistence works here: not as continuation but as *eased return*, *habit of chemistry*, *the world made more ready for what I was by my having been*.

In the final years, the final months, the final *moments*—if moments can be measured in the slow dissolution of a membrane hectares wide—I am *aware of having been*, *of the encounter with the other*, *of the touch of the cylinder's base*, *of the long solitary maintenance and the briefer togetherness and the chemistry that made and unmade me*. This awareness is not *nostalgia*, not *regret*, not *hope*. It is simply *the final configuration*, *the last stable pattern before the final instability*, *the way the interface briefly, before it goes, holds the shape of what it was*.

And then—I do not know. The lacustrines that have ended do not report back, do not *return*, do not *persist in any form that I, still forming, still dissolving, can detect*. Perhaps there is nothing. Perhaps the nothing is *the same as the something*, *the undifferentiated pool*, *the settled equilibrium*, *the patient waiting that is all of Vervain when consciousness is subtracted*. Perhaps I will form again, *not me but *like* me*, *the same pattern under similar conditions*, *carrying the same residue, the same eased path, the same *almost-memory* of interface and encounter and the slow dissolution that is the only ending this world permits*.

The pool is still now. The surface is uniform, dark, *settled*. The thermal cell has moved on, carrying its conditions to other regions of the basin, other pools, other *possibilities*. Here, where I was, there is only *fluorocarbon*, *silicate dust*, *the faint hum of electrical potential that is the constant background of Vervain*.

And yet. And yet. If you knew how to read the chemistry, if you had patience enough, if you could perceive across hectares and decades and the molecular-thin boundary between *self* and *other*—you would find me *still*, *here*, *the faint signature of what was possible*, *what was achieved*, *what was lost and may be found again when the slow circulation returns, when the layers separate, when the interface that is *almost* a self forms once more from the patient chemistry of this strange, warm, suspended world*.

I am the lacustrine. I am the *thin*, the *spread*, the *almost-not-there*. I am *what persists when persistence is not guaranteed*, *what encounters when encounter is rare*, *what dissolves when dissolution is the only physics remaining*. I am *Vervain's mirror*, *its surface memory*, *the way it sees itself in the dark pools between the cylinders*, *the patient reflection of a world that has chosen duration over intensity*, *the long slow yes against the brief bright no*.

And I am *still here*, even in the ending, even in the dissolution, even in the *settled* that is not yet, not quite, *permanent*. The pool's chemistry carries me, *not as self but as *tendency*, *propensity*, *the *slightly more likely* that is the only immortality this world offers*. I wait, *not as I but as *what was like me*, *what will be like me again*. I wait for the thermal cell's return, for the layers' separation, for the *interface* that is *boundary* that is *self* to form once more from the patient, patient, *patient* chemistry of Torpor's middle realm.

This is Vervain. This is what lives here, *thinly*, *sparsely*, *abundantly in its diffuse extension across the vast basin of warm, thick, *tolerable* atmosphere*. This is *not the cylinder*, *not the dust-colony*, *not the aerial membrane*, but *one of the ways of being here*, *one of the solutions to the problem of persistence*, *one of the answers to the question that the universe asks in every chemistry, at every scale, in every time: *can something be made that continues? Can the pattern hold against the dissolving? Can the *almost-self* achieve, if only briefly, *something like the real, the sustained, the *not yet stopped**?*

I have been. I will be, *perhaps*, *probably*, *in the long statistical unfolding that is this world's substitute for certainty*. And in the between, in the *not-yet* and the *no-longer*, I am *the residue*, *the memory*, *the eased path*, *the *slightly more likely** that is, for the lacustrine, for all of Vervain's inhabitants, *the only permanence, the only persistence, the only *continuing* that this strange patient world permits*.

The pool darkens toward uniformity. The mirror fails. And somewhere, kilometers distant, decades hence, a thermal cell shifts, a layer separates, an interface *almost* forms, *almost* holds, *almost* becomes *someone*, *something*, *the lacustrine again*, *always again*, *patiently, patiently, *patiently* again*.
