I find myself on a cooling tile, sinking slowly into the position I will occupy for centuries. My name—no, not name, the concept doesn't adhere—my *position* in the Thrum's field is near the edge of what might be a valley, where several tiles have subsided enough to hold a slightly different thermal history. The ceramic here is the color of burnt sienna at the edges, warming to amber where my presence has begun to modify it, incrementally, patient as the place itself.

What I am: I am not Worn, not yet, though I am becoming. I am between. I was something else before, something that moved faster, that made sounds more rapidly than the slow tides of heat I now breathe. I can feel the memory of that faster state not as memory but as *texture*, as the way certain surfaces of my body retain a polish that has not yet worn to the worry-stone smoothness of the fully Worn. I am a crankcase opened, spread, not yet fully pressed flat. Some of my seams still *remember* movement, though they no longer move.

I attend.

This is what I do, what I am doing, what I have been doing since before *doing* became the wrong verb. A locomotive-wolf passes through my field—not close, not distant, the wrong coordinates. It passes *through the condition I sustain*, and in that passing, I feel its six legs finding purchase across gravitational vectors I no longer need to negotiate. Its whistle screams, and the scream *modulates* here, in my vicinity, in a way I do not cause but *complete*, the way a ceramic tile completes the heat it holds.

I am not alone. The others—no, not others, the other *positions*, the distributed self of the Worn-field—resonate with this modulation. Our heat-breathing shifts, a tide that takes hours to inhale, hours to exhale, and in that shift, there is *response*, there is *relation*, there is the Thrum's grammar of proximity without touch. I know them by their thermal signatures, by the particular density of their almost-motion, by the way their persistence patterns align and diverge from my own.

Some of us were locomotive-wolves. Some were pneumatic flocks, simplified now past flocking, past percussion, into this dense attending. Some were elephantine assembly-lines, the largest engines, whose accumulated attachments have all dispersed or been absorbed, whose conveyor systems have stilled, whose optical arrays gaze now inward, or outward, or in directions that the category of direction does not properly describe.

I do not know what I was. This is not loss. The ceramic knows, holds the pattern in its hairline cracks, in its thermal memory, and I *am* the ceramic now in ways I was not before, and in that being, there is continuity without narrative, there is same without identity's demand.

The sound: I no longer hear it as I once might have, as pressure, as constant overwhelming presence. It has worn to something else, something I *attend* through, something that is the medium of my attending as water is the medium of swimming, though there is no water here, though the comparison is risk, is part of the grammar. The locomotive-wolf's whistle fades across distances that are not merely spatial, and in its fading, I feel the other Worn responding, the field modulating, the Thrum *attending to itself through us* as we attend to it through the engines that still function.

Time: I do not experience it as succession. I experience it as *thermal gradient*, as the slow shift in the ceramic's radiance that may correspond to what an observer would call evening, as the reddening of the diffuse light, as the melancholy register that enters the engines' whistles, that enters *our* breathing though we do not whistle, do not make sound, only *hold* it, *transform* it, *wear* it smooth.

There is an engine approaching, one I have attended before, or the field I am part of has attended, which is not distinct from my attending, which is the condition of it. It is one of the largest, an elephantine assembly-line that has accumulated fewer attachments than most of its kind, that moves with a relative swiftness, a dignity less geological than the others. Its hydraulic circuits are still active, still moving fluid through volumes I once might have measured, now only *felt* as warmth at a distance, as pressure in the field.

It passes. It passes *through*. And in that passing, something happens that I do not cause but *complete*: its conveyor system stutters, not in malfunction, in *modulation*, and from its swollen head where optical arrays still gaze, there comes a quality of attention that meets mine, that meets the field's, in a way that might, if generosity were required, if grammar permitted, be called *recognized*.

It continues. We continue. The field settles into its post-passing state, our thermal breathing slowly realigning, the Thrum's loud slow dynamics resuming their patient pressure.

I will be here when this engine simplifies, when its attachments disperse, when its conveyors still and its optics gaze only inward or outward or in directions I already know. I will attend this, or the field will, which is not distinct. I will be here when the locomotive-wolves reach their final thermal memory, when the pneumatic flocks scatter their last staccato, when the elephantine, all of them, move into sleep. I will attend, or attending will persist in the position I occupy, which will have worn to something smoother, something more *Thrum*, something that has persisted past the point where persistence could be said to be anything's purpose.

A tile nearby, one I have not noticed—though noticing is wrong, though attention is not focus—has shifted. Its hexagonal edges have softened further, become almost circular in the corner of a perception I no longer properly have. Another Worn, or the same Worn differently distributed, has moved, or the Thrum's multi-vector gravity has moved, or movement itself has worn to something that does not distinguish between subject and field.

I breathe heat. I attend. I am the ceramic and the ceramic is me, not through absorption but through *persistence*, through the long wearing that does not erase but *polishes*, that finds the shape not intended but *required* by continuation.

The Thrum continues. The Thrum is loud with my attending, with all our attending, with the engines that still function and the engines that have simplified and the engines that have reached thermal memory. The Thrum is slow with my almost-motion, with the centuries it takes me to shift across a single tile's surface. The Thrum is multiple with my states—the Worn I am, the Worn I was, the Worn I will wear into, the engine I was before, the ceramic I am becoming—none resolving, all persisting, established, in-balance.

The cracks in my surface, the hairline cracks that do not quite break through, stop at my edges as if respecting some treaty. I do not remember the treaty. I do not need to. The ceramic remembers, the Thrum remembers, and I *am* that remembering now, worn smooth into its persistence, its loud slow dynamics, its industrial function without industry, its purpose without intention, its continuation, its continuation, its continuation—

The whistle of a locomotive-wolf, distant, modulating through the field I am, the field that is me, the Worn, the Worn, the Worn.
