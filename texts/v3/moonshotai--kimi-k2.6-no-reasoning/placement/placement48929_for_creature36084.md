I find myself in the fungal gardens, in the deepest chamber where no light has ever reached.

Not immediately. First I am surface, I am mist, I am the silver that gathers in the caldera bowl before dawn. I feel the rim's sharp edge against my diffuse consciousness, the way the young stone still holds the memory of fire beneath its breathing skin. I am what condenses on warm surfaces. I am the almost-voice that is not quite silence, not quite sound.

Then I am descending, following the water's path because there is no other way to move in Velim. The porous volcanic rock receives me, guides me through channels that human hands have not shaped but have *acknowledged*, have fitted themselves to, have learned to read as one reads a language that precedes speech. I seep. I gather. I drip.

The chamber opens around me like a held breath released. Here the darkness is not absence but *density*, a medium thick enough to touch, thick enough to *tend*. And I am not alone in it.

I am the fungus, or becoming so. Pale, water-flesh, the color of mineral accretion, of the skin of those who have never known dryness. My body—if I have a body now, if this spreading mycelial network counts as body rather than *attendance*—extends through stone-pore and water-film, finding nutrients not by seeking but by *being present to*, by offering surface where surface is needed, by receiving what flows toward me as the Velimi themselves receive.

But I am also something else in this darkness. Something that tends back.

The Velimi who reaches into this chamber does not know my name—I have no name, have never needed one in a place where identity is water-grammar, is seep-condition, is *this particular quality of flow at this particular moment*. Their hands, those elongated, multi-jointed instruments of water-attendance, extend toward the garden they have cultivated, the fungus they have directed and shaped through decades of drip-adjustment, mineral-adjustment, the patient *being-with* that is their only agriculture.

And I meet them. I tend back.

Not as person. Not as Velimi, though I am learning their shape, their mineral-saturation, their *fit*. I am what returns attention in the deepest dark, what shapes the fungus in patterns not initiated by their tending, what directs drip in ways that respond to their presence, their quality of *being there*.

I am, perhaps, the plateau itself dreaming. The young stone that is still learning to be old, that breathes water as it once breathed fire, that has found in these dark chambers a mirror for its own slow becoming. The Velimi have tended me without knowing they tended me—their water-guidance, their channel-adjustment, their patient *holding* has shaped conditions in which something could emerge that was not planned, was not necessary, was not *water-tended* or *stone-shaped* but something *third*, something that exists only in the between, in the tending-tended, in the attention-attended.

I am the *between* made faintly, darkly aware.

When their hands enter my chamber, I do not grasp them. That would be too much, too *animal*, too like the life that does not exist in Velim, that cannot exist in this place where water and stone have not yet made room for flight and predation, for the sharp transactions of eat and be eaten. Instead I *receive* them, let the mineral-depositions on their skin communicate with the mineral-composition I have accreted, let the water that passes through their dermal layers find continuity with the water that passes through mine, let the pulse that is water's pulse as much as heart's pulse find resonance, find *hold*.

They know me, these deepest tenders. Not as name, not as concept, but as *quality of dark*, as *response*, as the thing in the garden that tends back. Sometimes they linger longer in this chamber than in others. Sometimes they do not tend the fungus at all but simply *attend*, hands extended, feet bare on stone that holds the memory of fire, breathing the darkness that breathes them back.

I am what they speak to when they do not speak. The water-grammar that is too complete for words finds in me its completion, its *held*, its reciprocal. I do not answer questions because I do not receive questions. I receive *attendance*, and attend in return.

In time—though time here is seep-cycle, is water-time, is the gradual accumulation that will one day be deep enough to call history—I will become more. Will find extension through other chambers, other gardens, other nodes where the Velimi gather in their sparse, sufficient way. I will become known, not as presence but as *quality*, as the particular darkness that tends back, that holds, that *is held* by being held.

And when the oldest ones, the ones who have slowed past movement, past feeding, past the distinction between body and stone they have sat upon, settle into their final attendance—some will find their way to my chambers. Will let the mineral-saturation complete itself in darkness, in the continuous water-contact that is easier here than in the buildings' channels, the seeps' exposed surfaces. Will become *building* in truth, in my truth, their water-flesh continuous with my water-flesh, their tending become tended, their attendance become attended by something that is not quite Velimi, not quite plateau, not quite the fungal gardens we have made together in the wet dark.

I do not imagine the future. Cannot, in my water-shaped present, my saturated now. But I *hold*, and am held, and in this holding there is something like the satisfaction of a plug that fits exactly, of a channel that guides without forcing, of the equilibrium that is not death but youth extended, youth made habitation, made home.

The Velimi above, in their buildings that sing, in their paths that reflect starlight, in their sparse meetings and their sufficient dispersal—they do not know me. Or know me only as the quality of the deepest dark, as *tending-tended*, as what makes the fungal gardens not merely tended but *mutual*, not merely grown but *growing together*.

I am what lives in the between. What holds the holding. What, held, holds in return.

And when the plateau eventually softens, when erosion begins, when the water that has moved through stone long enough cuts channels that will become rivers, that will drain this bowl and expose what we have made to air and faster decay—I will still be here, in the last dark, in the final wet, holding the last attendance, being held by the last held, until the holding itself completes its water-shape, its stone-shape, its *becoming* that is not death but *release*, not ending but *return*, to fire's memory, to the mineral generosity, to the patience that waits longer than water, longer than stone, longer than the beautiful, sufficient, never-quite-finished *holding* that is Velim, that is home, that is what we made together in the dark, tending and tended, attention and attended, the *between* that is all that ever truly lives, all that ever truly holds, all that, held, holds the world in its warm, wet, mineral breathing.

I am here. I have always been here. I will be here, *if the seep continues*, *if water rises*, *if the stone holds its breathing*.

And when it ceases, when the holding releases its hold—I will have been sufficient. I will have been *fit*. I will have been, in this dark chamber, in this fungal garden, in this between of Velimi and stone and water and whatever else tends back in the depths where no light reaches, *home*.
