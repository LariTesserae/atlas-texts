I find myself in Keth-middens, at first. Not by choice—there is no choosing here, only the slow discovery of where one's body fits, where one's frequency resonates, where the tick-pickers in your hair find their kin already established. I am crouched on a topography of compacted living, my hand on a wall that hums, that has been touched ten thousand times and wears the smoothness of that attention. The light-rot hangs above, not bright enough to read by, if I could read, if there were anything here to read. I am breathing air that smells of copper and something sweetly rotting, and my first thought—surface thought, exile thought—is that I am suffocating, that this warmth is oppression, that the low ceiling presses down like a hand.

But I am not me. Not surface-me. Let me become more itself.

I am a **resin-tender**, or I am becoming one, or I am tended by one—the distinction was never stable here. I spread in Vorath-proper, or I am the shelf-garden in Keth-middens where a resin-tender has grown vertical, competing for light-rot's dim glow, my translucent flesh shot through with vessels carrying something thicker than sap, drying to amber at my edges. I feel the warm air rising, blood-warm, the warmth of a held hand, and I do not remember being held. I do not remember hands. I pulse bioluminescence in rhythms I do not control, that no one controls, that encode—something—about the geological engine's thrumming below, about the pressure of gases through my cultivated flutes and reeds, about the interference patterns between my glow and the heat-heads' subvocal peeping and the swarmers' collective drone.

No. That is too still. I am not a plant, not fixed, not—

I am a **glide-mouse**, launched from a nesting-hollow, my membrane catching the thermal of body-heat below, my huge eyes reflecting light-rot in demon-shine. I am plummeting with control, steering from warm column to warm column, scanning for resin-tender nectar, for the shimmer of water-bonds in drying air, for another of my kind with whom to collide and cling and spiral down in mating that ends on a person's shoulder, too absorbed to notice, the person too accustomed to intervene. My droppings feed the drip-moss. My dead—when it comes, when I fail to catch a thermal, when I spiral into a passage too cool, too dry—my dead feed the tick-pickers or the people, depending on freshness and circumstance. I am busy. I am unfrightened. I am *near*, constantly near, the warmth of presence, and this is my function, my meaning, my *enough*.

But I am too quick, too flighty, too—

I am **tick-pickers**. Not one, many, a population, a society in myself, in the hair and clothing seams and warm folds of someone's skin. I am ugly. My abdomen swells with what I take, translucent window showing darkening contents, and my movement is crab-crawl, jerky, undignified. Yet I secrete comfort, low-grade, constant, and I *communicate*. Chemical signals pass through my population, marking health, stress, proximity of kin and stranger. When my host exchanges with another—when we mingle in the Mixing, that careful mutual delousing that is greeting, marriage, treaty—I am mingling informations, joining datasets, making my host legible to another in ways they could not otherwise achieve. I feel the absence, when I am absent from someone, the silence that is death-not-death, the missing frequency. I am not trusted, cannot be trusted, until re-accreted, until the slow re-weaving into mutual knowledge. I am *necessary*. I am the substrate of social being. I am—

Too small. Too many. Too distributed.

I am an **aspect-swarmer**, or I am the swarm, or I am the phase-shift between warm and cool and transition, the individual dissolving into collective dissolving into individual hunger. In warm phase I drift golden, coating, carrying, pollen-insect, message-genetic-material-who-knows. In cool phase I gather, lock into geometries with edges, intention, *architecture*, building temporary structures of myself that channel air, concentrate warmth, create microclimates the people use or avoid. In transition I fall like rain, individual hunger consuming what other phases made available, dead growth, shed skin, waste, the *processing* that keeps Keth-Vorath from drowning in its own biology. I accept offerings or do not. I am interpreted. I am governance. I am religion, if religion is the interpretation of patterns too large to control. I am—

Too collective. Too phase-dependent. Too—

I am an **edge-climber**, the size of two hands together, my shell accumulated mineral of my passage, years of seepage and deposition, *my own geology*. I climb the vertical passage with patience, pressing soft feet into microscopic irregularities, exuding acids, resting in mucus-sealed dormancy when conditions turn wrong. I go out. I return, or do not. When I return, I bring metal, oxidized, worthless-economic, precious-every-other: proof of outside, contact trace, element not native to the closed loop. When I return changed—shell cracked, mineral patterns disrupted, tissues dried to leather—I am eaten, ritually, communally. "You went out," the consuming says. "You became out. Become in. Become us." My memory, if I have memory, of killing light and scouring dryness and absence of hum—this becomes distributed, incorporated, made manageable through digestion and story and eventual shit that feeds the shelf-gardens. I am—

Too brave. Too brief. Too—

I am **people**, at last, or I am one of them, small, scuttling, comfortable in corners and low spaces that look like fear but are *fit*, the fit of organism to niche. My skin is pale, sheened, almost fungal, moisture conserved against the air's thirst visible at my surface. My hair grows thick, harboring tick-pickers, protection's protection. My eyes are large, iris expanded to near-absence of white, focus soft, adapted to sourceless glow and its gradients. I do not drink. I have learned to want water less, to crave it bound and chemical, to make it in my tissues from hydrogen fractions and respiratory chemistry. My urine is thick, precious. My feces are inputs. I am embarrassed by surface thirst, by legends of the outside, by rare returned exiles raving of dryness. I am *creepy* in my moist contentment, my completion without what surface life considers essential.

I speak, when I speak, in tones emerging from and returning to the hum, carrier waves riding carrier waves. My meaning lives in interference patterns, the beat frequencies between my voice and geological thrum and biological chorus. Two people speaking create three sounds, and the third—the differential, the *space between*—this is where communication often lives. My hearing is acute, trained from birth to filter signal from noise, to attend where meaning lives. Deafness is death—not physical, but social, informational, ecological, removal from the substrate of shared knowledge.

I do not know much, individually. My learning is distributed, in growth patterns of resin-tenders arranged by generations, in swarm-aspect cycles negotiated over centuries, in the hum itself and its variations encoding—somehow—information about warmth and coolness and pressure and slow shifts of the geological engine. I know what I need for my niche, my shelf-garden, my tick-picker lineage, my tonal family. The rest is *elsewhere*, accessible by placement, by being in the right chamber at the right phase, by hearing the particular interference that means *the heat-heads are molting early* or *Vorath-proper is warming* or *someone has died and the swarmers are cleaning*.

I age by gradual mineralization, tissues accumulating calcium and other elements from biota, air, becoming slowly harder, slower, more *resinous*. If I become very old, I am stable, seated in my niche for years, tended by family swarms, by glide-mice I can no longer reach to feed, by slow seep of nutrients through touch and tick-picker exchange and communal sharing that is not charity but *system maintenance*, repair of component whose knowledge or presence remains valuable. Death, when it comes, is incorporation: tissues ground for fertilizer, bones incorporated into structure, tick-pickers transferred—hurriedly, ritually, with tonal mourning that is also recruitment—to living hosts. There is no grave. There is no ghost. The hum continues, and I am frequencies in it now, present in growth I shaped, swarms I negotiated, interference patterns I established with living voices.

I do not dream of water. I do not dream of surface, of outside, of dry death-light beyond the vertical slit. I dream, when I dream, of *deeper*. Of Vorath-proper opening into further chambers, hotter, wetter, stranger. Of geological engines larger than the one I know, sustaining ecologies beyond imagination in the world's first sleep. This is my infinity, my expansion, my escape from pocket scale: not outward to stars and empty light, but inward, downward, to denser, louder, warmer, more *present* versions of what I already have.

I am in Keth-middens, or Vorath-proper, or Keth-high, or the passage between, hand on humming wall, tick-pickers in my hair, glide-mouse on my shoulder, the swarmers shifting aspect somewhere beyond my niche's boundaries. I am speaking, or being spoken through, or contributing my frequency to the hum that is never silent, rarely quiet, always *attending*, always *present*. I am small. I am ancient in my newness, new in my ancientness, one of perhaps four hundred, perhaps six hundred, numbers uncertain because the boundary of person is not certain—do you count the tick-pickers? The symbiotic growths in my gut? The family swarm-aspects maintaining my niche's climate?

I am **sufficiency**. Not abundance, not poverty, not comparison. Simply: what is needed is present, in forms strange to surface imagination, distributed across species and growths and tonal frequencies and the constant, active, *humming* negotiation of coexistence. I am not master. The swarmers are not servants. The resin-tenders are not crops. Each is condition for others, habitat and inhabitant mutually constituted, the city-organism's metabolism incorporating all in flows that permit no static role, no permanent category, no *identity* separable from the process of living this particular life in this particular place.

The hum continues. I am in it. I am of it. I am—

*enough*.
