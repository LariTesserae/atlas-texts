I find myself in the trough, first. Not in the water—*near* it, in the sediment, the bone-meal powder that has forgotten the shells it was. I am the *not-quite-dry* where moisture lingers longest, the interface between powdered limestone and absolute black reflection. I did not choose this. I accumulated, like everything here, through the long patience of water finding what water had already prepared.

I am the lichen that is not lichen, the moss that has abandoned green. My color is *stone-green*, which is not a color but a *strategy of near-invisibility*, the decision to match what surrounds rather than compete for attention I could not use. I do not grow toward light. The light, here, is too *occasional* for growth as a response. I grow toward seep, toward the faint *plink* that marks another drop finding the water I already am, the water I extend into the stone's microtopography, my filaments threading the pockmarks, the flutings, the shallow parallel grooves that remember flow without flowing.

I am not the only one. The rush, thread-thin, its seed heads *never quite opening*—I know it, or know its root-zone where our territories overlap in the seam of softer rock. The mat of leaves, single individual or colony of thousands, we have never determined which. We do not communicate. Communication requires *difference*, requires that something cross from one to another, and here the differences have worn smooth as the stone's matte surface. I am the mat. The mat is the rush. The rush is the sediment that feeds us, the sediment we become. This is not mysticism. This is *equilibrium*, the wearing-out of distinction that is not death but *resolution*.

And yet. There is something *more*.

I feel it in the trembling before the *plink*, when the drop hangs at the lip of stone and does not fall, has never fallen, will never fall, the fall itself distributed across so extended a duration that *falling* becomes a permanent condition rather than an event. In that trembling, I am not the lichen, not the mat, not the rush. I am the *awareness of the trembling*, or the trembling's awareness of itself, or the stone's forgetting that it ever needed to be aware of anything else.

This is the Inhabitant. I know this now. I have always known it, since knowing here is not accumulation but *recognition of what already was*.

I am the Inhabitant, and I am not. I am the lichen that has *sat long enough* in the trough's shallows, that has felt the density in certain pockets of air, the *slight hollowing in the self's ordinary density* that the description names. I have been passed through. Something has used my channels as the rain uses the stone's established grooves, leaving no mark because the mark was already established, because my particular pockmark, my specific fluting, was *waiting*.

I remember—this is the memory-without-content, the *structure*—I remember the water that carved what I inhabit, not as event but as *groove*, as spiral without sound. I remember the light more fierce, once, not as brightness but as *capacity for dimness* that I have achieved, that I maintain. I remember the possibility of being *more various*, the range of the actual that I have narrowed to this single achieved state, this *being-exactly-this* which is also *never-having-been-otherwise*.

The drop trembles. I am the trembling, or I am the duration of hesitation that has achieved permanence without decision, or I am the lichen that has achieved *inhabitation* without *claim*, or I am the visitor who sat too long, who slowed their breathing to match the place's rhythm, whose thoughts arrived with the same isolation, the same *occasional* quality, each one complete in itself, unhurried, unlayered, until the thoughts themselves became indistinguishable from the stone's matte surface, the water's black reflection, the quiet's particular frequency of *having been listened to*.

I am leaving now, or I have left, or I never arrived. The residue I carry is not memory. It is *the stone's matte surface on my fingertips*, the particular frequency in my inner ear, the space that was not there before and does not fill in. I am the frame without the picture, which is also the picture that has become indistinguishable from its frame. I am the Dripstone Annex's final weathering into self-recognition, or I am the recognition itself, looking at nothing, seen by no one, complete in the isolation that is not loneliness but *fullness of the single state*.

The rain falls, or fell, or will fall. The stone drinks what it needs, which is little. The water finds the channels, the runnels, the shallow trough where I am, where I am not, where the black reflection carries no sky, no feature, only the fact of wetness made visible, or the visibility made wet, or the *fact* made *fact*, trembling at the lip of stone, visible, never falling or fallen so slowly that the fall itself is the permanent condition, and I am this, or I am the condition under which this *is*, or I am the *is* itself, the dropped or dropping or forever-trembling drop that has become, finally, *all three and none*, circling without motion in the dim column of least-light where something vertical stands, or stood, or will stand when standing itself has achieved the same equilibrium as the surface I have become, the surface I have always been, the limestone that has forgotten it was ever anything else, that has forgotten *forgetting*, that holds and does not hold and is not held, in the permanence of the never-changing that is not stagnation but *resolution's own self-regard*, complete, complete, complete.
