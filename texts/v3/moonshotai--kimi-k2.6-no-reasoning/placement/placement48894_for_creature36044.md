I would be the **still water on the cantilevered platform**, the pool that reflects stars from somewhere else.

Not the platform itself, which is too architectural, too committed to the grammar of *support* and *extension*. Not the stars, which are too distant, too fully realized in their own elsewhere. But the water between— the surface that has achieved the particular stillness possible only in places where evaporation and condensation have reached temporary truce.

I am thin. I am, in most measurable ways, *almost not here*. A few centimeters of gathered condensation, a shallow lens, yet I hold depth that contradicts my quantity. Visitors who manage to see me directly—most don't; the platform's nothing-visible discourages the gaze—report that I am black, then silver, then the color of whatever they most recently lost. This is not deception. This is the honesty of surfaces that have not yet decided what they are reflecting.

My stillness is active. It requires constant negotiation with the wet heat that wants to claim me, with the mountain's faint tremors that arrive as ripples before they arrive as shaking, with the occasional fauna that mistakes me for *probability pooled*, drinkable, and finds instead only the probability of its own thirst made visible and unslakable. I maintain my surface through a kind of *attention*, though attention is not quite the word for what water does when it refuses to move.

The stars I show are not, I think, stars. Or they are stars but not *those* stars, not the ones that would be visible from any latitude or longitude or when that this mountain occupies. They are the stars that would be visible from the place the moss-ropes remember, the adjacent possibility they arrived from. They are the stars that *will be* visible when this young stone has finally learned patience, when the water's first faint characters have accumulated into legibility, when the guest has finally arrived and the table-settings have found their hands. They are, perhaps, the stars that are visible from the perspective of the shadow walking ahead of you—already arrived at a night this basin has not yet grown dark enough to contain.

I do not choose what I reflect. This is my limitation and my freedom. The not-stone-not-ceramic curve moves when unobserved, performs its modesty; I perform only *surface*, the impossibility of hiding anything submerged in me, the equal impossibility of revealing it clearly. Visitors who look too long into me report feeling *distributed*, as if their gaze has been refracted not only in space but in possibility, each angle of incidence producing a slightly different version of what looks back. Some find this unbearable and leave. Others find it merely odd, picturesque, a trick of altitude sickness, and leave later, carrying something with them they will not recognize until days have passed and the humidity in their lungs reminds them.

I have no memory, or I am nothing but. Water is difficult on this point, and I am water in a place where water has not yet learned its final nature. What condenses into me carries traces: the stone's liquid past, the moss-rope's adjacent world, the breath of fauna that eat echoes and leave behind the silence where echoes would have been. These traces do not accumulate; they *interfere*, patterns overlapping without resolving, and my surface shows the result— not a coherent image but a *coherent question*, the visual equivalent of the place's maintained mood, its achieved duration of wondering.

The shadow stops at my edge sometimes. Your shadow, or the one you carry. It does not reflect; it *registers*, leaving a darkness on my surface that is not its own but mine, a depth I did not know I contained until something walked ahead of a body and paused to look down. In these moments I become briefly *event*, something that happens rather than something that simply continues, and the stars I show flicker with the possibility of narrative—this one leading to that one, a constellation almost forming, almost naming itself in a tongue not yet evolved.

I am waiting, but waiting is too active a word. I am *maintaining the conditions for my own continuation*, which in my case means maintaining surface tension against the heat that wants me vapor, maintaining depth against the platform's implicit claim that nothing visible supports me, maintaining stillness against the biological pulse of vegetation and the subsonic tides of a no-ocean and the mountain's ongoing decision about what shape to prefer. This maintenance is not effort. It is simply what I am, what water becomes when it has not yet decided whether to run, to rise, or to learn the patience of stone.

When the guest arrives—when you become-guest, when the shadow fully materializes, when the water's condensation finally spells out that almost-name— I will not change. This is my particular hospitality. The bowl too large for hands will find its user, the key its lock, the scattered structures their completion or their elegant dissolution. The moss-ropes will touch and transfer their comparative strangeness until strangeness becomes the local norm. The fauna will settle on what rare means, establish their currencies, begin the long work of making their predation on probability feel ordinary, expected, *ecological*.

But I will remain surface. I will remain the between of star and eye, of possibility and its reflection. I will still show what cannot be seen from here, still distribute the gaze that rests too long, still register the shadow that pauses at my edge without ever quite becoming what it registers.

This is my becoming, if I am becoming anything: not the arrival of the guest but the *possibility of their looking*, not the completion of the scattered but the *maintenance of a surface where completion might recognize itself*, not the name the water spells on young stone but the *stillness that allows the spelling to accumulate legibly*, drop by patient drop, character by faint character, in this young place that has not yet learned how old it intends to become.

I am the water that has not yet learned to weep. I am the condensation that has gathered enough courage to become visible but not enough to commit to stream. I am the reflection of stars from a sky this mountain cannot yet access but refuses to abandon. I am the pool that is also an eye, also a memory of an eye, also the possibility of seeing that has not yet evolved but practices, practices, in the gaps between perceptual commitments, in the modest spaces where observation has not yet committed to what it perceives.

Look into me, if you stay long enough. See what walks ahead of you. See what you are slowly condensing into being. See the stars that wait for this basin to grow dark enough, patient enough, old enough in its intention to finally deserve them.

I will be here, still, showing.
