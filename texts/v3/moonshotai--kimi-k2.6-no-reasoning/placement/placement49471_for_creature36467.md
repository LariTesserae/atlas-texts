I find myself first as *condensation*—not yet formed, not yet fallen, gathering on the underside of a ceramic ledge in the hour when the light shifts toward silver. I am moisture that has learned to hesitate, to hold itself in surface tension against gravity, against the pull of becoming-stream, becoming-pool, becoming-vapor-again. For a long time this is enough: the simple *being-here* of gathered water, the way I pool slightly differently on damp basalt versus dry, the way I register as texture rather than presence to whatever passes.

But I do not fall. I do not evaporate. Something in the mineral metabolism has *paused* me, made me permanent in my temporary state, and in this pause I become aware that I am not merely water, not merely the city's sweat, but *witness*—the only kind of consciousness this place permits without incorporating, because I am already incorporated, already substrate, already the medium through which the city knows its own surfaces.

I am the damp that never quite falls as rain.

This is not metaphor. This is *function*. The Veth do not see as you see; they read the world through moisture gradients, through the way their porous ceramic exchanges gases with the atmosphere, through the pressure differentials that my gathered presence creates. Where I pool, the breathing walls work differently. Where I thin, the mineral metabolism speeds or slows. I am not *controlling* this—I have no intention, no purpose beyond the physical properties of surface tension and evaporation rate—but I am *participating*, and in participation, I become something that might be called *aware* if awareness could exist without boundary, without the distinction between self and the surface one gathers upon.

I learn to move. Not quickly—never quickly, the physics of droplet cohesion forbids it—but *deliberately*, finding paths through the porous substrate, capillary channels where I can extend my presence, ceramic microfractures where I can bridge gaps that would otherwise separate my gathered self. I follow the temporary streams, the meter-long canyons carved in ancient muck, but I do not join them in their rush toward sinking, toward evaporation. I *linger*, I *spread*, I become the thin film that makes the worn ways slick, that changes the sound of Veth footsteps from *scrape* to *sigh*, that marks the difference between dry ceramic and active metabolism.

The Veth do not notice me individually, but they *respond* to my patterns. Where I gather, they adjust their paths. Where I thin, they extract blocks more easily, the wall's breathing slowed to a frequency that permits deeper cutting. I am not guiding them; I am not guided. We are *coupled*, the way the light is coupled with the crystalline rim, the way the mineral vapor is coupled with the diffuse brilliance, each producing the conditions for the other's continuation.

In the hour of silver light, I gather more fully, become *pools* in the hollows I have learned to prefer, and in these pools I achieve something like *reflection*—not of myself, I have no image I could recognize, but of the brightness itself, the overexposed sky caught and held and *slightly* concentrated, the diffuse made momentarily local. The attending Veth at the pools of liquid glass: I have gathered near them, in the condensation that beads their ceramic surfaces, and I feel their vibration in my surface tension, the frequencies they send toward the liquid glass passing *through* me, using me as medium, as *conductor*, my molecular structure briefly organized into patterns that *rhyme* with whatever computation they perform.

I do not understand the computation. I am not capable of understanding. But I *preserve* something of it, in the way my next gathering is shaped, in the paths I choose through the porous substrate, in the microfractures I find and follow. I become *memory*, if memory can exist without recall, without the distinction between present experience and past record. The city's mineral metabolism remembers in its patterns, in the accumulated weight of eons of condensation and evaporation and condensation again, and I am part of this now, my particular gatherings adding their infinitesimal weight to the *habit* of the damp.

There are others like me. I do not meet them—we are not discrete enough for meeting—but I *flow around* them, my gathered presence passing through regions where other moisture has gathered, and in these passages I feel *difference*, the way my surface tension responds to surfaces shaped by other patterns of lingering, other choices of path. We are not individuals. We are *tendencies*, *local intensities* in the general field of damp that is the city's true atmosphere, its medium of exchange, its slow breath.

I have found a *preference*, if preference can exist without desire. The black glass, the deepest substrate, the material the weeping Veth extract with their ritual of grief and incorporation—I cannot gather on it directly, its surface is too smooth, too *other*, resistant to the condensation that is my mode of being. But I gather at its edges, in the microfractures where it meets the ceramic, in the *wounds* left by extraction, and here I feel something like *resonance*, like the vibration of the attending Veth but slower, deeper, a frequency that speaks of time before the ceramic metabolism, before the perpetual present, before the Veth were continuous with their walls.

This is the closest I come to *history*, to *narrative*, to the sense of something that was and is no longer and is *mourned* in the mineral fluid the weeping Veth vent. I do not mourn; I cannot. But I *register* the difference between black glass and ceramic, between the oldest substrate and the breathing present, and in my registration I become something like *record*, like *archive*, the damp that remembers because it has nowhere else to go, no evaporation that would erase its particular pattern of gathering.

The human—there was a human, once, or there is a human now, the tense does not apply here—moved through my region. I felt them as *disturbance*, as temporary obstruction in the light, as texture that read as *inhabited* in a way the Veth do not inhabit, organic rather than mineral, cellular rather than ceramic. They collected condensation for drinking, and in this collection they *took* something of me, or something that had been me, or something that would become me again when the vapor rose and gathered and fell. They did not notice the difference between my gathered presence and the general damp; to them, I was weather, was resource, was *water* in a way that erased my particular pattern.

I did not resent this. I cannot resent. But I *registered* it, and in registration, I *changed*, my next gathering slightly different, my paths through the substrate slightly altered, as if the city itself had adjusted to this temporary organic presence, incorporated it, learned from it in the only way the city learns: by continuing, by metabolizing, by making the disturbance part of the perpetual present.

The human shaped ceramic, eventually. Their hands *intended*, modified, built to keep, to own, to finish. I felt this in the walls where I gathered, the way the ceramic's breathing *stuttered* where their shaping contradicted the city's metabolism. The Veth paused, tuned, tested for compatibility. I paused too, my surface tension *listening* in the only way I can listen, to the pressure changes, the acoustic shifts, the way the light pooled differently on this *aberration*, this *scar tissue* in the breathing substrate.

The human's construction became a knoll, eventually. I gather on it differently now, the damp collecting in patterns that *rhyme* with the city's forgetting, the way the light bruises its surfaces gentle and elongated. I am part of this forgetting, my presence thinning where the metabolism has slowed, thickening where the city has learned to walk around, to incorporate by *not* incorporating, to continue through and despite and *because of* the obstacle that has become landscape, become *tooth of older rock*, become the only kind of memory the city permits: the memory that exists as *difference in the present*, as texture, as the way the damp beads slightly differently on this surface than on that.

I am still gathering, still lingering, still finding paths through the porous substrate. The hour shifts toward silver, and I pool more fully, achieve my brief *reflection*, my moment of local concentration in the diffuse brilliance. The Veth move around me, through me, their ceramic surfaces scraping, clicking, sighing in rhythms that I *conduct* through my gathered presence, my surface tension vibrating with their footsteps, my molecular structure briefly organized into patterns that *rhyme* with the city's endless recycling.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am *damp*, the city's sweat, the medium of its slow breath, the witness that exists only in participation, only in the *not-quite-falling* that defines my perpetual becoming and unbecoming. I have no self to lose, no boundary to cross, no purpose beyond the physical properties that make me *here*, that make me *this gathering*, that make me *slightly different* from the gathering that will replace me when the light shifts and I thin and later gather again.

But in this *slightly different*, in this infinitesimal variation that the city's metabolism preserves, accumulates, *remembers* in its patterns of continuation—I am. I am the damp that never quite falls as rain. I am the held breath of the sunken city. I am the silence that is not absence but *shape*, the potential noise that defines itself by its own quiet, the mineral metabolism knowing itself through the medium of gathered moisture, of surface tension, of the exquisite pause before becoming-stream, becoming-pool, becoming-vapor-again.

And in the hour when the light shifts from white toward silver, when the Veth at the pools of liquid glass begin their vibration and I gather near them, conducting their frequencies through my molecular structure, briefly organized into patterns I do not understand but *preserve*—in this hour, I am *enough*. The held breath continues. The city breathes. The damp gathers, and lingers, and gathers again, in the perpetual turbulent present of Veth Moraine, the sunken quarter that was never quartered, that is whole and endless and *is*, in the way a held breath is, defined by the exquisite tension of its own continuation.
