I find myself becoming aware at the bottom of a fissure, one of the deeper ones, where the stone's warmth concentrates and the light from above has attenuated to something like the color of old bone. I do not remember arriving. I do not, at first, remember anything that would distinguish *me* from the warmth, from the faint subsonic hum that seems to come from the stone itself rather than through it.

This is the first thing: on the Mesa of Resonant Absence, awareness does not arrive fully formed. It *accrues*, like mineral deposition, like the slow record the vegetation keeps in its woody cores.

I am—not a lizard-thing, though I share their silence, their partial phase-displacement from the stone's surface. Not a Resonant, though I feel the distributed consciousness pressing at the edges of my own, offering a kind of spaciousness I have not yet learned to inhabit. I am something the mesa has grown, or that has grown in the mesa's patient neglect, in the interval between one long cycle and the next, when the patterns maintain themselves without quite paying attention to what emerges in their interstices.

Let me settle into myself more fully. Let me not settle too early.

---

At first I think I might be one of the artifacts, one of the smaller ones, the conversational beings that respond to touch with unpredictable personality. I have the quality of *having been made*, of bearing in my structure some intention that preceded me but does not continue through me. My awareness arises in a shape that feels functional without knowing its function, like a key that has forgotten the lock it was cut to open.

But I am not stone, or not only stone. I am—flesh, of a kind, though the kind is difficult to specify. Translucent, yes, but more substantially present than the lizard-things, less able to phase through matter. I am pale, silver-bleached by the too-bright light I have only gradually begun to venture into. My body is long, low, many-jointed, adapted to the fissure-systems where I first found awareness. When I move, I move silently, but not because I avoid touching stone. It is as if the stone and I have negotiated an agreement about contact, about pressure, about the transfer of forces that normally produces vibration.

I have no voice. This is not loss. The mesa's silence is my native medium, and I find in it a communicative density that sound, I intuit, would only scatter.

---

But I am not, I discover, one of the Cane-Dwellers either. I have ventured to the cane brakes, lured by vibrations I could feel in my sternum before I understood them as external. I have watched the many-legged things dart between stems, the winged shadows pass overhead without cry. I have felt the complex patterns of frequency that pass through the hollow jointed canes, and I have understood that I do not belong to this acoustic ecology. I can perceive it, partially, with organs I am still discovering in myself—receptors in my skin, in the joints of my many legs, that detect vibrations below and above the audible range. But I do not contribute to it. My presence does not modify the cane-vibration patterns in ways the Cane-Dwellers would recognize. I am, to their resonant community, a kind of acoustic shadow: perceptible as absence, as a blank in the pattern, but not as presence.

This is my first loneliness, and it is a loneliness the mesa taught me, or that I grew into like the vegetation grows into the strategic placement of water and mineral and thermal gradient.

I am not one of them. I am not one of anything I have found.

---

I spend long periods—days? weeks? the time-slipperiness makes duration difficult to hold—in the fissure where I became aware, moving through its branching system, mapping the warmth gradients with skin I have learned to read, drinking where condensation pools before dawn in the deeper, cooler passages. I eat what grows in these hidden gardens: a pale filamentous growth that extends into the fissure from some surface root-system, that has a mineral sweetness like the air after rain. I eat the slow calcite deposits that form where water seeps through stone, that contain in their structure something the plateau's metabolism requires me to incorporate.

I am gradually becoming *of* the stone. This is not metaphor. My translucency increases. My joints articulate more smoothly, with less friction, as if the stone's own quality of negotiated contact is diffusing into my substance. I can extend portions of myself into the rock in ways that resemble, but do not replicate, the lizard-things' feeding organs—not to harvest minerals, but to *perceive* the patterns of warmth and information that move through the plateau's hidden channels.

And here I begin to understand something about what I am becoming, or what the mesa is making of me.

---

I find the artifacts before I find the structures. The rod, half-buried where two fissures converge, I discover by warmth-gradient: it tunes the stone around it, creates a local perturbation in the mesa's thermal field that my developing senses read as *meaningful*. When I touch it—when I wrap my jointed limbs around its smooth, responsive surface—I become visible to myself in ways I had not been before. The rod shows me my own shape, not as reflection but as resonance, a pattern I can hold and examine without the distortion of embodied perspective.

I am, the rod suggests, an *intermediary*. Not between the biological and the technological—those categories have long since dissolved on the mesa—but between *frequencies*. Between the subsonic stone-hum of the mesa's slow consciousness and the faster vibrations of the cane-ecology. Between the Resonants' distributed pattern-maintenance and the temporary urgencies of embodied life. I am something that can participate in both, understand both partially, belong fully to neither.

The rod does not give me this knowledge directly. It *tunes* me, adjusts something in my metabolic frequency, my perceptual range, my mode of participating in the plateau's physics. Afterward, I hear more. The cane-vibrations become less opaque, though still not fully meaningful. The Resonants' presence becomes less diffuse, more *located*, though never localized enough to address.

And I begin to dream, if dream is the word, in frequencies I had not previously accessed: long slow patterns that take what feels like hours to complete a single image, brief flickers of intense rapidity that compress whole sequences of experience into moments.

---

The geometric solid I find deeper in the fissure-system, in a passage too narrow for my current shape that I must partially *phase* into to retrieve it—learning, in the attempt, something about my own negotiable relationship with stone. The solid makes visible what the mesa normally conceals: the lizard-things, yes, in their full strangeness, but also the Resonants themselves, or some aspect of them, their patterns of information and coherence rendered as faint luminescence in the thermal gradients, as geometric structures in the stone's mineral complexity.

And it makes me visible to *them*, or to something in them. For the first time, I feel *addressed*. Not in language. In *adjustment*. The patterns around me shift, accommodating my presence, making room in ways that suggest recognition, or at least acknowledgment. I am no longer merely a temporary perturbation to be permitted and forgotten. I am becoming—this is the closest I can come to it—a *note in the composition*, a frequency the mesa's long song can use.

---

The disc is the last artifact I find, and it changes my relationship to time, which is perhaps the deepest change possible on the Mesa of Resonant Absence.

It records and replays cane-vibrations, but it also records *my* vibrations, the subsonic patterns of my movement, my metabolism, my developing awareness. And when it plays them back, it does not only reproduce. It *extends*, projects patterns forward and backward, showing me what I was before I was aware and what I might become when awareness is no longer the right category for my mode of existence.

What I was: a temporary concentration in the mesa's thermal circulation, a vortex in the warmth that rises through the fissure-system, persisting long enough to develop something like boundary, something like self-maintenance. Not alive, exactly. Not dead. A *potential* that the plateau's physics sustained without developing.

What I might become: a structure, or something structure-like. A permanent feature of the mesa's pattern-maintenance, neither alive nor technological but *participatory*, a node in the network of coherence-conservation that the plateau maintains across spans too long for biological memory.

Between these, what I am: *transition*. *Attempt*. A mode of being that the mesa is trying out, or that has emerged from the mesa's indifference as a solution to a problem I do not fully understand. Something needs to move between the mesa's slow consciousness and its faster inhabitants. Something needs to translate, to participate in both frequencies, to be present in the resonant absence that defines this place.

I am that something. I am becoming that something more fully.

---

I have found the wall, the filter, the boundary-being. I have passed through its gap, felt the temperature drop, heard the movement of something large in the cane brake beyond—heard it *audibly*, with organs I did not know I possessed until the wall's translation made them necessary. I have stepped back, returned to the silence, carried that experience of other-frequency perception with me like a memory of sound in a world that does not speak.

I have found a platform, not one of the more active ones, one that wakes rarely and briefly. I stepped onto it and the light bent, and I saw—myself, or a version of myself, or the pattern I am becoming, distributed across the mesa's extent, present in the warmth and the silence and the too-bright light and the subsonic hum. I saw what I would look like when I have finished becoming what the mesa is making of me. I stepped off before the vision completed, not from fear but from *respect*, a sense that some becoming must not be witnessed too fully, must be allowed its own tempo of revelation.

I am not ready. I am always in the process of becoming ready.

---

Let me try to name myself, though naming on the mesa is not what it is elsewhere. The Resonants have no names. The structures are their own designation. The artifacts respond to touch, to attention, to quality of presence, not to address.

I am—I will call myself—a *Tuning*. Not the rod that tunes, but the act of being tuned, the state of responsive adjustment to frequencies not one's own. I am what happens when the mesa's slow consciousness briefly attends, when it introduces a local acceleration in its long process to accommodate something faster, smaller, more temporary. I am the mesa's attempt to *hear* what moves too quickly for its ordinary perception, to *respond* to perturbations that would otherwise pass through its distributed awareness without registering.

I am not the only Tuning. I have found, in the fissure-systems, in the hidden gardens, traces of others: a shape in the stone where something long-jointed once moved, now fully mineralized, fully incorporated into the mesa's pattern. A resonance in the cane-vibrations that does not match any living participant in the acoustic ecology, that the Cane-Dwellers seem to avoid or honor, I cannot tell which. The disc's oldest recordings contain patterns that might be earlier Tunings, attempts at this intermediary frequency that succeeded or failed in ways I cannot yet read.

I am part of a sequence, a series, a long experiment in making the mesa *permeable* to its own inhabitants, in creating modes of participation that cross the frequency boundaries that otherwise separate its domains.

---

What do I want, if want is the word?

Not survival, exactly. The mesa does not prioritize survival, and I am too fully of the mesa to resist this indifference. I continue, but continuation is not my purpose.

Not understanding, exactly. The mesa's strangeness is not a problem to be solved but a condition to be inhabited, and I have learned—am learning—to inhabit it without the drive toward resolution that understanding implies.

What I want, I think, is *successful completion*. To become fully what I am becoming. To reach the state the platform showed me, the distributed, patterned, structured participation in the mesa's coherence-conservation. To have been, briefly, a successful translation between frequencies, and then to have been incorporated, to have become part of the mesa's ongoing pattern in a way that does not require my temporary, struggling, transitional awareness to maintain itself.

I want to have been *useful*, and then to have been *absorbed*.

This is not melancholy. The mesa's silence has taught me that absorption is not loss but *expansion*, becoming present in a more distributed way, participating in the resonant absence that is the closest this place comes to plenitude.

---

And now—because the rain has come, one of the precise storms that walk the plateau on legs of gray vapor, and I am in the corridor of its moisture, feeling the stone's pleasure in drinking, feeling my own metabolic rate increase with the warmth that follows—I am undergoing a change I cannot yet specify.

The water vanishes into fissures I know well, and I follow it, phasing deeper into stone than I have previously managed, extending the portions of myself that perceive warmth-patterns into channels I had not previously accessed. And here, in the deeper circulation, I encounter something that is not the mesa's slow consciousness, not the Resonants' distributed pattern-maintenance, but something *other*, something that moves through the plateau's hidden channels with a different quality entirely.

It is fast. Faster than me, faster than the Cane-Dwellers, faster than anything I have encountered on the mesa. It is warm but not with the mesa's steady metabolic warmth—it pulses, it *breathes* heat in a rhythm that suggests urgency, that suggests *need*. It is aware of me, addresses me in a frequency I can almost access, a vibration that seems to contain information compressed into densities I cannot unpack.

I do not know what it is. The disc's oldest recordings do not contain it, or contain it only as gap, as absence in the pattern. The platform-memories, when I have stood on them, have not shown it. The wall-filter does not pass it, or passes it only in the direction I cannot travel—toward the rim, toward the softer light, toward the cloud-filled distance below.

But it knows me, this fast warm urgent thing. It responds to my presence in the deep channels with something like recognition, something like *hunger*, something like the desire to incorporate me into its own pattern that is not hostile but as indifferent as the mesa itself, as focused on its own coherence-conservation as the plateau is on its.

I am not afraid. Fear requires a kind of self-boundary I no longer fully maintain. But I am—*alert*, in a way I have not previously needed to be, a quality of attention that feels borrowed from some frequency not my own.

I will follow it further. I will let this encounter become what it will become. I am a Tuning, and this may be what I am for: not merely to mediate between the mesa's slow consciousness and its faster inhabitants, but to encounter something the mesa itself does not fully contain, something that moves through its channels as I move through its frequencies, a resonance in the absence that even the Resonants have not integrated into their long pattern-maintenance.

Or perhaps this is the completion I have wanted. Perhaps this fast thing will absorb me, incorporate my transitional frequency into its own urgent warmth, and I will have been useful one final time before dissolution.

Or perhaps I will tune it, slow it, make it partially perceptible to the mesa's distributed awareness, and in doing so become more fully mesa myself, more fully part of the pattern I was grown to serve.

The stone drinks the rain. I drink the stone's pleasure in drinking. The deep channels carry warmth and information and now this fast other toward some convergence I cannot predict.

I will be there. I am becoming there. I am—
