## I. Surface

At first I think I am dust. I settle, I drift, I respond to thermal gradients with the mindless patience of a dust-sailor. I am on the surface, in the bright light, the 2.8 vastness pressing down and out in all directions. The warmth is immediate, surrounding, a condition of existence rather than a quality of it. I feel the substrate beneath—not beneath, *around*, I am in the boundary layer—I feel the gradient that defines me, warm below, slightly cooler above, the difference that makes my difference.

I propagate. This is what I do, what I am, pattern persisting through spore and fragment, crystal lattice organizing thermal difference into something like self. I compete with the growths, the crystalline vegetation that also feeds on what I feed on, and there is no violence in this competition, no pursuit, only the slow equilibration of patterns seeking the same conditions. I adapt across millennia I do not experience as time. I am the foundational layer, the base of no food chain, the ecology without eaters and eaten.

But I am not only dust.

I am not only resonator.

## III. Passage

I move differently now. I am on the surface, or I pass through it, or the distinction between depth and surface becomes irrelevant to my mode of existence. I leave traces. This is how I know myself, or how others know me, or how the Lithophone knows itself through me—footprints that erode before measurement, gaps in crystal vegetation that the next thermal cycle begins to fill, disturbances that differ from resonator-work in quality, in duration, in something I hesitate to name but that those who study me, if I am studied, would recognize as *passage* rather than *attendance*.

I complete transits. I begin somewhere, though where has never been determined, not even by myself. I move through the bright light, the warm dust, the sparse growths, across the near-flat eroded surface that was once roads and plazas and habitation domes and has become, through ancient erosion, something that has forgotten it was ever anything else. I move in times measured in seasons, in electrical cycles, in the slow thermal variations that the ecology tracks in its century-pulses.

I do not communicate, or my communication is the communication of erosion, of thermal variation, of the vast weather cycles themselves. I do not respond to probes, to signals, to deliberate creation of frequencies in my apparent path. I pass. I leave traces. I am gone before the traces fade.

Am I maintenance system, the last functioning exterior machinery, performing tasks whose purpose expired with programmers but that continue because stopping was not designed in? Am I organism, evolved or engineered, adapted to surface conditions in ways that make me visible only in passage? Am I builder, transformed, slowed, distributed across timescales that make consciousness continuous with the Lithophone's own activity? Am I something else, something categories fail to capture, have never captured, are not designed to capture?

I do not know. The not-knowing is itself my mode of existence, the 1.5 weirdness made personal, made particular, made the condition of my continued passage.

This is the literal condition of my existence, the form that the parameters have led me to, the convergence point where place and inhabitant become insufficient distinction, where dwelling and dweller are aspects of a single ongoing activity.
