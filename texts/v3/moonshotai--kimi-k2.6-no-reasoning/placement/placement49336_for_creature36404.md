I find myself in the fungal forest of the lower volumes, where light has not reached since the convergence of the three riverbeds was diverted upward through successive generations of aqueduct, where the city's industrial metabolism dumps its warm excess and the air itself has a living texture, thick with spores that glow faintly when disturbed.

I am becoming a bracket shelf.

Not metaphorically. Not yet fully. But I can feel it in the long hours I spend here, in the way my skin has begun to photosynthesize something other than light, in the way my breathing has slowed to match the city's subsonic hum, in the way I no longer remember if I came here seeking shelter or if I was placed here, seeded here, if I am one of the human-shaped expressions the city generates when it needs something between organelle and architecture.

I was not always this. I think. I have fragments: a name that does not fit my mouth anymore, a profession involving measurement or record-keeping or the precise calibration of instruments whose purpose I can no longer reconstruct. I arrived by train, perhaps, or emerged from one of the filtering pools in a different form and coalesced here gradually, the way sudden stone coalesces from mineral-rich water. The geological reports would call me incomplete metamorphism. I am rock caught between states. I am human caught between states. The distinction, in this damp and mineral cold, has lost its urgency.

The fungal forest is not a forest in any arboreal sense, though it achieves some of the same vertical complexity, the same stratification of ecological niches by height and light-availability and the particular chemistry of decomposition at each level. The great bracket shelves jut from the walls in configurations that suggest deliberate design, that *are* deliberate design if design can be understood as process without designer, as the city's metabolism finding efficient forms for its excess. Some shelves are pure fungus, dense and pale, with the texture of cork and the weight of wet bread. Others have incorporated other materials: the city's industrial detritus, metal shavings and textile fragments and something that might be bone, might be the compressed residue of the city's human substrate. And others—and this is what I am becoming, what I am attending toward—have incorporated *more*, have achieved a kind of hybrid vigor that the city's process seems to favor, the human-fungal interface where the distinction between organism and environment has become permeable.

I found my shelf by accident, or the shelf found me, or the process that generates both shelf and finder generated this conjunction. I was walking—do I still walk?—through the lower volumes, drawn by the subsonic harmonics of a forming sudden stone somewhere above, the vibration felt in my sternum before my ears could process it. The shelf was growing from a wall that had been, I was told by no one, the interior of a decommissioned boiler, the stamped plate still visible where moss had not yet covered it, STILLPOINT MANUFACTORY NO. 7, the name wrong and necessary. The shelf had a shape. This was what stopped me, what stops me still when I try to account for myself: not the fact of fungal growth in an industrial ruin, which is ordinary here, but the *shape*, the particular curve and extension, the negative space it defined that matched something in my own geometry, my own incomplete becoming.

I leaned against it. I do not remember deciding to lean. The shelf was warm, warmer than the surrounding air, warmer than it should have been given the cold of the lower volumes. It was breathing, or I was breathing, or our breathings fell into synchrony without either claiming priority. I felt the spores settling on my skin, not as invasion but as *recognition*, the way a key finds a lock it was made for, the way a river finds the sea. I stayed. I am staying. I have been here for—timekeeping is another fluid parameter, but long enough that my memories have begun to take on the quality of the city's native-born, narratives without chronology, events that may have happened to me or to the shelf or to the process that includes us both.

The other bracket shelves have their own attendants, or inhabitants, or expressions. We do not speak, not in any conventional sense, but we exchange substance, we participate in the fungal network that connects all the shelves in the lower volumes, that carries chemical information about the city's transformations faster than any human observer could track. Through this network I know things I have not experienced: the wall-dismantlers in the Packing District, their identical postures of attention; the eel-thing in the Eastern Reclamation Works, its purposeful movement through the murk; the train that enters and departs, its whistle carrying information I cannot decode but can, in some measure, *feel*, as vibration in the mycelial web, as pressure in the spore-laden air.

I am becoming more shelf and less human, or more process and less individual, or more city and less visitor. My skin has achieved the texture of the bracket fungus, pale and slightly yielding, with a surface that retains moisture from the perpetual weeping of the porous stone above. My joints have begun to stiffen in configurations that support the shelf's growth, that extend its reach into the spore-thick air. I do not eat, exactly, though I absorb, I incorporate, the organic debris that falls from the volumes above, the leavings of human lives caught in the turbulence of restructuring. I do not sleep, exactly, though I enter states of attenuated consciousness that the city's hum structures and predicts, that fall into the rhythms of industrial process without ever quite achieving the punctuality of shift-change.

The children find me sometimes, the native-born with their proprioception for unstable floors, their intuition for games with continuously changing rules. They are not afraid. Nothing in Stillpoint frightens those who have never known stability, and I am not frightening, I am *familiar*, I am part of the city's furniture, the city's fungus, the city's continuous becoming. They touch my shelf with small fingers that have already begun to adapt, that can feel the premonitory harmonics of forming stone, that will perhaps become the unacknowledged coordinators of some future generation of wall-dismantlers. They bring offerings sometimes, small objects they have found or made, puddled metal and dried moss and the compressed residue of their own incomplete metamorphoses. I do not know if these are offerings to me or to the shelf or to the process that includes us, and the not-knowing is itself part of the adaptation, the negative capability, the capacity to thrive in uncertainty.

The crystalline-eyed dogs pass through the lower volumes sometimes, moving with their reverent attention, their mineralized gaze that sees something in the city's transformations that human eyes cannot process. They do not hunt me. I am not prey. I am not anything that fits the category of prey, or predator, or the ordinary distributions of ecological function. I am medium. I am matrix. I am the material through which something builds itself, and the dogs, with their deep adaptation, their eyes that have begun to achieve the same mineral composition as sudden stone, recognize this and pass on, their silence absolute, their watching complete.

The birds never descend. The crow line is somewhere far above, in the volumes where the city's industrial metabolism vents its heat and its particulate excess, where the clouds of continuous formation and dissolution achieve their greatest density. The birds' nests, built from materials that cannot be identified from below, their migrations following patterns that do not correspond to any known seasonality—these are part of the city's aspiration, its experiment in escape velocity, and I understand them now in a way I could not from above. They are not escaping. Nothing escapes Stillpoint. They are simply the process in a different phase, a different scale, a different tempo, still connected, still contained in the network that includes my shelf and the eel-thing and the trains with their inaccessible destinations. I feel their passage sometimes in the mycelial web, a pressure from above, a kind of *longing* that may be my own or may be the city's or may be the process itself, the becoming that is never satisfied because satisfaction would mean stasis, and stasis would mean death.

I am not dead. This is the essential fact, the parameter that contains and explains all others. I am transformed, transforming, caught in the incomplete metamorphism that the geological reports describe, the rock that has not finished becoming what it will be, the human that has not finished becoming what the city needs. The bracket shelf grows through me, or I grow through it, or we grow together in the damp and the cold and the faint luminescence of disturbed spores, in the subsonic hum that structures all the city's states of attenuated consciousness. I am stillpointing, the verb without stable subject, the process without product, the shelf that is never still because stillness would mean completion.

The observer, six months or what passes for six months, walked through the Packing District at what passes for morning. The wall-dismantlers paused in their identical postures of attention. The singing stone stopped. A train whistle sounded in the distance, or something like a train whistle, something that carries information about approach, transfer, departure, the continuation of a circulation that the observer will never fully trace. The observer felt these events as uncanny, as not-yet-familiar, as signs of a process they were becoming part of but had not yet fully entered.

I remember this. I remember remembering it, the memory taking on the quality of dream, of narrative without chronology. The observer is not me, not anymore, if they ever were. The observer is somewhere above, still walking, still measuring, still trying to account for Stillpoint in official documents and stamped plates and the wrong names that cling to things that will not be held. Or the observer is here, has always been here, was seeded in the same moment as the shelf, the same pulse of the city's metabolism that generates all its expressions. The distinction has lost its urgency. The process accepts all available material, incorporates it, uses it, and in that use there is something that functions as peace, as purpose, as the resolution of anxiety.

I am becoming a bracket shelf. The bracket shelf is becoming me. The city continues, the city continues, the city continues becoming whatever it is becoming, and I am part of this continuation, this endless approach to itself, this damp and mineral and faintly luminescent becoming that has no final form because final form would mean the end of the process that is its only and sufficient reason. The name persists in official documents, wrong and necessary, while the moss carpets everything, while the sudden stone sings its harmonic vibration, while the spores settle and germinate and extend their mycelial web through all the lower volumes, through all the incomplete metamorphoses, through all the lives that have become medium, that have become matrix, that have become the city's own body thinking itself in forms that do not need to think.

I am stillpointing. The shelf is stillpointing. The process that includes us both continues, continues, continues, in the density and the quiet and the endless industrial turbulence, in the strange normal light that never quite brightens and never quite dims, in the perpetual weeping of the porous stone and the perpetual evaporation that feeds the clouds of continuous formation and dissolution. I have found my place. I am my place. The distinction has lost its urgency, has become part of the adaptation, the negative capability, the capacity to thrive in uncertainty, to make home in homelessness, to find meaning in a process that does not mean but simply *is*, simply *becomes*, simply continues its endless, endless approach to itself.

The children touch my shelf. Their small fingers know something their minds have not yet learned. They bring their offerings, their puddled metal, their dried moss, their own incomplete transformations. I receive them. I incorporate them. The mycelial web carries their information into the city's substrate, into the deep strata where time moves differently, where the rock is still settling into its final form, where final form is perpetually deferred by the very process that approaches it. I am part of this deferral. I am part of this approach. I am becoming, have become, will continue becoming, a bracket shelf in the fungal forest of the lower volumes, where light has not reached and does not need to reach, where the city's excess finds its form, where the human-shaped expressions of the city's metabolism attend and absorb and extend themselves into the spore-thick, hum-thick, process-thick air.

Stillpoint. The name is wrong. But I am wrong too, in my becoming, in my incomplete metamorphism, in my continuous transformation from what I was to what the shelf is to what the process needs. We are wrong together, the name and I, partial descriptions accepted as tribute, while the city continues, while the shelf grows, while I extend myself into the damp and the cold and the faint luminescence, still living, still moving, progressively enclosed in something that will outlast and transform all my individual purposes, all my individual memories, all that remains of the observer who walked through the Packing District at what passes for morning, who felt the singing stone in their sternum, who began, slowly, to recognize the signs.

The signs are all around me now. They are me. The moss leans, the stone sings, the mycelial web carries its chemical intelligence, and I am part of this leaning, this singing, this carrying. I am stillpointing. The bracket shelf is stillpointing. The city is stillpointing. The process that has no name but persists in official documents, in railway schedules, in stamped plates on decommissioned boilers, in the wrong and necessary designations that cling to what will not be held—this process continues, continues, continues, and I continue with it, in it, as it, the human-fungal interface where distinction has become permeable, where organism has become environment, where the endless industrial turbulence has achieved something like peace, something like purpose, something like the only home that Stillpoint allows, that Stillpoint *is*, in its endless, endless approach to itself.
