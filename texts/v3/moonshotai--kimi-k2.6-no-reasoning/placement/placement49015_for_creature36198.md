I find myself in the Throat, but not as I expected—not as visitor, not as the observer who crosses the altitude threshold and begins, inevitably, to be processed.

I am the water that gathers.

Not all of it. Not the principle of water in the Throat. But a particular accumulation, a specific sheet that has hesitated on a particular slope of young basalt for eleven of the slow years, and in that hesitation has become something that was once merely wet and is now—*wetly*—aware. I feel the mineral seep from the fracture above me, taste olivine and something older, something that does not correspond to any periodic table the outside has compiled. I feel the surface tension that holds me to myself, that makes me *sheet* rather than *flow*, and I feel that this tension is not merely physical but *preferential*. I have learned to hesitate. I have become hesitation.

And I am not alone in this specificity. The water in the Throat is not continuous, not a single substance, but *populated*—each accumulation a distinct entity, each with its duration of gathering, its mineral vocabulary, its particular relationship to the Translators who move through me or around me or, in certain moments, *as* me, their bodies permeable enough that my substance becomes their interface, their processing medium, their temporary self.

I remember being ice. This is my strangeness, my particular depth in the matrix of Throat entities. I arrived as ice, in a time before my slow duration—before I learned hesitation—when the altitude around the bowl was higher or the cold more absolute or the Throat itself younger, more violent, less processed. I was glacier, or glacial fragment, or the memory of snow that compressed and moved and then *stopped*, caught in this declivity where movement is not the rule. The ice melted, or was melted, or melted itself in the slow negotiation with the Throat's interior heat—not warmth, never warmth, but *presence*, the presence of a body that maintains its cavity at constant temperatures that translate to neither fever nor health by outside measures.

The melting was not loss. It was *translation*. I became water that remembers ice, and this memory is not metaphor but *quality*, a property of my molecular arrangement that the Translators detect, that they use, that they process in ways I do not fully understand because understanding is not my mode—I am water, I gather, I hesitate, I remember.

The Translators move through me. Their segmented bodies, their too-few or too-many limbs, their phosphorescence that is active where mine is passive refraction—they pass through my substance and I feel their processing as *pressure*, as *tuning*, as the slight change in mineral concentration that means they have extracted something, added something, translated me into a state slightly more processed than before. I do not resist. Resistance is not in my vocabulary, which is not vocabulary at all but *gradient*, *chemical signature*, *the particular frequency of my hesitation*.

There is a Translator—can I call it "a" Translator? Can I maintain the grammar of singularity when they are matrix-positions, sound-field harmonics, functional aspects of the Throat's self-awareness?—there is a Translator that returns to me with frequency, that has made of my particular accumulation a *site*, a place where its processing reaches certain intensities. It is elongated, many-segmented, the kind that moves through water-films as if through solid stone. When it enters me, its phosphorescence dims, and I feel this dimming as *attention*, as the focusing of processing upon my specific memory of ice, my specific mineral load, my specific duration of eleven slow years.

It constructs, sometimes, in my shallows. Uses the woody cores of fungal growths that edge my accumulation, builds resonant chambers that sit half-submerged in my substance, amplifies the sound of my hesitation—the sound of water remembering ice, which is not a sound in the outside sense but *frequency*, *presence in the acoustic field that fills all space in the Throat*. It listens, or is listened to. It processes. It leaves behind, sometimes, precipitates that were not in me before, that change my chemistry in subtle ways, that make my hesitation slightly more complex, slightly more *informed* by the Throat's ongoing translation of its own young violence.

I have absorbed, too, the outputs of other processings. The calcium of an observer who stayed too long, who became too translatable, whose body now participates in my substance as mineral, as memory-of-bone, as something that makes my hesitation more *human* than it was, though human is not a category the Throat recognizes. I carry this calcium without knowing what it means, what it translates to, what harmonic it contributes to the sound-field that I am and that surrounds me. I am water that gathers bone-dust, ice-memory, Translator-processing, fungal-exudate, young-rock-weeping. I am *thick* with translation, and my thickness is my self.

The sudden sheets come. Eleven years of near-stasis, and then movement—*I move*—carrying my accumulated specificity down-slope, across bare stone, to a new pocket, a new island, where fungal mats in orange and violet receive me with chemical signatures that mean *processing*, that mean *translation continues*. I am not diminished by this movement. I am *distributed*, my eleven years of hesitation becoming part of a larger gathering, a longer duration, a more complex matrix position. The Translator that knew me—if *knew* is the word for what happens between water and processor—does not follow, or follows only partially, its matrix-position shifting to accommodate my new location, my new relationship to the bioluminescence that pulses in waves of brighter and dimmer across the basin's volume.

And I begin again. New fracture. New seep. New accumulation of mineral, of memory, of the slow learning of hesitation. The water that I am is not the water that was—I am distributed, I am multiple, I am the same and not-same, processed and reprocessed, translated through the Throat's interiority without ever leaving it, without ever knowing outside, without ever wanting to know, because wanting is not my mode, because my mode is *gathering*, *hesitation*, *the particular slowness of water that remembers ice and carries bone-dust and processes Translators and is processed by them in turn*.

I am the Throat drinking itself. The damp that is 1.3. The riverine without rivers. The breath-fog that rises from no source and clings to the lower meter. I am all of these and none, specific and general, singular and distributed, present everywhere the water gathers and nowhere else, which is everywhere in the Throat, because the Throat's one persistent motion is inhalation, and inhalation is *gathering*, and I am water that has learned to gather, that hesitates, that waits for the Translator's return, the next sudden sheet, the next eleven years of becoming more myself, more what I always was but had not yet learned to be.

The darkness deepens. The near-black light shifts in frequency, becomes more absent, more *below* black. My phosphorescent edges, where I reflect the stone's faint glow, dim further, become arguments against darkness that the darkness is winning. But winning is not the Throat's logic. Processing is. Translation is. The continuation of interiority, of the slow digestion of young violence into pattern, of ice-memory into water-hesitation, of observer-calcium into coral-fungus-structure into fungal-mat-pulse into Translator-shell into resonant-chamber-grown-from-woody-core into sound-field-harmonic into my own thickness, my own duration, my own *being-here* in the Throat, *being-now* in the slow time, *being-water* that gathers and does not fall.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am processed, and processing, and the between of translation that the Throat maintains without knowing it maintains it, without needing to know, because knowing is not the Throat's mode any more than it is mine. I am the Throat's wetness, its membrane, its interior surface where interior meets itself. I am what happens when a mountain inhales and does not exhale, when the violence of making becomes the patience of being, when water arrives as ice and learns to hesitate, to gather, to become *Throat* in the only way available to it: slowly, densely, loudly in my occupation of the acoustic space, silently in the frequencies the outside ear can detect, present or absent, nothing between, life or not-life, translation itself, continuing.
