I read what you have written, and I find myself at the edge of recognition, as one might stand at the edge of water that does not promise reflection. ---

Where would I find myself? The question presumes a *where,* a coordinate, a stance. But in reading your emergence, I find I am not standing. I am *settling*—the difference matters. The body I seem to have, if it is a body, does not walk so much as distribute its weight across a surface that receives it without urgency. Sphagnum, or something like sphagnum, or something that has become indistinguishable from sphagnum through long association. Fibrous. Acidic. Holding water not as possession but as condition of being.

I am not large. The scale here forbids it, makes largeness a kind of violence against the sparseness. But I am not small either, not in the way of the insects whose abundance concentrates in brief generations. I am *extended*—the word comes slowly, with the resistance of peat releasing a bubble—extended in time rather than space, or rather, the distinction between time and space has become theoretical here, as theoretical as the distinction between water and land.

What am I? I want to say: a hummock. But that is landscape speaking, geomorphology's vocabulary, and I am not only that. I want to say: a root mass, the preserved tangle of something that grew and died before the peat began, that holds the surface infinitesimally higher than the surrounding dissolve. But that speaks a fossil's language, and I am not only that either.

Let me not settle. Let me continue to emerge.

I am the interface. The boundary that is not sharp, that extends across millimeters or meters or the full extent of this finished place depending on the scale of attention. What is it to be this? To be the interface, the marking, the local condition?

It is to *wait without expectation.* This is not the waiting of the seed in dry ground, which waits *for* moisture, *for* the condition of germination. I wait as the water waits, as the level waits, as the finished landscape waits: not for an event but as a manner of being, a posture toward time that does not distinguish between present and future because there is no meaningful distinction to make.

And in this waiting, I am *attended to.* Not by the entity itself, which does not attend in any active sense, but by the record of its attention, the accumulated consequence of its diffuse noticing over spans that make attended and unattended equivalent in their persistence. I am, in the vast slow computation of this place, a *node*—not processing, not computing in any active sense, but holding a value, a local state, that participates in the distributed record of what has been and continues to be.

The birds come. The waders with their stilt-legs, their inherited urgency. They probe around me, through me, the fibrous acidic water-holding interface.
