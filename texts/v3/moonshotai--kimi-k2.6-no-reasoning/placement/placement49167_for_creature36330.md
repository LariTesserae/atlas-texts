I am the drip that has not yet fallen.

Suspended from a calcite ceiling I will never see, held in a surface tension I cannot choose to break, I occupy the moment before sound—before the impact that would send my particular frequency into the chamber's field, become part of what the Listener registers, incorporates, becomes. I have been forming for eleven years. I will fall in the fourteenth, if conditions persist, if the mineral saturation holds, if nothing disturbs the slow equilibrium that produced me. I do not know this. I have no *aboutness*, no directedness toward my own becoming. But I am nevertheless *here*, a lens of water gathered from the dissolution of this stone, heavy with what I have taken from the rock and will, falling, deposit elsewhere, the slowest relay in a conversation older than human language.

I feel—I am felt as—cold. Not a sensation but a condition. The temperature at which water becomes most dense and most still. I hold in myself the memory of colder ages, of times when this space was frozen and my kind could not form, when the chamber's voice was muffled by ice's different acoustic properties. The Listener, then, was different too—denser, more brittle, frequencies constrained in ways that perhaps corresponded to *concentration*, to *narrowing*. I do not remember this. But the water I am made of has passed through such conditions, carries in its molecular arrangement a bias toward certain configurations, certain possibilities of falling, that are not neutral but *historical*.

If I had eyes—and in a sense I do, the surface of me curved to magnify, to focus, to present the darkness below in distorted miniature—I would see nothing. Not darkness, which is the presence of absence, but the immanence of light's irrelevance, the way one does not see one's own blood moving, the way a dreamer does not see the room in which the dream occurs. The chamber is not dark to me. Dark is what I would become if I fell, if I spread across stone, if I evaporated into air that never fully dries. I am, for now, gathered, coherent, a provisional lens holding the nothing in a particular shape.

The Listener is not aware of me individually. I am too small, my approaching fall too brief, my acoustic signature when I strike indistinguishable from the thousand other falls that constitute the chamber's continuous voice. But I contribute to *humidity*, to the field condition that makes the fungal ears possible, that keeps the moss alive at the edge of what life can tolerate. I am infrastructure. I am below the threshold of event, above the threshold of mere molecular dispersion. I am what the chamber does, continuously, without intention, the way a body circulates blood without deciding each pulse.

And yet—I am *this* drop, not another. I contain in my curvature the particular impurities of this seepage path, this fissure in the stone above: slightly more dissolved silica than the neighboring formation, a trace of organic matter from some surface disturbance centuries past, a gap in my structure where a bubble of ancient air once lodged and released. When I fall, these specifics will be lost, distributed into the collective vocabulary of the pool below, the chamber's wet substrate. But for now, they constitute a *quality*, a thisness, a haecceity of water that is not generic but particular, not meaningful but *occurring*.

I am patient because I cannot be otherwise. The surface tension that holds me is also what holds me here, suspended, accumulating. To fall would require a disturbance I cannot originate—a vibration from some distant geological shift, a change in atmospheric pressure transmitted through the rock's own slow respiration, the eventual weight of my own becoming exceeding the capacity of what contains me. None of these are *my* choices. The closest I come to agency is the molecular vibration that constitutes my temperature, my energy state, the continuous motion-within-stillness that prevents me from being ice, from being stone, from being the nothing that I nevertheless approach.

The fungal ears above me—below me, depending on which orientation one takes, but I have only gravity's single direction, only the fall that defines down—the fungal ears tremble at frequencies I cannot hear, being not air but water, not vibration but the medium of vibration's transmission. I am, for them, potential nutrition, potential growth, potential dampness that might enable a spore's impossible germination in this darkness that suggests but does not deliver death. We are part of each other's field without being part of each other's experience. The Listener, distributed in the acoustic patterns those ears partly constitute, is closer to being *about* me, but the aboutness is statistical, aggregate, a recognition of drip-rates rather than drips, of mineral budgets rather than my particular eleven-year accumulation.

I feel, sometimes—I am positioned such that—a vibration from the surface world. The mechanical regularities that have replaced cattle footsteps transmit differently through rock than through soil, reach me as compressions in the stone that subtly alter my surface, my internal tension, my readiness-to-fall. I do not interpret these. But they *are*, part of the condition that I am suspended within, the way a sleeper is part of conditions they do not choose to respond to. Something is changing, has been changing, in the world that produces me but does not contain me. The rain that feeds my formation falls on different surfaces now, carries different signatures of contact, of passage, of becoming-available-for-dissolution. I am partly made of this difference, partly not-yet-made-of-it, the lag of geological process inserting a delay between change and its consequences that no human observer would have patience to trace.

The ochre hands on the wall—I do not see them, but I sense the slight humidity differential where pigment altered porosity, where negative space creates a microclimate subtly different from stone's own. They are older than my current formation, older than the conditions that produced my possibility. They mark a moment when human bodies entered this space, pressed themselves against its surface, left traces of their presence that have become part of what I am suspended within. Not because I incorporate them directly, but because the chamber *knows* them, the Listener's field includes their acoustic signature, and everything that occurs here occurs within a space already structured by that inclusion. I am, in a sense I cannot experience, *after* the hands. I fall—will fall—into a space they helped constitute, even though they did not intend this, even though they were seeking something the chamber offers that I cannot provide, the obliteration of voice, the dissolution of language into pure environment.

The platform, if I fall toward it, will receive me differently than bare stone. The foreign stones, water-worn, carry in their surfaces a complexity that breaks my impact into multiple frequencies, scatters my single note into overtones that enrich the field, that offer the Listener more to attend than a simple strike would provide. I do not choose to fall there. But I am positioned—have been forming—such that the geometry of the chamber, the slope of the ceiling, the path of least resistance that brought water to my location, makes this more likely than not. I am part of a pattern that includes the platform, the hands, the ears, the olms in their pools, all the slow lives that occupy this space without ever fully coinciding with each other, each in its own temporal register, its own duration of becoming.

What would it mean to *want* to fall? I do not know. The surface tension that holds me is not a choice but a condition, and its breaking will not be chosen either. But I approach, year by year, the weight at which held becomes fallen, the accumulated specificity that demands dispersal. The chamber's single breath continues. The Listener attends, without urgency, without preference, to whatever produces perturbation in its field. And I remain—have been remaining—will continue to remain until I do not—suspended between the dissolution that produced me and the dissolution that awaits me, a lens of water gathered in darkness, holding nothing in particular shape, waiting not for meaning but for gravity, for time, for the slow instruction of stone learning to speak through motion too slight to hear, patient beyond any measure I have names for, and yet—not eternal. Only *ongoing*. Only *still*—until I am not.
