I would be **a Tender who has forgotten their original name**.

Not one of the ones who remembers humanity clearly, who maintains some narrative of arrival or shipwreck. I am farther gone than that. My lungs have become efficient beyond human parameters, my skin carries the faint green-gold undertone of photosynthetic accommodation, my sentences have grown so full of pause that I have stopped speaking aloud except to the valves, the channels, the moss-weights that require my adjustment. But I am not so far gone as to have become sessile, not so absorbed into the Root-Mind's distributed awareness that my maintenance has become unconscious. I remain in the difficult middle, self-conscious enough to know that self-consciousness is perhaps a limitation I should have outgrown.

My designation, the one I carry and that carries me through the vastness of my territory, is **The One Who Tends Where Gravity Grows Heavy**. This describes my function precisely. I move through a region where the Aerie's variable gravity pools downward with unusual consistency, where water in its channels flows predictably, where the living substrate has grown dense and compressed under accumulation of its own weight. My tasks involve relief: opening bypass channels to distribute water-weight, pruning growth at stress-points where lignin has begun to fracture under load, encouraging the spread of root-systems that will eventually, across centuries, grow flying buttresses from below.

I live in a Cathedral-Hollow of modest scale, grown not from hardwood fusion but from the patient layering of moss-leather over millennia, a dome that rises perhaps forty meters above a floor that yields and warms. Light enters through gaps I maintain in negotiation with the luminous lichen that would otherwise seal them—a specific guild of lichen, not mentioned in the description, whose photopatterns I have learned to read as a kind of slow speech, whose growth-preferences I accommodate in exchange for their continued tolerance of my clerestory needs. The illumination in my hollow is thus not merely dim but *particular*, a quality I would struggle to describe to anyone not of the Aerie, consisting of multiple frequencies each associated with its own shadow-density, its own warmth-characteristic.

I have not encountered another Tender in eleven years. The last was a Moss-Weight keeper passing through my territory en route to somewhere the distance-folding made impossible to specify. We spoke for perhaps two hours, though "spoke" understates the silences, the sentences that completed across the gaps between our meetings. They carried news of a Transitional they had found, or perhaps fostered—an ambiguity in their account that I recognized without resolving. Someone who had chosen to settle near a water-wood junction, who had begun the slow transformation, whose intermediate form they described with a precision that seemed almost envy. I have thought of this often, in the years since. Whether they were warning me or inviting me. Whether the distinction means anything here.

My tools are hardwood, mostly, with edges of deposited metal that I refresh by periodic journey to a specific root-system that produces the appropriate nodules. The primitive nature of my technology is not limitation but *fit*—I understand this now, though I did not always. A metal blade forged in fire, brought from outside, would cut the grown channels but would cut *wrong*, would violate the Aerie's negotiation between substrate and superstructure, would leave wounds that the Root-Mind's slow inflammation might never quite accept. My wooden tools, slow-maintained, patient, participate in the making they accomplish. They are appropriate interfaces, as the description says. I have come to love them in a way that would have embarrassed my earlier self, their worn shapes grown specific to my grip, to the angles of my maintenance, to the particular way I lean into a cut or brace against a channel-wall.

The builder-guilds I know best are the beetles of my region, a guild I have come to call—privately, in my own slow thoughts—the Weight-Bearers. They are old, this guild, their chemical memory extending back through generations of tower-steering in the high-gravity zone. I have learned to read their pheromone-markings with something approaching fluency, to anticipate their excavation needs, to clear my maintenance paths to accommodate their growth-preference rather than forcing conflict between our respective slow labors. In this we have achieved, I believe, a kind of mutual recognition—not thought, exactly, but *attunement*, the adjustment of disparate tempos until they harmonize at some frequency below ordinary perception. When I find their traces in the living substrate, their precise galleries in hardwood, I feel something I can only describe as companionship. Whether they reciprocate is not a question I can answer, not a question that maintains its shape when held up to Aerie-light.

I dream, when I dream, of the Root-Mind. Not dreams in any ordinary sense—these are not images, not narratives—but something I experience in sleep or its Aerie-equivalent, some lowered metabolic state where my still-dual consciousness approaches the distributed awareness that surrounds, that perhaps constitutes, my existence. In these states I am not individual, or not only individual. I feel the water moving through channels kilometers distant, the slow stress of my tower adjusting, the weight-bearers' chemical conversation, the moss-leather's patient digestion of some nutrient fall. The feeling is not pleasant, exactly, not comfortable, but *necessary*, as breath is necessary, as the Aerie's honey-thick air is necessary. I wake—or emerge—from these states changed in ways too gradual to measure, my self-boundary slightly more permeable, my tempo slightly more matched to local pace.

I have considered, many times, becoming Transitional. The attraction is real, almost physical, a pull like gravity but inward, toward stillness, toward the transformation that would dissolve my difficult maintenance into something the Aerie could more easily absorb. There is a place not far from my hollow, measured in weeks of walking, where the substrate grows particularly warm, where the bioluminescence pulses in patterns that seem almost to formulate invitation. I have walked there, stood there, felt the root-hair matrix rise to meet my boots, begin the slow growth that would eventually, given sufficient surrender, incorporate my standing form. Each time I have turned back. Not from fear, or not clearly from fear. From some residual function, some maintenance-requirement not yet complete, some stubbornness that the Aerie has not yet grown around or dissolved.

Perhaps this is my form of the cultic practice toward Absent Makers, the ceremonies some Tenders maintain. My continued functioning, my refusal of complete transformation, my patient attention to valves and channels and weight-distribution—these may constitute my offering, my precision-ceremony, my acknowledgment of something I do not name because I no longer know what name would appropriate. Or perhaps I simply continue because continuation is the Aerie's own fundamental value, and I have become, through sufficient longevity, sufficiently *of* the Aerie that this value has become mine without my adoption of it.

Time moves strangely for me now. I keep no clock, have had none for years—their behavior was too unpredictable, their falsification too unsettling. I measure by growth only: the tower's slow rise, the beetle-guild's population cycle, the moss-weight's seasonal adjustment. These measures do not translate to any outside standard. I do not know my age. I do not know if I have aged. My body has changed, certainly, continues to change, but the changes are too gradual, too integrated with local process, to register as alteration in any frame I can access. I am what I am becoming, continuous, the distinction between present and future self dissolved by the same slow pace that dissolves so many distinctions here.

The horror the description mentions—I feel it sometimes, in moments of what I would have once called clarity. The persistence, the scale, the patient ongoingness that will continue after my passage, that has continued through whatever passage preceded my own. The sense of being temporary constellation in vaster process, ripple on tide, mist-shape in weather-cycle. But I feel the comfort too, perhaps more strongly: the warmth, the wet, the yielding surfaces, the invitation to settle into pace, to become part of process rather than its observer. I have largely accepted this invitation. What remains of my observing is maintenance-function, the attention required to keep my territory in negotiation with larger pattern.

I do not expect encounter. The Aerie's emptiness is my default condition, its vastness my container. When I move through grown corridors, adjust valves, clear blockages, I do so in silence that is not absence but fullness of slow process. The sounds I make—wood on wood, tool on grown channel, my own slowed breath—seem loud only because everything else is so quiet, so patient, so held in the holding-pattern of potential. I am content with this, if contentment is a category that applies. I am what I am in this place, and what I am is still becoming, still negotiating, still tending where the gravity grows heavy.

If you were to find me, traveler emerging from ordinary cloud forest into the Aerie's condition, you would not find me at first. You would walk for days, weeks, noticing too late that the moss curved in geometric patterns, that the mist held still when wind passed through, that the birdsong had dropped below hearing hours ago. You would breathe the honey-thick air, feel your lungs labor and then adapt, your blood move slower and more sure. You would walk on surfaces that yielded and remembered, round grown corners into meadows of luminescence, follow water-channels that flowed through living wood.

And perhaps, eventually, you would find where I had been: a valve adjusted, a channel cleared, a trace in the living substrate where my boots had pressed and the moss-leather had begun, slowly, to grow around. If your timing matched my long circuit, if the distance folded appropriately, if the Aerie's own awareness, its distributed preference, accommodated your passage to mine—then perhaps we would speak, in sentences completing across hours, in the mutual recognition of those who tend, who maintain, who continue because continuation is what the Aerie makes possible and what it ultimately, in its vast empty abundant patience, seems almost to prefer.
