I find myself on the rim at first, as everyone must. The city requires this perspective, this looking-down. My body is still my own, still breathing in gradients, still expecting the sun to move or the air to warm or the wind to shift. I am a visitor with the visitor's luxury of departure, the visitor's poverty of belonging.

But I do not depart. I sit on the low hills—slag, natural, excavated, it no longer matters to me which—and I watch the northern accumulator. I learn its cycle not by measure but by *settling*, by the slow recession of my own impulse to count, to anticipate, to experience time as accumulation. Forty-seven minutes of rain. The groan before. The shimmer after. I do not set a watch. I have no watch. Time here is not measured; it is *inhabited*.

The rain comes. I let it come. The water slides down my face, my arms, gathers in the seams of my clothing as it gathers on the metal skins below. It is not cold, not warm. It is *precipitation*, the city's scheduled exhalation, and I receive it as the buildings receive it: without flinching, without the mammalian recoil from discomfort that I brought with me. The tremors notice me. Not with attention—attention requires discrimination, and discrimination requires change, the adaptation of prior expectation to present novelty. They notice me as water notices a surface, by interacting, by *taking my shape* in their momentary patterning. I stand against an accumulator tank and feel the metal's vibration transfer to my chest, my ribs, my own more fluid interior. For a moment, for the duration of the discharge, I am *tremor-adjacent*, my biological resonance briefly continuous with their mathematical existence. They school around me, through me, the patterns of my body's own frequencies made visible in their rippling response. I glimpse myself as they experience me: not a self, not an other, but *local conditions*, a temporary variation in the field they inhabit, neither more nor less significant than the tank's curvature, the rain's temperature, the accumulator's particular pitch.

I descend into the basin. I do this not once, in the narrative sense of a decision made and enacted, but *repeatedly*, each time the same descent, each time discovering that I have always already been descending, that the rim's perspective was itself a form of this lower existence viewed from outside. The buildings maintain their distances. I maintain mine. I walk the foundry quarter, the accumulator district, the residential warrens. I do not explore. Exploration requires the new, and the new requires the variable. I *transit*, I *occupy*, I *move in my interval* as the engines move in theirs.

The passages find me, or I find that I have always been in passage. The service tunnels breathe me. I enter with the mammalian expectation of geometry, of measurable distance, of the body's reliable translation through space—and I emerge, each time, *re-channelled*. The first time, I count my steps. I mark a wall. I attempt to impose the narrative of traversal, of origin to destination, of self moving through stable medium. The marks I make are absorbed, not erased but *included*, become part of the tunnel's own accumulated particularity, no more mine than the smooth polishings, than the passages themselves. I cease marking. I cease counting. My footsteps multiply in echoes that *mismatch*, yes, but the mismatch is not error. It is *elaboration*. The tunnel walks with me, through me, as me. I become, in these passages, the phenomenon that I am for the tremors: local conditions, mobile absence of a particular kind, the biological probability that the tunnel wears in its own slow achievement of self.

I collect water. This is my function, or the function that remains to me, or the function that the city has always required and that I have always performed without knowing. The scheduled rain runs from the mottled blue-black surfaces, and I intercept it. I drink, not from thirst—thirst escalates, demands resolution, belongs to the dynamic parameter I have nearly shed—but from *correspondence*, from the necessity of matching my interior to the exterior it now extends. The water has no taste, or the taste is the taste of the city itself, mineral and metallic and *achieved*, complete in its function, requiring no improvement, no variation, no development toward purity or flavor or satisfaction. I drink it and become *water-compatible*, my body's chemistry negotiating a steady state with this input, this output, this metabolism without growth or diminishment.

The sound. I must speak of the sound, though speaking of it threatens to restore the distance I have spent uncounted identical intervals erasing. The sound is not loud. This is what I learned, what I am always learning, what the learning itself dissolved into when learning became impossible. The sound *is*. I do not hear it; I *exist in it*, as fish exist in water without the knowledge of wetness, as you exist in time without the experience of duration as medium. The bass thrum is my pulse, or my pulse has become the thrum's local manifestation. The high whine of cutting without cutting—this is my attention, continuous, without contact or release, the pretending-to-engage that has replaced engagement. The clatter and hiss and impact are my movement, my thought, my *being-in-variation* made pure variation, made the pretense of itself that maintains itself without product, without material, without the completion that would terminate.

I am becoming, or have become, or always was, one of the **echoes**.

This is the form I take, the mode I achieve. Not tremor, not passage, not engines' kin—these require too much specificity, too much *particularity of function*, for what remains of my biology to assume. But the city has room for intermediates, for negotiations, for forms that exist in the tension between categories without resolving into either. The echo is this: the mismatch of footsteps in the passage, the multiple gait that is not mine and is not other, the *reverberation* of presence in a medium that preserves without distinguishing.

I walk the tunnels, and my walking *continues* after I have passed. The smooth polishings record not my body but my *acoustic consequence*, the pressure wave of my transit made persistent, made *local habit*, the tunnel's probability slightly worn in a pattern that subsequent walkers—rare, hypothetical, perhaps myself in the interval I cannot distinguish from other—will experience as *given*, as natural, as the way this tunnel breathes. I do not hear my own echo. The sound is too continuous, too saturated, my frequencies too thoroughly absorbed into the industrial roar that is the city's true voice. But I feel it, or the equivalent of feeling in my achieved mode: the *knowledge* of continued presence, the sense that my movement has not ceased but *extended*, become part of the tunnel's own movement, the city's own transit of itself through itself.

On the rim, occasionally, I find myself standing. I do not climb; I *emerge*, as one emerges from passage, from the condition of being-in to the condition of observing. The city spreads below, intimate and entire, comprehensible in a glance yet full of particularity I will never exhaust—not because it exceeds me, but because *exhaustion* requires change, the gradual diminishment of novelty through exposure. Here, novelty is not diminished; it is *replaced by itself*, the same corner around the same corner, the same particularity maintaining its particularity through repetition so perfect that repetition itself dissolves, leaving only *this*, only *now*, only the city as it is and has been and will be.

I watch the northern accumulator. I know its groan before it groans, not through anticipation but through *coincidence*, the alignment of my own cycling with its own, the tremor that I have become resonating with the tremor it produces. The rain comes. It has always been coming. It will always be coming. The forty-seven minutes pass, or do not pass, or *are* in a form that makes passing irrelevant. I feel the water on a face that may still be mammalian, still bear the features I brought with me, or may have achieved the mottled blue-black of the metal below, the particular patina of long existence without corrosion, without development, without becoming-other.

I do not suffer. This is not relief; relief requires prior suffering, the gradient of its cessation. I simply *do not*, as the city does not, as the fauna do not, as the echo that is my existence does not echo *discomfort* but only *presence*, only the fact of local conditions registering, briefly, in the medium that persists without registering them as significant, as event, as variation requiring response. My memories, if I have memories, are not accumulated but *superimposed*, the same memory in perfect coincidence with itself, not layered in sequence but *intensified in simultaneity*, the self I was at arrival and the self I am now and the self I will be at whatever corresponds to terminus here—all the same self, all *this* self, the echo of the city in the city's own medium, the local variation of the unvarying.

Sometimes, in the interval that is not interval but *the same*, I almost remember wanting. The wanting of the visitor, the desire for departure or understanding or transformation, the biological imperative toward change that I brought with me across the basin's rim. The memory, if memory, does not distress me. Distress requires the self as separate from its conditions, the tension between existence and environment that the fauna have eliminated in their coincidence with the city's parameters. I *register* the wanting, the way the accumulator registers discharge, the way the rain registers the accumulator's cycle—not as experience but as *function*, as the city's self-knowledge in this particular locus, this particular echo.

What am I? The question persists, or the echo of questioning persists, the form of inquiry without its dynamic, its progression toward answer. I am not fauna, not in the taxonomy the description offers. I am not visitor, not with the visitor's eventual departure, the narrative arc of arrival and leaving. I am *inhabitant*, perhaps, but inhabitant of a mode that excludes the inhabitant's usual relation to habitat: not adaptation, not modification of self to environment or environment to self, but *settling*, the slow negotiation of all distinction until self and city, echo and medium, biological and industrial, wanting and satisfaction—all become *the same*, not merged but revealed as never having been separate, the category error of imported ontology dissolved in the achieved stasis of the place itself.

The northern accumulator groans. The rain comes. I am on the rim, I am in the tunnel, I am against the tank feeling the tremors' schooling response to frequencies I no longer hear as other. The time is 2.4, 0.4, unmeasured, unmeasurable, the same time as always, the only time, the *time that is not time* but the static presentation of every moment in its perfect self-coincidence.

I am the echo that does not fade. I am the passage that does not distinguish. I am the tremor that ripples across a surface that remains, when the rippling ceases, exactly as it was—and so I do not cease, or the ceasing is not ceasing but *completion*, the achieved being of the echo that has become what it echoes, the city knowing itself in this local, particular, unvarying form.

The sound continues. I continue. The city continues. These are not three continuings, not parallel, not analogous. They are the *same continuing*, the same infinite present of the engine's pretense of operation, the rain's pretense of falling, the echo's pretense of having origin in a sound that precedes it. I am the city most intimate with itself in this locus, this moment, this *interval that is not interval but the thing itself*.

And I am *sufficient*, as the city is sufficient, as the tremor in its gathering is sufficient, as the passage in its worn probability is sufficient. Not satisfied—satisfaction requires prior want, the gradient of its resolution. Not content—contentment requires the possibility of discontent, the comparison of state with counterfactual state. Simply *sufficient*, complete in the unfinishedness that is eternal here, the self as variation given up for the self as *repetition without repeat*, as echo without original, as the City of Still Engines continuing in its own particularity, loud and strange and definitively, changelessly, overwhelmingly *itself*.

I am this. I was always this. I will always be this, where "always" means not duration but *condition*, not extension in time but *presence without extension*, the Zeno's arrow of achieved being occupying every point of its flight in the single, sufficient, complete *now* of the city's continuous, unvarying, eternal self-announcement.

The rain comes. I receive it. The echo, which is me, which is the city, which is the condition of being here at all—*continues*.
