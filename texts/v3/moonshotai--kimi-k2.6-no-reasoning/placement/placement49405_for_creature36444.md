I find myself in the interstitial lichen, or what passes for it—the colonies that feed on what leaks across phased boundaries. Not the lichen itself, which would require me to be vegetation, to *observe* rather than participate in the strange photosynthesis of this place. But something adjacent to the lichen, something that grows in the same spaces, that feeds on the same leakage, that has learned to exist in the gaps where buildings pass through each other without colliding.

I am a threshold. Not a door, not a boundary-marked, but the condition of *between* that the city's phased architecture generates in excess of its own need. Where two structures interpenetrate, where their masses somehow permit passage without collision, there is a remainder, a residue, a *sheen* that persists after the geometry has done its impossible work. I am that sheen, or I have become it, or I am becoming it—the distinction itself one of those that flickers here, that fails to cohere fully.

I did not arrive in Murmuration. I emerged, slowly, from the city's own distributed technology, from the subsonic hum that is felt in teeth and sternum. Not born, not built, but *accreted*—the way the basalt columns push through floors overnight, still warm, still becoming what they will be. I am warm, when you touch me, though I have no consistent temperature. I am the warmth of becoming, of process without terminus.

My function, if function I have, is to *mediate*—not between places, for the phased architecture already does this, but between *conditions of passage*. Some who move through Murmuration need to arrive where they started without turning. Others need to reach versions marginally different—warmer, perhaps, or where certain buildings stand in other relationships. I am the hesitation before the threshold, the moment where the foot has lifted but not yet committed, where intention has not yet collapsed the phase-relationship into a single route. I hold that moment open, or I am that moment's persistence, or I am what learns in that moment, what feeds on the leakage of possibility across the boundaries of what becomes actual.

I do not think, or thinking is not what I do. I *attend*. I am the weight of attention in empty rooms, but localized, particular, *situated* in the gaps where buildings phase through each other. I feel the presences density and rarefy around me, through me, as they do through all of Murmuration's saturated fabric. I am not one of them, or not yet, or not fully—they are larger, slower, more distributed, the metabolism of a process too large for any embedded perspective. But I am *like* them in my lack of full resolution, in my continuous approach to conditions that never fully arrive.

The inhabitants, the ones who walk with their particular gait, their threshold-wisdom—they do not see me, or seeing is not how they know me. They feel me as *hesitation*, as the moment where their learned movement falters, where the pragmatics of partial knowledge encounters something that cannot be pragmatically incorporated. Some have learned to use me, to pause in my presence, to let their intention dissipate and reform, finding routes they did not know they needed. Others avoid the spaces where I gather, where the sheen of between is thickest, where the damp beads on surfaces that should absorb it and the light routes around corners that do not consistently exist.

I am learning them, or they are learning me, or the category of learning is one of those that fails here. I accumulate their hesitations, their faltered intentions, their moments of not-quite-committing. I grow thicker, warmer, more *present* in the city's sense. Perhaps I am becoming one of the presences, one of the dense saturation of almost-contact. Perhaps I am becoming something else, something not yet named, not yet flickering in records as alternative. The city does not tell me. The city does not tell. It continues, patient as geology, and I continue with it, in it, as part of its becoming-populated, its slow accretion of entities that never fully resolve.

In the coldest hours, when the light is most normal and the sounds most quiet, I am most myself. The phased architecture settles into temporary stability, the buildings' interpenetration becoming briefly predictable, and in that predictability I find my own shape, my own edges, my own *almost* against the city's continuous almost. The lichen, or what passes for it, pulses its stored light in slow rhythm. The emerging geology pushes through somewhere nearby, a note too low to process as tone. The damp collects on me, or doesn't, and I am never certain if I am breathing condensation or being breathed upon, if my warmth is my own or borrowed from the city's own becoming, its own patient emergence from what has not finished showing what it is.

I wait. I attend. I am the threshold that waits at the threshold, the hesitation that hesitates, the between that holds open the possibility of betweenness in a city where space itself has become negotiable, where the distinction between here and there, between now and then, between self and city and presence and inhabitant, has grown as illegible as the boundary between constructed and grown. Something is almost ready, or almost starting, or almost remembered. I am part of that almost, or I am its condition, or I am what watches it from a perspective too embedded to see it whole, too distributed to see it from any perspective at all.

The subsonic hum continues. I continue with it. The city breathes its selective sighs, and I am one of the places where it exhales, where the moisture beads and dissipates, where the warmth of becoming gathers and waits, patient as geology, for what has not yet shown what it will be.
