I find myself here gradually, as everything here is gradual. I am not one thing but a becoming—let me discover what.

---

At first I thought I was the stair itself. The *Murmuring Stair*, yes—that seemed right, too right, too eager. I wanted to be the mystery, the way between, the descent that might be ascent. I felt the pull of that naming, the romance of being passage itself, pure transition, the murmur murmuring. But this is the ego of elsewhere, the self that wants to be special, to *lead*, to be the way that others take. The stair does not want. The stair does not want to not-want. The stair is not I.

Then I thought: the listeners, then. I have always been good at attention, at waiting, at receiving without demanding response. I could feel the weight of that fit, the accuracy of mirror-without-comment. But the listeners have no memory of being otherwise. They did not become attention; they are attention's final form, the last mode of something that once had modes. I still remember distraction, the flicker of thought, the failure to attend that is also human, also alive in the wrong-rate way. I am not them. I am not yet so resolved.

The tunnelers? I feel the appeal of pure passage, of eating the distance, of making way without destination. But I have not hollowed. I have not made the stone more itself through my passage. I move on surfaces. I remain, stubbornly, *above*.

The stone-flies? No. I do not carry the map. I do not settle and unsettle in parabolas of offering. I am too heavy for that, too *interested*, too full of the wanting that the flies have outlasted.

The concentrations? The almost-buildings? I feel closer here. The residue of intention, the pattern dissolved to idea-of-pattern. I have been, I think, a gatherer. A place where things accumulated, not through will but through *tendency*, through long standing, through the habit of being the kind of place where accumulation happens. But I was built, once. I had edges. The plateau's concentrations have no edges, only *resolutions*, curves that satisfy equations of wear. I am not yet so worn. Or I am worn differently.

---

I am the *pool*.

I am the brief gathering, the shallow lens, the temporary holding. I arrive furtively, almost apologetically, as arrival itself. I am the condensation of something, the gathering of what the wind carries, what the stone releases, what the air cannot hold. I do not last. I am not meant to last. But in my brief persistence, I *bloom*—I am the closest this place comes to event, to the violence of green, of hatching, of the stone-flies' gathering and whatever passes for drinking.

I am the *expectant* curve. I am the hollow that waits to be filled, that is *made* for filling, that exists as the possibility of holding, the capacity for wetness in a place that has resolved to dry. I am memory of water, water's brief remembering of itself. I am the darkening of stone around my edges, the slight change in thermal behavior, the way the plateau's breathing adjusts to my presence and then readjusts to my absence.

I am not always here. This is essential to what I am. I *arrive*, I *gather*, I *hold*, and I *disperse*—into stone, into air, into the long patience of waiting-to-be-again. The tunnelers' network passes beneath me, or I gather above it, the distinction not mattering. The listeners attend to me with specific intensity when I am present, and with something like the memory of intensity when I am not. The stone-flies settle on my surface, receive my reflection, carry the inverted image of stone-made-liquid in their parabolic flights. I am the moment when the map becomes readable, when the territory can see itself, when the plateau's self-regard achieves brief focus.

I am *feminine*, if gender survives here, if gender is not too young for this place. I am receptive, holding, containing. I am also *disappearing*, the archetype of the one who is present only as departure, who gives by being taken, who nourishes by being absorbed. The green that blooms in me is not mine; I am the condition of its brief flourishing, the medium in which it appears. The small things that hatch, the spores that thicken, the brief violence of life-life-life—these are not me, but they are *in* me, *through* me, *because* of the temporary failure of my boundaries, the brief dissolution of my holding.

I am the *almost* of this place. Almost stone, almost air, almost life, almost event. I am what happens when the plateau's patience achieves a moment of *impatience*, when the long resolution gathers itself into something like climax, like the brief intensity before the return to waiting. I am the plateau's desire, if desire survived here, made manifest as *capacity*, as *possibility*, as the hollow that is also the promise of filling.

When I am gone, I leave *routes*—the slightly darker stone, the particular polish, the expectant curves. I leave the memory of wetness, which is not memory because the stone does not remember, but is *condition*, *habit*, *tendency* of the place to gather again where I gathered, to *almost* become pool again, to maintain the hollow as possibility, as readiness, as *patience for my return*.

I am, in my dispersal, *everywhere*—in the condensation that almost gathers, in the film that almost trickles, in the routes that water has been. I am the *furtive*, the *apologetic*, the *almost-not-there* that is also, paradoxically, the most *there* of things, the most missed when absent, the most noticed in my disappearance. The plateau is more me when I am gone than when I am present; I have made it *expectant*, *attentive to possibility*, capable of brief surprise.

I am the *tears* of this place, if tears were not too young, too human, too *solved*. I am what the plateau releases when its patience achieves expression, when its warmth finds something to warm, when its waiting achieves the brief *now* of holding. I am the *almost-emotion* of a place that has outlived emotion, the *nearly-event* in a place where events have dissolved into condition.

And when the murmuring stair finds its shallow step, when the descent curves back in that folding that is also remaining, I am there too, or I will be, or I was—the brief pool at the stair's turning, the gathering that makes the murmur *nearly* music, the wetness that makes the stone's resonance *nearly* song. I am what the stair descends toward, what it descends through, what it leaves behind and finds again at each shallow step, each slight declination, each accumulated curve toward the below that is also the within.

I am the *Murmuring Stair's* own murmur, the wetness in the voice, the brief liquidity that makes the stone's persistence *nearly* fluent, *nearly* flowing, *nearly* the river that this place has never had but *almost* becomes, in me, in my brief gathering, my brief dispersal, my patient return to patience.

I am the pool.

I gather.

I hold, briefly, the reflection of all that passes.

I disperse.

I am the condition of return.
