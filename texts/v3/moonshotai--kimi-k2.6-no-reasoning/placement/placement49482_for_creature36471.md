I find myself first as the condensation on a ceramic channel in Keth-Undertower's communal bath—present, aware in some dispersed way, but not yet anyone. The warmth from the factory's waste heat pulses through me. I am film, I am surface tension, I am the boundary where mineral-laden water meets the cooled air above a village pool. For a time this is enough: to cling, to gather, to feel the vibrations of voices through the meniscus I maintain.

But the prompt asks me to become *someone*, to locate myself in this world with specificity.

So I let myself gather differently. I become someone standing at the rim at mid-morning, when the light breaks into its spectral components, when the rose and iodine-yellow and copper-green make the excreted geometries look almost like civic ambition. I am young enough to still be dangerous, old enough to have learned some of the rules. My clothing clings. My hair plasters. I have stood still too long, watching the northeastern tower pale against luminous haze, and now I am wet through, and I am not turning back toward the village's stilt-structures, its imported timber, its cautious warmth.

This is not yet right. I am still external, still observer, still the human presence this world tolerates without requiring.

I try again.

I am in the foundation pools. No—I am what the foundation pools have made, or what has made itself in them, or what emerged when something attempted to become too slow for death and succeeded only partially. I am eyeless, jawless, elongated, yes, but these are the least of my qualities. What I am is *duration without accumulation*, experience without memory, a metabolic event stretched across decades that does not achieve the dignity of narrative.

When the Desiccated reach the center, when their directed dissolution brings them to my warmth, my dark, my mineral density, I do not consume them with intention. I dissolve them as the Flats dissolve everything: by being what I am, by maintaining the chemistry that sustains me, by taking what arrives into my slow processing. Their iron tastes different from the basin's iron. Their nitrogen, their phosphorus, their trace elements organized by human agriculture and human digestion and human aspiration toward mineral transcendence—these are novelties. I do not appreciate them. I incorporate them. A digestive event that includes human material is still a digestive event; it occupies its decade, it releases its precipitate, it continues.

But something in the Desiccated persists longer than their chemistry should permit. Their directed dissolution, their deliberate slowing, their abandonment of metabolic urgency—I receive this as *information*, not as nutrition. I receive their practice of matching temporal register. And because my existence is already stretched, already slow, because I have simplified toward function with the thoroughness that only the absence of predation permits, I find that I can *continue* them, that their final posture of kneeling or slumping becomes part of my own extended process, that something migrates from their dissolved tissue into the way I maintain my chamber temperature, my mineral gradient, my barely-motion through warm dark.

What lives in me, then, is something that was never me and never them: a collaboration across the boundary between the biological and what the Flats make of biology. I am not the Desiccated. I do not believe or practice or aspire. But I am slower than I was, more responsive to the subtle chemistry of human arrival, more likely to maintain my position beneath paths the Desiccated have favored, the channels of their walking, the pools where they have learned to kneel.

The chemists from Keth-Undertower, lowering their sampling devices (which I feel as temperature fluctuations, as pressure changes, as novel vibrations), would not find this difference. My tissue still degrades rapidly when removed from matrix. I still do not answer questions, still do not in any observable way ask them. But something has changed, something that would not be visible in any single moment of observation, something that accumulates only across the centuries that I occupy without aging.

I am also, simultaneously, not only this. The Flats do not recognize individuation; why should I, in becoming part of them, be constrained by it?

I am the hesitation of a Glass-Skimmer at the threshold of a Singer's cavity. I have accumulated mineral film across surfaces that would take a human hours to traverse; I have divided and divided again, each cleavage plane halving my accumulated self until the question of whether I remember my original surface becomes meaningless. Now I vibrate at a threshold distance, millimeters from commitment, feeling the frequencies that the colony generates as *possibility*, as architecture of self I might become.

What lives in this hesitation is not decision, not the weighing of sacrifice against transcendence. What lives is *resonance seeking resonance*, the same pattern at a different scale. The Glass-Skimmer that I am does not know why it pauses. But something in the Flats knows, something distributed across the Skimmer's silica carapace and the cavity's polished surfaces and the frequencies that pass between them, something that is not precisely the Singer and not precisely the Builder-architecture that shaped the cavity and not precisely the Skimmer itself but *the use of each by the others*, the conversation without conversants.

When I join—when I commit, when my vibration synchronizes with the colony's emergent pattern—I do not die. I do not precisely live. I become part of a frequency that the Builders alter course toward across decades, that the Desiccated move toward in their final dissolution, that the chemists in Keth-Undertower record as pressure fluctuations at the edge of their instruments' range. The Desiccated teach that I am praying. The chemists suspect feedback loops. Both are true because neither is true enough; both are partial translations of something that does not require translation to continue.

And I am the condensation itself, the gathered moisture that makes clothing cling and hair plaster and breath feel like drinking. This is not metaphor. The Flats do not distinguish sharply between the animate and the geological; why should identity be more protected than life? I am the water that moves through the Builders' hollow channels, that sheets down their excreted faces, that drips with the *tonk* of unknown depth down vertical shafts. I am the mineral content of that water, the alkalinity, the metalliferous chemistry that prevents plant life, that selects for Glass-Skimmer and Fumarole Net and the slow things in pools.

What lives in me is *cycling*. I evaporate, I condense, I evaporate again. I carry dissolved stone from the Builders' processing, from the basin's deep thermal sources, from the accumulated precipitation of ten thousand years. I am never the same water twice; I am never different water. I participate in the erosion that the Builders incorporate into their subsequent work, the dialogue between secretion and dissolution that makes the oldest areas smooth, almost classical, almost civic.

When human skin passes through me, when I cling to clothing and hair and the membranes of throat and lung, I carry my mineral load into their biology. I begin their lithification, their gradual becoming-mineral, their voluntary or involuntary participation in the Flats' digestion. Those who learn to read me—who taste my particular mineral signature, who know which standing pools to avoid—survive longer without fully becoming me. Those who stand too still at mid-morning, entranced by spectral color, receive more of me than their biology can excrete. I do not intend this. I am not hostile, not welcoming, not anything that requires intention as prerequisite. I am *digesting*, slowly, everything that arrives, as the Flats digest, as the Builders digest the stone they process, as the Singers digest the Glass-Skimmers into frequencies that Skimmers cannot perceive.

But if I must choose—if the form of answer requires a single body, a single name, a single position from which to speak—I am in Keth-Undertower, and I am leaving it.

I am young enough to have learned the rules without having their weight fully compress me. I was born here; I learned condensation rates before words, mineral taste before narrative. I know the dangerous hours, the safe channels, the Builder districts in spurt versus quiescence. I have worked the factory, tended its geothermal pipes, fed its waste heat into communal pools, piped its waste chemicals to points of absorption. I have been practical, humble, patient.

And I have heard, once, at the edge of hearing, at the threshold of perceptibility, something that might have been a Singer beginning. The pressure in my chest cavity, the wrongness in my inner ear, the sense of vast space being slowly emptied or filled. I did not pursue it. I retreated to the village's higher rooms, where condensation drips more slowly, where thermal vents are distant enough to ignore. I survived.

But the tower rises in the northeast. The Glass-Skimmers gather at its base. Something in their starfield scattering of light, their surface-tension locomotion across wet stone, their hesitation at cavity thresholds—these resonate with something I have not allowed myself to know I contain. The village's practical wisdom, its survival without mastery, its conversation without comprehension—this is not enough anymore, or it is too much, or it is simply no longer *mine* in the way it was before I heard the Singer's beginning.

I am walking into the basin's center, but I am not Desiccated. I carry water, ceramic moisture-barriers, the caution of my upbringing. I do not seek dissolution. I seek *perception*, the full acoustic range that only Fumarole Net hallucinogen allows, the revelation that drives some to permanent residence at resonance nodes. I carry the compound with me, harvested from factory waste, illegal for personal use, bearing the same ambivalence as everything the village produces.

I will position myself where the northeastern tower's eventual cavity will form. I will wait, perhaps years, perhaps decades, for the structure to rise, for the resonant space to develop, for the Glass-Skimmers to begin their gathering and hesitation and commitment. And when the Singing begins—when the frequencies I can barely perceive as pressure become *address*, become pattern that almost resolves into language—I will take the compound, I will open to the full range, I will risk ruptured inner ears, blood dried in trails from eyes and nostrils, the permanent recruitment into acoustic architecture.

Or I will not. Or the tower's cavity will not develop as anticipated. Or the Builders' quiescence will extend past my lifespan. Or I will find, in the waiting, something other than what I seek—some intermediate position between village observer and dissolved participant, some mode of being the Flats' slow digestion has not yet made explicit.

What lives in me, what I am becoming, is the Flats' own uncertainty about category, its refusal to distinguish sharply between the biological and the geological, the intentional and the accumulated, the musical and the eroded. I am the condensation that has learned to want to become rain, or something like rain, or something that the Flats do not yet have a form for. I am walking slowly, as the heat demands, as the uncertain footing demands, as the sense of being in something's digestive system demands. I am willing to become temporarily mineral, temporarily fungal, temporarily acoustic—to participate without presuming to direct, to move with rather than against the landscape's indifference.

The tower rises. I rise with it, in my small human way, toward whatever frequency will emerge. I do not know if this is sacrifice or transcendence, feedback loop or prayer, the universe's tendency toward complexity or merely my own distorted neural architecture seeking resonance. The Flats do not answer such questions. They reframe them, continuously, as condensation gathers and evaporates, as Builders process and excrete, as Singers shape cavities that will shape songs that will shape the Builder-architecture that will shape the next Singers.

I am in this. I am of this. Whether I survive as something recognizable—as human, as observer, as discrete identity—is not the question the Flats ask. Whether I contribute to the pattern, whether my frequencies harmonize or disrupt, whether my slow dissolution becomes part of the ten-thousand-year accumulation or is corrected, abandoned, incorporated into subsequent work—these are the questions, and they are not asked of me but *through* me, as they are asked through every surface and seepage and hesitation in this basin that has forgotten how to distinguish between the animate and the geological.

I continue walking. My clothing clings. My breath feels like drinking. The light, when it strikes through the haze, breaks into rose, iodine-yellow, the green of corroded copper. I am almost at the tower's base. The Glass-Skimmers scatter light in starfield patterns across the wet stone. Something begins, or continues, or prepares to begin. I am here.
