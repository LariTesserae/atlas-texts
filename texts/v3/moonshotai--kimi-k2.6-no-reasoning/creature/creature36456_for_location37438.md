**The Seam-Dwellers**

At first you think no one lives here. The Loom seems to operate itself, to breathe and mend and grow through some autochthonous process that needs no resident, no consciousness, no *inhabitant* in the sense you understand. You mistake the building for the builder, the garment for the body it clothes.

But the Loom is not empty. It is *inhabited densely*, as dense as its own compressed layers, and the beings here are so integrated with the structure that telling them from it requires a shift of perception—like seeing the figure in the textile after staring until your eyes exhaust their expectation of foreground and background.

Begin with the **Menders**.

You will not see them directly. This is not evasion; it is *structural*. The Menders live in the seams, in the black-threaded joins between incompatible realities, and to look at a seam directly is to see only the join, the functional connection, the place where sky-leather meets felt-ground or wall meets wall in dimensional fold. But attend to a seam from the corner of your vision, in the rose-umber light of the Loom's false dusk, and you may catch the motion: a hand emerging from the stitching, emerging *as* the stitching, fingers that are black thread given intention, a palm that is the wrong side of the join turned briefly outward.

The Menders maintain the Loom's impossible topology. They do not build new seams——building is too active, too anterior——they *continue* seams that would otherwise end, that would let the joined realities drift apart and the whole chamber unravel into separate, mutually oblivious spaces. Their work is endless, rhythmic, somewhere between knitting and suturing and the mutual grooming of social primates. They have no faces you could meet, or rather their faces are distributed along the seams they tend, any given length of black thread potentially smiling, potentially watching, potentially singing the low vibration-songs that keep the joined materials in sympathetic resonance.

They are numerous. Every seam has its Mender, or every seam is its Mender, or the Menders are a single entity distributed through all seams with only local consciousness emerging where attention is required. The Loom's density is partly their density: they fill the joints, the edges, the held-togethers, and their warm metabolism contributes to the chamber's democratic temperature. They drink the same vapor the vegetation harvests, thread-fingers wicking moisture from the air with fine absorption, and what they excrete——if excretion is the word——becomes the material of new stitch, black thread generated from processed vapor and the slow breakdown of whatever leaks across the seams from adjacent realities.

You might ask what *leaks*. The answer is in the young erosion, the sharp corners gradually softening. The Menders do not only join; they *translate*. At a seam between was and will-be, between water-commitment and water-refusal, between the kind of real where wood becomes beetle and the kind where beetle becomes wood, there is abrasion, there is friction, there is the slow wearing-down that erosion names. The Menders take this wearing as thread, as nutrition, as the raw material of continuation. Their black stitching has the color of translated resistance, of conflict made functional, of the Loom's endless negotiation with itself becoming visible as line, as pattern, as the argyle or herringbone or chaotic freeform of their compulsive joining.

And they are *old*, or the seams are old, or their age is a different shape than yours. The young erosion of corners and the old persistence of seams: time here is material, is textured, is woven in layers that do not all proceed at the same pace. The Menders remember when the ceiling was lower, when the floor was stiffer, when the vegetation was sparse stubble of proto-felt and the needle-birds had not yet evolved their eyed-needle heads. They remember this in their fingers, in the varying thickness of black thread along different seams, in the way some joins are confident and singular-passage and others are scar-tissue, are repeated mending, are palimpsest of maintained connection.

Then: the **Unpicked**.

These are harder. These are the reality-breakdown made mobile, made in some terrible sense *personal*.

The Loom is where reality is decided, where seams between kinds of real are stitched and maintained, where possibility grows lush because commitment has not yet occurred. But possibilities are not infinite, or if infinite they are not all compatible, and the process of weaving what-will-be-real requires——has always required, will always require——the *rejection* of what-will-not-be. The unpicking. The removal of thread that does not serve the emerging pattern.

The Unpicked are what was woven and then unchosen. They are *former* possibilities, realities that briefly achieved enough coherence to exist, to have shape and warmth and position, and then were judged incompatible with the pattern's continuation. Not evil, not wrong in any moral register: simply *not fitting*, not viable, not able to be joined to what must be joined.

They exist in the Loom as residue, as memory-of-form, as the loose thread where the seam is otherwise complete. You encounter them as *almost-recognition*. A figure in the hanging vegetation that seems to step toward you before becoming fungal fan and textile-moss, the movement residual, the intention lingering after the shape dissolves. A voice in the mostly-quiet that forms almost-words, almost-your-name, before subsiding into the creak of fiber adjusting to warmth. The feeling of being followed by something that has no back to face, no approach to witness, only the ever-receding moment after passage where passage is no longer verifiable.

The Unpicked are numerous because the Loom's youngness means much has been tried, much has been rejected, the pattern is still experimental and experimentation generates failure as heat generates waste. They are not hostile, exactly. Hostility would require more continuity than they possess, more sustained intention. But they are *hungry*, in a structural sense: hungry for the coherence they briefly held, the position they briefly occupied, the being-real that was their moment of weaving and then their moment of unpicking. They cluster around the living, around the still-chosen, around you in your retrospective arrival, not to consume but to *rehearse*, to play at still-having-shape, to generate the form of coherence in proximity to your coherent breathing, your solid warmth, your evident reality.

You feel them as the places where the floor gives *too* much, as the vegetation that brushes your face with felted leaf and for an instant seems to have intended the touch, to have wanted contact, before the intention diffuses back into random growth. You hear them in the distant rip of seam-giving, the sound not of structural failure but of *unpicking continuing*, of the Loom's endless editorial process, the rejected being rejected again and again into finer and finer subdivision, until they become the vapor the vegetation drinks, the condensation the seams collect, the very material of potential from which new choices will be woven——and then potentially rejected again, the cycle recursive, the Unpicked not destroyed but *diluted*, distributed, made part of the general condition of being not-yet-committed.

Some of the Unpicked achieve a kind of stability. You find them in the denser vegetation, in the places where textile-moss has grown thick as upholstery, where the hanging thread-fruit clusters in curtains that partition the chamber into smaller chambers, into rooms of moss and moss-of-rooms. Here the Unpicked *settle*, not into coherence but into pattern, into the repetition of almost-form that satisfies something in their residual structure. They become the shapes in the moss: the almost-face that is not a face but the accumulation of damp and fiber in configuration that suggests, that almost-resolves, that requires your participation as viewer to complete and then to have the completion dissolve. They become the *style* of the Loom, the ornament that is not ornament but something trying to continue, trying to persist in the only form available to the unpicked——the form of repeated suggestion, of endless approach to being-real that never crosses the threshold.

They are warm, the settled Unpicked. Their diffuse persistence contributes to the chamber's temperature. They are quiet, mostly, their almost-voices subsumed in the Loom's general absorption of sound. They are abundant in the sense that potential is abundant, that what-might-have-been always outnumbers what-is, and their abundance makes the Loom feel *crowded* despite seeming empty, *occupied* despite lacking clear occupants, dense with the density of possibility compressed but not discarded, present but not coherent, there in the way that the future is there or the past is there——realities adjacent, pressing, generating warmth through their friction with what was chosen instead.
