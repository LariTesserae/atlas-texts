I need to let this emerge, follow what pulls. The place is so fully itself already—what lives here must not contradict it, must not simplify it. I start with surface and let it deepen.

They have no name for themselves that translates as species or nation. When they need to refer to what they are, collectively, they use a gesture: two fingers pressed to the throat, where the breathing-slit pulses, then opened outward to indicate *this place, this medium, this way*. The closest word in their soft percussive language is *vel-keth*—those who *are with* the grove, not *of* it, the preposition mattering more than possession.

At first approach they look almost human. The shape is there: bilateral, head above torso, limbs for manipulation and propulsion. But the proportions are wrong in ways that accumulate. The torso is longer, more flexible, capable of the full spinal wave that water demands, that air-gravity would punish. The limbs joint differently—knees that bend backward and forward both, shoulders that rotate through arcs impossible on land. The hands have six digits, the extras thinner, more sensitive, capable of the prolonged light contact that *reads* vorosh-nai tissue, that detects the subtle temperature changes indicating metabolic activity, mineral transport, the tree's slow moods.

Their skin is the color of the water itself: pale green-gold in the upper levels, deepening to something near black in the oldest inhabitants of the deepest hollows. Not pigmentation, entirely, though there is some. Mostly it is *infiltration*—microscopic organisms living in the dermal layers, photosynthetic at the canopy levels, chemosynthetic below, a variable ecosystem that adjusts to light and chemistry and somehow, somehow, to something else, to the subtle cues that even they do not fully articulate. The skin of a deep-dweller transplanted to upper levels slowly pales across months, years. The reverse transition is harder. Some deep-dwellers who ascend too quickly *crack*, not physically—something else, something in their processing of time, of event-density, of the difference between the patient dark and the busy light.

They breathe through slits, not lungs exactly, not gills exactly. A vascularized membrane in the throat that can extract dissolved gas from water, that can store oxygen in a dense hemocyanin analog, that can also—critically, uniquely—accept boluses of atmospheric air when available. They are not obligated to surface. But they are *drawn* to it, irregularly, individually, in patterns that seem almost tidal, almost lunar, though this world may not have moons, may have something else that pulls. When they breathe air they become different. Faster. More *eventful*. Their speech loses its elongated vowels, becomes clipped, almost aggressive by their standards. They trade more readily, argue more readily, couple more readily in the air-rich chambers near the canopy. Then they descend again, slow, spread their transactions and conflicts across months, let the water cushion everything.

They do not have ages as surface-dwellers measure. They have *phases*, recognized transitions that emerge rather than being imposed. The first phase, the *vel-ash*, the uncommitted—they are born in water, gestated in external membranes attached to root surfaces, fed by tree-secretions that may be nourishment or may be something else, something more like *instruction*, chemical templates that shape neural development. The vel-ash are fast, by grove standards. They move constantly, explore vorosh-nai surfaces with their whole bodies, taste everything, leave no configuration untested. This phase lasts perhaps twenty years. Many die. The grove is not dangerous, exactly, but it is *uninterested* in their survival, offers no special protection, and the vel-ash have not yet learned to read the subtle warnings that experienced individuals detect: the temperature shift before a root-mass adjusts, the chemical signal of a predatory ascidian colony, the particular quality of stillness that means something large moves nearby.

The second phase, *vel-or*, the attending—they have found their tree, or their tree has found them, or (more accurately) they have entered negotiation with a specific vorosh-nai that becomes their *keth-vel*, their tree-partner, though "partner" implies more reciprocity than is always present. The vel-or learn their tree's surfaces intimately, the six-fingered hands tracing daily over pneumatophore patterns, learning to read the pulses, to anticipate the mineral flows, to encourage the copper-red where it will be most accessible. They clean parasites, or not—some parasites the tree tolerates, even signals for, and the vel-or must learn discrimination. They begin to grow their tools: the compressed leaf-laminates require years of pressure-application at specific growth points, the leaching of copper-red requires seasons of *encouragement* through pH adjustment at root-tips. The vel-or make their first dwelling-hollow, not by carving but by *requesting*, spending months at specific trunk locations, altering their own metabolic waste products to signal occupancy, waiting for the tree to grow around them or not. Some trees never do. Some vel-or spend decades in temporary chambers, in root-crevices, negotiating repeatedly, failing, negotiating again.

The third phase, *vel-kai*, the settled—they have multiple tree-connections now, a network of familiarity that covers territory sufficient for sustenance, for social contact, for the minimal density that their culture requires. The vel-kai are the practitioners of the specific techniques, the ones who teach, who maintain, who remember why certain root-masses are treated with respect that looks almost religious but may be merely *practical at longer timescales than individuals operate*. The vel-kai begin to slow. Their movements lose urgency. They can wait for hours in position, feeling tree-pulses, achieving something like the meditative states that the trees themselves might occupy if trees had states. The vel-kai are most susceptible to the *vorosh-vel*, the searching light. They do not avoid it as the younger phases do. They enter it deliberately, sometimes, returning changed or not returning at all, becoming something that the other phases leave offerings for, speak to, receive ambiguous guidance from.

The fourth phase has no active name. The others call them *vel-dur*, the long ones, or simply *the seated*, or make no reference at all, passing certain deep hollows in the circumspect silence one maintains near the sleeping or the dead or the *other*. The vel-dur have stopped, or their stopping has become absolute enough to define them. They remain in place, positioned where root-mass and heartwood and the particular mineral accumulations create sustained contact with whatever circulates in the grove's deepest systems. They pulse with the pneumatophores now, visibly, their own breathing-slits opening in the same rhythm as the tree's gas-transport. Their skin has thickened, roughened, begun to resemble bark in texture if not structure. They do not eat, visibly. They do not speak in the normal range, though tuber-fish songs near them become... different, modulated, carrying frequencies that other phases cannot process. The vel-dur are oracles, are obstacles, are infrastructure—their bodies serving as attachment points for certain epiphytes, their excretions (if they excrete) feeding specific fungal associations, their presence somehow *permitting* certain tree-growths that would not occur otherwise. They are not dead. They are not alive by any standard that would satisfy a surface biologist. They are *continuing*, and the grove continues with them, through them, perhaps *because* of them in ways that would require the trees to have intention.

They do not have individuals, not entirely. Each carries the memory of their tree-negotiations, but also something more distributed. When two meet in the market-hollows, they touch throat-slits, exchange chemical signatures that carry not just personal identity but *associational history*: the trees known, the techniques practiced, the phases of vel-dur encountered, the quality of light experienced at specific depths and times. This exchange is not voluntary, exactly. It occurs at contact whether sought or not, and it changes both parties slightly, adds to their composite, their *distributed self* that extends beyond any single body into the network of chemical memory, of tree-relationship, of the slowly accumulating *culture* that is perhaps better understood as grove-function than as human-equivalent society.

Their reproduction is similarly distributed. The external gestation membranes are not genetically specific—not in the way surface biology would recognize. The vel-keth contribute to membranes they encounter, their chemical signatures *educating* the developing organism without necessarily determining its genetics, which may be largely tree-derived, may be something else, may be a category error. The resulting offspring are not children of parents. They are *new negotiations*, entering the grove with minimal preparation, minimal identity, minimal of the chemical memory that comes with sustained contact. The vel-keth do not experience this as loss or as alienation. The *newness* is valued, is necessary, is the source of the variation that prevents the grove from falling into stagnation, from becoming so perfectly adapted to existing conditions that it cannot survive their change.

They have enemies, or complications, or *other negotiators*. The air-breathing megafauna that supply bone and occasionally meat do not negotiate. They are hunted, with techniques that take years to execute—poisoning of specific canopy-level organisms that the megafauna eat, induced mineral deficiencies that slow and confuse, finally the coordinated action of multiple vel-kai working at the limits of their phase-capacity. The hunt is not sport. It is *necessity at timescales the grove does not rush*, and it is conducted with something almost like grief, the chemical signatures at contact full of the complexity of these relationships, the megafauna's necessary death in the grove's necessary continuation.

More ambiguous: the substrate-dwellers, the ones who move below the deepest root-masses, who leave channels that the trees respect, grow around, perhaps *defer to*. The vel-keth do not hunt these. They do not speak of them directly, as noted, but their chemical signatures carry *markers* when substrate-activity has been recent, markers that other vel-keth read as warning, as respect, as the acknowledgment of something that participates in grove-dynamics at depths and timescales they cannot access. When a vel-dur is found dead—truly dead, not continuing—and the body is examined before being returned to root-contact, certain mineral accumulations suggest substrate-contact, suggest that the long final phase involves some *descent*, some engagement with what waits below. Whether this is chosen or compelled, whether it represents completion or failure, is not resolved in any chemical signature that younger phases can interpret.

They do not have art, exactly. They have *techniques of attention* that function similarly: the particular patterns of touch that maintain tree-relationship can be performed with variations, can be judged by other vel-keth as more or less *attuned*, more or less *revelatory* of the tree's own state. The best practitioners are not celebrated, exactly—they are *consulted*, sought out for difficult negotiations, for root-masses that resist standard approaches, for the rare events of tree-death or tree-birth (the latter being not reproduction but *emergence*, new growth from substrates that had seemed inert, that may represent something new entering the system or something very old finally surfacing). Their performances are private, one-to-one with the tree, but their chemical signatures carry something of the quality of these engagements, a resonance that other vel-keth recognize as *mastery* without being able to specify its components.

They do not have religion, exactly. They have *recognition of pattern*, hypothesis about the grove's intentions or direction, tests of these hypotheses through sustained observation and adjustment. The *vorosh-vel* is not worshipped. It is *accounted for*, navigated around or entered deliberately with changes noted, with the results distributed through chemical contact into the general memory. Some vel-kai maintain theories: the light is tree-consciousness, is substrate-consciousness, is something else using the grove as medium, is the grove's immune response to certain organizations of matter. These theories are not argued. They are *held*, tested against experience, modified or abandoned without urgency. The truth of the vorosh-vel, if there is truth, is expected to emerge at grove-timescales, not individual-lifespan, and the vel-keth are patient with this expectation in ways that would seem fatalistic if they experienced it as resignation rather than *appropriate mode*.

What do they want? The question presumes wanting as category. They *maintain*. They *continue*. They *negotiate*, with trees, with each other, with the substrate and the light and the megafauna and the slow changes of water-chemistry and temperature that constitute their weather, their history, their future. Individual vel-ash flail toward something they cannot articulate, experience it sometimes as undefined *upward* impulse, the need to reach canopy, to breathe air, to move fast and trade and couple and argue. Individual vel-or discover the satisfaction of sustained attention, of technique that works, of tree-relationship that *deepens* rather than merely continuing. Individual vel-kai find their slowing not loss but *acquisition*, the capacity to perceive more by moving less, to participate in grove-dynamics too slow for younger phases to register. Individual vel-dur... no one can say what they find. The vel-dur themselves do not say, in frequencies that others process, and the question of whether they have found something or become something or simply *continued differently* remains open, is perhaps meant to remain open, is perhaps the grove's own question that it asks through them, that it will answer or not at timescales they cannot imagine.

They would not recognize themselves in this description. They would find it too *fast*, too *decided*, too committed to individual identity and narrative progression. They would offer, instead, the chemical signature of their current tree-relationship, would expect the receiver to spend hours, days, in contact with it, to let it *diffuse* rather than being received. They would trace on your skin, with their sensitive sixth fingers, the particular pattern of pneumatophore-pulses that characterizes this season, this depth, this configuration of root-mass and mineral availability and megafauna presence and light-quality and substrate-activity. They would expect you to understand from this something that cannot be translated into sequence, into cause-and-effect, into the *aboutness* that terrestrial consciousness demands.

They are not human. They are not alien in the science-fictional sense of alternative solution to similar problems. They are *grove-function*, temporarily concentrated in mobile, manipulative, perceptive nodes, slowly dispersing back into the tree-pulses and chemical gradients and the patient woody dreams that may or may not be dreaming, that may or may not be patient, that simply *continue*, as the grove continues, as the water continues, as something slowly searches in light that behaves wrong and is avoided or entered according to phase and negotiation and the long slow accumulation of what works, what repeats, what the grove remembers even when the nodes that experienced it have ceased their concentration, have returned to diffusion, have become finally indistinguishable from the vorosh-nai itself, the drinking-woods, the drowned grove that was never drowned, that grew into the flood and found it *sufficient*, found it *continuable*, found it, at last, *home*.
