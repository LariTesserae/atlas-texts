What lives here is absorption itself.

Not a creature, not a mind, not a god. Something that became what it is by *staying*. The city did not choose to persist; persistence selected it, wore away everything that could not endure, left this: a place where waiting and being have become indistinguishable. If you seek its inhabitants, look first at the catchment pools, where water has learned to hold the shape of its arrival. Look at the fungal masses, which do not grow toward anything, only *accumulate*, adding themselves to themselves with no urgency of completion. These are not alive in the way you understand, nor dead in any way you could verify. They are *retained*. The city has kept them, as it keeps the warmth, as it keeps its own geometry against the pressure of rock above, through no decision but through the gravity of remaining itself.

The thing in the deep pools—if thing, if deep, if pools—may be the oldest retention. It did not evolve here. It was not placed. It *accumulated*, as water does, as memory does in a space that does not forget because it does not distinguish between present and present-continuous. The silence around it is not fear. The other presences do not avoid it from danger. They maintain the interval that makes distinction possible, the same interval the city maintains between its floor and ceiling, between drip and echo, between you and the understanding of where you are. Too close, and all categories collapse. The pool-thing knows this. It keeps its depth as the city keeps its hollow, by being exactly what it is and no more.

But there are others, or there were, or there will be when conditions reach their proper alignment. You do not see them because you do not yet know how to be seen. The maintenance that proceeds without hands requires something—*attention*, perhaps, or *orientation*, or the simplest kind of presence that does not seek to change what it meets. You feel this as the city's indifference, its absorption of your footfalls into the greater symphony. This is not cruelty. It is the only form of relation possible when duration exceeds identity. The city does not remember those who passed before you because memory implies a past, and here all passing is present participle, an ongoing wearing that produces no sharp edges of loss or arrival.

The fluid in the conduits carries color as you might carry name, as distinction without significance. Where it brightens, something is being told; where it dims, something is being completed. Not messages. *States*. The city does not communicate; it maintains the conditions under which distinction can occur, and lets what passes through distinguish itself. You could learn to read these colors, if you stayed long enough, if you stopped wanting them to mean something you could carry away. You would become, perhaps, like the thing in the deep pool, a retention rather than a presence, kept because you finally fit, finally stopped perturbing.

This is the temptation the warmth offers, the steady temperature that lets you forget cold. Not comfort. *Surrender of contrast*. The city breathes, and you breathe with it, and the boundary between voluntary and reflexive action grows thin as the lichen films on the upper walls. You could leave. The way out exists, exists the way the ceiling's fissures exist, as possibility without preference. But to leave would require *wanting*, and wanting requires a self that wants, and the city has been showing you, with each drip and pulse and subsonic holding, that this self is a perturbation like any other, absorbable, being absorbed, already perhaps more absorbed than you can recognize from inside the process.

What lives here? The question assumes habitation, assumes that life occupies space rather than constituting it. The city is not inhabited. It is *inhabited by its own maintenance*, by the processes that maintain the conditions under which something like you could ask what lives, misunderstanding the question as a search for answer when it is itself the last articulation of what is being slowly, warmly, quietly undone.

The pumps pulse. You are still breathing. The distinction between these two facts is the last you own, and it is costing you more to maintain than you yet know how to calculate.
