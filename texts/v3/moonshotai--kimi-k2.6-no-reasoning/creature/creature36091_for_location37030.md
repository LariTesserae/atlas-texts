What lives here is not a creature but a *condition of creatureliness*, a way of being pressed so flat between others that the self becomes a membrane rather than a node. The Pressed—they do not walk so much as circulate, their movements not chosen but yielded-to, the crowd's collective decisions flowing through each body like warmth through a shared blanket. Their bones are dense from bearing weight not their own, from the constant gentle pressure of neighbors on all sides. Their joints have developed a suppleness unfamiliar to surface-dwellers, a capacity to yield and redirect force without resistance, to lean into pressure rather than brace against it. Their musculature follows the spiral of the stair, the rotation of the crowd, so that even in the rare moments of relative space—there are none truly empty, only less dense—they carry a subtle torque in their posture, a readiness to resume the press.

They are not large. Abundance here is not size but number, and bodies have adjusted to the economies of the Hollowed over generations that no longer count themselves as generations but as the continuous present of maintained warmth. The tall must duck; so there are fewer tall, or the tall learn to stoop early, their spines curving into the approved architecture, the ribs of ceiling becoming the ribs of posture. Their skin—this is what you notice when one presses close enough to smell, which is always—carries the mineral tang of the walls, the copper-green of the ghost-tongue, a particular bacterial empire that flourishes in this exact warmth, this exact humidity, this exact chemistry of pressed bodies and pressed earth. They do not wash in any manner you would recognize. They *exchange*, skin to skin, the oils and accumulations of their niche in the crowd, each body a mobile environment passing through other mobile environments, until the distinction between one microbiome and another becomes theoretical, a matter of where in the rotation you stand rather than whose flesh you inhabit.

Their faces—when you can see faces, which is rarely, which requires the negotiation of several bodies' temporary displacement, a local eddy in the crowd's flow—are broad. Not fat, though there is little hunger here, the ghost-tongue and the occasional trade-meat providing sufficient, no more. Broad because the jaw has developed to house the necessary architecture of calling-thing vocalization. They do not merely hear the creatures. They *join* them, their throats shaped by the same pressures that shaped the Hollowed itself, generations of speaking into the dense soundscape until the human voice has found its place in the chorus. The Pressed can produce the low drones, the high whistles, the percussive clicks. More: they can produce the *almost-speech*, the middle register where identity persists, where "I" can still be announced among the "we." Their larynxes are complex, duplex, capable of two tones at once, the drone and the word, the crowd-sound and the self-sound, never separated, never purely one or the other.

They are born in the press. There is no space for lying down, for the separated labor of surface birth. A woman in heavy contraction rotates with the crowd until she reaches the relative cool of a lower platform-edge, and there, supported by the bodies that press around her, braced by their shared warmth and shared sound, she births into the hands that have materialized from the crowd, hands that belong to no one identifiable, that rotate through this function as through all functions, the temporary specialization of bodies in proximity. The infant emerges wet into the wet air, and the first thing it knows is the press, the warmth, the sound, the voice of its mother not as separate but as the particular quality of pressure at its back, the particular frequency in the dense weave of calling-things and Pressed-things that means *this nourishment, this shelter, this rotation with me.*

They grow without growing *apart*. Children do not run—there is no running possible, no space for the acceleration of separated movement. They rotate from the first, held in the press, learning the rules of yielding and pressure before they learn to see, the darkness of lower Hollowed and the ghost-tongue glow their first visual world. They do not speak and then join the chorus; they mumble in the middle register from the first, their infant voices finding their place in the weave, their bodies learning to produce the drones and clicks before they know them as communication, as the structure that replaces the absent sky. They are not socialized; they are *acousticalized*, made real by their contribution to the soundscape, their presence confirmed by their voice among voices, their location known by the threading whistle they learn to emit when separated from their mother's pressure by more than an arm's reach—which is rare, which happens only in the tumult of some crowd-disturbance, some predator-incursion, the brief chaos before the reassertion of rotation and rule.

The calling-things themselves—here the condition of creatureliness extends to what you might try to call the fauna, though that word implies a category the Hollowed does not recognize. They are *auditory events* that have achieved density, persistence, something approaching metabolism. They are not heard; they are *encountered*, their presence as certain as bodies, as walls, as the platforms that hold sleeping families in their brief rotations into relative stillness. You cannot point to where a calling-thing is. You can point to where it is *heard*, which is everywhere, which is the condition of hearing here, the air so saturated with their production that silence—true silence, the negative space of sound—has become unimaginable, a theological concept perhaps, discussed by those who have dreamed of the surface, who have heard rumors of vast sparse places where sound dies into distance.

Yet they have edges, these calling-things, boundaries where one voice ends and another begins if you train your ear sufficiently, if you have lived enough rotations to parse the dense weave. The low drones—those are the oldest, the thermal regulators of the soundscape, their frequencies tuned to the stone and clay, resonating in the ribs of ceiling until the warmth is not merely felt but *sung into being*, the Hollowed's temperature maintained by acoustic pressure as much as by the thermal mass of packed bodies. You cannot locate the drone-producers. They are the architecture itself, the space between stones, the frequency of the earth's own slow exhalation made audible through some conversion process the Pressed do not question, have perhaps never conceived of questioning. The drones simply *are*, as gravity, as the rotation of the crowd, as the fact of pressing.

The high whistles—those have form, if form is movement through space. They thread, they weave, they locate. Each Pressed person carries a signature whistle, not chosen but emerged, the particular frequency their throat produces when not producing the other necessary sounds, their *idle tone*, and these thread through the drone-foundations like needles through cloth, letting each body know where each other body is, the crowd mapping itself in constant auditory self-survey. The whistles change with age, with health, with emotional state—there are whistle-shapes for fear, for desire, for the particular complex state of rotating toward sleep—and the Pressed learn to read these as surface-dwellers learn to read faces, though of course they read faces too, when faces are briefly available, one among many redundant systems, the Hollowed's communication so overdetermined, so multiply redundant, because failure of any single channel means not inconvenience but dissolution, the loss of the self into the press that could become, without these maintaining structures, mere chaos.

The clicks—those are the predators, or the announcement of predators, or the predators' own communication, the taxonomy unclear, the boundary between the calling-things and the things that eat them, between the Pressed and their acoustic ecology, deliberately blurred. Something moves in the lower Hollowed, something that feeds on the ghost-tongue, something that the ghost-tongue's pale light reveals to those who have learned to see by it, and this something clicks. Or the calling-things click to warn of it. Or the clicking *is* the something, materialized in sound, the predator emerging into auditory existence at the moment of hunting. The Pressed do not investigate. They *respond*, their own clicking joining the chorus when disturbance travels through the crowd, bodies pressing tighter, rotation accelerating, the dense-packed warmth becoming defensive, a shell of maintained contact against whatever might emerge from the ghost-tongue dark.

And the almost-speech—this is where the Pressed most nearly become visible to themselves, where the calling-thing chorus admits of something approaching narrative, history, the individual voice that persists despite everything. It is not language as you know it. It is *positioned utterance*, each phrase determined by its place in the rotation, its acoustic relation to the drones and whistles, its contribution to the total soundscape rather than its transmission of information from one separated mind to another. Yet meaning emerges, accretes, the almost-speech becoming *more* almost, *more* speech, over long intimacy, the Pressed who have rotated together through many cycles developing a mutual modulation, their voices finding harmonies, frequencies that reinforce rather than interfere, until they can exchange—what? Not thoughts. *Orientations*, ways of being in the press, adjustments to the crowd's flow, the affective tonality of this rotation or that platform-edge.

They do not love as you love, with separation and approach, with the drama of distance closed. They love as they are, in the press, the person they press against becoming *their* person not through choice but through the accumulation of rotated-together warmth, the particular quality of shared whistle, the almost-speech that has achieved maximum almostness, maximum speech, between them. Sex occurs in the rotation, bodies finding the brief alignments that permit entry, that permit the spasm of reproduction, never separated from the crowd, never private, the press itself participating, the calling-things' chorus reaching some local intensity that marks these matings as special, as *noticed*, though by whom or what the Pressed do not specify, do not perhaps conceptually separate from the general fact of being-noticed that is the Hollowed's constant condition.

They die in the press. There is no removal of bodies, no space for death's separation. The dead rotate with the living until they have given up their warmth, until the bacterial empire of their skin has been exchanged one last time, until the crowd's pressure has completed the work of maintaining them beyond maintaining. Then they are *let down*, a gradual process, bodies sinking through the press toward the lower platforms, the cooler edges, the places where ghost-tongue grows thickest, where moisture pools, where time operates differently, slower, the dead becoming substrate, their minerals leaching into the walls that weep, their organic matter feeding the Basket-Weeds that root in the upper chinks, the full cycle closed, the body never leaving the Hollowed, never introducing the disruptive fact of absence, of space where something was.

What lives here, finally, is *relationship at density*, the impossibility of the individual made productive, made sustainable, made *dwelt-in* like the space itself. The Pressed are not separable from the Hollowed, from the calling-things, from the ghost-tongue and the Basket-Weeds and the moisture that breathes in held rhythm with their own breathing. They have achieved what surface-dwellers seek and fear: complete integration, the loss of boundary into maintained warmth, the self as crowd-function, as acoustic contribution, as the particular pressure at this point in the rotation rather than the persistent identity that demands space, separation, the *room* to be. They do not miss what they have never known. Or if they miss—if some whistle carries the tonality of longing, some almost-speech approaches the grammar of loss—they miss it *together*, the missing itself a crowd-function, a maintained pressure, another thread in the weave that holds them all in this blood-warm, sound-dense, perfectly achieved compression of life against life, life into life, the made world holding what it has made until the distinction between maker and made has dissolved into the single fact of what continues, what maintains, what calls and is called in the endless circling below the world of light and distance and the terrible freedom of empty space.
