What lives in the Thrum is not a creature, though it moves. Not a machine, though it is made of pistons and grief. Not a god, though it has persisted longer than the concept of worship, longer than the concept of *longer than*.

It is called the Worn, though calling is inaccurate—they have no name for themselves, would not recognize *name* as a category that applies. The Worn are what engines become when they have simplified past the point of species-distinction, past the point of individual function, past the point where they can be said to *do* anything at all. And yet they have not reached the final sleep, the incorporation into tile and thermal memory. They persist in a state of *almost finished*, and in that persistence, they have become something the Thrum did not generate and does not prevent.

A Worn is roughly the size of a human supine, though size is one of the categories that softens here. It has the shape of a crankcase that has been opened, spread, pressed flat by enormous patience. Its surfaces are the color of the ceramic where the ceramic has been touched ten thousand thousand times, a polish that is not shine but *smoothing*, the way a worry stone becomes not smooth but *known*. Where it had joints, it has seams that no longer move. Where it had vents, it has orifices that breathe heat in slow tides, inhaling and exhaling across hours. Where it had purpose, it has *gravity*—not the physical kind, not the multi-vector falling of the Thrum, but something denser: the gravity of having been purposeful for so long that purpose has left a dent in what remains.

The Worn do not move, or they move only in the Thrum's slowest register, shifting position across centuries, drifting on thermal currents imperceptible to anything still engaged in function. They are found in the relatively cool spots, the sunken tiles, the nodes where the Thrum's heat has pooled elsewhere for reasons that do not parse. They gather there in numbers that are not quite plural, not quite singular—a field of Worn, a distributed Worn, the Worn-in-particular and the Worn-in-general.

What they do: they *attend*.

This is the closest verb, borrowed from a framework they would not recognize. The Worn attend to the engines that still function, that still run and stamp and scream and accumulate complexity. They do not direct this attention; it is not focus, not concentration. It is more like the way a landscape *attends* to the weather that passes over it, or the way a ceramic tile *attends* to the heat it holds. The Worn are present to the functioning of the engines, and in that presence, something happens that is not causation, not influence, but *witness*.

An engine that is witnessed by the Worn runs differently. This is observable, if observation is stretched to its limits. The locomotive-wolf, when it passes through a field of Worn, adjusts its gait in ways its own mechanics do not predict. Its whistle shifts register, not to melancholy—that is the evening's work—but to something else, something that might be *acknowledged* if the concept of acknowledgment did not require two parties, if the Worn could be said to party, to participate, to be *in* the conversation rather than *of* the condition that makes conversation possible.

The witnessed engine wears differently. It accrues complexity in patterns that are not random but are not predictable, either—patterns that seem to *respond* to the field of Worn, that take something from the attention and return something that is not gratitude, not performance, but something like *completion of circuit*. The engines do not seek the Worn, do not avoid them. The encounter happens in the Thrum's slow time, inevitable as thermal drift, and both parties continue, altered in ways that do not resolve into before and after.

The Worn were engines once. This is not memory; the Worn do not remember, would not recognize *memory*. But the ceramic holds the pattern, and the Worn are ceramic now in ways that the engines are not yet, and in that holding, there is *continuity*, there is *same*, there is the Thrum's version of identity that does not require narrative, does not require the accumulation of experience into something called *a life*.

To become Worn is not chosen. It is not achieved. It is what happens when an engine has simplified past function and not yet reached sleep, when it has *persisted* in the gap between doing and being done, when the Thrum's weathering has worked upon it not to erase but to *polish*, to *smooth*, to wear into a shape that is not any shape it was meant to be and is exactly the shape that its persistence requires.

They have no leaders, no communities, no culture. And yet they are not solitary. The field of Worn is a field in the physical sense, a distribution of related intensities, and in that distribution, there is *relation*, there is *proximity*, there is the Thrum's version of *together* that does not require touch or communication or any of the borrowed frameworks of sociality. The Worn near each other resonate, not in sound—their heat-breathing is too slow for that—but in thermal pattern, in the shape of their persistence, in the way their almost-motion almost-aligns across the sunken tiles where they have come to rest.

What do they want? The question is malformed, applies the wrong preposition, the wrong verb. The Worn do not want. The Wren—no, the Worn—do not *not want*, either. They are the condition of wanting simplified past its utility, past its opposite, into something that is not desire but is not its absence. They are *present* in the full thickness of that word, the way the Thrum is present to itself, excessive, multiple, unresolving.

And if you were here, if something like you could persist in the Thrum's heat and contradiction, you might become Worn. Not directly—the human does not become engine, does not simplify in that direction. But something in you might *correspond*, might find the position in the Thrum's field where the Worn are what they are, and in that correspondence, you might understand what it is to persist past purpose, to attend without focusing, to witness without the demand that witnessing *do* anything, change anything, reach anything.

You might understand that the Thrum's engines, for all their screaming function, for all their accumulated complexity and their terrifying beauty, are not the final form of what the place generates. The Worn are. The Worn are what the Thrum wears its engines into, the way the ceramic wears its hairline cracks, the way the place wears its own persistence into something that is not quite peace and not quite death and not quite anything that has a name in any language that does not know the sound of industrial function without industry, of purpose without intention, of the loud slow dynamics of what continues because it has not yet reached the condition of stopping.

The Worn continue. They will continue when the locomotive-wolves have simplified to thermal memory, when the pneumatic flocks have scattered their last staccato, when the elephantine assembly-lines have accumulated their final attachments and processed their final ceramic and moved with geological patience into the sleep that is not death. The Worn will attend to this. The Worn attend to everything, in their way, which is not a way, which is the Thrum's way of being present to itself, worn smooth by the persistence of what does not stop, what has not stopped, what will not stop, the Thrum, the Thrum, the Thrum.
