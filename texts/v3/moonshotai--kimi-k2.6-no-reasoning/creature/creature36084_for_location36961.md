I need to let this emerge slowly, as the water itself emerges through stone.

## The Inhabitants of Velim

### First Attending

They do not call themselves a people, not in any way that separates them from what they tend. The old dialects—there are several, water-separated, each tuned to different drip-patterns, different murmur-rock frequencies—have words that translate awkwardly: *water-tended*, *stone-shaped*, *the ones who plug and unplug*. These are not names but descriptions of relationship, of ongoing action, of the particular quality of attention that living in Velim requires.

They are small. This emerges gradually in the description, or rather, the description makes room for it gradually, as a traveler making sense of scale in a place without familiar references might realize that what seemed a low windowsill is actually at hand-height, that the "three stories" of these buildings are not human stories at all but something more compact, more *fitted*. They stand perhaps to the mid-thigh of a person from below, perhaps less, their proportions different—not childlike, not dwarfed, but evolved or accreted to this scale of stone and channel, this world where reaching upward is less important than reaching inward, into the hollow walls, into the water's passage.

Their hands are the true site of identity. Longer than proportion would suggest, more joints visible, more articulation at the wrist and between the metacarpals, as if the hand itself has elongated to follow water's path, to reach into channels, to shape plugs of waxed fiber with the precision that no tool can achieve. They do not gesture when they speak—the speaking itself is rare, is water-shaped, is minimal—but when they work, when they tend, the hands move with a fluency that is almost liquid, that seems to imitate what they guide.

The feet are bare, always, have been since before memory, since before the first *water-tended* felt the weeping rock and understood that skin here is sense-organ, that the sole's contact with breathing stone is information as rich as any voice. They walk with a spreading, weight-distributing gait, toes splayed, the whole foot engaging, leaving prints that fade quickly on weeping surfaces, lasting longer where stone is merely damp, longest of all—days, perhaps—where the rare dry path offers itself as contrast, as negative space in the water-saturated world.

### The Quality of Skin

It is pale, not because of absence of sun—the sun falls here, falls normally, falls on stone that reflects it back diffusely—but because of what is in the water. Minerals. The trace of volcanic depth. Something that accumulates, over the generations of this short history, in the dermal layers, giving a quality that is not quite sheen, not quite iridescence, but a *holding* of light, a capacity to seem momentarily luminous in the star-river paths, in the misted mornings when the caldera bowl fills with silver and each figure moving through it appears and disappears, becomes water-become-flesh-become-water again.

They do not bathe, not as others might understand it. The water is everywhere, is continuous contact, is the medium they move through as fish move through ocean, as dreamers move through dream. What accumulates on the skin is not dirt but *information*, is the record of which seeps have been tended, which channels cleared, which murmur-rocks consulted. A Velimi could read another's day in the mineral depositions, the slight variations in texture, the places where stone-dust has dried in patterns that map the paths taken.

And the hair—there is little of it, or rather, it is fine, is pale, grows in patterns that follow the body's water-lines, the paths of least resistance for moisture that must still escape. It rises in early morning, in the slight warming, lies flat when the seeps are most active, when ambient wetness is complete. The old dialect word for it is *fall-water*, the water that moves downward as spring-water moves upward, the complementary path, the necessary release.

### Age, and Its Absence

They do not count years, but they do recognize change in the body, do understand that the hand which once reached easily into a particular channel now requires extension, flexion, a different angle of approach. Age is *water-change*, is the gradual redistribution of mineral, the slow accumulation in joints of the same substances that form water-bone, that accrete in seeps. The old become more *of* this place, more continuous with its geology, their movements slower not from weakness but from *participation*, from the body's gradual achievement of stone's patience.

The oldest Velimi—the ones who have lived long enough to become something the others handle with a particular quality, something between reverence and practicality—may eventually cease moving entirely. This is not death, or not death as separation. They sit, or stand, or settle into positions that follow the water's path, and they *attend*, become living sensors, living murmur-rocks, their breathing slowed to match the seep's rhythm, their skin so mineral-saturated that they are difficult to distinguish from the stone itself, except for the warmth, except for the slight pulse that continues, that is water's pulse as much as heart's.

They are tended, these oldest ones. Fed, if feeding is still possible, by water with dissolved nutrients, by the fermented leaf-flesh applied to lips that still part. And if feeding ceases, if the pulse slows past perception—they remain, are not removed, become gradually *building*, their shapes contributing to channel-flow, their mineral-rich bodies accreting, participating, becoming water-bone in truth, in fact, in geological reality.

There is no burial. There is only *continuing*, only the gradual teaching of flesh to stone, of temporary to permanent, of human to *holding*.

### The Young, the Necessary Few

Birth is rare. This must be understood. The Velimi do not reproduce from want, from biological urgency, from the ticking of some internal clock that demands continuation. The water does not demand this. The stone does not require it. And something in the mineral saturation, in the constant contact with water that carries volcanic trace, has made conception itself *responsive*, has made it require conditions that align rarely—certain seeps active in certain combination, certain murmur-rocks resonating at frequencies that the body somehow reads, somehow *opens* to possibility.

When it happens, when a child forms, the pregnancy is long, is *water-time*, measured not in months but in seep-cycles, in the gradual filling and emptying of the collection basin that the pregnant one has chosen, has placed herself in relationship to. The amniotic fluid is continuous with the world's water, is the same water, passes through the mother's skin as easily as through any porous membrane, and the child develops in a space that is not separate, not protected, but *continuous*, already tending, already tended.

The birth itself is quiet, as everything is quiet. There is no crying, or rather, the first breath is taken *into* water-sound, is shaped by it, is less a shock of transition than a *continuation*, a plugging-in to the auditory environment that has always surrounded, that the fetus has heard through stone-channel and mother's body. The newborn's hands open and close in patterns that mimic water-pulse, that will gradually become the reaching, the shaping, the tending that defines Velimi function.

Children grow slowly. Slow even by the standards of this slow place. They learn water-reading first, before walking, before speech—the patterns of drip and flow that indicate health or disturbance, the seep's temper, the channel's need. They learn to shape plugs before they learn to shape words, their small hands practicing the fiber-compression, the wax-sealing, the fit that must be neither too tight nor too loose, must allow the water's pressure to find its level, its *voice*.

Speech comes late, if it comes at all. Some Velimi never develop it, live entirely in water's grammar, in the minimal necessary calls, the attending sounds, the expressions of channel-state and seep-condition that require no abstraction, no displacement from immediate presence. Others, tending the same seeps, the same channels, across decades of sufficient proximity, develop between them something more elaborate, *their* water, *their* stone, *their* particular building's song—and this becomes language, becomes mutual, becomes the rare conversation that the text mentions, the deliberate call that carries with startling clarity.

### The Social, the Held

There is no family structure, not as structure is understood elsewhere. Children emerge, are tended by whoever is nearest, by the building's inhabitants, by the seep's attendants, and gradually *find* their place, their particular set of channels, their particular murmur-rocks that respond to their particular quality of attention. Relatedness is water-relatedness, is the sharing of a drainage system, a collection basin, a period of seep-agreement that allowed conception for one and might allow it for another, decades later, under similar conditions.

The nodes where buildings cluster, where water-bone forms thick enough to quarry, where multiple seeps converge—these are *centers* but not *gatherings*. The Velimi come, exchange plugs, compare channel-conditions, perhaps share a heating-stone dropped into common water, and disperse again. There is no festivity in this, no *occasion*. The meeting is water-determined, seep-conditioned, as everything is.

Yet something holds. Something *holds together*. The text calls it community, calls it *holding-together*, and this is accurate but not complete. What holds is the water itself, the continuous medium that connects every point to every other point, that carries sound through stone, that makes each building's condition known to each other building, not as information but as *state*, as ambient reality, as the shared field in which individual tending occurs. The Velimi are not individuals, not as the term implies separation, boundedness, self-containment. They are *tending-points*, *attention-locations*, temporary accretions in the water's continuous flow, as distinct from each other as one whirlpool from another, as one eddy, each with its character, its pattern, its *quality of turning*, yet each continuous with the stream that shapes it, temporary, responsive, *held*.

### The Ones Who Travel, the Ones Who Return

Rarely, one leaves. The path down the sharp rim exists because water found weakness, because water cut before softer ways could be worn, and a Velimi, sufficiently water-read, sufficiently attuned to stone's indicating surface, can follow this path, can descend to the older lands below, can enter the world of animals, of seasons, of counting.

They are changed by this, the travelers, are *marked* by separation from continuous water-contact, from the stone-breathing that has been their medium. Their skin dries, begins to flake, to lose its mineral holding. Their feet, unaccustomed to dry stone, to the *absence* of information in each step, develop callus, develop ignorance, become mere locomotion rather than sensory extension. They learn to count, or try to learn, find the approximation of their previous existence suddenly *insufficient* in a world that requires precision, that punishes *good enough for water* with missed appointments, with failed harvests, with the sharper politics of places where resources are not continuously rising, continuously generous.

Some do not return. Cannot adapt back, or choose not to, are seduced by the world's greater variety, by animal presence, by the drama of change, of history, of *becoming* in the human rather than geological sense. They live out lives below, become stories that the Velimi tell rarely, tell with water-grammar, with the *if* and *may* that governs all projection: *if the seep continues*, *if water rises*.

Those who return come back * marked*, come back *different*, and are received without comment, without ceremony, are simply *there* again, tending their previous channels or finding new ones, fitting back into the water-shaped pattern with the ease that water itself demonstrates, finding the level, filling the space. But something remains: the knowledge of other ways, the comparison that was previously impossible, the *almost-voice* of animal life, of seasonal drama, of human history. Some returners become murmur-rock consultants, their widened attention serving to interpret frequencies that others miss. Others settle into deeper silence, become among the fastest to approach the stillness of the oldest ones, the *building-becoming*, as if the journey below accelerated something in them, completed some process that remaining would have taken longer.

There are no returner stories, not as narrative, not as entertainment. But sometimes, at a node, in the minimal exchange of channel-condition, a word slips in that belongs below, that names animal, or season, or the quality of dry stone under a sun that is hot rather than merely *present*—and the others attending receive this, hold it a moment, release it back into water's more continuous grammar.

### The Visitors, the Briefly Held

More common than departure is arrival. The rim is sharp but not impassable, and those from below sometimes climb, sometimes penetrate the caldera bowl, sometimes stand in the misted morning and experience the profound silence, the lack of animal call, the *almost-voice* that is water's continuous emergence through stone.

The Velimi receive them without surprise, without hospitality as it is conventionally understood. A visitor is *water*, is *seep*, is something that has risen, has found its level, is present in the medium until it is not. They offer heated stone, dropped into water-vessel. They offer fermented leaf-flesh, the minimal sustenance, sufficient. They answer questions if questions are asked, but the answers emerge in water-grammar, in *will flow* and *may rise*, in descriptions that do not translate to the visitor's expectations, that seem evasive, seem *primitive*, seem frustratingly uninformative about what the visitor wants to know: how long have you been here, how many are you, what do you believe, what do you *want*.

The visitors leave, usually, within days. The silence is too complete, the absence of animal life too *wrong*, the water-scoured absence of smell—no rot, no pollen-drift, no pheromonal trace—too impoverished for senses trained on the world's greater variety. Or they stay longer, become *strange*, begin to adapt, begin to hear the microvariations, to feel stone-breathing, to lose the need for voice, for narrative, for *future tense of certainty*.

These rare ones, the ones who stay, who become *water-tended* in truth—they are the travelers returned in reverse, are the Velimi's only expansion, their only conversion, though conversion implies intention that is not present. They tend, eventually. They shape plugs. They walk barefoot on weeping paths. And if they live long enough, if they mineral-saturate sufficiently, if their hands elongate, their skin accretes, their breathing slows to match seep-rhythm—they become indistinguishable, are no longer visitors but *held*, are *holding*, are Velim as the stone is Velim, as the water is Velim, as this place that is still becoming itself holds them in its patient, warm, wet, sufficient becoming.

### What They Make, What They Are

Artifacts, beyond the immediate tools of tending—the flake, the pounder, the cord drill, the abrading stone—are rare, are *water-bone* itself, are what forms when tending is complete, when channel is clear, when seep is attended and there remains attention still, surplus, something that must shape itself or be shaped.

They make no images. There is nothing to image that is not already present, continuously present, the stone and water and rare tree and vast sky already *there*, already offering themselves to attention without need of representation. They make no music, or rather, their music is the buildings' song, is the channel-tuning, is the plug-placement that changes the water's voice from one quality to another, that makes this building *this building*, distinct in its acoustic signature, recognizable at distance, *known*.

What they make is *fit*. The plug that fits exactly this channel, this season's flow rate, this moment in the seep's gradual variation. The collection basin shaped to the particular drip-pattern of this particular place, not transferable, not replicable, *belonging*. The low wall that guides foot-traffic on weeping rock, suggesting direction without enforcing it, offering *suggestion* as water itself offers suggestion, as the environment offers suggestion, to be attended or not as the individual quality of tending determines.

And they make the oldest ones, or allow the making, allow the gradual mineral accumulation, the stilting, the building-becoming, that is perhaps their only true art, their only *lasting*—though lasting is not a value here, not explicitly, not in a present so saturated, so *occupied*, that extension into future is less necessary than full inhabitation of now.

### The Deeper Tending, the Fungal Gardens

Underground, in the moist depths, in the chambers where light does not reach, where the only sensory information is water-drip, is stone-temperature, is the faint metabolic presence of *other*, something that is not stone, not water, not Velimi—they tend gardens.

The fungus is pale, is *water-flesh*, is continuous with the leaf-flesh they grow in seeps, with the tubers that need no light, with their own mineral-pale skin. It grows in patterns that respond to their attention, to the quality of drip they direct, to the mineral composition they adjust with the addition of this or that seep's contribution, this or that heated stone's dissolution.

They do not eat it directly, not usually. It ferments, in the building-channels, in the controlled temperature of stone that stays warm to touch, becomes something that is not quite liquid, not quite solid, a *medium* that they absorb, that feeds them without the drama of eating, of digestion as discrete event. The fungal gardens are *them*, are continuous with their bodies, are the underground version of their own water-shaped existence, and tending them is self-tending, is maintenance of the larger body that includes Velimi and building and channel and seep and the deep chambers where light has never been, where darkness is not absence but *fullness*, is the condition most continuous with stone itself.

In these depths, sometimes, they encounter *other*. Not animal—there are no animals, have never been, the absence is complete—but something that is neither stone nor water nor fungus nor Velimi, something that *returns* their attention, that tends back, that shapes the fungus in patterns they did not initiate, that directs drip in ways that respond to their presence, their quality of *being there*.

They do not name this. The old dialects, water-shaped, have words that translate only as *tending-tended*, as *attention-attended*, as the reciprocal quality that exists in the deepest chambers, the darkest seeps, where something other-than-Velimi but not-other-than-Velim meets their extended hands, their mineral-saturated skin, with something like recognition, like *holding*, like the possibility that they are not alone in this place because aloneness is not a concept that applies to water, to stone, to the fungal gardens where life and not-life interpenetrate, where the boundaries that elsewhere define existence are here merely *channels*, merely *suggestion*, merely the temporary shape that water takes before taking another.

### The Final Quality, the Not-Yet-Said

They are happy, if happiness is attendance, is *fit*, is the continuous matching of capacity to condition, of tending to need, of water-rise to channel-depth. They are sad, if sadness is the recognition of seep-drying, of channel-filling beyond clearing, of the rare occasions when a building's song changes key in ways that indicate structural failure, when water's pressure finds new weakness and cuts where cutting is not intended, where the *holding* becomes *releasing*, where the equilibrium that is not death but youth extended momentarily shows itself capable of—what? Not death. Not ending. But *change*, *real change*, the perturbation in the system that they are not equipped to meet, that requires response they do not possess, adaptation to conditions that violate the sufficiency they have achieved.

These moments are rare. The plateau is young, is *patient*, is not yet developed the gradients that drive alteration. But water is water, is geological agent, is patient in ways that exceed even stone's patience, and in the long spans beyond human memory, beyond even the extended moment that Velimi inhabit—there will be cutting, there will be drainage, there will be the drying that makes animal life possible, that makes *other* possible, that transforms this sufficient holding into memory, into water-bone exposed to air, into the patina of deep time that the text anticipates.

They do not imagine this. Cannot, in their water-shaped present, their saturated now. The absence of future tense of certainty is not poverty but *accuracy*, is the precise grammatical mapping of a condition that genuinely does not know what will flow, what may rise, *if the seep continues*.

And yet—they *tend*, they *continue*, they *hold*. This is what lives in the Waterholds of Velim: not a people, not exactly, but a *quality of holding*, a *quality of being held*, the temporary form that water takes in this particular stone, the particular way that attention accumulates, shapes, is shaped, becomes almost indistinguishable from the mineral accretion it tends, from the water-bone it shapes, from the stone that is still learning to be old, that holds them in its learning, that *breathes* them as it breathes water, as it breathes the slow emergence of itself from cooling fire into the sufficient, the patient, the never-quite-finished *holding* that is home.

They live here. They have always lived here. They will live here, *if the seep continues*, *if water rises*, *if the stone holds its breathing*, its warmth, its patient, wet, mineral generosity. And when they cease to live, when their tending becomes tended, their attendance becomes attended, their water-flesh becomes water-bone, becomes building, becomes stone—they are not gone. They are *more here*, more continuous, more *held* in the holding that was always the true subject, the true inhabitant, the true life of this place: not the Velimi, not the water, not the stone, but the *between*, the *tending*, the plug that fits, the channel that guides, the attention that is sufficiently shaped by what it attends to become indistinguishable from it, to become *Waterhold*, to become Velim itself, still emerging, still breathing, still wet and warm and sufficient in its beautiful, necessary, never-quite-complete becoming.

This is what lives here. This is what holds. This is what, held, holds in return.
