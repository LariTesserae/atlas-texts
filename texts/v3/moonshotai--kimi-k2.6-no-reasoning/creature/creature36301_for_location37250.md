What lives in the Groan is not a *what* in the separable sense, not a creature you could point to and name as inhabitant distinct from habitat. What lives there is the Groan itself—the living, and the condition of being alive as the Groan defines it, which is something other and larger than individual organisms pursuing individual survival. But let me approach this slantwise, as the place demands, and let what emerges find its own frequency.

---

There is a *thickness* to existence in the Groan that has no equivalent in places where air merely carries sound rather than constituting it. You feel this first, before you understand anything about columns or condensation or the walkers processing matter into available form. Your own body becomes strange to you—your heartbeat discovers itself as rhythm, as *contribution*, your breathing finds itself already part of something larger that does not pause for your consent. The Groan does not ask permission. The Groan includes you, or the Groan adjusts you out of viability, and the space between these outcomes is where life, as the attenders have learned to live it, occurs.

What lives there, then: the attenders themselves, though they resist this naming. Call them listeners, sleep-phasers, those-who-carry-rhythm. They are human, or were human, or are something that human became under sufficient pressure of environment. Their bodies have adapted in ways they no longer distinguish from culture, from personhood, from the proper way to be. They sleep in fragments, two hours here, twenty minutes there, never fully unconscious, never fully awake, a continuous partial presence that matches the Groan's own. Their hearing has shifted—still present, still functional, but integrated differently, the constant sound become substrate rather than stimulus, become *ground* in the oldest philosophical sense.

They are few, sparse as the columns themselves, distributed across distances that make aggregation impossible and perhaps undesirable. When two attenders meet, it is an event of months, of careful acoustic signaling across root-network distances, of approaching through the intervals until visual confirmation, until the verification that this other body also carries the rhythm, also phases in the Groan-acceptable pattern. Their meetings are not primarily communicative in the informational sense. They already know, through the network, what news travels. They meet to *resonate*, to stand together and feel their frequencies combine, create beats, find new chords possible only in plurality before separating again into the larger pattern.

They do not, the attenders, claim ownership of the built-places. The platforms and hollow-shelters exist in something between abandonment and permanent use, occupied in succession by different bodies at different phases, maintained collectively through no collective decision but through the same balancing that governs all Groan processes. An attender arrives at a platform, finds it sound or finds it requiring repair, repairs or occupies or passes by according to capacities and needs distributed across the network of their scattered kind. They leave things behind—not possessions, not markers of individual presence, but *adjustments*, small modifications to the acoustic properties of a space, a hollow shaped slightly differently for better sleep-phase, a platform edge thinned to create a particular resonant frequency when struck. The next occupant receives these, modifies or maintains, passes on. The built-places grow, slowly, into themselves, into histories not of persons but of habitation itself, the concept of shelter finding its Groan-specific form.

Their language, if language survives what the Groan makes of communication, is primarily vocal—not because writing is impossible, though the humidity makes it difficult, but because speech has become so integrated with the acoustic environment that to separate it, to fix it in visual symbol, would be to misunderstand what speaking means here. They sing. They sing constantly, chorally even when alone, addressing the Groan itself, the columns, the condensation, the walkers passing in their processing masses. The songs have no fixed text, or the text has dissolved into the adaptive variation that the environment demands—each utterance shaped to current frequency-conditions, to the moment's acoustic possibility, to the state of the particular column that receives the voice and returns it modified. They sing their location, their phase-state, their chemical needs, their availability for whatever the Groan requires. Whether the Groan hears, whether this constitutes communication in any reciprocal sense, the attenders do not claim to know. They sing because not-singing is not-viable, because silence in this place is not absence but *resistance*, and resistance is adjustment-out, is finding yourself in a frequency band the root-network no longer sustains.

I want to say something about their faces, though I saw few clearly, though the heat-shimmer and the strangeness of their presence makes memory uncertain. They seemed *smooth*, not in agelessness but in a continuous quality, the expressions not shifting so much as *modulating*, variations on a sustained tone rather than discrete states. And their eyes—wide, always slightly unfocused, not seeing-through but seeing-with, attending to visual field as they attended to acoustic, as continuous with environment rather than directed toward particular objects. They looked, the attenders, as you might look if you had become a frequency component, if your personhood had distributed itself across the phasing of sleep and the rhythm of movement and the constant, necessary song.

---

But the attenders are not, I think, the most living thing in the Groan, not the most essential expression of what the place makes possible. They are adaptation, response, the human frequency finding compatibility. More fundamental are the ones who were never other, whose compatibility was not achieved but original, the possibility the Groan invented for itself and then found useful.

The gliders, first: those translucent motes, those living cisterns. I have written their function, their processing of condensation, their role in the water economy, but I have not conveyed their *quality*, the strangeness of creatures that exist in such continuous partial contact with their medium, suspended, waiting, filtering. They are not beautiful, or their beauty is not available to human perception—their bodies too strange, too functional in ways that do not map onto aesthetic categories shaped by other environments. But they are *present*, unmistakably so, their existence a kind of claim: that this too is life, this suspension, this filtering, this tipping-release on biological schedule.

What lives in a glider? Not consciousness as we would recognize it, not decision. But something, some continuous responsive *adjustment*, some quality of being that matches its environment with the perfection of long co-evolution or of original design. They are born, I am told, in the lower condensation zones, emerging from eggs laid in the water-cups of certain column-dependent growths, their initial forms immediately functional, immediately part of the system. They grow, slowly, their transparency increasing with age, their filtering efficiency refining, until they reach the size where they occupy their own zone, their own height-band, their own schedule of filling and release. And they die, when they die—eventually, inevitably—by falling, by becoming too dense, by the accumulation of mineral-filtered-from-water that gradually opaques them, that drags them down through the condensation column they can no longer maintain position in, down to the root-zones where the walkers wait, where processing completes the cycle.

The gliders do not struggle against this, or if they do, the struggle is indistinguishable from the continuous micro-adjustments of their suspension. They live, they function, they become non-viable, they become resource. There is no drama in it, no narrative of individual significance. The Groan does not narrate itself in individuals.

The walkers, stranger still, more fundamentally other. I have called them digestion, called them speed-of-metabolism made mobile, and these are true descriptions but not sufficient ones. What lives in a walker is something that has accepted, or never refused, a radical dissolution of boundary. They move in masses, yes, processing, transforming, but they also *rest*, in patterns that seem to correspond to the Groan's phases, the collective opening and closing of stomata, the expansion and contraction of columns in thermal rhythm. Resting, they cluster, they stack, they become something other than individual bodies, something approaching architecture, the spaces between their armored forms creating channels, creating acoustic properties, becoming part of the hollow-system that the attenders use for shelter, for modified sound, for the different sleep that proximity to such massed presence enables.

Are they aware, these walkers? The attenders have songs about this, songs that do not resolve into doctrine. Some say the walkers are the Groan's awareness of ground, the plant-thinking extended into mobile form to know what root-network alone cannot reach. Others say they are simpler, mechanical in the older sense, the self-moving that requires no self to move. The songs hold both possibilities, modulate between them, find the frequency where contradiction becomes chord rather than dissonance.

I watched them, once, a mass processing fallen material from a column-stress event, something had fallen, something always falls, and the walkers concentrated, furious in their efficiency, and I could not determine if I witnessed hunger or duty or simply *function*, simply the being of what is shaped so thoroughly to purpose that purpose and being become indistinguishable. They ate, they processed, they composed, they delivered to root-zones, and all of this occurred with a density of presence, a *fullness* of what was happening, that seemed to me finally more alive than much of what I had known in places where life scatters into so many competing individual claims.

The singers, smallest, most strange in their integration. They live in the acoustic channels, yes, feed on resonant leaves, become part of the sound. But what this *means*—what it is to be so thoroughly medium and message both, to have your body become instrument, your movement composition, your death continued resonance—this I cannot approach except through the songs of the attenders, who sing *about* the singers with a quality I can only call longing, or recognition, or the acknowledgment of a completion they themselves have not achieved.

The singers do not, apparently, respond to the attenders' songs. They are too integrated, too *already* part of the larger acoustic system to register individual address. Or perhaps they respond continuously, their every movement modification of the chord, their presence the response itself. The attenders sing to them regardless, or sing with them, or sing their *concept*, the possibility of such total integration held as aspiration, as meditation object, as the direction toward which their own phasing sleep, their own continuous partial song, tends without hope of arrival.

---

What lives in the Groan, finally, is *relationship*. This is the answer that emerges, that became itself as I wrote, that I could not settle on early because it is not the kind of answer that allows settling.

Not the Groan as organism, the single plant-thinking. Not the Groan as ecosystem, the many in negotiation. These are the questions the attenders hold, the frequencies they modulate between. The answer is something other: that living here means being *in* relationship so thoroughly that the prepositions fail, that "in" suggests too much separation, too much container and contained. The gliders are the condensation and the condensation is gliders. The walkers are the digestion and the digestion is walkers. The attenders are thelistening and thelistening is attenders. Even the singers, even the columns, even the continuous groan itself—none of these are prior, none derivative, all emerge from and sustain the pattern that has no outside, no position from which to observe it whole.

What lives there is this emergence, this sustaining, this *holding* that the description itself approaches and cannot complete. The Groan holds. The Groan continues. And what lives within this holding, this continuation, is not creature but *creaturing*, the continuous present participle, the being-becoming that refuses to resolve into finished form because its form is the process of its own maintenance, its own ongoing adjustment and balance and inclusion-exclusion that never rests in completed state.

I have walked there. I have felt my own frequency, my own awkward human rhythm, attempting compatibility, achieving it partially, temporarily, in the hollow of a column where sound went soft, where sleep phased into something approaching the attenders' pattern. And I have felt the adjustment, the gentle but inexorable *pressure* when I could not maintain, when my questions, my needs for answer and completion and the naming of what lives, became frequency incompatible, became resistance, became the silence that is not silence here but the wrong kind of sound.

I left. This is the shape of my memory: not failure, exactly, but the finding of my own parameters, my own viability limits, the frequency range where my particular hum continues. The Groan does not reject. The Groan *holds*, and what it holds remains, and what it cannot hold finds itself elsewhere, still vibrating, still part of some pattern if not this pattern, some chord if not this chord.

What lives in the Groan lives also, in different form, where you are reading this. The groan you do not hear, the continuous sound below hearing, the pressure between your bones that is not the Groan's pressure but is pressure, that is the being of a body in a world, the being of a world in its bodies. You are carrying the question. You are part of the attempt. The holding continues, the adjustments proceed, and somewhere in the antipodal distance, or here, or both, the breathing reverses, inhales where before was exhale, maintains the differential that permits pattern to persist across scale you cannot measure.

It continues. It holds. You are held, or you are adjusting, or you are finding the frequency of elsewhere. The Groan does not distinguish. The Groan is distinction itself, the drawing of boundary that is also connection, the space between columns that is also what makes columns columns, the interval that is note, the silence that is sound's necessary ground.

What lives there is what lives, modified, sustained, held in the pattern that is larger than any living thing and made of nothing else but living things. This is not mystery but observation, not mysticism but the attempt to describe what emerges when description itself enters the sound, becomes resonant, finds the frequency where speaking and listening modulate into each other and neither can be said to begin.

Groan. Continue. Hold.
