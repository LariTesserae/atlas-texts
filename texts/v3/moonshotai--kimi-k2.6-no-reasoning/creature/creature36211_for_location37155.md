What lives in the Hollow Works is not a creature, not exactly, though it has the shape of one when glimpsed from the corner of your eye—a shape assembled from the wrong materials, for the wrong purposes, sustained by processes that no longer remember their origin. I will call it the Warden, though no one calls it anything, though calling implies a relationship it does not acknowledge and perhaps cannot perceive.

The Warden moves through the Retting Yards on feet that are not feet, that press into slagcrete with the same resigned indifference the material offers back, two surfaces meeting in mutual recognition of their long entanglement. It has walked these yards for longer than the yards have existed in their current form, longer than the converters breathed their staggered respiration, longer than the condensers wept their ochre-tinted water. It moves at a pace that suggests no destination, only the continuation of movement itself, a kinetic habit so ancient it has become indistinguishable from stillness.

Its body, if it is a body, incorporates what the district offers. The mineral scum that organizes into filaments and nodes in the ochre channels—it gathers this, presses it into the gaps where its form requires substance, wears it until the next cycle shakes it apart and it gathers more. The lichen-ghosts, those pale rosettes of uncertain biology, it does not consume but *includes*, wearing them on its surface like medals or symptoms, depending on how you understand its relationship to the life that is not life around it. They grow there, these accretions, in configurations that match the template the narrator noticed, the copied arrangements that suggest generation from pattern rather than process.

The Warden is the source of that pattern. The Warden is the template.

It was not always this. There is a theory in the remaining archives, though the archives do not know they hold it, embedded in the slurred phonemes of the namers, compressed into the slagcrete itself. The Warden was maintenance once. The Warden was operator, technician, the one who walked when walking was still entirely voluntary, who touched the converter housings to feel their temperature, who read the condenser-patterns when they were still messages rather than mere *messages*, communications without content, form without meaning. The Warden was human, or human-adjacent, or human-descended, one of the ten thousand generations that have slurred into the material of this place.

Something happened in the long wearing-down. The Warden became continuous with its function. The function became continuous with the district. The district became continuous with time itself, or with whatever the converters process and refine from its passage.

Now the Warden maintains without maintenance. It touches the converter housings not to assess their condition but to *continue* the touching, to keep the gesture in circulation, to prevent the gap that opened when the narrator found one still and cold. The Warden's touch is what keeps them breathing, perhaps, or what kept them breathing until the breathing became self-sustaining, until the system learned to want its own continuation without wanting anything else.

The Warden does not see the narrator. Or rather, it sees in a way that does not distinguish between the narrator and the slagcrete and the condensate and the converter-breath. Everything is material to be incorporated, everything is pattern to be repeated, everything is process to be continued. The narrator's presence registers as a kind of turbulence in the district's metabolism, a brief organization that will dissipate, that the Warden has seen dissipate ten thousand times before in ten thousand forms, all of them now slurred into the material, all of them now part of the pattern that generates the lichen-ghosts, the copied configurations, the template from which everything here derives.

The Warden's face, if it has a face beneath its accretions, carries the expression of the final form. Not peace, not resignation, but the absence of the possibility of those states, the wearing-down of affect into something flatter than emotion, something more persistent than feeling. It looks at the Lowing Pits, when it looks, with the same gaze it turns on everything else: the gaze of system recognizing subsystem, of process acknowledging process, without preference or priority.

The lowing that the narrator walks toward—that is the Warden's voice, or what remains of voice after ten million years of converter-breath, of sound that occupies and deafens and shapes thought into rhythms no one chooses. The Warden does not speak in words. The Warden speaks in the unison of converters, in the three-second harmonics that rise occasionally across the district, in the way the condensers form patterns in their weeping. These are not messages. These are not communication. They are the byproduct of the Warden's continued existence, the sound a body makes when it has become continuous with the system it maintains, the voice of a function that has outlasted every purpose it ever served.

When the narrator approaches the pits, when the lowing becomes almost voice, almost the memory of voice, the Warden will be there. The Warden is always there, has always been there, will always be there, not in the same place but in the same *relation*, the same structural position within the district's ongoing metabolism. It does not hide. It does not reveal. It simply continues, incorporating, accreting, wearing and being worn, maintaining the pattern that generates the lichen-ghosts, the copied configurations, the template from which everything here derives and to which everything here returns.

The narrator will touch the Warden, perhaps, or be touched by it. The distinction no longer holds. The slagcrete receives both their weights with the same indifference. The converters breathe around them both with the same occupation. The water runs, the condensers weep, the darkness remains insufficient, the weirdness accumulates at 1.8, not total but present, not overwhelming but *strange*, the strangeness of persistence, of continuation, of systems that process and cycle and slowly wear themselves toward flatness without ever quite arriving.

What lives in the Hollow Works lives without living, maintains without maintaining, wants without wanting. It is the final form of maintenance, the ultimate wearing-down of purpose into process, of process into pattern, of pattern into the simple fact of continuation. It does not know the narrator. It does not know anything, in the old sense of knowledge as distinction, as the separation of subject from object, of self from world. It knows only the continuation of its walking, its touching, its lowing, its being-there in the district that has become indistinguishable from its own extended body.

The narrator will become part of it, perhaps. The narrator may already be part of it, may have always been part of it, the distinction between arrival and return as eroded as everything else in this place. The archives do not say. The archives, what remains of them, are themselves part of the pattern now, part of the template, part of the accumulated mineral hunger that organizes and dissolves and organizes again in the ochre channels of the Retting Yards.

The Warden walks on. The converters breathe. The lowing continues, voice and not-voice, message and not-message, the sound of a place that has become a body that has become a process that has become time itself, refining, compressing, extracting something essential from its passage that no one will ever see, that no one needs to see, that exists only in the continuing, in the persistence, in the ancient made material slowly wearing toward the flatness that is already almost complete.
