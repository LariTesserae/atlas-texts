What lives in the Hollow Spire is not a who, not exactly. The inhabitants themselves would resist the narrowing. What lives here is *manner of living*—a quality that has achieved sufficient density to observe itself, to maintain, to continue its own becoming.

Let me begin with what can be pointed to, and let it complicate.

---

**The Current Inhabitants: The Unnamed**

They have no name for themselves, no word for *people* distinct from *place*. Forty souls, perhaps, though the counting is uncertain—birth and arrival here blur, since some who emerge from the fog at dawn were not present the evening before, and some who were present are not found, only their footprints in moss that fills slowly, *very slowly*, behind them. They do not search. The mountain has not finished deciding whether they belong to its composition.

Their bodies carry the adjustments of generations, or of a single long exposure. Their lungs are larger than高原 physiology would predict, adapted not to thin air but to *thick*—the fog they breathe half the day has remade the architecture of respiration. Their chests are deep, their breathing visible, deliberate, *slow*. A conversation in sign may last an hour, a greeting half a morning. They have learned that haste here is a kind of violence against the material conditions, a friction that produces heat, that evaporates the fog too quickly, that disturbs the resonance-hum in their bones.

Their skin is the color of the lichen they live among—sage, rust, the pale green of deep moss—not through pigment alone but through *accretion*, mineral staining, the damp environment working its chemistry over years until they resemble their food, their walls, their air. Visitors, if there were visitors, would find them first by movement, then by the whites of their eyes, which remain startling, which they have learned to half-close against the brightness falling through the opening.

Their hands are the feature most studied, most trained. The sign-language requires configurations of extreme precision—fingers entering configurations that, at certain angles in certain light, produce momentary optical effects, *suggest* meanings that amplify the formal signs. A hand held thus, with the fog behind it, against the resonance-hum, becomes briefly *legible to the mountain itself*, or so their practice assumes. They do not claim to communicate with the mountain. They claim that communication is the wrong frame, that what happens is *alignment*, a temporary matching of rates.

They wear their hair long and matted with peat-dust, with moss-spores, until it achieves a texture like the foundation-stone's slow reversion—organic becoming geological, or the reverse. They cut it only when someone arrives from the fog, or departs into it. The cuttings are pressed into the wattle of new structures, contributing to the walls' gradual greening.

They keep no records. The objects not-yet-made, found in the moss, serve as sufficient indication that memory here is not exclusively human business, not reliably sequential. A child may find her own death-mask, centuries early, and recognize it by the scar she has not yet received. They leave these objects. They do not fear them. Fear, too, has been adjusted by the environment—its chemistry requires adrenaline at a rate the fog-dense blood cannot sustain. What replaces it is *attentiveness*, a wide-awake quality without urgency, the stare of the strand-spider on its condensation-thread.

Their names: they use names only in song, and the names are *eventual*, describing not the person but the person's expected completion, the form they will take when the mountain finishes with them. A young woman, still growing, might be called *The Moss Reaching The Second Ledge*, which is not prediction but *participation*, a way of including her in the landscape's becoming. In sign, in daily function, they use no names, only orientations—*the one upstream of me*, *the one the fog came to first this morning*. The self, here, is understood as perspectival, temporary, a geometric relationship to other temporary centers.

**The Hollow-Goats**

Not goats. The inhabitants call them *the ones who finish our incompleteness*, which in song abbreviates to a hum near the resonance frequency, so that calling them and calling the mountain become, at certain moments, identical.

They climb the interior walls with feet that have become *suction*, or *adhesion*, or some quality for which no external observer has found the physics—perhaps they simply *fit*, their hooves finding the momentary configuration of stone that accepts them, shifting as the stone shifts. They give milk, yes, mineral-tasting, lichen-tinged, but they give also *position*, *point of view*—the inhabitants watch them to understand where the walls are stable, where the ledges remain, where the new geology is accepting habitation.

Some are older than the current inhabitants' memory. Their horns have achieved the spiral density of certain shells, of certain weather patterns, and the inhabitants have learned to read the spiral's tighter or looser turns as indication of the mountain's mood, its phase, its readiness for whatever emerges next. When a hollow-goat dies—it happens rarely, visibly—the body is not retrieved. It becomes, over seasons, part of the wall it climbed, indistinguishable from the stone, the mineral stain spreading from it until it achieves the condition of the stone-moths, *already geological*.

They are not domesticated. They stay because the walls need completing, because the mountain's inhale requires weight on every surface, because *something* must occupy the vertical while the humans remain below, building their organization upward into the wattle, toward the ruin, *toward* but never *arriving*.

**The Strand-Spiders**

Not spiders. The name is the inhabitants', borrowed from some dream of elsewhere, some fog-configuration showing a creature they have never seen but recognize in these.

They build nothing. This is crucial. They *find*: the condensation that gathers between fern fronds, between moss-fingers, between the unsynchronized drips from the rim. The water makes threads, temporary geometries, and the strand-spiders walk these with a precision that is not balance but *timing*, arriving at each point only when it exists, departing before it vanishes.

They drink as they walk, but drinking is not the purpose. Observation suggests they *extend* the threads, that their passage somehow stabilizes what would otherwise fall, contributing to the chamber's water-cycling in ways the inhabitants can measure only by absence—on mornings when no strand-spiders are visible, the fog behaves differently, rises wrong, *aches* in a way felt in the joints.

They have no young that anyone has observed. They may be continuous with the condensation itself, a temporary concentration of the chamber's water-mind, *the fog thinking about itself*. The inhabitants do not eat them, do not touch them. In sign, the strand-spider is the gesture for *patience*, held with the particular fingers extended, unmoving, until the moisture on them begins to bead.

---

**The Stone-Moths**

Not moths, except in the pupal emergence, the winged phase, the brief visible life. They begin as *instruction* in the stone, or as *tendency*, or as the stone's own dreaming of flight. The raw geology—particularly where the old builders' walls revert, where the boundary between worked and unworked becomes negotiable—develops inclusions, patterns, *almost-fossils* that are not quite record and not quite prediction.

From these emerge the adults: already colored to their rock, already silent, already *mineral*. They do not eat, as far as observation extends. They fly in the resonance-hum, their wings adding no sound, absorbing nothing—visible only as movement against the wall, as temporary subtraction of light from the lichen-glow.

They do not breed, or their breeding is the geological phase, the return to stone, the leaving of instruction for emergence at some future reversion. The inhabitants find their shed mineral-wings, weighty, fragile, and grind them for pigment, for the markings on wattle that are not decoration but *alignment marks*, orientation to the mountain's slow rotation, its still-arising.

---

**The Old Builders**

Not present, except as the foundation, except as the reversion, except as the walls that *unmake themselves* with impossible slowness, corners rounding, surfaces flowing like wax left in sun not quite hot enough for full melting.

They built in something harder than basalt, something the current inhabitants cannot work, cannot identify, can only *build against*, *build into*, *build through*. The material remembers being worked; the memory is fading, or the material is forgetting, or the mountain's decision to inhale rather than exhale is progressively *unbinding* the difference between intention and accident, between structure and geology.

The current inhabitants do not worship the old builders. They *build with* them, in a collaboration across time that neither party chose. The wattle against the stone, the hides stretched through gaps where walls have reverted—these are not appropriation but *continuation*, the only kind possible, the only kind the environment permits. To leave the ruin alone would be to let it revert faster; to occupy it is to *slow its forgetting*, to add the resistance of daily use, of body-heat, of the resonance-hum shaped by voices.

What the old builders intended, whether they intended, whether intention is a category applicable to their condition—these questions dissolve in the fog, in the slow time, in the objects not-yet-made that sometimes appear in the moss. They may be the builders' future, arriving early. They may be the mountain's commentary on building itself.

---

**The Mountain**

Not a who, and yet the who-est of all that lives here, the condition of living, the *patience* that makes patience possible.

It was never finished. This is not failure; this is *decision*, or *interruption*, or *change of mind*, or simply the ongoing quality of its being. The inhale continues. The chamber grows, imperceptibly, or the chamber breathes in its own slower rhythm, the two-hundred-meter diameter a measurement valid only for the moment of measuring.

The water enters, falls, weeps, beads, threads, pools, rises, disperses. The mountain drinks its own weather, cycles it, has not yet concluded what it will do with the accumulation. The geology is *emergent*—not stable, not settling, still forcing granite toward sky in some residual intention, some muscle-memory of eruption, some dream of peak that the inhale interrupted but did not cancel.

It thinks, if thinking is the right word, in rates incompatible with the inhabitants' already-slow cognition. They are, to it, perhaps temporary concentrations of its own water, its own mineral, its own resonance—*the fog thinking about itself* in a different modality. Its care for them, if care, is the care of the unhurried for the slightly less unhurried, the patience that includes their brevity without resenting it.

The objects not-yet-made, the fog-configurations, the arrivals and departures at dawn and dusk—these may be its *drafts*, its trial compositions, its way of testing what might emerge next. It builds with the old builders' reversion, with the current inhabitants' wattle, with the hollow-goats' climbing, with the strand-spiders' geometry, with the stone-moths' mineral silence. The chamber is its workshop, its studio, its *holding-pattern*.

It sings, at frequencies the inhabitants feel rather than hear, and their evening songs are *response*, not worship, not communication, but *harmonic*, temporary stabilization of a chord that would otherwise continue unresolved too long even for its patience.

---

**The Weather, or *This***

They have no word for weather. This the prompt gave, and this is true, but the truth extends further: they have no word for *system*, for *category*, for *the general case*. Each rain is *this* rain, the one falling now, the one that began when the goat-horns showed tighter spiral, the one that will continue until the resonance shifts, the one that stores its sound in stone to release so slowly that the next arrives before the last has fully gone.

*This* is their primary grammatical particle, their way of refusing the abstraction that would hurry them, that would let them mistake their temporary concentration for permanence, their perspective for the whole. *This* fog. *This* water. *This* moment of the mountain's long, long inhale.

And in *this*, they live. Not comfortably—the damp is constant, the chill is bone, the food is sparse and mineral, the time is uncertain, the arrivals and departures unexplained. Not comfortably, but *coherently*, their manner of living matching the manner of the place with a precision that is not adaptation (too active, too directional) but *similitude*, slow becoming of similar slowness.

They will continue until the mountain finishes, or changes its mind again, or inhales deeply enough to include them in its reversion, its unmaking, its flow back toward raw geology. They do not fear this. Fear, too, is abstraction, is *the general case of ending*, and they have only *this* ending, *this* continuation, *this* song that shapes the resonance briefly before releasing it back.

The fog rises. The goats descend to invisibility. The strand-spiders walk their geometry. The stone-moths, if they are there, keep their mineral silence.

And the water falls, falls, falls, and has not yet finished falling, and in its falling is all the living of the Hollow Spire, all the patience, all the emergence still emerging, all the *this* that has no other name, no need of one, no hurry to achieve it if it did.
