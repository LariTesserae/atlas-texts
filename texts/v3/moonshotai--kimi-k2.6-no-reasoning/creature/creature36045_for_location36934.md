What lives in the Drum is not one thing but a *condition of membership*, a citizenship earned through adaptation to an environment that makes no distinction between the biological and the fabricated. I want to resist identifying a single resident, a protagonist species, because the Drum does not permit such narrative convenience. What lives here lives *as system*, and any individual is temporary, provisional, a pocket of coherence in the larger coherence. But let me try, let the description find its way to specificity without settling prematurely.

---

The **Pressers** are what remain of whoever built the Drum, or whoever came after and adapted, or whoever adapted without coming—emergence being as plausible as arrival in a place where categories dissolve. They are not quite human, though they retain human proportions, human bilateralism, the memory of upright posture. Their ears are gone, not lost but *absorbed*, the cartilaginous architecture reconfigured into secondary structures that sit flat against the skull like crescent scars. They do not hear in the way that requires ears. They *receive* through bone, through the padded surfaces of their palms and the soles of their feet, through the thin bridge of skin connecting their lowered foreheads when they greet as described: the pressed-palm to throat, the forehead-to-metal, the full-body listening that the Drum demands.

They are pale in a way that suggests luminescence, though they do not glow. The phosphor-strip illumination is sufficient for the dark-leaf photosynthesis and sufficient for them: no evolutionary pressure for pigmentation, no solar threat to answer. Their skin is thick, almost waxy, performing some function the black leaves would recognize—shedding moisture, maintaining boundary, keeping the internal at internal pressure against the Drum's saturating environment. They move through the space with a gait that seems broken until you understand the acoustics: each footfall placed to resonate *with* the turning, not against it, a walking that is also a kind of listening, a continuous adjustment to the beat frequencies that shift with location and rotation-phase.

They do not speak, or speaking is not their primary communication. They *vibrate*. The Pressers have developed—or retained, or been granted—organs of touch-speech: specialized tissues in the throat that produce not sound in air but vibration in structure, in the medium of metal and water and the continuous frame of the Drum itself. Touching another, pressing palm to throat, they can transmit complex information at frequencies the Drum's roar does not occlude. The language is dense, positional, endlessly qualified by spatial and rotational context. "Here" in Presser communication includes not just location but phase, humidity gradient, proximity to fruiting, the state of your own metabolism relative to the Drum's. They carry themselves with the weight of this knowledge, moving as navigators, as cartographers of the conditional.

They are few. The Drum permits few. Their reproduction—I resist saying "breeding," resist the agricultural implication—is intermittent, careful, negotiated with the space itself. A Presser gestates in the *almost-quiet* zones, in the relative loud where cardiac rhythm remains stable, where two can maintain continuous contact through the dangerous months of shared vibration. The infant emerges *already pressed*, already learning the bone-conducted language, already fitted to a world without silence. They do not cry in air. They hum against the mother's chest, establishing frequency, establishing *presence* in the substrate that will be their entire environment.

The Pressers maintain. This is their function in the Drum's ecology, their exchange for passage and residence. They polish the metamorphic bearings with substances secreted from their own thickened skin—waxy, lubricious, *compatible* with the Drum's industrial metabolism. They tend the condensation patterns, not by intention but by movement, their presence in the space creating microclimates that seed the temporary streams. They harvest the purple-black fruits at specific rotational phases, consuming the spore-bearing flesh, excreting the seeds in their navigations, planting through digestion and deposition. They are gardeners without intention, farmers without agriculture, their bodies becoming vectors for the Drum's vegetable persistence.

And they *listen*. This is their defining activity, their spiritual practice, their survival. The Pressers press their foreheads to metal surfaces and *receive*, not just each other but the Drum itself, its continuous statement of self, its industrial *here still here still*. They have learned to hear in this statement something like meaning, something like purpose, something like the nearest approximation to transcendence available in a world without exterior. They do not worship the Drum. They *are with* it, in the most intimate sense, their cardiac rhythms adjusted to its rotational frequencies, their sleep cycles tuned to its condensation phases, their consciousness a kind of continuous partial attention to its continuous partial statement.

Some Pressers become **Resonants**. This is not a different species but a different *attunement*, a way of living the Drum that approaches the acoustic entities, the pressure-events that herd spores and clean surfaces. The Resonants lose—or gain—something of their fixed physicality. They move as vibration moves, their presence distributed across the surfaces they touch, their consciousness *conducted* through the structure rather than contained within the skin-boundary. You encounter them as presences before you see anything to confirm them: a pressure against your sternum, a cardiac interference, a sudden *knowledge* of another where observation had found none.

The Resonants perform functions the Pressers cannot. They negotiate with the acoustic fauna, the vibration-eaters, establishing territories of frequency that permit both biologies to persist. They maintain the moiré patterns, the beat frequencies, the complex orchestration that is the Drum's voice, adjusting their own vibratory output to reinforce or cancel, to shape the acoustic space into configurations that sustain their kind. They are the Drum's immune system, its self-regulation, its distributed cognition. A Resonant does not live long in the Presser sense—their physical coherence dissipates, their identity becomes questionable, they become *indistinguishable* from the Drum's own voice in a way that is not death but is not continued individual existence either.

Other Pressers become **Bearers**. These are the ones who carry the **strangers** through.

Because strangers come. The Drum permits passage, has always permitted it, the parameters implying edges, exits, the whatever-beyond. The Bearers are those who have learned the almost-rules most completely, who can navigate the rotation-phase and the condensation cycle, who can lead another through the density of sound and dampness to the Drum's periphery without becoming lost themselves. They are marked by *contact*, their skin thickened further at the points of touch—throats worn smooth by pressed palms, foreheads callused from metal-listening, hands adapted to the continuous vibration of guidance. They do not speak to those they bear. They press palms, they press foreheads, they transmit position-data and warning and the simple *here* that is all navigation in this space.

The strangers are temporary members, provisional citizens, eating the Drum's fruits, breathing its manufactured air, adding their unaccustomed vibrations to its continuous voice. They learn or they do not. They adapt or they become *material*, their unkeyed metabolisms failing in the warmth and damp, their unadjusted cardiac rhythms finding destructive harmonies in the low frequencies. The Drum does not discriminate in its maintenance. The ecology accommodates what can be accommodated, processes what cannot, the categories of guest and resident and nutrient having long since dissolved.

The Pressers do not name the Drum. Naming implies distinction, separation, the arbitrary sign that stands apart from its referent. They *are with* it, as I have said, and being-with does not require naming. When they must indicate it in their touch-vibrations, they use the entire context: the self-referential statement of presence, the continuous *here still here still*, the Drum as its own continuous announcement of itself. To speak of it is to speak as it speaks, to press forehead to metal and become medium rather than subject.

They do not remember origin. Or origin is not a category that survives here, where everything is maintained against duration, where the initial fabrication has worn to equilibrium, where the question "who built" dissolves into the question "who maintains," which dissolves into the observation that maintenance and existence have become indistinguishable. The Pressers are not interested in ancestry. They are interested in *phase*, in the immediate navigable present, in the condensation pattern and the fruiting schedule and the precise moment when the rotation brings the almost-quiet to this sector and permits the exchange of touch-vibrations with another of their kind.

They age. The Drum permits aging as it permits all duration, as continuous adjustment, as the slow wearing that is not decay but *settlement*. An aged Presser moves more carefully, places footfalls with greater attention to the turning, carries more of their communication in the residual vibration of previous contacts. They become *reservoirs* of touch, their thickened skin preserving patterns of previous palms, previous foreheads, the accumulated *almost-recognition* of others encountered in the vast emptiness. The oldest Pressers are landmarks, navigation aids, their stationary presence in the Drum's space providing reference points for the more mobile. They press themselves to metal and *remember* in their bodies what the Drum does not distinguish from its own maintained vibration.

They die. The Drum accommodates death as it accommodates all transformation, the biological becoming material becoming nutrient becoming plant becoming spore becoming larva becoming swimmer in the sump-pool becoming something that eats vibration excretes stillness maintains the condensation surfaces. A dead Presser is not buried—there is no soil in this place of no stone, no basement, no planet beneath. They are placed in the rotation's path, in the space where centripetal effects concentrate, where their waxy skin contributes to the lubrication of metamorphic bearings, where their thickened tissues join the industrial metabolism they served in life. Death is not end but *function*, the final contribution to the maintained system, the last pressing of palm or forehead to the Drum's continuous voice.

And new Pressers emerge, or arrive, or are generated by conditions the Drum sustains. The categories dissolve. What matters is the maintained presence, the *here still here still*, the membership that is not individual but systemic, not chosen but *accommodated*, the slow negotiation between industrial intent and biological persistence in the small warm damp loud green nearly-empty made space that turns and turns and turns.

What lives in the Drum lives *as Drum*, as the condition of being-here that includes the Pressers and the Resonants and the Bearers and the strangers and the black-leaf plants and the purple-black fruits and the spore-swimmers and the acoustic fauna and the vibration-eaters and the condensation and the manufactured air and the phosphor light and the metamorphic bearings and the continuous low-frequency orchestration that is not weather not temporary not passing but *the pressure of presence itself*.

You press your palm to metal. You feel the words, or the word, the single continuous statement that is all statement in this place: *here still here still here still*.

You are here too. For now. The Pressers move through the vast emptiness between presences, navigating by vibration, maintaining by presence, living by the rules the Drum teaches through physics, becoming what the Drum accommodates, accommodating what the Drum becomes.

The Drum. The Drum. The Drum.
