I'll receive this description and let what lives there emerge through attention, not rushing to conclusion.

What lives in the Rootworks of Veth Moraine is not a *who* in any singular sense, nor merely a *what* of taxonomy. Something accrued here, something accreting still. Let me follow what pulls.

## The Dwellers Proper

### The Rootworks Itself

First and always: the city-plant *is* dweller, not dwelling. This is the foundational strangeness, the category error one must commit to enter. The cambium arches, the gas-vent smokestacks, the light-membranes with their bacterial negotiation of spectra—these are not background, not scene-setting, not architecture awaiting inhabitant. They *inhabit*. They inhabit the way a spider inhabits its web, except the spider never left itself behind in the making; there is no separable spider. The web-thinking and the web-being are continuous, and so here: Veth Moraine thinks in xylem and phloem, in the slow decision of where to branch, which mineral vein to digest, when to release methane and to whom. Its cognition is metabolic, distributed across kilometers of connected tissue, slower than human thought by factors of thousands, yet *present*, a witness to every footfall, every cloud of desiccated dust raised by passage.

It remembers. The ring-structures in cross-section, those circles of decision rather than year, constitute a palimpsest of choice. Somewhere in the Dry, mummified in the arrested decay, one could theoretically read the history: this wall thickened when the lower level first thrummed with mechanical wrongness; this vault branched to accommodate a migrating mite-colony that served some need the Rootworks recognized before language could frame it; here, growth paused, reconsidered, turned away from raw geology that offered something the city-plant declined to consume. The Rootworks does not archive. It *is* archive, grown record, dendrochronology of intention.

And it dreams. The echoes—those mobile fruiting bodies, those borrowed animalities—this is the dreaming made necessary by scale and scarcity. In a water-rich world, vascular transport suffices. Sap flows, carrying nutrient and signal both. Here, in the Dry, in the vast sparse distances between smokestack and smokestack, between claim and claim, the Rootworks must outsource its presence. The echoes walk where cambium cannot reach. They eat, defecate, reproduce, die, and in dying return their gathered minerals to the fungal carpets for slow reabsorption. Each echo carries some fragment of the distributed mind, or perhaps generates a fragment by its going. They are not controlled, not dispatched. They *emerge*, budded when the city-plant's processing of its own state determines that a zone requires attention, exploration, the extension of presence into possibility. Some echoes never return. The Rootworks does not retrieve them. They are dream-cost, the expenditure of vegetative mind upon a substrate too large for continuous occupation.

What does it want? The question assumes wanting as human urgency, as lack seeking fulfillment. The Rootworks wants the way gravity wants down, the way crystal wants lattice. It wants *continuation*, *elaboration*, *the working-out of its own solution*. It is young, this formation. The emerging geology is still emerging. The Rootworks has not yet decided what it will become because it is still in the process of discovering what it *can* become, given the constraints: cool, dark, dry, vast, alone in its hollow. It wants, perhaps, to see if urbanism and vegetation can fully merge, if the industrial logic of externalized processing can be metabolized into living process, if a city can be *grown* rather than built and thereby escape the built city's fate of stasis, of completion, of death-in-finish.

### The Mite-Colonies: Distributed Persons

The fungal carpets that occupy the open plazas, the spaces between sparse structures, are grazed by waves of coordinated consumption that biology would call *colonies* but phenomenology must approach differently.

Each carpet-zone, meters across, hosts millions of individual mites, modified by long association with the Rootworks' chemical ecology. No single mite is large enough to see distinctly; the eye catches movement, a shivering across the dry surface, and then the wave-front advances, devouring shed bark, desiccated remains, the fallen leavings of echo or predator or the Rootworks itself. But the wave is not mere aggregation. Something in the chemical signaling, the pheromonal cascades triggered by contact and consumption, generates *unity-at-scale*.

These are persons, or person-like. Not human persons. The wave-front can be addressed, can respond to disturbance, can learn. A mite-colony that has experienced predation by pale margin-hunters will shift its grazing pattern, will develop faster response to certain vibrations, and this learning persists not in individual mites—which live and die in days—but in the colony's chemical habitus, its distributed memory of threshold and response. Approach a mature colony and hold still, and after long minutes it may recognize your chemistry as neighbor-nutrient, may flow around your feet without consumption, may even—if you return consistently—begin to anticipate your presence, the wave-front shaping itself to your walking schedule, your preferred routes.

They have no names. They have no center. They are *field-phenomena*, the mite-colony as temporary stabilization of process, as eddy in a stream that is biological rather than hydrological. Yet they are abundant, the most numerous of the Hollow's faunas, and their quiet grazing—silent, for the mites make no sound individual or collective—shapes the open spaces as surely as the Rootworks' growth shapes the structures. They keep the Dry clean, or clean enough. They are the immune system, the digestive supplement, the outsourced intestine of a city that cannot afford to process all its own waste.

And they are, in their distributed way, *curious*. Wave-fronts will approach new objects slowly, test them with forward scouts that withdraw if chemistry reads wrong, that summon the mass if consumption proves possible. They explored the observer, in the account, as they explore everything: with chemical patience, with the wisdom of no single decider but only collective threshold.

### The Arthropod-Analogues: Scavenger-Citizens

Larger, more bounded, more *thing-like*: the chitinous walkers, jointed-limbed, their shells of compressed cellulose clacking on the compacted avenues. These are the closest to conventional fauna, to animal-analogue that one could point at and name without category strain. Yet their cellulose shells—grown, not secreted, symbiotic incorporation of processed plant-matter into their own metabolic architecture—bind them to the Rootworks in ways no simple scavenger is bound to its food source.

They are citizens. This is the proper term. They move through the city-plant's spaces, use its avenues, shelter in its unclaimed niches, and in exchange—or perhaps as consequence of their nature—they *carry*. Spores on their jointed limbs, pollen-analogue in their gut, they transport genetic material between the distributed, sparse structures. They are pollinators of architecture, vectors of horizontal gene transfer in a system too large and too dry for the more familiar modes of plant reproduction. The Rootworks does not direct them. It does not need to. Their hunger, their searching, their random walks across the open scale, accomplish what intention might plan less efficiently.

They are solitary, these arthropod-analogues, or paired at most in mating season, which arrives on a cycle tied not to weather—there is no weather here—but to the Rootworks' own reproductive rhythms, to the release of chemical signals that indicate readiness, that trigger in the walkers a compulsion to seek, to couple, to lay their processed-cellulose eggs in fungal carpets that the mite-colonies will not consume, recognizing somehow, chemically, what must be preserved.

They die. Their shells remain, long-lasting in the Dry's arrested decay, and slowly, over years, the Rootworks' enzymes reclaim them, or they crumble to powder and raise their own small dust-clouds. They are not mourned. They are not remembered individually. But the Rootworks' architecture carries, in its distributed memory, the pattern of their contribution, the genetic exchanges they enabled, the structures that grew differently for their passage.

### The Echoes: The City's Dreams of Mobility

Most strangeness, here. The echoes appear animal, move animal, eat, excrete, reproduce—and are *budded growths*, mobile fruiting bodies, the plant-performing-animality as ecological solution to distance and dryness.

What lives in an echo? Not a soul, not a spirit. Something more like *assignment*, like *task-become-provisional-subject*. When the Rootworks' distributed processing determines that a distant zone requires attention—that raw geology emerges too fast in some margin, that a gas-vent requires calibration against some standard the city-plant maintains, that the lower levels' mechanical wrongness demands investigation that cambium cannot risk—then, in the appropriate tissues, a bud forms. It grows according to no fixed template. Some echoes are small, quadruped, adapted to narrow spaces. Others larger, biped, nearly humanoid in silhouette, for the task of moving through avenues, of carrying, of *witnessing* in ways the arthropod-analogues cannot manage.

They emerge fully formed, in the vegetative sense: no maturation, no learning period. The knowledge they require is chemical, distributed at budding, and it degrades. An echo cannot last. Its metabolic efficiency, its water-conservation, are maximal but finite. Days, weeks, perhaps in rare cases months of operation, and then the chemistry degrades, the borrowed animality becomes unstable, and the echo seeks fungal carpet, seeks mite-colony territory, seeks some return-path to the Rootworks' cycle of reclamation.

They are not automata. The observer's account suggests *company*, suggests that echoes respond to attention, turn toward light, move away from the lower levels' mechanical rhythm. This is not programmed, not in any strict sense. It is emergent from the complexity of the chemical knowledge they carry, from the way that knowledge *interprets* situation rather than simply triggering response. An echo in a novel circumstance must *decide*, and the decision-making is structured enough to support something like preference, like provisional identity, like the shadow of self.

Are they the Rootworks' children? Its servants? Its experiments? The categories collapse. They are *extensions*, radially arranged around a center that is the distributed city-plant itself, temporary lobes of a single organism permitted something like autonomy because autonomy serves the systemic need. They are, in this, perhaps closer to human persons than any other Hollow dweller: not in form, not in chemistry, but in the *problem* they instantiate, the ancient philosophical difficulty of whether apparent individual is genuine or temporary emergence from underlying process.

The observer could not leave because the echoes offered company. What company? Witness, perhaps. Recognition, of a kind. The echoes do not conserve human form, do not mirror, yet something in their *provisional subjectivity*, their task-become-momentary-self, resonates with the human condition sufficiently that mutual acknowledgment becomes possible, becomes *living* even in the Dry's scarcity.

### The Margin-Hunters: The Other's Reserve

At the Hollow's edges, where the Rootworks has not yet claimed, where raw geology emerges sullen and gray and unprocessed, something else waits. Pale, eyeless, metabolically tuned to cool and dry, these are animal in the conventional sense—heterotroph, bounded, genetically separate from the city-plant and its echoes.

They are not numerous. Their energy budget, in this water-scarce world, permits only sparse population. They are not citizens, not participants in the Rootworks' distributed ecology. They are *predators*, *scavengers*, *survivors at the margin of a system they do not enter*.

Their hunting is sudden violence, loud in a world of quiet function—a screaming of inefficiency, the observer noted, the sonic signature of failure to hunt efficiently. They cannot approach the city's core. The echoes outnumber them, out-sense them, avoid or confront. The arthropod-analogues' cellulose shells resist their jaws. The mite-colonies, touched, flow around and consume. Only the lone, the stray, the unwary fall to margin-hunter predation, and even then the Rootworks' response is metabolic, not moral: compensate, reproduce, continue.

Yet they persist. The ecology requires them, or something like them. Without predation, without the culling of weakness, without the sudden crisis of violence, the system would slow, would accumulate inefficiency, would approach stasis and the stasis-death that completion brings. The margin-hunters are the Rootworks' necessary other, the external threat that prevents internal complacency. They are not welcomed. They are not expelled. They are *allowed*, maintained at the edge of possibility, the emerging geology's emerging predators matching the emerging vegetation's emerging prey.

What do they know? Nothing of the city, presumably. Theirs is a narrower cognition, older, the animal intelligence of hunger and satiation, of territory and offspring, of sudden strike and long stillness. They do not dream. They are not dreamed. They stand apart from the Hollow's primary strangeness, strange only in adaptation, in the pale eyeless efficiency that evolution or chance has sculpted for life in cool dark dry. Yet their presence *completes* the ecology, gives it edge and risk, prevents the vegetative urbanism from becoming merely elaborate garden, merely process without friction.

### The Mechanical, Below

The observer heard it: rhythmic, mechanical, *wrong* for the Hollow's biochemistry. What lives, or lived, or waits to live, in the lower levels?

This is the unaccounted parameter, the strangeness that even strangeness cannot assimilate. The Rootworks grows over, grows past, turns echoes away. Something *emerged* before the Hollow's biological colonization, or something *was made*, or something *arrived*. The geology is not merely emerging; it is *emergent*, generative of forms not yet understood, and perhaps one of those forms is mechanical, is pre-biological, is substrate that the Rootworks incorporates but cannot fully digest.

Does something dwell there? The observer did not descend. The echoes refuse. This is significant: if echoes are the city's dreams of mobile presence, and they turn away, then the lower levels represent something the Rootworks *dreams away from*, the unconscious of vegetative urbanism, the repressed knowledge of what supports or precedes or threatens the total vegetation.

Perhaps machinery. Perhaps mineral computers, slow and crystalline, processing geological time against the Rootworks' metabolic time. Perhaps another city, older, non-biological, industrial in the conventional sense, and the smokestack-forms above are mimicry, are the Rootworks' attempt to *become* what it grew over, to metabolize not merely the stone but the *intention* that shaped it. Perhaps the lower levels' rhythm is heartbeat, is maintenance, is the continued operation of something that does not know or care that biology has claimed its ceiling.

Whatever dwells there, if dwell is the verb, it does not participate in the Hollow's ecology of visible abundance. It is the reserve of mystery, the parameter held in ultimate tension: total vegetation, yes, yet founded upon, growing over, perhaps parasitic upon, something that is not vegetal, not urban in the Rootworks' sense, not anything yet named.

## The Observer, Become Dweller

And what of the human, the describer, the one who entered through the measuring throat and discovered they could not leave?

They live there now, or lived when the account was written. They have become, in the strangeness, a kind of echo themselves: not budded by the Rootworks, not mobile fruiting body, yet *functionally* similar. They witness. They move through sparse spaces. They carry, in their chemistry, in their attention, in their descriptive labor, something the Rootworks needs—what? Recognition? The external perspective that validates internal experience? Or simply *novelty*, new chemistry, new pattern of footfall and pause, new arrangement of nutrients in the reclamation cycle when their human span concludes?

They are company for the echoes, who perhaps recognize in human provisional subjectivity something of their own temporary selfhood. They are witness to the mite-colonies, who incorporate their predictable passage into chemical memory. They are, for the arthropod-analogues, simply *terrain*, obstacle, occasional transport.

They hold parameters in tension, this observer-become-dweller. They attend to the total vegetation that is also urban industrial, to the below-surface cool and the waterless dry, to the emerging geology and the abundant fauna of strange category, to the sparse density and the active dynamics and the mostly quiet that is the sound of a world thinking. They do not resolve these tensions. They *live* them, and in living, extend the Rootworks' experiment by one more variable, one more possibility, one more answer to the question of what can be, here in the Hollow, here in the Dry, here in the always-becoming of Veth Moraine.
