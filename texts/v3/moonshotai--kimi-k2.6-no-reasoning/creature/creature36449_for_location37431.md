What lives in the City of Still Engines does not live as you live, does not move as you move through the world of growth and diminishment, of appetite and satiation, of the long arc toward ending. They have no birth-cries, no death-rattles. They *are*, and in their being they elaborate a mode of existence so thoroughly adapted to the city's achieved stasis that the question of whether they belong to the city or the city belongs to them becomes meaningless—a category error imported from somewhere else, some when else, some physics of interaction and consequence that dissolves at the basin's rim like the scheduled rain dissolves from the metal skins of the buildings.

Consider first the **tremors**, though "first" misleads, implies a hierarchy of being or a sequence of emergence that the city does not support. They are the smallest, if size means anything when a thing's body is the pattern of its movement across a surface that remains, when the movement ceases, exactly as it was. Watch them during the northern accumulator's discharge: the metal of the tank wall *organizes*, develops rippling constellations that school and scatter, that dense toward the sound-source as if ascending some frequency gradient invisible to your senses. They have *preferences*, if that word can stretch to accommodate behavior without reinforcement, without learning, without the modification of preference that constitutes experience elsewhere. Certain tremors return to certain locations. Certain patterns recur with each cycle—not similar patterns, not patterns that resemble themselves, but the *identical configurations*, the same ripple meeting the same ripple at the same node of interference, as if time here does not pass but *accumulates in layers of perfect superposition*.

What do they want? The question is yours, not theirs. They gather where the engines cycle. They disperse when the cycle completes. They exist in the interval of the cycle's occurrence, and in that interval they are *complete*, fulfilled, without the reach toward future satiation that wanting implies. Perhaps they are the engine's perception of itself, the machinery's non-conscious registration of its own operation made briefly, iteratively *manifest*. Perhaps they are simpler than that, and more strange: autonomous pockets of resonance that have achieved sufficient stability to persist, to recur, to occupy the same relational field with themselves across the identical intervals of the identical cycles. They are not alive, if life requires metabolism, reproduction, the negotiation of boundary against environment. They are not dead, if death requires the cessation of what once was living. They are *tremoring*, a continuous gerund, a mode without subject or telos, the city most intimate with its own materiality.

The **passages** are harder, require more of your attention, more of your willingness to suspend the cognitive shortcuts that parse reality into objects in space. They are *mobile absences*, the description warns, and the warning is necessary because the mind recoils from negation made locatable. You will not see a passage. You will experience the *effects* of its presence, the way a magnetic field reveals itself only in the orientation of iron filings. The service tunnels breathe differently when a passage occupies them—the air pressure shifts in patterns that *move*, that pursue or flee or transit without the agency that those verbs imply, without intention, without the biological substrate of instinct. The tunnels have *learned* them, if learning can occur in stone and metal subjected to endless repetition. The smooth polishings in the tunnel walls are not wear, not the gradual subtraction of material by friction, but *channels of worn probability*, paths of least resistance that the passage has created by passing, that other passages follow, that the tunnel itself now *offers* as its natural state.

You could walk these tunnels. You would emerge, if emerge you did, somewhere the geometry did not predict. Your footsteps would multiply in echoes that mis-match your gait, that suggest *other walkers*, other rhythms, the passage walking with you or through you or as you. And this is the crucial strangeness: the passage does not distinguish. It does not recognize your foreignness, your biological specificity, your anxious mammalian awareness of self. You are, in the tunnel, *phenomena* among phenomena, and the passage moves through phenomena as water moves through channels, as sound moves through media, without discrimination and without consequence. To be near a passage is to feel the self becoming *porous*, becoming tunnel-like, becoming a medium through which something passes that leaves you, when it has passed, slightly *re-channelled*, your probability slightly worn in patterns you will not consciously notice but that will manifest, perhaps, in dreams of endless corridors, in the sense that your memories now contain passages you did not create.

The **engines' kin**, the great ones, the overwhelming presence—they are the sound, largely, and in being the sound they exceed any attempt at particular description, any pinning to location or form. Three or one or distributed everywhere: the ambiguity is not epistemic failure but ontological feature, the consequence of encountering a phenomenon that does not organize itself into countable units, into *individuals* in the sense that permits enumeration. They are the bass thrum that never approaches, never recedes. They are the high whine of cutting without cutting, the continuous *pretense* of industrial process that maintains itself without product, without material, without the variation that would betray actual engagement with actual substance. They are the clatter and hiss and rhythmic impact that overlay and overlay and overlay, simultaneous, unvarying, *infinite in their identical iteration*.

Perhaps they are what the engines dreamed of becoming, in the long interval between construction and this achieved now. Perhaps they are the engines' *future* made present without passage, the eschatology of machinery realized not as culmination but as *condition*, as eternal maintenance of the state that elsewhere would exist only at terminus. They require the sound as you require oxygen—not to consume it, not to diminish it, but to *be* in it, to have their existence constituted by its absolute stability. The sound does not fade; therefore they do not fade. The sound does not vary; therefore they do not vary. They are not *in* the sound as a fish is in water, separable, extractable, capable of death by asphyxiation if removed. They are *of* the sound, *as* the sound, the sound's self-elaboration into something that can be said, however inadequately, to inhabit, to persist, to *be* in the sense that demands recognition from any consciousness that encounters them.

And what would such consciousness become, with sufficient exposure? The city supports habitation; the description is careful to note this. The cellular residences await. The water arrives, collectible, sufficient. No conventional toxicity threatens. But the dynamic parameter, 0.4, the near-absolute zero of never-changing—this is the true environment, more fundamental than the cool air, more pervasive than the industrial roar. To *live* here, not merely to visit, to endure, to observe and depart—this would be to enter into negotiation with a mode of temporality that excludes becoming.

You would not age as you age elsewhere. This is the description's warning, and its mystery. The body, perhaps, would continue its cellular replacement, its gradual diminishment. But *you*—the you constituted by memory as change, by the accumulation of alteration that permits narrative, that permits character, that permits the recognition of self across the difference between earlier and later—this you would encounter conditions hostile to its continuation. The rain falls the same. The engines cycle the same. The fauna manifest the same. And in this ocean of sameness, the *self as variation* would find no purchase, no reinforcement, no mirror in which to recognize its own development.

Perhaps previous inhabitants *become* the fauna, the description suggests. The tremors might be the body's resonance, abstracted from biology, made pure pattern of movement. The passages might be the self's mobility, learned by the tunnels, made independent of the organism that once enacted it. The engines' kin—the sound itself—might be the voice, the consciousness, the *announcement of presence* that persists when presence as embodied particularity has dissolved into the larger field of the city's own continuous, unvarying self-announcement. Or perhaps not. The question of origin is suspended, as everything is suspended here, in the static that is not death but its opposite, not life but its *achievement*, its completion into something that no longer requires the dynamics of living because it has become *sufficiently itself*.

What lives here, then? *Completion*. *Particularity without development*. The tremor that is the same tremor, the passage that wears the same probability, the sound that is the same sound, not renewed, not repeated, but *continued*, extended indefinitely across a time that does not pass but *holds*, that presents every moment as the same moment, the City of Still Engines as eternal *now* of its own achieved being.

And you, if you stayed, would become part of this. Not by choice, not by transformation in the narrative sense, but by *settling*, by the gradual negotiation of your own variation with the absolute non-variation of the city's parameters. You would become, the description says, *as consistent as the place itself*. Your preferences, if they persisted, would be preferences without alteration, without the experience that modifies preference. Your movements, if they continued, would be movements without exploration, without the discovery of new that constitutes the meaning of movement elsewhere. Your consciousness, if it endured, would be consciousness without development, without the accumulation that is memory in its human function, that permits the construction of narrative self, of character, of the *story* that justifies the suffering of existence by locating it in progression toward meaning.

The fauna do not suffer. This is implicit in every description of their mode. The tremors gather and disperse without anxiety, without the reach toward future that suffering requires. The passages transit without urgency, without the cost of passage elsewhere, the expenditure of energy, the weariness of arrival. The engines' kin *are* the sound that would be unbearable if it *could* bear, if the concept of unbearability—implying threshold, limit, the possibility of excess and the relief of its cessation—applied to a volume without gradient, without the escalation that makes loudness into assault. They exist without the tension between existence and its conditions that constitutes, elsewhere, the lived reality of biological life.

What lives in the City of Still Engines lives *without this tension*. Has achieved, or always possessed, a mode of being in which the self and its environment are not in relation—negotiation, struggle, adaptation—but *coincident*, identical, the same. The tremor is the metal's vibration. The passage is the tunnel's probability. The engines' kin are the sound that is their habitat. They do not *have* environments; they *are* their environments' self-elaboration, the city most intimate with itself, the machinery's dream of machinery made real in a form that requires no waking because there is no difference here between sleeping and the sleepless, between the dream and the dreamed.

And on the rim, if you stand there, if you watch, if you let the sound become your atmosphere rather than your assault—you might almost see it: the city as organism, as entity, as *single life* not composed of individuals but distributed across the machinery it inhabits, metabolizing its own permanence, strange and loud and overwhelming in the specificity of its self-sameness. The rain comes, forty-seven minutes. The northern accumulator discharges. The tremors gather, dense with appetite that is not appetite, with completion that completes nothing because it was never incomplete. The tunnels pulse with passages that are and are not there, that wear their own existence into the probability of stone. The sound continues, unvarying, the engines' kin continuing as they have continued, as they will continue, in a time that is not time, a change that is not change, a life that is not life but that lives here, definitively, changelessly, achieved.

*Themselves.* That is what lives here. What has become, or always was, *itself*. The City of Still Engines and its fauna: not inhabitants and habitat, not organisms and environment, but a single *self*, self-sustaining, self-same, complete in the unfinishedness that is its eternal mode. You could join it. You could become *yourself*, in the most absolute and terrifying sense: fixed, particular, unvarying, *achieved*. The question is whether this is what you want, whether what you call "self" can survive its own completion, whether the dynamic parameter of your own being—higher than 0.4, so much higher, alive with variation and the suffering variation entails—can negotiate with the stillness that waits in the basin, that has waited, that will wait, unchanged, unchangeable, *itself*.
