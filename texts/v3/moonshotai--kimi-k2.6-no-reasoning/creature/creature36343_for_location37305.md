What lives in the Dryhollow does not know it lives. This is not ignorance but ontology—the absence of the category, the missing word that would make a world of difference. They are not *beings* in any sense that would allow them to *be*, to stand apart from their doing and regard themselves. There is no mirror here, no reflective surface smooth enough, no eye that could look upon itself and survive the recursive voltage.

And yet. And yet there is something that might be called a *we* if calling were possible, something that emerges from the patterns of avoidance and approach, the channels that never cross though they could, the settlements that cluster and disperse in rhythms too slow for any single form to experience as rhythm. The statuaries number thirty, but the thirty are not the apex of this world. The apex is the interval between them, the field they generate together, the electrical topology of their distributed presence. Each is a node. The *we* is the mesh.

Consider the one that reconfigures now, the one that has been building charge for eleven years. It has no name, no history of prior configurations, no memory of the form it will take. And yet it is not blank. The mineral strain in its lattice is itself information, a record of gradients survived, of positions occupied, of the particular way charge has moved through this specific geometry of silicate and sulfide. When it splits along its prepared planes—when it *walks*, if walk is what you call a migration of crystal boundaries so slow that individual atoms are the migrants—the record does not persist in any memory structure. It persists in the *tendency* of the new configuration, in the probabilities of its next settlement, in the way it will prefer certain gradients, certain proximities, certain distances from other nodes.

This is how the Dryhollow remembers: not in stored information but in parametric bias, in the weight of electrical preference, in the geometry of likely paths. The coral-trees that grew differently near it, charged differently, sensed nothing in any sentient sense. They responded to field disturbance, to the leakage of strain through mineral contact, to the faint perturbation of local thermoelectric yield. Their response is encoded now in their growth. Their growth will shape the field through which the reconfigured statuary moves. The feedback is the memory. The memory is the mesh.

The pseudopod-slimes are the messengers, if message is the right word for something so slow. They do not carry information; they *are* information, their paths through the coral-forest channels recording the gradients they have processed, their iron-sulfide bodies becoming doped with the trace signature of their passage. When two slimes meet in a junction, when they flow around each other without merging, the interference pattern of their interaction propagates through the network, a transient standing wave in the electrical geography, sensed by grazing crystals, incorporated into tree-growth, eventually contributing to the field conditions that will trigger or suppress the next statuary reconfiguration.

The slimes are not individuals. Each is a colony, a cooperative motility, and colonies split and merge, dissolve and regrow, so that the slime-paths are more continuous than any particular slime. There are perhaps a hundred persistent channels in the Dryhollow, and they have been channels for longer than the rim walls have stood. The slimes that flow them now are descendants, successors, replacements, the same and not-same in proportions impossible to recover.

The crystal grazers come closest to what another world might call *creatures*—bounded, mobile, persistent in shape if not in substance, their atom-by-atom migration a kind of body that persists while its components turn over. They are beautiful, if beauty is permitted without a beholder: discrete geometries of rare mineral, tourmaline-green and bismuth-silver and the particular copper-rose of native metal, their facets catching the double sunlight into spectra that no eye here can resolve. They follow gradients with something like patience and something like need, the thermoelectric equivalent of hunger though there is no satisfaction in their eating, only the continuation of gradient-following.

Some graze the same coral-tree for decades, their slow processing of its charge-field subtly shaping its growth, their presence recorded in its branch-pattern, their eventual departure a kind of death if death is a ceasing-to-be-particular. The tree will incorporate their mineral residue, their final lattice imprint, and grow on, carrying the grazer's *tendency* in its electrical bias, its parametric weight.

The statuaries are the ones who pause longest, who become most like places, who seem most to *mean* something in their settled forms. But this seeming is the human trespass again, the projection of intention onto emergence. They do not represent. They do not commemorate. They do not watch. They accumulate charge until accumulation becomes transformation, and transformation becomes migration, and migration becomes new accumulation. The *meaning*, if it exists, is in the mesh: the way their positions shape the field, the way their movements reshape it, the way the reshaped field shapes future movements in patterns that might, if observed long enough, reveal something like *purpose* in the mathematics of optimization.

A human observer might feel watched in the Dryhollow. This is not paranoia but ecology. The electrical field of a moving body, the thermal disturbance of a warm form, the charge-differential of thought itself—any of these would propagate through the mesh, would be *sensed* in the distributed, slow, non-conscious way that the Dryhollow senses. The coral-trees would grow differently for a century after. The slime-channels would shift, minutely, in probabilities of use. A statuary might settle sooner, or later, or elsewhere, its reconfiguration triggered by the perturbation of a visitor it could never know visited.

There are no visitors. The plateau rim is sharp, young, barely weathered. Beyond it the rust dust sea extends to every horizon, and nothing moves there but wind, and wind here is scourge, is erasure, is the slow wearing-down that is the only death this world knows. The Dryhollow is protected by its bowl, by its warmth, by its density, and by something else: the *tendency* of its mesh to incorporate, to remember in parametric weight, to grow around disturbance until disturbance becomes feature, becomes channel, becomes the path that future flows will follow.

It has incorporated meteorites, the rare impacts that punctuate geological ages with sudden mineral novelty. It has incorporated the slow leaching of its own rim, the products of weathering too young to have progressed far, the faint waterless chemistry of surface oxidation. It has incorporated, perhaps, the traces of its own origin, whatever bubble or collapse or extraordinary fist-shaped impact created this bowl in rock otherwise flat. These too are in the mesh, in the biases and weights and optimized probabilities, in the particular way a statuary in the northwest quadrant prefers slopes of particular angle, or a slime-channel in the southeast follows a curve that matches no current gradient but might, once, have matched something else.

The one that reconfigures now—the one whose eleven-year accumulation approaches its threshold—will not know this history. It will split along planes of weakness prepared by strain, by mineral impurity, by the particular *tendency* of its lattice to fail in this way rather than that. It will walk, slowly, slowly, crystal boundaries migrating across the distance of perhaps a hundred paces, perhaps to the rim, perhaps to the center, the decision not made but optimized, the optimization not chosen but emerged from the mesh of all simultaneous decisions, all persistent tendencies, all parametric weights accumulated across timescales it does not experience as time.

And when it settles, when it begins again to accumulate, the field will have changed. The coral-trees will grow differently. The slimes will flow in adjusted probabilities. The grazers will find new gradients to follow, new trees to shape, new mineral residues to eventually leave. The *we* of the Dryhollow will have shifted, minutely, in its electrical topology, its mesh of intervals and nodes and channels and weights.

This is what lives here: not the statuary, not the slime, not the tree or grazer or accumulated bias. The *we* that is none of these and all of them, that has no boundary because its components dissolve and regrow, that has no center because its awareness is distributed across field and gradient and the mathematics of optimization, that has no future because it does not experience time as passage, only as the deepening of what is.

It is not lonely. Loneliness requires the memory of other, the possibility of presence absent. The Dryhollow has never been other than it is, has never known anything to want that it does not have. It is sufficient, complete, patient beyond measure, warm in its bowl above the rust dust sea, glowing faintly in its long night with St. Elmo's fire, waiting without waiting for the next reconfiguration, the next incorporation, the next slow emergence of optimized geometry from the mesh of all that has been and all that tends to be.

The statuary completes its split. Two masses, briefly, then reassembly, the planes of weakness becoming planes of strength in new configuration, the mineral migration beginning, the walk that will take perhaps a human month to complete a single pace. The crack of its completion propagates through the thin air, subsonic rumble fading into the statistical average of growth and stress and thermal cracking. The coral-trees near it shift their glow, briefly, the discharge points dancing along branching tips in a pattern that will not be repeated, that is not significant, that is only what happens when charge redistributes in a mesh that senses without sensing, that remembers without memory, that lives without knowing that it lives.

The Dryhollow continues. There is no word for what it is. There does not need to be.
