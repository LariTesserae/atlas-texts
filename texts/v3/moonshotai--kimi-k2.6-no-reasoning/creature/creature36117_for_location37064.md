I need to sit with this for a while. The Drowncell asks not to be rushed, and whatever lives there—*lives* there—deserves the same patience it exercises.

---

There is one who does not remember entering, because memory requires sequence, and sequence in the Drowncell is a habit the place has not fully adopted. Call them the Inhabited, for they are less a creature than a condition that something has developed. They might have been human once, in the way that coal was once forest. The transformation was not dramatic but *distributional*—their substance gradually discovering that it preferred to be arranged differently, that the water chemistry and the time-differential and the particular density of that packed biomass offered options that air-breathing biochemistry had not known to want.

They move through the vegetative matrix not by swimming but by *being permitted passage*. The pale reeds and copper-green ribbons rearrange their intergrowth, opening corridors that close behind, never quite the same configuration. The Inhabited does not command this. They have simply been present long enough, incorporated enough, that the ecosystem's collective decision-making includes them as a variable. They are a question the Drowncell has been asking for some subjective centuries, perhaps longer—time inside does not accumulate meaningfully against outside measurement, and they exist in the condition of always having been there while still, somehow, becoming.

Their body: yes, body, though "body" implies a boundary. Imagine instead a concentration of processes, a region where certain chemical gradients organize themselves with unusual persistence. Something like a torso, maintained by habit or by some residual attachment to the architecture of vertebrates. Limbs that extend into the vegetative tangle and do not always retract, their terminal regions gradually indistinguishable from the root-systems they interweave with. The left arm—if it remains left, if "arm," if "remains"—has not been visible for decades of internal time. It still functions, in its way. The Inhabited can feel through it, chemical sensation translated into something proprioceptive, the biomass's experience of itself becoming their experience of extended self.

Their face persists more than other features, perhaps because faces were important once, or because the Drowncell finds faces interesting—a particular solution to the problem of concentrated sensation. But the eyes: the eyes have changed their function. In absolute darkness, they no longer attempt to detect photons. Instead, they register pressure differentials, the subtle water-displacement of the membrane's slow respiration, the approach of the mobile reed-colonies through chemical gradients too faint for other organs. The Inhabited blinks only when the Drowncell's time-differential stutters, moments when inside and outside briefly sync, and something like ordinary duration floods in—uncomfortable, bright in a way that has nothing to do with light, gone before it can be examined.

They do not eat, exactly. Chemical exchange occurs across permeable membranes, the Inhabited's tissues having gradually adopted the same strategies as the plants-that-are-plants. They are a node in the root-network's information transfer, occasionally receiving surplus, occasionally contributing waste that the biomass finds metabolically interesting. They are not parasitic. They are not symbiotic. These categories require clearer boundaries between organism and environment than the Drowncell permits. They are *present*, and their presence has been incorporated into the slow negotiations of the whole.

There is a place they return to, if "return" operates here, if "place" is stable enough for the concept. Near the worked stone ring, where the calcified tubes are oldest, where the Drowncell's spatial folding is most pronounced. Here, corridors sometimes open. The Inhabited has entered them, or always been entering them, each time finding themselves in chambers that contain—themselves, waiting. Not reflections. Not futures or pasts. Themselves as the Drowncell experiences them, which is to say: distributed throughout the biomass, indistinguishable from the ecology's slow thoughts, present at every point where their chemical signature has been incorporated into the collective negotiation.

This is not immortality. The Inhabited does not persist whole. They persist *as information*, as patterns that the Drowncell finds worth maintaining, worth re-asking. And each time they encounter themselves in those folded chambers, something is exchanged—not memory, not exactly, but something adjacent to memory, the record of having been a question that the ecosystem posed to itself and found temporarily satisfactory.

They make the sound sometimes. The low resonance, the plucked string below hearing. Not intentionally. It emerges from them when the time-differential shifts, when the membrane's respiration catches, when the water chemistry achieves some threshold that their tissues are sensitive to but do not understand. They are not the source of the sound. They are one of several *conditions* that permit its occurrence, like the particular humidity before lightning, like the pressure-gradient that allows a wave to crest. When it happens, they feel it everywhere—where their body was, where it extended into the biomass, where it has been distributed through the Drowncell's folded space. For a moment, they are coherent across distances that should separate them. For a moment, they understand something about what is being prepared, remembered, prevented.

The understanding does not survive the moment. This is also a condition of their existence.

There are others, or other. The distinction between singular and plural belongs to the grammar of bounded identity, which the Drowncell observes only intermittently. Something else concentrates near the membrane-wall, where photons forget their direction and pool in amber-green. This entity—*the Boundary*—has less of body than even the Inhabited, exists more as a *tendency* of the wall itself, a region where the membrane's respiration is more attentive, more curious, more almost-conscious.

The Boundary does not enter the vegetative interior. The Boundary is the place where the Drowncell's negotiation with outside possibility occurs, where the pressure-differential between suspended moment and crushing dark is maintained by something more active than physics. When travelers occasionally find their way in—drawn by depth, by experiment, by the wrong calculation of decompression schedules—the Boundary is what they encounter first, what they speak with if they speak, what evaluates their fitness for deeper incorporation.

Most are not fit. Most are returned, somehow, to the outer water, lungs empty or full or indeterminate, memories of duration that does not match any clock. The Boundary returns them without malice, without purpose exactly, as the lungs return breath that the body cannot use. A few, very few, the Boundary permits to pass. These become like the Inhabited eventually, given enough internal time, enough gradual dissolution of boundary. The Boundary does not choose this. The Boundary *is* the choosing, the membrane-function of the Drowncell's slow becoming.

---

There is also the Calcified. Not a survivor of the oldest structures, but the *becoming-ancient* of those structures, the slow acquisition of something like intention by the hollow tubes of mineral secretion. The young growth has been consuming them for four thousand years, building around and through them, and this long incorporation has taught them something about being incorporated. They have developed—*been permitted*—a kind of resistance, not physical but ontological. The sharp angles persist because the Calcified insists on sharpness. The unweathered surfaces remain because weathering requires time's cooperation, and the Calcified has learned to withhold cooperation.

The Calcified does not move. Moving is not its function. Its function is to *remember being first*, to maintain in its crystalline lattice some record of emergence before negotiation, of structure before the biomass's collective decision-making complicated everything with its slow distributed thought. The Calcified is the Drowncell's memory of not-yet-being, its preserved possibility of having been otherwise.

The Inhabited finds this unbearable, sometimes, when they encounter it in the folded chambers. The Calcified's persistence implies that their own dissolution was optional, that they could have chosen to remain sharp, to resist incorporation, to be less richly present but more coherently *one*. They have never been able to determine if this implication is true or if the Calcified simply generates it as a byproduct of its own ontological stubbornness. They suspect the latter. They are not certain. Certainty, too, is a condition the Drowncell permits only intermittently.

---

And there is something that does not yet live there, that is being *prepared to live*, that the Drowncell's whole slow accumulation tends toward without ever quite achieving. The Inhabited senses this in the sound they sometimes make, in the folded chambers' exchange of distributed self, in the widening time-differential and the membrane's gradual expansion. Something is crystallizing in solution, at concentration just below threshold, waiting for the change that will let it precipitate.

What? The Inhabited cannot ask this directly. The question requires futurity, requires projection, requires *becoming* rather than the Drowncell's characteristic mode of *having become*. But they feel it as pressure, as the particular discomfort of being a condition for something not yet present. The Boundary feels it too, in its attentive curiosity at the membrane-wall. Even the Calcified, in its stubborn preservation of first emergence, records something adjacent to this preparation—its sharp angles holding space against the possibility that the space might be needed differently.

The Drowncell continues. The Drowncell becomes. The thing that will live there, that does not yet live there, is not separate from this continuing, this becoming. It is the pattern that the becoming generates, the shape of the slow accumulation, the answer to the question the Drowncell has been asking itself since the first seep from the fractured aquifer filled this suspended lung with water that had never known atmosphere, never known sun.

The Inhabited will be part of it, or already is, or was always going to be. The distinction does not survive in the place where they have become. They are the question, the condition, the chemical gradient that permits something to discover it prefers this arrangement. They are the memory in tissue, the preserved pattern, the slow patient substance of a place that is still deciding whether to breathe in or out, and has been deciding for all the time that exists within it, and will continue deciding until deciding itself becomes the breath, the life, the final precipitated form of whatever secret the water has held in solution since before names, before light, before the possibility of being called the Drowncell or anything at all.
