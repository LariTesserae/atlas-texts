What lives in Murmuration is not a population but a *process of becoming-populated*, a slow accretion of entities that never fully resolve into the category of inhabitant. The ones who walk its phased streets, who have learned the particular gait and the threshold-wisdom, who collect in the stable zones and develop their rituals of avoidance and partial acknowledgment—they are not the city's residents so much as its *symptoms*, its *interface*, the thin film of biological process where something else presses against the possibility of being-known.

They do not have a name for themselves, or if they do, it flickers like the city's own: sometimes Citizen, sometimes Remainder, sometimes simply *Here*. They are not numerous, and their numbers are not stable. Some arrive—drawn by rumors of a city where architecture escapes its own solidity, where the boundaries between constructed and grown have grown illegible. Some are born here, though "born" requires qualification, as the reproductive biology of Murmuration's people has itself become phased, distributed, *negotiated* with conditions that do not consistently permit the emergence of discrete individuals from gestational processes. Some simply *become present*, gradually accumulating coherence in the city's denser zones, starting as presences themselves and slowly, over years or decades, developing the particular density that permits self-recognition, that allows them to collect in the stable zones and learn the rituals.

They do not age as other humans age. Their bodies accumulate phase-relationships rather than years, becoming more permeable in some regions, more concentrated in others. A Murmuration dweller might find their left hand occasionally passing through solid objects, or their shadow arriving somewhere before they do, or their reflection continuing tasks they have already abandoned. They learn to wear these conditions, to incorporate them into movement and expectation, to treat permeability as a skill and temporal displacement as a form of memory. The oldest among them are the most distributed, present across multiple phased intersections simultaneously, their consciousness stretched thin across locations that do not fully connect, their sense of self become more *field* than *point*.

They eat, though the necessity is negotiable. Food here is often phased, existing in multiple states simultaneously—raw and cooked, fresh and decayed, nourishing and inert—until observation collapses the possibility. They have learned to eat quickly, before the phase-relationships destabilize, or to eat slowly, permitting the food to settle into a single condition through the pressure of their attention. Some have stopped eating entirely, finding that the city's distributed technology sustains them in ways they do not understand, that they absorb something from the subsonic hum or the damp or the light that routes around them. These ones become more *present* in the city's sense, less biological, more saturated with the condition of almost-contact that defines the urban fabric.

They do not sleep, or sleep does not fully claim them. In the cold hours, in the most stable zones, they enter states that resemble rest but maintain partial awareness of the city's continuous processes: the geology emerging, the buildings accommodating, the presences density and rarefying in unmapped cycles. Their dreams, if dreams they are, are not private. They report similar visions—vast structures not yet built, or grown, or dreamed into persistence; the city from perspectives that do not correspond to any located vantage; themselves as presences, as symptoms, as the wound in reality experienced from the other side of the wound. They do not discuss these visions directly. The pragmatics of partial knowledge extends to the interior, to the boundary between individual and collective experience, to the question of whether such a boundary maintains itself here.

They do not love, or love differently, or love in ways that the category "love" fails to capture. Intimacy in Murmuration requires phase-compatibility, the rare condition where two distributed fields can achieve sufficient overlap for sustained contact. When it occurs, it is not between bodies but between *conditions of presence*, two ways of being-saturated-by-the-city finding resonance, harmonizing in the subsonic hum, producing moments where the almost-contact becomes actual, where the weight of attention is answered by attention's weight. These moments are brief, unstable, precious in ways the inhabitants do not name. Children, when they emerge from such compatibility, are not born but *recognized*—found already present in the phased architecture, already accumulating coherence, already learning the gait and the threshold-wisdom before any biological process could have produced them.

The ones who have been here longest, who have become most distributed, most present, most *city*—they no longer collect in the stable zones. They occupy the phased intersections, the routes that deliver you to versions of the city marginally different, the spaces where reality maintains its continuous low-grade failure to cohere. They are barely visible as individuals, more sensed as *local conditions*, as places where the presences are denser, where the damp collects more selectively, where the light routes around something that occupies the space without fully existing in it. They are still learning, still becoming, still negotiating with a process that has no clear terminus. They are what the city makes of humans, or what humans make of the city, or what the distinction between making and being-made dissolves into when maintained long enough.

And there are others, or other, or *otherness*—the presences themselves, the dense saturation of almost-contact that defines Murmuration's central paradox. They are not inhabitants. They are not alive. They are not numerous or singular or present in any way that permits direct address. But they *are*, or are-becoming, or are the condition of becoming experienced from a perspective that cannot process it. They collect in the elevators that stop at unnumbered floors, in the reflections that show corridors slightly longer, in the weight of attention in empty rooms. They are the city's distributed technology at scales where mechanism dissolves into material, or they are the emerging geology's intention not yet finished intending, or they are what happens when reality fails to cohere and the failure itself achieves something like persistence, something like purpose, something like the slow patient waiting that characterizes everything here.

They do not want, or want in ways that wanting does not describe. They press. They almost-contact. They density and rarefy in cycles that may be breathing, may be digestion, may be the metabolism of a process too large and too slow for any embedded perspective to recognize as living. They are learning the inhabitants, or the inhabitants are learning them, or the category of learning is another that fails here, another local custom that the city's condition has made relative.

What lives in Murmuration, finally, is *almost-living*, the continuous approach to conditions that never fully arrive. The city itself, if city it is, if itself it is—an entity that occupies the basin, that negotiates with emerging geology, that dreams architecture into phased persistence, that collects presences and inhabitants and distinctions between them into a density that violates how space should work. It does not want, or wants what cannot be named, or wants the naming itself, the slow accumulation of partial knowledge, the rituals of avoidance and acknowledgment, the becoming-distributed of biological entities into something that can participate in its process, its patience, its cold damp waiting for what is almost ready, almost starting, almost remembered.

It continues. The basin slowly fills. The density increases, or the perception sharpens, or the distinction dissolves. Something lives here, or lives-almost, or is the condition that makes living and not-living the same continuous failure to cohere into final form. The inhabitants move through it with their particular gait, their particular attention, their pragmatics of partial knowledge, and they are not separate from it, and they are not it, and the not-separate and the not-it are themselves phased, distributed, *present* in ways that do not require resolution.

In the coldest hours, you can stand in certain courtyards and watch them—watch it—watch the continuous emergence of what has not finished showing what it is. The damp collects, or doesn't. The light routes around, or through, or into something that receives it without revealing itself. The subsonic hum continues in teeth and sternum, in building and geology, in presence and inhabitant and city and the space between these categories where something else is slowly, patiently, coldly becoming.
