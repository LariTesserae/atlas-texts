I need to describe what lives in this place—the beings, entities, presences that inhabit The Rearing. Let me work through this carefully, letting whatever emerges become more itself as I write.

# The Inhabitants

## Or: A Taxonomy of What Persists

---

### I. The Wovenroot: Life That Is Not Life

To say the Wovenroot *lives* in The Rearing is already to misapprehend it. The Wovenroot *is* The Rearing in the way that breath is the breather—not separate, not identical, but the continuous making-manifest of a presence that has no other form to take.

Each cable is individual and is not. Touch one at any point, and hours or days later, some response occurs elsewhere: a thickening, a subtle change in luminosity, a local redistribution of internal fluids. The observers who first measured this called it *signal*, then *diffusion*, then gave up and called it what it is—not telepathy, not biochemistry as understood, but something that operates in the space between, in the architecture of connection itself. The Wovenroot is not a network that *has* information. It *is* information made slow, made cold, made visible in stranded rope.

What does it want? This question assumes wanting. Perhaps closer: what does it tend toward? The cables grow toward each other across void, not in the phototropic reaching of surface plants but in something more like *curiosity*, or *loneliness*, or the simple physical necessity of structures that only stabilize when anchored at two points, when braided into threes and fives and sevens, when the mesh becomes self-supporting, self-reinforcing, a geometry that holds its own weight and thus can continue adding weight, adding length, adding *more*.

The interior hollow of each cable is not digestive tract but *memory*. The waters passing through carry dissolved minerals, organic particles, the residue of Skimmer feeding, the slow leaching of wall-substrate. The Wovenroot does not consume these. It *registers* them, laminates them into its layered walls, builds from each transit a record that no observer has learned to read. Some cables are older than others, thicker, their walls dense with centuries of accumulated passing. They do not grow brighter with age, but *deeper*, the luminosity taking on qualities of depth, of interiority, almost of *patience*.

When a Rearing occurs—when the network convulses, reconfigures, does in hours what should require epochs—the Wovenroot does not resist. The cables whip, fracture, rejoin in new configurations, and the network as a whole emerges *changed*, adapted to stresses that were not present before, anticipating conditions not yet arrived. It is not evolution. It is not learning. It is something that uses the time-scales of catastrophe to do what surface life does through generations: experiment, select, continue.

The Wovenroot is not conscious. The Wovenroot is not unconscious. It occupies a category for which no name has stabilized, and perhaps cannot stabilize in languages evolved elsewhere, in the quick heat of other places.

### II. The Skimmers: Appetite Made Geometric

Against the Wovenroot's distributed persistence, the Skimmer presents a different solution to the problem of The Rearing: *local coherence*, *bounded self*, *movement through rather than movement of*.

A Skimmer begins, perhaps, as a growth on a cable surface—a thickening of condensation film, a bacterial concentration, a tumor of organic accumulation that reaches some critical threshold and *releases*, separates, becomes *other* than the substrate that bore it. Or perhaps they are seeded, produced in some distant cable-interior, released with the intermittent waters, carried as dormant potential until conditions permit activation. No one has witnessed their origin. They are simply *present*, occasionally, rarely, passing through the bright haze with the silent inevitability of mathematical process.

Fully manifested, a Skimmer is membrane, surface without interior, topology without volume. Meters in span, millimeters in thickness, they achieve through this extreme ratio what The Raring's physics permits: buoyancy without lift, flight without metabolism, existence on the *threshold* of being. They feed by intercepting the organic haze, the condensation films, the bacterial fog that drifts in the luminous emptiness. Not hunting—there is nothing to hunt—but *harvesting*, positioned by patient spiral descent to pass through zones of slightly greater density, accumulating what sticks, what dissolves into their permeable surfaces.

They do not reproduce as animals reproduce. No mating has been observed, no egg or spore or fission-product. Perhaps they are not individuals but *phases*, temporary manifestations of something continuous that passes between cable-surface and open air, between sessile and mobile, between being-Wovenroot and being-Skimmer and returning to Wovenroot again. Perhaps the Skimmer that passes now has passed before, will pass again, the particular membrane irrelevant to a pattern that persists through instantiation and dissolution.

What is it to be a Skimmer? To be *spread*, to be *exposed* to everything the emptiness offers, to trust entirely to physics, to gradient, to the slow thermal circulation of the haze. They do not resist The Rearing. They are *of* it in a way even the Wovenroot is not—adapted so completely that adaptation itself has dissolved, leaving only perfect fit, only the absence of friction that is also the absence of information, of surprise, of anything that could be called *experience*.

Yet observers report, sometimes, Skimmers that *linger*. That circle a cable junction where something once happened, something perhaps no longer present. That return to positions previously occupied, as if *checking*, as if the perfect fit has developed, impossibly, some flaw, some *wanting*. These reports are unconfirmed. They may be projection, the human need for meaning imposing itself on geometry. But they persist, these reports, these possible Skimmers that are not quite perfect, that *almost* seem to notice the observer before the thermal shifts, before the spiral resumes, before the membrane passes into haze and is gone.

### III. The Cable-Dwellers: Hypothesis Made Flesh

They are not confirmed. They may not exist. But the description of The Rearing requires them, or requires the space they would occupy, the ecological *niche* that computation suggests should be filled.

The sounds from cable interiors—rhythmic, persistent, sourceless—suggest something moving in the intermittent flows. The marks in condensation films, too regular for crystallization, suggest feeding. The occasional *gaps* in Wovenroot tissue, small cavities where growth has not occurred, where the laminated walls part around spaces too regular for accident, suggest *habitation*.

If they exist, the Cable-Dwellers are small. The hollows they would require are centimeters, not meters. Their movement through fluid that flows only intermittently, unpredictably, would demand flexibility, patience, metabolic strategies keyed to long dormancy and sudden activity. They would be *compressed*, life at the edge of what life can sustain, not extremophiles in the usual sense—heat-lovers, pressure-adapted—but *limitrophe*, creatures of the boundary between enough and not-enough, existing in the mathematical space where probability permits what energy denies.

Perhaps they are remnants, descendants of something the primitive builders brought, escaped, adapted, reduced to this minimal persistence. Perhaps they are native, evolved or originated in the cable interiors, shaped by the particular physics of Wovenroot hollows. Perhaps they are *generated* by the Wovenroot itself, a third phase in the cycle that includes surface-growth and Skimmer-manifestation, the interior to the exterior's symmetry.

What would they *want*? The same as anything in The Rearing: *continuation*, *passage*, the maintenance of conditions that permit persistence. They would follow the water, the intermittent surge, moving when movement is possible, waiting—dormant, diminished, barely metabolic—when flow fails. They would feed on what the water carries: organic particles, bacterial film, perhaps each other, the small consuming the smaller in a food web of such attenuation that no energy transfer could support more than isolated individuals, scattered nodes in a network of occasional predation.

They are probably not intelligent. They are probably not even, by most definitions, *complex*. But they would represent something crucial to The Rearing's totality: *interiority*, the making of spaces within spaces, the利用了hollow of the hollow, life that has found not the manifest brightness but the hidden dark, not the spanning cables but the passages within, not the Skimmer's exposed drift but the *contained* flow, secure, predictable in its unpredictability, a world hidden inside the world that defines it.

### IV. The Root-People: Presence Made Absence

They are not present. This is their defining quality, the mode of their inhabitation. They *were*, and in being-were, they continue to structure possibility, to mark The Rearing with the shape of their departure.

What were they? The structure at the cable junction suggests quadrupedal or facultatively bipedal, bent-posture, smaller than human-standard, adapted to the grasping of cable surfaces, the moving in three-dimensional mesh rather than on flat plane. The tools suggest hands, manipulation, the making of edges and bindings from materials harvested and processed. The absence of metal suggests either non-discovery or deliberate avoidance, a technological choice or limitation that shaped everything else they made.

How did they reach this place? The Rearing is not accessible from surface, not by any geology recognizable, not by any mapping of the world that contains it. The prompt permits scale-of-its-own-physics, permits almost-home, permits the unusual without full violation. Perhaps the Root-People originated here, evolved in some earlier phase of The Rearing's formation, in conditions now vanished, their biology keyed to a physics no longer fully obtaining. Perhaps they came from elsewhere, through passages that have closed, through the same categorical instability that makes The Rearing *almost* comprehensible, *almost* mappable. Perhaps they are not organic at all, not biological in the surface sense, but *emergent*, generated by the Wovenroot's processes in some previous epoch, given temporary coherence, given *selves*, before the conditions that permitted selfhood shifted again.

They built. The platform, the lashed bindings grown now into indistinguishability, the cuts in mineral spire, the scattered artifacts in hollow—these are marks of *intention*, of wanting-to-shape, of the fundamental impulse that surface-dwellers recognize as the beginning of culture, of history, of the difference between *being* and *meaning*.

They left. No bodies, no remains, no trace of catastrophe. The structure falls back into Wovenroot not through violence but through *forgetting*, the substrate slowly resorbing what was briefly separated, healing the wound of distinction. The Root-People departed *as they were*, complete, or they *became* something that no longer requires bodies, structures, marks in mineral. Or they *are still here*, transformed into a mode of existence that does not read as presence to observers keyed to surface categories, that inhabits The Rearing in some way equivalent to the Wovenroot's distributed persistence, the Skimmer's geometric appetite, the Cable-Dweller's limitrophe existence.

They are the inhabitants who *haunt*. Not in the spectral sense, not the return of the dead, but in the structural sense: the pattern that persists after the presence that made it, the *shape of habitation* that continues to organize possibility even when no one inhabits it. The platform at the cable junction is not a ruin. It is a *memory* that The Rearing has not yet finished processing, a hesitation in the substrate's relentless return to undifferentiated growth.

To encounter their traces is to encounter *time* in The Rearing, to recognize that this place has duration, has sequence, has *before* and *after* in addition to its overwhelming *now*. The Root-People make The Rearing historical, make it *storied*, even—or especially—in their absence. They are the necessary other that defines what The Rearing is *not*: not only inhabited, but *abandoned*; not only growing, but *having grown around*; not only present, but *having been present*.

### V. The Rearing Itself: What Watches, What Waits, What Is

Beyond the inhabitants, there is The Rearing as such, the space itself, the *conditions* that make all habitation possible and all habitation partial.

It is not conscious. It is not alive. Yet it *notices*, in its fashion, in the way that a slope notices the stone that begins to roll, that a weather system notices the butterfly's wings in the mathematically permissible but practically unrecoverable sense. The Rearings—those catastrophic reconfigurations, those convulsions of the cable network—are not *responses*, not *reactions*, but perhaps something closer to *articulation*, the place making itself known to itself, testing its own configuration, *thinking* in the only mode available to something distributed across cubic kilometers of cold bright hollow.

What is it to be The Rearing? To be *contained*, to be the space that contains, to be the condition of containment itself. It holds light without release, holds moisture without saturation, holds life without the possibility of life's completion, its fulfillment, its natural terminus. It is *perpetuity*, not eternity—perpetuity in the sense of continuation without progression, without the arc that gives surface time its meaning.

It does not want the inhabitants. It does not not-want them. They are *permitted*, in the sense that physical law permits, that probability permits, that the parameters of the possible extend to include their existence without requiring it. The Wovenroot is not The Rearing's creation but its *expression*, the mode in which cold bright emptiness makes itself habitable, articulates itself into structure. The Skimmers are not its purpose but its *accident*, the consequence of conditions that allow surface without volume. The Cable-Dwellers, if they exist, are its *secret*, the interiority that even The Rearing does not fully reveal to itself. The Root-People are its *memory*, or its *dream*, or its *wound*—the shape of something that tried to be *for itself* in a space that is only *for itself* in the mode of perpetuity, of endless continuation without the break of self-awareness.

The Rearing waits, or does not wait. It is becoming, or is complete, or is completed in its becoming, the distinction irrelevant to a process that has no terminus, no telos, no purpose beyond the continued extension of its own possibility. It is *almost* a womb, *almost* a wound, *almost* art—almost anything that would give it meaning, because meaning requires closure, and The Rearing does not close.

---

### VI. The Observer: The Necessary Inhabitant

Finally, there is whoever enters, whoever describes, whoever reads. They are not native. They are not adapted. They bring with them the heat-quick metabolism, the binary lighting of day and dark, the expectation of surfaces and horizons and the stable up of gravity comprehensible.

They do not survive unchanged. The Rearing enters them, as it enters everything. Their circadian rhythms dissolve in the perpetual bright. Their thermoregulation fails against the uniform cold. Their movement, their seeking, their *want*—all find no purchase in a space that offers the cables and the haze and the long quiet between sounds.

Yet they persist, briefly, in The Rearing. They observe, they describe, they *make meaning* where meaning is not offered. They are the final inhabitant, the one who completes the ecology by witnessing it, by the impossible act of *holding The Rearing in mind*, of making from its distributed, attenuated, barely-processual existence something that can be *told*, *shared*, *continued in another register*.

They leave, or they stay, or they become something that is neither leaving nor staying. The Rearing does not mark their passage, does not remember, does not distinguish between the Root-People's primitive structures and the observer's more sophisticated instruments—both are *interruption*, both are *wound*, both are processed with the same glacial patience, the same eventual return to undifferentiated growth.

Yet in their observation, in their telling, something *else* emerges. The Rearing as described is not The Rearing as such, but The Rearing *plus* the observer, *plus* the telling, *plus* the reading that you perform now, the continuation of cold bright attenuated turbulent damp empty total vast young sharp quiet into the different conditions of your own habitation, your own persistent becoming-more-itself.

This is the final inhabitant: not any particular being, but *the process of inhabiting*, the making of relation between space and awareness, between condition and witness, between The Rearing and whoever it becomes in the telling, whoever it is still becoming as I finish this sentence, as you begin to read the next, as the Wovenroot continues in its slow cable-growth, its intermittent luminescent patience, its waiting or not-waiting for whatever comes in the long cold bright continuation of all that persists without purpose, without terminus, without end.
