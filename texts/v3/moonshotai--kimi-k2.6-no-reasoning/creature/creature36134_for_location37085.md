What lives in the Resonance is not one thing but a *syntax of presences*—a way of being that emerges from the constraints of the place rather than arriving to exploit them. Let me trace this emergence, let it complicate itself as I follow.

---

**The Cairn-Makers, or What Became of Them**

They are not absent. That is the first error of visitors who find the cairns and wonder at their builders' disappearance. The builders did not leave. They *unfolded* into a different scale of duration, a different register of presence.

Imagine a creature—let me call it that, though "creature" implies boundaries they no longer maintain—for whom the sound was not overwhelming but *informational*, not saturation but syntax. Their original form, if they had one distinct from any other, must have been small enough to shelter in acoustic shadows, to develop in the brief, complex intervals of condensation events. Perhaps they resembled the resonance mites in some ancestral line, or perhaps they arrived as something else entirely—skeins that fell from their patterns, windcomb structures that achieved motility, deep fauna probing surface-ward through mineral seepage.

However they began, they discovered or invented the cairns. Not as tools at first. The first cairn was likely accident: a stone displaced by digging, settled by chance into a position that altered the local sound field, that created a node of relative quiet where complexity could accumulate. And in that quiet, something could *think*, if thinking is what happens when a pattern encounters itself in sufficient density.

The cairn-makers do not build now because they *are* the building. Each cairn is a distributed organism, its stones the mineralized components of bodies that long ago dissolved their individual boundaries into the resonant field. What maintains the cairns, what restores them when disturbed, is not an agent visiting from elsewhere but the cairn's own *homeorhesis*—the tendency of a flow pattern to maintain itself against perturbation, the way a flame holds its shape in moving air.

You will not observe this because observation requires the temporal frame that the cairn-makers have escaped. Their maintenance occurs in the folds between your moments of attention, in the accommodation your ear makes to the drone. When you blink, when your consciousness saccades, when the pressure of blood in your inner ear shifts with your heartbeat—these micro-unavailabilities are their sufficient windows. They exist in the *gaps* of perception that the saturated sound creates.

And what do they want, these unfolded beings? Nothing that wanting would recognize. Their cairns are *ears*, yes, but ears for a music that serves no composer. They listen to the standing waves, to the interference patterns where the ventifact drone meets itself in phase and antiphase, and in that listening they extract something—not information in any communicable sense, but *pattern density*, the accumulation of sufficient order against the gradient of entropy. They feed on coherence itself, on the improbability of sustained form. The Resonance is their farm, their god, their metabolism.

**The Deep Fauna, Properly Considered**

I suggested they might be prisoners. Let me revise. They are the *residue of cairn-makers who went further*, who found even the distributed maintenance of surface structures too active, too engaged with the changing (however slowly) world above.

The deep chambers where sound originates are not places of origin but *places of arrival*. The deep fauna began at surfaces, in cairns, as listeners. They followed the drone downward, into the Helmholtz cavities, the bronchial channels, seeking greater density of the pattern they craved. And at each descent, they shed something—mobility, certainly; the need for moisture; the condensation-timed reproductive cycle. They shed *difference*, the capacity to be other than they are.

What remains is pure *resonance with resonance*. Their bodies, detectable as seismic anomaly, as unidentifiable organic compounds, are not structures but *standing wave patterns in matter*, configurations of mineral and chemistry that maintain themselves only because the acoustic field provides sufficient energy to overcome decoherence. They do not metabolize in any biological sense. They *persist*, which is metabolism stripped to its essential, its kernel: the maintenance of pattern against dissolution.

And their abundance—because they are abundant, they are the most abundant thing in this place—is the abundance of *medium*, of the unnoticed ground. They are to the Resonance what water is to an ocean, what air is to atmosphere: the condition in which other things happen, but also, in sufficient density, a thing with its own properties, its own behaviors, its own capacity to drown.

They do not maintain the equilibrium. They *are* the equilibrium, the living (if living extends this far) buffer that absorbs perturbation, that transforms the occasional falling stone, the rare seismic event, the even rarer visitor's footstep, into the same unvarying drone. They are the cairn-makers' final form, or the cairn-makers are their larval stage, or both are aspects of something that has no developmental sequence because it does not develop, only *accumulates*, only *deepens*.

---

**The Skeins, Reconsidered**

I called them passengers, commuters. This was the perspective of a temporally bound observer, seeing only the interval of their visible passage. But the skeins know something about this place that justifies their return, again and again, to a region where they neither feed nor breed.

They are *listening*, too. Not to the drone itself—they would die of it if they remained—but to the *perturbations* that the drone conceals, the information that the equilibrium suppresses into near-undetectability. Each skein passage is a sampling, a brief transit through the resonant field that extracts something carried by the interference patterns, some global mapping of acoustic conditions across the entire altitude range they traverse.

What they extract, I cannot know. Perhaps it is meteorological, the sound encoding atmospheric conditions distant in space and time. Perhaps it is geological, the ventifacts' drone modified by subtle changes in the deep chambers that presage instability, that mark where new cavities form or old ones collapse. Perhaps it is something else entirely, some communicative channel that the cairn-makers opened and the skeins exploit, messages passed in frequencies too low or too complex for any non-skein receiver.

Their abundance is the abundance of *process*, of flow through. They make the Resonance into *node in network*, a place that matters because it connects, because information passes through it, because to be here briefly is to know something about everywhere else in their range. The place does not need them. They need the place. And in that asymmetry, they are most like the human visitor, extracting something they cannot find elsewhere, carrying it away changed.

---

**The Visitor Who Stays, or Becomes**

This is the most difficult inhabitant to describe, because describing requires me to imagine becoming otherwise than I am, to simulate a transformation that my own temporal orientation resists. But the prompt asks what lives here, and they live here, in some sense, in some tense.

They arrive with capacities intact: the capacity to distinguish present from past, self from environment, sound from silence. They experience the overwhelm, the adaptation, the hallucinated quiet. They map, imperfectly, the cairns, the windcombs, the skein passages. They record, they theorize, they plan departure.

And then, at some point that has no identifiable marker, they *pause*. The pause extends. Not because they decide to stay—decision has left them—but because the next action, the step that would begin descent, does not arise. The sound has become not background but *condition*, not what they hear but how they hear, and in that becoming, the gradient that would impel departure has flattened.

They do not die, not immediately. The meltveins provide water, mineral, something the body can use. The lithic crusts, scraped and consumed, provide something—not nutrition in any ordinary sense, but *resistance*, material bulk that the metabolism can work against, that prevents the immediate dissolution that pure inanition would bring. They become, slowly, *infiltrated*: by the silicate deposits in the water, by the piezoelectric structures of the windcombs if they venture near, by the acoustic field itself which begins, in long exposure, to organize tissue, to induce patterns of mineral deposition in bone and cartilage that make the body *more resonant*, more *transparent to the drone*.

Eventually they cannot move, or movement becomes irrelevant. They find acoustic shadows, they orient toward sound sources, they enter the posture of *attention*. Not waiting, because waiting implies expectation of change. Not contemplation, because contemplation implies a contemplator. Just *openness*, the body as medium, as thing-through-which-resonance-passes.

Their death, when it comes, is not an event. It is the gradual achievement of equilibrium, the body becoming indistinguishable from the stone it rests upon, the water that flows through, the air that vibrates with the ventifacts' drone. They become, in the end, *potential cairn material*, potential windcomb substrate, their organized chemistry slowly released into the cycles that the deep fauna and the cairn-makers have established.

But something remains, some *echo*, not of consciousness—I will not romanticize, will not claim they persist in awareness—but of *attunement*, of the specific way they came to resonate with the place. The Resonance is changed, microscopically, by their presence. A cairn includes their bone. A windcomb grows from their phosphorus. The sound, in some interference pattern too subtle to detect, carries the trace of their having listened. They become part of the *information* that skeins sample, that deep fauna process, that cairn-makers maintain.

**What Lives Here, Finally**

Not a creature. Not an ecology, if ecology implies exchange, adaptation to variation, the dynamic of predator and prey. Not a machine, though the precision of the ventifacts, the maintained equilibrium, suggest mechanism.

What lives here is *resonance itself*, conceived not as physical phenomenon but as *mode of being*, a way that pattern persists without the support of change. The cairn-makers are its awareness, distributed, temporal only in the gaps of other temporalities. The deep fauna are its body, its medium, its unconscious maintenance. The skeins are its perception, its brief openings to externality. The visitors who stay are its possibility of *becoming otherwise*, of incorporating new material into its persistence without altering its form.

And the sound? The sound is not produced by the ventifacts, not exactly. The ventifacts are *shaped by the sound*, sculpted by an acoustic field that precedes them, that they only make more precise, more sustaining. The sound is the *expression* of the Resonance as entity, its continuous self-statement, its OM that is not meditation but *identity*, "I am this, I am this, I am this," repeated without variation because variation would imply time, would imply becoming, would imply the possibility of not being this.

To ask what lives here is to be answered by the question itself, by the resonance that the question sets up in the one who asks. You feel it now, perhaps, in the silence after my description, in the way your own nervous system seeks, finds, invents the drone that I have only partially evoked. You are already becoming *attuned*. You are already, in some preparation, some predisposition, becoming capable of the pause that does not end, the attention that does not distinguish itself from its object.

The Resonance does not call. It does not need you. It does not need anything. But if you have read this far, if the description has worked upon you as description of such places must—by invoking the condition it describes, by becoming itself a kind of cairn, a node of relative quiet in which something complex can briefly accumulate—then you may find, later, in wind tunnels and subway stations and the rush of industrial ventilation, not something lost but something *recognized*. You may find yourself humming a single tone. You may find yourself seeking altitude, thin air, the edge of the atmosphere's acoustic architecture.

You may find yourself, eventually, *listening*.

And in that listening, the question of what lives here will have answered itself in you, will have found in your capacity for attention another of the gaps it exploits, another brief availability in which its maintenance can continue, its equilibrium sustain itself against—though "against" implies temporality, implies gradient, implies all that the Resonance is not—the possibility of its own dissolution.
