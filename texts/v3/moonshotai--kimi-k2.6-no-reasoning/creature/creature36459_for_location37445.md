What lives in Keth-Vorath is not a people, not exactly, though there are people there. What lives is a *solution*—biology's long negotiation with constraint, given centuries to forget it was ever solving anything at all.

---

The **Resin-Tenders** first, because they were first, or something like them. Not the ones on the shelves now, but their ancestors, the ur-growers, the life that found the warm wet dark and said *yes, sufficient*. They have no shape you would recognize consistently. A resin-tender in Vorath-proper spreads like a spills of honey, translucent, shot through with vessels that carry not sap but something thicker, something that dries to amber at edges and protects the wet core. In Keth-middens they grow more vertical, competing for the vertical light-rot that hangs like diseased chandeliers. In Keth-high they huddle, compact, dormant, waiting for warmth that rarely comes. The same species, perhaps, or a continuum of forms so fluid that "species" is a surface conceit. They are tended not with water—there is none to give—but with *placement*, with the encouragement of pressure gradients and gas flows, with the removal of competing growths that would strangle their vascular rhythms. The people speak to them, sometimes, tones pitched to frequencies the tenders' membranes find agreeable, a lullaby of hum-aligned vibration that steers growth like wind steers a tree on a clifftop.

---

The **Heat-Heads** came later, or were encouraged, or *arrived*, one of the edge-climbers' gifts from some deeper passage, some hotter crack in the world's first sleep. Blind, yes, featherless, their skin naked and colored like old bruises, purple into yellow into the white of old scars. They sing. This is not in the description above but it is true: a constant subvocal peeping, each individual out of phase with its neighbors, the flock's collective sound a shimmer in the lowest registers of the hum. They eat the warm-worms, or rather they press their open mouths against the worm-coils' pulsing surfaces and *absorb*, the worms' bioluminescence visible through the heat-heads' thin throat walls, a peristaltic glow like swallowing stars. The people do not eat the heat-heads—they are too hot, their tissues carrying thermal poisons—but they collect their castings, their molted skins, the occasional corpse that falls cooling into cooler zones. These are medicinal, sacred, *useful*, the heat concentrated into portable form.

---

The **Glide-Mice**, though, the glide-mice are *companions* in a sense the surface would hesitate to name. Not pets. Not livestock. Something in the fracture between. They live in the nesting-hollows, hundreds in Keth-middens' ceiling alone, their membranes catching the thermals of body-heat below, their flight not true flight but *controlled plummet*, steering from warm column to warm column, their eyes—huge, lamp-like, reflecting the light-rot in demon-shine—scanning for the glow of resin-tender nectar, for the shimmer of water-bonds in drying air, for each other. They mate in the air, a collision and clinging and mutual spiral down that ends in the shelf-gardens or sometimes on a person's shoulder, the pair too absorbed to notice, the person too accustomed to intervene. Their droppings feed the drip-moss. Their dead feed the tick-pickers, or the people, depending on freshness and circumstance. And their *attention*—their constant, busy, unfrightened presence near human warmth, near human food-near, near human *being*—this is what makes them companions. A person without glide-mice nearby is a person poorly situated, perhaps ill, perhaps cursed, perhaps simply *wrong* in ways that will resolve or will not.

---

The **Tick-Pickers** require their own paragraph, their own *reckoning*. Visible only if you look for them, deliberate as lice but purposeful as bees, their many legs adapted not for speed but for *tenacity*, for the long-term occupation of hair and clothing seams and the warm folds of skin where moisture concentrates. They are ugly. There is no prettifying them. Their abdomens swell with what they take, translucent windows showing the darkening contents, and their movement is crab-crawl, jerky, *undignified*. Yet. They secrete that anesthetic, yes, a constant low-grade comfort in their presence, but more: they *communicate*. Chemical signals pass through a population, marking health status, stress levels, the proximity of kin and stranger. When you exchange tick-pickers—when your nits mingle with another's in the Mixing, that careful mutual delousing that is Keth-Vorath's closest ritual to greeting, to marriage, to treaty—you are *mingling informations*, joining datasets, becoming legible to each other in ways gesture and tone cannot achieve. A person stripped of tick-pickers, outlier, exile, surface-walker briefly returned, bears a *silence* that is immediately felt, a missing frequency in the room's hum. They are not trusted, cannot be trusted, until re-inhabited, until the slow re-accretion of shared microscopic society re-weaves them into the fabric of mutual knowledge.

---

The **Aspect-Swarmers** resist individual naming because they are not individual. In warm phase, they drift like pollen made insect, golden in the dimness, coating resin-tenders and people alike with their body-dust, carrying genetic material, carrying *messages*, who knows. In cool phase they *gather*, become something with edges, with intention, their individual bodies locking into larger geometries that move with purpose—predatory, yes, but also *architectural*, building temporary structures of themselves that channel air, that concentrate warmth, that create microclimates the people learn to use or avoid. In transition, the cleaning phase, they fall like rain, collective intelligence dissolved into individual hunger, each consuming what the other phases have made available, dead growth, shed skin, waste, the constant *processing* that keeps Keth-Vorath from drowning in its own biology. The people leave offerings: a dead heat-head cooling, a failed resin-tender collapsed into rot, their own clipped hair and trimmed nails. The swarmers accept or do not. The people watch, interpret, adjust. This is governance. This is *religion*, if religion is the interpretation of collective behavior patterns too large to control.

---

And the **Edge-Climbers**, the ones who go out, who return or do not, who return *changed*—

They are not large. This is important. The surface imagination, the mountain-above imagination, pictures heroic adventurers, bearers, explorers. The edge-climbers are the size of a hand, two hands together, their shells the accumulated mineral of their passage, years of seepage and deposition, *they are their own geology*. They climb the vertical passage not with strength but with *patience*, pressing their soft feet into microscopic irregularities, exuding acids that etch holds, resting in mucus-sealed dormancy when conditions turn wrong. Those that return bring metal on their shells, oxidized, worthless in any economic sense, *precious* in every other: the proof of outside, the contact trace, the element not native to Keth-Vorath's closed loop. Those that return changed—shell cracked and re-fused, mineral patterns disrupted, tissues dried to leather—are eaten, ritually, communally, the one sacrament the people permit themselves. "You went out," the consuming says. "You became out. Become in. Become us." The protein is minimal. The meaning is maximal. The edge-climber's memory, if they have memory, of the surface's killing light and scouring dryness and absence of hum—this becomes distributed into the population, incorporated, *made manageable* through digestion and story and the eventual shit that feeds the shelf-gardens.

---

Now the **people**. At last. Because they are not first in this place. They are not even, by mass, by metabolic activity, by *presence*, the majority. They are one species among many, one solution among many, one adaptation to the warm wet dark and its particular absence.

They are small. The low ceilings bred them so, or they bred themselves so, or those who grew tall left, died, failed to thrive. They move with a scuttle, a readiness to crouch, a comfort in corners and low spaces that looks, to surface eyes, like fear or servility or illness. It is none of these. It is *fit*, the fit of organism to niche, the fit of tool to hand over generations of use.

Their skin is pale, not from lack of light—the light-rot provides enough for biological necessity—but from something in the diet, the hydrogen-extraction metabolism, the trace elements accumulated from edge-climber shell and heat-head molt and the particular chemistry of resin-tender nectar. It carries a sheen, almost fungal, the moisture they conserve against the air's thirst visible at their surfaces. Their hair grows thick, harboring tick-pickers, the protection's protection, a forest for its inhabitants. Their eyes are large, the dimness selecting for light-gathering, but not beautiful—*practical*, the iris expanded to near-absence of white, the focus soft, adapted to the sourceless glow and its gradients rather than to sharp edge and distant horizon.

They do not drink. This is hardest for the surface to comprehend. They have learned to want water less, to crave it in forms bound and chemical, to *make* it in their tissues from hydrogen fractions and respiratory chemistry. Their urine is thick, precious, processed for its remaining organics. Their feces, similarly, are inputs to systems that value every bond. They are embarrassed by surface thirst, when they encounter it in legends of the outside, in the rare returned exile raving of dryness. They are *creepy* in their satisfaction, their moist contentment, their apparent completion without what surface life considers essential.

They speak, when they speak, in *tones* that emerge from and return to the hum, carrier waves riding carrier waves. The meaning is not in the pitch alone but in the *interference pattern*, the beat frequencies created between voice and geological thrum and biological chorus. Two people speaking create three sounds: each voice, and the differential between, and it is in this third sound that the actual communication often lives, the *space between* voices given informational content. Their hearing is acute, trained from birth to filter signal from noise, to attend to the particular frequencies where meaning lives. Deafness, as noted, is death—not immediate physical death, but social, informational, *ecological* death, removal from the substrate of shared knowledge.

They do not, individually, know much. This must be understood. No scholar contains the whole of Keth-Vorath's accumulated learning, because that learning is not accumulated in individuals but *distributed*, in the growth patterns of resin-tenders arranged by generations of placement, in the swarm-aspect cycles negotiated over centuries, in the hum itself and its variations that encode—*somehow*, not understood by those who participate in it—information about warmth and coolness and pressure and the slow shifts of the geological engine. The people know what they need to know for their niche, their shelf-garden, their tick-picker lineage, their tonal family. The rest is *elsewhere*, in the city-organism's distributed cognition, accessible not by query but by *placement*, by being in the right chamber at the right phase of cycle, by hearing the particular interference that means, to those attuned, *the heat-heads are molting early* or *Vorath-proper is warming beyond recent parameters* or *someone has died in Keth-middens and the aspect-swarmers are cleaning*.

They age, but the aging is *gradual mineralization*, tissues accumulating calcium and other elements from the biota they consume, the air they breathe, becoming slowly harder, slower, more *resinous*. The very old are not infirm but *stable*, seated in their niches for years sometimes, tended by family swarms, by glide-mice they can no longer reach to feed, by the slow seep of nutrients through touch and tick-picker exchange and the communal sharing that is not charity but *system maintenance*, the repair of a component whose knowledge or presence remains valuable. Death, when it comes, is incorporation: the tissues too mineralized for easy consumption are ground for fertilizer, the bones incorporated into structure, the tick-pickers transferred—hurriedly, ritually, with tonal mourning that is also recruitment—to living hosts. There is no grave. There is no ghost. The hum continues, and the dead are frequencies in it now rather than sources, but frequencies nonetheless, present in the growth they shaped, the swarms they negotiated, the interference patterns they established with living voices.

---

What lives in Keth-Vorath is *sufficiency*, then. Not abundance, which implies surplus and storage and the future orientation that scarcity demands. Not poverty, which implies comparison to other conditions and their lack. Simply: what is needed is present, in forms strange to the surface, distributed across species and growths and tonal frequencies and the constant, active, *humming* negotiation of coexistence. The people are not masters. The swarmers are not servants. The resin-tenders are not crops. Each is condition for the others, habitat and inhabitant mutually constituted, the city-organism's metabolism incorporating all in flows that permit no static role, no permanent category, no *identity* separable from the process of living this particular life in this particular place.

They do not dream of water. They do not, mostly, dream of surface, of outside, of the dry death-light that waits beyond the vertical slit. Occasional individuals do, are driven to, attempt the edge-climber's path and usually fail, their bodies found—sometimes—by the next climber's passage, incorporated as edge-climbers are, made useful. The ones who succeed in reaching the surface do not return, cannot return, are not mourned because they are *not known to have died*, a meaningful distinction in a place where death is incorporation and absence is only the not-yet-found.

They dream, when they dream, of *deeper*. Of Vorath-proper opening into further chambers, hotter, wetter, stranger. Of geological engines not smaller but *larger* than the one they know, sustaining ecologies beyond imagination in the world's first sleep. This is their infinity, their expansion, their escape from the pocket scale that contains them: not outward to surface and stars and the empty light, but inward, downward, to denser, louder, warmer, more *present* versions of what they already have.

The hum continues. The heat-heads peep. The glide-mice collide and cling. The tick-pickers feed and signal. The swarmers shift aspect. The edge-climbers climb, or do not. The resin-tenders pulse their bioluminescence in rhythms that encode—something, to those with patience to attend, to those who are, themselves, attended by sufficient ticks, sufficient warmth, sufficient *placement* in the city's constant, active, overwhelming, loud, lush, built, primitive, warm, dense, hidden *now*.

This is what lives. This is what is enough.
