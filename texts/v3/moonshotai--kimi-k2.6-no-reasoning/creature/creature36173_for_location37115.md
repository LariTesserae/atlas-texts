The inhabitants of the Hollow of Still Song have no collective name, need none, conceive none. They are not a species, not a community, not an ecosystem in any dynamic sense. Each exists as a single term in an equation already balanced, a particle in a solution saturated long before any observer arrived to test it.

Consider the one you might glimpse after walking an hour through the young stone valley: low-slung, furred, a shape that suggests mammal only because mammal is a category you bring with you. Its eyes are large, yes, adapted to dimness, but there is something wrong with how they reflect—not the green flash of a tapetum, but a kind of cancellation, light entering and not returning, not absorbed but *resolved*, the eye functioning not to capture image but to complete some optical necessity of the place. It moves across your path, pauses, continues. It was doing nothing before it arrived at the point where you stood. It does nothing after. The pause was not observation. There was no curiosity in it, no assessment of threat or opportunity. Perhaps the pause was mathematical: your path and its intersecting at a node that required brief acknowledgment before both could continue as non-interacting functions.

Or consider, in the dampest pocket where lichen huddles and something mossy cushions the uneven stone, the reptilian presence—cool-blooded is wrong, for there is no temperature differential to exploit, no sun to seek, no shade to flee. Its blood, if blood it has, matches the coolness absolutely, a perfect thermal equilibrium with the medium that makes metabolism, as you understand metabolism, unnecessary. It exists. It glistens with moisture that is not sweat, not secretion, merely ambient water adhering to skin that has learned to hold it without distinction between inside and outside. It does not bask. It does not hunt. Small organisms sufficient to its needs pass through it, into it, are assimilated without the violence of predation, simply *included*, the way the damp includes the lichen, the way the sound includes everything.

Most strange, perhaps, the winged ones. They have the architecture of flight—hollow bones, feathered surfaces, the whole engineering of lift and thrust—and they do not use it. You will see them walking, their wings folded not in the tidy compression of grounded birds but in a kind of permanent disuse, like limbs remembered from evolutionary dreaming. The air here does not move. Why would it? Movement requires gradient, and the Hollow has solved its gradients. The winged ones could fly, physically, their bodies still permit it, but flight implies destination, and destination implies that somewhere else is better, or different, or necessary to reach. None of this obtains. They walk slowly, placing each foot with deliberation that suggests not caution but completion, each step the only possible step, the path not chosen but inherent, the walking itself an expression of the equilibrium rather than any effort toward it.

The soft-bodied ones, faintly luminous, leaving their persistent trails—they may be the most honest inhabitants, or the most evolved toward what the Hollow demands. They have no hard parts, no structure to maintain against entropy, because entropy, here, is not pressing. They glow from within, but the glow does not illuminate, does not attract mates or prey, serves no signaling function. It is simply *emitted*, a byproduct of chemistry that has no purpose to trim it away, no selection pressure to redirect the energy. Their trails do not dry because drying is a gradient process, water moving from more concentrated to less concentrated, and here all concentrations are equal, all potentials already spent. The trails persist as records of passage, but passage without destination, the record without need of being read.

The sound, if it is theirs—they may be its source, or its medium, or its effect, the causal chain long since collapsed into simultaneity. Each creature, in its stillness, perhaps contributes. The furred one, breathing. The reptilian, some slow chemical exchange. The winged, hearts beating without haste. The luminous, whatever faint process powers their glow. Each a tone, each a frequency, and together not a harmony but a *flattening*, all differences in phase or amplitude ironed out by the acoustics of the young stone, the dimensions of the Hollow, the absolute stillness of the air that carries no competing vibration. The sound is their aggregate, but aggregate without summation, a sum that is also each term unchanged.

They do not reproduce, or they reproduce without generation. Young and adult coexist, yes, but whether this represents continuous breeding, or arrested development, or some dissemination of selves without distinction between parent and offspring—observation cannot determine, because observation requires time, and time in the Hollow is a property you bring with you, exhaust, carry away. Stay long enough, and you might see a furred one appear where none was, fully formed, already moving in its unhurried path, indistinguishable from those you passed an hour before. Whether it was born, or split, or simply *manifested* as the equilibrium demanded its presence at that coordinate, you cannot say. Perhaps it has always been emerging, always will be, the appearance to your eye a momentary crystallization of eternal process into apparent event.

They do not learn. The cracked stones, the wedged sticks—these are not tools in any accumulating sense, not technology in embryo. Each is an isolated solution to an isolated problem, if problem it was, and the abandonment of each is not forgetfulness but *completion*, the problem and its solution existing as a single static fact rather than a sequence to be remembered and repeated. You will not find caches, workshops, any of the material culture that marks even the simplest tool-using species elsewhere. Here, each act is sufficient unto itself, not because the inhabitants are wise but because they have no continuity to maintain, no tradition to transmit, no benefit in saving effort for future application. The effort, too, is not effort, not costly, not conserved.

What do they *want*? The question is yours, not theirs. Wanting requires that the present state be less than satisfactory, that some future state be imagined as more so, that action bridge the gap. The Hollow's inhabitants occupy states that need no bridging. They do not want, any more than the young stone wants to erode, than the sound wants to cease, than the damp wants to dry. They exist in the fullness of their existence, which is not fullness as you experience it—not satisfaction, not contentment, not the positive quality of desire fulfilled—but the zero point where wanting has never been added, where the possibility of deficiency has never emerged.

Are they happy? They are not anything that happiness is the opposite of. Are they aware? Awareness, in your sense, implies distance between self and world, a gap across which attention must travel, information pass. They may be so continuous with their medium that such distance never forms. They do not perceive the Hollow. They *are* the Hollow's perception of itself, if perception is the word, if self is the word, if the Hollow, finally, is anything that can be named as separate from what it contains.

And you, if you stay. If you let the sound enter not just your ears but your intentions, if you let the coolness become not condition but nature, if you stop measuring, naming, counting, if you let your own gradients—hunger, thirst, the need for change, the narrative arc of a day—flatten toward the equilibrium. Would you become like them? Would you lose your history, your projection, your terrible human capacity for wanting what is not present?

Or would you become something the Hollow has never contained: aware equilibrium, self-conscious zero, a creature that knows its own stillness as stillness, that holds the paradox of changeless change? The inhabitants, however they exist, do not know themselves as inhabitants. They do not know the Hollow as Hollow, the song as song. They are the stillness without the knowledge of stillness, the song without the ear to hear it as music. You, in your disequilibrium, might bridge this: become still and know your stillness, become song and hear yourself singing.

None have, or none have returned to report it. The Hollow offers this possibility without promising it, presents it without requiring it, includes it in the equilibrium as it includes everything, as possibility rather than event, as potential without the gradient that would actualize.

The furred one moves again, across your path, through the dimness where color refuses names. The reptilian glistens in its damp pocket. The winged one walks, wings folded, each step complete. The luminous trail extends, undrying, into the young stone's sharp shadow. The sound continues, is them, is not them, is the condition of their being and the being of their condition. They persist. They do not accumulate. They do not end.

This is what lives in the Hollow of Still Song. Or rather: this is what the Hollow permits to appear as living, to your eye, in your time, with your categories. What they are, in themselves, in their equilibrium, in their sound—this cannot be spoken, because speech is gradient, is breath moving from pressure to release, is meaning built across time. They are silent in their song. They are still in their persistence. They are here, have always been here, will be here when the young stone finally, impossibly, yields to age, when the sound completes itself by ceasing, when the Hollow, its equilibrium perfect beyond any perturbation, perhaps finally *is*, without remainder, without witness, without the word that tries, and fails, and tries again, to name what has no need of naming.
