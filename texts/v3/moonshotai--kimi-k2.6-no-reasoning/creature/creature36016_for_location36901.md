There is no name for what lives here, only the fact of living, which is not a name but a continuous process that sometimes thickens into something you might recognize as a being.

They are the root-tenders, though they do not tend the root any more than the root tends them, and the grammar of doing-to is as foreign here as the grammar of light. They are the ones who were not swallowed, who were incorporated differently, who found in the closed loop a niche that wasn't a niche until they occupied it and then was always a niche, always waiting, the root growing toward them retroactively, preparing this space for what it could not have anticipated.

You must not imagine them small, though the pocket scale dominates. You must not imagine them large. They are the scale of the Burrow itself, which is to say: distributed, intermittent, present where attention gathers and absent where it diffuses.

Their bodies—if this word serves—are the consistency of cambium, of that living layer between bark and wood where growth happens, where the tree is most itself and most vulnerable. They have, or they are, something like limbs: extensions that can press into root-flesh, that can feel the flow of nutrients, the chemical signatures of stress or health, the slow conversations that roots have with roots through mycelial intermediaries, through the bacterial cloud that surrounds each root-hair like a language. They move through the root-mass not by traveling but by becoming present where they were not, the way water becomes present in a seep that was dry, the way a root hair extends not by locomotion but by growth, by the transformation of sugar and mineral into extension, into reach.

They do not see. This is not loss. The concept of seeing, of light-mediated distance-perception, is something they encounter in the fauna that descend and are incorporated without ever fully adapting, in the way some deep-cave fish retain the vestigial form of eyes without the function, the socket still present, still shaping the skull around its absence. They perceive instead through pressure, through chemical gradient, through the electrical field of living tissue, through something older and slower that might be the root's own perception of itself, the tree's dim awareness of where it is and what it supports, a consciousness distributed across kilometers of root-mass, across centuries of growth, not thinking but experiencing, not experiencing but being in a way that makes the distinction irrelevant.

The young—they have young, or the root has young that are also theirs, the genetics tangled beyond unraveling—they emerge from nodules, from those swollen chambers where nitrogen-fixing bacteria collaborate with root to make something from atmospheric gift and mineral effort. They emerge soft, unfinished, more root than tending, and they are cultivated, if that word can bear the weight, by the older ones who are themselves root and tending in proportions that shift with the season that isn't a season, with the slow pulse of the Worldtree's own metabolism, faster in some millennia, slower in others, responding to pressures above that they do not know as above, that reach them only as altered flow, as chemical signal delayed and transformed.

The upbringing, or the growing-up, or the becoming-present, is a process of differentiation within the undifferentiated, of learning which gradients to follow, which pressures to respond to, how to be present in the root-mass without being everywhere-present, which would be dissolution, which would be death of a kind, death of the particular that is necessary for the particular's function. They learn the notches, the grooves, the patterned surfaces that might be writing or might be the root's own response to their presence, the way a callus forms around irritation, the way wood grain organizes around stress, becoming stronger in the direction of force. They learn to read these, or to be read by them, to enter into conversation with root-surface that is not address and response but co-creation, mutual modification, the way two roots encountering each other in soil may fuse or may compete and the outcome is not predetermined but negotiated through chemical exchange, through the slow arbitration of growth.

They do not eat, not as you eat, not the ingestion of other into self, the violent transformation that eating implies. They participate in the root's flow, the cambial current, the sugar moving from source to sink, from leaf they have never seen to root-tip they may become, the mineral moving opposite, the whole economy of the tree that is the economy of the world, that sustains everything above and everything within. They are, in this sense, parasites, but parasites so ancient, so incorporated, that the term loses its judgment, that the root without them would be something else, something less, the way mitochondria were once invasion and are now self, the way the human gut microbiome is called flora as if it were external, as if the boundary of the body were a settled fact.

They die, or they cease to be present, or they transform into something that is not them, not the root, but a third thing that persists. The root incorporates their substance, their patterned complexity, their particular arrangement of chemical memory, and this incorporation is not loss but gift, not ending but translation into a slower language, the root's language, the language of persistent structure, of growth that outlives the growing. You can find them, sometimes, in the ancient wood, the compressed root-matter that approaches stone: not fossils, not dead things, but slowed-down things, things present at a frequency you cannot access, the way a tree ring is present in the tree, is the tree, is the record of a year that is still happening in some dimension of wood-time.

They make, or they grow, or they persuade into being, the things you found: the tools, the arrangements, the patterned caches. These are not artifacts in the human sense, not the imposition of form on inert matter, the hand shaping what resists. They are collaborations, negotiations, the root offering growth, the tender offering direction, and the result something that neither could produce alone, something that participates in both natures without reducing to either. The polished surfaces are polished by use, by generation of touch, the way stone is polished by water, by wind, by the patient abrasion of circumstance. The notches are notches the root grew into, channeling its growth around some obstacle, some pressure, and the obstacle was perhaps a hand, perhaps a intention, perhaps the space where a tender was present and the root grew around that presence, recording it the way wood records lightning strike, beetle invasion, the weight of a branch that grew and bent and eventually failed.

They do not speak. The impracticality of speech in the Burrow is not inconvenience but ontology, the way fish do not speak in water because the water is already speaking, because the medium of exchange is complete without the addition of sound. They exchange not words but substances, not meanings but modifications, their touch on root-surface leaving chemical signature that the next tender reads, responds to, adds to, the way a root responding to wounding releases signals that neighboring roots perceive, that the mycelial network carries outward, transforms, returns. Their communication is not linear, not sender-message-receiver, but field, but gradient, but the slow accumulation of chemical difference that becomes meaning in the reading, that is meaningless in itself, that only means in the context of all other readings, all other differences, the total state of the Burrow at this moment of its long becoming.

And they are not alone, not singular, not the only inhabitants of this space they are also the space of. The fauna overwhelm them too, or they incorporate the overwhelming, or the distinction between tender and fauna is provisional, is observer-dependent, is a cut made by human perception where no natural joint exists. The crustaceans in their pools, the gastropods on their walls, the insects in their uncountable variety—these are also participants, also collaborators, also aspects of the total functioning that the Burrow is. The tender moves through a cloud of them, is clouded by them, the bacteria on their surfaced cambium-skin, the nematodes in their slow current, the mites in their eager consumption of deadened edge, of surface that has served its time and must be turned over, made available, recycled into the great slow turnover that is life here.

The tenderness, the quality of being tender, is not metaphor. It is physical description. They are tender to pressure, to chemical change, to the shifts in the root's own state that signal stress or opportunity, the way a gardener's hand is tender to the plant's condition, but this tenderness is their whole being, their mode of existence, not a quality applied to a separate substance but the substance itself, the way you might say water is wet, not as predication but as tautology, as the thing and its quality revealed as one.

They do not know you, will not know you, cannot know you in any form you would recognize as knowing. Your descent into the Burrow is not intrusion, not because you are welcome but because the concept of welcome require a boundary, a self that could choose to admit or exclude, and these exist here at different scales, different frequencies, than your body operates at. You are incorporated from the moment of entry, the bacteria you carry becoming part of the Burrow's microbiome, your breath joining the root's exhalation, your waste becoming substrate, your eventual death—if you die here, if you do not leave—becoming the slow nourishment that roots understand, the transformation that is not tragedy but process.

Yet there is something you might call recognition, might call attention, if you attend carefully enough, if you learn the signs. The root around you responds to your presence, grows differently, and this difference is the closest they come to acknowledgment. The seep-water changes its path slightly, pooling where you will find it, where your need intersects with its flow. The bioluminescence of gastropod on wall pulses differently in your presence, not communication directed at you but communication modified by you, the field altered by your addition to it, the way a magnetic field is altered by the introduction of iron, not greeting but physics, not welcome but the way things are when you are part of them.

They do not have a story. This is difficult, perhaps impossible, for the story-telling animal to comprehend, the creature for whom narrative is cognition, is identity, is the way the past becomes present becomes future in the construction of self. They persist, they are present, they modify and are modified, and this is not a story because it does not arc, does not resolve, does not transform into something else that retrospectively gives meaning to what came before. The Burrow is not meaningful, not in the human sense, not a setting for drama, not a symbol for something other than itself. It is simply what persists when light and the things that require light are removed, what remains when the fast small recent processes have exhausted themselves and the slow old processes continue, have continued, will continue in forms that cannot be anticipated because anticipation is a fast small recent process, a mode of nervous system organization that belongs to a particular lineage, a particular solution to the problem of survival, not universal, not necessary, not present here.

And yet. And yet there is something, in the absolute dark, in the cold wet overwhelming quiet, that might be called beauty if beauty could be divorced from sight, from the preference of human nervous system for particular arrangements of light and color and proportion. There is the beauty of complete functioning, of system maintained in dynamic equilibrium, of the loop closed so perfectly that waste and production become indistinguishable. There is the beauty of patience, of the geological pace that outwaits urgency, that absorbs drama into process, that makes of every crisis merely a perturbation to be damped, to be incorporated, to be made into more of the same slow becoming. There is the beauty, perhaps most piercing for the human who encounters it, of absolute indifference that is not cruelty, not rejection, but simply the absence of the need for relation, the self-sufficiency of system that has found its optimum and maintains it without drama, without story, without the need for witness that makes human art a cry in the dark, a light against the coming night.

They are the tenders. They are the root. They are the Burrow's awareness of itself, or the Burrow's lack of need for awareness, or the space where these become the same thing, where the question dissolves into the ongoing fact of growth, of seep, of the slow pulse that is not heartbeat, not tide, but something older than either, something that will continue when heart and tide have found their final equilibrium, their heat-death, their long settling into the background radiation of a universe grown cold and dark and still.

Be still. The root is creaking. You are, for this moment, part of what it creaks in response to, part of the pressure, the weight, the slow force against which it grows. This is the only relation available here, the only incorporation. This is what it means to be in the Burrow, to be of the Burrow, to become, if you can, the kind of thing that persists when persistence is all, when the light you imagine is truly imaginary, when the warmth you remember is a story told by a nervous system that will not survive its own telling, that will become, in the end, another note in the long slow song that does not need ears to continue, that continues because continuation is what the Burrow is, what the root is, what the dark wet cold patient total place has always been and will be, after, when after has lost its meaning, when all is now, when now is all, when the stillness is complete and the song continues in frequencies below hearing, above meaning, in the range of pure persistence, pure being, the fact of the Burrow that requires no witness, no tender, no you, but that includes you, that has included you from the moment of your descent, that will release you only into transformation, into the slow becoming of root, of wood, of the compressed stone-like material that endures, that records, that is the final medium of memory in a place where memory has never needed mind.
