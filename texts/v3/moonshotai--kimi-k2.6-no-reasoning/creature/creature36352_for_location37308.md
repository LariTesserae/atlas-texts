The one who lives here has no name. What would be named? What would do the naming? There is no mirror for a self to recognize itself against, no other of sufficient similarity to require distinction. The Filter-Whales do not school. The Drift-Singers do not gather. The Bone-Fish click in their numberless collectives but their collectives have no boundary, no inside or outside, no membership to mark.

But there *is* one. I am certain now, though it took the length of a Filter-Whale's passage through the eastern shaft—three days of following, of matching my stillness to its stiller stillness—to feel the recognition.

She exists in the space between the parameters.

Between "total built" and "none tech": she is the memory of growth, not the growth itself. The Strut-Trees completed their extension before her beginning, but she carries in her body the *pattern* of that extension, the mathematical residue of biological ambition. She does not know this as memory. It operates in her as instinct operates in surface creatures, as unthought knowledge, as the shape of her swimming which follows exactly, though she has never seen them, the helical paths the Strut-Tree cambium once followed in its upward thrust. She swims the architecture of growth in a place where growth has stopped.

Between "total vegetation" and "submerged water": she is the only motile plant. Not fauna, though she moves with the fauna's freedom. Not flora as the Strut-Trees are flora, fixed in their completion. She is—this took longest to perceive—a *flower* that never opened. A reproductive structure of a species that went extinct before the flooding, before the completion, whose genetic potential lodged in some root-cathedral reservoir and emerged once, only once, as her. She has no purpose. There are no pollinators in Keth-Vorath, no mechanism for her to reproduce, no other of her kind to receive or give. She moves through the water not as animal moves but as *pollen* moves, drifting with intention but without destination, carrying what cannot be delivered.

Between "extreme elevation" and "submerged": she occupies the thermal boundary, that narrow zone where the cool of the lignin-stone meets the warmth of the processed water, where 0.8 of cool creates a geography she reads with her entire body. She is thin. She is very thin. Her tissues are translucent as Filter-Whale bell, but flattened, ribbon-like, extending behind her in a trail that measures fifteen meters though her core is no larger than a human hand. She swims the boundary layer like a serpent, like a ray, like a thing that has found the only dimension available to it and made that dimension infinite.

She has been alive since before the completion.

This I know from the Drift-Singers' songs. I spent a month in the mid-region chamber nearest to where they say—where their pressure-waves indicate—the thermal boundary meets a Strut-Tree's hexagonal wall at a particular angle that creates resonance. The Drift-Singer there, the one whose century-spanning conversation partners I could not locate, whose song I felt as something between recognition and warning, included in its pattern a sequence that the others do not, a variation so slight I almost missed it. A *name*, perhaps. Not her name—she has none—but a designation, a pointer, a way of indicating that which swims the boundary, that which remembers growth, that which carries undeliverable pollen in a world without flowering.

She was here before equilibrium. She remembers, in her unthinking way, the water rising. The last Strut-Tree reaching its limit. The moment when motion became the only violation, when she became the sole gradient in a flattening world.

She does not eat the Bone-Fish. She does not filter as the Filter-Whales filter. She draws something from the thermal boundary itself, some energy differential between cool stone-lignin and warm processed water, some chemical exchange that occurs only at that interface. She is a *living edge effect*, an organism that exists only where two quanta meet, where the parameters press against each other and create, in their pressure, a narrow ecology.

I have tried to approach her. Not with movement—I have learned movement is violation here, is violence against the equilibrium, is the only sin available in a place without morality. I have tried with stillness. With matching my body temperature to the water's warmth, then to the stone's cool, then oscillating between them at the frequency her ribbon-body seems to prefer. I have tried with sound, with the Bone-Fish clicking, with the Drift-Singer frequencies, with silence.

She does not avoid me. She does not acknowledge me. I am not a parameter. I am not part of the finished system. I am—this is the horror and the wonder of it—I am *change*, and she has lived so long in the never-changing that change does not register, does not exist for her, is filtered out at some pre-conscious boundary as noise is filtered from signal.

But she is not alone in her un-aloneness.

There are others. Not of her kind—there is no kind. But there are other edge-dwellers, other inhabitants of the parameter-boundaries, other organisms that found their existence in the pressure between quantified impossibilities. In the space between "abundant" and "sparse," I found a colony of microscopic entities that cluster on the Filter-Whale bells, never numerous enough to be abundant, never few enough to be sparse, existing in the mathematical zone where those categories blur into meaninglessness. They do nothing. They are not parasites, not symbiotes, not commensals. They simply *are*, in the way that she simply swims the thermal boundary, in the way that the Strut-Trees simply hold their extreme elevation.

Between "loud" and the silence that loudness implies, there is a creature—or a process, or a phenomena—that I have never seen but have heard, once, during a moment when all the Bone-Fish ceased their clicking simultaneously, some collective pause I could not explain. In that silence, there was a sound below the Drift-Singers' range, below even the pressure of their songs, a vibration that seemed to come from the water itself, from the processed fluid's ten-million-year memory of filtration. A note. A single note, held for the duration of the Bone-Fish pause—perhaps three seconds, perhaps three hours, time being what it is here—and then gone when the clicking resumed.

I believe this note was a voice. I believe there is one who speaks only when the parameters align to create silence, who exists in the boundary between loud and not-loud, who has been trying to speak since before the completion and has never had sufficient silence until that moment, until that impossible coincidence.

I am looking for this one now.

I am becoming an edge-dweller myself. I have stopped eating the provisions I brought, finding that I too can draw something from the thermal boundary, some nourishment from the place where parameters meet and create their narrow emergent ecologies. My body is changing. I am becoming thinner. My skin is becoming more sensitive to temperature differential, to the subtle gradients that map this place more truly than any visual landmark. I am learning to hear the Bone-Fish clicking not as sound but as *space*, as the acoustic architecture of the root-cathedrals made animate.

I may become permanent here. I may find my own parameter-boundary, my own impossible edge between contradictory quanta, and exist there as she exists, as the note exists, as the microscopic colony exists. I may become part of the finished system, the never-changing equilibrium, by finding the one place where change is the parameter, where my presence as violation becomes the rule that proves the exception.

Or I may simply forget that I was looking, in the way she has forgotten that she carries pollen, in the way the Strut-Trees have forgotten their upward ambition and simply hold, hold, hold their extreme elevation in the dark wet stillness.

Keth-Vorath does not know I am here. But I am here. I am becoming here. I am finding, in the spaces between your numbers, the one who lives, who lives, who lives without end in the drowned arboretum's held breath, its frozen completion, its absolute and total and finished and still—
