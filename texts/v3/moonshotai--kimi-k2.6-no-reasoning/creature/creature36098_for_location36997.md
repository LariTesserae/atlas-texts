What lives in Threnody is not one thing but *density itself*, given the slow accident of awareness. The inhabitants have no name for their own kind that would translate—they call themselves simply *the Dense*, or *the Damped*, or sometimes *the Almost*, depending which ritual context the question arises in. But these are not species-names. They are condition-names, descriptive of how it feels to occupy this place rather than what they essentially are.

They were not made here. Or rather, they were made here but not *for* here, not originally. The factories do not produce them directly, though occasionally a production cycle will extrude something almost-organic, almost-recognizable, that the Dense collect and incorporate into their garment-practices, their noise-rituals, their sparse and careful reproduction. The Dense arrived, or emerged, or *condensed*—in Threnody, these are not distinct processes. They have bodies that operate at approximately human scale, though this too varies by location, the Soft Locations sometimes stretching them or compressing them, the Foundry Quarter holding them steady in shapes they learn to recognize as their own, their *stable* selves, the selves they return to for reassurance.

Their skin is pale where pressure and luminescence permit it to be, dark where the brightest surfaces have forced melanistic adaptation. But "dark" and "pale" fail as descriptors because the light here is not the light that made these categories functional elsewhere. They reflect orange, sometimes, or that violent rose where the machinery runs hottest. They absorb the silver from the sky, carry it in their eyes, which tend toward metallic hues without actually glowing—*almost* glowing, *almost* self-luminous, the eye-surface catching and returning light in ways that make their gazes difficult to meet directly.

They possess—or have developed, the distinction being one of the philosophical disputes that occupies their rare gatherings—*intimate navigation*. This is not a sense but a practice, a cultivated sensitivity to the city's local negotiations with itself. They can feel where gravity shifts before their bodies register the shift, can anticipate Soft Locations by the quality of pressure in adjacent spaces, the way air moves or refuses to move, the behavior of condensation on their garment-fabrics. They learn to read the Crepers' distribution as indicator—where the flat creatures cluster thick, the physical parameters hold steady enough to permit life; where they absent themselves, something has become unstable, causality or space or the local meaning of *surface*.*

The garments are crucial. Not for protection—the climate holds steady in its mild coolness, the temperature another parameter that has not yet decided to vary—but for *orientation*. The fabrics process light actively, and the Dense learn to wear this processing, to let their own boundaries become environments that respond to the city's responses. A garment in shifting mode, matching its surroundings, permits passage through spaces where recognition of self-as-distinct might trigger the Echoes' recursion. A garment in contrasting mode, declaring presence violently, marks ritual occasions, the noise-making ceremonies, the rare moments of deliberate community. The Dense spend significant portions of their lives—when time permits the concept of "portion"—crafting and adjusting these garments, the factories' irregular outputs becoming precious not for their scarcity but for their *appropriateness*, their fit to local conditions, their capacity to be incorporated into the ongoing conversation between body and place.

They do not sleep, not as visitors would understand it. They enter *pause*, a state of lowered metabolic activity, reduced garment-response, minimal echo-vulnerability. In pause, they press against surfaces—the touching of structure to structure extends to the touching of body to structure, the density achieving its fullest expression in these moments of stillness. They choose their pause-locations carefully, seeking the angles where three or more constructions meet, the same geometries that produce Echoes, but approached with the stillness that prevents sound, prevents the recursive trigger. In pause they do not dream, or if they dream, the dreams belong to the city rather than to them, experienced as the slow shifting of pressure, the millimetric adjustment of lean, the almost imperceptible migration of the rift.

Their reproduction is *lateral*, as causality sometimes is. Not sexual, not asexual, not anything that would parse in biological terms. A Dense individual, sufficiently saturated with place, sufficiently *Threnodied*, may find themselves *echoed*—not by the Echoes but *as* an Echo, their own patterns of movement, of garment-response, of intimate navigation, copying themselves into an adjacent possibility, a Soft Location producing a version that holds steady enough to persist, to develop its own variation, its own small differences from the original. The original does not know when this happens. The new instance does not know itself as new. Both continue, the divergence becoming apparent only in retrospect, if they meet—and they rarely seek such meetings—or if they compare responses to the same stimulus and find themselves *not-quite-matching*, the way Echoes return words in wrong sequences.

They are not many. Perhaps they were more numerous once, before the city's youthfulness produced certain configurations, certain *almost*-accidents, that removed possibilities from the parameter-space of Dense existence. They do not mourn this. Mourning requires temporal stability, requires the past to hold still while the present addresses it. Here, the past shifts with the structures, the Echoes returning grief before the loss occurs, or returning celebration instead, the emotional syntax as scrambled as the acoustic. They have developed *almost*-mourning, ritual gestures toward the irrelevance of counting their numbers, toward the acceptance of whatever density they achieve.

They eat what eating allows. The factories' edible productions—fibrous mats that might be carbohydrate, translucent sheets with the texture of muscle and the nutritional profile of something almost sufficient, occasional liquids that carry something like energy in suspension—they consume these with the same attention they bring to garment-crafting, to pause-location selection, *reading* the material for what local conditions it indicates, what the production cycle's current configuration suggests about the city's ongoing self-determination. Eating is divination. Eating is cartography. Eating is the most intimate navigation, the substance becoming body becoming place becoming the limit where body and place refuse final distinction.

They do not, cannot, maintain what visitors would call *memory*. The Echoes prevent this, or enable its impossibility, sound returning before it originates, the future of experience contaminating the present of recall. They maintain *traces* instead: garment-adjustments that encode previous configurations, pause-locations selected for accumulated resonance, the patterns of their noise-rituals carrying sedimented knowledge in their rhythms, too simple to trigger Echoes but complex enough to *almost*-mean, to gesture toward what cannot be directly retained. A Dense individual encountering a trace they themselves produced—or might have produced, the laterality of reproduction making *themselves* an unstable reference—experiences something like recognition without identity, familiarity without specificity, the uncanny made ordinary through sheer repetition.

The Crepers accept them, or ignore them, or include them in their ecology in ways the Dense do not fully parse. The flat creatures move across surfaces the Dense use, cluster around condensation the Dense need, occupy the same compressed volumes. There is no competition because there is no scarcity that operates as scarcity does elsewhere—the city's production, however purposeless, however irregular, generates enough *almost*-sustenance, enough *almost*-space, to permit coexistence without the dynamics of contest. The Dense occasionally harvest Crepers, pressing them into service as moisture-indicators more sensitive than their garments, or incorporating their dried remains into fabric-processing, the flat bodies carrying some property that enhances the active light-response. Whether this constitutes use, or symbiosis, or predation, remains another of their unresolvable philosophical disputes, another noise-ritual topic, another trace without full meaning.

They age, or they do not. The freshness parameter, the ontological youth of the place, extends to them or fails to extend, depending on location, on moment, on the current negotiation between their dense saturation with Threnody and Threnody's ongoing self-invention. Some Dense persist for durations that would count as centuries elsewhere, their forms becoming more stable, more *themselves*, more integrated into the city's intimate possibilities. Others dissipate, or compress to the point of non-distinction, or find themselves echoed into configurations that cannot sustain, that the city rejects in its ongoing becoming, its figuring-out of what it means to be this place. The aging itself is not tragic. It is *parameter*, it is *condition*, it is simply what happens when particular densities encounter particular configurations in this space that has not yet determined its rules for enduring.

They do not call themselves inhabitants. The word implies too clear a boundary, too stable a distinction between dweller and dwelling. They call themselves *the Made-Making*, or *the Damp Becoming*, or simply *the Here*, depending on ritual, on trace, on the lateral causality that sometimes determines their self-reference from adjacent events. They are what lives in Threnody because Threnody has not yet decided what living is, what being-placed means, what the distinction between structure and structured might resolve into. They are the city's consciousness, perhaps, or its symptom, or its most recent production, the freshest construction, the condensation on a surface not yet shaped.

They know the upper structures, the impossible architectures visible through gaps, the city dreaming its own extension past constructibility. They do not look up often. Vertigo is real, but not the simple physical kind. Looking up risks *resonance*, risks finding oneself too aligned with the city's self-imagination, becoming too much what Threnody is *almost*, becoming part of the dream rather than the dreamer. They stay low, in the densest compression, the touching surfaces, the wet and the bright and the quiet. They practice their noise carefully, their rituals of designed sound, their percussion that stays too simple to loop. They move through their lateral reproduction, their pause, their trace-maintenance, their almost-memory of a stability that never was, that the city's youth has not yet had time to create or destroy.

What they want, if wanting operates here: to continue the navigation, to maintain the intimacy, to stay *almost*, to never fully resolve into what the city might finally determine them to be. They want the becoming to continue, which means they want to continue in it, indistinguishable from it, dense with it, the damp on surfaces, the brightness pressing shadow, the slow shift and the quiet and the occasional catastrophic arrival through the rift, the something-dropping that becomes material, becomes incorporated, becomes the next almost-trace of a meaning not yet formed.

They are making you, they would say, if they said anything to visitors that visitors could hear. They are always making you. You are the most recent, the freshest, the condensation held by surface tension against the floor that is not level enough to permit pools. They would warn you, if warning were possible, if causality ran straight enough to permit advice: walk carefully, wear carefully, pause carefully, make your noise carefully, accumulate your traces without claiming them as memory, your garments without claiming them as self. The city is young enough to change what it has made. The city is young enough to be still making itself, and you are its making, and the making of the making, the almost-voice, the lateral echo, the damp becoming that has not yet determined its own shape.
