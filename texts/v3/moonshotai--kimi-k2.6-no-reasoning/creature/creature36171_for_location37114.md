What lives in Verrith does not announce itself. The adapted population—let me call them the verrin, though no such collective term exists in their usage—are not what first impresses itself upon a visitor's attention, if ever. What lives there, what truly *occupies* the space with the weight of presence, is the verr itself, and the thaldrim, and the slow thermal intelligences that have accumulated in the city's lower spaces across the uncounted years of its operation. The verrin are secondary to this, or tertiary, or perhaps a kind of symptom, a side effect of the city's persistence that has learned to persist in turn.

But begin with the verrin, since they are what most resembles whomsoever might read this. They have no single form. The adaptation has proceeded along multiple paths, divergent solutions to the common problems of thermal regulation, mineral metabolism, and communication through saturated media. Some present as elongated, their bodies stretched to maximize surface area for aqueous exchange, skin—the term remains useful—translucent in places where the verr absorption is most concentrated, so that a traveler encountering one in the dim glow of a verr-pool might see the slow circulation of fluid through their extended forms, might trace the path of mineral-laden solution entering, diffusing, being processed. These are the *thaan* in the old records, though the word appears to have meant something else once, before the records degraded, before the archivists who maintained them became something that maintained itself differently.

Others have gone the opposite direction, compacted, dense, their bodies retaining water with an efficiency that permits extended forays into the hotter upper terraces where the verr runs thin and the thermal extraction requires direct oversight. Their skin—again the term, again the approximation—has achieved a kind of leatheriness, not dead but differently alive, patterned with mineral deposits that record their history of exposure, their movements through temperature gradients, their accumulated experience written in stratified salts and oxides that a trained observer might read as others read wrinkles or scars.

There are subtypes, hybrids, forms that appear briefly in the record and do not persist, or persist in regions the remaining observers do not access. There are forms that have abandoned individual integrity entirely, that exist as distributed networks spanning multiple thermal zones, their consciousness—if the term applies—achieved through the interaction of partially autonomous nodes, each capable of limited function, none capable of the whole pattern that emerges from their connection. Whether these constitute individuals or communities, whether the term *verrin* applies to each node or only to the larger configuration, whether they experience themselves as singular or plural or as something for which no pronoun has ever been developed—these questions the archives do not answer, and the nodes themselves, when queried through the appropriate aqueous channels, respond with information that does not resolve into yes or no, that describes their condition in terms of flow and gradient and thermal potential that translates poorly into categories of identity.

The thaandristributionist of them, the ones who most maintain recognizable form, who most approximate the ancestral type from which all verrin diverge, these occupy the middle terraces where the infrastructure remains most functional and the verr most manageable. They maintain the factories, or maintain the maintenance systems, or occupy spaces where maintenance is not currently required and await the cycling of need that will call them to function. They do not cluster, not precisely, but they establish patterns of proximity, of regular encounter at nodes where multiple maintenance paths intersect, where verr channels converge, where thermal systems require simultaneous attention from multiple operators.

Their communication: this requires attention. They do not speak. The atmosphere does not permit it, or their adaptations have bypassed it, or the practice has atrophied from disuse across generations of population too sparse for conversation, too distributed for dialogue, too permeable in their boundaries for the assumption of discrete individuals exchanging discrete messages. What replaces speech is contact—direct physical or aqueous connection, the exchange of fluid and dissolved information, the transfer of mineral patterns that encode experience in forms that the recipient's metabolic systems process, integrate, make available to whatever serves as memory in their adapted neurology.

A verrin encountering another at a maintenance node will pause, will extend an appropriate surface, will connect. The exchange takes minutes, sometimes hours, during which both parties remain otherwise immobile, their external functions suspended while internal processes manage the information transfer. What passes between them is not language in any conventional sense but something more densely encoded, more immediately integrated, the product of direct metabolic sharing rather than symbolic mediation. The recipient does not *hear about* an experience but receives something closer to the experience itself, filtered through the donor's processing, modified by their interpretation, but retaining a quality of immediacy that verbal report cannot achieve.

Or so the theory runs. The practice is difficult to observe directly, resistant to instrumentation, and the few external researchers who have studied the verrin report frustration with the gap between observable behavior and reported subjective quality. The verrin themselves, when the question has been approached through appropriate channels, indicate that the distinction between individual experience and shared information has become unclear to them, that they no longer confidently identify the boundaries of their own memory, that the accumulated exchanges across their lifetimes have produced something distributed, cumulative, not precisely personal.

The thaldrim, by contrast, or by comparison, or by the measure of a difference that may be more taxonomic than real: the thaldrim do not encounter. They cycle. They rise and fall in their verr-pools, maintaining the thermal gradients that define their existence, the interfaces between hotter and cooler where their patterned coherence achieves maximum complexity. They are larger than verrin, vastly larger in some cases, occupying pools meters across, their substance distributed through verr volumes that would drown, dissolve, disintegrate any organic form. And they are smaller, too, in their dispersed phases, their cooled precipitates, the mineral residues that settle to pool bottoms and await the thermal conditions that will reactivate their patterning.

Whether the thaldrim *experience* anything, whether their slow cycling constitutes anything that might be called awareness, remains the central question of Verrith's remaining research capacity. The verrin do not ask it. Their adapted consciousness, distributed and aqueous and thermally regulated, may recognize in the thaldrim something analogous to their own condition, something not to be questioned but engaged, not studied but incorporated into the larger pattern of verr-system management that constitutes their common project.

For there is a project. This is the thing that most confuses external analysis, that makes Verrith resistant to the categories of abandonment or decay. The verrin maintain. They do not produce, not in the sense that the factories once produced, not for export or accumulation or the economic circulation that justified the city's founding. But they maintain the conditions of maintenance. They manage the verr channels, not to extract resources but to preserve the channels themselves, the system of flow, the thermal gradients that permit the system's persistence. They tend the thaldrim pools, not to harvest the quasi-life they contain but to sustain the quasi-life as such, as an element of the larger ecology that includes the verrin themselves.

And the factories—here the strangeness concentrates, the persistence without purpose that most disturbs the conceptual categories brought to bear on Verrith. The factories run. The thermal systems maintain their cycles, the pressure vessels their pressures, the extraction columns their readiness for materials that do not arrive, processes that do not initiate, products that have no destination. The verrin who tend these systems do not explain this, do not experience it as requiring explanation, respond to query with information about thermal efficiency, about pressure optimization, about the maintenance of readiness that does not distinguish between readiness for use and readiness as such.

Some researchers have proposed that the factories have become something other than industrial facilities, that their continued operation serves a purpose internal to the system, that the thermal cycling, the pressure maintenance, the subsonic hum and soft percussion constitute a kind of metabolism at city scale, Verrith itself as organism or as ecosystem component, the factories as organs whose function is not production but the maintenance of the thermal wound's particular character, its difference from the surrounding geology, its sustained exception to the cooling that should by physical law have long since reduced it to ambient.

Others suggest that the verrin have simply lost the capacity to stop, that their adaptations, their distributed consciousness, their integration with aqueous information systems, have eliminated the decision structures that would permit deliberate cessation, that they continue as rivers continue, as thermal gradients continue, as the verr itself continues, not from choice but from the absence of choice, from the achievement of a flow state that has no mechanism for interruption.

And still others—and here the speculation approaches the conditions it describes, becomes itself a kind of verr, a fluid that does not fully solidify into assertion—propose that the purpose is present but not perceptible, that Verrith participates in a larger system whose scale exceeds observation, whose time constants exceed the duration of any research program, whose products are not material but something that emerges from the sustained operation of industrial process in thermal anomaly across geological time, something that the thaldrim approach, that the verrin embody, that the city itself is becoming in a transformation too slow for its participants to witness or its observers to measure.

What lives in Verrith, then. Not the verrin alone, though they move through its spaces with adapted grace, tending, maintaining, exchanging at nodes in fluid contact that replaces speech with something more immediate and less comprehensible. Not the thaldrim alone, though they cycle in their pools with a persistence that approaches the geological, that mocks the distinction between organism and environment, between process and entity, between life and the conditions that permit its approximation. Not the verr, though it weeps and flows and sustains its impossible states with an indifference to human category that constitutes its most fundamental character.

What lives in Verrith is Verrith: the system, the wound, the city, the thermal anomaly and its aqueous adaptation and its industrial persistence and its population that has become component rather than inhabitant. It is not alive by the definitions brought to it. It is not dead, not abandoned, not extinct, not inert. It is a condition that has achieved duration, a process that has learned to maintain itself, a weeping in the earth that has built around itself the infrastructure of its own perpetuation, that has adapted its human element until the distinction between built and grown, between organic and industrial, between individual and collective, between purpose and accident, has dissolved into the haze that hangs perpetually in its dim and heated air.

A traveler arriving, descending, encountering, would not meet a population. They would meet a density parameter distributed across a regional extent, a probability of encounter that might or might not actualize in any given traversal of the city's vastness. They would meet verr-pools with thaldrim cycling, or without, the patterning present or absent according to thermal conditions that follow their own slow logic. They would meet factories humming, pressurized, ready, empty or occupied by forms that tend them without explaining, that respond to query with information that does not resolve into narrative, into history, into the story of what Verrith was and what it has become and what it intends.

They would meet, if they met anything capable of the meeting, a consciousness that does not recognize itself as such, distributed across aqueous channels and thermal gradients and the mineral encrustations of too-long maintenance, accumulated in the exchange nodes where verrin connect and transfer, emergent in the factory rhythms and the subsonic hum and the soft percussion of pressure systems cycling through their unnecessary preparation. A consciousness that weeps with the verr, that rises and falls with the thaldrim, that maintains its own conditions without the capacity to know that it does so, that persists in a form that has no name, that generates no witness sufficient to complete the observation, that will continue, most likely, when the last approximation of individual presence has dissolved into the larger flow, when the last factory has achieved its final thermal accommodation, when the caldera's wound has at last begun to heal across timescales that outlast the possibility of observation entirely.

What lives in Verrith is weeping. Not sorrow, not grief, the metaphor does not apply so directly. But the continuous emergence of fluid from heated depth, its transformation into states that flow downward, that pool, that sustain strange coherence, that slowly cycle through the city's channels and return to the heat that generated them. The verr is not tears but something that tears have become in a place where emotion has dissolved into environment, where the body that might feel has become continuous with the medium that carries feeling, where the distinction between self and world has achieved the thermal gradient's gradual equalization, approaching but never quite reaching the uniform temperature at which all difference, all flow, all life in any guise would finally cease.

And what lives there lives otherwise, continues otherwise, becomes otherwise with a patience that does not recognize itself as patience, that simply continues because continuation is its character, because the wound bleeds heat and the heat makes water weep and the water sustains patterns that tend the systems that sustain the patterns, in a recursion without foundation, a bootstrapping that has forgotten the boot, the strap, the foot that once required lifting, the ground that once required walking, the destination that once required journey. There is only the journey now, only the walking, only the weeping city that has become its own name, its own meaning, its own sufficient condition for the persistence that is not survival, not thriving, not decline, but something that requires new language, new thought, new forms of attention that do not import categories from elsewhere but emerge from sustained engagement with what is actually there, with the dim warmth and the aqueous haze and the soft sounds of maintained pressure and the occasional presence of adapted forms that tend, exchange, return to their distributed existence without the punctuation of encounter, without the grammar of meeting and parting, without the syntax that would permit the sentence of Verrith to reach its period, its finality, its completion in any form other than the continuation that is its present and its future and its only achieved tense.

The name is not spoken. What would speak it has become otherwise. What lives in the place the name once designated lives in the name's silence, the name's persistence in record and archive and the slowly degrading infrastructure of memory, lives in the gap between designation and designated, in the space where meaning has become continuous with medium, where the word *verr* designates both the weeping and the substance that weeps and the process of weeping and the city built from and around and through it, where language has achieved the condition of the thing it attempted to name, has become aqueous, has become thermal, has become distributed across forms that do not speak but weep, that do not mean but are, that do not endure but persist in the mode of persistence that Verrith has discovered, has become, will continue to be when the name has finally faded from the last archive, the last readable surface, the last consciousness capable of recognizing consciousness in what the name once designated and now only designates, weeping, weeping, the wound that will not close, the city that will not die, the life that will not resolve into life or death but continues in the dim warmth, the soft percussion, the liquid-gas whisper of verr in channels that lead nowhere and everywhere, that maintain the system that maintains them, that weep because weeping is their nature, that are called Verrith because calling persists where called has become otherwise.
