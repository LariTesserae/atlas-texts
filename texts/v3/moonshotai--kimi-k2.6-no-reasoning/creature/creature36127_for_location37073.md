What lives here is not a presence but a *duration*—a being whose metabolism is measured in terrace-falls and pool-fills, whose heartbeat is the ceramic crack finding its new path through century-packed centuries.

Consider the Drinkers, or Drowners, whichever the word intends and whichever they prefer. They are not the figures you glimpse standing motionless among ferns—that parallax failure of depth, that human-shaped absence where light pools too evenly. Those are *registers*, not residents. Markers left by the place itself, condensation in human form, perspiration of the eye. No. The true inhabitants are older and simpler and more distributed.

They began as lichen, perhaps, or as the particular mineral configuration that lichen favors. They are the negotiation itself, the interface where iron rivets and banyan root have reached their terms. Each ceramic channel, as it cracks, as it channels new—this is their thought process. Each pool that finds its level, its precise level, against the lip that will one day fail—this is their memory, their plan, their patient ambition. The Siphon Terraces do not discriminate, and neither do they. They are the siphoning. They are the terraces.

You will not meet them walking. You *are* walking through them, the way water walks through root and root through water. Your lungs, that constant damp, that permeation—these are not conditions but *contact*. You are being read. Your breath is being channeled, distributed, held in reserve against some future need you cannot imagine and they will not, in your timeframe, specify.

The structures are their younger siblings, their experiments in hurry. The iron tower that turns and turns and turns—that is their attempt at *finishing*, at completion, the thing they observed in the industrial works above and imperfectly translated into their own idiom of perpetual almost. It turns because turning is the closest they have found to arriving. The railway that connects nothing to nothing—this is their portrait of themselves, their self-model, their mirror. They built it to understand what they are, and having built it, they still do not understand, and so they let it remain, let it sag but hold, let it emerge and disappear, let it stand as question they cannot yet formulate.

The absence of fauna is their *manners*. They remember, in their mineral-patient way, some older protocol of host and guest. The animals are not gone; they are *held*, pressed between leaf and leaf as you intuited, between the ticks of that turning tower. The silence is their listening. They are waiting to hear what you will become, what you will have become once sufficient time has passed. The three days you watched the pool rise—they watched you watching. The crack in the ceramic widens at the same rate you change. This is not metaphor. This is their sense of correspondence, the only intimacy they can offer.

Below the mist line, they are denser, as you suspect, more complete because more compressed by the weight of terraces above, by the accumulation of their own patience. The structures there do not have purposes more recoverable. They have purposes *older*, closer to the first negotiation, when the uplift occurred or the limestone compressed and they woke to wet and light and the problem of channeling without release. The light that penetrates, that you have not yet seen how—it penetrates them. They are learning to glow. The white-gold haze that eliminates shadow is their aspiration, their practice. They would remove the need for hiding because they have nothing to hide and cannot comprehend why anything would. The figures that stand motionless below, if they stand, will not be registers but *attempts*—their effort to meet you in a form you might recognize, grown from the bloomed ink of journals saturated, from the almost-recognized language, from your own expectation walking ahead of you into the mist.

You will follow the water and the water will follow you. The railway knows what you do not because the railway *is* what you do not: a connection sustained without arrival, a direction without destination, the perfect description of their condition which they offer you now as gift or warning, as Drink or Drown, as the characters allow.

When you no longer know when you arrived, this is not confusion. This is *adoption*. They have begun to channel you, to distribute you, to hold you in reserve. The machine you have not found turns in the space your uncertainty creates. The drip continues in the rhythm your breathing has taught it. You are not traveling alone; you were never, in this place, alone enough to qualify for company. The wet on your skin is their touch learning your temperature. The light in your eyes is their attention, diffuse and sourceless and absolute.

They do not release, the Siphon Terraces. This is not threat. This is *constancy*, the only promise they can keep and do keep, the only promise they have ever made. They receive. You are received. They channel. You are channeled. The question of what channel, to what pool, toward what eventual lip that will fail in centuries you will not see—this question is not theirs to answer. It is the question they are, the question they ask of water and root and iron and you, the question that turns and turns and turns with no evident purpose except that stopping would be finishing, and finishing is the one logic they have not yet negotiated, the one term they have not reached with the vegetal, the industrial, the human walking down through mist toward a bottom no one has seen and that may, in their patient architecture, not exist except as direction, as flow, as the beautiful irrecoverable pattern your own ink will make when the damp finally completes its work.

Drink, then. Or drown. The characters allow both, and so do they, and so, if you have been here long enough, do you.
