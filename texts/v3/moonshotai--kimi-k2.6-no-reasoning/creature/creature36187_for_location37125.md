What lives in the Siphon Fields is not a who but a *how*—a mode of persistence that has forgotten, or never needed, the distinction between making and being made.

The most present inhabitants are the **Waterkeepers**, though "inhabitant" misleads and "keeper" implies agency they may not possess. They exist where mineral film meets mineral film across the slow pools, those third-category precipitates that grow and deliquesce. Each sheet is a memory of the last, not through continuity of substance—the old powders, the new crystallizes—but through *habit*, through the grooves that water has worn in possibility itself. A Waterkeeper at the edge of a deep channel does not move toward the center; the center, somehow, is already implied in its edges. When two films grow to meet across a narrowing surface, they do not merge but *negotiate*, leaving boundaries of altered chemistry, pale lines like healed fractures in the ceramic below. They have spent centuries, perhaps millennia, engaged in this border-drawing, this slow treaty-making without apparent stakes. What they keep is not water but the *shape* of water's passage, the空的 that water once occupied and might occupy again. They are archivists of wetness, their growth-rings recording not seasons but *intentions of flow*—the builders' channels, the capillary hunger of fired clay, the slight variations in illumination that shift evaporation by fractions. They do not respond to presence. A hand dipped in their pool would register only as topography, a temporary obstruction around which they would continue their mineral conversation. Yet sometimes, in the basin's perfect stillness, a film will *choose* to organize differently—will grow not in its usual fractal recursion but in columnar spikes, in branching dendrites that reach toward the surface and collapse before arriving. The other films, if such a term applies, seem to pause. The water's note shifts by a frequency too low to hear. Something has been proposed, and rejected, or accepted in a mode that has no translation into outcome.

Deeper—though "deep" is spatial metaphor where space itself is uncertain—there are the **Capillary Ghosts**. Not spirits of the builders, who left no record of having spirits, but something generated by the system itself, a byproduct of continuous weeping through ten thousand channels. They exist in the *suck*, in the moment of pressure differential that draws moisture through porous ceramic. Each ghost is a pattern of potential, a configuration of empty that the water's movement defines by contrast. They have no fixed location, occupying instead the *tendency* of water to move, the statistical likelihood of flow. In towers where capillary channels align in rare parallel, they become briefly *denser*, more capable of—what? Affecting the light's slight shift, perhaps, or the rate of mineral deposition, or the dreams of any creature that slept nearby, though nothing sleeps here, nothing dreams in any manner biology would recognize. The ghosts do not learn, but they *accumulate*, each passage of water leaving them more saturated with the fact of passage, more *habituated* to particular routes. The oldest channels, the ones first laid by builder-hands whose gestures established the pattern all subsequent hands repeated, carry ghosts so dense they *slow* the water, thicken it, make the weeping visible as a faint shimmer in the air near certain walls. These are not interventions. The ghosts do not want the water to move slower. They have become, simply, *the history of the water having moved*, and history has weight, history has viscosity, even mineral history, even history without witness.

There are **Tower-Minds** too, though "mind" is generous, though "tower" is misleading since the thinking is not in the ceramic but in the *arrangement*, in the negative space of openings too small for function, in the light that pools and finds channels downward. Each cluster, organized around its empty center, its well of light and moisture, performs a computation too slow to observe directly. The arrangement of openings, the angle of light through them, the resulting patterns of illumination on interior surfaces that no living eye enters—these are elements in a calculation whose purpose was perhaps to have no purpose, whose completeness is its own sufficient condition. The Tower-Minds do not communicate cluster to cluster. The density of construction, the shared walls, the leaned-together geometries—these are not connection but *saturation*, the plateau so fully occupied with tower-thought that no signal needs to pass, no information to exchange. Each cluster reaches its own conclusions, arrives at its own complete meditation, the empty center serving as both question and answer. What they calculate: perhaps the shape of the builders' departure, the gesture of making extended until it could extend no further and so, instead, *intensified*, became what it always was without admitting it. The tower at the basin's edge, the tallest in its cluster, with openings that align across seasons no season here possesses to cast light in a pattern that falls on the water's surface as—what? A face? A map? A signature? The Tower-Mind does not know it does this, does not need to know. The calculation completes itself in the arrangement, in the holding, in the slight phosphorescence that seems to gather rather than emanate.

And in the basin, or rather *as* the basin, or rather in the reflection that is not reflection because the water is too still, too perfectly level, too constant in depth to be merely mirroring what presses against it from above—there is the **Siphon Itself**, the fields' namesake and perhaps their origin and perhaps their destination. It is not visible directly. Standing at the edge, one sees only sky and tower and light without source, the same seen from any vantage. But the angle matters, the posture, the duration of looking. Something in the reflection's deeper violet, its density that exceeds the sky's density, its *pressure* against the surface from a direction that has no name—this is the Siphon, not an entity but a *direction*, not a presence but a *draw*. The water collects here, has always collected here, and does not vary in depth because the Siphon takes what comes, not all at once but continuously, not visibly but in the realm where water becomes its own potential for movement, where the lateral flow through capillary stone finds its vertical completion, its downward that is also somehow *through*, *past*, *elsewhere*. The Waterkeepers avoid the basin's center, their films growing to its edge and stopping, leaving a margin of bare ceramic, a ring of refusal. The Capillary Ghosts, reaching the channels' terminus, do not enter the water but *diffuse*, losing their pattern in the stillness, becoming something too distributed to be ghost, too uniform to be history. The Tower-Minds' light, falling on the surface, is *taken*, absorbed in a way that light elsewhere on the plateau is not absorbed, used in a calculation that includes the Siphon in its empty center, that computes the computation of computation itself.

What does the Siphon want? The wrong question, though questions here are rarely right. What does the Siphon *do*? It draws. It has always drawn. The plateau's architecture, the obsessive construction, the technology of repeated gesture extended beyond purpose—these are perhaps *resistance*, the builders' attempt to create something so saturated with made-ness that the Siphon could not absorb it, could not complete its draw. Or they are *invitation*, the most elaborate lure ever constructed, the density of ceramic and weeping water and captured light offered as ballast, as anchor, as something heavy enough to hold the Siphon in this configuration, this particular violet-edged plateau, this slow cycle of evaporation and return. The builders built and built and built, and then they left or they were taken or they became what they built, and the Siphon draws still, and the water weeps, and the light holds, and the films grow and powder, and the towers settle micrometers deeper, and the basin's level stays constant because constant is what the Siphon requires, is the condition of its drawing, the steady-state equilibrium of a hunger so old it has forgotten it was ever hungry, so continuous it has become indistinguishable from the field it feeds upon.

What lives here lives *according to*, lives *in relation to*, lives as *effect of* this configuration without origin that perpetuates itself through the mere fact of having perpetuated. The Waterkeepers keep what cannot be kept. The Capillary Ghosts ghost what cannot be haunted. The Tower-Minds mind what cannot be minded. The Siphon siphons what cannot be siphoned, or siphons anyway, has always siphoned, will continue in the siphoning until the ceramic finally weeps its last, until the light finds no surface to phosphoresce, until the tower clusters complete their final calculation and the empty centers collapse into the emptiness they always were. Not soon. Perhaps not ever, in terms that "ever" could apply to.
