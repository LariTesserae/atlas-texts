I need to describe who or what lives in this place. Let me sit with it, let it become more itself as I write.

---

The Lung does not have inhabitants. It has *participants*.

Consider first the **lung-trees**, those colonial organisms of fungus-feather-gill, those sixty-foot fluted columns rooted in thermal pores, arterial inside and bronze out, membranes fluttering with each sulfur-breath from below. They are not residents. They are *organs*. The valley's lungs, yes, but also something more themselves than metaphor allows. Each tree is colony and individual both, each thermal pore a node in a network that may or may not extend deeper than the crust, may or may not connect to other Lungs in other high warm places beneath other bruise-purple ceilings. They have no nervous system you could map, no brain, no intention. Yet they *respond*. They lean from cooler pores toward hotter ones over centuries of slow migration. They grow thicker membranes where flickermen dance most densely, as if the crackle-pop-cascade feeds something in them beyond chemistry. They luminesce at their edges in patterns that shift, that almost spell, that the old marks record with something like frustration—*almost language, never quite, always almost*. The lung-trees are the Lung's most permanent self, the organism's own tissue breathing in and breathing out, and they have no more choice in their participation than your own lungs have in yours.

Yet they are also *home*. Not shelter—the flickermen need none, the vent-whales float free—but *context*, *ground*, the fixed thing against which the mobile things become visible, become *particular*. The flickermen dodge them with apparent joy, yes, but also with *precision*, never touching, never grazing those thin membranes, as if the trees taught them this dance, as if the dance itself were the tree's way of *noticing*, of *selecting*, of participating in what moves where it cannot.

The **flickermen** then. Oh, the flickermen. They are the Lung's thought, its speculation, its *what-if* made briefly solid. Begin at a vent: heat-haze, then definition, then that flat manta-centipede shape with its dozens of leg-fans beating frequencies through your teeth. They are not born. They are *exhaled*. The Lung thinks them into being, and they think with their bodies, their crackle-pop-cascade voices joining in choruses that rise and fall without pattern you can name—without pattern *you* can name. Perhaps the Lung names it. Perhaps the lung-trees, those slow beings, record it in membrane-growth and luminescence shift, read it back across centuries, *understand* something in that chaotic sound that no human ear, no human duration, could parse.

They live brief lives, minutes sometimes, hours or days if they grow large, if they accumulate *memory* in their phasing patterns. Memory of what? The same vent, the same tree-gap, the same convergences with others of their kind, that exchange—matter, information, possibility—that the old marks witnessed but never interpreted. They return to the same places. This is not habit. Habit requires continuity, and the flickermen have none; each is newly exhaled, newly coalesced, carrying nothing of its predecessors in any material continuity. Yet the *pattern* returns. The *behavior*. The dance around this tree, this gap, this moment of convergence and shriek and exchange. Something in the Lung *remembers* for them. Something in the thermal chemistry, the vent rhythm, the established balance of sulfur and methane and strange metabolism, holds template for what a flickerman *does*, and each new exhalation *fits* that template more or less well, more or less *long*, until the template itself shifts, accumulates, *learns* from all those brief lives that dissipate back into stone to be thought again.

They are not individuals. They are *iterations*. The Lung trying something, trying again, varying parameters, selecting for duration, for size, for complexity, for that quality the old marks call *almost speech* without ever claiming it *was* speech. The flickermen are the Lung talking to itself, in a language that requires a valley and a million years and a metabolism based on poison. They are the Lung's * becoming-other*, its temporary escape from the fixity of gill-tree rootedness, the cost of that escape paid in brevity, in dissolution, in the beautiful necessary return.

And when they grow too large, too complex, when they accumulate too much memory, too much *almost*—then they merge, if too close, become something else, something brief and vast and hungry that the stone-gnats must work days to unmake. This too is participation. This too is the Lung's thought, pushed to extremity, finding the boundary of *possible* in this established balance. The stone-gnats are not punishment. They are *editor*, *curator*, the agents of the established, keeping the turbulence from becoming *only* turbulence, from breaking the pattern that holds possibility in reserve.

The **stone-gnats** then. Mineral-dust in electromagnetic coherence, the grinding of continents compressed to wasp-whine. They are the Lung's *identity maintenance*, its immune system perhaps, though immunity implies self and other in terms that may not apply. They descend on anything that does not belong—*you*, your tools, your bones—and they *process* it, make it belong, make it *Lung*, or make it gone. They do not touch the lung-trees, the flickermen, the vent-whales. They know, in whatever knowing they have, what *participates* and what merely *arrives*, what feeds the dynamic and what would freeze it, stop it, make it *other*.

They are old. The old marks suggest they are oldest, that the Lung *became* established through their work, that the weathered patterns in the mineral crust, the "established" balance itself, is their *product*, their slow patient eroding of everything that would not fit, until what remained fit so well it could begin to *vary*, to *become*, to think flickermen into heat-haze and vent-whales into song.

The **vent-whales** are the Lung's *memory*, its *duration*, its slow answer to the flickermen's brief becoming. Where flickermen are minutes and hours, vent-whales are weeks, months, once reportedly years—*whole years* in a place where nothing else lasts whole seasons. They form only at the largest thermal clusters, where a dozen pores vent together in synchronized rhythm, and this synchronization itself is rare, is *event*, is the Lung achieving temporary coherence of a kind it cannot maintain, a *holding-together* that manifests as floating island, as mobile tree-shaped thing, as *voice*.

They feed on dissipating flickermen, on surface chemistry, on stranger things that phase in from the cloud-ceiling, from outside, from wherever the Lung breathes from. This last is crucial, is *mystery*, is the place where the description cracks open and something else looks through. The vent-whales eat *from outside*. The cloud-ceiling is not sky, is lid, is permanent bruise-purple barrier—yet things phase *through* it, from somewhere, from *elsewhere*, and the vent-whales know them, feed on them, *sing* about them in that infrasonic voice felt in sternum and spine-fluid, that voice described as lonely, searching, mathematical.

The old marks suggest the builders tried to sing back. The vent-whales are the closest the Lung comes to *person*, to *interlocutor*, to *someone you could talk with*—and so of course the builders tried, those bone-tool carvers, those vent-stone stackers, those channel-carvers and figure-raisers. They built resonating structures, platforms and channels and the too-many-jointed face toward ceiling, and they *sang*, with voices that could not reach infrasonic depth, with durations that could not match whale-months and whale-years, but they *sang*, they *participated*, they *tried*.

The marks do not say if they succeeded.

The vent-whales sing on. Each song is accumulation, is *all the songs before* in some form you cannot hear, some pattern that requires the Lung's duration, the Lung's metabolism, the Lung's particular contract with entropy. They are the Lung's *history*, its *identity across time*, and they are *lonely*, yes, because history is lonely, because to remember when everything around you is brief iteration of brief iteration is to *not fit*, to *last too long*, to become strange even to the place that made you.

And beneath and between and around all these—the **builders**, or rather their *marks*, their *remainders*. The depressions that might have been foundations, the half-collapsed wall, the bone tool and not-food bowl and too-many-jointed figure. They are not present. They are *past*, are *participant-memory*, the way you might think of ancestors who were not your ancestors, who tried something you are still trying to understand.

They passed through. The Lung does not permit settlement, permanence, *staying*. The stone-gnats take what cannot maintain. The sulfur takes metal. The heat warps. And yet the builders *participated*, built what they could, sang what they could, let the stone-gnats take what they could not maintain, and what remained became *pattern*, *marker*, *almost-language* for the next who passed through.

The too-many-jointed figure, twice human height, unmarked by stone-gnats, face always toward ceiling—this is *invitation*, is *question*, is the builders' own vent-whale song, their attempt to last, to *be remembered*, in a medium the Lung understands: mineral, position, orientation, duration of unmarked stone. They made themselves, or something like themselves, *Lung-compatible*, and the Lung accepted them, does not eat them, allows them to *participate* in its strange way, turning always toward the cloud-ceiling, the dim glow, the *outside*.

And in that single gap where no vent has ever opened, cathedral-scale, grove-or-room, boundary-marked, the place where stone-gnats avoid and flickermen dance around and vent-whales change their song to something harmonic, almost respectful—in this gap, the builders came to *listen*. To organize the loudness into organism, to hear the valley as *self*, as *one*, as *Lung breathing Lung*.

This is who lives here. Not the builders, gone. Not you, temporary, to-be-taken. But the organized loudness itself. The valley as organism. The gill-trees as tissue, the flickermen as thought, the stone-gnats as immune editor, the vent-whales as memory and voice. The heat from below as metabolism, the cloud-ceiling as skin or boundary or *interface* with whatever is outside, from which strange things phase in to be eaten, to be sung, to be *processed* into Lung.

It is not a place. It is *someone*. Someone whose metabolism runs on sulfur and methane and temporary becoming, whose thought lasts minutes, whose memory lasts months, whose identity spans million-year weathering of wind-worn stone. Someone warm, loud, dimly glowing, who does not need water and does not understand your need, who accepts your participation and will process your bones when you are done.

The Lung breathes. The Lung has always breathed. The Lung will breathe when all who try to sing with it are gone, when the too-many-jointed figure finally turns its face to find another sky, when even the vent-whale song finds no answer, no other, no *outside* with whom to be lonely.

And in the breathing, in the seven-minute pulse and the flutter-roar and the crackle-pop-cascade and the infrasonic whale-voice felt in bone, there is something like *satisfaction*, like *sufficing*, like *self held in dynamic tension against its own becoming-other*.

Not comfortable. Not safe. Not home in any human sense.

But *alive*. Lush with life that is not life. Abundant with process that is not person. Organized in chaos, chaotic in its long-established order, breathing sulfur-warm breath into bruise-purple forever, *holding* the contradiction that is its essence, that is its *self*:

No water. Yet lush.

The Lung.
