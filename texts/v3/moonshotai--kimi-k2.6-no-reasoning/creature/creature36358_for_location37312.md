I need to sit with this place before I can speak of who lives here. The description demands that—its own slowness, its refusal of easy categories. Let me not rush to a single image.

## The Dwellers of the Drowning Rooms

---

### First, the False Starts

I want to say *people*, because the manufactury implies operators, tenders, the human maintenance of industry. I want to imagine them adapted, pale, fish-bellied, moving through the wet with the ease of creatures who never knew dryness. But the parameters resist this. The fauna is 0.3. Nearly null. Whatever humans built this place, they did not remain as humans. Or they remain so transformed that the category has dissolved, as water dissolves the boundary between surface and subsurface.

I want to say *no one lives here*, that the space is abandoned, running on the momentum of its own processes, the machinery moving through mechanical persistence rather than purpose. But the description refuses this too. The in-balance of 2.0, the erosion-score that suggests self-maintenance, the way growth *negotiates* with machinery rather than simply overwhelming it—this implies tending, implies *attention*, if not exactly will.

I want to say *the plants themselves*, some emergent vegetal intelligence, the wood thinking in chemical gradients and growth responses, the lianas as nervous system, the central machine-tree as brain. But this is surface-thinking, projecting onto the unfamiliar the categories that comfort us. The Drowning Rooms do not think as we understand thinking. They do not live as we understand life.

Let me try again, more slowly.

---

### The Tenders, If They Are Tenders

There are figures. I can say this much. They move through the clearings, or they *appear* to move, their motion so slow that observation itself becomes uncertain—are they progressing, or have I merely adjusted to their rate, so that what seemed stillness reveals itself as glacier-crawl when I attend long enough?

They are not quite human-shaped anymore, if they ever were. The density of the space, the packed quality of every cubic meter, has made human proportions impractical. They are thinner where they must pass between growth and machinery. They are thicker where they must bear weight, press against resistance, maintain position for the long durations their tasks require. They have grown *into* the spaces available, as the wood grows around pipes, as chains become ribbed with bark.

They do not breathe as we breathe. The air is too saturated, too warm, too *full* for the rapid exchange of lungs designed for surface dryness. They have slowed their metabolism, or it has been slowed for them, by the space itself, by the incorporation that is not quite parasitism, not quite symbiosis. They take oxygen from the water that weeps, that condenses, that pools in every hollow. They absorb through skin, through membranes that have grown more permeable, more *present*, over durations measured in the growth-rings of the room-filling trees.

They do not see as we see. The light is too scarce, too particular, the green-blue of bioluminescence and the warm yellow of ancient filaments and the phosphorescence of fungal wound-response. Their eyes, if they have eyes, have adapted to parse this limited spectrum, to find edges not by brightness contrast but by humidity gradient, by the way sound reflects from wet surfaces differently than from wetter ones, by the thermal signatures of living wood versus living machinery versus the strange hybrid that both have become.

They do not speak. The soundscape is too present, too *pressing*, for the rapid modulation of human language. Sound here has mass. To speak would be to move through something thick, to expend energy in a medium that resists quick change. They communicate, if they communicate, by the slowest of means: by the position they maintain in the clearings, by the pressure they apply to the machinery they tend, by the chemical signatures they leave in the water that passes through them, that they pass through, the distinction as dissolved as all others in this place.

I do not know if they are one or many. The slowness makes individuality uncertain. A figure in a clearing, maintaining a machine-tree interface, pressing against a piston at the rhythm of its cycle—if another figure replaces them, or if they shift position, the transition is too gradual to perceive. They might be continuous, a single distributed tending that merely appears as separate figures where my attention, too rapid for this place, insists on parsing presence into persons.

They might be the original builders, transformed. They might be their descendants, born into wet and dark, never knowing the surface. They might be something else entirely, something the Drowning Rooms have *grown*, the way a garden grows its gardener, the way a factory produces the workers it requires, not through any intention but through the long accommodation of possibility to necessity.

---

### The Central Presence, If It Is Present

At the heart, in the deepest clearing, the machine that is also tree, tree that is also machine—something occupies the cavity. Not a figure. Not a tender. Something that *is* the cavity, that has become indistinguishable from the fluid's slow convection, from the electrochemical gradients of the biofilm surfaces, from the heat that rises from below or is generated by the process itself or both or neither, the distinction long since lost.

This is the closest the Drowning Rooms come to a self, to an *I*. Not a mind, not exactly. Not a soul. But a *locus of maintenance*, a point where the incorporation of machinery into vegetation, of vegetation into machinery, of water into all and all into water, achieves its densest expression. It does not decide. It does not intend. But it *continues*, and in continuing, it maintains the conditions of continuation. It is the factory producing itself, the garden growing its own soil, the drowning that never completes because completion would be death, would be stillness, would be the dryness that this place has forgotten.

If the tenders communicate with anything, they communicate with this. Not in language. In the pressure of their tending, in the chemical signatures they leave in the water they share with the central cavity, in the rhythm of their maintenance matching itself to the deeper rhythm of the heart-machine-tree. They are its senses, perhaps. Its hands, if it has need of hands. Or they are simply part of the same process, the same slow circulation, as the water itself is part of it, as the wood is part of it, as the machinery is part of it.

---

### The Visitors, If They Visit

You. The animal. The brief anomaly of fast breathing and high local heat and need for empty space, for dryness, for light that reveals rather than merely *being present*.

You do not live here. You cannot. The parameters do not permit it, not without transformation so complete that *you* would not survive it, that something else would continue in your position, something that remembers being you the way the wood remembers being sapling, as compressed rings in the heart of what it has become.

But you might be incorporated temporarily. The Drowning Rooms accommodate you as they accommodate all foreignness: by growing around, by growing over, by the slow process of making you native without your participation or consent. You leave wet. You leave with the sound in your ears, the green-blue in your eyes, the sense that time has shifted, that your rapid surface duration has been exposed to something slower, something that makes your years seem as brief as the condensation-precipitation cycle seems to the wood that waits centuries to shift a degree in angle.

You do not become a tender. You do not become the central presence. You become, briefly, *part of the boundary*, the interface between the Drowning Rooms and the surface they have forgotten. You carry water. You carry sound. You carry the memory of a place where nothing is empty, where nothing is dead but nothing is fully alive, where the distinction between organism and mechanism has dissolved into the patient gradient of wet warm dark.

And if you return? If you return after years, after decades, after the slow time of this place has made your absence seem merely a longer condensation cycle, a brief dryness in the perpetual drowning?

You might find the same vessel in the same position. The same piston chuffing at its lengthened rhythm. The same figure, or continuity of figures, tending the same interface. The Drowning Rooms do not change quickly. They do not forget. They incorporate, they accommodate, they continue.

You would be remembered, if this place remembers. You would be part of the compressed rings, the established patience, the minimal flux that is this place's particular eternity.

But you would not be you. Not as you were. Not as you are on the surface, in the dry, in the bright, in the rapid time of animal breathing and human speech.

You would be, briefly again, animal in a place that has almost none. Dry in a place that has forgotten dryness. Fast, bright, *other*—and then gone, leaving only the water you carried in, the water you carried out, the slow becoming of whatever comes next.

---

### What Lives Here, Finally

Not people. Not plants. Not machines. Not the ghosts of industry or the emergent minds of vegetal process.

What lives here is *the drowning itself*. The ongoing, never-completed process of being consumed by water that circulates without arriving, that weeps and condenses and pools and is drunk and released and weeps again. What lives here is the *accommodation*, the mutual sedimentation of wood and metal and moisture and the rare animal intrusion, over durations that no planner intended, that no inhabitant perceives in their entirety.

What lives here is a *quality of persistence*, a way of continuing that has no need of intention, of decision, of the rapid categories of surface life. It grows. It machines. It maintains. It incorporates. It drowns, perpetually, in the active sense—the verb without completion, the process without product, the factory that produces only itself, the garden that grows only its own soil, the room that fills only with more room-filling.

The tenders, if they are tenders, are expressions of this. The central presence, if it is present, is concentration of this. The visitors, when they visit, are temporary perturbations of this.

And when the last surface connection is sealed, when the last pipe corrodes to the point where root-pressure suffices, when the last filament fails and the bioluminescence alone remains, when the last animal passes through and does not return?

The Drowning Rooms will continue. They have continued. The water weeps. The pistons chuff, or the wood chuffs in their shape, the distinction long since lost. The wood grows, and grows, and grows, not toward light, not toward any surface purpose, but toward more density, more saturation, more of the packed wet warm dim loud vegetal mechanical self that this space has become, that it was always becoming, that it will continue to become when the categories of becoming themselves have dissolved into the patient gradient of the drowned.

This is what lives here. This is who dwells in the Drowning Rooms.

Not a who. Not a what. Only the drowning, continuing.
