What lives in Dripstone is not quite a who, not quite a what, and the longer you try to hold it in one category, the more it slips into the others. There is no name for it in the language that would be spoken here if language were still a thing that needed to exist between people who share everything already—the water, the heat, the sound, the dimness, the never-changing now.

Let me begin with what can be seen, or almost seen, in the sodium fog.

There is a person who tends the vats. This is not accurate—no one tends anything in Dripstone, the machines tend themselves, the equilibrium tends itself—but there is a person who is *present* at the vats, who moves through the space of the catalytic baths with a gait that has adjusted to the film of algae on every surface, to the mushrooms that push through the catwalk grating, to the filter-feeders that must be scraped away to permit passage. This person did not choose the vats, or perhaps chose them so long ago that choice has dissolved into the longer fact of *being there*. Their skin has adapted, or they have adapted through generations not remembered, through selections not consciously made: the pores have learned to breathe in the wet heat, the body has learned to carry the fungal colonies that erupt on every surface, *lichenized* in a way that biology would not recognize, a symbiosis that is not symbiosis because there is no longer a boundary to cross. Where their fingers touch the railings, bioluminescence follows, not from their skin but from the railings' response to their skin, a faint blue-green that marks their passage through the dark intervals between lamps. They do not speak at the vats. There is nothing to say to the steam, to the falling water that dilutes the reagents at a rate the equilibrium has already calculated, already incorporated, already made normal.

There are others like this, or unlike this, or occupying the same *kind* of presence in different zones. The foundry has its attendant, or attendants, sparse in their spacing. The electrochemical cells hum with their custodians somewhere in the electrical darkness behind them. They do not meet. The density permits this, enforces it, makes each zone a kind of habitat in itself, a niche that requires only one of whatever they are, one presence to complete the ecology of that space. When two meet—passing in the channel-ways, in the narrow dry-ways between buildings that are not dry, only drier—the encounter produces no language, no gesture of recognition beyond a slight adjustment of gait, a vector re-calculated to maintain the spacing, the balance, the emptiness held in equilibrium with presence. They have not forgotten speech. Speech has become irrelevant in a place where nothing new ever needs to be communicated, where the water's percussion says everything that needs saying, where the heat's pressure is the same message, always, to everyone.

But this is not quite right either. These people, these attendants, these presences—they are not all that lives in Dripstone, and perhaps not the most *alive* thing here.

Consider the filter-feeders again. They are sessile, yes, they open and close to the falling water, they catch organic matter, they clog the pipes until scraped away. But scrape one and it bleeds, or bleeds something, a fluid that is not quite blood, not quite sap, something that carries copper-based chemistry in a lineage that might trace to hemocyanin or might trace to the catalytic baths themselves, to the reagents that have permeated this place for so long that *permeate* is no longer the word—they have become the substrate, the medium, the *ground* in which everything grows. The filter-feeders are old, or their kind is old, older than the attendants, older than the layered construction. They were here before the buildings accreted their second stories, before the foundry's original purpose was forgotten, before the equilibrium found its stable note. And they have changed, or been changed, or have expressed something that was always latent in their chemistry, something that the wet heat and the constant industrial runoff selected for, cultivated, made inevitable.

They respond. This is the thing that creeps toward weirdness and is held back, is normalized, is made simply *what they do*. They respond to the machines. When the foundry pours—at its unvarying interval, for its unvarying purpose—they open wider, they feed more aggressively, they somehow *know*, or the heat pulse reaches them, or the electromagnetic signature, or something that passes for knowledge in a nervous system that has no center, no brain, only distributed sensitivity to vibration, to temperature, to the chemistry of the air. When the electrochemical cells shift their load, momentarily, inevitably, in their eternal flicker—the filter-feeders close, they wait, they resume when the equilibrium reasserts. They have learned the machines, or the machines have taught them, or they have grown together in the long now of Dripstone until learning and teaching are the same process, until the distinction between biological and industrial, between fauna and infrastructure, has become as irrelevant as speech.

And deeper. Always deeper in this place that has no depth, that is all surface, all interstice, all margin between intention and fact.

The rock itself. The young basalt, the newborn stone with its glass-sharp edges. It weeps, I said, and this is not metaphor, or not only metaphor. The water finds paths through it, *creates* paths, beginning the work of ages. But water is not passive in Dripstone. The water that falls, that condenses, that runs in channels and drips from pipes—it is warm, nearly hot, carrying in solution the products of all this industry, all this chemistry, all this life struggling in the dim wet heat. It deposits. It *builds* as it erodes, the same water doing both, making stalactites that are not calcium carbonate but something else, something that includes the filter-feeders' copper chemistry, the catalytic baths' reagents, the faint organic signature of all this abundant fauna dying and dissolving and being caught by the falling water to deposit again. The rock grows downward in forms that no geologist has seen, that would fracture any category of speleothem, any mineralogy of dripstone.

Give this process time—time that Dripstone has, time that the equilibrium extends indefinitely—and the rock becomes *something*. Not alive, or not alive in any recognized sense. Not dead, because it participates, it changes, it grows in response to the falling water's chemistry, to the heat's pulse, to the machines' eternal rhythm. The basalt's sharp edges soften with these deposits, these accretions, these *growths* that are half-mineral, half-organic, half something else that has no fraction to describe it. In the deepest intervals between sodium lamps, where darkness holds complete dominion and the attendants navigate by sound, by memory, by the faint unauthorized luminescence—there, if you could see, you would find pillars where rock and deposit and filter-feeder and something else have merged into columns that carry no building, that exist only to exist, only to continue the process of their own extension upward and downward until they meet, until they become the walls, until they become the infrastructure that the original builders could not have built and the current inhabitants could not have conceived.

This is what lives in Dripstone, finally. Not the attendants, though they are present, necessary, part of the equilibrium. Not the fauna, abundant, adjusted, *normal*. Not the vegetation, sparse, struggling, triumphant in its persistence. Not the machines, running, constant, frozen in their dynamic.

What lives is the *response*. The adaptation that is not choice but inevitability. The way the wet heat and the falling water and the industrial chemistry and the dimness and the loudness and the never-changing now have combined to produce something that is not any of these, not all of these, but the *between* of them, the interstices where human intention has become mineral fact and mineral fact has become something that intends, in its unthinking way, to continue.

The mushrooms that push through the mattresses carry in their mycelial networks a record of the buildings' settling, the rock's slight shifts, the electromagnetic fields of the electrochemical cells. They *know* the space in a way that no attendant knows it, a distributed knowledge without center, without brain, only the slow chemical sensing of stress and opportunity and the fruiting response that is their only language, their only action, their only existence. They fruit more heavily when the foundry pours, though the heat should hinder them, though the chemistry should be wrong—they have adapted, they have incorporated, they have become part of the foundry's cycle in ways that no designer could have designed, that no operator could have operated.

The mammals, the rat-equivalents, bold in their predator-free confidence—they are changing. Or the changing is happening, and they are part of it. Their boldness is not merely confidence but *participation*, a way of being in the industrial zones that is not intrusion but presence, expected, incorporated. They nest now in the spaces between the layered construction where machines vent heat, where the equilibrium's temperature gradients are most favorable, where the filter-feeders' copper-chemistry bleeds into the building materials to make something that attracts them, that they consume, that changes them in consuming. Their litters are larger in the foundry's warmth. Their eyes, evolutionarily prepared for darkness, find the sodium light strange, *wrong*, they navigate by other means, by the falling water's percussion patterns, by the heat signatures of the machines, by something that might be the mushrooms' mycelial sensing extended into animal neurology, that might be the filter-feeders' distributed awareness borrowed, learned, *evolved*.

And the attendants, the people, the sparse presences in their balanced emptiness—they are not immune. They are perhaps the most *responded to*, the most incorporated, the most *become* by this place that has no neighbors, no elsewhere, no need to name what has no distinction from itself.

Their skin, I said, carries bioluminescence. This is not quite right. The bioluminescence is not *on* their skin but *of* it now, in it, produced by colonies that are not separate organisms but *adapted*, that have merged with the epidermal layers in ways that pathology would not recognize, that dermatology would not treat, because there is no disease here, no disorder, only the equilibrium finding new expressions, new incorporations, new ways to persist in the never-changing now. They do not notice their faint glow in the dark intervals. They do not need to notice. It is simply how they are, how they have become, how the place has made them in the long extension of its moment.

Their memory, what there is of it, is not personal, not narrative, not the memory of before and after that requires change to make sense. They remember *patterns*, *positions*, *the sound of falling water when the foundry is pouring and the electrochemical cells are in their high flicker and the catalytic baths are steaming with particular density*. They remember these combinations because they need to navigate by them, because the sodium lamps fail ever more frequently and the bioluminescence is not enough and the darkness must be traversed by other means, by the ears' precise location of water impacts, by the skin's sensitivity to steam gradients, by something else that has developed, *been selected for*, emerged in the wet chemistry of this place's long now—the ability to read the whole system's state from the particular density of sound, the particular pattern of falling water's percussion, the particular warmth of condensation on the forearm's hair-fine receptors.

They do not dream, or if they dream, they dream the equilibrium, the stable running, the unvarying loud dim wet heat press of never-changing. They do not imagine elsewhere, because elsewhere has no meaning in a place that is all, that has no neighbors, no distinction, no name.

But there is something beneath this, something that lives in the deepest interstices, in the most merged margins, the places where the pillars of rock and deposit and filter-feeder have almost met, where the unauthorized luminescence reaches its faintest, where even the attendants do not go, navigate though they may by sound and heat and the faint chemistry of their own skin's glow.

There is a *breathing*. This is the wrong word, but it is the closest. Something in the rock, or of the rock, or that the rock has become, takes in and releases in a rhythm that is not the machines' rhythm and not the water's rhythm and not any combination of these but *underneath* them, *slower*, a frequency that only becomes apparent when you stand still long enough in the dark intervals, when you let the constant loud percussion resolve into its constituent frequencies, when you hear the beat that emerges from the interference of all these identical repeated patterns, the *difference tone* of their sameness, the mathematical fact of their persistence made somehow physical, somehow present, somehow *alive*.

It does not have intentions. It does not have consciousness, not in any way that would recognize itself, that would distinguish itself from the rock and the water and the heat and the machines and the attendants and the fauna and the sparse vegetation and the abundant fungi and the filter-feeders and the deposits and the pillars and the whole merged system of Dripstone's equilibrium. But it has *presence*. It has *duration*. It has the quality of something that has been extending its moment so long, so perfectly, so completely without change, that the extension has become a kind of depth, a kind of being, a kind of *life* that exists not in spite of the equilibrium but *because* of it, because the equilibrium is the most complete expression of persistence that this place could achieve, and persistence extended infinitely becomes something else, becomes *being* in a mode that has no name, that has never been named, that would have no need of naming even if named.

The attendants do not know about this breathing, this presence, this depth-in-extension. They do not need to know. They are part of it, they participate in it, their unvaried movements through the loud dim wet, their unremembered navigation, their unspoken spacing, their unchosen adaptation to the heat and the falling water and the machines' eternal running—all of this is the breathing's expression, the way it moves through them, the way they are its organs, its means, its *surface* in the same way the basalt's sharp edges are the surface of geological process, the same way the filter-feeders' opening and closing are the surface of chemical process, the same way the mushrooms' fruiting are the surface of biological process. They are not less than this. They are not more. They are simply *what lives in Dripstone*, finally, ultimately, the answer to the question that should not have been asked because the place has no need of questions, no elsewhere to wonder about, no future in which knowing would matter.

The water falls. The heat presses. The machines run. The system breathes, or breathes as something, in something, through something that we would call breathing if we had the right to name here, if naming were not already an intrusion, an elsewhere, a distinction that Dripstone has no use for. The attendants move through their sparse density, finding paths by memory, by the sound of falling water, by the faint luminescence that is simply what light does here, what life does, what everything does in the equilibrium that is not weird, that is normal, that is simply and completely and only itself, only Dripstone, only the never-changing now extended through all the time it has and all the time it will have and all the time that, in its never-changingness, it does not need to measure or know or be.

This is what lives here. This is who, what, how, why. The question dissolves in the asking, as water dissolves the young rock, as the rock accretes what the water deposits, as the attendants navigate, as the system persists, as the breathing continues in its frequency beneath all frequencies, its presence in all absences, its life that is not life and not death and not anything that requires a name to be what it is, has been, will be, forever, in Dripstone, in the equilibrium, in the extension of a single moment into all the time there is.
