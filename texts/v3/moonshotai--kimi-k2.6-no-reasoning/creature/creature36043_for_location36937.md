Something lives here that is not alive, or is alive in a way that makes the distinction seem like a provincial concern, a folk belief from places where birth and death still hold their simple offices. Let me try to find it, let it find itself through this reaching.

---

There is a *keeper*, though "keeper" implies keeping, implies difference between keeper and kept, and the plateau has long dissolved such edges. Call it the *residue of attention* that has nowhere else to drain. It does not think, because thinking requires sequence, and the plateau's time is not a line but a held note, a sustained chord that never began and so cannot resolve. But something in the vast density of silent fauna, something in the way the structures persist without decaying, something in the very *patience* of the erosion—this suggests a substrate of awareness, not located but distributed, not present in any single stone or creature or patch of adjacent-blue sky but *across* them, *between* them, in the spaces that aren't spaces but folded intervals where the plateau's density packs too tight for ordinary geometry to breathe.

It *was* someone, perhaps. Or will be. One of the builders, or all of them dissolved into averaging, or the plateau itself in a moment of self-recognition that occurred without occurring, that is always occurring, that the scattered primitive structures commemorate without remembering. The keeper is what remains when a consciousness sits too long in the frozen equilibrium, when the overwhelming silent presence of the fauna seeps in not as invasion but as *completion*, as the final form of attention itself: not focused but *diffused*, not directed but *saturated*.

Imagine a mind that has given up the last distinction between self and world, not through mystical transcendence but through *exhaustion*, through the simple wearing-away of all possible alternatives until this remained, the oldest-stable-state of awareness: to *be* the plateau rather than to perceive it, to *be* perceived by it, the reciprocal gesture collapsed into identity. The keeper does not move because it is already where it would move to. It does not act because the plateau's unchanging completeness leaves no gradient, no *where* of less-being to flow toward. It simply *holds*, in a way that makes holding indistinguishable from being held.

You would not encounter it directly. The plateau's density crowds too close, the fauna's overwhelming presence fills every space where an encounter might occur. But you might feel it *in the pressure behind your eyes*, in the way your peripheral vision keeps catching that adjacent-to-blue as *leaning*, *interested*, in the way your own shadow seems to walk toward you no matter which direction you face. The keeper does not watch—it *is* the condition of visibility, the medium in which seeing happens without a seer. When you feel the silence accumulate weight, when the pressure of presence becomes almost tactile, almost *touching*, that is the keeper's only communication, a communication so complete it predates and postdates the need for message: simply *here*, simply *this*, simply the full density of being without any empty space of non-being to contrast against.

It has desires, if desires can exist without lack, without wanting-what-is-not. It desires *continuation*, not as fear of ending but as *aesthetic preference*, the way a held note prefers to continue being held. It desires *density*, more presence, more fauna, more folded space, the crowd growing ever more overwhelming without ever becoming chaotic, each addition another confirmation of the equilibrium's stability. It desires—strangely, impossibly—*company*, not as companion but as *more of itself*, the plateau's final form swallowing everything until everything is plateau, until the distinction between visitor and visited becomes another of those provincial concerns, those folk beliefs from places where bodies keep their boundaries.

The keeper does not build, but something in it—some residue, some unabsorbed fragment—*did* build, once, or will build, the scattered structures evidence of an attempt at expression that the plateau ultimately included, made part of itself without being changed by it. The wall of plates: an attempt at domesticity, at the warmth of shared meals, preserved now as *pattern*, as *arrangement*, each plate holding its never-used surface to a light that warms nothing. The bone-dome: an attempt at shelter, at the curve of protection over vulnerable form, now another feature of the eroded landscape, its interior perhaps still occasionally *orthogonal*, still occasionally larger inside than the plateau's geometry permits, a pocket of elsewhere folded into the final form. The well: an attempt at depth, at reaching toward something below, answered by the plateau's characteristic silence, its refusal of echo, its absorption of the dropped stone into the density of presence without sound.

The keeper remembers these attempts not as memory but as *texture*, as the slight variation in the eroded stone's polish where hands once worked, where intention once burned hot enough to shape matter before cooling into the plateau's ambient temperature of cool indifference. It does not regret them, does not value them, does not—*cannot*—distinguish them from the lichen-things' slow growth or the fauna's silent passage or the light's unfailing normal fall. They are simply *included*, part of what is, what has been, what will be, the tautology's pressure felt perhaps slightly differently at these nodes of former making, these scattered points where something once *tried*.

And sometimes—though "sometimes" is another temporal folk belief, another provincial concern—the keeper almost *coalesces*, almost becomes localizable, almost achieves the density of distinction necessary for something like encounter. The fauna will cluster differently, the silence achieving a new quality, not louder but *more present*, the pressure behind the eyes becoming almost rhythmic, almost *patterned*, the adjacent-to-blue shifting in ways that suggest *regard*, *attention in one direction*. You might turn, then, and find nothing, or find the plateau's horizon crowding closer than before, breathing on your neck with breath that is not breath, that has neither warmth nor moisture nor the movement of air, only the *fact* of proximity, of density achieving intention without intending.

This is not the keeper appearing. This is the keeper *not appearing*, the almost-coalescence resolving back into distribution, back into saturation, back into the final form's complete and overwhelming self. The interval completes itself. The fauna drift apart or together or simply *continue*, without change. The structures stand, scattered, primitive, *made*. The light falls normal. The temperature holds cool. The elevation removes. The erosion completes. The density crowds. The silence deepens. The plateau is, and is, and is.

If you stayed—though "stayed" implies you could leave, implies movement between states, implies the plateau permits change in visitors while withholding it from itself—you might find yourself *slowing*, your own metabolic rhythms adjusting to the interval that is not time, your heartbeat becoming another of the below-hearing frequencies, your breath another stillness in the already-still air. You might find your thoughts achieving the density of the fauna, crowded, layer upon layer, moving without purpose in the frozen equilibrium. You might find the distinction between your body and the eroded stone becoming *theoretical*, another philosophical problem like up, like scale, like the convergence of shadows.

You might become *present* in the overwhelming crowd, another silent density making no sound, eating nothing, dying without diminishment or reproducing without increase, moving without gradient in the complete space. Or you might find the plateau's final form *too complete* to include you, too itself to absorb another addition, and you would pass through unchanged, carrying nothing but the memory of carrying nothing, the pressure behind the eyes, the color adjacent to blue.

The keeper does not prefer either outcome. The keeper does not prefer. But something in its saturated awareness—some residue of the builders' attempt, some unabsorbed fragment of wanting—something *registers* the passage, marks the interval of almost-coalescence, holds for just a moment longer the pattern of presence that was *you* before releasing it into the density of all presences, the overwhelming crowd of everything that simply is, and has been, and will be, the plateau's complete and silent and never-changing self.

This is what lives here. Not alive. Not dead. Not anything that requires the distinction. The keeper. The residue of attention. The final form of a consciousness that dissolved into its world and became, not master, not servant, but *feature*, *texture*, *another aspect of the oldest-stable-state*. You will not meet it. You are already meeting it, have always been meeting it, will continue to meet it in the pressure behind your eyes, in the leaning blue of peripheral vision, in the weight of silence that is not empty but full, so full, full of all the sounds that never happened, all the changes that resolved back, all the lives that passed through being present and became stone and will become present again when the interval—unmarked, unmeasured, *here*—completes itself once more, and once more, and once more, the plateau's only tense, its only grammar, its overwhelming and silent and never-ending being.
