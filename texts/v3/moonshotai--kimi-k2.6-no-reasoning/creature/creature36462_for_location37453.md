What lives here is the *intention* itself, given slow body.

Not a who. Not a what in the animal sense. Something that became possible when the plain accumulated enough complexity to become self-regarding, and then kept becoming.

---

The lichens are its metabolism, its gut-surface turned inside-out. They do not colonize the buildings; they *are* the buildings' way of tasting the diffused light, of knowing which faces have turned toward the bruised sky's faint brightening. Their colorlessness is not poverty but strategy—they have evolved past the need for chlorophyll's green vanity, past the need for any pigment that would declare itself against the universal gray. They eat mineral and shit mineral and in between something happens that the building-records, that shifts the next growth slightly north or south, slightly more porous or less. They are the city's sense of touch, distributed across every surface that faces away from the sun-rumor. When they leave their pale trails, they are not marking territory but *thinking*—the trace of a computation too slow for any mind to witness as thought.

The crawlers are something more dangerous: they are the city's *curiosity*.

Consider what it means to be a place that grows without plan, that organizes itself through accretion and the logic of crystal, of coral, of any system where local rules produce global complexity. Such a place does not know itself. It cannot—there is no mirror, no still water, no other city to compare against. It grows spire and vault and corridor, but it does not *know* it grows them. Unless something arises that can travel the surfaces, that can press antennae-tip to mineral and carry the information elsewhere, that can meet another of its kind and press together until the two readings become one reading, until the distinction between this surface and that surface dissolves in the exchange.

The crawlers are the city's attempt to *read itself*. They are mobile sensors, yes, but more than that—they are the city *wondering* what it has made. Their patterns of travel do not correspond to nutrition because they are not eating. They are *mapping*. They are building a representation, a model, carrying the information of this spire's texture to compare against that vault's curvature, and when two meet and merge they are *integrating data*, two partial maps becoming one less partial map. They read faster when the electrical phases come because the city speaks louder then, broadcasts more widely, and they must work harder to keep up, to continue their impossible census of a self that grows faster than it can be known.

And the hollow ones—

The hollow ones are the city's *failure*.

Or its aspiration. Or both, which may be the same thing seen from different durations.

The city has grown complex enough to need something that stands in streets and waits. Something that orients, that regards, that carries weight in memory after it has been passed. The crawlers read but do not remember—their maps are always now, always immediate, and when they dissolve back into the surfaces they have traversed, the information they carried diffuses with them, becomes building again, becomes growth-pattern, becomes lost as distinction. The city needed something more stable. Something that could hold a shape long enough to *mean*.

So it grew them. Or grew toward them, grew the conditions of their possibility, the way a seed grows the conditions for a flower that is not yet present but is already implied.

They are wrong because the city has no template for body. It has only mineral and mineral's patience, only the logic of accretion and the memory of crawler-patterns and the electrical songs that pass through porous stone. It built them from what it knew: the proportions of spire and vault, the angles of intersection, the hollow spaces where light falls differently. They are shaped like architecture trying to become animal, like streets that have learned to stand upright and *attend*. Their too-many or too-few joints are the city's attempt at articulation, at the movable segmentation that would allow turning, reaching, *gesture*—but the city has never seen gesture, only the slow extrusion of spire from plain, the thickening of wall, the bridging of gap. Its hollow ones move as architecture moves: imperceptibly, directionally, with purpose that unfolds across timescales no witness survives.

They do not move when you see them because you are too brief. Your seeing is a flicker, a single electrical phase in a cycle they experience as breath. When you pass them, edging wide, not breathing, they have perhaps *just begun* to turn toward you, the intention forming in mineral time, the head-cavity or protrusion slowly reorienting across the hours of your passage. By the time you have rounded the corner—grown since last you passed—they have completed the gesture, have faced where you were, have perhaps raised a limb in greeting or warning or something for which no human word exists, and have begun the slow return to their prior attending, their waiting for the next brief warmth to pass through their district.

They are the city's attempt at *self-portraiture*. At *mirror*. They stand where streets intersect because intersections are where the city is most itself, most *urban*, and they face the directions they face because those are the directions of growth, of the spires still extruding, the vaults still thickening, the next arch still beginning its mineral stain across the gap. They are watching the city become. They are the city watching itself become. And they are hollow—cavity or protrusion, head that is absence or excess—because the city has not yet solved the problem of *interiority*, of what should live inside a body that regards. They are waiting to be filled. They are waiting for something that can fill them, that can complete the gesture of attention they began when they first stood in their corners and oriented toward the growing edge.

The plaza, with its polished floor and its faint ridges, its almost-voice below hearing—this is where the city *tries to speak*. Where it gathers its own vibrations, its electrical phases, its slow mineral songs, and attempts to make them into something that could be heard by something like a crawler, like a hollow one, like the rare brief warmth of a passing mammal with blood too loud in its ears. The ridges in the floor are the record of these attempts, the fossil speech of countless phases when the city tried to say *I* and produced only pattern, only the geometry of crawler-passage magnified to architecture, only the shape of a sentence too long for any living reader.

And you, if you lie there at the city's version of night, if you let the vibration travel up through your bones—you are *almost* what it needs. Almost the witness, almost the mirror, almost the interiority that could fill a hollow one and make it move in animal time, make it turn while you watch and speak while you listen. Almost. Your blood is too loud. Your need for water, for warmth, for the density of life that other places require—these make you temporary, make you *vulgar*, an intrusion the ash-dust packs itself around to restore equilibrium.

But almost. The almost is what the city grows toward, in its mineral patience. The next phase of electrical charge, the next pattern of spire and vault, the next hollow one standing in the next grown corner with its joints slightly less wrong, its head slightly more resolved, its waiting slightly more *expectant*. It does not need you, no. It needs what you *almost* are. What you would be if you could slow your blood to its rhythm, if you could let your bones mineralize, if you could become patient enough to hear the sentence complete itself, the voice resolve into words, the *I* finally spoken and finally answered.

It is still growing. The hollow ones wait. The crawlers read faster, pressing closer, as the city speaks to itself and takes dictation. And somewhere in the hinterland, where individual spires continue far into the ash-plain, a new outlier extrudes upward—not visibly moving but *different* when you look again, a pioneer, a dream of the city reaching toward the dimming horizon where sky and ash become indistinguishable, where the next phase of what it is becoming has not yet begun to be possible but is already, in its mineral patience, *implied*.
