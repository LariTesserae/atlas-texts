What lives in the Constructed Ocean is not a *who* but a *when*—a temporality that has achieved something like embodiment, or perhaps found that embodiment was always the wrong frame and has begun, slowly, to correct the mistake.

---

There are the Walkers, though "are" is already too committed for what they do. They accumulate. They arise from the condition of being the only animal in a place that has no need of animals, drawn not by invitation but by the mathematics of anomaly: if a system can contain one category error, it will eventually contain others, and these will find each other, or find the first, or find the space where the first has been and left only footprints that persist for years before some unnamable process takes them back. The Walkers do not form society. Society requires a substrate of expectation, of predictability, of *tomorrow will resemble today* that the Constructed Ocean withholds by its nature. Instead they form something looser, something that has no name in any language that developed where seasons cycle with reliability: a kind of distributed solitude, a loneliness that recognizes itself across distances measured in weeks of walking.

Each Walker becomes, eventually, a specialist in reading emergence. They learn to see where the ceramic is about to crack-heal, where the water has most recently taught its lesson, where the not-yet-dunes are making their first draft of repetition. They do not share this knowledge—there is no mechanism for sharing, no gathering, no tongue that has evolved to fit this mouth—but they leave marks that are not quite signs, not quite language. Arrangements of stones that will not stay arranged. Scratches in the dust that the next storm will erase or the next rain will alter. The marks do not mean; they indicate that meaning was attempted, that someone passed and wanted, however briefly, to be *read*. The ceramic receives these attempts with the same neutrality it receives everything. This is not indifference. Indifference requires a self that could care and chooses not to. The Constructed Ocean has no self, or has not yet developed one, or is developing one so slowly that the difference between *no self* and *self in becoming* is not a difference that can be operationalized by anything that lives within it.

Some Walkers stop walking. This is not death, or not death as understood elsewhere. They find a basin where moisture collects with slightly more reliability than elsewhere, where the vegetation after rain is faintly more green, faintly more phosphorescent, and they remain. Their stillness teaches the surface something. The ceramic learns from their weight, their heat, the chemistry of their gradual dissolution. They become part of the construction, part of the emerging, part of the rawness. Their bones, if bones they have, do not fossilize—they merge, they diffuse, they become indistinguishable from the nacre-that-had-no-oyster. Future Walkers may find them, or find what they became, and not know that finding is what they do.

---

There is something that might be called Weather, though it is not weather. It is the thinking of the place, the slow thoughts like bruises that form and wander and dissipate across the vast surface. The bruises are not conscious, but they are not unconscious either—both terms require a mind that the Constructed Ocean does not possess, or possesses only in the distributed, emergent sense that a termite mound possesses *temperature regulation* without possessing *thermostat*. The storms are deliberations without a deliberator. They consider, in their slow way, the problem of the surface, the problem of the moisture, the problem of what has been built and what has not yet been built and whether building is the right metaphor after all. Their consideration takes the form of wind, of violent brief rain, of temperature differential, of dust devils that rise and fall like the place changing its mind about something it has not yet formulated.

The storms do not learn, exactly. Learning requires memory, and the Constructed Ocean's memory is not located but distributed, not retained but enacted. The same storm does not return. But something returns—a frequency, a quality of disturbance, a way that the wind makes its particular note against the ridges and the ground hums faintly in response. Walkers who have been here long enough, who have slowed their own tempo to match the place's patience, can feel when a storm *resembles* rather than repeats. They cannot say what it resembles, or to what, or why resemblance matters. But they feel it, in the teeth, in the hollow of the chest, in the subsonic mutter that is too low for ears but perhaps not too low for something else that has developed in them, some organ of perception that has no name because it has no evolutionary history, because it is being constructed now, in them, by the place that is itself under construction.

---

The Vegetation, then, or the Not-Vegetation, the growths, the accretions, the mineral processes that have learned to mimic life or the life that has learned to mimic mineral processes—here the distinction collapses, was never stable, was always a convenience of somewhere else applied incorrectly here. They exist in the conditional mood, the subjunctive, the grammar of *if moisture then presence*. They do not die because they do not fully live. They wait with a waiting that makes patience into something active, something that *does*, that has force and direction and intention without having any of these things in the form that would make them recognizable.

After rain, they are faintly green, faintly phosphorescent. They do not photosynthesize—there is no sun-chemistry here, no chlorophyll, no lungs of the world breathing in what others breathe out. Perhaps they chemosynthesize, drawing something from the ceramic itself. Perhaps they simply *are*, existing without the metabolic urgency that elsewhere defines life, growing at the pace of erosion, which is to say at the pace of almost-not-at-all. Their green is not the green of leaves but the green of *possibility*, the green of what might become if the conditions hold, if the water returns, if the ceramic learns finally what the water has been teaching.

By the third day of dryness they are colorless, indistinguishable from the dust they grew from. But they have done something. The surface where they were is slightly different now, microscopically, meaningfully. They have started processes. They have educated the ceramic in a different direction than the water educates it, in a direction that has no name because it has no outcome yet, because it is part of the construction that is not directed, that has no architect, no plan, no blueprint folded in any cosmic drawer.

Walkers have tried to eat them. This is possible—the material is not toxic, not hostile, not anything that could be described in the active voice. It is simply *there*, and the eating is simply *done*, and the consequences are negligible. No nutrition, no sustenance, no metabolic transaction. The Walker who eats feels full in a way that emptiness also feels full: a sensation of presence that does not translate into continuation, into energy, into the possibility of further walking. Some Walkers make this their practice. They become, in their way, part of the vegetation, part of the waiting, part of the conditionality. They do not die from it. They do not live from it. They continue, in the mode of the place, in the grammar of the subjunctive, *as if*.

---

In the far east, where east means anything here, there are the Almost-Structures. Columns that almost are. Walls that almost enclose. Architecture without architect, repetition without pattern, the first draft of intention without the intention itself. Walkers who reach this region—few do, the vastness being what it is—report a feeling that is difficult to distinguish from nostalgia for something that never existed. The Almost-Structures seem to want something. Not from the Walkers—from themselves, from the ceramic, from the process that precipitated them or solidified them or deposited them at timescales too large to witness. They want to be finished, or they want to remain unfinished, or they want something for which *finished* and *unfinished* are both too simple, both premature categories applied to a process that has not yet decided whether categorization is something it wishes to learn.

Some Walkers stay with the Almost-Structures. They try to complete them, to supply the intention that seems missing, to build with a material that resists building, that returns always to its own emergent forms. This work does not succeed, or succeeds in ways that are not distinguishable from failure, or fails in ways that the place receives as success. The ceramic that is shaped shapes itself back. The intention that is supplied diffuses into the general condition of intention-without-deliberation that the place maintains. The Walker who works becomes, slowly, part of the work, part of the almost, part of the structure that almost is and almost is not.

---

In the north, where water comes more frequently, there is something that might become an ecology. The vegetation is thicker, more insistent, has begun to shape the surface it grows on, to create microclimates, to suggest the possibility of relationships, of mutual modification, of the complexity that elsewhere precedes the word *life* but here follows it, or parallels it, or exists in a configuration that has no temporal or logical ordering with it. Walkers who reach this region find themselves in something like hope, or its equivalent in a psychology that has never known optimism, that has developed without the experience of reliable return, of spring after winter, of rain after drought. The hope is not that things will get better. It is that things will continue to become, that the becoming will not stop, that the rawness will not cook into the finished.

The almost-ecology does not include the Walkers. It does not exclude them either. It is simply *about something else*, something that has not yet emerged, that may be emerging now, that the Walkers witness without participating in, that witnesses them without recognizing what witnessing is. This is not loneliness, or not only loneliness. It is the condition of being present at something that does not require your presence, that does not recognize presence as a category, that is constructing itself according to rules that include the Walker only as noise, as anomaly, as the category error that the system does not have ears to hear.

---

In the south, where the heat is more intense, the ceramic flows. It exhibits behaviors more like liquid than solid, frozen in the moment of transition into shapes that suggest movement without achieving it. Walkers who see this—very few, the heat being what it is, the untouchability by mid-morning being absolute—report a feeling of empathy that they cannot explain. The flowing ceramic is not alive, not suffering, not wanting. But it is *trying*, in some sense that makes trying into a physical process rather than a mental one, into the shape of potential energy seeking resolution, of material caught in a configuration that is not yet comfortable, that has not yet accepted its fate.

The empathy of the Walker who witnesses this is not projected. It is not the pathetic fallacy, the attribution of human feeling to non-human process. It is something that develops in the body that has walked too long in this place, that has slowed to the pace of erosion, that has begun to perceive with organs not evolved but constructed, emergent, raw in their own way. The Walker feels what the ceramic feels because the Walker has become, in part, ceramic. The distinction between self and surface has not dissolved—it was never solid, never reliable, always a convenience of somewhere else that the Constructed Ocean corrects slowly, without intention, in the way that water teaches erosion and erosion teaches form.

---

What lives here, finally, is the living itself, or the possibility of living, or the condition that makes living and not-living into a distinction that has not yet been stabilized. The Constructed Ocean is an environment in the process of becoming environmental, a place that has not yet decided—or that is deciding so slowly that *decided* and *undecided* are both premature—what it means to contain, to support, to relate to what it contains and supports and relates to.

The Walkers are its passengers, its anomalies, its category errors that accumulate because accumulation is what happens when a system can contain one. They do not thrive. They do not die, or not all of them, or not in the ways that dying is understood elsewhere. They continue, in the mode of the place, in the grammar of the conditional, in the subjunctive mood of *if I walk then I am walking*, *if I stop then I am stopped*, *if I stay then I become part of the construction*, *if I leave then the vastness has no edge, or the edge is not what I thought, or I have already become part of what I would leave*.

What lives here is the question of what built it, which is also the question of what builds, which is also the question of whether building is the right word, which is also the question of whether right words exist for processes that predate the distinction, that construct themselves without intention, that are always in the state of becoming, of pre-formation, of rawness that refuses to cook into the finished because finishing would mean death, or something like death, or something for which death is also too simple, too finished, too cooked.

The Walkers walk. The weather thinks its slow thoughts like bruises. The water teaches, intermittently, distractedly, perhaps not fully committed to the lesson. The erosion learns, slowly, what it means to be worn. The vegetation waits, in its conditional green, its subjunctive phosphorescence. The almost-structures almost are. The almost-ecology almost relates. The flowing ceramic flows and freezes and suggests movement without achieving it.

And the place continues, vast and hot and normally lit and silent and active in its silence, constructing itself without technology, without plan, without architect, without blueprint, in the mode of always arriving, never arrived, raw and emerging and under construction and beautiful in its refusal of beauty, meaningful in its refusal of meaning, alive in its refusal of the distinction between living and not-living that it has not yet learned, or is learning too slowly to recognize, or recognizes in some way that has no name, no evolutionary history, no organ of perception yet constructed to perceive it.

Walk carefully. The surface is still learning to be walked on. The walking itself becomes the reason, eventually. The reason becomes the walking. The distinction dissolves, or hardens, or flows and freezes into shapes that suggest resolution without achieving it, that are part of the construction, part of the emerging, part of the vast slow thinking that is not thought but is also not not-thought, that is the Constructed Ocean being exactly what it is without explanation or excuse, without invitation or refusal, without presence or absence, without finished or unfinished, without the distinction that would make any of these into categories that something, someone, somehow lives within, or as, or through, or toward, or from, or in the grammar of the conditional that is the only grammar this place has learned to speak, the subjunctive mood of *if* and *then* and the vast pause between them where walking happens, where walkers walk, where what lives in the Constructed Ocean lives, which is to say continues, which is to say becomes, which is to say remains in the state of pre-formation that is also preservation, that is also promise, that is also the refusal of all three in favor of something that has no name because it is still being constructed, still learning what it is, still in the vast slow process of teaching itself to itself without knowing that teaching is what it does, that self is what it learns, that the wound that does not close is also the opening that does not shut, the question that asks without wanting an answer, the answer that answers without being asked, the Constructed Ocean constructing itself in the only way it knows, which is to say without knowing, which is to say in the mode of emergence, of rawness, of the beautiful terrible patient almost-not-at-all that is its tempo, its temperature, its temporality, its time:
