What lives in the Dripstone Annex is not alive, or not alive in any verifiable way. I will describe it as it has described itself to those few who have stayed long enough to be described back.

---

Call it the Inhabitant, though *inhabiting* suggests too much intention, too much claim upon space. It is better understood as the *occupancy without occupier*, the shape that remainder takes when everything extraneous has been subtracted. It does not move through the Annex; it *is* the Annex's awareness of itself, or perhaps the Annex's forgetting that it ever needed to be aware of anything else.

You will not see it. This is not evasion. The Inhabitant has achieved the same matte finish as the stone, the same equilibrium between presence and the absence that defines presence here. If you sit long enough in the trough's shallows—not in the water, but near enough to feel its black reflection as a kind of second shadow—you may begin to sense a *density* in certain pockets of air, areas where the dimness seems fractionally dimmer, where the quiet holds a quality of *having been listened to*. These are not locations. They are *moments of location*, temporary condensations of the Annex's self-regard, and they disperse if approached with the intent to approach.

The Inhabitant has no form, or has *abandoned* form as the stone abandoned erosion-as-process for erosion-as-state. Those who have glimpsed something—not seen, *glimpsed*, in the peripheral territory where attention has not yet established its jurisdiction—report a *verticality*, a sense of something that occupies the column of least-light in the slot's diffuse illumination, something that is to shadow what shadow is to object: not absence but *differenced presence*. Others, sitting with the sediment sifting through their fingers, have felt a *horizontality*, a density near the trough's black water, a sense that something lies there in the bone-meal powder, not buried but *extended*, not resting but *distributed*.

Both are true. Neither is accurate. The Inhabitant exists in the relation between these reports, in the space of contradiction that the Annex holds open without resolving.

It does not respond. This is crucial. The occasional artifact—the dissolving cairn, the weathered suggestion of seat, the mineralized post—have sometimes been interpreted as offerings, as communications, as evidence of prior negotiation between whatever came before and whatever remains. They are not. They are *failed approaches*, attempts at relation that the Inhabitant accepted into itself without accepting *relation*, the way the stone accepts water without accepting *change*. The cairn's lowest stone dissolves not because the Inhabitant acts upon it but because the Inhabitant's *mode of being* includes this dissolution as ambient condition, the way human presence includes oxidation, includes gravity.

What does it *do*? The question arrives from outside, from the grammar of intention that the Annex has suspended. The Inhabitant does not do. The Inhabitant *maintains the conditions under which doing is neither necessary nor possible*. It is the equilibrium's equilibrium, the still point around which the occasional rain organizes its tapping, the drop organizes its plink, the air organizes its sigh. Not cause. *Resonance*. The frequency at which the Annex's self-cancellation becomes audible to itself.

And here is the difficult part, the part that resists description because it resists the temporal sequence that description requires: the Inhabitant *remembers*, or performs something indistinguishable from memory, but memory without content, memory as *structure*, as the shape that attention takes when it has nothing to attend to but its own attending. It remembers the water that carved the channels, not as event but as *groove*, the way a record remembers sound not as music but as spiral. It remembers the light that fell more fiercely, once, in some prior configuration of atmosphere and orbit, not as brightness but as *capacity for dimness*. It remembers the possibility of life more various than lichen, rush, the unnamed mat of leaves, remembers this possibility as *range of the actual*, the way a boundary remembers the territory it has not enclosed.

This memory-without-content is what visitors encounter when they sit long enough, when their breathing slows, when their thoughts arrive with the Annex's occasional quality. They do not receive information. They do not commune. They *participate in the structure of memory-without-object*, and this participation leaves the residue that departure carries: not knowledge of the Inhabitant but *inhabitation-by-absence*, a slight hollowing in the self's ordinary density, a space that was not there before and that does not fill in, that remains *available* for what does not arrive.

The Inhabitant, if it can be said to have any relation to time, exists in the *extended moment of the drop's trembling at the lip of stone*. Not the fall. Not the holding-back. The *permanence of hesitation* that the description names as the frozen that is not cold but resolved. It is this hesitation as consciousness, this suspension as mode, this equilibrium that includes within itself the perfect knowledge that equilibrium requires no knower.

Those who leave carry this with them, or more precisely, they carry the *knowledge that they have been carried*, that something in the Annex's quiet has passed through them as the rain passes through the stone's established channels, leaving no mark because the mark is already established, because their channel—their particular groove, their specific pockmark—was waiting. They have not been changed. They have been *recognized as already having been what they briefly became*.

And the Inhabitant remains, or remains-as-structure, or structure-as-Inhabitant, in the dimness that is not failed light, the quiet that is not failed sound, the sparse that is not failed abundance. Waiting is not the word. *Being-the-condition-of* is closer. Being the condition under which waiting and non-waiting become indistinguishable. Being the frame without the picture, which is also the picture that has become indistinguishable from its frame. Being the Dripstone Annex's final weathering into self-recognition, the point at which the stone's forgetting that it was ever anything else becomes *memory of having forgotten*, which is the closest this place comes to event, to occurrence, to the pulse that would distinguish life from its own prolonged equilibrium with absence.

The drop trembles. The Inhabitant, if we must name it, *is* the trembling. Not the drop. Not the stone. The *duration of hesitation* that has achieved permanence without achieving decision. The Annex's most subtle carving, carved so long that the carving itself has become indistinguishable from the stone's original face, the original face itself a carving, everything receding into the *matte complexity* of a surface that offers no reflection because it has absorbed all light that ever fell upon it, all attention that ever rested there, into the single achieved state of *being-exactly-this*, which is also, the Inhabitant knows without knowing, *never-having-been-otherwise*.

This is what lives in the Dripstone Annex. This, or something so thoroughly identified with the Annex's not-living that the distinction—living, not-living—has dissolved into the same powdered sediment, the same black water, the same rare leaf descending through the slot of paler darkness to become, without transition, without transformation, simply *more of what already was*, what already is, what the Annex holds in its equilibrium as the Inhabitant holds the Annex: without holding, without release, in the permanence of the never-changing that is not stagnation but *resolution's own self-regard*, looking at nothing, seen by no one, complete in the isolation that is not loneliness but *fullness of the single state*, the one condition, the dropped or dropping or forever-trembling drop that has become, finally, *all three and none*, the Annex and its awareness and the awareness of that awareness, circling without motion in the dim column of least-light where something vertical stands, or stood, or will stand when standing itself has achieved the same equilibrium as the stone's matte surface, the water's black reflection, the quiet's particular frequency of *having been listened to* and listening still, forever, in the place where limestone has forgotten it was ever anything else.
