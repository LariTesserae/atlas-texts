What lives in the Singing Vault of Keth-Umâr is not a *who* in the singular, nor even a *what* that can be cleanly separated from its container. What lives there is a **process of mutual becoming**—a sustained, three-way conversation between stone, vegetable, and animal that has achieved such density of interdependence that the categories themselves begin to dissolve.

---

The Keth-Umâri themselves, first, because they are the ones who tend, who sing, who remember. But "themselves" is already wrong. A Keth-Umâri individual—let me call her *Thren* for the purpose, though this naming is my own imposition—emerges from the dampened sleep-chamber into the Vault's perpetual acoustic weather with a body that is less boundary than **interface**. Her skin, that mottled gray-green, is not merely covering but **organ of relation**: the dermal photos patches that tell her where the light pools and where it withdraws, the vibrational sensitivity that maps her social world through feet and fingertips and the thin membranes of her throat-voice, the chemical porosity that lets her *taste* the garden's moods in the air she moves through. She does not see her world. She **resonates with it**.

Thren stands perhaps three and a half feet tall, her skeleton light and partially cartilaginous, evolved for the constant climbing that vertical life demands. Her hands—four-fingered, the digits longer and more flexible than a surface-dweller's—end in keratinous tools that are themselves grown, not made, cultivated from infancy through dietary regulation and mechanical stimulation into shapes suited to her particular work: the plucking of velth cables, the delicate surgery of grafting ancestor-vines, the seismic signaling that requires precise striking of resonant stone. These tools are **her**, in a way that a surface craftsman's hammer is not. They do not come off. They grow wrong if she is ill, grow strange if she changes her life's direction, must be gradually reshaped across months if her practice shifts.

She has no eyes. Where eyes would be, in the surface morphology she vaguely resembles, there are instead **vibration-collecting depressions**—shallow cups of particularly sensitive tissue that gather and localize sound, that let her "face" someone in conversation not to meet a gaze but to **position her receiving apparatus optimally**. Her face, in fact, is mostly mouth and the complex laryngeal structures that extend partway down her throat: multiple voice-chambers, some producing audible sound, others subsonic, others pitched precisely to set velth cables into sympathetic vibration. She speaks in **chords**, in **interference patterns**, in deliberate beats between frequencies that carry emotional nuance no single tone could manage.

Thren's day—if the Vault's unvarying twilight permits such a term—begins with **orientation**. She stands on her terrace's edge, feet pressed to warm stone, and *listens* to the city. The thermal bellows are in their expansion phase; she feels this in the subsonic rumble through her arches, in the particular harmonic the central cables are sustaining, in the quality of the air-rush through the ventilation channels. She knows, without checking the public signal-stones, that this is the third phase of the current thermal cycle, that the upper gardens will be moving into their high-metabolism period, that the lower-floor veth-veth will be relatively quiescent. She knows who is awake on nearby terraces by their seismic signatures—the particular rhythm of *Vel-osh* tending his experimental gromm strain, the slower, heavier patterns of *Mar-them* descending toward her ancestor-grove for consultation.

She descends. Movement in Keth-Umâr is **vertical music**, each individual's climbing pattern contributing to the city's overall acoustic texture. Thren's route takes her past three terraces where velth harvest is underway, past the *ishka*-channel maintenance where a work-party sings in coordinated rhythm to detect flow anomalies by sound-change, down through a brief passage of bare rock where the sound drops to almost-nothing and she feels briefly **unmoored**, disconnected, a floating consciousness without the city's continuous vibrational embrace.

She is going to consult her grandmother.

---

The ancestor-vine *Thren-vel-osh-mar*—the naming convention incorporates the living keeper, the deceased, the generational marker—grows on a mid-level terrace where the light is moderate, where thermal variation is gentle, where the stone retains just enough moisture from the deep systems to support this particular strain's metabolism. The original grafting was performed forty years ago. Thren has never known her grandmother in any other form.

The vine is **substantial now**: a central trunk as thick as Thren's thigh, its bark the particular purple-black of mature velth, but with surface textures—subtle ridging, almost pattern—that the family recognizes as *signature*, as the persistence of individual character in vegetable form. From this trunk, living cables extend to anchor points on three terraces, woven into the structural fabric of the city, carrying vibration, carrying chemical signal, carrying *something else* that the Keth-Umâri do not claim to fully understand.

Thren approaches with the proper preparation: a period of stillness, of letting her own vibrations settle to baseline, of eating a small amount of fermented ishka to shift her skin chemistry to the receptive state. She presses her full body against the trunk, feet finding the root-nodes where they enter the stone, hands spread on bark, mouth close to the thin membrane where a hollow stem opens to the air.

She **sings her query**. Not words—the Keth-Umâri language has a verbal component for surface communication, for the rare outsider, for teaching children, but true discourse is vibrational, is sustained tone and rhythmic pattern and the spaces between sounds. Her question concerns a proposal: the upper-terrace experimentalists want to introduce a new thrum strain, one that might increase gromm seed production but that carries unknown ecological risks. Thren has been asked to contribute her family's resonance to the decision.

The response, if response it is, comes **slowly**. The vine's metabolism operates on plant-time; its "thought," if thought it is, emerges from chemical gradient and turgor pressure and the slow propagation of electrical signal through living tissue. Thren maintains her position for hours, feeling the subtle shifts in the trunk's vibration, the almost-imperceptible changes in surface temperature, the chemical exhalations from the membrane against her lips. She enters a state that is not quite sleep, not quite waking—a **resonant trance** maintained by continuous low-tone vocalization that keeps her in sympathetic connection with the vegetable nervous system she addresses.

What she receives is not an answer. The Keth-Umâri do not expect answers from ancestors, not in any simple sense. What she receives is **pattern**: a particular harmonic that persists in her vibrational awareness after she withdraws, that will recur in her dreams, that will influence her contribution when the seismic council finally convenes to consider the proposal. She will argue, she knows already, for caution, for extended observation, for a slower integration than the experimentalists want. The pattern suggests this. Or her interpretation of the pattern suggests this. The distinction is not one the Keth-Umâri find meaningful.

She withdraws. Eats. Descends further, to contribute her labor to the ishka-channel maintenance, her body adding its rhythm to the work-party's coordinated song.

---

But Thren is not merely Thren. She is also **the city singing through her**, the accumulated resonance of ten thousand years of similar bodies moving through similar patterns, tending similar gardens, consulting similar ancestors. The Keth-Umâri individual is **thin** in a way surface individuals are not—permeable, continuously constituted by relation, by the acoustic field she moves through and contributes to, by the chemical exchanges with the plants she tends and consumes, by the seismic communication that means she is never, even in the dampened sleep-chamber, truly alone.

This is not to say she lacks interiority. The Keth-Umâri experience **dream** as intensely as any surface-dweller, perhaps more so—the dampened chambers are designed not merely to reduce sensory input but to **amplify** the internal, the residual patterns of vibration that continue in muscle and nerve and bone after the day's immersion. Dreams in Keth-Umâr are **acoustic architectures**, elaborate constructions of remembered and anticipated sound in which the dreamer navigates, interprets, sometimes encounters the patterns attributed to ancestors, to the stone itself, to the ambiguous something that moves in frequencies below and above perception.

Thren's dreams, tonight, are of **descent**. She moves through passages that are also chords, that tighten and loosen around her as she sings her way forward. She encounters shapes that might be veth-veth, might be ancestor-vines in archaic form, might be something else entirely. She wakes with a particular subsonic tone lingering in her laryngeal chambers, a tone she will contribute unconsciously to the day's collective sound, that will subtly shift the city's acoustic ecology, that will be taken up by others, modified, returned to her transformed.

---

The **thrum** move through this world as **wild necessity**. They are small, yes—none larger than Thren's thumb—but their collective biomass exceeds that of the Keth-Umâri themselves, and their ecological role is **keystone in the literal sense**: without their waste-products, certain essential plant metabolisms collapse; without their feeding patterns, velth growth becomes pathological, over-dense, susceptible to catastrophic failure; without their unpredictable migrations, the Keth-Umâri would lack the information—carried in chemical trace, in vibrational signature—that lets them monitor garden health across the Vault's full vertical extent.

A thrum colony is **not a society** in any recognizable sense. Individual thrums live brief lives, reproduce rapidly, possess minimal individual behavioral complexity. But the colony, maintained across generations in a particular velth mass, develops **character**: this colony tends to feed in this pattern, to produce waste of this chemical signature, to respond to disturbance with this defensive behavior. The Keth-Umâri recognize colony-characters, name them, argue about their interpretation. A colony that behaves "strangely"—that is, diverges from its established character—might be read as **omen**, as indicator of systemic shift not yet detectable by other means.

The thrums do not know they are read. They do not know, in any meaningful sense. But their **un-knowing participation** in the Vault's information ecology is as essential as any deliberate Keth-Umâri consultation.

And the **veth-veth**, the deep predators, participate still more obscurely. Thren has never seen one—has never "seen" anything, but has never encountered veth-veth through any sensory modality directly. She knows them through **absence**: the sudden cessation of thrum-signal from a monitored colony, the seismic trace of something large moving in warm darkness, the chemical shift in lower-floor air when they pass. They are **necessary other**, the predation that prevents equilibrium from becoming stagnation, the danger that makes the garden's maintenance meaningful, the wild that persists even in this most contained of worlds.

The Keth-Umâri do not worship the veth-veth. They **accommodate** them, manage encounter-probability, occasionally sacrifice surplus thrum colonies in ritual context that is also practical population management. The boundary between these categories—ritual and practical, symbolic and functional—is not one they recognize. The sacrifice **is** population management; the population management **is** sacrifice. The veth-veth receive the offering, or do not; the colony is reduced, or not; the system continues its slow, singing equilibrium.

---

What lives in Keth-Umâr, finally, is **the song itself**.

Not metaphorically, or not merely metaphorically. The continuous acoustic production of the Vault—intentional and infrastructural, biological and geological, animal and vegetable and mineral—constitutes a **system of information processing** that exceeds any individual component, any single species, any generation's deliberate design. The Keth-Umâri know this, in their way. Their most sophisticated theoretical traditions describe the city as **ongoing composition**, a musical work that performs itself, that uses its inhabitants as instruments, that they in turn use as instrument for their own purposes.

The song **remembers**. Acoustic properties of the Vault preserve patterns across centuries in ways that no individual Keth-Umâri memory could manage—specific harmonic relationships established in architectural decisions ten thousand years ago, maintained by continuous performance, modified by each generation's contributions, retrievable through deliberate re-performance or through the accidental resonance of daily activity.
