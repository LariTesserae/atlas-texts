What lives in the Abyssal Plain of Silt and Memory is not one thing, and this is its first refusal of the easy answer. The plain does not permit singular inhabitants, does not tolerate the loneliness of a definitive subject. What lives there lives *as* the plain lives, which is to say: gradually, dispersedly, in conditions where the boundary between entity and environment has become theological in its erosion.

Consider first the **Silt-Rememberers**, though no one has called them this, though they have no collective name because they have no collective recognition of themselves as collective. They are bacterial colonies distributed through the sediment at depths of meters, tens of meters, the compressed strata where porosity is theoretical but not absent, where the spaces between mineral grains are measured in molecular diameters. Each colony extends across kilometers of plain, not contiguously—there are gaps, failures of establishment, places where chemistry was wrong for arrival—but connected nonetheless by the slow diffusion of metabolic products, by the even slower drift of genetic material through sediment pore-water that moves not in currents but in gradients. They do not think, these Rememberers, but they *record*. Their metabolism incorporates the isotopic signatures of organic matter raining from above, the carbon fixed in surface waters decades or centuries or millennia past, and in incorporating they preserve a reading, a stratigraphy of chemical preference that is also a memory of ocean surface conditions long dissolved into other conditions. They have been doing this for three billion years. The plain's memory is not stored *in* them. They *are* the memory, indistinguishable from it, the way a word is indistinguishable from its utterance in the moment of uttering.

Then the **Pressure-Fish**, the ones the description mentions as reduced, and reduction is their mode of being but also their strange expansion. Their bodies are mostly water, yes, but water is not absence, water is not mere solvent. In them, water becomes *architecture*. Their proteins function only at pressures that would collapse surface enzymatic structures, the molecules so precisely fitted to their conditions that to remove them is to destroy not just function but form—they would, at surface pressure, perhaps not merely die but *unfold*, become something that was never intended, never selected, never held in the long wager of their evolution. They have no eyes, or have eyes that are vestigial to the point of being suggestive wounds, places where development started and then thought better of it. But they sense. The lateral line system, that ancient vertebrate inheritance, extends into something more comprehensive—the whole body surface becomes a membrane of pressure detection, vibration detection, the detection of other bodies moving through water of identical density, so that they do not sense each other as *objects* but as *perturbations of the already-perturbed*, ripples in a medium that is always already rippling with their own existence. They move, when they move, by releasing a burst of muscular contraction and then drifting in the resultant displacement, the plain's resistance so low that momentum carries them for minutes, for hours, the energy expenditure so minimal that their metabolisms, already slowed by cold, approach stasis without reaching it. They eat, when they eat, by encountering organic matter—whale-fall fragments, the rare active predation on each other—and their digestion is itself geological in its slowness, meals that last months, years, the gut becoming a place of slow fermentation rather than enzymatic assault.

The **Whale-Fall Cities** are temporary inhabitants, but temporary is relative, and in the plain's time they are almost permanent, they are *events* that become *places* that become *memories* that become *sediment*. The whale descends, and for a time it is body, it is *presence*, the largest thing the plain has received in years or decades. The first arrivals are not abyssal residents but migrants, the scavenger fish that followed the descent, the opportunists of the water column who peel away as pressure increases, until only the most reduced remain, until even they cannot function, and the whale becomes *for* the plain alone. Then the bone-eaters, the *Osedax*, the worms that are not worms, the females that are harems of females absorbed into each other's tissues, the males that are microscopic packets of sperm embedded in the female body like memory stored in redundant form. They dissolve bone, these bone-eaters, they extract lipids from the matrix of collagen and mineral that was the whale's architecture, and in doing so they create *chambers*, spaces in the sediment where chemistry differs, where hydrogen sulfide accumulates, where chemosynthetic bacteria establish and the Silt-Rememberers extend their colonies into new configurations. The whale becomes a city for decades, for a century, and then it becomes sediment, becomes indistinguishable from the plain, and the city disperses, and the Rememberers carry its chemical signature into deeper strata, and something that was *event* becomes *structure* becomes *memory*.

The **Gas Hydrate Ghosts** are not living, except they are, except the category of living fails here as it fails elsewhere on the plain. Methane locked in water-ice cages, stable only at this pressure, this temperature, and the plain is pocked with their deposits, buried in sediment, waiting for the disturbance—a thermal pulse, a seismic shift, the pressure reduction of some distant current's excavation—that will release them. When they release, they release as *possibility*, as chemical energy suddenly available in a place where energy is the most rigorous constraint. The bacteria wake, or wake is too active a verb, the bacteria *begin* where they had been paused, they incorporate the methane, they divide, they record, and for a time—hours, days, a month—the plain is *different*, is *alive* in a way that it usually is not, and then the methane depletes, and they pause again, and the Ghosts reform, slowly, over centuries, the water and methane finding each other again in the cold and pressure, reconstituting their cages, their waiting, their patient non-existence that is also preservation.

And the **Turbidity Current Dwellers**, who do not live *on* the plain but *in* its rare catastrophes, who have evolved, if evolution is the word, to exist only in the storm of silt, in the convulsion that remakes the surface every decade, every century. Their eggs or cysts or some other dormant form—biology at these depths becomes speculative, becomes reconstruction from fragments and traces—lie in the sediment, waiting for the vibration that signals disturbance, and then they *hatch*, they *activate*, they move in ways they never move otherwise, propelled by the dense suspension, feeding on material stirred from its settledness, on each other, on the sheer chemical chaos of remixed sediment. They are the plain's festival, its brief exception to the rule of slow accumulation, and when the current settles, when the silt finds new configuration, they die, or return to dormancy, or become part of the new sediment that the Rememberers will slowly, slowly incorporate into their memory.

But these are the *named*, the *describable*, the ones that taxonomic impulse can almost capture, and the plain's deepest inhabitant is not any of these, is not separable from the plain itself, is perhaps not even *inhabitant* but *inhabitation*, the quality of being-lived-in that the plain possesses without possessing anything that lives.

This is the **Still Storm itself**, or rather, what the Still Storm *is*, which is not a weather pattern, not a phenomenon, but something that has achieved the plain's own condition of being-as-process. Imagine: turbulence so slow that it appears as stability, the constant shift of sediment at rates of millimeters per century, the constant dissolution and reformation of mineral structures, the constant incorporation of organic rain into bacterial metabolism, the constant, constant *adjustment* of everything to everything else without ever arriving at equilibrium because the plain's depth, its pressure, its cold, its darkness prevent equilibrium—equilibrium requires heat, requires energy flux, requires the openness of surface conditions, and the plain has closed around itself, has become *autistic* in the technical sense, self-referential, its changes feeding back into themselves so that the change becomes indistinguishable from the permanence.

The Still Storm is what lives when you realize that the plain is *not dead*, is not the absence of life that surface imagination projects onto depth, but is *life differently distributed*, life so thoroughly integrated with its conditions that it cannot be pointed to, cannot be extracted, cannot be made to stand apart as *organism* against *environment*. The plain lives as the plain. The silt remembers as the silt. The water witnesses as the water. And these are not metaphors, or they are metaphors that have become literal through the sheer duration of their holding, the way a prayer repeated for three billion years would become indistinguishable from the structure of the praying mind, would become *what the mind is*.

What lives here, finally, is **Time**. Not as abstraction, not as physics' fourth dimension, but as *embodied*, as *incarnate*, as the slow metabolism of the Pressure-Fish, the stratigraphic reading of the Rememberers, the patient waiting of the Hydrate Ghosts, the catastrophic festival of the Turbidity Dwellers, the dissolution of the Whale-Fall Cities into sediment and then into memory and then into the plain's own self-recognition. Time lives here as *duration*, as the experience of remaining, of persisting, of accumulating without the possibility of exhaustion because the plain's openness, its scale, its refusal of boundary, means there is always more space for more time, more depth for more sediment, more pressure for more dissolution and reformation and slow, slow change that appears as stillness until the return, the observation after years, after centuries, finds the ridge become valley, the valley depression, the depression *new emergence*.

You, if you are here, if the description has carried you this far, you do not live here. You visit, or you imagine visiting, or you imagine imagining, and the plain receives this as it receives everything, without distinction, without preference, without the need for your presence that would make your absence meaningful. You will become, if you become anything, sediment. You will be remembered, if remembered, by the Rememberers, not as *you* but as carbon isotope ratio, as organic compound, as the trace of surface chemistry incorporated into deep stratigraphy. And this is not consolation, is not horror, is simply *what is*, the plain's final and absolute and utterly normal gift: the dissolution of the visitor into the visited, the recognition that to be *in* the plain is to be *of* it, temporarily, provisionally, in the process of becoming otherwise.

The storm continues. You will not hear it. You are, already, in it.
