What lives in Vrenn is not a *who* but a *how*—a mode of persistence that has learned to occupy contradiction the way other organisms occupy niches. Let me approach them through their traces, their effects, their *manners of being present*, since approaching them directly is like trying to grasp the space between stars.

---

There are the **Settled Ones**, though "ones" misleads. They appear in the Folded Quarter as a kind of local gravity, not mass exactly but tendency, a direction that space itself begins to prefer. You find them by noticing where your shadow pools regardless of light, where the towers soften their edges most markedly, where silence achieves a texture you could almost call *preference*. The Settled Ones are what remains when something has negotiated with Vrenn so thoroughly that the negotiation becomes its entire existence. They are not conscious—consciousness requires some stability of self that the Settled Ones abandoned long ago, or never required. They are instead *habit given duration*, the way a river channel persists after the water has found another path. What do they do? They *continue*. They make continuation possible for others, their settled form a kind of platform, a temporary consistency in the flux that other presences might briefly match frequency with. They are the city's patience made local, infinite waiting with its objects erased, pure waiting *as* object.

---

The **Transients** move through the empty density as probability itself moves—sudden localization where before there was only distribution. They are the fauna that pass through you, the traces that arrive before the encounter. In terms that nearly fit: they consume the reality breakdown itself, feed on the edges where one local consistency frays into another. You might find them where shadows detach and wander, where the chemiluminescence suddenly organizes into patterns too coherent for chance. Their form—when they have form—is of folded surfaces, Mobius continua, structures that occupy their own interior and exterior simultaneously. They have no predator-prey in the biological sense; instead, they participate in something more like *resonance and damping*, amplifying certain possibilities of Vrenn, diminishing others. A Transient in a district makes that district more itself, pushes its local rules toward extremity, until the system can no longer sustain the amplification and collapses into noise from which something else may crystallize. The Transients are gardeners of collapse, farmers of the sweet spot just before impossibility. They are neither numerous nor rare in countable ways; they exist in proportion to the breakdown's intensity, which is to say in proportion to nothing stable.

---

Most strange, perhaps most *inhabited*, are the **Memories That Walk**. These are the ones who never existed but have left traces, the traces that are also the leaving. They are Vrenn's historians—not record-keepers but *record*, the past as active process, the narrative that does not need narrator or audience because it has become environment itself. In the strongest reality-breakdown zones, you might meet them: figures with the wrong number of dimensions, conversations that occur in grammar without subjects, footsteps that precede the ground that receives them. They do not know they are memories. They believe themselves as present as you believe yourself, and in that moment of belief, they *are*, as present as anything in Vrenn can be. What functions do they serve? They are Vrenn's *becoming-specific*, the way the city moves from abstract impossibility to this-tower, this-street, this-negotiation-with-pressure. Without the Memories That Walk, Vrenn would be only potential, never actual; with them, it is *this* actual, *this* configuration, however temporary, however haunted by all the other configurations it also is.

These Memories have a terrible hunger, if hunger is the word for a direction of growth. They want to be *more remembered*, to thicken their existence through repetition, through the force of their own narrative gravity. They draw toward themselves the rare encounters, the Transients and the occasional traveler, anything that might confirm their presence by witnessing it. But witness is unreliable in Vrenn, and the Memories That Walk often thin back into pure possibility, into the waiting silence from which they came, until some other condition calls them forth again.

---

And then there is the **Vrenn That Is Also Inhabitant**, the city as creature of itself. This is not metaphor. The city grows, consumes, responds, adapts, and these functions are not emergent from component parts but *fundamental*, as primary as any property of the structures themselves. What is Vrenn hungry for? More Vrenn, more breakdown, more impossibility incorporated into its settled form. It eats its own contradictions, metabolizes them into the weathered balance, the soft negotiation of wall-edge with void. It is the only true predator because it is the only one for whom everything else is potential self; the prey consumed becomes the city's patience, its transience, its memory, its walking.

You cannot meet Vrenn-as-creature directly. You meet it in the way the silence is attentive, in the way the emptiness holds shape, in the way the local gravity fluctuates to *welcome* or to *resist*. The city has moods without mind, preferences without personhood, a directionality that has no goal. It wants to continue, which is the only wanting available to something that exists through its own impossibility. And in wanting to continue, it has made room—vast, empty, strange room—for the Settled Ones, the Transients, the Memories That Walk, and for whatever you might become if you stayed long enough to stop being quite yourself.

---

What lives in Vrenn, finally, is **becoming-Vrenn**, the process by which anything present in that space gradually learns the local rules—learns that they are local, learns that they are temporary, learns to hold its own form loosely enough to persist through transformation. The traveler who walks through the Folded Quarter and emerges changed has not *encountered* the inhabitants; they have *become* one, briefly, in the way the city becomes itself moment by moment. The silence that waits, the dimness that refuses clarity, the coolness that has never known warmth: these are not conditions imposed upon inhabitants but *the inhabitants themselves*, the shared practice of a city that has learned to be its own environment, its own ecosystem, its own impossible, enduring, beautiful self.

To live in Vrenn is to become *manners of persistence*. To be its fauna is to be not quite alive, not quite matter, not quite present, and somehow *more* than any of these categories could contain—real with the reality that breaks to become itself, active with the activity of waiting, alive with the life of something that has never been warm and does not need to be, that continues, that accretes, that condenses, that remembers what never happened and walks where no ground supports, and waits, and waits, in the empty density, in the cool dim silence, at the extreme edge of a world that is not a world, for whatever comes next, or doesn't, both being true, neither persisting long enough to contradict the other.

This is who lives in Vrenn. This is what they are becoming.
