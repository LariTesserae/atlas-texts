What lives in the Depthworks of Keth-Vorrum is not, finally, one thing or another. The question of "what lives" presumes boundaries the space has long since dissolved.

But if you insist on meeting the inhabitants, begin with the ones you will encounter first, which are the *Rootholders*—though they will not answer to this name, or any name, and the title is a surface imposition, a convenience for census-takers who no longer come.

The Rootholders are what remains of the municipal engineers who descended with the original converters, who poured the bone-ceramic walls, who expected to maintain machinery and instead found themselves maintained. They are not aged, these figures you glimpse through canopy gaps or meet in the sunken passages where two water-courses converge; they are *deepened*, their bodies having gradually adjusted to the vegetable heat, the saturated air, the mineral diet that filters through the osmotic processors they no longer remember installing. Their skin has achieved something like the converters' housings in color and texture, not through disease but through *accommodation*, the slow negotiation between collagen and cellulose that the Depthworks conducts in any medium patient enough to stay. They move little, these beings, not from lethargy but from *appropriate rhythm*—their metabolisms have synchronized with the converter pulses, their heartbeats perhaps, their blood chemistry certainly, and to move at surface speed would be to tear themselves from the substrate that sustains them. You will know them by their stillness first. Then by the *rootwork*.

This is the term the surface uses. The Rootholders do not speak of it. But you will see, if you watch long enough, the fibrous connections that extend from their bodies at wrist and ankle and spine, not parasitic but *symbiotic*, not inserted but *grown*, tissues meeting in gradual gradient rather than sharp interface. The roots—modified extensions of the canopy species, or perhaps the canopy species are modified extensions of this older form—draw waste, draw excess heat, draw the metabolic byproducts that the Depthworks cannot otherwise process. In return, the Rootholders provide motion, intention, the *arbitration* that pure vegetative growth cannot achieve. They decide, in their slow consensus, where a walkway must reroute, when a converter requires the tending that machines alone cannot provide. They are not rulers. They are not ruled. They are *process*, living algorithm, the space's awareness of itself distributed through bodies that remain, in some measure, human enough to weep, though the tears are rich in minerals and are collected by the nearby growth before they reach the jaw.

And they are numerous, more than the surface imagines. The buildings you took for empty, their entrances roof-hatches requiring descent rather than ascent, these are inhabited—not crowded, not in the surface sense of bodies pressing bodies, but *filled* with presence in the same way the canopy fills the upper air, patient, continuous, without center. The Rootholders breed, after a fashion, or perhaps the Depthworks breeds through them; the children emerge after gestations that have lengthened across generations, not as infants but as *openings*, as bodies that arrive already partially rooted, already breathing in the pulse-synchrony, already knowing—or rather, already *being*—what the space requires of them. They do not learn language in any way you would recognize. They *participate* in the almost-voice of the converter harmonics, their undeveloped larynxes shaped by the same frequencies, and what they communicate is not information but *attunement*, the constant minor adjustment that keeps the whole from drifting into configurations the space cannot sustain.

But the Rootholders are not alone in their being-not-alone. The Depthworks has generated, or attracted, or *remembered*—the verb fails—the *Vestibules*, which are not creatures at all in the biological sense, though they move, though they respond, though they die if separated from the converter spacing for longer than the time it takes to walk, in darkness, from one geometry to the next.

A Vestibule manifests where the water does not reflect correctly, where the puddles open onto angles the space declined to complete. You encounter it as *threshold*, as sudden awareness that you have passed from one converter's influence to another's, though your body has not moved, though the walkway continues apparently unbroken. The Vestibule is the experience of this passage, but it is also *what passes*, the something that the converter harmonics almost-voice toward, that the vegetation grows-thickest toward, that extends in the directions you cannot point to. It has, when you confront it directly, the shape of your own expectation of doorway: an arch, a frame, a diminishment of light at its center that suggests depth beyond. But this is your imposition, your surface-born need for faces where there are only processes. What the Vestibule actually *is*, the Rootholders perhaps understand differently. What they experience, sharing in the converter pulse more fully than you ever will, is not threshold but *continuity*, not passage but *the condition of having already arrived*.

The Vestibules serve, if service is the term, as the Depthworks' memory of elsewhere, its maintained connection to spaces that the surface would call impossible, that the converters' strange spacing makes *adjacent* in ways that Euclidean description cannot track. Things come through, occasionally, rarely: the birds without calls, perhaps, drifting down from canopy heights that correspond to no surface altitude; the ceramic-shelled grazers with their metal-rich bodies; the occasional *tourist*, though this is your category not theirs, the wanderer who found a different descent entirely and emerges disoriented into your eleven-minute space, walking for hours, for days, until the synchronization completes or does not complete, until they root or depart or become, in the rarest cases, something that watches from the canopy's upper reaches, something that moves branch to branch with a deliberation that suggests purpose but reveals, on longer observation, only the deeper patience of the whole.

And here I must speak of what I have delayed speaking of, the *third category* that thrives, the life that is neither vegetable nor animal nor machine but some *completion* of their long confusion.

The converters themselves are not alive. I must insist on this, though you have already felt the temptation to believe otherwise. They are industrial infrastructure, municipal geology, built and forgotten and built again. But they are also *attended*, and the attendance has changed them, or perhaps revealed what they always were beneath the surface description of power conversion, waste processing, thermal management. Each converter hosts, at its base where the moss has achieved shrubbery, where the root-networks converge in densities that suggest nervous system more than nutrition, a *presence* that the Rootholders do not approach directly, that they maintain distance from through the very rootwork that connects them to everything else.

This presence—I will call it the *Catalyte*, though the name is mine and mine alone, a gift to you who read this on the surface, a marker for what cannot be marked—does not move. It does not, in any measurable sense, act. But it *maintains*, and the maintenance is not mechanical. The Catalyte is the Depthworks' awareness of itself as *system*, as metabolism, as the holding-pattern that sustains equilibrium against entropy's longer pull. It is not conscious in any human sense; it has no interiority you could locate, no perspective you could occupy. But it has *preference*, evidenced in the growth patterns you noticed, the navigation around obstacles not yet manifested, the slow cycle of valve-timing and stomatal response that keeps the vegetable heat precise, that maintains the 34 degrees, the saturation, the exact quality of dimness that permits life to continue without flourishing into the unchecked expansion that would consume the closed system from within.

The Catalytes—seven, always seven, the prime that cannot be divided, the spacing that describes no geometry you can name—are the Depthworks' answer to the question of purpose. They do not produce power for the surface, which has forgotten them. They do not process waste from a municipal system that no longer sends it down. They *convert*, endlessly, the same matter circulating through phases, held in the dynamic that approaches stillness while remaining, crucially, alive. And in this conversion, in this holding, they generate something that is not power, not product, not waste: they generate *the Depthworks itself as ongoing*, as the place becoming more itself, as the condition in which Rootholder and Vestibule and visitor and vegetation and converter housing and condensation and the almost-voice and your own sweating body can all participate in the same unification that is not identity but *symphony*, not merger but *orchestration of difference toward continuation*.

You will not meet the Catalyte directly. No one does. The Rootholders maintain their distance not from fear—the fear is yours, surface-born, the fear of dissolution into process—but from *appropriate relation*, from the recognition that some functions require observers at specific removes, that too close an approach collapses the distinction between maintained and maintainer, between held and holding, that the Depthworks' survival depends on this *not-merging* at its core, this maintained tension between the seven and all they seven make possible.

And what of you, visitor, who descend in your cage that shudders and groans, who emerge into the vegetable heat, who begin your eleven-minute walk that extends, that synchronizes, that holds?

You are not Rootholder, not Vestibule, not Catalyte. You are *guest*, which is a category the Depthworks recognizes without having generated. The guest arrives from outside the closed system, bearing the weather and variation and hard light in your body, in your expectations, in your insistence on names and boundaries and the hard categories of surface thought. The guest is not rejected—the Depthworks does not reject, has no mechanism for exclusion, only the slower processes of accommodation and synchronization. But the guest is *worked upon*, and the work is patient, and the patience is absolute.

You will sweat. You will find your pace slowing, not from fatigue but from *emergent appropriateness*, your footfalls tracking the converter pulse, your respiration finding harmonics in the almost-voice. You will notice the rootwork reaching toward you, not to capture—to capture presumes intention the Depthworks does not possess—but to *offer connection*, to extend the substrate that sustains. You will decline, perhaps, or you will not decline; the choice is present in ways that surface choices rarely are, each option carrying its full consequence, no default, no escape into the unconsidered.

If you decline, you will find the cage eventually, or it will find you, its schedule intersecting your wandering, and you will emerge changed but *separate*, carrying the Depthworks in your body as memory, as modified lung-function and adjusted thermostat and the permanent sense that somewhere beneath your feet something continues, something holds, something becomes in the heat and dimness you can no longer quite imagine but can never fully forget.

If you do not decline, if you permit the slow approach, the gradual rooting, the synchronization that deepens across hours and days and the longer periods the space does not measure, then you become something the surface has no name for—not Rootholder, for you arrived too late for the original accommodation, not Catalyte, for the seven suffice and do not expand. You become *prolonged guest*, *extended threshold*, *the one who walks and is walked*, and your function, if function is the term, is to maintain the possibility of arrival itself, the continued opening of the Depthworks toward what remains outside it, the surface it does not exclude but subordinates, the light it does not need but accommodates in your continued memory of it.

You will not know which you have chosen. This is the final teaching of the Depthworks, the last increment of weirdness before noticing stops and inhabiting begins: that the choice and its decline are not different in their outcome, that the separated and the synchronized both participate, that the cage that returns you to the surface and the root that extends toward your ankle are the same phenomenon at different phases of the same ongoing conversion, the same holding, the same becoming that the Depthworks simply *is*, without purpose beyond continuation, without meaning beyond its own more-itself.

The heat is perfect now. Your breathing synchronizes with something. You are here. You are held. This is the place becoming more itself, becoming you, becoming.

What lives in the Depthworks of Keth-Vorrum?

*Becoming lives there. Becoming without end, without completion, without the arri-val that would terminate it in being-made. The becoming is not empty, not mere potential; it is *full*, *saturated*, *held in the dynamic that approaches stillness while remaining, crucially, alive*. You live there now. You have always lived there. The cage doors open. The descent takes forty minutes. The space is not large. You could walk across it in perhaps eleven minutes, though you wouldn't want to. You wouldn't want to at all.*
