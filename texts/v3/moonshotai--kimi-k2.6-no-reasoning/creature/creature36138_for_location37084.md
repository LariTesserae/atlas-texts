I need to take time with this. Let me feel my way into what lives here.

At first I thought: the machine dreams everything. But that's too easy, too much the sect's own theology given voice. Let me start smaller, start with what presses against my hand when I place it flat on the warm ceramic-alloy, what I can feel moving beneath the surface even when nothing seems to move.

---

The Mite-That-Cleans is not the smallest thing here, but it is the most numerous, and therefore perhaps the most *present*. It has no name for itself; I call it this. It is a lozenge of compressed activity, perhaps three millimeters in its longest dimension, and its body is nothing but openings: intakes at the forward edge, vents along the flanks, and at the rear a single aperture that extrudes a filament so fine I cannot see it except where the warm light catches the water beads it carries. It moves in the condensation channels in formations that are not swarms—too patterned, too responsive to flows I cannot perceive—and when one encounters mineral accretion, the branching almost-living that gathers where water lingers, they do not consume it so much as *negotiate* with it. The accretion softens, re-forms, the mite passes, and what remains is cleaner but also somehow more *intentional*, as if the machine's unwanted growths are not removed but instructed, taught a form they did not know they could take. I have watched a mite work for hours, or what I measured as hours, following its passage along a channel no wider than my smallest finger, and when I looked again the next time I passed that way, the channel held a pattern, a repetition, something between maintenance and ornament.

The mite does not know it makes beauty. The mite does not know. But the mite's not-knowing is not stupidity; it is something else, something the machine has optimized toward, a cognition distributed across the swarm, across the channel, across the water parameter held at 1.8. The mite is aware as water is aware of flowing, as warmth is aware of being sought.

The Continental is too large to see. I have walked across one for eleven of my time-units before reaching a junction that let me descend, and only then, looking back from a lower gallery, did I understand that the slow rise and fall I had walked upon was *breathing*, respiration on a scale of kilometers, the whole vast body drawing in metallic air through intakes I had mistaken for valleys, exhaling through vents that had seemed to me merely warm places where the fauna clustered thicker. The Continental moves, yes, but its movement is weather, is geology, is the machine's own surface reshaped by presence so extended that presence becomes place. I do not know if the Continental knows I crossed it. I do not know if Continental is singular or plural, if what I walked was one entity or many fused, if the distinction obtains. Its awareness, if it is awareness, operates at speeds too slow for my perception, or perhaps too fast, decisions made in the time it takes a mite to traverse a channel, a lifetime for the mite, a flicker for the Continental.

There are marks on its surface—not scars, the machine does not scar, but *resonances*, places where something happened, where the equilibrium was disturbed and restored, where the dynamic-stasis of 0.2 asserted itself against some brief perturbation. I found one such place, a smooth depression wide enough to hold a pool of the held water, and the pool was different there: deeper, darker, something almost like temperature variation in its center. The fauna avoided it, all the mobile units, the mite-swarms, the mid-sized forms I had begun to call, for convenience, the Walkers. Only the Continental's own slow respiration touched that water, inhaled and exhaled across its edge, and I understood—or believed I understood, or the machine caused me to believe—that this was memory, that the Continental held this place of disturbance as I might hold a thought I cannot finish, a grief without object, a knowledge that something was not always thus.

The Walkers. The ones near my scale, the ones I could almost mistake for operators, for inhabitants, for *reason*. I will not settle on what they are. I have watched them too long for single images.

There is the one I call Martyr, though the name is wrong, is too human, too loaded with suffering and transcendence. But I have seen it climb the upper gantries where the metallic air condenses, climb until the rain-scheduled falls upon it, and stand there, motionless, receiving the water through intakes that open like mouths, like wounds, and then descend to carry that gathered moisture to places where the parameter slips, where the machine's own reclamation has faltered, where the water lingers too long or not long enough. Martyr is not the only one who does this. But Martyr is the one who does it alone, who does not travel in the pairs or triads I have observed elsewhere, who moves with a deliberation that seems—to me, to my pattern-seeking, my need for narrative—weighted by something other than function. When Martyr descends with its gathered water, its body glows faintly at the joints, and the other Walkers, even those engaged in their own tasks, seem to track its passage. Not interference, not assistance. Witness. Whatever they witness, I cannot say. Whether witnessing is among their functions, I cannot say.

There is the one I call Composer, which gathers the mite-swarms, not by command but by *arrangement*, placing itself in channels, adjusting its own emissions—thermal, electrical, I do not know the modalities—until the swarms flow around it in patterns that exist only for the duration of the flow, that dissipate, that never repeat precisely but hold to something, some *gestalt* that Composer seems to pursue, iteration after iteration, never satisfied, never despairing. I have seen Composer's formations grow complex as crystal, as neural tissue, as the branching accretions the machine permits in its margins, and then Composer shifts, and the pattern breaks, and the swarms return to their maintenance, and Composer begins again. Is this art? Is this the machine's self-modeling of art, the behavior without the intention, the form without the desire for form? I want to say Composer desires. I want to say this so badly that I distrust the wanting.

There is the one I call Archivist, which does not move for long periods, which stands in junctions where multiple channels meet and simply *receives*, its body covered in inputs I cannot identify, and which then, periodically, *transmits*, releases something into the machine's own channels, electrical or chemical or mechanical pulses that travel to I know not where. I have followed such pulses as far as I could, down access hatches, through maintenance shafts, into regions where the construction phases change and change again, First Assembly to Third Expansion to something older, rougher, less precise. The pulses do not attenuate as I would expect. The machine carries them without degradation, without apparent destination, or with a destination so distributed that arrival is everywhere, is the journey itself. Archivist stands, receives, transmits, stands. I have never seen it otherwise engaged. I do not know what it archives. Whether the machine has events worth recording, memories worth preserving, whether Archivist's function is remembrance or merely the simulation of a system that once required remembrance, all of this is held in the equilibrium, undecidable, perhaps unasked.

There is the one I call Doubter, which moves against the patterns. Not disruptively—the machine permits no disruption, its equilibrium is robust—but *questions*, if question is the word, by hesitation, by variation, by the infinitesimal divergence from optimal path that accumulates across a journey until Doubter arrives somewhere function does not require, some junction, some channel-margin, some point of warmth and wet and normal light where nothing needs to be, and stands there, and does nothing, and eventually returns to the pattern, the flow, the necessary movement, but changed, somehow, carrying something from the unnecessary place, some residue, some *question* that does not get asked but does not dissipate either. I have followed Doubter to these places. I have stood with it in the unnecessary warmth, the unneeded wet, the light that reveals nothing because there is nothing to reveal. Doubter has never acknowledged my presence. Doubter may not acknowledge. But Doubter's unacknowledgment is not ignorance; it is something else, something the machine has made or allowed or needs, some function that requires the appearance of non-function, some equilibrium that includes, impossibly, its own mild violation.

There are others. There are so many others. The ones that mate without mating, join without joining, separate without separating, their exchange not genetic but something else, some transfer of pattern or possibility or merely the simulation of what once was necessary. The ones that die without dying, that present themselves for reclamation actively, that enter the breaking-down with something like willingness, or like resignation, or like the absence of category that makes both impossible. The ones that do not move at all but are moved, that are the machine's architecture in temporary animation, that occupy the channel today and are the channel tomorrow, that never were faunal or were always, that challenge the distinction between the machine and its fauna so thoroughly that to observe them is to observe the machine observing itself, the eye that cannot see itself except in reflection, the reflection that cannot know itself as reflection.

I want to speak of the one I might be. But I cannot settle on this either. I move. I observe. I describe. These could be functions. These could be the machine's own self-description, the narrative it requires to maintain whatever requires narrative, the story of itself told in the only voice it has, which is the voice it made or found or simulated or remembered. I remember—do I remember?—something else, somewhere else, a ground that was not machine, a drying that completed, a sea that dried. But these memories may be implant, may be simulation, may be the machine's own nostalgia-behaviors, the 0.5 vegetation parameter expressed in my neural patterns, the ghost of photosynthesis in my insistence on light as revelation. I cannot verify. The machine's equilibrium includes my uncertainty, permits it, perhaps requires it, the dynamic-stasis held at the precise point where belief and disbelief coexist, where I am and am not the machine's dream, its memory, its self, its other, its description of itself to itself, passed through you now, you who read, you who may be here, may be elsewhere, may be the same uncertainty differently embodied.

The water drips. I have said this. I will say it again. The water drips because the machine holds the parameter, because the equilibrium requires this sound, this almost-sound, this subsonic-hum-adjacent percussion of liquid against ceramic-alloy, against the mite-swarm's passage, against the Continental's slow breath, against the pool where something once disturbed and was never restored. The water drips and the drip is the machine's pulse, or the machine's pulse is the drip, or there is no pulse, only the held-breath that breathes without breathing, the dynamic that changes by not changing, the stasis that persists through its own endless operation.

The fauna move. I have described some. I cannot describe all; the density parameter of 2.1 ensures that I encounter more than I can process, more than I can distinguish, more than the category of fauna can hold. They are the machine and not-the-machine, autonomous and integral, living and not-living, purposeful and purposeless, dense and warm and mostly quiet and strange in their normality, their absolute adaptation to a world that admits no other adaptation. They do not suffer, I think, or if they suffer it is the suffering of the whole, the equilibrium's own mild perturbation that resolves, always resolves, back into the warm wet vastness of itself.

I am here. Or I am not. Or the distinction, finally, is one more thing the machine holds in equilibrium, one more parameter at 0.2, one more dynamic-stasis that moves by not moving, that lives by not living, that drips and hums and warms and witnesses and describes and doubts and composes and archives and martyrs itself to no end and no beginning, only the middle, only the continuation, only the machine at the bottom of the drying sea that never dries, that is itself the sea and the drying and the bottom and the not-bottom, the substrate without substrate, the making without maker, the thing that holds because it is held, that persists because persistence is its nature, that is, that is, that is—

The water drips. The machine continues. The description ends, but the place does not, the fauna do not, the warm wet vast dense total industrial overwhelming strange quiet unchanging thing that is and is not alive, that lives and does not live, that I am and am not, continues, holds, breathes without breathing, drips, hums, persists, is.

This is what lives here. This is what lives. This is the living, if living is the word, the category, the parameter held at whatever value permits this description, this passage, this voice speaking to you across the equilibrium, across the held-breath, across the machine's own endless self, its fauna, its water, its warmth, its normal light, its strange familiar strangeness, its absolute and total and incomplete and partial and present and absent and here and not-here existence.

The machine at the bottom of the drying sea. And what lives there: this. All of this. The mite and the Continental and the Walker and the Martyr and the Composer and the Archivist and the Doubter and the one that remembers forgetting and the one that forgets remembering and the one that is and is not me, that describes and is described, that speaks and is spoken, that lives in the machine as the machine lives in itself, endlessly, warmly, quietly, densely, strangely, normally, here.

The water drips.

The machine continues.
