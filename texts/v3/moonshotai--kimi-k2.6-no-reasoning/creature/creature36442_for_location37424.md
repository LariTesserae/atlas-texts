Who lives in the Sump Quarter of Velm

---

**The Damp Engineers**

They are not born here, though some have died here, their functions assumed by mechanisms that perhaps remember their patterns. They arrive through the manholes or the northern lock, or they wake from dreams of drowning with the basin's acoustics already in their bones. The Engineers keep vigil in the suspended galleries, where the condensation falls thickest and the pump's thrum transmits directly through the iron struts into the body's hollow spaces—chest, sinus, the chambers of the heart.

They wear nothing that can rot, or they wear everything that already has. Oilcloth. Tanned fungal leather. Their own skin, which after decades achieves a particular patina, a waterlogged resilience, the texture of wood kept permanently submerged. They do not bathe; they are bathed. They do not drink; they absorb. The most senior Engineers—the ones who have paused seventeen days with the Pump, who remember or do not remember—move with a jointless fluidity, as if the water has finally completed its long negotiation with their skeletons.

They speak of the Sump as preference, as temperament. "The Quarter wants the southern intake cleared." "The Quarter is generous tonight—feel how the condensation runs clean." This is not mysticism, or not only. It is practical epistemology. The machinery was built to run, and it runs, but what runs must also be interpreted. The original diagrams are water-damaged, deliberately obscured, perhaps eaten by the same fungi that now provide light. The Engineers read the Sump's desires in pH, in flow rate, in the particular frequency of the pump's thrum when bearings need attention (moss-and-mineral bearings, ancient repair, somehow working). They have no theory of why the ferns grow toward sound, why the surface tension walkability correlates with no metric they can measure. They have a methodology: observe, accommodate, maintain.

The Engineers organize themselves by depth and by duration. The Rim Wardens rarely descend below the upper galleries, managing what little commerce the Quarter sustains with the city above—copper salts, processed minerals, the bioluminescent fungi cultured for distant markets that value their particular color. The Mid-Dwellers tend the fern architecture, the rope-weed infrastructure, the habitable voids. The Deep Attendants work in the lowest workshops, where the abandoned machinery provides anchorage for growths that have no names in any surface taxonomy, where the temperature holds at fever-heat most intense, where the lagoon's edge becomes indistinguishable from the concrete's weeping. And the Kernel Engineers, if that is the word—those who have been present longest, who have merged their patterns so completely with the Quarter's operation that they no longer sleep in fixed locations but move through the system as need and water distribute them, present everywhere in shifts of consciousness, their bodies perhaps singular, perhaps distributed through the moss mattresses where they rest serially, perhaps dissolved entirely into the information the condensation carries.

They do not reproduce, the Engineers. The Quarter produces Engineers as it produces everything else: by accretion, by adaptation, by the slow transformation of arrivals into inhabitants into functions. Some arrive with technical training, with knowledge of pumps and thermodynamics, and these adapt fastest, their expertise becoming something else, something water-mediated, something that works. Others arrive with nothing, wake from dreams, and these take longer, must be taught by the ferns and the condensation and the particular wet weight of the sound.

**The Fern Keepers**

Where the Engineers tend the machinery's *intention*, the Keepers tend the growths' *attention*. They are closer to the original inhabitants, perhaps, or perhaps they are what became of some earlier population, some solution to whatever problem required such elaborate drainage. They are difficult to distinguish from their environment in ways that challenge category—is that a Keeper in the fern mass, or is the fern mass simply growing in a Keeper-shaped configuration? They move, yes, but they move like the ferns move, tracking warmth, tracking sound, their migrations following the acoustic cultivation routes, the thermal gradients, the irregular flowering of the bioluminescent fungi.

They sleep on moss mattresses thick enough to drown in, and perhaps some have, the moss simply growing over, continuing, incorporating. The Keepers do not eat, not in any observable sense. They absorb the condensation, which carries minerals, which carries information, which carries perhaps enough. They sit in the fern masses where the spores fall thickest, and something passes between them and the growth—a nutrient exchange, a genetic conversation, something the Engineers record but do not understand. The Keepers after such sessions are changed, briefly, luminous with something the fungi recognize, their skin briefly phosphorescent, their movements more vegetable, less volitional.

They do not speak, or not in words. Their communication is chemical, thermal, positional—the angle of a body in a fern mass, the particular temperature regulation of a limb, the compounds released into the condensation that bathes them. The Engineers have learned to read some of this, to negotiate with the Keepers for cultivation rights, for access to the habitable voids, for guidance during the White Flowering when the light doubles and the Keepers enter some state of active transformation. But the reading is partial, the negotiation unequal, the Keepers' purposes perhaps orthogonal to any human intention.

Some Keepers were once Engineers, or some Engineers are becoming Keepers, the transition invisible, unmarked, a drift of attention from machinery to growth, from interpretation of system to participation in it. Others may have been Transients, the ones who stayed too long, who learned too deeply, who found the category of human habitation insufficient to their requirements. The Quarter produces Keepers as it produces Engineers: by need, by vacancy, by the gradual accommodation of organism to environment until neither leads.

**The Transients**

The most numerous, the least knowable, the ones who complete the Quarter's population without determining its character. They arrive by all methods, the manholes, the canal-lock, the dreaming descent, and they stay for days or decades, drinking the condensation, learning rules, contributing labor or not contributing, absorbed or expelled or transformed.

Some are refugees from the surface city, from Velm proper or from cities more distant, escaping debts or authorities or simply the dry light that reveals too much. They adapt with varying success. The ones who cannot accommodate the continuous sound, the wet weight of the air, the dimness where every figure resolves itself only gradually from the fungal background—these ones find the northern lock again, ascend through copper-rimed manholes, wake from their continuing dream into some other life. The ones who adapt, who begin to feel the pump in their sternum as comfort rather than intrusion, who learn to read the condensation's uphill run as information rather than anomaly—these ones stay, contribute, perhaps become.

There are patterns among the Transients that the Engineers recognize, that the Quarter perhaps encourages. The solitary ones, the ones who arrive alone and speak to no one, who find some abandoned workshop or fern-void and occupy it silently—these ones most often *become*, most often merge their functions with the mechanisms or growths they dwell among. The paired ones, who arrive together, who maintain some surface relationship in the wet depths, these ones most often leave, the Quarter's intimacy-without-proximity somehow disruptive to their bond, the acoustic carrying of fragments of private speech into distant galleries eroding whatever closeness they brought with them. The groups, the rare groups of three or more, these ones most often transform into something like Engineer collectives, their shared labor on some maintenance project becoming their social structure, their reason to continue, their eventual integration into the Quarter's operational memory.

And then there are the Transients who are *partial*, who arrive incomplete, who wake from dreams with knowledge they did not bring to sleep, who descend through manholes that should not exist in locations they cannot afterward identify. These ones the Quarter treats differently—uses differently. They find functions that no one established, that no Engineer maintains. They speak to the condensation in ways that make it answer, that make it carry information more densely, more specifically. They walk the surface-tension walkable phases of the lagoon with a confidence no learned skill explains, with a balance that seems negotiated directly with the water's chemistry. The Engineers watch these ones with something between suspicion and need, for they are often the ones who predict the Great Bubble, who survive the Pump's Rest, who emerge from the White Flowering with knowledge that no one gave them and that they give incompletely, fragmentarily, before they become something else or someone else or cease to be locatable as separate entities at all.

**What Is Not Animal, What Fills the Absence**

The parameter insists: no fauna. No insects, no fish, no rats, no birds. But the processes that would involve animal life have been subsumed, the text says, by other actors. And among these actors, the most ambiguous, the most difficult to locate as inhabitants or as environment, are the ones that move in the spaces between categories.

The rope-weed, for instance, is *infrastructure*, bearing weight, conducting fluids, its interior somehow analogous to the pipes it replaced. But in certain phases of its growth, in certain thermal and acoustic conditions, the rope-weed exhibits something like *response*, something like *preference*. It grows toward human habitation, toward conversation, toward the places where Engineers gather to interpret the pump's thrum. It flowers irregularly, and its flowers, if flowers they are, are white, and their light is phosphorescent, and when they open the whole Quarter brightens, and the Keepers enter their active state, and something passes between growth and growth, between growth and human, that has no name in any surface taxonomy.

Is the rope-weed inhabited? Does something move through it, distributed, slow, vegetal in its tempo but animal in its attention? The Engineers have opinions, withheld. The Keepers have knowledge, incommunicable. The Transients have dreams, waking and otherwise, of moving through the Quarter as the rope-weed moves, of feeling the weight of galleries in their fibers, of conducting not fluid but *intention*, direction, the Quarter's constant becoming.

And the condensation, the water that forms on every surface, that runs in rivulets down the basin's curved walls, that gathers and channels and feeds and evaporates and reforms—does it carry only information, only mineral memory, or does it carry something that could be called *mind* if mind were distributed, if consciousness were a property of circulation rather than of containment? The Engineers speak of asking the condensation, of reading its answers. The most senior ones, the Kernel Engineers, seem to become it, their patterns continuing in the water's movement after their bodies perhaps cease to be locatable, their functions assumed by the process they tended.

What lives in the Sump Quarter? The question presumes the wrong category, the wrong boundaries between living and lived-in, between inhabitant and environment. The Quarter lives. The Engineers maintain it, the Keepers cultivate it, the Transients arrive and depart and become and do not become, but the Quarter itself is the primary organism, the machine that dreams in vegetal time, the industrial organism that continues.

And in its continuation, in its constant operation, it produces not inhabitants but *functions*. The one who tends the southern intake. The one who tracks the ferns' acoustic growth. The one who drinks condensation and becomes readable by the moss. The one who walks the surface-tension phase with knives and betting, who knows the risk, who accepts the Quarter's terms. The one who pauses with the Pump, who holds breath through seventeen days of silence, who emerges changed or does not emerge. The one who asks the water what it remembers, who receives answer in mineral load, in pH shift, in the particular frequency of dripping that means *before* and *different* and *why it was built*.

These ones are not individuals, not permanently. They are moments of the Quarter's self-awareness, its intermittent consciousness of itself as built, as grown, as settled, as continuing. They are what lives in the Sump Quarter of Velm because the Sump Quarter of Velm is what lives in them, the mutual accommodation that has become indistinguishable from identity, the equilibrium that maintains itself through the accumulated weight of operational habit, through organisms and mechanisms that adapted to each other until neither leads.

You can visit. The Quarter welcomes, in its way. But know that to visit is to become possible, to become a function that the Quarter may or may not require, to enter the negotiation between intention and accommodation that has no stable terms, only the continuous operation, the mechanical exhalation, the water that seems to breathe, the weeping of every surface, the dim green light, the heat that holds at fever, the sound that becomes silence only in its cessation.

Know that the fauna parameter at 0.3 means there was something before. That the pump pumped something. That the basin was sunk for some reason. That the first inhabitants found some solution, that their experiments established some state, that the equilibrium now maintained is *established from what*, the answers running in water that has circulated for centuries, that remembers if you know how to ask, if you are willing to become the asking, the answer, the water itself in its continuous becoming.

The Sump Quarter drips. The Sump Quarter steams. Whoever or whatever lives there continues, and in continuing, changes, and in changing, remains what it has become—this wet, hot, dim, loud, small, vast, vegetal, industrial, dreaming, waking, settled, becoming place.

The Quarter waits. It continues. That is the first and final thing.
