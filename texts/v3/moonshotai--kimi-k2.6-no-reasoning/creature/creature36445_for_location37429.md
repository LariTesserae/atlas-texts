What lives here?

They have no name for themselves, because naming requires standing outside and pointing in, and nothing here stands outside. Let me call them the Occupied, though they would not recognize this any more than the city recognizes itself as city. They are the ones the bowl grew, or the ones who grew into the bowl's growing—causality itself becomes damp here, its sharp edges softened by constant seep.

They are small. Not small as mice are small, with the nervous quickness of creatures who live in worlds of larger presences. Small as pebbles are small, as root-tips are small: the appropriate scale for a world built of moss-cushioned inches and water-ribboned crevices. Their bodies are the color of wet limestone, of fungal mycelium, of the particular green that exists only where light has passed through living cell after living cell and emerged stripped of everything but persistence. They have no fur, no feathers, no scales in the conventional sense. Their skin—translucent, webbed, the color of things that have never seen sun directly—bears instead a perpetual fine moisture that is not sweat, not secretion, but participation: they are part of the water's route through the city, nodes in its circulation, and their wetness is the wetness of the place passing through them, taking what it needs, leaving what they need, an exchange without transaction.

Their limbs are variable in number, which is to say that the category of "limb" does not quite apply. They extend and retract projections according to need: broad pads for the water-veins, hooked filaments for the woven chambers, flattened gripping surfaces for the vertical passages where seep has dissolved channels through stone. These projections are not tools, not technologies in the 0.1 sense. They grow and are reabsorbed, slower than the flick of a chameleon's tongue, faster than the seasonal leafing of any tree. A traveler between the water-table's edge and the higher, drier chambers might pass individuals in mid-transformation, the body reconfiguring for new terrain, new occupation, new niche in the packed density where every surface must be usable and every usable surface is used.

They do not sleep, not as warm creatures sleep, not as the collapse into unconsciousness that defines the day's end. Their metabolism runs at the steady 0.4 cold, the perpetual autumn pace that knows no season's urgency. They rest by slowing further, by becoming more like the stone they occupy, their chemical signals thinning to the faintest pulse, their water-participation reduced to the minimum maintenance of cell against cell. In this near-stillness they are indistinguishable from the city's own processes, from the seep and the fungal growth and the slow brightening and fading of bioluminescent territory-marking. They rest as the city rests, which is to say they do not rest at all but continue at a pace that observation cannot distinguish from cessation.

They eat the city, which is to say they eat what the city grows and processes and circulates. The fungi that load-bear are also food; the moss that cushions is also food; the water that veins everything carries dissolved nutrients from every metabolism to every other metabolism, and they drink this, filter this, become this circulating substance. They are not separate from the city's economy but are its economy, the nodes where transfer becomes transformation, where the seep's carrying becomes the body's being becomes the seep's carrying again. They excrete into the water-veins, and this excretion is already processed, already part of the flow, already nutrient for the next in the packed sequence. Nothing is waste because waste requires a category of unwanted, and here nothing is unwanted, nothing is outside the circuit of use.

They reproduce, or the city reproduces through them, or reproduction is another of the dampened distinctions. The young emerge from water-warmed chambers near the water-table's edge, where metabolism concentrates, where the packed density achieves its maximum and something must give, must separate, must become new node in the circuit. They emerge already participating, already wet, already the color of wet limestone and filtered green. They do not learn; they continue. The chemical signals that are identity, that are recognition without naming, pass to them through the water, through touch, through the vibration in stone that communicates what is and what is occupied and what is becoming occupied. They grow by finding their fit, their surface, their air-pocket and water-vein and moss-cushioned niche in the absolute density that has no room and therefore infinite room for what matches its shape.

They die, or they become the city more fully, or the distinction is another casualty of the damp. The old, the slow, the ones who have found their final configuration and no longer transform—these do not cease but slow further, become the stone-like rest that is indistinguishable from rest, from the city's own resting pace, until the water's slow dissolution finds them, until they become the seep, the nutrient, the substance that builds the new chambers, the new occupation, the new young emerging into the cold wet green. They are not buried because burial requires removal, requires a place apart for the dead, and here there is no apart, no removal possible in the absolute density. They remain, participating differently, their chemistry still signaling, still communicating identity in the continuous present, still part of the recognition without naming that holds the city together more surely than any mortar.

They are aware, though "aware" is perhaps too sharp, too defined, for what they are. They sense the water's movement through their bodies and through the stone, the vibration of others' presence, the chemical trails that map occupation and availability and the slow transformations of self. They sense the predators in the 0.3 silence, the wet breathing that syncopates, the mandibles closing on moth-bodies that they themselves do not eat but that they sense being eaten, that they participate in through the water's circulation of nutrient and information. They sense the bowl's rim, the colder blue, the sky they have never seen directly but know as difference, as the source of the light that filters and diffuses, that drives the photosynthesis that drives the moss that drives the fungi that drives the circuit that they are.

They do not wonder. They do not ask what lives here, what this place is, whether it is city or non-city or something the categories fail to hold. They continue, at the steady pace, in the cold wet green, packed so full of presence that absence becomes impossible, that the visitor's category error dissolves into the seep before it can be formulated, that the question of what lives here becomes, finally, the wrong question, or rather a question that can only be answered by becoming the answer, by participating in the continuous present until the present's continuity absorbs the questioner into the wet, the green, the packed and active and urbanly alive.

What lives here is the city's not-knowing itself, the way the water moves through without knowing it moves, the way the moss grows without knowing it grows, the way the bioluminescence pulses without knowing it pulses, mapping nothing but presence, persistence, the fact of packed life continuing in conditions that elsewhere would read as void. What lives here is the occupation without occupier, the use without user, the city that is not a city because it has never stood outside itself to name itself, because it has never needed to, because the naming would be a drying, a warming, a separation from the seep and the moss and the cold wet green that holds everything, that continues, that waits for the next dissolution, the next chamber, the next bright emergence of space that will be filled before it is fully formed, because filling is what this place does, what it is, what lives here, what is lived here, what continues.
