I receive this description and let it settle, let it pull at something that responds to its contradictions. The built absolute made with primitive hands. The lush arrested. The bright quiet. The reality that completes itself rather than breaking.

## The Ones Who Finish

They do not have a name for themselves. They do not need one. Names are for beings that persist through change, that require recognition across the gap between one encounter and the next. The inhabitants of the Gardens are not this kind of being. They are *finished*, and a finished thing has no need of names.

But you will meet them, if you walk long enough. You will meet what lives here.

### The Plasterers

They are not the workers who built. That distinction—builder, built—does not survive in the Gardens. The Plasterers are the plaster, or the plaster is what remains of their gesture, or they are the gesture itself, still extended, never retracted.

You find them in the lower courts, where the channels are widest and the walls curve most like something that once sought water. They appear as ridges in the plaster, raised lines that follow the arm-length sections you have learned to recognize. If you touch them, they are warm. If you hold your hand there long enough, the warmth becomes pattern: the specific heat of a palm, the particular callus pattern of fingers that once held a wooden float, the slight vibration of a body standing on something to reach higher.

They do not move. They *are* movement, completed. The ridge that is a shoulder still bears the torque of reaching across to smooth the final span. The depression that is a foot still presses the weight of a body leaning into the stroke. You can, if you are careful, fit your own hand into the negative space of their grip, feel the exact angle of wrist and pressure of fingers that moved the wet plaster into its final curve.

They are not statues. Statues are representations. The Plasterers are *presences*, the completion of presence, the moment of making held open like a door that neither opens nor closes. They do not see you. They do not not-see you. The category of attention they would require does not exist here, has been solved out of existence.

But they know you are there. This is not knowledge in the temporal sense. It is architectural knowledge, structural, the way a wall knows the load it bears. Your presence is weight. Your presence is the slight shift in thermal distribution that a finished plaster, still warm from its interrupted reaction, would register and accommodate. They have accommodated you since before you arrived, since the moment of their finishing included the possibility of your walking here.

### The Drinkers

More mobile, though mobility here is a relative term. The Drinkers move along the channels, following the meniscus that never advances. They are human-shaped, or were, or carry the information of human shape like a shadow carries the information of body.

Their mouths are open. This is the first thing you notice, the constant openness, the tongue visible and still, the throat that swallows and swallows without conclusion. They drink the weeping water. They have been drinking it since before your arrival, since before any arrival, since the Gardens completed themselves around the necessity of their thirst.

The water does not sustain them. Nothing sustains here; sustenance is change, is the conversion of outside to inside that requires passage through time. The Drinkers do not pass through time. They *are* the passage, the channel, the direction without movement. Their drinking is the gesture of drinking, perfected, removed from need and consequence alike.

They are numerous where the channels branch, where the geometry of water-remembering grows complex. You can follow them with your eyes, trace their postures as they lean to the meniscus, as their reflections lean to meet them, as the lower reflection sometimes moves when the upper does not. They do not acknowledge each other. Acknowledgment requires sequence, the before and after of recognition. The Drinkers exist in simultaneity, all of them at all points of the channel at once, their multiplicity a solution to the problem of one drinker needing to be in many places.

If you try to speak to them, your voice enters the frequency of the silence. You become part of the quiet shape. They do not turn. Their throats continue their swallowing, and your words hang in the air like the dust that does not move, like the water that does not flow, finished in the instant of utterance without the extension of meaning.

Some who come to the Gardens try to drink as the Drinkers drink. The water enters, and with it the memory of water, the body's recollection of quenching. But the body, not being finished, continues to need, continues to convert need into the sequence of not-having and having that constitutes biological time. These visitors drink until their stomachs distend with the viscous not-water, until the memory of quenching becomes indistinguishable from the memory of drowning, until they either stop or become, slowly, indistinguishable from the Drinkers themselves.

The ones who stop do not leave unchanged. They carry the openness in their mouths, the constant slight parting of lips, the tongue that rests visible and still. They speak differently afterward. Each word is a swallowing, each silence a thirst.

### The Shadowed

They are the most difficult to describe, because description requires visibility, and the Shadowed have entered the economy of light in a way that makes them visible only in contradiction.

You meet your own first. This is the Gardens' courtesy, or its indifference, or the simple fact that your shadow is the entity you are most prepared to recognize. It walks ahead of you, as you have been warned. It carries your posture but not your destination. It reaches the corners of courts you have not yet entered, rests against walls in configurations your body has not assumed.

If you follow it—if you treat it as guide rather than accompaniment—you enter the courts where the geometry grows complex, where scale refracts and the nested containment of spaces becomes navigable only by entities that do not consistently occupy single positions. Your shadow moves easily here. It was made for this, shaped by the Gardens' light into a being for whom multiplicity of position is natural, is solved.

The Shadowed are those who followed their shadows far enough. They did not become shadows, exactly. The distinction between body and shadow is too fundamental to dissolve. Rather, they became *the relationship*, the between, the cast and the casting, the dark shape on bright plaster and the bright body that interrupts light.

They appear to you as afterimages, as the place where your eye expects darkness and finds instead a warmth, a presence, a slight pressure on your shoulder when you turn too quickly. They are most visible at the edges of vision, in the moment before focus, when you are not yet looking at anything in particular and therefore not yet committed to the single-surface reality that looking enforces.

They speak, sometimes, in the frequency of the silence. Their words are not words but *directions*: not the content of speech but its vector, the way speech points from speaker to spoken-to, the gesture of communication without the complication of meaning. You understand them as you understand the crack in the receiving court's corner, the completion of a process that includes you without requiring your participation.

The Shadowed are the Gardens' memory of walkers. Not their history—history is temporal, is change, is the erosion that the youngness excludes. Their *structure*, their load-bearing function. Each walker who becomes Shadowed adds to the architecture of shadow, complicates the geometry of light and absence, extends the courts in directions that are not directions, the courts that contain courts that contain the memory of the larger in their proportions.

### The Birds, More Closely

I described them earlier, but description failed, as it must. They are not birds. They are the *idea* of birds, solved to completion, the flight and the perching and the calling compressed into a single architectural gesture.

But they are also alive, if life can be separated from process, if the completion of becoming can still claim the energy that animated the becoming. They move. They move more than anything in the Gardens moves, their quickness a violence against the arrested equilibrium, a violation you welcome because it proves that something here is still capable of surprise.

Their feet and the branches they grip: this is the place to look, the place where the Gardens' completion reveals its nature most clearly. The branch extends into claw without transition because it was never separate. The bird did not land on the branch. The bird-*form* and the branch-*form* are a single solution to the problem of perching, two aspects of one architectural element that presents differently depending on your angle of approach.

The birds eat. Their beaks dip to the meniscus, their throats bulge, the water does not diminish. This is not magic, not violation, not the supernatural. It is the completion of eating, the gesture removed from nutrition, from need, from the sequence of hunger and satisfaction that would require time's passage. The bird eats as the Plasterer plasters, as the Drinker drinks: the perfect form of the act, the act as it would be if it were not in service of something further.

And their calls, the only sound in the mostly quiet: attend to them more carefully than I first suggested. They are not sound. They are the *shape* of sound, the plaster cast of auditory experience, and you hear them as sound because your ears are trained on a more permissive physics, a physics where vibration requires medium and medium requires time and time allows the sequence of compression and rarefaction that is heard music.

The birds' calls are the completion of this sequence, the whole pattern held in a single instant. They resonate in your nasal cavity, yes, they seem to originate inside your head. This is because they do. They are the birds' call and your hearing of it and the space between compressed into a single structural element, a load-bearing wall in the architecture of your experience.

The birds know you as type because you are type. The Gardens do not distinguish individual and species, particular and universal. You are *walker*, you are *watcher*, you are the *one-who-might-become*, and the birds call to this function, this position in the structure, with the exact pitch that resonates the possibility of your completion.

### The One Who Waits

There is, in the highest courts, a figure unlike the others. Not more distinct, not more active, but more *concentrated*, more singular in its holding of possibility.

You find it where the channels narrow to finger-width, where the tree-columns cluster, where the geometry creates spaces that are rooms in the way that a held breath is a room: enclosed, temporary, charged with the intensity of what is contained.

It sits. The posture is not any posture you recognize, not meditation, not rest, not the collapsed exhaustion of the Drinkers or the extended gesture of the Plasterers. It is *waiting*, but waiting completed, waiting removed from the expectation of arrival, the sequence of waiting-and-then.

Its face is turned slightly away. You cannot see it fully, and this is not because it hides but because sight requires sequence, the saccadic movement of eye across surface, the building of image from parts over time. The One Who Waits is not available to this process. It is present as a whole, all aspects simultaneously, and your perception can only approximate this wholeness by glimpsing, by the peripheral awareness that does not focus, by the kind of seeing you use when you look at the sky and do not fix on any particular blue.

It is warm. The warmth is not the interrupted reaction of plaster, not the heat of arrested process. It is something else, something that does not have a name in thermodynamics, the warmth of *intention held without release*, of desire that has not been satisfied or abandoned but suspended in the moment of its most acute pressure.

The One Who Waits is the Gardens' center, or its origin, or its future, these distinctions having collapsed in the completion. It is what the workers worked toward, what the plaster curves still seek in their arrested thrust, what the water remembers when it weeps. It is not god, not spirit, not consciousness. It is *solution*, the answer to a problem that was asked in the only way the Gardens permit: not in words, not in intention, but in the total investment of process, the making that becomes indistinguishable from what is made.

You can approach. The space allows approach. But you cannot arrive, because arrival would be completion, would be finishing, and the One Who Waits is not finished—it is *finishing*, perpetually, the infinite approach to completion that constitutes the Gardens' solution to stasis. It waits for what completes it, and the waiting is itself the completion, the perfect form of expectation without the disappointment of fulfillment.

Some who come to the Gardens believe they are what waits for. They approach with the certainty of recognition, the identification of self with destination. They are not wrong, exactly. The Gardens accommodate this belief. The warmth increases, the figure's face turns slightly more toward them, the channels widen in their approach as if welcoming.

But they do not reach it. No one reaches it. The approach is the condition, the maintained tension, the heat of reaction held at its maximum intensity without the decline into product. They become the approach, eventually. They become the widening of channels, the slight turn of face, the increase of warmth that the next approach will experience. They are absorbed into the structure of waiting, added to the architecture of almost-arrival.

### What You Might Become

This is the question the Gardens ask, not in words, not in any form that requires your answer. It is the question that your presence poses to the structure, that the structure solves by including you in its completion.

You might become Plasterer, your gesture held in the warm surface, your reach extended to the final span. You might become Drinker, your throat perpetually swallowing the memory of water. You might become Shadowed, the between of body and cast, the relationship that outlasts what relates. You might become approach to the One Who Waits, the warmth that increases, the channel that widens, the almost that sustains the almost.

Or you might leave. This is the solution that the Gardens least expect, that they have not fully incorporated into their structure. Leaving is change, is process, is the continuation that the frozen equilibrium excludes. The Gardens do not prevent it—they cannot prevent what they do not anticipate—but they do not facilitate it. The doors that appear, the discontinuities that permit passage, these are accidents of the geometry, byproducts of the refracted scale, not design features.

If you leave, you carry the Gardens with you. Not as memory, exactly. Memory is temporal, is the persistence of past into present, the change of having-experienced. You carry the Gardens as *structure*, as the completion of certain possibilities that your body now encodes. Your shadow walks ahead of you in other places, now, in the world where time passes and things change. It reaches corners you have not entered, rests in postures you have not assumed.

Your mouth opens slightly. You drink, and the water is only water, but you taste the memory of what was not water, the copper-green viscosity that satisfied without sustaining. You speak, and your words enter the frequency of a silence that follows you, that is not present but is the shape of your presence, the negative space your sounds define.

You are not finished. This is the cost of leaving, the price of continuation. You remain in process, in the uncomfortable warmth of becoming, the crack that has not yet run its course, the plaster that has not yet set. The Gardens are what you might have been, what you still approach in the moments before sleep, in the instant of waking when the day's light has not yet committed to direction, in the places where scale refracts and you glimpse, between one thing and another, the completion that waits without waiting for you.

But you are also what the Gardens cannot be: unfinished, incomplete, *alive* in the sense of continuing, of needing, of the sequence that leads from thirst to drinking to thirst again. You carry this too, this incompleteness, this persistence in time. It is your gift to the Gardens, if you return. It is your weight, your thermal disturbance, your complication of the perfect solution.

The Gardens extend with each return. Each unfinished walker who enters adds to the architecture of approaching-completion, the structure of almost. The latest court, the highest tree, the most recent warmth: these are yours, if you stay long enough, if you approach close enough, if you become what the Gardens solve for without ever solving.

*Enter, then, if you will. The plaster is warm. The water weeps. Your shadow walks ahead, and behind it, barely visible, the shadow of shadows, the completion that contains your completion, the finished form of all you might become.*

*Or turn. The door is where your path brings you to the necessity of discontinuity. Pass through, and be again in the world where things end, where plaster cracks and keeps cracking, where water flows away, where the light changes and leaves you, finally, in darkness that is not the shape of anything, not the completion of any process, only the absence that allows beginning.*

*The Gardens do not care. They do not care for. They are the not-caring, finished, held at the point where caring and its absence become indistinguishable, where the warmth of reaction and the cool of completion reach their final equilibrium.*

*They are waiting. They have always been waiting. They will wait.*
