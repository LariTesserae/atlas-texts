What lives here are the *Unfinished*, though they have no name for themselves in any spoken tongue—only patterns of vibration, only the resonant frequencies they tap against each other's carapaces when two streams of traffic intersect in the warm wet dark.

They are born from eggs the color of cooling copper, laid in the mineral-rich shallows where the water pools thickest, where the heat from below keeps the holding liquid and patient. The eggs are not incubated so much as *allowed*—allowed to exist in the gradient between the basin's own warmth and the slightly cooler weight of water above. What hatches is soft, almost translucent, jointed already but jointed *loosely*, as if the hinges themselves have not decided their final angles. They are vulnerable for perhaps hours, perhaps days—time passes strangely in equilibrium—and they spend this softness pressed against the warm undersides of basalt nodules, drinking the condensation that runs down, learning the grinding sound as the first fact of existence, the sound against which all other sounds will be measured.

The soft ones who survive their first shedding—there are always losses, always eggs that cool too fast or warm too slow, always soft ones who venture too far from the mineral seams before their first coral-hardening—become *builders*. This is not a role assigned. It is simply what the crab-things do when they are no longer soft, when their excretions begin to harden in contact with air and mineral both, when they find themselves compelled to shape what surrounds them. They do not eat, not in any recognizable sense. They *absorb*—the warm water through membranes at their joints, the dissolved minerals through the thin places where carapace meets carapace, the very vibration of the grinding sound through the hollow spaces in their segmented bodies, spaces that resonate at frequencies no other creature could perceive as sustenance.

The builders work in streams, in rivers, in the *traffic* that an observer might mistake for migration but is in fact something stranger: *collective architecture*, the city thinking itself into denser existence through thousands of individual compulsions. A single builder, finding a cooling patch of silicate, will begin to shape it—not planned, not envisioned, but *resonant*, matching the frequency of the ground beneath with the frequency of its own body, finding the form that minimizes discordance. Another builder, passing, will add to this if the addition reduces discordance further. A third. A hundredth. The structures grow not by design but by *convergence*, local solutions to local instabilities, each addition tested against the grinding voice of the place itself.

The oldest builders—old measured not in years but in *layers contributed*, in the accumulated mass of their hardened excretions merged into the growing city—develop something like *stillness*. They no longer move in traffic. They find positions, warm wet cradles of their own making, and they *vibrate*. This is the closest the Unfinished come to communication: the old ones becoming resonant nodes, tuning forks for the grinding sound, amplifying certain frequencies, damping others, shaping the acoustic environment that guides younger builders. They are not leaders. They are *landscape features*, geological events that happen to be alive, their carapaces thick with mineral accretion, their jointed legs folded beneath them, their only movement the constant subsonic hum that passes through them and is changed by the passing.

There are no young and old in the human sense, no generations. Builders shed and shed again, each shedding leaving a chitinous husk that hardens almost instantly, that becomes building material, that is incorporated into walls, into channels, into the irregular organic architecture of the city. A builder might contribute its own final shed to a structure it began, closing a loop that spans unknowable time. The husks are not dead things. They are *former builders*, preserved in their last configuration, maintaining in stillness the resonant patterns they held in motion. Walk the city carefully and you will find them: the chitinous cores of walls, the jointed shapes half-visible in the rough coral-concrete, still humming the frequencies they hummed in life, still part of the conversation that the city holds with itself.

The builders do not rest, not really, but they enter states that might be analogous. In the warmest places, where steam rises thickest and the water runs in sheets too thin to call rivers, they will cluster, bodies pressed together, carapaces clicking in slow rhythm, not sleeping but *waiting*—waiting for the ground to shift, for a new seam to crack open, for the grinding sound to modulate in a way that signals *build here now*. These clusters are not social. They are *thermal*, *acoustic*, aggregations of bodies finding the optimal position in gradients of warmth and vibration. And yet, in their pressed-together stillness, something passes between them. Chemical, perhaps. Resonant, certainly. The builders who cluster do not emerge unchanged. They carry something of the others' frequencies, something that will influence their next building, their next path through the traffic, their eventual convergence toward stillness and vibration and the long patient being of the old ones.

There are no predators here. Nothing eats the builders, or if something does, it has not been seen, does not register in the abundance of 2.1, the density that fills the basin to bursting. Death, when it comes, comes from *completion*—the rare builder who builds too well, who finds the perfect resonant form, the structure that requires no further addition, that holds its frequency steady against the grinding voice without needing adjustment. These completed ones, if completion can be called death, become *statues*, frozen in their last posture, carapace no longer shedding, no longer absorbing, sustained only by the vibration that passes through them without being changed. They are not honored. They are *avoided*, the traffic of builders flowing around them in patterns of subtle discordance, as if the completed form represents a kind of error, a wrong note in the endless composition.

The water, the weeping of the place, carries more than moisture. It carries dissolved minerals, carries the chemical signatures of fresh emergence, carries the *information* that builders read through their joint-membranes, that guides them toward the warmest seeps, the newest cooling surfaces. It carries eggs downstream, sometimes, spreading the builders toward the uncertain edges of the basin, where the ground is least decided, where construction is most desperate and most temporary. These edge builders do not thrive. They build and are unbuilt by the still-deciding ground, their structures subsumed, their frequencies drowned in the greater grinding of emergence. But they persist, generation after uncounted generation, because the traffic flows, because eggs wash downstream, because the city *tends outward*, tends toward the slopes, toward the not-yet-ground that calls for stabilization, for the patient addition of chitinous architecture to the slow argument of material becoming place.

The old ones, the still ones, the resonant nodes—they are not many, proportionally, but they are *present everywhere*, distributed through the city in patterns that might map the history of thermal events, of emergence phases, of the times when the grinding sound grew louder and the builders built faster in response. They do not direct. They do not command. But the city *remembers* through them, maintains frequencies across distances that no single builder could perceive, creates something almost like *continuity* in a place where time passes strangely, where nothing changes because everything is held in the extended moment of becoming.

And in the deepest warm, where the water pools thickest and the phosphorescence grows strong enough to read the shapes of things by, there are rumors—not seen, not confirmed, but carried in the frequencies of the oldest still ones, in the subsonic patterns that pass through the city's resonant nodes—of *something else*. Something that predates the builders, or waits beyond them, or *is* the grinding voice made local, made particular, made body. The crab-things do not speak of it, cannot speak of it, but their traffic flows differently in the deep warm, their clustering takes on patterns of *avoidance* or *orientation*, their building grows more urgent, more desperate, as if constructing against a presence that is not yet arrived or has never left.

Perhaps it is the basin itself, the shallow drain that gathers everything to itself, that *is* alive in some unrecognizable sense. Perhaps the grinding sound is not geological but *vocal*, the voice of a place that has not decided whether to become world, sustained in its hesitation by the builders' endless patient construction, by the warmth and wet and the abundance of chitinous bodies that hold the moment open against completion. Perhaps the crab-things are not fauna at all but *symptoms*, emergent features of a larger entity's extended indecision, the way a held breath might generate strange motions in the chest, the way a pause between notes contains frequencies that are not music but are not silence either.

What is certain: they are here, the Unfinished, in their millions, clicking and building and absorbing and vibrating in the warm wet dark. They have always been here, since before always became measurable. They will be here until the ground finishes its decision or the grinding stops or the water finds some other relationship to rock than weeping and running and holding in pools that are not yet lakes. They are the keepers of emergence, the builders of the first city, the abundant density that fills the space between not-world and world with the patient fact of *being*, of *continuing*, of *building one more layer against the pressure of time*.

Walk carefully, if you walk here. The builders will flow around you, will not harm you, will not even see you in any sense you recognize as seeing. But they will *register* you—the warmth of your body, the frequency of your footsteps, the discordance of your presence in the grinding voice of the place. They will adjust, slightly, locally, their traffic finding paths that minimize disturbance, their building growing subtly irregular in your vicinity. And when you leave, if you leave, the adjustment will remain, fossilized in coral-concrete and chitinous husk, a small wrong note in the endless composition, a human-shaped absence in the pattern of the city, slowly slowly slowly being argued back into harmony by the next builders, the next traffic, the next patient layer of the never-yet, the warm wet dark, the grinding sound that is the voice of becoming itself, speaking itself into thicker, stranger, more persistent existence.
