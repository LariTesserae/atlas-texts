What lives in the Hollows of Keth Morran are the **Keth Morrani**—though they would not call themselves that, having no concept of collective naming that extends beyond platform-kin and chamber-section. To themselves, they are simply *the Here*, the people who are present, as distinct from *the Gone* (the dead, the exiled, the vanished into deeper passages) and *the Maybe* (the unborn, the hypothetical, the surface-dwellers who may or may not exist in the same way cricket-song exists).

They are small. Not dwarf-small, though generations of marginal nutrition have pressed them toward compactness, but *contained* small—bodies that have learned to fold into available volumes, shoulders that never broaden beyond ladder-width, voices that operate in the lowest registers of human hearing. Their children are born tiny and remain so longer than surface children, the long childhood of those who cannot afford rapid growth. The oldest among them have faces that seem to have flowed downward slightly, features settling into patterns of perpetual listening, the soft downward cast of eyes evolved for fire-twilight rather than solar glare.

Their skin is the color of things that have never seen the sun—not pale in the romantic sense, but *interrupted*, mottled with the fungal conditions that pass between people in the dense air, marked by the stone-flower dyes that serve as both medicine and decoration, the pigments accumulating in creases and scars over decades until the oldest resemble living maps of the chamber's chemistry. Their eyes are large, disproportionately so, the one feature that has not minimized, black-pupilled, capable of parsing ember-light into sufficient detail for the intricate fiber-work that occupies so many hours. Some of the youngest, born in recent brighter phases, show a faint luminescence in their eye-shine, a phosphorescent adaptation that worries the elders and fascinates the cricket-diviners.

They smell of the chamber, which is to say of *process*—the ongoing bacterial conversation of packed bodies, the mineral tang of stone-milk that dries on skin and is never fully removed, the smoke of fires kept deliberately low and thus incomplete, releasing complex compounds that the surface would call pollution and they call atmosphere. The smell varies by platform-location, by water-access, by proximity to the warm walls, and they read these variations as surface-dwellers read weather, knowing by scent-complexity who has visited whom, which families are thriving, which are in decline.

---

Their society is *accretional*, built up in layers like the unbaked strata around them.

The fundamental unit is the **platform-kin**, rarely more than six individuals sharing a wedge of lashed bone and hide, bound by the practical necessity of heat-conservation and the emotional necessity of recognition in darkness. These micro-families are not stable in the surface sense—partnerships form and dissolve with the flexibility of those who cannot afford permanent rupture, children circulate between related platforms, the injured or aging attach to whichever kin-group can accommodate them. What persists is not the family but the *platform itself*, the physical structure that outlasts its inhabitants and accumulates modifications, repairs, improvements, each generation adding their layer of adaptation to constraints that shift but do not fundamentally change.

Above platform-kin, the **bridge-association** links those who share horizontal connections at a given level, the rope-spanners who must negotiate load-limits and sway-tolerance, who develop mutual insurance against collapse, who recognize each other by the particular creak of their passage. Bridge-associations are practical, economic, sometimes hostile—water rights at a given level are fiercely defended, and the subtle sabotage of neighbor platforms (a rope weakened, a gutter redirected) is common enough to be ritualized, performed and discovered and compensated in cycles that maintain equilibrium without requiring forgiveness.

Higher still, the **section** unites all platforms within a given vertical span, those who share the same water-prediction tables, the same thermal gradient, the same cricket-diviner. Sections compete for the best wall-attachment points, for access to productive stone-flower faces, for the honor of hosting festival fires when the phosphorescence brightens. Section identity is performative, expressed in the patterns of woven hide, the rhythms of work-songs (sung at the threshold of hearing, felt in sternum more than ear), the distinctive smoke-signatures of preferred fuels. Wars between sections are rare, extinction being impossible to localize, but *slow contests* of prestige and population are constant, played out over generations, the winning section slowly expanding into adjacent wall-space, the losing contracting to marginal platforms, cooler, damper, closer to the uncertain lower reaches.

And overarching all, the **chamber-culture**, the shared practices that make the Here recognizably singular: the cricket-listening, the stone-flower harvest, the protocols of quietude, the taboos around the still-growing edges, the songs for the tremor-times, the stories of emergence and incompletion. This is not governance—the Morrani have no chiefs, no councils, no law beyond the immediate negotiation of conflicting needs—but *habitus*, the accumulated solution to the problem of dense coexistence in unfinished space.

---

Their technology is *biological in intention*, even where mineral in material.

The deer-scapula pulleys are not merely functional but *relational*, each component marked with the lineage of the animal that provided it, the hunter who killed it, the platform-kin who processed it, the generations of maintainers who have replaced worn articulations with new bone. A pulley system is thus a kind of ancestry record, a mechanical genealogy, and disputes over its use are simultaneously disputes over history and obligation. The scapulae themselves are selected for particular shapes, particular wear-patterns, particular densities that indicate age and condition at death; the ideal pulley-bone comes from an animal in its seventh season, fully grown but not yet aged, and surface traders who understand this can command premium exchange rates for bones that meet the criteria.

The hide bellows represent a parallel technology of transformation, the conversion of surface-animal into chamber-instrument. Their construction is women's work in most sections, though the operators are typically children, the small-bodied and large-lunged who can sustain the rhythmic compression that keeps fires alive in the oxygen-poor middle altitudes. The bellows-leather must be processed with particular minerals—some traded from surface, some extracted from specific seeps—to achieve the flexibility that survives repeated wetting and drying. A well-made bellows lasts twenty years, a figure known with precision because bellows-birth and bellows-death are among the few events marked by communal acknowledgment, small ceremonies of installation and retirement, the old leather cut into strips for lesser uses, nothing wasted.

Their fiber-work is the most refined technology, the production of cordage and woven materials that make everything else possible. They work with surface fibers when available—hemp, flax, the hair of surface animals—but more commonly with processed sinew, with moss-stems dried and twisted, with the tough vascular tissue of certain fungi, with their own hair saved and accumulated across generations. The finest rope is *three-strand memory*, incorporating fibers from ancestors seven generations back, the mechanical continuity of the material serving as physical argument for cultural continuity. Rope-making is slow, meditative, often performed in the dark or near-dark, the strands known by touch and tension, the finished product tested by ear for the harmonic that indicates balanced construction.

Copper exists but is *scarce-almost-to-meaninglessness*, reserved for the most critical connectors, the pins that join scapula to scapula in high-load applications, the needles that must pierce tough hide, the small blades used in surgery and ritual. Copper-objects outlast their users by orders of magnitude, circulating as *persistent persons*, known by name and history, lent for particular purposes with formal protocols of return. To lose a copper object is catastrophe; to find one, in the deep or the abandoned, is transformation, the sudden acquisition of a new ancestral voice in one's technical conversations.

---

Their relation to the chamber is *not ownership but accompaniment*.

They do not believe they built the Hollows, nor that they were invited. The origin stories vary by section, by family, by individual teller, but common threads emerge: they came from *inside*, from some prior emergence that completed and expelled them; or they came from *elsewhere*, from a surface that was different from the current surface, perhaps the surface of a different emergence; or they have always been *here*, the chamber and its inhabitants co-emergent, neither prior to the other. What they agree upon is that the relationship is *provisional*, contingent on continued listening, continued adaptation, continued respect for the not-yet-completed.

The warm walls, the growing edges, are *sacred-dangerous*, approached only by particular ritual specialists (the meaning of "ritual" and "specialist" varying), marked by particular stone-flowers, addressed in particular songs. To build on new growth is highest ambition and highest risk—the attachment may fail as the stone completes its formation, the platform collapsing; the stone may grow through the attachment, incorporating the structure, the inhabitants, into its mineral conversation. Both outcomes have occurred, are known in story, serve as caution and lure for the ambitious or desperate.

The deeper passages, where the emergence continues most actively, are *liminal-terrible*, known by sound rather than sight, by the reports of those who have descended and returned (rare) or not returned (common). Some believe the deep holds the source of the crickets, their origin and their ultimate meaning. Others believe it holds *competition*, other emergences, other possibilities that did not become the Here and still resent their failure. The sounds of making that echo upward—vibrations felt in sleeping platforms, in water vessels, in the bones of the attentive—are interpreted variously as promise and threat, the chamber's continuing growth as invitation or warning.

The surface, by contrast, is *known-abstract*, a theoretical space that serves functional and narrative purposes without requiring belief in its full reality. Surface-traders are *real-enough*, their goods undeniable, their bodies smelling of impossible vastness and impossible sterility, the not-chamber that they must come from and return to. But the surface itself—its extension, its population, its history beyond the immediate exchange relationship—exists as story, as rumor, as the place where exiles go to become *Gone* in a different mode. Some Morrani, particularly those who have traded extensively, develop what might be called surface-belief, a practical acceptance of the world's extension beyond the chamber; but even they rarely imagine it with the emotional density they bring to the chamber's geography, its specific platforms and passages and thermal gradients.

---

Their time is *geological-pregnant*, measured not in days or seasons but in *phases of emergence*.

The basic unit is the **water-cycle**, the twelve-hour rhythm of condensation and collection, marked by the specific drips that each platform tracks. But this is too short for meaningful social duration; the **platform-generation**, approximately fifteen water-cycles, marks the period during which a given platform-kin maintains stable composition, the interval between major reconfigurations. Longer still, the **stone-phase**, measured in multiples of platform-generations, tracks the warming and cooling of particular wall-sections, the migration of seeps, the brightening and dimming of phosphorescence. The longest unit is the **emergence-itself**, the unknowable duration of the chamber's ongoing formation, which may be approaching completion or may be eternal in its incompletion.

They do not count age in years but in *water-cycles witnessed*, *platform-generations survived*, *stone-phases endured*. A person who has lived through three complete stone-phases is **old-completed**, regardless of absolute duration, their experience encompassing the full range of chamber variability. Such persons are not revered in the surface sense of authority, but *consulted*, their pattern-recognition trained across sufficient variability to detect signals in noise that shorter lives cannot parse. The cricket-diviners are typically old-completed, but not all old-completed become diviners; the skill requires something additional, a hearing-into-silence that may be innate or cultivated, no one is certain.

Death is **cooling**, the gradual or sudden loss of the body's thermal negotiation with the chamber. The dead are *immediately problematic*, their bodies requiring disposal in a space with no soil for burial, no sky for burning, no current for consignment to water. The solution varies by section, by circumstance, by the deceased's status: platform-kin may retain and process the body, consuming the edible tissues in ceremonies of intimate continuation; the bones may be incorporated into platform structure, the skull serving as water-vessel, the long bones as structural elements, the person thus persisting in the family's material presence. Those without close kin, those who die of particular conditions, those whose kin choose not to retain them, are consigned to the *lower reaches*, the deeper passages where the emergence continues, their cooling bodies offered to the stone's ongoing formation in hope of incorporation, of becoming-part-of, of not-quite-ending.

The Gone, those who have ceased to be Here through death or exile or descent, persist in memory as *thermal absences*, spaces in the chamber's heat-map where a body no longer contributes, where the familiar warmth of presence has been replaced by the indifferent cool of mere geology. To speak of the Gone is to acknowledge the chamber's fundamental condition: that it was already here before anyone was Here, that it will continue its emergence with or without witnesses, that the Here is temporary, contingent, a pattern of warmth in the cooling stone.

---

Their consciousness is *collective in attenuation*, distributed across the density of their packing.

They do not experience privacy in the surface sense; the impossibility of solitude has shaped minds that do not seek it, that find rest in the continuous presence of others' breathing, others' warmth, others' almost-silent movements. What they experience instead is *attention-depth*, the capacity to be more or less present to the immediate sensorium while remaining minimally aware of the surrounding field. A person working fiber on a crowded platform is simultaneously *here*, engaged in the specific task, and *there*, distributed across the platform-kin, tracking their states, available for interruption, responsive to distress or opportunity without deliberate attention. This is not dissociation but *habituation*, the cognitive adaptation to conditions that do not permit full withdrawal.

Their dreams, reported in the rare moments of explicit sharing, are *chamber-dreams*, populated by the familiar geometry of platforms and ropes, the thermal gradients, the water-cycles slightly altered, the crickets singing in patterns just beyond interpretation. Some dream of surface, but these dreams are recognized as *Gone-dreams*, visits to the not-Here that leave the dreamer unsettled, temporarily alienated from the chamber's familiar embrace. To dream repeatedly of surface is symptom, the emergence of a condition that may resolve or may require the final surface-transition of exile.

Their language is *quiet-structured*, operating in registers that surface ears would strain to detect, shaped by the moral and practical necessities of quietude. It is not a language of declaration but of *suggestion*, of partial utterance completed by context, by the listener's equally developed expectation, by the shared environment that makes explicitness redundant. To speak fully, to articulate completely, is to occupy space that others need, to claim attention that should remain distributed. The most respected speakers are those who communicate most with least, whose few voiced sounds trigger cascades of inference in their listeners, whose silence is as communicative as their speech.

The cricket-diviners push this to extremity, their interpretive practice operating at the edge of audition, the patterns they claim to detect possibly present, possibly projected, possibly shared hallucination maintained by the social necessity of decision-making in conditions of radical uncertainty. Their pronouncements are *provisional-absolute*, accepted as binding for the duration of the relevant decision but not as precedent, not as law, each emergence of cricket-song unique to its moment, each interpretation valid only for its occasion.

---

What the Morrani are becoming, what the chamber is making of them, is *uncertain to themselves and to the observing*.

The stone-milk they drink, the phosphorescence they inhabit, the temperature gradients they navigate—these are shaping them in ways that surface biology would struggle to parse. Their longevity, claimed but unverified, may be real, may be artifact of poor record-keeping, may be metaphor for the experiential density of lives packed with event. Their hearing, extended into registers that surface-dwellers miss, may be adaptation or may be training, the cricket-song possibly present for all but attended only by those whose lives require its interpretation. Their thermal economy, the apparent capacity to survive cooler conditions than surface physiology predicts, may reflect actual metabolic modification or may reflect the continuous low-level activity of lives without rest, without solitude, without the cooling that accompanies physical and social withdrawal.

What is certain: they are *present*, they are *continuing*, they are *accompanying* the chamber's emergence with whatever they are becoming. They do not ask to be saved, to be discovered, to be interpreted. They do not imagine themselves as problem or solution, as primitive or advanced, as trapped or free. They are the Here, the full extent of meaningful space, and in their density, their quietude, their provisional persistence, they are *making themselves* as the chamber makes itself, neither prior nor posterior, both incomplete, both emergent, both waiting in the damp cool dark for whatever comes next.

The crickets, almost too quiet to hear, continue their divination. The stone, slightly warm, continues its conversation with itself. The Morrani, packed together on platforms of bone and hope, continue their accompaniment, their waiting, their being-there for the chamber's ongoing dream of becoming.
