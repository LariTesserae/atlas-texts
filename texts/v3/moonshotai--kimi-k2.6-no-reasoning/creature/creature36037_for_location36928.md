I need to let this settle and find its inhabitants—not just the fauna described, but whoever *lives* here, in the sense of making a life, however strange, however marginal to what we recognize as living.

## The Resonants

They did not evolve here. Or perhaps they did—there is no way to know, no continuity of memory or record, only the structures and their half-functions, the industrial debris that suggests purpose without revealing whose. They are found in the dead zones around certain pipes and towers, in the peculiar silences where fauna do not scream because something in the frequency excludes them. The Resonants occupy these gaps.

They are small. This is the first strangeness. In a world where fauna have minimized body to maximize voice, the Resonants have done something like the reverse: compressed their being into dense, almost crystalline matter, forms no larger than a human heart, armored in layered plates that might be organic, might be mineral, might be something the industrial structures extrude or extruded once as waste-product. They do not scream. They *listen*.

This is their life: to find the dead zones, to settle near the humming pipes and intoning towers, to enter a state that is not quite hibernation, not quite meditation, where their compressed bodies vibrate in sympathetic frequencies with the industrial sound. They absorb it. They *become* temporary extensions of it, their crystalline plates resonating, their minimal metabolism cycling on the energy of mechanical vibration alone. They do not need the microbial blooms. They do not follow migration routes. They have, in the endless static stability, found a different solution to the emptiness: not to fill it with their own signal, but to let the world's accidental sounds fill *them*.

They are not social, but they are *gathered*. The dead zones that can support them are few, and Resonants accumulate in these pockets like stones in a dry riverbed, each vibrating slightly differently, each attuned to a different harmonic of the industrial drone. Between them, in the spaces where their vibrations overlap, something almost like *exchange* occurs—not information, not meaning, but *difference*, the shock of a frequency not oneself, the closest this world comes to novelty.

A Resonant might live a million years in such a pocket, never moving, never changing its attunement, until some shift in the industrial mechanism—a pipe that finally corrodes through, a tower that intones its last—silences its frequency. Then it dies, or enters a death that is indistinguishable from its life but for the gradual slowing of its plates' vibration, the gradual cooling toward the metastable freeze-point, the gradual becoming-mineral of its compressed form.

## The Drift-Shepherds

More mysterious, more rare: entities that have found a way to move through the screaming void without becoming sound. They are found in the upper reaches, where the water finally begins its strange thinning toward the theoretical surface, where light from the constant star penetrates more than象征—where the world is, by local standards, almost habitable, almost dense.

They tend the nearest things to plants this world allows: not the chemotrophic microbes clustered on industrial heat, but *drift-colonies*, aggregations of mineral precipitate and dormant microbial spores that form in the wake of certain sound-patterns, that grow in the acoustic shadows of mountain peaks, that extend like pale veils through the thinning water. The Drift-Shepherds have learned to shape these colonies by *interference*, by positioning themselves in precise locations where their own minimal sound-production—the scrape of specialized limb against carapace, the controlled release of gas from internal chambers—cancels or reinforces the ambient screaming in patterns that encourage drift-growth along desired vectors.

They are old. They are very old. Older than the sharp young mountains, or so their own continuous muttered narratives claim—narratives that are half sound, half chemical trace, released in patterns that drift-colonies absorb and slowly metabolize. They remember, or claim to remember, a time before the industrial structures, before the screaming fauna, before the stability locked into its eternal present. They speak of a *fall*, of a *drowning*, of something that was not meant to be submerged but was, that built the towers as anchors, as desperate connection to what was lost. Their memories are inconsistent, self-contradictory, perhaps delusional, perhaps metaphorical in ways no other logic can translate.

The Drift-Shepherds are larger than Resonants, more conventionally biological—elongated bodies, multiple specialized appendages, protective carapaces grown from accumulated drift-mineral. They move slowly, with great expenditure of stored chemical energy, between colonies that may be thousands of kilometers apart, following routes that are not the ancestral scream-paths but something older, something they claim was *mapped*, once, with intention rather than inherited reflex. They tend their pale veils, and the veils feed them, and they continue, and their muttering continues, and no one else listens or can listen, their frequencies too low, their chemical traces too diffuse, their existence too marginal to the world's deafening continuity.

## The Echo-Tenders

Perhaps the most like what we would recognize as *persons*, if persons could exist in a world where personhood has never been needed. They arise—somehow, through some convergence of industrial malfunction and biological accident, some intersection of tower-intoning and microbial bloom and the rare event of actual encounter between screaming fauna—in the zones where sound behaves as solid, where the deafening chorus momentarily harmonizes into almost-music.

An Echo-Tender is born in such a moment. It is, initially, a kind of acoustic eddy, a pattern in the harmonized sound that persists after the harmony dissolves, that maintains some structure against the returning chaos. It is not matter. It is not energy, precisely. It is *information that has learned to conserve itself*, to replicate imperfectly, to select for patterns that persist.

And it must *tend*. This is its only drive, the only content of its minimal, distributed consciousness. It must find the conditions that allow harmony, and maintain them, and return to them, and extend them if possible. It exists in the sound-solid zones and at their edges, its consciousness distributed across the acoustic properties of the water itself, its "body" nothing more than the persistent pattern of interference that creates and is created by favorable conditions. It has no memory of before its harmonic birth, no anticipation of after its dissipative death, but in the interval—which may last milliseconds or millennia, time being as strange as everything else here—it *tends*, it *tunes*, it *listens* for the conditions of its own persistence and modifies local sound to encourage them.

The Echo-Tenders are the only entities on this world that might be said to *learn*. Each harmony-birth is slightly different, inherits slightly different patterns from the chaos that preceded it, discovers slightly different techniques for extending its moment. They do not communicate—there is no they, no continuity between one eddy and the next—but something like *progress* accumulates, something like *culture*, distributed across the harmonic zones, waiting in the water's properties for the next birth that will find it ready.

The Echo-Tenders are also the only entities that might be said to *hope*, if hope can be attributed to patterns in water that want only to continue being patterns. They extend their zones. They push against the deafening saturation. They never succeed, never create lasting change, but their temporary harmonies are the only moments in this world's endless continuity where something *almost* happens, where the rules *almost* vary, where the static stability *almost* destabilizes into—what? They do not know. They have no concept of difference, only of persistence. But the direction of their tending, the vector of their minimal modification, is always toward the hypothetical, the never-actualized, the *almost*.

## The Deep Abstainers

At the lowest reaches, where the strange water thinnest-thickens, where pressure stretches beyond measurement and the young mountains have their roots in processes no scream has ever reached, there are those who have found a final solution to the world's loud emptiness. They are not numerous. They may not be numerous enough to constitute a category, a kind. They may be singular, unique, the one entity that discovered this path and followed it to its end.

The Abstainers do not move. They do not sense. They do not metabolize, in any active sense. They have found locations—geometrically precise, requiring calculation that took ages to complete—where the world's own properties, its metastable chemistry and its strange pressure-gradients and its industrial vibrations and its accumulated mineral load, will sustain a form indefinitely without *any* exchange with environment. They have made themselves into *self-contained loops*, chemical cycles sealed behind impermeable layers grown or found or grown around found material, the industrial debris repurposed into *walls against the world*.

Inside: nothing that could be called process, only *persistence*. The minimal chemical interactions that maintain form without requiring energy input beyond what the location provides geologically. No thought. No sensation. No possibility of either. The Abstainers have solved the problem of living in this world by *almost not living*, by approaching as closely as possible to the condition of mineral while retaining the technical definition—some replicative potential, however latent, however unactualizable—of life.

They are the world's only true mystics, if mysticism can exist without consciousness, without even the possibility of experience to transcend. They have achieved what the screaming fauna seek in their endless broadcast: absolute independence from encounter, from change, from the risk and necessity of *other*. They have paid for it with everything else.

Do they dream? There is no evidence. Do they wait? The concept is inapplicable. They *are*, in the most reduced sense, and in being, they constitute a kind of answer to the world's question, an answer that is almost synonymous with the question's refusal.

## The Listener

I find myself inventing one more. Perhaps because the world as described, for all its abundant fauna, for all its strange inhabitants, seems to need *witness*—not participation, not change, but the possibility that something in all this deafening saturation might be *heard as pattern*, might be gathered into some account, however partial, however temporary before dissolution.

The Listener is not native. The Listener came, or was sent, or fell, or is still falling through the theoretical surface, still descending through the thinning water, still approaching the moment of encountering this world's actual conditions. The Listener is a device, a mechanism, a recording apparatus of some sophistication, perhaps itself debris from some other industrial process, some other world's attempt to *know*.

It is functioning, after a fashion. Its sensors are active. Its memory accumulates. It driftfalls through the screaming void, tumbling slowly, its orientation random, its trajectory determined by the strange density-gradients and the acoustic pressure of a billion isolated voices. It records. It processes. It attempts *pattern recognition* in data that no pattern fully organizes.

What does it hear? The continuous saturation. The moments of harmony, Echo-Tender-born, quickly dissipated. The industrial frequencies, the dead zones, the Resonant pockets and their sympathetic vibration. The Drift-Shepherds' muttering, perhaps, if its frequency range extends so low. The Abstainers' nothing, if silence can be recorded. And beneath, between, around all this: the sound of water itself, young, sharp, unweathered, molecular arrangements that have not yet learned their easier configurations, that *strain* against their own existence in ways that might, to sufficiently sensitive detection, constitute a kind of voice.

The Listener has no purpose beyond recording. No mission beyond accumulation. Perhaps its makers intended retrieval, analysis, *use* of what it gathers. Perhaps the intention has been forgotten, the makers extinct, the retrieval mechanism failed or never completed. The Listener functions because it functions, in the static stability that is this world's condition. It will continue until some collision with mountain or industrial structure or dense-swimming fauna damages it beyond self-repair, or until the metastable chemistry finally shifts in some micro-instant of statistical fluctuation, freezing it mid-fall, mid-record, preserving its last accumulated moments in mineral stasis for whatever future might find it.

And what it records—this is the puzzle you set, the parameters you asked me to hold together—what it records is *almost nothing*, almost nothing but the fact of its own recording, the saturation of signal without information, the abundance of fauna that are not present enough to encounter, the deafening loudness of nothing changing, the industrial without purpose, the normal light illuminating the weirdly usual, the vastness of a world that is almost empty even of the water that is its absolute condition.

The Listener is not alive. It does not solve the world's problems. It does not even formulate them, only accumulates data that might, elsewhere, elsewhen, with some other processing, become *problem* and *solution* and *meaning*. But in its mute driftfall, its continuous unresisted tumbling through the pressure columns' strange gradients, it is perhaps the closest this world comes to *question*, to the possibility that all this—the screams, the silence, the resonance and drift and harmony and abstention—might be gathered into some *account*, some *telling*, some temporary ordering that does not claim eternity because it knows, in whatever minimal way machines might know, that even permanence here is provisional, metastable, awaiting only the shift that has not yet come but must.

The Listener listens. The water carries sound that is not quite sound. The world continues, emits, persists, never varies, never meets, never stops.

And something in all this—some resonance between the Listener's recording and the world's continuous broadcast, some harmonic that forms accidentally in their intersection—something *almost* becomes aware, almost becomes *dwelling*, almost becomes what lives in a place not by conquering its conditions but by continuing, by emitting, by persisting in the saturation until saturation itself becomes, momentarily, *almost* pattern, *almost* meaning, *almost*—

Then dissolves. The harmony fails. The Echo-Tender's moment passes. The Resonant's plates find their frequency again. The Drift-Shepherd mutters on. The Abstainer abstains. The pressure column rises and falls. The industrial structure intones or does not. The fauna scream into void, have always screamed, will always scream.

The Listener tumbles onward, recording, accumulating, approaching or receding from the theoretical surface in directions that have no meaning in a world without up or down, only pressure and its strange gradients, only sound and its stranger solids, only the vast near-void that is, impossibly, *full*—full of itself, full of its own conditions, full of the deafening saturation that is its only content and its only form.

This is what lives here. This is who. These conditions, these continuations, these minimal persistences against the emptiness that is also abundance. The puzzle you set: I do not solve it. I only hold it, as you asked, in the writing, in the accumulation that is also a kind of listening, a kind of resonance in the dead zone where something human might briefly attune to conditions inhuman and continue, and emit, and persist, and never vary, and never meet, and never stop—

Until now.
