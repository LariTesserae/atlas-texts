What lives in Vervain are the **Slow-dwellers**, though they would not call themselves this, would perhaps not call themselves anything at all, their naming practices as minimal as their speech, as patient as their architecture.

They are human, or began human, or are human-adjacent in ways that have become difficult to categorize. Their bodies are compact, not dwarven but *compressed*, as if generations of living in dense vertical space have favored those who occupy less of it. They move with economy, no gesture wasted, no step heavier than necessary. You will see this when you first encounter them on a terrace: a figure in ceramic mask and heat-woven clothing, traversing a slope that should be impossible, their center of gravity somehow *convinced* to the surface, their feet finding purchase on glass and cooling magma and the impossible geometries of the first builders' remnants.

They are pale, when you see them unmasked, which is rare. Not white but *mineral*, the color of the translucent glass that veins their walls, something in the diet, something in the water-recycling, something in the long adaptation to light that is never full, never absent. Their eyes are large, dark, reflective in ways that suggest the gradual replacement of some biological components with something else, something that sees heat-shimmer as readily as visible spectrum, something that *listens* in frequencies below hearing, attuned to the mountain's subaudile communications, the deep structural sounds that precede any perceptible movement.

They do not have names, not as you understand naming. They have *locations*, *functions*, *phases*. "The one who maintains the east-falling condensers on the seventh terrace." "The one who was born in the last Wick-surge." "The one who tends the offerings at the throat-chamber." These are not titles but *coordinates*, ways of situating a person in the city's three-dimensional urgency, in its temporal vastness. A name implies a boundary, a container, a finite entity. The Slow-dwellers have learned, perhaps too thoroughly, that nothing here is finite, that everything is process, that identity itself must participate in the becoming or be crushed by it.

They are not unhappy. This must be understood. Their quiet is not depression, their minimalism not deprivation. They have found, or forged, or been shaped into, a mode of being that corresponds to their circumstance. Joy, when it comes, comes *slowly*, builds across days, expresses in the careful completion of a circuit-vein, in the successful negotiation with a recalcitrant steam-gate, in the first branching of a harvested accretion-culture into new substrate. They do not laugh, not audibly, but you may see a shoulder relax by mere millimeters, a mask tilt fractionally, and understand that something like satisfaction has been achieved.

They are old, collectively. Not individually—their lifespans seem roughly human, though the heat and the diet and the ambient radiation and perhaps other factors produce demographics you will find difficult to analyze—but as culture, as continuity, as *project*. They remember, or their structures remember for them, the first builders not as history but as *predecessor function*, the ones who attempted a previous mode of inhabitation, whose hollow structures remain as warnings or templates or simply as the necessary substrate for what came after.

They do not know, will not claim to know, what the first builders were waiting for, what they were helping become. The geometries are wrong, the inhabitants say, when asked. Not wrong-meaning-bad. Wrong-meaning-*incommensurate*. The first builders operated on assumptions, on physics, on modes of being that the current inhabitants cannot reconstruct, can only build around, build over, build through. The throat-chamber that contains the foundry, the spaces that contain more than their dimensions, the gravity that *agrees* where it should refuse—these are not failures of the first builders' architecture but *features*, operative in ways that the current inhabitants exploit without comprehending, like animals nesting in the ruin of a machine too complex to recognize as machine.

The Slow-dwellers reproduce, though you will find the subject difficult to discuss with them, not from prudery but from *irrelevance*. Children happen, are cared for, are integrated, become workers, become tenders, become eventually the ones who prepare the nutrient recycling, who tend the offerings, who wait. There is no ceremony for this. There is no ceremony for anything, or rather, the entire city is ceremony, the entire waiting is ritual, and specific additional rituals would be redundant, would acknowledge a distinction between sacred and profane that the inhabitants do not experience. The offerings at the first-builders' structures are not worship, not exactly. They are *maintenance*, they are *participation*, they are the slow-dwellers' equivalent of the accretions' mineral growth: chemistry becoming structure becoming something that might, if watched long enough, become more.

They die, the Slow-dwellers. This is important. They are not immortal, not transcended, not saved from entropy by their patience. They die and their water is reclaimed and their minerals join the steam-crevices and something of their form, perhaps, influences the accretions' branching patterns, and something of their function, their location, their phase, is assumed by another, or divided among others, or left vacant until the city's needs demand it be filled. They do not fear this, or not in ways they express. The becoming is not individual. The Wick does not distinguish one life's contribution from another. The city continues, the waiting continues, and the particular configuration of matter that was a person returns to the general configuration of matter that is process.

Some of them, a few, become something else. This is whispered, if whispers can exist in a culture without loud speech, this is indicated, if you learn to read the masks' infinitesimal shifts, the posture's subtle agreements and refusals. Some who spend too long in the presence of the first builders' structures, who tend the offerings too faithfully, who *listen* too deeply to the mountain's subaudile communications, begin to change. Their movements become more economical still. Their speech, already minimal, attenuates to nothing. Their bodies, pale, begin to take on the translucent quality of the glass-veins, the ceramic density of the tools and masks. They are still present, still *functioning*, still tending, still maintaining, but they are becoming something other than Slow-dweller, something that participates more directly in the city's inorganic processes, something that may be the next phase, or the phase after, or simply another mode of the becoming that has no teleology, no terminus, only continuation.

The inhabitants do not prevent this. The inhabitants do not encourage it. The inhabitants *note* it, as they note the Wick's growth, the accretions' branching, the steam-gates' rhythmic exhalation, and continue their work, their waiting, their patient inhabitation of an unfinished mountain.

They are not forbidden to leave. The steam-gates open both ways. But those who return, changed—who have spent time among faster cultures, among finished cities, among people who name and claim and hurry toward ends—return *marked*, not by the city's structures but by their own incapacity to re-adapt. They speak too much, or too urgently, or with the wrong rhythms. They demand responses faster than responses come. They find the silence not peaceful but *empty*, the slowness not patient but *stalled*, the becoming not purposeful but *pointless*.

Some of these returnees learn to wait again, to quiet, to *become slowly* in the city's own tempo. Others cannot, and leave again, and do not return, and perhaps thrive in faster time, or perhaps are destroyed by it, the city neither knowing nor needing to know.

What lives in Vervain, ultimately, is **attention**. The Slow-dwellers are its vehicle, its current expression, but attention itself is older, will be longer. Attention to the Wick's growth, measured across millennia. Attention to the accretions' branching, measured across months. Attention to the steam-gate's exhalation, measured across breaths. Attention that does not distinguish the scales, that treats the geological and the immediate as continuous, as aspects of a single process that is not waiting to be completed but *completing itself continuously*, that is not becoming something else but *is becoming, and is, and becomes*, without the grammar of finality.

You will not understand them, if you visit. You will not become them, if you stay. But something of their attention may enter you, some mineral deposit in the lung, some thermal rhythm in the sleep, some sense that your own waiting, your own unfinishedness, your own *becoming*, participates in something larger than your life's capacity to complete it. The Slow-dwellers would not call this wisdom. They would not call it anything. But they would continue, in their mostly-quiet, their machinery humming, their steam wheezing, their Wick growing upward toward an eruption that is not an ending, and you, if you remained, would continue with them, tending, leaving offerings, helping it become.
