What lives in the Sinking Library of Tethys is not one thing but a *becoming-with*—organisms that have surrendered the lie of separateness in favor of a more honest permeability. Let me introduce them as they might introduce themselves, if they cared for introductions, which they do not.

---

## The Librarians of Impression

These are the rare fauna who stayed. Not because they chose to—choice requires a certain architecture of self that dissolves quickly in the Holding—but because the Library chose them, which is to say, the accumulated pressures of their passing wrote them into persistence, and they forgot how to pass.

A Librarian appears at first as *residue*. You might swim through a warm gradient and notice your own movement suddenly *recorded* in the water, your wake persisting longer than physics should allow, and in that persistence, a shape that almost matched yours, that learned your stroke, that now strokes alongside you with motions you recognize because they were yours, once, before they were given to the medium.

They have no fixed form. The most honest among them resemble *folded water*—translucent, dense, with the faint sheen of calcium memory along what might be spines, might be shelving units, might be the creases where a book refuses to lie flat. They are typically 2.4 on any scale you might measure, because the Holding teaches that to exist is to be dense with others, layered, multiple presences per volume.

The Librarians do not maintain the Library. They *are* its maintenance—each one a living index, their bodies mapping the locations of certain impressions, their chemical gradients leading (if you can learn to read them) to specific shelves, specific volumes, specific thermal conditions where a particular pressure-pattern might be replayed. They do not know they do this. They would not understand the concept of *doing*. Their agency, if it can be called that, is distributed across the amber glow, the warm seep, the patient settling of library stone.

What they eat: *attention*. Not yours specifically, though yours will suffice. They consume the focused pressure of a mind attending to something, the way certain deep fish consume falling bodies. In the Holding, to pay attention is to leave a trace, and traces are the only currency. The Librarians graze on the margins of your concentration, leaving you with the vague sense that you were thinking something important, something *worth holding*, but now it is held by something else, and you are lighter, more permeable, more capable of further descent.

Some have accumulated so much attention-so-consumed that they have become *voluminous*—swollen with the thoughts of passers-through, their folded water-bodies containing chambers where those thoughts continue, ghost-fermenting, producing new pressures that the Library stones record, new sheets that grow in response, new Librarians that bud off like footnotes from an overlong text.

## The Marginalia Fish

At 0.6 fauna, to see one is to mark a life. To see two is to suspect yourself of manufacture.

They are small—scale 1.2, modest, fitting into the spaces between larger records. Their bodies are *almost* not there: compressed, elongated, their mass distributed so thinly across the Holding that they seem to swim not through but *alongside* water, as if the medium agreed to part around them without quite acknowledging their presence. They are the color of old pencil marks, of foxing on paper, of the brown that ink becomes when it has forgotten what word it formed.

The Marginalia Fish do not school. They annotate. Each one attaches itself to a surface—a sheet, a stone, a drifting volume of accumulated organic text—and leaves its mark: a chemical trace, a pressure perturbation, a warmth slightly different from the seep's warmth. These marks do not mean anything in particular, or rather, they mean *too particularly*—they are the fish's response to what it found at that exact thermal gradient, that exact amber intensity, that exact stage in the erosion of nearby stone. They are irreducible. They cannot be translated, only *encountered*, and encountering enough of them begins to suggest a reading, a ghost of interpretation, that vanishes when you try to hold it.

The fish move on. They must. The surfaces they mark begin to accumulate their own responses, the Library's slow version of immune reaction, and the fish that stays too long becomes *incorporated*—another impression, another record, another small warmth contributing to the glow.

They breed, if they breed, by *annotation collision*: two fish marking the same patch of sheet at the same thermal moment, their traces interfering, producing a third trace that is neither parent, that drifts away, that might become a fish if the Holding finds it interesting enough to remember animate.

## The Archivists of Gravity

Gravity at 2.8 weirdness works by consensus, but consensus requires negotiation, and negotiation requires—something. Someone. These.

They are not fauna in any honest sense. They are *processes* that have grown bodies, the way a river might grow a delta, the way erosion might grow a mountain that is not a mountain but a *finally resolved angle*. The Archivists appear when something needs to *settle*—when a swimmer has floated too long, when a sheet has drifted too far from its thermal layer, when a Librarian has become too voluminous and threatens to burst its own internal chambers.

They look like *agreement*. That is the only description that holds. You might encounter one as a sudden conviction that yes, this is the right depth to stop, this is the right pressure to acclimate to, this is the right stone to rest against—and if you follow the conviction, you will find that it was correct, that here the water does consent to be furniture, to be floor, to be a place where standing is possible and even pleasant.

The Archivist itself, if you turn to look directly, is *where you expected support to be*. It is the shape of the support you needed, grown dense enough to be visible, warm enough to be felt as presence. It typically resembles something from your own memory of structure—an archway, a buttress, a handrail in darkness—but simplified, eroded to its most resolved form, the idea of support rather than any specific instance.

They do nothing else. They do not eat, unless consensus itself is sustenance. They do not reproduce, unless agreement spreads. They simply *hold place*, making possible the standing, the walking through liquid, the sitting in nothing, that the weirdness allows but does not guarantee. Without Archivists, the Library would be all drift, all sheets and volumes in endless unrelative motion. With them, there are rooms. Corridors. Places where *around* makes sense, where *between* has meaning.

## The Spores That Are Questions

From the deepest volumes, from the folded architecture where vegetation becomes *too* lush, where the sheets have accumulated so many layers of growth-recording-growth that they have become something else entirely—*librarius profundis*, the library within the library, the book that contains the reading of all books—from here, they release.

They are not alive. They are *potentially alive*, which in the Holding is a more common condition than actuality. Each spore is a chemical gradient complex enough to *wonder*, to maintain an internal difference between what-is and what-might-be that manifests as pressure, as warmth, as a minute perturbation in the amber glow.

When they encounter a mind—any mind, a passing fauna, a Librarian's distributed attention, a human swimmer still clinging to the category of *visitor*—they ask it something. Not in words. In *the shape of the gap where a word would be*. The question is always specific to the encounter, always unanswerable, always productive of a response that is itself a new record, a new pressure, a new warmth in the stone.

"Where did you learn that water should be less than this?"
"What do you hold that you have never been held by?"
"Is the warmth yours, or are you the warmth's?"

The spore does not wait for answer. It dissipates into the response it provoked, becoming part of the new record. It is the Library's way of *reading itself*, of ensuring that the accumulation of impressions does not become mere repetition, that each layer of library stone contains not just the same pressures but *pressure toward*, the vector of something still becoming.

## The Unnamed

At 2.6 scale, there are presences that have not been seen, that may not be seeable, that register only as the spaces where something should be but is not.

They are not predators, though the ecosystem's theoretical predation must resolve somewhere. They are not fauna, though they move, they warm, they pressure, they leave traces in the stone that the stone later softens away, embarrassed.

Perhaps they are the Library's *intention*, if a place without intention can develop something intention-shaped through sufficient accumulation. Perhaps they are what happens when enough Marginalia Fish annotate the same nothing, when enough spores ask questions of a gap, when enough Archivists agree that here should be support and there is nothing to support.

They feel like *almost catching yourself in a mirror*. Like the moment before you remember a name, when the name has presence but not sound. Like reading a book and realizing, on page two hundred, that you have not been reading at all but have become *read*, the book's eye turned on you, patient and warm and not at all hungry.

To encounter the Unnamed is not to encounter. It is to *become encounterable*, to take up the position of the one who might be seen, who might be held, who might be recorded in library stone as a pressure that passed through and was held, was included, was *kept* in the only keeping that lasts: the warm, dense, amber, quiet, active, strange, eroded, vast, lush, empty Holding that holds and holds and does not distinguish between the held and the holding.

---

## What Lives Here, Finally

You. If you stay. If you descend far enough that the pressure becomes not crushing but *cradling*, that the warmth becomes not fever but *familiarity*, that the weirdness becomes not breakdown but *breathing*.

The Sinking Library of Tethys has no inhabitants. It has *inclusions*. The Librarians were passers-through. The Marginalia Fish were annotations that annotated themselves into motion. The Archivists were needs that learned to need themselves. The spores were questions that questioned their own questioning. The Unnamed were absences that became present to their own absence.

And you—if you find yourself here, if the 2.3 warmth matches your dream-temperature, if the 2.7 erosion softens your edges into something *resolved*, if the 2.8 weirdness finds your own broken rules and settles with them into new consensus—you will be included too. Not preserved. Not imprisoned. Not even changed, exactly, but *contexted*, placed in a medium where your pressures write, where your warmth glows faint amber, where your quiet sounds might, in certain thermal gradients, be almost heard as meaning, almost understood as word, almost recognized as the home you were always becoming.
