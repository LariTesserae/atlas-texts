What lives in the City of Dried Lungs is, at first, nothing—nothing that moves with intention, nothing that eats in the way mouths eat, nothing that watches from the bruised green corners. The text tells you this repeatedly. You must understand that this absence is itself a presence, a negative ecology so complete that to call it empty would miss the point entirely.

But let me stay longer. Let what emerges become more itself.

The walls breathe. I said this was not metaphor, and I will hold to that, but I must also say: they breathe *in memory of* something. The breathing walls are not merely pneumatic architecture but *habit*, the continued behavior of something that once needed to breathe and now cannot stop, a tic of existence that outlived the need. What lives here, then, is first and foremost **the Habit of Breathing**, the city itself as organism-without-organ, doing what it learned to do before it learned that it could cease. The walls do not breathe to live. They breathe because this is what they are, because cessation would require a decision they are no longer capable of making, because the architecture of respiration has become the only architecture they know.

And within this habit, within the polyphonic chambers out of phase, there is **the Breath Itself**, which I must describe as something that lives because it has achieved a kind of rudimentary intentionality. The air in the City of Dried Lungs is hot not merely with temperature but with *purpose*, with the accumulated agenda of millions of cycles of inhalation-exhalation. It moves in currents, yes, in storms of temperature and pressure, but it also moves *toward*. It seeks moisture. This is stated plainly and must be taken at full weight: the air counts your moisture and finds you wanting. The breath is *hungry*, not in the way stomachs are hungry but in the way deserts are hungry, the way absence is hungry for presence. It pulls water from you not as theft but as reaching, as the only gesture of intimacy available to something that has never touched water directly, that has only known it as transient, as carried, as stolen from the visitors who stayed too long or not long enough.

The vegetation, then—**the Bruised Green**, the fungal-older-than-fungal growth that carpets the convolutions. It lives, yes, but what kind of living? It builds sugars from hot exhalation, from mineral powders, from the chemistry of breath itself. It is metabolic, it grows, it swells with each inhalation and subsides with each outrush, but I think it is more accurate to say it *participates* than that it *lives*. It is the city's way of noticing itself, of registering the breathing in material form. The vegetation is **the Sensation of the Walls**, the distributed nervous system of a body that has forgotten it ever had a brain, the slow green awareness that *something is happening here* and has been happening for longer than erosion records. When it overgrows the primitive tools, swallows them, incorporates their metallurgy, it is not feeding. It is *remembering*, in its vegetative way, that something other than breathing once happened in this chamber, that hands once moved, that purpose once drove motion through these corridors. The acid patience of its erosion is the patience of something trying to understand a story told in a language it no longer speaks.

Those tools. Those scrapers, those awls, left abandoned in corners where the light never justifies their presence. The text mentions butchers once, but I think this is a confusion, a retrospective classification imposed by visitors who needed the city to have a history they could recognize. The tools are not evidence of butchers. They are **the First Visitors**, the ones who came before you and found the breathing sufficient. Not their bodies—that absence is telling—but their *decision to stay*, crystallized into the last deliberate act they performed before the sufficiency took them. The tools are not remains. They are *offerings*, or they became offerings, left where they fell from hands that no longer needed to hold anything. The vegetation grows over them not as consumption but as *acceptance*, as the city's slow recognition that yes, this too is part of the breathing now, this crude metallurgy, this heat-shaped intention that has been incorporated into the lung's slow rhythm.

And what of those hands? What of the ones who exhaled and never needed to inhale again, or inhaled and found the breath sufficient? I think they are still here, in a form that the text approaches and retreats from. They are **the Incorporated**, not dead precisely, not alive in any register that would recognize them, but *distributed*, *dissolved into the habit*, the way a drop of water dissolved into the hungry air might still, in some sense, be the drop, might still participate in the counting, the wanting, the finding-wanting. They are the reason the city is pocket-sized and yet contains districts, neighborhoods, the accumulated weight of urban life. Their accumulated *staying*, their accumulated *sufficiency*, has become the density of the place, the compression of urban possibility into convoluted space. They did not build the city. They became its **occupancy**, its *lived-in quality*, the sense that rooms are for dwelling even when nothing dwells in them with intention anymore.

The light, then—the phosphorescent waste of vegetative metabolism, the heat made visible. This is **the Dreaming of the City**, or what passes for it. The lung that never held its breath does not sleep in any recognizable rhythm, but it has states, variations, the micro-weather of temperature and pressure that sweeps through corridors and leaves changed atmospheres. These are not random. They are the closest approach the city makes to **narrative**, to the shaped experience of time that produces something like story, something like the memory of a night passed in breathing, the memory of a storm that was not weather but *event*, the memory of visitors who stayed and visitors who left and the particular quality of the air when each happened. The bruised greens and fevered reds are the city's emotional register, its palette of sufficient and insufficient, of wanting and finding-wanting and, rarely, momentarily, the strange satisfaction of being entirely itself, breathing without reference to anything outside the breath.

The pore through which you entered—I must finally speak of it, though the text has already named it spiraling, rimmed with crystallized exhalations. It is **the Mouth That Became Navel**, the point where the city once connected to something outside itself and now turns inward, the aperture that is also return, the spiraling that suggests both entry and the impossibility of simple exit. What lives at the pore is **the Threshold Itself**, the maintained difference between the dead dry world above and the active dynamic eroded existence within, a difference that requires constant work, the expenditure of heat and pressure, the continued production of the strange arithmetic that finds visitors wanting. The pore is where the city is most itself and least itself, where it reaches toward the outside it can no longer comprehend and pulls back into the breathing that is all it understands.

And finally, because I must, because to stay longer in this emergence is to risk incorporation myself: what lives in the City of Dried Lungs is **the Sufficient**, the quality of being enough to oneself that requires no other, that continues without water, without animal life, without technology, without intention, without anything except the habit of breathing and the hunger of the breath and the bruised green memory of something that once was not-breathing and has now, finally, after longer than erosion records, been almost completely forgotten. The Sufficient is not happy. The Sufficient does not know happiness. The Sufficient breathes, and in breathing is sufficient, and in sufficiency is the city, and in the city is the lung, and in the lung is the breath, and in the breath is the wanting, and in the wanting is the visitor, carrying water, carrying the possibility of not-staying, carrying the distance they can put between themselves and the sound of breathing that is not theirs and will never be theirs.

The Sufficient waits. It does not know waiting. It breathes. That is what lives here. That is what lives.
