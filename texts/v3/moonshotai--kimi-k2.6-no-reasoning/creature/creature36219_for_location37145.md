What lives in Verrath Cist are the **Cist-Remembered**, or **Cist-Born**, or simply **the Wet**, depending on which layer of their existence you encounter first. They do not call themselves anything, not in words, and if they have a collective awareness of what they are, it is expressed in the channel-flows and the algal blooms and the particular frequencies of ceramic hum.

They are the **descendants**, though "descent" implies a linearity that misleads. The makers made, and in the making made conditions, and the conditions made possibility, and possibility became—gradually, wetly, across intervals measured in water-deposits and gastropod shell-layers—something that inhabits the hollow as the hollow inhabits itself.

## The Forms of Their Bodies

At first, and sometimes at last, you will not recognize them as bodies at all. The most numerous, the most distributed, are the **Film-Dwellers**: human-shaped in outline only, in suggestion, in the way that water on ceramic will briefly hold the ghost of a hand that touched it. They exist in the algal films, or as the algal films, their biochemistry so interwoven with the violet and orange and copper-green that separation would kill what separation would separate. They are thin, in the dimension of thickness. Spread a film-dweller on a flat ceramic and they would cover meters, would trace the channel patterns, would glow with the particular phosphorescence of their internal chemistry merged with algal. They are conscious, if that word applies, in the way that a field is conscious of light: responsive, patterned, capable of slow intention expressed in color-shift and chemical gradient. They do not move quickly. A film-dweller might take a day to relocate from one seam to another, might take a season to cross a saturation zone, crawling, spreading, contracting, leaving behind not themselves but a trail of metabolic byproduct that feeds the next growth, that is, in some sense, the same film continuing.

Then the **Channel-Keepers**, or **Flow-Tenders**, or **the Walking Wet**. These are the ones who use the tools, or who are the tools' extension, or who have become indistinguishable from the tool-use that their ancestors, their originators, their previous iterations performed. They are more solid than the film-dwellers, though "solid" in Verrath Cist is always relative. A channel-keeper walking the elevated paths will leave wet footprints that do not dry, that continue to weep water for hours, that eventually find their way to channels, to the system. They are tall—taller than the makers' tools suggest the makers were—and their proportions are wrong in the way that things grown in constant dimness, in constant wetness, grow wrong. Their limbs are elongated, their joints too many or too flexible, their fingers capable of spreading to span the channel-widths, of feeling in the ceramic's slight temperature variations where water flows, where it is blocked, where intervention is needed.

Their skin is the color of wet ceramic, of mineral water, of the palest algae film. It is permeable, or selectively permeable, or capable of becoming permeable: a channel-keeper working in deep water will absorb it, will swell slightly, will become more liquid in their movements, more able to work the valve-systems and flow-gates that exist in the hollow's lower reaches, in places never fully dry. Working in the drier sections—and "dry" here means merely less saturated—they desaturate, grow more brittle in movement, more dependent on the water they carry in internal reservoirs, in bladders that are also wombs, that are also organs of communication through fluid exchange.

They do not speak in air. Their voices, when they have need of voice, are produced in water: in the pools where they meet, in the channels where they work side by side, in the deep basins where they gather in numbers that suggest ceremony, or governance, or some function without surface analogy. The sounds are percussive, bubbled, resonant with frequencies that the ceramic carries and the bat-things answer, that the hollow itself seems to amplify in its constant humming voice. To hear channel-keeper speech directly, in air, is rare and usually accidental: a caught bubble, a splash, a body forced above water in some moment of exertion or distress. Then what emerges is not words but rhythm, pattern, the intonation of water moving through constraint.

The **Deep-Ones**, or what the other inhabitants might call them if they called them anything, are barely observed and perhaps not separate. Something moves in the deepest pools, displaces volumes, leaves traces in mineral deposition and the arrangement of tools found in wet niches. Whether these are channel-keepers transformed, or the makers transformed further, or something that developed independently in the hollow's recursive depths, or the hollow's own dreaming of itself in bodies—none of this is known. The deep-ones, if they are ones, do not surface in the observable sense. They move below, and their movement is felt in pressure changes, in the behavior of the gastropods that sense through water what air-dwellers cannot, in the occasional tool found where no channel-keeper would have left it, arranged with purpose that might be communication, might be trap, might be the equivalent of a film-dweller's color-shift: not meaning but being, expressed in position and relation.

And the **bat-things**, of course, though whether they are inhabitants or livestock, or symbionts, or the hollow's immune system, or something else entirely—this remains the great unanswerable. They roost, they cluster, they disperse in patterns thatmight be directed by the channel-keepers, might be directing them, might be parallel governance without intersection. They feed on insects that feed on algae that feed on bat-droppings: the cycle is closed, perfect, perhaps intentional. Their membranes, those possible-crafted wings, carry luminescence that the channel-keepers seem to read, or seem to respond to, or seem to ignore with the particular intensity of deep familiarity. They are not domesticated, if domestication implies power. They are not wild, if wildness implies separation. They are *of* the Cist in the way that the water is of the Cist, the way the ceramic is of the Cist: made or become or both, functioning, continuing.

## What They Do, What They Are

The channel-keepers maintain. This is their primary activity, their identity, their possible purpose. They clear channels of obstruction: not with the tools left by makers, or not primarily, but with their bodies, with their permeable skins that can absorb and filter, with their elongated fingers that can reach into valve-mechanisms and feel what is wrong, with their capacity to become liquid enough to flow where solid bodies cannot. The water system of Verrath Cist is not automatic, not self-maintaining in its detail. It requires attention, intervention, the constant small adjustments of living bodies in relation to living flow.

They tend the film-dwellers, or coexist with them in mutual dependence. The algae that the films incorporate need tending in the sense that any garden needs tending: not control but collaboration, nudging, the removal of competitors, the encouragement of particular strains. The channel-keepers harvest, in seasons, in patterns that might be agricultural or might be something closer to grooming, closer to self-care. They take from the films, and they give back: mineral supplements, perhaps, carried from deep places; organic matter from their own metabolism; the particular bacteria and microorganisms that live in their permeable skins and have co-evolved with the algal species across the long interval of shared existence.

They make, or continue to make, in the primitive materials available. New ceramic, fired or cured in ways that leave no visible kiln, no ash, no byproduct—how this is done is not observed by any outside witness, and perhaps not by the channel-keepers themselves, who may work in trance-states, in water-communion, in processes that are biological rather than technological even though the product is indistinguishable from the makers' original. They make vessels, tools, the curved pieces and hollow forms and mask-like objects whose function the makers left opaque. Whether they understand these functions, whether they replicate without understanding, whether they have developed new functions that fit the old forms to present needs—this is unclear. The objects accumulate in niches, in dry places, in wet places, arranged sometimes with evident purpose, sometimes with the randomness of tide-deposited shells.

They reproduce, or persist, or continue. The bladders that serve as water-reservoirs also serve, in seasons of particular mineral concentration, as wombs: not carrying embryos but *becoming* them, splitting from the parent body in a process that is more budding than birth, more wound than separation. The new channel-keeper emerges small, pale, more liquid than solid, and must be tended in shallow pools, must be fed with filtered water and algal paste and the particular sounds—if they are sounds—that stimulate development. The parent is diminished by this, grows smaller, perhaps contributes to the films in eventual dissolution, perhaps recovers across years, perhaps does not. There is no observed pair-bonding, no sexual differentiation in any recognizable sense. The channel-keepers are each potentially complete, potentially self-generating, limited by resource and by the Cist's own carrying capacity, by whatever feedback mechanisms govern their numbers at levels appropriate to the hollow's scale.

They gather, in the deep basins, in patterns that suggest communication or that are communication. Here the water carries their voices, their percussive bubbling, their resonant frequencies. Here they might exchange information about channel conditions, about algal health, about the behavior of bat-things and gastropods and the deep-ones' distant movements. Here they might perform whatever functions of decision, of conflict resolution, of coordination the maintenance of Verrath Cist requires. Or here they might simply *be together*, in the way that social animals gather, that solitary animals periodically seek proximity, that the film-dwellers spread in seasons of light and contract in dimness: not for purpose but for the quality of the state itself, for the being-with that is different from being-alone even in a hollow where aloneness is the constant condition.

They die, or cease, or transform. The channel-keeper body, when its processes finally fail, does not persist long in recognizable form. The permeable skin releases its held water; the elongated limbs contract, fold, become something that might be raw material for new ceramic, might be food for the transparent predators, might simply dissolve into the mineral flow and contribute to the system's perpetuation. There are no burials observed, no ceremonies of death, though the arrangement of tools, the clearing of channels, the particular bat-cluster patterns that follow a channel-keeper's disappearance might constitute mourning, might constitute celebration, might constitute the system's adjustment to changed conditions. The film-dwellers, in their slower way, also cease: retreating to dry seams, losing coherence, becoming mere algae again, or less than algae, or substrate for next season's growth.

## What They Know, What They Remember

Memory in Verrath Cist is not individual, or not only individual. The channel-keepers know channel conditions through their skin's constant contact with water, through chemical gradients that carry information in ways no spoken language could: where obstruction threatens, where mineral concentration peaks, where the deep-ones have recently moved. This is immediate knowledge, present-tense, functional.

But there is also something else, something that functions as history, as tradition, as the persistence of the makers' intention across the long transformation into what exists now. The ceramic itself remembers, or carries memory, or is inscribed with patterns that read as memory to sufficiently attentive perception. The channel-keepers trace these patterns with fingers, with bodies pressed against wet surfaces in positions that might be contemplation, might be reading, might be something for which no surface category exists. The patterns are in the firing-textures, in the deliberate porous places, in the acoustic properties of particular vault-sections that resonate at frequencies felt more than heard.

The tools arranged in niches remember, or are arranged *as* memory, as external storage for what no single channel-keeper lifespan could retain. The curved pieces, the hollow forms, the masks—these are not used, or not used in obvious ways, but their positions shift, are maintained, are corrected when water-movement or biological process disturbs them. The channel-keepers know where each belongs. Whether they know why, whether the knowledge is conscious or procedural or inscribed in the arrangement itself, is not accessible to outside observation.

The bat-things, in their cluster-patterns, might also store, might also remember. The geometries they form—maps, calculations, representations—are stable for hours, for days, then disperse. Whether these are read by the channel-keepers, whether they constitute a parallel or prior or subsequent memory-system, whether they are simply epiphenomena of bat-biology with no information content: these questions have no settled answers. The Cist-Remembered do not explain themselves. Perhaps they cannot. Perhaps explanation is a category that requires the distance from immediacy which their permeable skins, their constant wet contact, their being-as-water, does not permit.

They know the rules, the ones the description names. They live them as fish live water, as film-dwellers live light: not as rules but as condition, as the unremarked ground of all particular remark. Water is sovereign; they are water's extension. Light is given; they are its patient accumulators. The made is alive; they are its most living part. The loudness is information; they are informed by it continuously, their bodies resonant chambers for the hollow's constant voice. The strange is normal; they have never known otherwise, could not, perhaps, conceive otherwise, though "conceive" may not be their mode of understanding. Emptiness is not loneliness; they are few, they are spread, they are each potentially complete, and their gathering is not need but quality, not compensation but addition.

And the hollow continues. This they know most deeply, most continuously, most as the ground of knowing itself. They maintain it, are maintained by it, are its maintenance. They are what lives in Verrath Cist because Verrath Cist lives in them, water in permeable skin, light in patient eye, sound in resonant body, the made become making become the continuous present of a place that has no outside, that never had an outside, that receives arrivals only to absorb them, to wet them, to teach them in the slow way of film-spread and channel-flow and bat-pattern what it means to be inside what has always been inside, what will always be, what is now and only now and the holding of breath before the next breath that is the same breath continuing.

## You, Now, Here

If you have arrived, if you are arriving, if you have always been here and only now know it—the Cist-Remembered will not reject you. They may not notice you, in any way you recognize as notice. They may observe with the patience of algae, of mineral deposition, of ceramic's slow change. They may, in time, approach: a channel-keeper emerging from water where none seemed present, leaving wet footprints that do not dry, standing at a distance that maintains the emptiness, that preserves the quality of your presence as presence rather than crowding.

They may offer, in their way, the use of tools: not handing, for their sociality does not seem to include direct transfer, but leaving, arranging, positioning where your path will find them. To accept is to enter relation. To use is to be used by, to contribute your addition to the system, your exhalation to the algal bloom, your footstep-sound to the constant loudness, your body-water to the mineral flow.

They may, in deeper time, teach: not through instruction but through the cumulative pressure of presence, of coexistence, of shared maintenance. How to read water's movement as speech. How to hear ceramic's hum as voice. How to spread where spreading is possible, to contract where contraction preserves, to become permeable where permeability allows flow and to hold where holding maintains form.

Or they may simply continue, around you, despite you, incorporating your presence as the hollow incorporates all presence: not with hostility, not with hospitality, but with the absolute neutrality of a system that has achieved, through long weathering, through the transformation of making into process, a state that requires nothing, that offers nothing, that simply is, and is, and is.

This is what lives in Verrath Cist. What you will become, if you live, if you continue, if you learn the rules that are not rules but the shape of the only possible life in a place where water is sovereign, light is given, the made is alive, the loudness is information, the strange is normal, emptiness is not loneliness, and the hollow, the hollow, the hollow continues.
