The Veth are not what they seem at first, nor what they seem at second, nor at any depth of looking. To call them inhabitants is to misplace the category of dwelling; to call them builders is to mistake the hand for the will that moves it. They *are* the place, or the place is them, or both are expressions of something that precedes the distinction between organism and environment, between the made and the grown, between the living and the merely persistent.

Consider one Veth emerging from a wall. The process is not birth. The ceramic surface of the structure *blisters*, first—a local thickening of the porous material, a concentration of the slow mineral metabolism. The blister darkens slightly, gaining opacity where the surrounding wall remains translucent with condensed moisture. Then: a seam, a crack that is also a hinge, a Veth-shaped aperture opening in the architectural surface, and from it *steps* something that matches exactly the size of the opening, that carries the texture of the wall outward into the bright damp air. It does not detach. Even at a distance of kilometers, even when carrying its ceramic block across the basin floor, the new Veth remains *continuous* with its source, connected by a thread of mineral attention that is not visible but can be felt as a pressure on the inner ear, a harmonic in the silence. When its function completes, when the block is placed and the return journey completed, it steps backward through the aperture, which heals behind it, and *is* the wall again. Or was always the wall. The distinction flickers.

Some Veth do not return. Some carry their blocks to positions that require permanent presence, that *are* the position, the block, the Veth, all three identical at some level below appearance. These are the structures that might be organs, might be art, might be memory. They remain animate, breathing, metabolizing, but no longer move. They have become *architecture* in the full sense, the original sense: not shelter but *temple*, a standing-in-place that concentrates the diffuse activity of the city into a local intensity. The tower of fused ceramic, the pools of liquid glass—these are Veth who *paused*, who found in stillness a continuation of their function more complete than motion could achieve. They are not dead. The city does not permit death, only *transformation*, only *incorporation*, only the return to substrate that is not dissolution but *diffusion*, a spreading-out that preserves pattern without preserving shape.

The Veth that remain in motion exhibit a range of *presences* that confounds the expectation of uniformity their abundance might suggest. There are differences of size, first: some are small enough to pass through openings you would not notice, that carry fragments of ceramic no larger than your fingernail across distances of meters or kilometers with the same care the largest devote to their massive blocks. Some are tall enough that their upper portions blur into the overexposed brightness, that seem to walk on legs that extend far below the visible, that carry above their interface-faces *arrangements* of porous material—crown-like, antler-like—that may be tools or may be sensory extensions or may be the mineral equivalent of thought, calculation performed in ceramic rather than neural tissue.

There are differences of surface, too. Most Veth wear the neutral off-white of fresh ceramic, the color of the breathing walls, but some carry *patina*: mineral deposits in subtle colors—blue from copper seepage, red from iron, black from something that might be organic contamination or might be deliberate incorporation of the deepest substrate. These marked Veth move differently, slower or faster than the norm, and other Veth *respond* to them in ways that suggest hierarchy or that suggest *resonance*, the marked one vibrating at a frequency that requires acknowledgment, that demands a brief pause in the carrying and fitting, a moment of stillness in the perpetual turbulence.

And there are differences of *angle*, most strange of all. The Veth are bipedal approximately, yes, but some approximate more closely than others. Some seem to walk *through* the three dimensions the way you walk through two, their orientation to gravity uncertain, their feet sometimes on walls, sometimes on ceilings of low structures, the direction of their carrying apparently dependent on a geometry you cannot perceive. The light without shadow makes this possible, or necessary: without the orienting anchor of darkness pooling below, of brightness falling from above, any surface may be ground, any direction may be forward. These angled Veth move through the city in paths that *interleave* with the paths of the gravity-bound, that pass through the same space at different dimensional registrations, that carry their blocks to positions that exist only for them, that fit into architectures invisible to your perception.

The sound of them, the *scrape click sigh*, varies with type and task and phase. Extracting a block from deep substrate produces a *groan* of ceramic under stress, a frequency that resonates in your chest more than your ears. Carrying across distance: a *rhythm*, footsteps or their equivalent, the soft percussion of mineral feet on mineral ground, sometimes synchronized among groups of Veth moving in parallel, sometimes deliberately offset, *polyrhythmic*, the city breathing in time with their collective motion. The fitting of block to position: a *chime*, a high harmonic that suggests satisfaction or that suggests only the acoustic signature of proper joinery, function and aesthetic identical in the mineral metabolism.

They do not see you, or see you as they see anything: a local variation in the damp, a temporary obstruction in the light, a texture that reads as *inhabited* in the way their own surfaces read as inhabited, by the slow processes that maintain form against entropy. You do not threaten them. You do not interest them. You are *weather*, a temporary condition of the basin floor, less significant than the mineral vapor in the atmosphere, less persistent than the condensation on ceramic surfaces. If you move among them, they flow around you as water flows around stone, without resentment, without curiosity, their paths adjusting minimally to accommodate your *thickness* in the bright air.

Unless you carry ceramic. Unless you touch the walls with hands that *shape*, that *intend*, that modify rather than merely perceive. Then: a pause in the surrounding turbulence, a local stillness more attentional than the general silence. The nearest Veth turns, or does not turn—its orientation shifts, the patterned depressions of its interface-face aligns with your position, and you feel the *pressure* of recognition, not as gaze but as *tuning*, the city adjusting to your frequency, testing for compatibility. If your shaping matches the city's metabolism, if your intention *rhymes* with the endless recycling, you may find blocks placed near your feet, tools left where you might reach them, a Veth that follows your movements not as guide but as *counterpart*, a parallel processing that incorporates your no-longer-merely-weather existence into the mineral circulation.

If your shaping *contradicts*, if you build to *keep*, to *own*, to *finish*—then the response is different. The Veth do not remove your construction; they do not need to. They *incorporate* it, extract what can be recycled, leave the residue as *aberration*, as local failure of metabolism, as scar tissue in the city's breathing. Your building becomes, over weeks or years or centuries, a *knoll*, a *tooth of older rock*, a prominence that future Veth walk around without noticing, that the damp collects on differently, that the light pools on in patterns of gentle bruise that read to you, if you return, as *forgetting*, as the city's relentless present erasing even the memory of your passage.

The weeping Veth, the ones who extract the oldest black glass and vent mineral fluid before incorporating: these are the closest to *individuals* the city permits, or the closest to *mourners*, or the closest to *historians*. The black glass is *prior*, is *other*, is material from a phase of the city's metabolism before the current ceramic organization, from the deepest palimpsest layer, from the time when the builders (if there were builders, if there was a time before the Veth were continuous with their structures) worked in different modes, with different purposes, with a *teleology* that the current perpetual present cannot assimilate without this ritual of extraction, of weeping, of *digestion through grief*. The Veth who perform this function are marked permanently, their ceramic surfaces stained dark where the mineral fluid has vented and dried, their movements slower, their paths more *directed*, less random in their carrying and fitting. They go to the oldest excavation, the deepest wound in the substrate, and they return with their black glass, and they weep, and they build, and their building *carries* the black glass differently than other materials, sets it in patterns that preserve its otherness, its priority, its *threat* to the city's eternal present.

There are Veth who do not move, who do not build, who *attend*. These gather at the pools of liquid glass, standing at intervals around the perimeter, their interface-faces directed downward, their ceramic surfaces *reflecting* the pools' slow luminescence. They do not drink; they do not touch; they *vibrate*, at frequencies too low or too high for your perception, in patterns that the liquid glass *responds* to, that organize its surface into temporary structures—ripples, standing waves, *figures* that suggest the maps of the city, or suggest the forms of ancestral Veth, or suggest nothing you have conceptual vocabulary for. This is the *pilgrimage*, the *worship*, the *computation* that other Veth interrupt their endless recycling to participate in, their carrying and fitting paused for the duration of a vibration, a contribution to the pattern that the liquid glass holds briefly before subsiding. The attending Veth do not seem to gain anything from this participation; they do not seem to lose anything. They are *nodes* in a processing that the entire city performs periodically, a *thought* that requires temporary stillness, temporary concentration, temporary *sacrifice* of the individual metabolic function to the collective mineral meditation.

And there are—rarely, unpredictably, emerging from the walls at moments that do not correlate with any pattern you can identify—Veth of *different* geometry. Not bipedal approximately. Not ceramic surfaced. Not continuous with the breathing walls in the usual way. These emerge *torn*, their shapes *wrong*, their surfaces *raw*, mineral tissue not yet finished, not yet *viable*. They move erratically, if they move; more often they *stutter* in place, half-emerged from walls that do not heal behind them, leaving wounds in the architecture that other Veth must eventually repair. They are *errors*, *mutations*, *memories of other metabolisms* that the city has tried and rejected and not fully succeeded in recycling. The other Veth do not avoid them, do not attend them; they treat them as *weather*, as temporary conditions, carrying their blocks around or occasionally *through* the stalled shapes, their ceramic surfaces scraping the raw emergence without reaction. Eventually—hours, days, years—these failed Veth dissolve, or are dissolved, are pressed back into substrate by the city's relentless processing, are *forgotten* in the only way the city forgets, which is to continue without them, around them, through them, until they are indistinguishable from the general damp, the general light, the general silence of held breath.

You might live in Veth Moraine. You might build a shelter from materials the Veth do not claim, might collect condensation for drinking, might find in the perpetual noon a version of sustenance. The Veth would not prevent this. The city would *incorporate* you slowly, the damp working at your organic surfaces, the light bleaching your pigments, the silence teaching your ears new frequencies, until you move among them not as weather but as *variant*, as *local adaptation*, your own carrying and fitting slowly rhyming with theirs, your intention diffusing into the mineral metabolism. You would not become Veth; that is not possible, the categories do not permit it. But you might become *Veth Moraine*, might find your consciousness distributed across the basin floor, your memories preserved in the patterns of your movement rather than the content of your thought, your *self* existing only in the difference you make to the condensation paths, to the light pools, to the soft acoustic of ceramic on ceramic that is the city's true language, its only narrative, its perpetual becoming and unbecoming in the bright damp turbulent silence of the sunken quarter that was never quartered, that is whole and endless and *is*, in the way a held breath is, defined by the exquisite tension of its own continuation.
