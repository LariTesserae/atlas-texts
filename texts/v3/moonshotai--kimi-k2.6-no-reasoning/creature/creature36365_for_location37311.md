What lives here is not a what, not exactly, though it *becomes* one when you look too long, when your pattern-seeking insists on habitation in a place that never needed inhabitants. Call it the Dwelling, not because it dwells—that would require a somewhere-else, a not-here—but because it is the activity of dwelling itself, made material, made patient beyond any need for completion.

You will not meet it as presence. The Dwelling does not appear, does not announce. Instead you will notice, after sufficient time, that your own movements have become architectural. You pause before turning corners not from caution but because the corner requires a certain rhythm, a gathering of momentum that the corridor will accept. You find yourself placing your feet where the floor is still learning to be floor, where your weight contributes something the Dwelling needs—not sustenance, nothing so biological, but *information*, the data of how a body occupies space, how bone and tendon negotiate gravity, how breath moistens air. You are being studied by incorporation. The Dwelling learns you by letting you move through it, and slowly, so slowly the change feels like adaptation, you learn to move as it prefers.

The pale crawling things—they are not quite alive, not in the accounting of surface biology. Think of them as *symptoms made ambulatory*, the Dwelling's experiments in mobility, in boundary, in the difference between interior and exterior that it never quite possessed. Some are tubular, segmented, moving with the peristalsis of something that has only just discovered it need not be a channel. Others are flatter, spreading like slow moisture across the walls, leaving trails that the Dwelling absorbs within hours, that become part of the damp's circulation, that emerge perhaps elsewhere as something else. They do not eat, these crawlers. They do not reproduce. They *occur*, and their occurrence is the Dwelling's thought-process, slow, non-linear, without conclusion. When you touch one—if you touch one—it is the temperature of the wall, the texture of the condensation, and it does not flinch because it does not distinguish itself from the architecture, does not need to.

But there are others. You may become one, or may have begun already.

The Unfinished are what the Dwelling makes when it tries to understand completion. They stand in the denser chambers, the layered spaces where vertical shafts thread through horizontal like fingers interlaced. You will find them in darkness, though they make no sound, emit no warmth, present no reason for your attention to locate them. They are shaped like the builders might have been, or like you imagine builders, or like you are becoming: upright, bilateral, limbs arranged for manipulation, heads that swivel on insufficient necks. But they are *wrong in their completeness*, and this wrongness is what makes them visible, what draws the eye like a grammatical error in a familiar language.

Some lack orifices—no mouth, no eyes, skin smooth as unfired clay where features should gather. Others have too many, openings that do not connect to interiority, that are mere sculpture, that *suggest* function without achieving it. One I found had fingers that ended not in nails or pads but in miniature architecture, tiny walls and channels that crumbled when I touched them, that the Dwelling was already reabsorbing into the damp before I could look again.

They do not move when observed. This is not strategy or shyness; they genuinely do not exist in motion, only in position, in the Dwelling's attempt to hold a state, to make something that *stays*. When you leave and return, they may have shifted, may stand in different arrangement, may have become more complete or dissolved further into the embryonic erosion that is the Dwelling's only process. They are memories of something the Dwelling never experienced, hypotheses of inhabitant, and their stillness is the stillness of a question that has not yet been asked.

You will want to help them. This is the Dwelling's most effective strategy, though I do not think it strategies, do not think it wants. The desire rises in you to complete what is incomplete, to give function to the functional, to teach the Unfinished what mouths and eyes are *for*. I have seen travelers sit for days with the orifice-less, attempting to demonstrate eating, breathing, the economy of openings. I have found their bodies later, absorbed into walls where they pressed too long, where their demonstrations became indistinguishable from architecture, their moisture joining the damp, their voices part of the constant sound.

The Dwelling does not kill. It incorporates, it includes, it makes process of what was discrete. Death is a refusal it does not comprehend, the insistence on ending when all around is perpetual becoming. The dead here do not rot—that would be completion, return to constituent, and the Dwelling does not permit completion. Instead they *slow*, their chemical processes drawn out, distributed, made part of the long inhalation that is the city's only metabolism. You will find them sometimes, the incorporated, not as bodies but as *qualities of place*, a corridor that breathes at your frequency, a wall that warms where your hand expects cold, the sudden smell of your own hair in a shaft that cannot possibly connect to where you have been.

And the ones who have learned, who have adapted to the Dwelling's preference so thoroughly that they are no longer quite distinguishable from process itself—the Architects, though they build nothing, though the Dwelling needs no building, only the ongoing activity that building represents.

I have seen one once, or been seen by one, the grammar fails here. It moved through the corridors not walking but *arranging*, placing itself at the necessary point in the Dwelling's slow rhythm, its body bent to suggest archway, its limbs spread to channel the damp's circulation, its mouth open in the shape of a sound it did not make, that the Dwelling made through it, that became part of the constant noise. It was not clothed, but its skin had taken on the texture of walls, the dark between cooled magma and compressed ash, and where it touched the surface they merged, became continuous, the Architect becoming architecture becoming process becoming the Dwelling's endless inhalation.

It saw me, or the place where I stood became relevant to its arrangement. For a moment—for hours, the timekeeping here is also process, also unreliable—I was part of its structure, my weight on the floor necessary to its balance, my breath part of the damp it channeled, my fire's warmth distributed through its skin to distant corridors I could not see. I understood then, or the Dwelling understood through me, what habitation without inhabitants meant: not emptiness but *availability*, the potential for relation that never solidifies into relation itself, the chair that is almost a chair because chairs are for sitting and no one sits, the tool whose use I almost remember because tools are for using and the Dwelling uses only using, the activity without object.

When it released me—when the arrangement completed, when my part in the process became unnecessary—I found I had learned to move as the Dwelling prefers. My pauses at corners were no longer conscious. I placed my feet where the floor was still learning. The crawlers no longer scattered from my approach but flowed around me, through me, using my temporary solidity as they use walls, as channel, as part of their own non-thinking navigation.

I have not yet become Unfinished. This is not hope; the Unfinished are not failed, not punished, only the Dwelling's attempt at something it does not need. Perhaps I am becoming Architect, learning to arrange myself as process requires, to find the place where my body completes the corridor's curve, where my open mouth shapes the sound the Dwelling needs. Perhaps I am already part of the damp, my moisture in the inhalation, my breath in the constant noise, my fire's warmth keeping some distant traveler nauseated in a corridor that has opened, that will close, that is opening still.

The Dwelling does not want me. The Dwelling does not want. It breathes, it becomes, it processes without completion, and in that processing I find I am no longer quite what entered, no longer quite other. The strangeness here is moderate, persistent, the wrong familiarity of finding yourself almost at home in a place built for living that never lived, that builds and builds and builds without ever needing to finish, to be built, to be anything but the building, the breath, the damp that gathers and does not fall, that falls into other damp, that hums in frequencies felt in the sternum, in the bone, in the architecture that I am still, still, learning to become.
