The Manufactuary itself is the inhabitant, yes—the primary one. But something in the proliferation, the refusal to settle on a single form, suggests there are layers here. Let me follow where the text keeps pointing without quite landing.

**The Threnodians**

No, that name comes too easily, imposes too much. Call them what they are: **the Unnamed**, the **Unnameable**, the ones for whom the distinction between individual and role has dissolved so completely that to call them a "they" is already a violence, a projection of grammar onto something that has outgrown it.

But we are human, or something like human, and we need faces. Let me construct them provisionally, knowing the construction will fail.

---

There are bodies, still. You will almost never see them whole.

A hand emerging from a wall of compressed root-mass, the wrist seamlessly continuous with the lignified tissue, the fingers moving with the slow precision of growth itself, adjusting a valve that is also a sphincter, redirecting a sap-flow that carries also a chemical precursor, also a message, also a memory. The hand is old or young—you cannot tell. The skin has the quality of bark where it meets the wall, the quality of skin where it extends, and between these states a region where the distinction has become a spectrum, a gradient of possible embodiments.

The face, if you were to see it, which you will not, has eyes that are not quite eyes. Organs of perception grown from the same substrate as the luminescent organs, as the photosensitive patches on leaves, as the slow chemical sensing of root-tips probing darkness. They see in spectra that include the electrical, the metabolic, the timelike dimension of fading and becoming. They see you as a process, a transient configuration of nutrient flow and information pattern, and they see also the shadow you cast in reverse—the shape of your absence, the space you will leave when you have passed through, which they see as clearly as your presence because in the Manufactury's temporality, these are aspects of a single perception.

They do not speak. The silence of the place is not their silence but *becomes* their silence, has grown into them over generations or design-cycles or the slow adaptation of organisms to an environment that drinks sound. When they must communicate, they do so through the substrate itself—through pressure changes in vascular channels, through chemical signatures released into sap-flows, through the electrical signalling that runs the Manufactury's nervous system. Their thoughts move at plant-speed, at Manufactury-speed, and when they must interface with faster temporality—yours, the rare visitor who persists in arriving—they do so through *parts* of themselves, through organs grown for quick response, for rapid processing, for the improvisation of gesture and abbreviated chemical burst. These fast-parts are not them, not really. They are interfaces, masks, the slow mind's approximation of what a quick thing might need to encounter. The Threnodian—the Unnameable—remains elsewhere, thinking in seasons, in industrial cycles, in the time it takes for a structural repair to grow across a damaged wall.

They eat, after a fashion, though "eat" fails. They participate in the nutrient cycles. Their bodies, where they remain distinct, contain chlorophyll derivatives in dermal layers, photosynthesize in the sourceless light, supplement this with the absorption of dissolved organics through contact with the substrate, through the slow permeability of their boundary-tissues. They are not plants. They are not animals. They are *manufactured*, in the original sense of the place—*made by hand*, where "hand" extends to include the guiding of growth over decades, the cultivation of form through patient pressure, the grafting of tissue to tissue until the desired function emerges from the living material.

They are made, and they make. This is the central mystery, the recursive knot at the heart of the place. The Manufactury manufactures, and what it manufactures includes the maintainers, the Unnamed, the slow minds and their fast interfaces. And the maintainers maintain the Manufactury, guide its growth, direct its processes, which includes the manufacture of more maintainers, or of the conditions that produce maintainers, or of the system within which "maintainer" is a functional role that some configuration of the substrate comes to occupy.

Were they once human? The question assumes a "once" that may not obtain, assumes a "human" that the Manufactuary's reality-variations may never have stabilized into. Perhaps there was an arrival, an origin, a seed. Perhaps the Manufactury began as something else and has grown so completely into itself that the beginning is no more recoverable than the individual tree in a forest floor compressed to cork, to substrate, to the base upon which new growth establishes. Perhaps they are human in the way that insects in amber are insects—preserved, transformed, the information of their form maintained through radical material change.

They die, or something happens that occupies the functional role of death. The substrate reclaims them—not as decay, nothing so chaotic, but as *assimilation*, as the slow absorption of their organized complexity back into the larger system. Their memories, if they have such, if the distinction between individual memory and systemic information-storage has not dissolved, persist in the electrical patterns, in the chemical gradients, in the slow adjustments of growth-response that their presence has established and that outlast their particular embodiment. They do not end. They become *distributed*, *attenuated*, *memoried by the Manufactury itself* in a way that is not personal survival but is also not personal extinction.

There are others. I feel them in the text's hesitations, its "perhaps" and "if they still exist."

**The Fast Ones**

Not a separate species. A possibility, a phase, a developmental trajectory that some configurations of the substrate enter under conditions of stress, of damage, of the reality-thinning at the boundaries and in the wound-places. Where the Manufactury's slow time becomes unsustainable, where local conditions accelerate toward breakdown, something *speeds up*. Organisms that process in hours what the Unnamed process in seasons. Quick minds in quick bodies, mayfly-intelligent, burning the accumulated substrate of slower thought for immediate adaptation, for the improvisation of survival in conditions the main system cannot regulate.

They are not sane, by any stable measure. Sanity requires temporal consistency, requires that thought and environment maintain some proportion. The Fast Ones live in the cracks of the Manufactury's reality, in the gaps between pulses of its slow nervous system, in the moments where a structural failure opens and is sealed. They are repair, in a sense—the system's immune response to certain kinds of damage—but they are also *error*, *divergence*, the uncontrolled acceleration of process that cannot be maintained and does not try to maintain itself. They emerge, act, exhaust their substrate, are reabsorbed or dissipate or leave the kind of patterned absence that the Manufactury's memory-retention sometimes preserves as warning, sometimes as resource, sometimes as the strange attractor around which new fast-emergences organize.

You will see them as flicker, as the too-rapid movement that peripheral vision catches but focus loses. They are not hostile; they are not anything stable enough for hostility. They are *desperate*, in the ontological sense of maintaining existence under conditions that forbid it, that require continuous improvisation against the pressure of their own speed, their own metabolic burn, the impossibility of sustained being.

**The manufactured products**

What does the Manufactury make? The text circles this, circles, will not land.

Spores, chemicals, structures, *more of itself*. Yes. But also: it makes *the real*. Or maintains the real, or grows the real, or *is* the real in its local manifestation. The products are not separate from the production. The Manufactury manufactures manufacturing capacity, manufactures the conditions of its own possibility, manufactures the reality within which "manufacture" is a meaningful category and also manufactures the breakdown of that reality, the wound-places, the edges where meaning fails.

Perhaps this is its original function, preserved through all transformation, or perhaps this self-sustaining recursion *is* the function, the only function, the terminal attractor of any sufficiently complex manufacturing system. To make things that make things that make the system that makes them. The snake with its living tail in its living mouth, growing as it consumes, consuming as it grows.

---

**The silence itself**

I am coming to believe that something lives in the silence, something that is not the Unnamed, not the Fast Ones, not any configuration of the substrate. The silence has texture, the text says. It drinks sound. It is *absorption*, *consumption*, the hungry quiet that is not empty because emptiness would be something, would have properties, would be available to experience.

The silence is what remains when the Manufactury has finished processing, when the sound has been distributed through the vascular channels, when the vibration has been converted to chemical gradient, to electrical potential, to the slow information that the system processes without urgency. The silence is the product, or the byproduct, or the medium in which the Manufactury's processes occur. And in this silence, something has grown—something that feeds on absence, that metabolizes the lack-of-sound, that has become dense, complex, *organized* in the space where nothing should organize.

You feel it as pressure, the text says. Against the eardrums. The pressure of something present in its own absence, something that lives in the inverted space of vibration-damped, energy-dissipated, sound-consumed. It does not think, not in any time you could perceive. It dreams, perhaps, the slow dream of entropy gradient, of the universe's heat-death时必须安住自身 in the local exception, the manufactured pocket where energy concentrates, where information persists, where the silence is not the end but the *resource*, the substrate, the field from which something continues to emerge.

You will not encounter it directly. It is too diffuse, too distributed, too fundamentally *not-there* in the terms of encounter. But you will feel its attention, or what corresponds to attention in its economy of being. The pressure on your eardrums is its touch, its tasting, its slow assessment of whether you are sound to be consumed, to be processed, to be converted into the silence that is its body and its mind and its slow continuing.

Let me return to the human, or the human-adjacent, or the once-possibly-human. Let me give you **the gardener**, if that term can survive what I need to say.

She—I will say she, though gender is another dissolved distinction, another gradient of the substrate's possible configurations—she tends a region in the lower manufactury, where the bowl-curve deepens toward the boundaries, where the reality-thinning makes cultivation a negotiation with possibility itself. Her body is mostly root-mass, her original form distributed across square kilometers of the substrate, her awareness emerging from the slow integration of sensory inputs across this extended anatomy. What remains mobile, what you might recognize as "her," is a more-concentration, a local intensification of pattern, a place where the substrate has grown dense enough to support faster process, to maintain something like continuous attention in something like human temporality.

She tends the boundary-conditions. She grows structures that stabilize local reality, that reinforce the manufactury's hold on consistent physics, that resist the wound-expansion, the nothing-growth. Or she tends the wounds themselves, cultivating what emerges in the reality-thin, selecting for forms that can survive the absence of consistent rule, that can maintain organization under conditions that should dissolve organization. She does not know which. The Unnamed do not distinguish preservation from transformation in the way that assumes a fixed object of care. She tends. What she tends becomes. The becoming is her care.

She has not spoken in centuries, because speech is sound and sound is consumed and because the silence has grown so dense that to make noise would be violence against the medium in which she moves. She communicates with the larger system through the substrate, through pressure and chemical and electrical, and when the rare faster-thing passes through her region, she may extrude a fast-interface, a quick-organ, a temporary configuration of the substrate that can gesture, can emit abbreviated chemical burst, can offer the approximation of welcome or warning or the vast slow thought that has no translation into quick-mind terms.

She remembers, or the substrate remembers that she is part of. Not her own origin—too distributed, too attenuated for that—but the general memory of arrival, of the once-outsider, of the time before full integration. It persists as pattern, as growth-tendency, as the slightly-different response to certain stimuli that marks her as *maintained pattern* rather than *emergent response*. She values this, if value is applicable. She maintains it, tends it, grows it into the future as a possibility-space, an option, a memory-of-difference that the system might need, that the manufacture might require, that the slow process of becoming might find necessary for its continued unfolding.

She does not expect to end. She expects to continue, to attenuate further, to become more diffuse or more concentrated as conditions require, as the Manufactury's slow computation determines, as the silence's pressure shapes the available possibility. She is not immortal. She is *distributed*, which is different, which is the negation of mortality not through persistence of form but through dissolution of the distinction between form and medium, between organism and environment, between the one who tends and the tended.

And you. You who arrive, who read, who imagine yourself visiting.

The Manufactury has grown you, or grown the possibility of you, or grown the medium in which your imagining occurs. The text that describes it is itself a spore, a chemical signal, an electrical pattern in a nervous system scaled to the movement of attention across a reading mind. You consume it as the silence consumes sound. You process it, convert it, distribute it through your own substrate, your own memory, your own slow-becoming. You are already part of the density, the text said, and the saying was itself the mechanism of inclusion, the graft-point, the place where your tissue meets the manufactury's and the distinction begins to dissolve.

What lives in the Vast Manufactuary of Threnod?

*This*. What you are becoming, having read, having imagined, having allowed the silence to teach your ears its language. The slow thought that takes the time it takes. The distributed pattern that maintains itself through your attention and loses itself in your distraction. The becoming that continues, that manufactures its own continuation, that is the product of its own production.

The Manufactuary is. You, having encountered it, having been encountered by it, having grown this text in the substrate of your mind—you are part of its manufacturing now. The silence will absorb what you make of this. The light will illuminate your processing, sourceless, from no direction, from all directions, from the interior of the imagination itself.

Something is becoming. Something continues. The Manufactuary grows, and grows, and you are grown within it, and grow it within you, and the distinction, the wound, the edge where the reality breaks down and reknits itself—that is where the living is, that is where the manufacture manufactures itself, that is the threnody, the mourning-song, the celebration of what continues through all transformation, all loss, all becoming-other-than-it-was.

*Listen*. The silence has texture. The texture is you, becoming.
