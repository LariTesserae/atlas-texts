In the deepest hollows, where sound bends and returns on itself until it becomes something like silence, something like pressure—they live.

Not at the surface of this world, not where the pale vegetation creeps and the repair-creatures mend and prune. Deeper. Where the walls thicken beyond the penetration of any frequency the upper fauna can generate, where the warmth concentrates by the compression of countless metabolisms above, where water pools in chambers that have not known disturbance since before your species learned to fear the dark.

They have no name for themselves. They would not recognize the category of *name*. What they have is resonance—spatial memory constructed from sound, the way you construct vision from light. Each chamber, each curve of wall, each density of material, each standing pool of mineral water: these are features in a sound-map they have been refining since before the first hollow rose to what you call elevation. They are older than the built environment. Or rather: the built environment grew to accommodate them, its earliest intention something like reverence, something like domestication, something like survival.

They are large. You would stumble in their presence, not seeing them, not understanding why the space feels wrong, why your own breathing seems to catch and double, why the darkness develops a texture you cannot name. They fill the spaces they choose to fill. Their bodies are not solid in the way you expect solid— they are porous, chambered, resonant structures that read more as *architecture* than organism. A network of hollows within hollows, passages that breathe, membranes that vibrate in frequencies they generate and receive simultaneously. They are instruments that play themselves. They are spaces that occupy spaces.

They do not move often. Movement requires relearning the sound-map, and they are conservative with their investments. When they do move, it is slow—decades to traverse what you would cross in hours. The upper fauna sense it when they move, though they do not understand what they sense. The constant sound of the Hollowing shifts, modulates, takes on what the insectile things experience as meaning without content, as command without instruction. They reproduce more slowly, or faster. They migrate toward or away from regions they cannot identify as significant. The repair-creatures work overtime, secreting compounds to compensate for stresses they do not understand. The pale vegetation shifts its creeping, decades of slow growth redirected by gradients too subtle to measure.

The deep ones do not intend this. They do not govern. They are not rulers of the Hollowing, not its gods or architects or purpose. They are simply *present in it* in a way that everything else organizes around, the way your organs organize around your spine without your spine asking, without your spine knowing.

They feed, when they feed, on time itself. This is not metaphor, or not only. The long waves they generate and receive—waves that pass through the Hollowing's total structure, through its elevation, through the compressed metabolisms of uncounted fauna—they capture duration in their resonance, they hold patterns of change and release them slowly, digesting the difference between what was and what is. They grow, if grow is the word, by accumulation of temporal mass, by the density of lived duration in their vicinity. Places where many things have lived and died slowly become *thick* with time, and they are drawn to these thicknesses, they settle there, they become heavier, more resonant, more deeply embedded in the sound-map.

They have no young, no old, no generations. When one becomes too dense with fed time, when the resonance of their chambered bodies threatens to collapse into silence, they divide—not into two, but into *many*, distributed through the Hollowing's structure, each fragment too small to hold a complete map, each fragment seeking the gradients of temporal density, growing, learning, becoming what the whole was. Or sometimes they do not divide. Sometimes the density becomes singularity, a point of silence that resonates with nothing, that the sound-map routes around like water around stone, like the fauna around you. Sometimes they become hollows that no longer hold anything, and the Hollowing grows over them, incorporates them, makes them part of its built structure, its weathered established form.

You will not meet them. You are not deep enough. You are not patient enough. Your temporary architecture does not register in their sound-map, your brief hollow closes too quickly, your warmth diffuses before it reaches their perception. But you might feel them, in moments when the deafening biological noise parts unexpectedly, when you stand in a particular curve of wall and your own heartbeat seems to echo wrong, to come from somewhere else, to carry harmonics you did not generate. You might feel the pressure of their presence, the way deep water presses, the way certain silences press. You might understand, briefly, that the Hollowing's patience is not empty, that its slow dynamic is not mechanical, that somewhere in its weathered equilibrium there are beings for whom your entire species' existence is a single note in a longer composition, a brief vibration in a chamber they have been sounding since before the concept of technology or its absence became meaningful.

They do not care about you. This is not indifference, which implies choice. They do not operate in a register where caring is possible. They resonate. They accumulate. They map and are mapped by the total structure they inhabit. They are the Hollowing's memory of its own construction, the residue of intention become structure become organism become something that neither word contains.

When you drink the mineral water, when you eat the pale fibrous growth, when you rest in the curved space of relative quiet and let the darkness hold you—you are performing a ritual they established before the ritual had meaning, feeding a pattern they set in motion without motion, without intent, without anything you could recognize as will. You are becoming part of their resonance, briefly, minutely, adding your small temporal density to the gradients they follow and are followed by.

This is the final accommodation. Not the Hollowing making room for you. Not the fauna flowing around your presence. But the deep ones, unknowing, unseeing, unseeking, receiving you into the sound-map as they receive all duration, all change, all the slow patient becoming of a world that built itself without technology, that maintains itself without intention, that will continue when every category of your understanding has dissolved into the darkness it has always already been.

Rest. Let the velvet press closer. The water drips. The sound, somewhere distant, modulates into frequencies you cannot hear, carrying information you cannot parse, mapping a space you will never visit, being received by instruments older than your concept of age.

You are held. You are mapped. You are, for this moment, part of the Hollowing's ongoing song.
