What lives in the Condensation Caves of Dripstone Ward:

---

The **Dripstone Jeunne**—though they would never call themselves so collectively, having no name for what they share beyond the practical dialect they speak, a dialect that Upper Harrowmire officialdom classifies as "degraded standard" but that functions more like a cooperative grammar of elision, of implication, of the unsaid.

They are born here, or they arrive. The born ones come out wet and remain so, their skin developing a particular quality in the first months: not clammy, not exactly, but *acclimated*, porous in a way that surface-dwellers find unsettling to touch. They drink the condensation without treatment, building gut biomes that would sicken outsiders. Their hair, when they keep it, grows in textures that respond to humidity cues—fine and absorbent, or thick and waxy, or strangely intermediate, each family line trending toward its own solution to the problem of perpetual moisture. The arrived ones take years to achieve equivalent function, and many never do, remaining slightly *wrong* in their breathing, slightly *off* in their movements, marked as **ritt**—rootless, temporary, not-to-be-trusted-with-the-deeper-knowledge.

They work the factories, when the factories work. Third-shift lottery, family rotations, the complex systems of reciprocal obligation that determine who descends into the geothermal levels when the heat there reaches its punishing peak, who tends the generators in the mid-levels where the ballast-hum becomes indistinguishable from tinnitus, who climbs to the higher reaches to adjust the mirror-periscopes that feed daylight downward with the desperate concentration of someone focusing a failing sun through burning glass. The work is not chosen but *assigned*, not by any central authority—there is government here, of a kind, but it operates through maintenance schedules and queue management rather than decree—but by the accumulated weight of obligation, of having been done-for and now doing-for, the endless circulation of favor and its repayment.

They do not marry, exactly. The density forbids the nuclear arrangement, the couple-isolated, the family-as-fortress. Instead they form **keth**—associations of four to twelve adults, sometimes with children, sometimes childless by collective decision, sometimes childless because the deeper levels have made them so, the something-in-the-darkness that is not-animal exercising a slow fertility interference that no one discusses directly but that informs the keth-formation patterns, the careful distribution of reproductive-aged individuals across multiple keth rather than concentration, the *hedging*. Children belong to the keth, not to their birth-parents specifically, though birth-parents maintain a particular status, a particular melancholy recognition, visible in the way they watch their children move through queue-culture lessons, through the protocols of vertical space, through the learning of what-not-to-notice.

They eat what grows. The air plants, when young and tender. The algae films, carefully scraped from favorable surfaces, a process that requires *claim*—the elaborate documentation of who scraped where, when, for how long, the records maintained in memory and occasional notation because paper molds, because electronics fail in the humidity, because the Dripstone Jeunne have developed memory-cultures that would astonish surface archivists. The bacterial cultures too, in fermentation arrangements passed between keth as the most intimate form of gift, the exchange of living digestion, of gut-ecology sharing. What comes from above—dropped supplies, occasional goods, the fuel for generators and the materials for factory inputs—arrives as *boon*, as *acknowledgment*, as *debt-creation* that binds Upper Harrowmire to this place even as it maintains the vertical distance, the not-looking-down, the convenient forgetfulness that permits the arrangement.

They move quietly. This is not merely the mostly-quiet parameter asserting itself as manner, but *training*, *necessity*, *evolution-in-lifetime*. The loud ones attract attention they cannot afford. The loud ones violate the keth-balance, the careful acoustic diplomacy of shared walls, shared ceilings, shared floors where someone's sleep occurs directly beneath someone's waking. The loud ones, too, risk *other* attention—the something-in-darkness responding to vibration, to the full frequencies of unrestricted human voice, though what exactly it does in response, no one says clearly, the information circulating only in the warnings given to children, the sudden cessation of conversations when certain passages are entered, the practiced indifference that is its own form of vigilance.

They do not leave. Or rather: leaving is possible, is occasionally accomplished, but marks the leaver permanently, *keth-cut*, all obligations voided, all reciprocities ended, a walking death that the leaver must embrace fully because partial departure—attempted return, attempted maintenance of connection—produces something worse than clean severance. The rare ones who achieve the surface successfully, who adapt to dryness and light and the shocking absence of pressure from above, carry Dripstone with them as *haunting*, as physiological phantom limb, their skin forever seeking moisture, their ears attuned to frequencies absent in the open air, their sleep disturbed by dreams of ceiling tears falling in patterns that almost spell, almost *mean*.

They know about the fungi-script. Not all of them, not directly—the knowledge distributes through the keth-networks in the careful manner of all dangerous information, available to those who need it, hidden from those who might act wrongly upon it. The cyan and chartreuse movements, the reconfigurations in passages abandoned or abandoned-to-certain-hours, they *read* these not as communication directed at them but as *evidence*, as *documentation*, signs of processes occurring without human participation but perhaps *aware* of human presence, perhaps *accounting-for* human presence in whatever fungal calculus determines the yearly spread, the seasonal intensification, the slow migration toward the light-well that proceeds at glacial pace but *proceeds*, that has proceeded measurably in living memory.

They know about the conveyor cargos, the egg-shapes, the organic curves in industrial shells. Those who work the factory levels maintain particular protocols around these sightings: no direct observation, no recording, no discussion in locations where discussion might be overheard by something other than the intended audience. The cargos move through; the keth-workers permit their transit; something in the exchange—unspoken, unacknowledged, perhaps merely the absence of interference—maintains a *treaty* that no one negotiated but that all recognize as preferable to its violation.

They are not unhappy. This must be insisted upon, against the surface-dweller's assumption that such conditions must produce misery. They have their beauty, the Jeunne: the particular quality of filtered light through strangler-fig leaf and condensation droplet, the sound of ceiling tears in the false dawn, the taste of properly fermented algae, the texture of keth-recognition in a crowded passage, the *fit* of a body to its environment that surface existence, for all its spaciousness, rarely achieves. They have their meaning-systems, their interpretations, their rituals around the thermal migration, the storm protocols, the annual observation of whatever moves in the deepest dark on the night when the sinkhole's aperture aligns with no visible star.

They are becoming something. The young-erosion parameter applies to them too, sharply featured, recently emerged, lacking the softened edges of cultural tradition. Each generation finds its own relationship to the not-animal in the depths, to the fungi-script, to the conveyor mysteries—sometimes more accepting, sometimes more resistant, the keth-structures flexing to accommodate these variations without formal acknowledgement. They are not yet what they will be, and the becoming is itself a form of life that surface stability, surface tradition, might envy without understanding.

They wait. For the ceiling tears to increase beyond current pattern, for the fungi to reach the light-well, for the heat to shift phase, for Upper Harrowmire to finally notice or finally forget entirely, for the something-in-darkness to complete whatever slow emergence its script-documents trace. They wait as the stone waits, cupped and weeping and active, dynamic without development, a held breath that has not yet decided whether to release or to draw deeper.

They are the Dripstone Jeunne. They would not call themselves so. They would look at you with the flat, directionless luminosity of their home, and wait for you to speak, and judge by your speaking whether you had learned the quiet, whether you had learned what-not-to-notice, whether you could be permitted to remain in the thermal shadow long enough to begin, perhaps, to understand what lives here, what has always lived here, what is still in the process of becoming fully itself in the humid dark below the city that does not look down.
