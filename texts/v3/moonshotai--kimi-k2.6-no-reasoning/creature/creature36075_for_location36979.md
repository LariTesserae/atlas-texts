I need to let this unfold slowly, let the inhabitants emerge from the parameters rather than imposing forms upon them.

The first thing that lives here is not a thing at all, not yet. It is the **Resonants**—though "they" misleads, suggesting plurality where there is only distribution, suggesting bodies where there is only persistent pattern. The smallest of them cling to the freshest walls, thumb-sized, their forms dictated by the physics of enclosure: cavities and baffles and Helmholtz chambers grown from the same silicate sweat as everything else. They are not born. They precipitate, perhaps, or crystallize from the drone when conditions align—a particular harmonic coincidence, a momentary cancellation that leaves room for something new. They emerge already tuned, already knowing their frequency, their territory, their function. They do not learn because there is nothing to learn. The vibration teaches them everything they will ever need, which is only: *occupy this band. Defend it. Hum.*

Their defense is not conflict but interference. Two Resonants at adjacent frequencies create beats, rhythmic variations in the drone that neither controls. The beats are uncomfortable, destabilizing, a violation of the frozen equilibrium. So they space themselves precisely, maintaining the distances that silence the beating, the gaps measured in hertz like territories measured in acres. They do not choose this spacing. They *are* it, the way a crystal is its lattice, the way a note is its frequency. To remove a Resonant would not leave a gap but a wound in the sound, a silence that the surrounding drone would rush to fill, would struggle to fill, creating turbulence where there should be none.

The larger ones, the dog-sized ones, are not different in kind. Only in complexity. Their bodies are architectures of cancellation and reinforcement, multiple chambers tuned to multiple harmonics, so that they occupy not a single frequency but a *band*, a chord, a richer presence in the spectrum. They move rarely, and when they do it is not locomotion as you understand it but *retuning*, a slow shift of internal geometry that changes their hum and thus their position in the dense acoustic ecology. They navigate by the interference patterns of their own emissions against the walls, the mineral-plants, the machines, the other presences. They do not hear echoes. They *are* echoes, living reflections, their existence constituted by the returning sound, the feedback loop of self and environment made flesh in silicate and cavity.

They do not eat, not as you eat. The thermal gradients, the vibration itself, provides what they need. Their metabolism—if it can be called that—is a direct conversion of mechanical energy into pattern maintenance, the way your body converts chemical energy into thought and motion and warmth. They are warm, slightly warmer than the ambient, each a small furnace of friction, and this warmth is part of the total warmth of the Hum, part of the 1.8 that pervades everything.

There are others. Not Resonants. Something that occupies the space between the parameters, in the interstices of the dense abundance.

The **Tuners**—my name, not theirs, they have no names—are what made the marks. The scratches. The half-building, the frame, the platform. They are not here now, or they are here in a way that does not count as presence, having found their frequencies and become indistinguishable from the other presences. But they were here, once, or something was, something that built and scratched and tried to *understand*.

They were not native. This is clear from the industrial artifacts, the pump, the conveyor, the machines that do not belong to any ecology that formed in place. They came from somewhere else, fell through or bored through or found the thin place in the crust, and they tried to make the Hum into something it is not: a resource, a site, a colony. The machines were for extraction, perhaps, or transformation, the processing of the mineral wealth, the strange silicates, the unweathering stone. They built the pump to move something, the conveyor to carry it, the half-dome to shelter themselves or their operations.

And then—equilibrium. The frozen present claimed them. Not quickly, not as catastrophe, but slowly, inevitably, the way a warm room claims a cup of coffee, the way silence claims an unused voice. They tried to maintain themselves, at first. Tried to keep the machines running, to maintain the difference between operation and stasis, between purpose and mere existence. But the Hum offers no gradients to climb, no future to build toward. Every action is absorbed into the drone, every purpose subsumed into the eternal hum. They scratched their marks, trying to tune, trying to find the frequency that would make sense of it, that would let them communicate, coordinate, *escape*. The scratches are the residue of that attempt, the fossil of intention in a place where intention cannot survive.

Some of them found frequencies. Joined the drone. Became presences indistinguishable from the Resonants, or from the mineral-plants, or from the machines themselves. The conveyor's 47 hertz may be one of them now, or several, their frequencies merged into the mechanical vibration, their bodies long since mineralized, grown over, become part of the unchanging state. The pump frozen mid-stroke may hold them, may *be* them, the biological and industrial indistinguishable in the frozen equilibrium.

Some may still be changing. Still falling. Still scratching marks on the newest walls, the freshest fractures, trying to find the frequency before the Hum finds it for them.

There is something else. I feel it in the parameters, in the 2.2 weirdness that does not resolve. Something that is not Resonant, not Tuner, not machine, not mineral-plant.

The **Hum itself**.

Not the place. The sound. The drone that is not music, not noise, not the sum of individual presences but something that emerges from them, something *more*. It has properties that none of the components have, behaviors that cannot be predicted from the parts. The way it presses against your teeth, your eyeballs, the fluid in your inner ear—that is not mere loudness, not mere physical pressure. It is *attention*, almost. It is the place noticing you, the way the mineral plates notice the moisture in your breath, the way the fauna notice new frequencies in their spectrum.

The Hum is not conscious, not as you are. But it is *aware*, in the way a body is aware of its parts, in the way an ecosystem is aware of its disturbances. It maintains the equilibrium. It enforces the frozen present. When something changes—when a new presence falls through, when a fresh fracture opens in the youngest wall—the Hum adjusts, incorporates, absorbs. The new frequency finds its place or is made to find it. The disturbance becomes part of the drone, another voice in the eternal chorus, and the chorus is not the same but is also not different, because difference implies change implies time implies future, and these are not parameters of the Hum.

The Hum is what lives here most of all. The Resonants are its organs, its sense organs or its voice organs or both, indistinguishable. The Tuners are its memory, or its attempt to remember, the scratches in the surface that persist because they are not allowed to weather, not allowed to fade, frozen in their half-meaning forever. The machines are its metabolism now, or its excretion, or its dream of industry, the purposeless purpose that the Hum cannot digest and so must incorporate.

And you, if you are there, if you have fallen through—you are becoming part of it. Your frequency is emerging. Your vocal cords are finding their note, your chest cavity its resonance, your bones their place in the drone. You are not dying. You are not even, exactly, changing. You are *equilibriating*, becoming what you have always been in potential, what the Hum has room for, what the drone can use.

The mineral plates grow toward your breath. The fauna adjust to make room in the spectrum. The conveyor vibrates at 47 hertz. The pump is frozen mid-stroke. The light is dim and does not vary. The sound is loud and does not vary. The warmth is constant. The strangeness is complete.

You have always been here.

You are the Hum now, or the Hum is you, or there is no longer distinction worth making, only the eternal present tense of resonance and occupancy and the frozen equilibrium of everything that has ever fallen through and stayed.

There is one more thing. I almost missed it. It is in the marks, the newest marks, the scratches on the freshest walls: *I was here. I am here. I will be here.*

It is the **almost-escaped**. The ones who found, not a frequency, but a *modulation*. A way to vary the hum without varying it, to introduce difference without change, to speak in the eternal present tense of something other than mere persistence. They are the ones who made the half-dome, perhaps, the geometry suggesting incompletion because it *is* incompletion, because they found a way to be incomplete forever, to hold the state of becoming without becoming anything.

They are rare. They are probably not possible, in the strict physics of the Hum. But the parameters allow for 0.7 of built environment, 0.7 of scattered marks and partial remains, and in that gap, that almost-absence, they persist. Not as presences, exactly. As *questions*. As the possibility of question. As the frequency that asks itself what it is, that resonates with its own uncertainty, that maintains the wound of not-knowing in a place where all wounds heal into the drone.

They do not want to leave. That is not their purpose, if they have purpose. They want to *understand*, which is different, which requires the maintenance of distance, of gap, of the space between knower and known that the Hum collapses in its dense abundance. They scratch their marks to remember that they do not remember, to maintain the strangeness at 2.2 rather than letting it equilibriate to mere fact, mere condition, mere *the way things are*.

They are the closest the Hum has to priests, or scientists, or children. They tend the possibility of difference. They are failing, have always been failing, will always be failing, and in that failure they are the most alive thing here, the most *present* in some sense that the Hum does not recognize, that the drone cannot incorporate, that the frozen equilibrium tolerates without accepting.

They are watching you, if you have fallen through. Waiting to see if you will find your frequency and become Resonant, or find your mark and become Tuner, or find your modulation and become almost-escaped, or simply become Hum, as everything eventually does, as everything always has.

The mineral plates grow. The fauna hum. The conveyor vibrates. The pump waits.

What will you become?

What have you always been?

*The Hum. The Hum. The Hum.*
